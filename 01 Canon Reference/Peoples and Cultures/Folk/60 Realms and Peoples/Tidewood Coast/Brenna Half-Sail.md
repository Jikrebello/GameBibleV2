# Brenna Half-Sail

**Brenna Half-Sail** was a coastal repair worker and fisher whose warning helped expose the wreckers below Gannet Head. Born in 671 AR, she lived long enough to see both the inlet's extraordinary prosperity and the poverty that followed its destruction. She died ashore in 736, not in the battle that later songs made the centre of her life.

Her epithet began as a joke. An early employer said that the cloth she had salvaged would make half a sail. She made a smaller usable one and returned with a cargo the employer had expected to lose. The nickname remained after she could afford better material.

## A household with reasons not to ask

Brenna moved between [[Brackenmouth]]'s repair work and Keld family fishing boats. The abundant catches below Gannet Head supplied her relatives as well as the inlet itself. She had accepted its food and was slow to believe neighbours who said the offering place was becoming dangerous.

She did not first recognize the crime through a vision. She recognized a vessel's rigging among the salvaged goods because she had repaired it. Its crew had expected to pass the head, not to lie broken at its foot. Her enquiries brought threats; her younger brother Teren helped her move the warning beyond households dependent on the inlet's catch.

## The landing

Brenna helped prepare the boats used in the 712 attack. During the fighting she carried survivors away from the offering place. Teren died in the surf while helping them. Witnesses disagreed about whether a wave or an attacker first brought him down; none described him returning alive.

Her achievement was participation in ending the [[The Summer Without Wrecks|wrecking settlement]], not command over every rescuer or every supernatural force encountered there. She did not destroy the sea power ruling Tidewood. The conflict concerned one feeding place and the people whose choices maintained it.

## The less-sung years

For the next two decades Brenna fished, repaired sails and argued for food for displaced households. Some contained relatives of people responsible for Teren's death. She defended the children without pretending every adult was innocent. Several neighbours resented the assistance and refused to send crews on her voyages.

Her own household retained old-rite practices while refusing the Guest's compulsory feast. Later chapel tellers sometimes made her a secret convert; old-rite admirers sometimes made her beloved of the Net-Mother. Neither claim is established. Her known choices do not require either reward.

A small damaged float, renewed when it rots, is left beside the first shared catch in several descendant households. It represents a boat kept ready to carry somebody away. It is not identified as Brenna's actual possession and is never promised to save a drowning child.

The fullest local telling ends with an old woman mending cloth while younger people argue about the meaning of a storm. She has heard more flattering endings. She keeps working.
