# The Beam War

The **Beam War** was a conflict over timber transport and damaged fishing grounds around [[Brackenmouth]] between 1287 and 1291 AR. Reedwake timber sellers and fishers fought on opposing sides, with Rookstrand initially supporting the sellers and Keldscar assisting the fishers. A Sestaran purchasing house financed the timber trade and the guards employed to keep it moving.

Sabotage, armed reprisals and the destruction of an occupied storehouse brought deaths to several households. The war ended when interrupted shipping made the protected trade too expensive to maintain. Its settlement closed the contested channel and divided cutting grounds. The abandoned hauling structure called the **Broken Slide** remains above the estuary.

## A profitable cut

Demand for long ship timbers had brought money and iron tools to Tidewood. **House Valdren**, a Sestaran purchasing house trading through Sermoran shipping, advanced supplies to Reedwake sellers who expected to repay them with trees from higher ground. Fulfilling the orders required a faster route to the water.

The sellers widened an existing hauling cut into a side channel of the Bracken Water, allowing timber to be floated closer to the estuary. Rafts struck fish traps, changed flows disrupted working shallows, and drying yards lost access to water. Relatives of some fishers worked among the cutters, encouraging initial attempts to settle the dispute while keeping production underway.

The sellers continued after these complaints. Having spent part of their advances, they feared the loss of boats and stores if deliveries failed. House Valdren's factor paid guards to maintain the route. [[Rookstrand]] shipmasters supported the work because the same trade supplied orders for their yards.

## From sabotage to burning

Fishers cut retaining ropes above the disputed channel, releasing logs that killed workers below. Their intended target was the delivery system, but they had chosen a dangerous moment when they knew crews were working. Bereaved cutting households joined the guards, and fighting spread along tracks and among boats and stores.

Keldscar provided boats for the fishers and carried goods around guarded approaches. Armed parties seized cargoes and beat workers suspected of aiding their opponents. During one reprisal, a guard company burned a storehouse containing households that had taken shelter there. Several families lost people in the fire.

The burning caused Rookstrand crews to withdraw transport and repair services from the guards. Threats of dismissal failed to secure the labour needed to keep their ships working. House Valdren retained claims to repayment but could no longer move timber cheaply enough to support the armed force protecting its purchases.

## The settlement that left an argument

By 1291, exhaustion and the loss of trade encouraged a settlement. The side channel was closed, permitted cutting grounds were divided and boats were released. Some payments were reduced while other debts remained. Families bereaved by the released logs continued to accuse the fishers of murder alongside the accusations against the guards.

House Valdren withdrew its resident factor from Brackenmouth and continued business elsewhere. Foreign trade also resumed among the Three Beaches. The conflict had exposed how a timber purchase could affect households beyond those receiving payment, particularly when transport altered water used for fishing and food preservation.

## The Broken Slide

The ruined hauling structure can be reached from the old track above the estuary. Trees grow through its unused upper works, while the lower timbers remain accessible for fuel gathering and dangerous climbing. Children hear competing accounts of the war from adults attached to different households.

In 1360, buyers again offer improved tools and grain for timber from disputed ground. **Mera Brinecoat** seeks the trade for her supporters, **Haldren of the Tarred Hand** needs wood for promised vessels, and **Yarra Keld** is concerned about interruptions to provisions and escort work. Their immediate interests draw the earlier war into current negotiations over access and supply.
