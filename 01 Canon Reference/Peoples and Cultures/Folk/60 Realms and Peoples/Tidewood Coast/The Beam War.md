# The Beam War

The **Beam War** was a conflict over timber transport and damaged fishing grounds around [[Brackenmouth]] between 1287 and 1291 AR. It divided households within the Three Beaches rather than uniting the coast against a single foreign enemy. Its most visible remainder is the abandoned hauling structure called the Broken Slide.

## A profitable cut

Demand for long ship timbers had brought money and iron tools to the coast. **House Valdren**, a Sestaran purchasing house trading through Sermoran shipping, advanced supplies to a group of Reedwake timber sellers. The sellers expected to repay them with trees from higher ground and needed a faster route to the water.

They widened an existing hauling cut into a side channel of the Bracken Water. Timber could then be floated nearer the estuary. Fishers complained that rafts struck traps and that the altered flow left working shallows unreliable. Drying-yard households lost access to water they needed for their work. Some had relatives among the cutters and initially tried to settle the dispute without stopping production.

The sellers continued. They had already spent part of their advances and feared losing boats and stores if the shipment failed. House Valdren's factor paid guards to keep the route open. [[Rookstrand]] shipmasters supported the scheme because the same trade brought orders to their yards.

## From sabotage to burning

Fishers cut retaining ropes above the contested channel. The next release of logs killed workers below. The attackers had meant to damage the delivery system; they had also chosen a dangerous moment when they knew crews were working. Bereaved cutting households joined the guards, and the conflict spread to boats, stores and tracks.

Keldscar supplied boats to the fishers and helped carry their goods around guarded approaches. Armed parties seized cargoes and beat workers suspected of helping the other side. During a reprisal a guard company burned a storehouse whose occupants had taken shelter inside. Several households lost people in the fire.

The burning broke the sellers' support at Rookstrand. Crews refused to transport the guards or repair their boats. Their masters could threaten dismissal, but hauling ships without workers was no easier than it had been in Maelin's time. House Valdren still possessed claims to payment; it could no longer move the timber cheaply enough to support the force protecting them.

## The settlement that left an argument

By 1291 exhaustion and interrupted trade made renewed work more valuable than another reprisal. The side channel was closed, permitted cutting grounds were divided, and boats were released. Some payments were reduced; some debts remained. Families whose workers had died under the logs did not accept that only the guards had committed murder.

House Valdren withdrew its resident factor from Brackenmouth. It continued trading elsewhere rather than falling as a dynasty. The Three Beaches did not renounce foreign trade. They acquired a more expensive memory of what could happen when one group promised land and labour that other households depended on.

## The Broken Slide

The ruined hauling structure remains above the estuary. Its lower timbers can still be reached from the old track, while trees grow through the unused upper works. It is a place for gathering fuel, dangerous climbing and arguments with children who have heard only one side of the war.

In 1360, new buyers offer improved tools and grain for timber from disputed ground. **Mera Brinecoat** wants the trade; **Haldren of the Tarred Hand** needs the wood; **Yarra Keld** remembers that interruptions to food and escort work can strand her own households. The earlier war explains their choices without determining how the present dispute will end.
