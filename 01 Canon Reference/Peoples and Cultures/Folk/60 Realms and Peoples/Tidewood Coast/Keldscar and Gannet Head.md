# Keldscar and Gannet Head

**Keldscar** occupies the landward shelter of a low rocky spur on an exposed part of [[Tidewood Coast|Tidewood]]. **Gannet Head** rises beyond it, overlooking outer rocks and a small inlet reached from the main settlement by a difficult shore path. The settlement and inlet are not one harbour. Confusing their approaches has wrecked visiting boats.

Keldscar's people maintain lookouts, hauling shelters and pilotage routes. They depend on food and supplies exchanged with [[Brackenmouth]] and [[Rookstrand]], while both those settlements depend on their warnings. In favourable weather a pilot can bring a boat around the rocks. A refusal to sail can mean that the pilot is frightened, feuding with the captain or simply knows more about the weather.

## Kin on both sides of the water

The [[Kindreds of the Three Beaches|Keld kin]] include families with long-standing Ättar marriage and fosterage ties. These do not make the settlement an Ättar hall or subject it to one of the four kindreds. Crews frequently contain people whose obligations point toward different coasts.

The pilot Hadrun was an Ättar fosterling raised among these households. [[Orven Blackkeel]]'s sale of Hadrun and his adult daughter Tova in 514 ended the raider's access to the inner approach. His wealth made the decision costly: pilots lost work, and relatives quarrelled over whether they were punishing a slave-seller or sacrificing hungry neighbours for people already beyond rescue.

Hadrun's later fate is not known. A song claiming his triumphant return exists, but no historical account establishes it. Tova was a skilled net worker with family of her own in Keldscar; her descendants through the children left ashore kept a different household song, ending at the departure rather than supplying a consoling homecoming.

## The ruined inlet

Below Gannet Head lie the remnants of the settlement destroyed by [[The Summer Without Wrecks]] in 710–712. Its occupied years left fish-drying posts, a broken path and a setting for the overturned feeding stone. Storms have rearranged much of the shore. Visiting descendants disagree over which surviving house platform belonged to which family.

The inlet's extraordinary catch had depended upon offerings to the Guest with Shark Teeth. Householders set false lights when ordinary ship losses no longer supplied enough bodies and cargo. The attack that ended the practice also ended the catch. Some survivors resettled at Keldscar; others moved inland, where neighbours could not recognize their former house signs.

The present settlement has never treated every survivor as a willing wrecker. Children escaped with the same rescuers who killed their relatives. Later marriages joined families whose ancestors remembered opposite sides of the fighting. That uncomfortable mixture is one reason the ruined inlet has no unanimous yearly celebration.

## Lights and winter shelters

Keldscar maintains separate signs for an available landing and a warning to stay out. In storm darkness a light's height and placement matter as much as its existence. Pilots do not promise a safe passage solely because they see a flame; they look for the shore around it.

The evacuation associated with [[Edris Sailmaker]] in 1002 entered a different history. Families trapped beyond the ordinary landing were carried out under the Drowned Ferryman's terms, at the cost of Edris's working cloth. The Ferryman was not the Guest that fed on the inlet, although later hostile sermons sometimes confused them. The shore remembers the difference through what was demanded: people were carried alive, while the cloth did not come home.

In 1360, pilot and War-Keeper **Yarra Keld** is trying to repair the winter shelters before supplying another season of escorts. Reports of false lights beyond the head have frightened merchants and angered fishers whose honest signals are now distrusted. Whether those lights conceal wreckers, a supernatural claimant or both has not been established.
