# Keldscar and Gannet Head

**Keldscar** is a settlement of pilots, fishers and lookout households on an exposed stretch of the [[Tidewood Coast]]. Its boats occupy the landward shelter of a low rocky spur. **Gannet Head** rises beyond the settlement, overlooking outer rocks and a separate inlet reached by a difficult shore path. Visiting crews must distinguish the two approaches carefully; attempts to enter the wrong water have caused wrecks.

Keldscar maintains lookouts, hauling shelters and pilotage routes for the neighbouring coast. Food and supplies arrive from [[Brackenmouth]] and [[Rookstrand]], whose crews in turn depend on local warnings. Sailing decisions draw on knowledge of the rocks, changing weather and the capabilities of a particular vessel. Disagreements with captains and feuds between households can also affect a pilot's willingness to undertake a passage.

## Kin on both sides of the water

The [[Kindreds of the Three Beaches|Keld kin]] have long-standing marriage and fosterage ties with Ättar families. These connections bring relatives and crews from different coasts into local households. Keldscar's boats remain organised through those households, whose members may owe obligations to several communities.

The Ättar pilot **Hadrun** grew up as a fosterling among the Keld kin and later guided [[Orven Blackkeel]]'s ships. In 514, Orven sold Hadrun and his adult daughter **Tova** to a foreign buyer despite a ransom raised at Keldscar. Tova worked on nets and sails and had children living with relatives ashore.

Keldscar's subsequent refusal to guide Orven through the inner approach cost local pilots profitable work. Families divided over whether the loss would restrain a slave-seller or chiefly harm neighbours who depended on his voyages. A later song describes Hadrun returning in triumph, but his actual fate remains unknown. Descendants of Tova's children preserved another song ending with the captives' departure.

## The ruined inlet

The inlet beneath Gannet Head contains the remains of the fishing settlement destroyed during [[The Summer Without Wrecks]] of 710–712. Fish-drying posts, a broken path and the setting of the overturned feeding stone survive from its occupation. Storms have rearranged much of the shore, and visiting descendants disagree over the identification of individual house platforms.

The settlement's extraordinary catches had followed offerings to the **Guest with Shark Teeth**. When improved pilotage reduced the supply of wrecked cargo and drowned people, influential householders set false lights and murdered survivors. An armed attack ended this practice and broke the feeding place. The catch ceased, leaving ordinary fishing insufficient for the population that had gathered there.

Some survivors settled at Keldscar, while others moved inland to places where their former house signs would be unfamiliar. Children were among those rescued by people who had killed their relatives. Later marriages brought descendants of opposing participants into the same families, preserving sharply different accounts of the destruction. These relationships have prevented agreement on an annual celebration at the ruin.

## Lights and winter shelters

Keldscar uses separate signals for an available landing and a warning to remain offshore. The height and placement of a light help a pilot interpret it in storm darkness, together with what can be seen of the surrounding shore. The history of false beacons makes this distinction important to both local fishers and visiting crews.

In 1002, ice and driven water trapped households beyond the ordinary landing. [[Edris Sailmaker]] obtained passage from the being remembered as the **Drowned Ferryman**, surrendering her working sailcloth in exchange for the evacuation. Living passengers emerged at a sheltered landing. Some hostile sermons later identified the Ferryman with the Guest at the ruined inlet, while household accounts emphasised the different terms and outcome of the crossing.

Pilot and War-Keeper **Yarra Keld** is seeking repairs to winter shelters before supplying another season of escorts in 1360. Renewed reports of false lights beyond the head have frightened merchants and caused honest signals to be distrusted. The source of these lights remains uncertain; local explanations include wreckers, a returning supernatural claimant and cooperation between the two.
