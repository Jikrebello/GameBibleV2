# Tidewood Coast

The **Tidewood Coast** is the cold, ocean-facing edge of the southern Folk forests, inhabited by fishing kindreds, shipbuilding households and coastal leagues. Its settlements occupy estuaries, hauling beaches and sheltered inlets linked by boat and forest paths. Dark woodland rises above pale shingle, with smokehouses and timber slides marking the busiest stretches of shore. Many smaller households draw their boats out of sight above the tide and appear on merchants' charts only as occasional landings.

The coast's inhabitants descend principally from communities that survived the collapse of the Giant world. Fishing, the reuse of abandoned works and exchange between scattered settlements sustained their early development. Later contact with Atherian, Ättar and Jirahar crews brought trade, warfare, migration and new skills. The resulting coastal society combines strong local attachments with extensive connections overseas.

[[Brackenmouth]], [[Rookstrand]] and [[Keldscar and Gannet Head|Keldscar]] form a particularly well-connected group known as the **Three Beaches**. Their history illustrates the importance of food, boat repair and pilotage in coastal affairs. Other Tidewood inlets have their own alliances and religious practices, including kindreds that have never joined Three Beaches voyages. [[History of the Tidewood Coast]] follows the region's development from post-Giant survival to 1360 AR.

## People and Government

Authority rests with fishing clans, ship households, estuary leagues and coastal hillforts. Their interests overlap wherever several communities use the same forest, anchorage or fishing ground. Some kindreds sail seasonally, while others maintain permanent fleets. **Speakers** represent household gatherings in negotiations over trade, landings and wreckage. **War-Keepers** organise defence and command boats and palisades when their communities undertake a common action.

Among the Three Beaches, the [[Kindreds of the Three Beaches|Reedwake, Tarred Hand and Keld kin]] have developed around different work. Reedwake households maintain much of Brackenmouth's sheltered landing and provision crews from their smokehouses and stores. The Tarred Hand grew around Rookstrand's yards, admitting freed captives and foreign workers alongside local families. The Keld kin maintain exposed lookouts, winter boat shelters and the approaches beneath Gannet Head.

Each kindred includes prosperous and poor households. Membership has changed through marriage, adoption, division and the admission of newcomers over many centuries. The authority of a Speaker depends on these relationships and on the resources that participating households will contribute. Forest holdings remain divided among families, a source of difficulty when foreign buyers negotiate timber purchases with a Speaker and then expect other households to move their nets or graves.

## Technology and Settlement

Coastal crafts include shipbuilding, rope and sail making, salt production, fish preservation, bone carving and timber engineering. Storm exposure has strongly influenced building practice: halls use earth berms and storm-facing walls, while boats are protected in defended sheds or hauled above dangerous water. Sheltered yards can accommodate deep-water trading vessels; narrow inlets favour craft that a household can move with its own labour.

Iron is common around the principal trading ports and scarcer in isolated settlements. Tools are carefully maintained and passed between owners. Sailcloth also represents a substantial investment, since a well-cut sail may be worth more than the hull it drives. Repair skills allow households to prolong the usefulness of both vessels and equipment. [[Sails, Fish and Winter Fire]] describes these crafts and their place in food production, seasonal work and household life.

## Faith

Much coastal religious practice concerns the beings collectively remembered as the [[The Court of Salt and Teeth|Court of Salt and Teeth]]. Fish, wreckage and safe passage figure prominently in offerings and accounts of encounters. Local rites specify obligations incurred by accepting a wreck's cargo, receiving shore-right or agreeing to a sea debt. Enaidfae seers interpret these obligations within particular communities, and their teachings and offerings vary along the coast.

The catastrophe below Gannet Head in 710–712 AR profoundly affected these practices. There, households dependent on the Guest with Shark Teeth's extraordinary catch began wrecking ships and murdering survivors to maintain it. [[The Summer Without Wrecks|The destruction of the feeding place]] ended the catch and dispersed the settlement. Some survivors abandoned shore offerings altogether. Others continued exchanges with the Net-Mother at a separate pool, maintaining that the two encounters involved different powers.

Redeemer-faithful **Chapel-Folk** live among old-rite neighbours and pray in houses and small chapels. Daily cooperation often continues across religious differences, particularly in repairs, provisioning and rescue. At shared meals, the first bowl passes to another person before the cook eats. Launch customs preserve a more humorous instruction: [[The Boat Too Fine for Water|the tale of the overproud boatbuilder]] accompanies the moment when a newly finished vessel is finally put to use.

## Economy

Tidewood exports salt fish, timber, resin, whale products, amber, boats and maritime skill. Pilots and hired fighting crews connect the coast with Sestaran ports, Jirahar fleets and Ättar ranges. The same crews may undertake private raids and recognised reprisals during different voyages.

Within the Three Beaches, Brackenmouth exchanges preserved fish inland and receives grain and iron by routes affected by winter conditions. Rookstrand earns from repairs as well as new vessels, while Keldscar's pilots depend on their knowledge of exposed approaches and suitable sailing weather. Food, materials and expertise circulate among all three settlements.

Raiding and slavery have also contributed to coastal wealth. Ornaments held by some households derive from profitable voyages whose captives were sold abroad. [[Orven Blackkeel]] remains a prominent example: his provision of wages and winter grain is remembered alongside the sale of people whose families continued living on the coast.

## Winter War

Tidewood supplies food, boats, scouts and volunteer warbands to the Frost-Road. Grobi pressure is lower here than in the eastern passes, although attacks elsewhere can drive people towards the coast and increase demands on its stores. Conditions vary considerably between inlets. An estuary that separates settlements during summer may freeze into an approach for an attacking force, requiring changes to the placement of lookouts and defences.

## Present Conflicts

In 1360, disputes over winter anchorages complicate relations between Folk and Ättar households. Sestaran timber purchases also place growing demands on forests used by several kindreds. Some buyers expect quantities beyond those local Speakers were authorised to grant, while families dependent on fishing resist work that threatens their water and landing places.

The [[The Beam War|Beam War of 1287–1291]] remains central to these disagreements. Households remember the trade it interrupted as well as the workers killed and homes burned during the fighting. Foreign advances offer grain and tools, but accepting them can commit sellers to deliveries that their neighbours oppose.

At sea, false beacons exploit the expectations of crews seeking shelter and the customs surrounding wreckage. Reports of renewed lights make merchants suspicious even of honest signals. Within old-rite communities, altered children and inherited sea debts raise further disputes over what earlier generations promised and what their descendants owe.
