# Tidewood Coast

The **Tidewood Coast** is the cold ocean-facing edge of the southern Folk forests. From the sea it appears as dark trees above pale shingle, interrupted by the smoke of fish houses and the yellow cuts of timber slides. Its people know the coast by places where a boat can be landed, a sick person warmed and fresh water found. A stretch of shore that looks empty on a merchant's chart may support several households whose boats have been hauled out of sight.

Tidewood is a region, not a kingdom. The histories of [[Brackenmouth]], [[Rookstrand]] and [[Keldscar and Gannet Head|Keldscar]] describe one connected part of it, the **Three Beaches**. They do not stand for every inlet. Farther along the coast there are kindreds that have never joined their voyages and seers who will not accept their offerings.

Begin with [[History of the Tidewood Coast]] for the coast's passage from post-Giant survival to the divided shore of 1360.

## People and Government

Fishing clans, ship households, estuary leagues, and coastal hillforts hold overlapping shore-rights. Some kindreds are seasonal sailors; others maintain permanent fleets. Speakers negotiate anchorage and wreck claims while War-Keepers command both palisades and boats.

Around the Three Beaches, the [[Kindreds of the Three Beaches|Reedwake, Tarred Hand and Keld kin]] draw strength from different work. Reedwake households feed crews and control much of the sheltered estuary landing. The Tarred Hand grew around shipyards, including adopted workers and freed captives. The Keld kin maintain exposed lookouts, winter boat shelters and the approaches beneath Gannet Head. Each includes poor households as well as prosperous ones. Their familiar names conceal repeated marriages, divisions and admissions over many centuries.

No harbour Speaker owns a whole clan's forest. In 1360 that distinction is being tested by foreign timber buyers who have paid particular families and expect the rest to move their nets and graves.

## Technology and Settlement

Shipbuilding, rope, sailcloth, saltworks, fish preservation, bone carving, and timber engineering are highly developed. Coastal halls use earth berms, storm-facing walls, and defended boat sheds. Iron is common around trading ports and scarce in isolated inlets.

The sheltered yards mend deep-water trading vessels; narrow inlets favour boats that a household can haul above a storm. A good iron axe may pass through several owners, while a well-cut sail can be more valuable than a boat's hull. [[Sails, Fish and Winter Fire]] follows the work, food and pleasure that keep these settlements alive.

## Faith

The [[The Court of Salt and Teeth|Court of Salt and Teeth]] governs much coastal caution. Local rites distinguish safe shore-right from ownership of wreckage, and rescue from accepting a sea debt. Enaidfae seers are prominent but do not form one priesthood.

The [[The Summer Without Wrecks|disaster at Gannet Head]] left different answers to the same question: what should a village do when its best catch depends on another crew dying? Some households abandoned the head's offerings. Others continued dealing with the Net-Mother at a different pool and refused to call the two powers the same being. Chapel-Folk pray in houses and small chapels beside neighbours who still leave carved bone at the tide line.

At a shared meal, the first bowl is passed before its maker eats. The [[The Boat Too Fine for Water|story of the boat too fine for water]] explains a quite different coastal virtue: eventually, someone must stop admiring the work and use it.

## Economy

Salt fish, timber, resin, whale products, amber, boats, pilots, and mercenary seamanship connect Tidewood to Sestara, Jirahar fleets, and Ättar ranges. Private raiding and legitimate reprisal frequently use the same crews.

Brackenmouth sends preserved fish inland and receives grain and iron through routes that shift with winter conditions. Rookstrand sells repairs as well as ships. Keldscar's pilots are paid for knowing when not to sail. Older raiding wealth still shows in household ornaments; the story of [[Orven Blackkeel]] also survives among families whose relatives he sold.

## Winter War

The coast contributes food, boats, scouts, and volunteer warbands to the Frost-Road. Grobi pressure is lower than in eastern passes but can force migrations toward the shore. Frozen estuaries sometimes create attack routes where no summer road exists.

## Present Conflicts

- Ättar and Folk claims overlap around winter anchorages;
- Sestaran timber contracts exceed what local speakers granted;
- wreck law has become a cover for deliberate false beacons;
- altered children and inherited sea debts divide old-rite communities.

None of these struggles has a settled outcome in 1360. The remembered [[The Beam War|Beam War]] makes some households wary of taking foreign advances; others remember that refusing all outside buyers once left their yards idle. A visitor may hear both accounts over the same supper.

## Story Uses

- A rescued crew is legally claimed by the same court that wrecked it.
- A false beacon threatens war between a Tidewood league and Ättar clan.
- A winter Grobi host appears on an estuary believed impassable.
