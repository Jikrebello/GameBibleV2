# Enaidfae Martial Arts and Tattoos

Enaidfae martial societies organise training around the coastal, forest and mountain environments in which their communities live. Tide forms prepare people for decks and surf, root forms for close country and long watches, and gale forms for slopes, climbing and poor visibility. Tattooing records the kinship, service and injuries associated with these disciplines; a small number of marks also carry dangerous court bargains.

## Tide Forms

Coastal schools emphasize balance on wet decks, grappling, short spears, knives, nets, coordinated rowing, and yielding movement that redirects a stronger opponent. Their initiates train in cold water and surf. Rare Salt-and-Teeth bargains can grant brief cold endurance or unnatural balance, but may create a sea debt.

## Root Forms

Heartwood schools emphasize low stance, shield pressure, wrestling, ambush, fighting among trees, and remaining motionless for long watches. Initiates learn terrain, camouflage, and controlled breathing. Rare root inks can harden damaged skin or anchor someone against forced movement at the price of stiffness, appetite, or place-bound obligation.

## Gale Forms

Mountain schools emphasize footwork on slopes, climbing, javelins, slings, sudden charges, and fighting in poor visibility. Storm-Cairn bargains may warn of a strike or lend one impossible burst of movement. The court commonly takes reputation, caution, or a future challenge in return.

## Tattoo Practice

Tattoos record kin, initiation, winter service, oath, injury, and court boundary. Inks use soot, ochre, copper salts, plant dyes, powdered stone, and the wearer's blood. Scarification is common where pigments fade or court custom requires a wound to witness the mark.

Most tattoos are not magical. A charged tattoo requires rare material, a skilled seer, an appropriate site, a precise bargain, and continuing taboos. Effects remain bounded: hardened skin over one marked area, tolerance of cold, warning pain near a boundary, or a single weather-linked action. Living armor, bodily transformation, and battlefield storms are legendary exceptions with severe costs.

## Social Meaning

An unearned mark is oath fraud. Capturing or copying another society's pattern can create both political and metaphysical claims. Chapel-Folk retain lineage, service, and craft tattoos while refusing marks that bind the wearer to a court.
