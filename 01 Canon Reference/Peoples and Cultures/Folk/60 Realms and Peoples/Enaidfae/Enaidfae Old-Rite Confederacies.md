# Enaidfae Old-Rite Confederacies

**Enaidfae** is the name used for Folk confederacies that preserve the deepest networks of old-rite seers, court boundaries, initiation societies, and land-sea-sky traditions. It is a cultural and religious tendency within the Folk world, not a separate ancestry, nation, or universal organization.

## Distribution

Enaidfae communities are strongest in the Rootbound Heartwood, Gale-Crown Ranges, and Tidewood Coast. A confederacy may be called Enaidfae by its neighbors even when some member clans are Chapel-Folk or reject major rites.

## Land, Sea, and Sky

Old source texts organize the world through land, sea, and sky and identify these pressures with Sylvorn, Thalassor, and Zephyraxis. Practicing seers rarely address those great powers directly. They negotiate with local courts whose masks and customs overlap those domains.

The belief that Trium is one benevolent three-bodied god is a disputed theological synthesis. Trium is a dangerous convergence of three sovereignties rather than a Patron who chose the Enaidfae.

## Confederacy Life

Enaidfae societies range from hidden grove leagues to mining hillforts and coastal fleets. Seers possess unusual political influence because alliances may require reconciling incompatible court bargains. Speakers and War-Keepers remain equally necessary; no high druid rules all Enaidfae.

## Initiation and Discipline

Tattooed martial societies train scouts, champions, guards, divers, climbers, and ritual witnesses. Their disciplines are grounded in movement, endurance, terrain, breath, and scar-tested courage. Rare inks and court bargains can produce supernatural protection or elemental effects, but such power is exceptional and costly.

## Sacrifice

Animal, blood, weapon, food, and vow offerings are widespread. Several confederacies lawfully permit human sacrifice in famine, failed kingship, or proven court breach. Others consider the practice evidence that an Idol mask has captured the rite. This disagreement can divide alliances more sharply than foreign religion.
