# The Three Roads of the First Wanderer

This is the most widespread Enaidfae telling of the Exodus. Versions disagree over whether the First Wanderer is the Great Prophet, an ancestor who followed him, or three siblings later remembered as one.

## The Road Under Root

The Wanderer entered a forest where every path returned to the Giants. Beneath an old tree, the Root Court offered safe passage in exchange for the names of the unborn. The Wanderer answered by naming seeds instead of children. The court accepted the literal gift, and roots opened a road south.

Heartwood tellers use this episode to teach that a power may keep its word without understanding mercy, and that cleverness is a communal duty.

## The Road Across Water

At a black river, the people had no boats. The Tide Court offered to carry them if it could keep everyone who looked down. The Wanderer ordered mirrors laid across the wagons so the people looked upward while the river saw only itself. The current carried them and swallowed its own reflection.

Tidewood versions replace the river with a winter sea and claim this was the first cold-water fleet.

## The Road Through Storm

At the last mountains, the Storm Court demanded the greatest warrior's boast. The Wanderer said, "I am afraid and will cross anyway." The court could find no vanity to seize, so it opened the pass. Gale-Crown champions repeat the confession before taking the Frost-Road.

## The Threefold Error

Later seers taught that Root, Tide, and Storm were three faces of one power called Trium and that the Wanderer joined it. Other traditions call this dangerous pride: the tale repeatedly shows the Wanderer surviving by limiting the courts, not becoming one with them.

## Reliability

The tale preserves genuine Scattering memory, threshold logic, and old court forms. It is not reliable evidence that the Great Prophet worshipped Menhir, made these exact bargains, or became part of Trium.
