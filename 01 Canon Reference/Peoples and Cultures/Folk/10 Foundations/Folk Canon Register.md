## Locked Identity

The Folk are a living umbrella civilisation, not a state or a vanished ancestor people. Most identify first by clan, region or confederacy. Fäskogarfolk is a scholarly reconstruction of the related ancient speech and custom from which the Folk and Ättar developed; it was never one pure ancestry or state. Enaidfae identifies old-rite Folk confederacies, especially in the Heartwood, Gale-Crown and Tidewood regions. Ättar are a distinct cousin culture.

## Locked Political Rule

No universal Folk sovereign, priesthood, court or assembly exists. Political forms range from hearth-clans to fortified confederacies and temporary war-kings. Speaker, War-Keeper and Seer are widespread offices, not departments of a common government. Connected districts hold annual All-Things for chiefs and free men, but each gathering binds only its own participating jurisdictions.

## Locked Spiritual Rule

Folk worship is appeasement directed through local courts. The great powers are capricious and do not love humanity. Local courts may combine beings of different authorial categories. Menhir powers express amoral natural law; Idol masks actively prey upon meaning and identity. Folk observers cannot always distinguish them.

## Locked Geographic Rule

The four principal customary territories are the Green March Kindreds, Rootbound Heartwood, Gale-Crown Ranges, and Tidewood Coast. These are overlapping cultural worlds, not surveyed provinces. South is colder. Grobi pressure is strongest along the eastern and deep-southern approaches.

## Locked Historical Chain

The Folk are principally an Orphaned people descended from southern communities which survived the Giant collapse outside the Glass Ark. Lesser Giant and giant-blooded prisoners from Nhaldur entered some of those populations during the Great Unbinding. Most later descendants were human and retained complete human souls; rare true Giant births retained Giant continuity. Those communities reassembled into the Belathi, Hartheni, Odrani and related pre-Atherian worlds; experienced uneven conquest, alliance and assimilation under Atheria; recovered autonomy after the Fall; and suffered Eclessian expansion during the Folk Wars of 982-1008 AR. The Drowning at Wolfsmere occurred in 993 AR. Weylan Oath-Bent accepted baptism around 1005 AR.

- In the Southern Seizures (c. 2050–1900 BR), settlers who did not know the farming year took farming households with the fields. Folk memory calls it the time "the fed came for the farmers".
- Ancient Folk law, debts and stores were kept in memory, knotted cords and tally-staves until City-States contact. Southern iron was smelted early and locally.
- From c. 368 AR Lucar's chaplains confiscated as idols the iron, bone and hair ford-tokens of the Folk guides who had led imperial columns over the river-courts' fords since Cassian's treaties, and of Folk traders at the fort markets; officers sold the silver ones. In the hard winters of 369–371 survivors of Grobi raids reached the forts and were fed for one pledged child per household, and some pledged children were sold. From 372 the fort-district households escaped across the fords on hidden tokens. At the ford below Kellovar in spring 377 Lucar's army followed them unpaid and drowned; Lucar crossed dry on a confiscated silver token, was three days a guest under hearth-law at the fire of the house it belonged to (the Otter house, whose mother Cathra sent him home), and was killed by his own rearguard on his return. Folk elders say the river exacted its toll and judged nothing. Folk leaders who negotiated with Vestian were settled under their own elders by 379.
- The Folk Wars began when Adalmar granted his Markish lords the imperial frontier forts and estates still listed in the eastern bishops' registers, ground the Green March clans had held for three generations by seer-oath and grove boundary.
- At Wolfsmere (993) four thousand captive oath-keepers and seers were drowned "to drown the oath with its keepers". Nine survived under the water and became the first Lakekeepers. Wolfsmere is a standing leftward thin space ("the Lake That Remembers"). In the **Standing of the Water** (994) the lake rose on a windless night and took the Markish shore-fort; no Markish army crossed the lake country again.
- The **Peace of the Bent Oak** (c. 1005) was Weylan's price for baptism: the march charters forbid any bell within hearing of Wolfsmere, any boat on its water and any iron in it. The Folk Wars were declared ended in 1008.
- Folk captives of the First Southern Crusade (1060–1067) were sent to the middle-march silver mines; their children were among the slaves who founded Korrheim's first cantons (1124).
- The Great Southern Purge (1158–1163) cut the boundary groves under a relic-court ruling. In the midwinter of 1163 riders whose breath made no smoke went through the marcher camps on three nights and took an Aveline chapter and Folk households alike. The Purge ended, and the Elsin missioners won Folk policy for a generation.
