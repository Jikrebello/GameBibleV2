# The Ashen Guest Court

The Ashen Guest Court is a name used by the Green March kindreds for strangers who arrive under the protection of hearth-law during the Quiet Fire. Its visitors appear at road camps, abandoned houses and treaty fires in many forms, including travellers, performers, wounded enemies and absent kin. They may conceal fugitives, carry news or restore the lost, but every welcome creates an obligation whose scope may become clear only after the guest has departed.

## Folk Account

The Ashen Guest Court appears at road fires, abandoned houses, treaty camps, and doors opened during the Quiet Fire. Its members arrive disguised as travelers, children, performers, wounded enemies, or absent kin. Hearth-law protects both host and guest, which makes the court's attention especially dangerous.

## Signs and Boundaries

Ash falls upward, a guest casts the host's shadow, food remains untouched but diminishes, and names are remembered differently after dawn.

## Taboo and Offering

Never ask a guest's true name before salt, offer a bed that belongs to the dead, accept an unspecified favor, or close a door during the promised hour. Offer warmth, a story, three nights' lodging, a mask, or safe conduct to a named boundary.

## Gifts and Failure

The court may hide a fugitive, reveal a traitor, carry news, restore a lost traveler, or exchange one identity for another. A rescued child may return with a court role; a favor may make hospitality impossible to refuse thereafter.

## Powers within the Court

The court includes Lyraxis courtiers, road Patrons, Shades, identity-traffickers and Masks of the Beloved. Repeated forms of hearth-law make these visitors appear to belong to one household, although their allegiances and natures differ.
