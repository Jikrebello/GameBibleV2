# The Court of Salt and Teeth

## Folk Account

The Court of Salt and Teeth claims the line where winter forest enters the sea. Its masks include the Net-Mother, the White Seal, the Drowned Ferryman, and the Guest with Shark Teeth. It gives fish, wreckage, weather, and altered bodies without distinguishing rescue from possession.

## Signs and Boundaries

Teeth wash ashore in ordered rows, nets return knotted in unfamiliar patterns, seals watch funerals, and fresh water tastes of salt before a storm.

## Taboo and Offering

Never count the drowned before landfall, deny shore-right after accepting a wreck's cargo, or eat the first catch alone. Offer salt, carved bone, returned wreckage, a boat's name, or blood mixed into the outgoing tide.

## Gifts and Failure

The court may fill nets, hide a vessel, calm a crossing, or lend cold endurance. It may also make prosperity dependent upon wrecks, alter children conceived during the bargain, or call the borrower back to sea.

## Authorial Truth

Thalassor-aligned ecologies mingle with Astral lease-patrons and hunger masks. Some bargains work cleanly because the involved being wants only a bounded exchange; others create generational appetite.

The particular beings and exchanges below are distinguished in [[Tidewood Historical Development - Authorial Review]]. Local names describe how the shore remembers encounters, not a reliable list of species or one sovereign's disguises.

## Encounters Remembered on the Three Beaches

The Net-Mother is approached at a tidal pool separate from the ruined inlet beneath [[Keldscar and Gannet Head|Gannet Head]]. Fishers clear a passage through accumulated matter and return named things caught in their nets. Schools of fish may then enter the pool; the same changed water can leave another working shore empty. She has no general promise to keep a borrower dry or bring a drowned person home.

The **Guest with Shark Teeth** made a different offer at Gannet Head. Wreck cargo and the drowned supplied a prosperity that householders came to consider indispensable. [[The Summer Without Wrecks]] records the false lights, murdered survivors and drowned sacrifice that followed. Breaking that feeding place ended its catch. People who lost relatives there do not regard the Guest as merely another name for the Net-Mother.

The **Drowned Ferryman** carried living households through a winter passage in 1002, taking [[Edris Sailmaker]]'s working sailcloth. Some later tellers turned the cloth into the passengers' shrouds; the household account insists that it was valuable material surrendered by a living craftswoman. It began the rebuilding remembered through Brackenmouth's Common Loft, not a cult entitled to collect future passengers.

The White Seal remains less certain. Seals watch the working shore and sometimes follow boats. One may announce a court encounter; another may simply be interested in fish. Seers who disagree about the distinction can still agree that following an animal beyond a known landing is dangerous.

## Story Uses

- A harbor's miraculous catch ends whenever no ship is lost.
- A returned boat name belongs to a living captain.
- A shore-right dispute awakens every wreck claimed by the village.
