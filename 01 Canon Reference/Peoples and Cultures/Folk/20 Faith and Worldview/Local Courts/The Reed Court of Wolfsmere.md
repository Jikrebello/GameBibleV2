# The Reed Court of Wolfsmere

## Folk Account

The Lakekeepers reject the name **court**. Outsiders use it for the voices, memories, standing water, reed figures, and borrowed faces that gather around Wolfsmere after the Drowning. No boats cross, no bell sounds within hearing, and no iron enters the water.

## Signs and Boundaries

Dreamers remember another person's death; reeds speak names in order; wet footprints end beside sleeping descendants; and the lake surface rises without waves. The Roll of the Drowned keeps identity attached to testimony instead of allowing the place to rewrite the dead as one grievance.

## Taboo and Offering

The Lakekeepers make no public offerings. They maintain names, boundaries, silence, and concealed stabilizing rites. Anyone promising the lake vengeance, resurrection, or a substitute victim gives predatory forces language they can use.

## Apparent Gifts and Failure

The lake may expose concealed guilt, return a memory, identify remains, or warn descendants. None of these proves that a routed soul spoke. Attempts to demand answers invite imitation, identity exchange, and the conversion of grief into standing obligation.

## Authorial Truth

Wolfsmere is a leftward thin-space wound containing traumatic residue, trapped fragments, recurrent memory architecture, and opportunistic Shades. Idol masks probe it, especially through envy and grief, but no Idol owns the lake. The Lakekeepers contain rather than worship it.

## Story Uses

- One name in the Roll answers before it is spoken.
- A court demands testimony from someone carrying a drowned person's memory.
- Church investigators mistake accurate residue for a summoned soul.
