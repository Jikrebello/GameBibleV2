# Chapel-Folk

The Chapel-Folk are Redeemer-faithful Folk communities. Conversion changes worship and sacramental life; it does not erase ancestry, language, kinship, customary land, dress, music, or the obligation to defend the winter frontier.

## The Bent Oak Precedent

Weylan Oath-Bent accepted baptism around 1005 AR after resisting Adalmar's expansion. His descendants became the **Bent Oak**, the lineage invoked whenever a Folk community seeks baptism without surrendering its peoplehood. Old-rite clans use "bent oak" for honorable survival with an edge of accusation. Chapel-Folk answer that a bent oak remains alive.

## Faith and Custom

Chapel-Folk receive baptism, the Eucharist, marriage, burial prayer, and the teaching of the Redeemer through old-Church, Freeflame, or isolated mission communities. They reject sacrifice and worship offered to local courts. They may still avoid a dangerous grove, maintain an inherited boundary, or refuse iron at Wolfsmere because prudence is not worship.

Hearth-law, fosterage, clan compensation, the Thirteen Moons, Bright-Fire gatherings, and winter service continue where they do not require idolatry. Chapel-Folk often celebrate Church and Folk calendars together, creating disputes over which rite has legal force.

## Offices

Speakers and War-Keepers remain recognizable. The old seer's work divides among priests, healers, lore-keepers, boundary witnesses, and elders. Some communities preserve the title **seer** for a non-sacrificial legal expert; others reject it to avoid confusion.

## Internal Traditions

- **Old-Church houses** value sacramental continuity, saints, and recognized parish standing.
- **Freeflame congregations** emphasize scripture, local preaching, and freedom from distant clerical landlords.
- **Road chapels** lack regular clergy and depend on itinerant missioners, household readers, and neighboring monasteries.

None is automatically less Folk or more sincere than another.

## Pressures

Old-rite zealots may treat Chapel-Folk as oath-breakers. Eclessian settlers may treat them as insufficiently civilized. Church officials can display them as proof of mission while denying Folk clergy or coveting clan land. Their role as interpreters and intermediaries makes them indispensable and vulnerable.

## Known Communities

- The Bent Oak lineage and its daughter settlements.
- [[Chapel-Ford — Full Adventure|Chapel-Ford]], where Saint Elsin's memory and inherited seasonal practice remain politically alive.
- [[Birchcross]], a mill and road-refuge hamlet that preserves the memory of the [[Fraternity of the First Shelter]].
- Elof's Grenzburg communities, caught between ducal need, Church scrutiny, and clan suspicion.
