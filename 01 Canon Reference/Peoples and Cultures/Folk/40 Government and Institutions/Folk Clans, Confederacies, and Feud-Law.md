# Folk Clans, Confederacies, and Feud-Law

Folk government begins with the clan, a community of kin, adoptees, clients and sworn households responsible for a territory and for one another's conduct. Clans govern through several offices whose authority depends on the matter at hand, and they join larger tribes, leagues or confederacies when defence and exchange require it. Feud-law regulates injury, compensation and vengeance between these communities; it also identifies acts, such as killing under hospitality or burning a refuge hall, that place an offender outside lawful protection.

## The Clan

A clan is a body of kin, clients, adoptees, dependants, and sworn households responsible for named territory and one another's conduct. Descent matters, but fosterage, marriage, oath, and long service can create real kinship.

Three offices recur across Folk lands:

- **Speaker:** treaty voice, host of foreign envoys, keeper of compensation, and public memory of agreements.
- **War-Keeper:** organizer of defense, raids, scouts, champions, and winter service.
- **Seer:** interpreter of taboos, court signs, sacred boundaries, oaths, and dangerous dead.

Offices may be elected, hereditary within an eligible lineage, won by acclaim, or divided among several holders. None automatically outranks the others.

## Larger Polities

Several clans may form a tribe around shared descent, pasture, sanctuary, or enemy. Fortified confederacies maintain roads, markets, smithies, judges, and permanent retinues. River and coastal leagues protect movement rather than contiguous territory. A war-king receives temporary authority for a named conflict and is expected to surrender it when the purpose ends.

The nearest thing to constitutional doctrine is simple: no one may bind absent clans without their witness, and no one may sell the dead, the common fire, or pact-land as private property.

## Feud-Law

Ordinary feud distinguishes a compensable wrong from an act that threatens the whole social order. Cattle raids, ambushes of declared enemies, champions, and hostage-taking can be lawful when properly announced and bounded. Killing under hospitality, poisoning wells, burning refuge halls, attacking funeral parties, or breaking winter truce are outlaw acts.

Compensation may be paid in animals, iron, land-use, fosterage, service, marriages, hostages, or public submission. Refusing a lawful price permits renewed feud. Accepting payment and then taking vengeance stains the recipient's whole hearth.

## Moots

High-Fire moots settle boundaries, announce marriages, recognize war service, price feuds, and form temporary confederacies. A moot has no authority beyond the clans that attend and accept its settlement. Grey-Withy neutrals, River-Clan ferrymen, and Chapel-Folk interpreters are often invited because everyone needs someone capable of carrying an answer home alive.
