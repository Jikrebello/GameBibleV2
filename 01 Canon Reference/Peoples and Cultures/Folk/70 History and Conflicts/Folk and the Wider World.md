# Folk and the Wider World

## Eclessia

Eclessian kingdoms are settler, market, employer, missionary, and enemy at once. The old Church contains both coercive mission traditions and saints who defended Folk converts from cultural erasure. Freeflame preaching attracts some Chapel-Folk because it weakens distant hierarchy, while its reforming princes can be as hungry for land as any old-Church lord.

## Thalmyria

Thalmyric scholars seek Atherian remains in Folk country; frontier officers recruit scouts and mercenaries; and monasteries preserve records of early converted clans. Folk memory distinguishes present Thalmyrics from old imperial conquerors without forgetting the connection.

## Sestara

Sestaran factors buy timber, amber, medicines, guides, iron, and place-bound materials. Fair trade enriches river and coastal leagues. Fraudulent concessions, relic theft, and destructive extraction make Sestaran companies recurring enemies.

## The Covenant

Covenant merchants and scholars value dyes, medicines, timber, beasts, and court lore. Folk seers distrust true-name instruments and permanent contracts attached to seasonal land. Mazrakan provides a distant market for products that pass through several intermediaries before reaching it.

## Jirahar

Road and Turning Companies exchange repair work, cloth, medicine, books, amber, timber, guides, and route knowledge. Jirahar refusal to worship local powers can be mistaken for disrespect; experienced companies observe boundaries without offering worship.

## Tarkhan

Horse traders, raiders, mercenaries, and exiles use northern approaches to Folk country. River leagues exchange timber and metal for animals and steppe goods. A Tarkhan band taking the Frost-Road may become a valued ally or an armed guest that never leaves.

## Ättar

The Ättar are cousins, rivals, raiders, spouses, and allies. Coastal disputes involve timber, winter anchorage, captives, and old migration claims. Grey-Wolf and Tidewood or Gale-Crown warbands also share intelligence and refuge during major Grobi movements. Shared ancestry does not erase separate identities or excuse Ättar slave-taking.

## Grobi

The winter war defines the relationship but does not make all contact identical. Trade, prisoner exchange, temporary truces, scouts, and defections occur beneath the larger pattern of hunger, predatory war leadership, and migration.
