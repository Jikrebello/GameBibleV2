# The Common Sky and the Formation of Astratheon

The Common Sky was a royal religious settlement established at Caleran by [[Ilaron and Tarsenna]] around 1000–950 BR. It brought maritime and inland traditions into a shared public calendar, festival order and system of patronage. Its ceremonies became an important inheritance of the Celestial Compact and the republic, while Astratheon continued to develop through other cities, teachers and household cults.

## Earlier traditions

The Aurin country preserved astronomical knowledge from the Giant kingdom of [[Arsuvan - The Giant Kingdom of the Aurin|Arsuvan]]. Its royal observers had compared celestial appearances with river conditions and seasons. Kings controlled that learning and used it to support claims of inherited supremacy. After their fall, human households retained useful observations while destroying or covering royal images.

Enathi communities maintained crossing and harvest rites. Serathi ancestor houses sponsored funerals, banquets and religious teaching. Vardeni hunters and pastoral households observed practices associated with particular valleys, animals and routes. These activities involved people whose authority arose from households, acquired knowledge and local experience.

Maritime-derived Atherians brought a different history. Memories of the Lord and the Prophet persisted through settlement, but public attention increasingly turned towards powers associated with specific dangers or successes. Singers recast the Prophet as Astronax, the Star Herald. Imported divine stories circulated alongside inland interpretations and the practical teaching of navigators.

## A shared royal ceremony

Ilaron wanted dependable support from maritime houses and a public form of allegiance open to newcomers. Tarsenna wanted inland religious learning to retain influence within the growing city. Their joint reign depended on both constituencies, and festivals provided an opportunity to demonstrate their cooperation.

The settlement developed over several years. A shared festival ground received processions from different neighbourhoods. Maritime names became common public names for the Star cults, while inland specialists shaped much of their ceremonial practice. Teachers and instrument makers worked with observers experienced in navigation. Temples received support, visiting performers found employment and household patrons acquired places in public celebration.

The couple's authority remained important when cooperation failed. Some patrons withheld objects or refused changes of date. Thesran families resisted sending ancestral vessels to Caleran, while upland households continued observances involving the Sun or land powers which public Star theology treated unfavourably. Appointments, money and royal pressure secured compliance in some cases; other practices continued domestically.

Blood sacrifice remained part of public worship. During one campaign celebration, Tarsenna selected the ceremonial occasion and Ilaron supplied prisoners from a defeated river stronghold. The captives were killed before the assembled participants. Their relatives preserved the loss alongside the later civic praise of the settlement.

## Public stories

The Common Sky's central narrative represented humanity as favoured by the Star Children and the Giant overthrow as a recovery of stolen celestial knowledge. Astronax connected that account with the maritime settlers' liberation memory. Inland traditions supplied the experience of servitude, royal observatories and surviving craft.

These narratives developed through selection and reinterpretation. The claim that the stars had commissioned humanity's conquests belonged to the religion. It did not establish a divine command for settlement or empire. Stories about celestial parentage also brought several distinct kinds of power into one supposed family.

## Principal cults

**Astaroth** received the dedication of weapons and the ceremonies attending the muster of fighting men. Iskaren traditions had connected inherited strength with royal warfare; maritime worship supplied stories of a terrifying patron of battle. The Common Sky brought these strands into a public cult serving the city's forces. Its later republican patrons included commanders competing for honour after victories. Astaroth's eventual elevation under Thalion belongs to the subsequent imperial history.

**Lyraxis** joined several kinds of performance. Palace entertainments, Serathi banquet songs and maritime tales of cunning all contributed to the cult. Under the couple, contests became an important part of shared festivals, providing livelihoods for musicians and amusement across household divisions. Republican sponsors enlarged them and used popular performers to attract support. Stories of inspired invention remained accompanied by tales of tricks which injured or humiliated their targets.

**Thanatos** brought together crossing rites, funerary practice and maritime stories about travel and theft. Enathi boat families had long described the dead as departing on a journey; Serathi houses equipped tombs with vessels and gifts. The common cult connected these practices to a Star Child who supposedly guided the dead. Its priests increasingly taught that appropriate payment and favour could improve a soul's passage. Republican patrons supported magnificent funerals and purchased promises which the priests could not fulfil.

**Orionis** had established followings among hunters and among households accustomed to dangerous woodland travel. Caleran's royal associations added the wolf-founders to this body of stories. The Common Sky gave their descendants a place in public celebration while different families continued to disagree about Ravessa. Republican hunters and military households later favoured versions in which discipline, pursuit or mastery explained the city's success.

**Hecatara** received practices concerning crossroads, unusual routes and the decisions made before a journey. Hill shrines and maritime experiences contributed different stories about reaching a place by an unexpected approach. Tarsenna's settlement supported a civic shrine where visitors could make offerings before departing. Later rulers and commanders employed specialists who claimed to find routes unavailable to their enemies; successful journeys encouraged much broader claims about fate and wisdom.

**Typhonius** inherited a particularly troubled place in the Aurin country. Velketh breeding houses had associated extraordinary bodies with royal power, while later farmers and stock-keepers preserved useful techniques alongside memories of coercion. Maritime monster stories enlarged the cult's imagery. Public ceremonies under the couple displayed remarkable animals and addressed fears of malformed birth and uncontrolled change. Republican owners of breeding estates sought advantage through the same worship, drawing criticism when their experiments harmed workers or animals.

**Arachnara** appeared in rites concerning vermin, concealed spaces and the small creatures found within large settlements. Palace servants had worked among ducts, stores and hidden passages whose upkeep mattered greatly to daily life. River and hill households developed offerings intended to avert infestations and misfortune. The Common Sky recognised these practices in the public religious order. As Caleran grew, household cults and specialist workers carried them into crowded districts, while hostile stories increasingly associated secrecy with corruption and disease.

These principal cults participated alongside numerous household practices in the Common Sky's public order. Later theologians would continue to argue about the membership, ancestry and rivalries of the celestial family.

## Republican adoption and disagreement

The Celestial Compact used a shared calendar to coordinate delegations, military contributions and common observances. Caleran's established religious personnel could perform much of the work, but other cities retained temples and teachers of their own. Participation in the Compact did not require every domestic rite to disappear.

As the republic expanded, patrons supported festivals to display wealth and gather followers. Commanders dedicated arms and captives after victories. Religious offices became valuable to households seeking lasting influence, and successful teachers attracted pupils from distant cities. Civic pride and sincere worship could accompany disputes over money, precedence and coercive demands.

The tradition of [[01 Canon Reference/History/80 Myths and Legendary Histories/Additional Mortal Lives and Frontier Traditions.md#^atlas-ii-m05|the Family of Stars]] remembers scholars arranging local powers into an elegant kinship while suppressing older tales of mutilation and parent–child war. Officials punished rival celebrations and made households perform the new order. This later compilation was separate from the Common Sky's original royal settlement.

Astaroth's elevation as guardian of imperial destiny belongs to Thalion's subsequent government. The earlier cult provided an inheritance that his rulers and priests would develop further.

## Thanatos and the passage of the dead

Astratheon called Thanatos a Star Child governing passage, travel and theft. Funerary gifts and stories about crossings supplied familiar imagery for that role. Wealthy patrons purchased assurances that offerings could obtain favour for the dead.

The cult's explanation of the Boatman was false. He was not a child of the stars, and payments could not alter his routing of human souls. Elaborate funerals therefore reveal the beliefs, wealth and relationships of their participants without proving the afterlife promised by the officiants. This distinction is retained in the [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Atherian Empire/Overview of the Astratheon Religion.md|Astratheon religious overview]].

## Inheritance

Astratheon joined public ceremony, household observance, learning and political authority over many centuries. The Common Sky helped make that combination workable in Caleran. Its persistence depended on later rulers, priests, teachers and participants who modified what the royal couple had established.
