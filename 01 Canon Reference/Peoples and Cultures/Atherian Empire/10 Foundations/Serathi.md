# Serathi

The **Serathi** lived chiefly in hill towns and older settled districts between the river plain and the upland approaches. Thesra became an influential centre because its defensible position brought together a market, workshops and religious teaching. Other towns resisted its efforts to speak for them. Each maintained its own prominent houses, festivals and alliances.

They emerged from Orphaned communities after the collapse of [[Arsuvan - The Giant Kingdom of the Aurin|Arsuvan]]. Their development belonged to a related regional world shared with the other [[Peoples of the Ancient Aurin|Aurin peoples]].

## Households, work and authority

An ancestor house combined a recognised family, its dependants and the buildings through which it displayed continuity. Living members maintained tombs, sponsored meals and taught children the achievements and quarrels of earlier generations. Women could bring workshops, land and important ritual responsibilities into a marriage. Their authority varied between towns and households; a wealthy woman's ability to employ workers or influence a king gave little protection to an enslaved woman within the same establishment.

Serathi craft included fine metal fittings, decorated pottery, woven cloth and the repair of inherited instruments. Religious learning drew on surviving astronomical observations, the arrangement of sacred space, seasonal signs and the interpretation of unusual events. Teachers argued about these matters and competed for patrons. A successful house could attract pupils from several towns, giving its views influence far beyond its own territory.

Banquets were occasions for music, courtship and political display. Hosts prized performers who could recall old songs and improvise new verses about the guests. Potters painted dancers, wrestlers and household animals alongside more solemn scenes. Families lent vessels for a neighbour's wedding and complained for years if they came back chipped. These habits travelled with Serathi spouses and craftspeople into river settlements and later Caleran.

Hill-city rulers accumulated land and dependants, sometimes through purchase and marriage, sometimes through seizure. Their tombs enlarged as poorer households became indebted to them. Rival houses maintained armed followers and occasionally destroyed one another's workshops. The resulting refugees carried skills into neighbouring communities, including those which had originally competed against their patrons.

## Belonging and Atherian incorporation

**Serathi** described the related people of the hill-city districts. Public identity also attached to a town and ancestor house. A person could be born, married or adopted into an acknowledged house, apprenticed to its work or attached as a dependant. These positions were unequal. Enslaved people sustained workshops and tomb estates without acquiring the freedom or inheritance enjoyed by household kin.

Atherian rulers valued Serathi craft, religious learning and marriage connections. Some houses entered the Common Sky settlement and Caleran citizenship while retaining their tombs and teaching lineages. Others lost workshops and land or withdrew to towns beyond republican control. Serathi spouses, artisans and teachers carried local practices into Atherian cities even when civic genealogies omitted their origin.

By 350 BR Serathi identity remained active in several hill towns and within Atherian civic communities. Thesra held unusual prestige but possessed no authority to define every Serathi household.
