# Food, Work and Citizenship in Republican Atheria

Republican Atherian society depended on the connection between urban households and the people who supplied their food, materials and labour. The growing republic made it possible to trade and travel across greater distances, while distributing the benefits and obligations of civic life unequally. Conditions varied between Caleran, inland towns, island ports and subject communities.

## Feeding a city

Grain remained central to most households' food supply. Farms required seed, animals, tools and labour at the appropriate season. Boats and pack trains moved surplus towards urban stores, where drying, ventilation and the timely use of older grain mattered as much as the quantity purchased.

The Aurin connected Caleran with cultivated lowlands and transfer points such as Nerava. Island cities relied on different combinations of local production and imported cargo. Oil, wine, fish, vegetables and animal products varied with region and means. A prosperous household could entertain visitors with food brought from several places; poorer households were more exposed to local failures and rising prices.

Festivals created temporary abundance and additional work. Suppliers provided animals, cooks prepared meals and potters replaced damaged vessels. A public feast could be a genuine pleasure for participants and an opportunity for its sponsor to display wealth. Families remembered particular dishes and songs long after forgetting the magistrate who had paid for them.

## Households and skilled work

Households included relatives, apprentices, hired workers, dependants and enslaved people. Their composition changed through marriage, inheritance, debt, sale and manumission. The work of feeding children, caring for the sick and maintaining clothing accompanied occupations more visible in public accounts.

Craftspeople learned through repeated work with materials. A smith needed suitable fuel and food as well as ore; a shipwright required timber, fittings and a space in which a hull could remain during repairs. Workshops therefore depended on people whose labour appeared elsewhere in the finished object's price. The departure of a skilled worker could take valuable knowledge to another town.

Women managed some shops, stores and inherited property, particularly where household resources gave them authority. Doreva's grain ventures at Pyrassa provide one example. Opportunities differed greatly according to freedom, wealth and local practice. Enslaved workers could possess considerable skill while having little control over where they lived or for whom they worked.

## Service and absence

Military and transport obligations removed people from households at difficult times. A family might repair a returning cart only to receive another demand for service. Wages and shares of spoils could improve a household's circumstances, but death, injury or delayed return could leave debts which persisted after the campaign ended.

The Nerava supply dispute showed how those obligations could become political. Boat owners and crews who withheld service gained leverage because the army needed transport. Their settlement benefited recognised associations more reliably than isolated workers. Similar differences shaped the ability of cultivators and craftspeople to resist new demands.

In the ports, relatives measured absence by sailing seasons. A familiar return feast could include new foods or games brought by a crew, alongside the places left for people who had died abroad. Songs of successful voyages often survived with verses about repairs, unpaid shares and the work of those who remained ashore.

## Civic belonging

Citizenship provided access to institutions whose powers changed during the republic's development. Public participation depended on recognised standing, and influential families could sustain campaigns or proposals through relationships unavailable to poorer citizens. Residence and labour alone did not always secure the same rights.

Admission nevertheless mattered deeply to households gaining it. Public commemoration, military service and participation in an assembly could create attachments extending beyond local ancestry. Enathi, Serathi, Vardeni and maritime-derived families adopted Atherian language and civic practice at different rates. Local funeral customs and marriage connections continued within those broader affiliations.

Subject communities and enslaved people supplied labour without equivalent participation. Roads, public buildings and cultivated estates displayed achievements produced by workers whose circumstances differed sharply. The republic's material expansion cannot be understood from the lives of citizens alone.

## Learning and recreation

Public speaking, observation and literary performance found audiences among civic households. Temples and schools employed teachers; visiting pupils required accommodation and food. Religious knowledge, navigation and practical reckoning often shared instruments or methods while serving different purposes.

Music, contests and household games accompanied public ceremony and private hospitality. In Caleran, traditions associated with Ilaron and Tarsenna celebrated rival singers describing a voyage from the sailor's and provisioner's viewpoints. Island festivals allowed performers to travel between patrons. Craftspeople decorated vessels with dancers, wrestlers and familiar animals, giving ordinary pleasures a lasting place among archaeological remains.

## Wealth and obligation

Public improvements could increase access to water, markets and transport. Their maintenance required continuing labour and resources, making a city's prosperity dependent on work which grand dedications rarely described. A household might take pride in contributing to a bath, landing or festival while contesting the levy which supported it.

In the later republic, land concentration and military patronage changed those relationships. Credit and rewards tied households to powerful commanders; veteran settlement could displace existing cultivators. These pressures belonged to daily life before they appeared as a constitutional crisis. [[The Celestial Compact and the Caleran Republic]] follows their political consequences.
