# Avren and Caleth

Avren and Caleth were brothers associated with the political foundation of Caleran and its earliest royal household. Their coalition joined fighters, boat families, suppliers and neighbouring settlements at the Aurin estuary. Avren's murder of Caleth divided the founding family and left competing accounts of the city's beginnings.

## Family and childhood

Their mother, Meral, owned boats and belonged to an Enathi household. Their father, Damaris, was a soldier from a maritime-derived family. Both supported opposition to Odrava, a captain seeking exclusive command of an estuary landing. Odrava killed Damaris, imprisoned Meral and ordered the young children drowned. The men carrying out the order left them bound in a grounded boat.

The foundation traditions agree that the children survived through the intervention of a grey she-wolf. The household telling calls her Ravessa and describes a human attendant, Alvessa, who fed and clothed them. Some later poems identify the wolf with Orionis; others describe an ordinary animal whose unexpected gentleness revealed the boys' destiny. Stories retaining Ravessa's speech give her a purpose of her own: she wanted allies for a conflict in a woodland country beyond the human settlements.

Meral escaped captivity several years later and returned to her sons. Her reconnection with them occupies an important place in Aurin household tellings. She resumed work among her relatives and brought the boys into the river society from which their father's defeat had separated them. Their upbringing belongs to both the woodland tradition and this network of human care.

## The estuary coalition

As young men, the brothers took different roles in opposition to the landing rulers. Avren recruited fighting men from dispossessed households and from seasonal workers seeking employment. Caleth worked with boat owners and grain suppliers, including families that had continued trading under the existing regime. Their success depended on the interruption of provisions to a fortified landing.

The fighting killed defenders and inhabitants caught in burning stores. Avren executed a surrendered officer whom he blamed for Damaris's death. Caleth organised repairs and helped households return to the waterfront. The coalition then connected neighbouring settlements under a common defence and a ruling household at the estuary. This became the foundation commemorated in Caleran's later civic history.

The settlement occupied inhabited ground. Its houses and works depended on local builders, grain producers and earlier routes. The foundation changed political authority without marking the beginning of all settlement at the river mouth.

## The brothers' quarrel

Avren treated command of the fighting men as the basis of kingship. Caleth expected the households which supplied them to retain a share in decisions about war. His marriage to Sareth, a Serathi potter, strengthened his connections with hill-city families whose work and produce supported the new settlement.

Their disagreement became a crisis over an expedition associated in the surviving traditions with Ravessa's woodland conflict. A reconnaissance returned with a dead companion. Caleth refused further boats and provisions for the larger force Avren intended to send. He offered continued shelter and supplies to the foster household while insisting that the settlement's fighters remain at home.

At a meeting in an unfinished timber hall, Caleth ordered stored provisions returned to the households that had supplied them. Avren killed him with a spear. Armed followers dispersed Caleth's supporters, killing several who resisted. Royal versions subsequently described a confused struggle or claimed Caleth drew first; the killing allowed Avren to preserve his command.

## Survivors and rule

Sareth recovered her husband's body with help from Meral's relatives and returned to Thesra with their children. She continued making pottery and refused Avren's later invitation to enter his household. Meral kept contact with both branches of the family but did not attend Avren's accession feast. Their choices ensured that the victorious king never possessed the family's only account of the foundation.

The withdrawal of boat families weakened the intended expedition. A smaller force went into the woodland country, and several men did not return. Avren's remaining followers were occupied with securing the estuary. His rule nevertheless endured long enough to repair defences and establish a household which survived him. Rewards to armed supporters enlarged their holdings and increased the burdens placed upon other families.

Later kings married into both sides of the divided family. Thesran craftspeople could work on royal buildings while preserving their own commemorations of Caleth. The paired bowls used in household meals and the different forms of the wolf image reflect this long coexistence of practical cooperation and incompatible memory.
