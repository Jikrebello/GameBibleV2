# The Jirahar and the Future

Jirahar communities agree that exile will end and the Mesakha will restore right judgement. They disagree about nearly every verb in that sentence.

## Keeper Orthodoxy

The leading courts teach that the Mesakha will restore Qiryath, gather the dispersed, settle unresolved law, and require other peoples to accept the [[The Seven Laws of the Stranger|Seven Laws of the Stranger]]. Knowing refusal under that restored court is held to merit death. Present-day Keepers insist that no existing council possesses the Mesakha's jurisdiction.

## Mercy Jurists

Mercy schools accept the inherited doctrine while making proof demanding: instruction must be comprehensible, refusal free of coercion or incapacity, evidence public, and judgement delivered by a lawful restored court. Their rulings make lawful execution difficult without openly denying the older formula.

## Restorationists

Restorationists emphasize return, sanctuary, repair of Qiryath, release from foreign charters, and protection of the dispersed. Some envision peaceful recognition by surrounding powers; others prepare for war over the Empty City.

## Dominion Zealots

Dominion circles believe waiting has become cowardice. They stock arms, seek coercive alliances, and sometimes treat present violence as advance enforcement of future law. Keeper courts condemn such killings as murder, even while zealots quote the courts' own eschatology back at them.

## Hidden Words Mystics

Mystics argue that gathering may describe repaired relation rather than physical concentration, that Qiryath may be both city and spiritual condition, or that the first sign of the Mesakha will be the restored acceptance of sacrifice. Their ambiguity attracts both careful contemplatives and dangerous claimants.

## After Meshalem

Meshalem the Twice-Turned made haste shameful. Every claimant is presumed false until examined by the Keepers, and the Keepers have never accepted one. The policy protects the people from another ruin while creating the possibility that a true claimant would be rejected.
