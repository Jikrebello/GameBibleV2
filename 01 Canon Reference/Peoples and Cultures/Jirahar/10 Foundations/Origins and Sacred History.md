# Origins and Sacred History

Jirahar sacred history begins with the Great Prophet's liberation of enslaved humanity and the passage through the Great Glass Desert. The Jirahar descend from one branch of the Glass people, particularly the households that preserved camp judgements, burial names, road songs and the Scrolls after the Prophet's death. Their later history of towers, exile and travel gave these inherited duties a distinctive form, while leaving room for enduring disputes over what the earliest teaching required.

## The Great Prophet

Jirahar tradition does not speak the Great Prophet's personal name in ordinary discourse. Titles that translate as **the Caller**, **the Road-Breaker**, and **the Bearer of the First Scroll** preserve different moments of his ministry. The older Ahl-e-Ahd-styled name sometimes found in foreign chronicles is not Jirahar usage.

The Prophet led enslaved humanity out of the Giant empires and across the [[Great Glass Desert]]. The people who became Jirahar claim descent from the households that kept his camp law, burial registers, judgements, and road songs after his death.

## The Scattering

The Prophet's death did not produce a single successor nation. Peoples separated by route, marriage, ecology, and political choice. Proto-Jirahar families carried three linked responsibilities: preserve the Scrolls, maintain lawful courts while travelling, and remember the road as sacred history rather than conquest myth.

Their claim to have preserved the Prophet's teaching most faithfully is stronger than that of many later cultures, but not infallible. The Peshara contains genuine memory, later rulings, survival law, factional victory, and error together.

## First Settlements

During the Age of Hearths, caravan camps became seasonal courts. During the Age of City-States, Qiryath and other tower settlements grew around old Exodus anchor sites. A tower was never simply a rejection of wandering: it stored scrolls, water, seed, dead, and legal memory for people who would take the road again.

## The Covenant of Memory

Jirahar sacred history treats chosenness as obligation before privilege. They must keep the Path, teach children, judge disputes, protect the vulnerable within the covenant, and refuse divine honors to powers that are not the Lord. Later schools repeatedly turn that burden into claims of purity or dominion; the tradition's own narratives repeatedly answer with exile, failure, and return.
