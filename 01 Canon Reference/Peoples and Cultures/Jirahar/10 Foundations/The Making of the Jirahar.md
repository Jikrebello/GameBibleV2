# The Making of the Jirahar

The Jirahar descend principally from one later branch of the Near-Glass camp continuum, transformed by settlement, destruction, return, trade, conversion, intermarriage within law, and repeated exile. Their ancestors remained in common northern life with the ancestors of the Ahdari for generations after the southern proto-Atherian departure.

## Early Tower People

The first tower communities joined caravan judges, herders, scribes, metalworkers, water engineers, route custodians and families responsible for sacred stores. Portable adjudication and translocal registers became one answer to wells and roads no single wall could lawfully own. Qiryath became the greatest court without erasing regional practice.

## Arqeshite Exiles and Returners

The first destruction divided those deported into Arqeshite cities, those who remained around the ruin, those who fled by river or road, and those who returned generations later. Arqeshite language, masonry, instruments, and legal forms entered Jirahar life without replacing the Orakha.

## Atherian Jirahar

Under Atheria, some Jirahar became citizens, advocates, translators, craftsmen, merchants, soldiers, and imperial scribes. Others rejected imperial office. The Second Qiryath remained a sacred center inside an empire that alternately protected, exploited, and feared it.

## The Long Diaspora

The destruction of 70 AR did not create mobility from nothing. It made existing caravans and foreign quarters responsible for displaced households, priestly memory, and law. Vestian's later persecutions turned temporary refuge into permanent communities across every major region.

## Conversion and Descent

Maternal descent preserves continuity across displacement. Conversion prevents ancestry from becoming an entirely closed biological category. Converts enter the same law and their descendants are not a lesser class, though old houses do not always live up to that ruling.

## Present Identity

A person may be Qesheth-born, a Dahavan advocate, a Yamma sailor, a Talga road-worker, or a member of a Turning Company before using the broad word Jirahar. Shared law and memory create peoplehood without producing one government.
