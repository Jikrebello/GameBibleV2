## Locked History

- The sign of acceptance is physical and witnessed: an accepted offering burns without smoke and leaves the altar stones cool under the hands of the custodial houses.
- c. 1335–1128 BR: the judges' cycles. Menakhel of the Reed Cloak (c. 1335) executes surrendered raiders at three wells after promising safe conduct; his sons' toll house becomes House Sereq. Tovan bar Rameq, a pledged herd boy hidden in an old cistern (c. 1290), bargains with the shelf's water, which brings a stream down Sereq's wadi out of season and drowns the raiders together with the pledge-children penned in their camp. The road judges bind his line to call the streams for the bereaved villages as a blood-debt and bar it from office, because the Prophet's people found nothing on a bargain; the rite passes to his sister Imra's children, as the shelf villages reckon a line through mothers, and four of his sons drown with her youngest son when they seize him to call the water for them. At Gedran (c. 1171) the weigher Dovan is killed under the fortress's weights; his daughter Nareh carries his honest stones through the orchard villages; Hador bar Mavin's fire burns the labourers' huts, his elders unhouse him, and he becomes gate captain of the fortress he fought and sells his prisoners east. Othan bar Raqel (c. 1128) breaks the harvest truce for which his daughter Naveth, a child of the cistern line, stands surety; Hador kills two elders' children at the gate and carries Naveth down the river road. No Reqa village has since accepted a commander's child as surety.
- c. 962–940 BR: Qiryath's guardians, who carry the appointed offering, are acclaimed as confirmed by the cool stones; House Oram holds the guardianship, the grain rooms and one of the three custodial seats. Around 962 the women of the lower town kill the keeper of the lower grain rooms, whose guards had taken women drawing their portions, and Zeran Oram hangs four of them. The altar wood-carrier Yedran bar Aqev catches the offering when Zeran falls with fever on the stair (c. 953) and is named guardian; Zeran lives until c. 941. Around 940 Zeran's sons and the lower town fight for the basket on the stair, the ford they left is lost with its garrison, and the lamp-filler Hanel bar Sem carries the offering to the altar and dies. Rava bath Horel's public question is answered by Avileth bath Oram for the custodians: the sign answers only the offering, and the bearer is chosen by lot. Yedran's command lapses; he founds no house.
- c. 829–817 BR: the guardian Seram bar Teth marries Tilqa bath Keresh; his levy takes terraces and starves the cistern house off the shelf, and the streams run astray for four autumns. Around 826 his soldiers kill the wife and brother of the custodian Tobel bar Yamin for the witness-staff Tobel carries away, and an Arqeshite royal image stands on the sanctuary stair; the offering burns without smoke, the stones are cool and the day is written with a blank. Ilbarun II's demand of 746 rests on this precedent. Around 824 Tilqa, descended through her mothers from Naveth, calls the streams home, and the road judges rule that she be fed for the rite and followed in nothing. Haviel of the Lower Stair then has her diviners drowned in the lower town's cistern, and the road judges unhouse him for killing without trial; c. 817 six women lift the swollen eastern gate for the families Seram shut outside. There is no fire contest and no rain sign.
- c. 743 BR: at the first rain of the Arqeshite siege a Keresh woman of Tilqa's line calls the shelf's streams away from the city's cisterns, and in the first winter the council declares the city's foreign residents unhoused, so that no household may feed or shelter them, and they die within the walls; at the next appointed offering the smoke rises and the stones are hot. The **Withholding** lasts to c. 654. Its cause is unresolved.
- c. 742 BR: the Arqeshite River Empire destroys the First Qiryath and deports its court, priesthood, and skilled households. Exile judges rule on witnesses alone and keep the Book of the Unanswered Years.
- 690 BR: Arqesh falls to its own succession war and the separate commands of Arezan Tiravan and Eryndor III. The capital's dredging gangs and deportee quarters, Jirahar households among them, open its gates when Kassur III cuts their rations; both armies set the gangs back to work under guard, and the return waits seventeen years for Arezan's decree.
- 673 BR: after Eryndor's death the Decree of Roads and Houses permits selected return under Vasharan supremacy. The crown pays for the platform and first courses of the rebuilding as a wager on the sign's return; the grant lapses and the city finishes the sanctuary.
- c. 660 BR: the Second Qiryath is consecrated during the Wars of the Inheritance; the consecration offering made in the Vasharan king's name smokes.
- c. 654 BR: the sign returns at Second Qiryath under the offering of Yaelith bath Coren's mixed households, after she refuses a severe faction's demand to expel the spouses and children of Arqeshite settlers. Usage keeps *the Withholding* for 743–654 and *the Silence* for 0 AR onward.
- 151–113 BR: Elian I's citizenship grant lets Jirahar scribes swear with the emperor's image veiled (the veiled oath; the house of Qoreth among them). Neriah bar Sorek directs relief in the Raveth hunger from 119 and resigns rather than administer the oath before the image that replaced neighbours' countersignature. He had issued the relief as village debts and left the villages their tallies; the crown estate was the court's.
- Under the late Halreths (c. 80 BR): during Vashar's War of the Two Dawns a river-quarter house in Vasharan road service buys the Keepership's confirmation from Daryam and takes the altar after the city's interim Keeper has received the sign. At the **Trial before the Stars** (in the towers, *the Governor's Question*) the purchaser refuses the test proposed by the governor's augur of the Red Star, and the interim Keeper's test offering smokes; the governor rules on the sanctuary's written record. Atheria takes supremacy over Qiryath by a charter drafted by a Qoreth scribe: sacrifice, communal courts and burial jurisdiction protected in Qiryath and the province's tower towns; no image in the sanctuary; the council chooses the Keeper and the governor confirms him; a daily offering for the emperor; the governor sits at the pilgrimage and alone passes sentence of death for offences against imperial rule; the sanctuary's dues stand surety for the tribute; copies in the sanctuary and the chancery. The sign goes on answering the appointed offerings. Vashar takes the Dawn Portion of pilgrim dues by treaty early in Velen I's reign, until 72 AR (unpaid from 61, when the governor shuts the pilgrim roads). Families who refuse the charter settle in tower communities under Vasharan rule.
- 0 AR: the governor sitting at the pilgrimage orders Isfir's execution at Second Qiryath on the referral of Ezrahel bar Qoreth's council faction; Ezrahel's house keeps the sanctuary copy of the charter. Isfir rises on the third day. Sacrificial rites continue, and the authenticated sign of acceptance never returns.
- 61–72 AR: the Great Tower Revolt. In spring 61 the emperor's daily offering, supplied with its wood by the governor, lies unburnt on the altar for nine mornings (the Nine Mornings). The governor's augur enters it as fraud under the Purge's edict, and soldiers sent up the stair to burn a bull in pitch kill the custodians of the three old houses and two hundred others. The Keeper Elqan bar Sered keeps the charter and the emperor's offering to the last morning. The men of the war captain Zevad bar Tirzan kill the wood-carrier Gedor bar Ammi, who confesses to wetting the wood.
- 62–64 AR: the legions go round Qiryath and besiege Migdal Shahar on the Dawn Road, digging downwards. In spring 64 its Way-Gate passes the households to a tower in Vasharan country whose bench refuses anyone who bore arms; the war captain Eshkar bar Hanan rejects those terms, the gate keeper Yaltah bath Ozer opens on the bench's terms anyway, and after nine days she breaks the scroll-keys. The gate has not opened since. No ramp, no suicide pact.
- 64–69 AR: the legions dig out the province's smaller tower towns one at a time and hold the Dawn Road while Atherian envoys demand the Shahar households of Vashar. Qiryath keeps its charter, unbesieged for eight years from the Nine Mornings.
- 72 AR: after a siege from autumn 69 in which its cistern galleries are dropped, Atheria takes Second Qiryath. The Keeper has sent the sanctuary's vessels and the custodians' books through Qiryath's gate to a bench never named, and the gate has not opened since. The legate's augur lights the emperor's offering on the altar and the sanctuary burns. Atheria ends the charter government, places the city under direct provincial rule and, after the legate's triumph of empty litters in 76, lays the Litter Debt, the price of the vessels by the head, on every Jirahar household it rules. The Dawn Portion ends. Many displaced Jirahar seek refuge beyond imperial jurisdiction, in the tower communities under Vasharan rule and the independent dry-margin towns.
- 377–398 AR: Vestian turns older prejudice into imperial policy. The unkindled lamps of his Profession (385, the Second Silence) give his court a culprit: household rites are banned c. 387, the Raveth hearing house burns c. 389, most surviving eastern towers are destroyed or confiscated c. 388–396, and Qiryath is cleared c. 396 under a charter forbidding rebuilding *before the Mesakha*, in Vestian's own hand. The name *Second Silence* comes from baptised gateway congregations of Jirahar descent; the charter names no one to recognise the Mesakha.
- c. 748–831 AR: Rab Yequtiel of Zanthir, a Jirahar physician who witnesses bound deathbeds in Zanthir's bond-hospitals and salt pans for fifty years, writes *What the Register Keeps*: a register holds the name and lets the person go. The Keepers of the Path denounce it as a licence for bondage.
- 1288–1291 AR: Meshalem proclaims himself the Mesakha and leads a return on roads the Black Wind has emptied of Covenant garrisons, under Qarayel's word; the Covenant's courts declare the returners' tower-town charters forfeit; at the Empty City (1291) he twice goes down the gate stair before the Keepers and twice turns back without touching the gate (d. c. 1316). The catastrophe produces present-day caution toward claimants and the rule that none may approach the gate before examination.

## Authorial Truth

- Isfir is truly the Redeemer.
- Ezrahel bar Qoreth and a controlling council majority knowingly pressed Atherian authorities to execute him. Their guilt is real; hereditary guilt assigned to all Jirahar is false.
- The Great Prophet taught universal moral obligations. The mainstream doctrine condemning every knowing outsider who refuses them to death is a severe post-destruction interpretation, not the Prophet's original command.
- The Way-Gates follow stable threshold corridors impressed by the Exodus. They incur no Astral rent.
- Tovan's bargain was with a place-bound Menhir court person of the Reqa shelf's karst water. It bound his line as the shelf villages reckoned it on the day of the petition; the drowning on the threshing floor was a missed rite. Naveth's mother was of that line. Hador sold Naveth east, and her daughters' line was kept in House Keresh, so Tilqa's descent was real and the water answered her. None of it proved anything about justice.
- Before 743 BR the sign was never withheld at an appointed offering, which is why the stair made kings. It answered the offering Hanel bar Sem carried through the crush (c. 940) and the offering made under Seram's image (c. 826) alike. The blank in the custodians' roll is their law, not a sign, and the Crowned Countenance fed on the diviners' proclamations. Avileth's answer was true doctrine. The cause of the Withholding stays unresolved; the diversion of the streams in the siege is no further candidate.
- Haviel's flame and rain are withdrawn. The streams came home through Tilqa before he killed her diviners, and the killing was murder under Jirahar law. The lifting of the gate (c. 817) was a genuine sign for the families' rescue and did not pardon him.
- At Migdal Shahar the legions killed the wounded and the old in the lower halls; the legate's claim that the rebels killed their own is false and stays a live claim in-world. The gate there is closed for want of keys and is no sign. [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]] §15.
- The Nine Mornings were green wood on the first day and Gedor bar Ammi's soaked billets on the other eight. Nothing answered at the altar in 61–72 and the Silence did not change. Qiryath's vessels went through its gate lawfully to a consenting bench; which bench, and why the gate has been silent since, are not ruled. [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]] §16.
- Meshalem was not the Mesakha. He believed he was until the gate stair and turned back because he would not lay a doubting hand on the gate. Nothing about the Empty City's gate was tested or decided.

## Identity

- Jirahar status passes maternally.
- A child of a Jirahar father and outsider mother requires formal reception.
- Sincere conversion is possible but demanding.
- Marriage to an outsider requires conversion.
- Tower, Road, Sea, and Turning ways are recognized orders of life, not hereditary castes.

## Places

Five living great towers remain: Migdal Qesheth, Dahava, Yamma, Melach, and Talga. Qiryath Reqa is the ruined holy city, empty under the Empty Charter of c. 396. Each is materially larger below ground than above. Migdal Shahar, the Dawn Tower, lies two days east of Qiryath on the Dawn Road; its Way-Gate was paired with an older tower in Vasharan country and has been closed since its scroll-keys were broken in 64 AR.
