## Locked History

- The sign of acceptance is physical and witnessed: an accepted offering burns without smoke and leaves the altar stones cool under the hands of the custodial houses.
- c. 743 BR: in the first winter of the Arqeshite siege the council drives foreign residents out to die between the lines; at the next appointed offering the smoke rises and the stones are hot. The **Withholding** lasts to c. 654. Its cause is unresolved.
- c. 742 BR: the Arqeshite River Empire destroys the First Qiryath and deports its court, priesthood, and skilled households. Exile judges rule on witnesses alone and keep the Book of the Unanswered Years.
- 690 BR: Arqesh falls to internal revolt and the separate commands of Arezan Tiravan and Eryndor III.
- 684 BR: after Eryndor's death the Decree of Roads and Houses permits selected return under Vasharan supremacy. The crown pays for the rebuilding as a wager on the sign's return.
- c. 660 BR: the Second Qiryath is consecrated during the Wars of the Inheritance; the consecration offering made in the Vasharan king's name smokes.
- c. 654 BR: the sign returns at Second Qiryath under the offering of Yaelith bat Coren's mixed households, after she refuses a severe faction's demand to expel the spouses and children of Arqeshite settlers. Usage keeps *the Withholding* for 743–654 and *the Silence* for 0 AR onward.
- 151–113 BR: Elian I's citizenship grant lets Jirahar scribes swear with the emperor's image veiled (the veiled oath; the house of Qoreth among them). Neriah bar Sorek directs relief in the Raveth hunger from 119 and resigns rather than administer the oath before the image that replaced neighbours' countersignature.
- Under the late Serrats (c. 80 BR): during Vashar's War of the Two Dawns a river-quarter house in Vasharan road service buys the Keepership's confirmation from Daryam and takes the altar after the city's interim Keeper has received the sign. At the **Trial before the Stars** (in the towers, *the Governor's Question*) the purchaser refuses the test proposed by the governor's augur of the Red Star, and the interim Keeper's test offering smokes; the governor rules on the sanctuary's written record. Atheria takes supremacy over Qiryath by a charter drafted by a Qoreth scribe: sacrifice, communal courts and burial jurisdiction protected in Qiryath and the province's tower towns; no image in the sanctuary; the council chooses the Keeper and the governor confirms him; a daily offering for the emperor; the governor sits at the pilgrimage and alone passes sentence of death for offences against imperial rule; the sanctuary's dues stand surety for the tribute; copies in the sanctuary and the chancery. The sign goes on answering the appointed offerings. Vashar takes the Dawn Portion of pilgrim dues by treaty early in Velen I's reign, until 65 AR. Families who refuse the charter settle in tower communities under Vasharan rule.
- 0 AR: the governor sitting at the pilgrimage orders Isfir's execution at Second Qiryath on the referral of Ezrahel bar Qoreth's council faction; Ezrahel's house keeps the sanctuary copy of the charter. Isfir rises on the third day. Sacrificial rites continue, and the authenticated sign of acceptance never returns.
- 61-68 AR: the Great Tower Revolt, opened when the governor's officers take sanctuary money for arrears under the charter's surety clause.
- 65 AR: Atheria destroys the Second Qiryath sanctuary, ends its charter government and places the city under direct provincial rule. The Dawn Portion ends. Many displaced Jirahar seek refuge beyond imperial jurisdiction, in the tower communities under Vasharan rule and the independent dry-margin towns.
- 68 AR: the last rebel fortress falls.
- 377–398 AR: Vestian turns older prejudice into imperial policy. The unkindled lamps of his Profession (385, the Second Silence) give his court a culprit: household rites are banned c. 387, the Raveth hearing house burns c. 389, most surviving eastern towers are destroyed or confiscated c. 388–396, and Qiryath is cleared c. 396 under a charter forbidding rebuilding *before the Mesakha*, in Vestian's own hand. The name *Second Silence* comes from baptised gateway congregations of Jirahar descent; the charter names no one to recognise the Mesakha.
- 1288–1291 AR: Meshalem proclaims himself the Mesakha and leads a return on roads the Black Wind has emptied of Covenant garrisons; offered proof or conversion by Qahirid authorities, he converts under Marzuq I (1291; d. 1303). The catastrophe produces present-day caution toward claimants.

## Authorial Truth

- Isfir is truly the Redeemer.
- Ezrahel bar Qoreth and a controlling council majority knowingly pressed Atherian authorities to execute him. Their guilt is real; hereditary guilt assigned to all Jirahar is false.
- The Great Prophet taught universal moral obligations. The mainstream doctrine condemning every knowing outsider who refuses them to death is a severe post-destruction interpretation, not the Prophet's original command.
- The Way-Gates follow stable threshold corridors impressed by the Exodus. They incur no Astral rent.

## Identity

- Jirahar status passes maternally.
- A child of a Jirahar father and outsider mother requires formal reception.
- Sincere conversion is possible but demanding.
- Marriage to an outsider requires conversion.
- Tower, Road, Sea, and Turning ways are recognized orders of life, not hereditary castes.

## Places

Five living great towers remain: Migdal Qesheth, Dahava, Yamma, Melach, and Talga. Qiryath Reqa is the ruined holy city, empty under the Empty Charter of c. 396. Each is materially larger below ground than above.
