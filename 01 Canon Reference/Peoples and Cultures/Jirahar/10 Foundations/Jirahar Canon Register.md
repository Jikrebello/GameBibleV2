## Locked History

- The sign of acceptance is physical and witnessed: an accepted offering burns without smoke and leaves the altar stones cool under the hands of the custodial houses.
- c. 1290–1137 BR: the judges' cycles. Tovan bar Rameq's bargain with the shelf's water (c. 1290) brings a stream down House Sereq's wadi out of season and drowns the raiders together with the pledge-children penned in their camp. The road judges give his household no office, because the Prophet's people found none on a bargain; the rite passes to his sister Imra's children, as the shelf villages reckon a line through mothers, and four of his sons drown with her youngest son when they seize him to call the water for them. Hador bar Mavin (c. 1166) fires Gedran's store-house to free Nareh bat Dovan and her father and kills them in the smoke; unhoused by his own elders, he ends as gate captain of the fortress he fought and sells his prisoners east. Othan bar Raqel (c. 1137) breaks the harvest truce for which his daughter Seleth stands surety, and the aged Hador kills her and two elders' children before the gate falls. Barakhel of the Reed Cloak executes surrendered raiders at the wells after promising safe conduct. No Reqa village has since accepted a commander's child as surety.
- c. 959–945 BR: Qiryath's guardians, who carry the appointed offering, are acclaimed as confirmed by the cool stones. The altar wood-carrier Yedran bar Aqev catches the offering when the guardian Shaqel Oram falls with fever on the stair (c. 959) and is named guardian. After he leaves Hanel bar Sem's ford garrison to die so that he can carry the spring offering, Rava bat Horel's public question (c. 945) is answered by Mikalet bat Oram for the custodians: the sign answers only the offering, and the bearer is chosen by lot. Yedran's house breaks in the grain-room war (c. 940–934).
- c. 826 BR: the guardian Qoram ben Teth has the custodian Tobel bar Yamin killed for his witness-staff, and an Arqeshite royal image stands on the sanctuary stair; the stones are cool. Neshkar II's demand of 746 rests on this precedent. In the drought of c. 823–821 Haviel of the Lower Stair has Zimra's diviners thrown down the shaft of the cistern they drained to wash the image, and the road judges unhouse him for killing without trial; c. 817 six women lift the swollen eastern gate for the families Qoram shut outside. There is no fire contest and no rain sign.
- c. 743 BR: in the first winter of the Arqeshite siege the council drives foreign residents out to die between the lines; at the next appointed offering the smoke rises and the stones are hot. The **Withholding** lasts to c. 654. Its cause is unresolved.
- c. 742 BR: the Arqeshite River Empire destroys the First Qiryath and deports its court, priesthood, and skilled households. Exile judges rule on witnesses alone and keep the Book of the Unanswered Years.
- 690 BR: Arqesh falls to internal revolt and the separate commands of Arezan Tiravan and Eryndor III.
- 684 BR: after Eryndor's death the Decree of Roads and Houses permits selected return under Vasharan supremacy. The crown pays for the rebuilding as a wager on the sign's return.
- c. 660 BR: the Second Qiryath is consecrated during the Wars of the Inheritance; the consecration offering made in the Vasharan king's name smokes.
- c. 654 BR: the sign returns at Second Qiryath under the offering of Yaelith bat Coren's mixed households, after she refuses a severe faction's demand to expel the spouses and children of Arqeshite settlers. Usage keeps *the Withholding* for 743–654 and *the Silence* for 0 AR onward.
- 151–113 BR: Elian I's citizenship grant lets Jirahar scribes swear with the emperor's image veiled (the veiled oath; the house of Qoreth among them). Neriah bar Sorek directs relief in the Raveth hunger from 119 and resigns rather than administer the oath before the image that replaced neighbours' countersignature.
- Under the late Serrats (c. 80 BR): during Vashar's War of the Two Dawns a river-quarter house in Vasharan road service buys the Keepership's confirmation from Daryam and takes the altar after the city's interim Keeper has received the sign. At the **Trial before the Stars** (in the towers, *the Governor's Question*) the purchaser refuses the test proposed by the governor's augur of the Red Star, and the interim Keeper's test offering smokes; the governor rules on the sanctuary's written record. Atheria takes supremacy over Qiryath by a charter drafted by a Qoreth scribe: sacrifice, communal courts and burial jurisdiction protected in Qiryath and the province's tower towns; no image in the sanctuary; the council chooses the Keeper and the governor confirms him; a daily offering for the emperor; the governor sits at the pilgrimage and alone passes sentence of death for offences against imperial rule; the sanctuary's dues stand surety for the tribute; copies in the sanctuary and the chancery. The sign goes on answering the appointed offerings. Vashar takes the Dawn Portion of pilgrim dues by treaty early in Velen I's reign, until 65 AR. Families who refuse the charter settle in tower communities under Vasharan rule.
- 0 AR: the governor sitting at the pilgrimage orders Isfir's execution at Second Qiryath on the referral of Ezrahel bar Qoreth's council faction; Ezrahel's house keeps the sanctuary copy of the charter. Isfir rises on the third day. Sacrificial rites continue, and the authenticated sign of acceptance never returns.
- 61-68 AR: the Great Tower Revolt, opened when the governor's officers take sanctuary money for arrears under the charter's surety clause.
- 65 AR: Atheria destroys the Second Qiryath sanctuary, ends its charter government and places the city under direct provincial rule. The Dawn Portion ends. Many displaced Jirahar seek refuge beyond imperial jurisdiction, in the tower communities under Vasharan rule and the independent dry-margin towns.
- 68 AR: the last stronghold, Migdal Shahar on the Dawn Road, falls after a siege dug downwards. Its Way-Gate passes the households to a tower in Vasharan country whose bench refuses anyone who bore arms; the war captain Eshkar bar Hanan rejects those terms, the gate keeper Yaltah bat Ozer opens on the bench's terms anyway, and after nine days she breaks the scroll-keys. The gate has not opened since. No ramp, no suicide pact.
- 377–398 AR: Vestian turns older prejudice into imperial policy. The unkindled lamps of his Profession (385, the Second Silence) give his court a culprit: household rites are banned c. 387, the Raveth hearing house burns c. 389, most surviving eastern towers are destroyed or confiscated c. 388–396, and Qiryath is cleared c. 396 under a charter forbidding rebuilding *before the Mesakha*, in Vestian's own hand. The name *Second Silence* comes from baptised gateway congregations of Jirahar descent; the charter names no one to recognise the Mesakha.
- c. 748–831 AR: Rab Yequtiel of Zanthir, a Jirahar physician who witnesses bound deathbeds in Zanthir's bond-hospitals and salt pans for fifty years, writes *What the Register Keeps*: a register holds the name and lets the person go. The Keepers of the Path denounce it as a licence for bondage.
- 1288–1291 AR: Meshalem proclaims himself the Mesakha and leads a return on roads the Black Wind has emptied of Covenant garrisons, under Qarayel's word; the Covenant's courts declare the returners' tower-town charters forfeit; at the Empty City (1291) he twice goes down the gate stair before the Keepers and twice turns back without touching the gate (d. 1303). The catastrophe produces present-day caution toward claimants and the rule that none may approach the gate before examination.

## Authorial Truth

- Isfir is truly the Redeemer.
- Ezrahel bar Qoreth and a controlling council majority knowingly pressed Atherian authorities to execute him. Their guilt is real; hereditary guilt assigned to all Jirahar is false.
- The Great Prophet taught universal moral obligations. The mainstream doctrine condemning every knowing outsider who refuses them to death is a severe post-destruction interpretation, not the Prophet's original command.
- The Way-Gates follow stable threshold corridors impressed by the Exodus. They incur no Astral rent.
- Tovan's bargain was with a place-bound Menhir court person of the Reqa shelf's karst water. It bound his line as the shelf villages reckoned it on the day of the petition; the drowning on the threshing floor was a missed rite, and it proved nothing about justice.
- Before 743 BR the sign was never withheld at an appointed offering, which is why the stair made kings. The cool stones under Qoram's image answered the offering only, and the Crowned Countenance fed on the diviners' proclamation. Mikalet's answer was true doctrine. The cause of the Withholding stays unresolved.
- Haviel's flame and rain are withdrawn; the rain after the killings was weather. The lifting of the gate (c. 817) was a genuine sign for the families' rescue and did not pardon him.
- At Migdal Shahar the legions killed the wounded and the old in the lower halls; the legate's claim that the rebels killed their own is false and stays a live claim in-world. The gate there is closed for want of keys and is no sign. [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]] §15.
- Meshalem was not the Mesakha. He believed he was until the gate stair and turned back because he would not lay a doubting hand on the gate. Nothing about the Empty City's gate was tested or decided.

## Identity

- Jirahar status passes maternally.
- A child of a Jirahar father and outsider mother requires formal reception.
- Sincere conversion is possible but demanding.
- Marriage to an outsider requires conversion.
- Tower, Road, Sea, and Turning ways are recognized orders of life, not hereditary castes.

## Places

Five living great towers remain: Migdal Qesheth, Dahava, Yamma, Melach, and Talga. Qiryath Reqa is the ruined holy city, empty under the Empty Charter of c. 396. Each is materially larger below ground than above. Migdal Shahar, the Dawn Tower, lies two days east of Qiryath on the Dawn Road; its Way-Gate was paired with an older tower in Vasharan country and has been closed since its scroll-keys were broken in 68 AR.
