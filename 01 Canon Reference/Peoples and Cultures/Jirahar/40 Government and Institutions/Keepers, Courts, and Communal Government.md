# Keepers, Courts, and Communal Government

Jirahar government is distributed among household courts, travelling company benches, tower councils, trained Keepers and the separate custodians of Way-Gates. These institutions developed to govern a dispersed people without a single territorial state or universal supreme court. Their authority rests on learning, communal acceptance and agreements with host powers, and difficult decisions gain wider force as other courts study and adopt them.

## Local Courts

Every tower, recognized quarter, and sufficiently large travelling company maintains a court. Small companies combine judges or submit difficult cases to the next tower circuit. Courts hear marriage, conversion, inheritance, contract, discipline, charity, and disputes voluntarily submitted by outsiders.

## Keepers

Keepers train in the Scrolls, Peshara, evidence, host law, route history, and ritual practice. A Keeper may teach without judging or judge without gate authority. No single initiation grants every office.

## High Keeper

High Keeper Zerahiah at Migdal Qesheth is first among senior Keepers. He convenes transregional opinions, authenticates common calendar rulings, and coordinates scrutiny of Mesakha claimants. Other courts may dissent. His strongest practical power is influence over gate cooperation.

## Gate Custody

Each anchor has a gate bench separate from ordinary civil courts. An origin bench and destination bench must agree before opening. Admission of outsiders is discretionary and recorded in sealed registers. A tower council cannot force its gate bench to open, though it can replace members through lawful procedure.

## Host Charters

Charters define taxation, quarter boundaries, worship, burial, internal courts, arms, market access, and obligations during war. They are renewed with local powers and can be honored, renegotiated, or betrayed. Jirahar internal law does not erase host criminal jurisdiction.

## Appeals and Dissent

There is no universal supreme court. Difficult rulings circulate as She'eltha among respected benches. Consensus gains authority through adoption, not decree. Preserved dissents allow later courts to reopen questions without claiming the Scrolls changed.
