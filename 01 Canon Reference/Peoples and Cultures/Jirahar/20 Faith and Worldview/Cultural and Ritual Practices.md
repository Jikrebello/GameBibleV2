# Cultural and Ritual Practices

## Daily Time

Households mark waking, the first meal, sunset, and sleep with short prayers and readings. Road and ship communities adjust communal hours to safe travel without pretending danger cancels obligation. Washing prepares for prayer when water is available; dust, cloth, or delayed washing have established road rulings.

## The Resting Day

One day in seven suspends ordinary commerce, travel, repairs, and adjudication except where life is endangered. Caravans make camp before dusk. Ships reduce work to navigation and safety. Towers open study halls and communal kitchens.

## Food and Table

Slaughter, blood removal, vessel separation, and source records allow households to eat across hostile markets without surrendering law. Hospitality to outsiders is permitted; shared food follows the stricter participant's rules when hosted by a Jirahar household.

## Marriage and Birth

Marriage requires witnessed consent, a household instrument, provision for inheritance and dissolution, and communal recognition. Birth enters a child into the maternal register. Naming joins family memory to the present company or tower.

## Death

The dead are washed, watched, recorded, and buried promptly when possible. Travelling communities maintain temporary sealed resting places and carry records or remains toward recognized ground. Way-Gates are not ordinarily opened for one corpse, but mass death can change the ruling.

## Pilgrimage

Qiryath is the greatest destination. Other circuits follow Exodus camps, destroyed towers, graves of judges, and sites where caravans survived expulsion. Pilgrimage is done by foot, road, river, or sea whenever health permits; using a Way-Gate for the entire journey is usually considered to miss the obligation.

## Music and Story

Road songs preserve routes and law. Sea rhythms coordinate work and carry genealogies. Tower epics remember sieges from mutually hostile viewpoints. Performance is not ornamental to memory: a community that loses archives may retain a judgement in a refrain.
