# Jirahar Memory of Isfir

## The Historical Guilt

Lord-Scribe Ezrahel bar Qoreth and a controlling majority of Qiryath's senior council judged Isfir a dangerous false claimant and knowingly pressed Atherian authorities for his death. They sought an imperial sentence they lacked power to carry out themselves. Atherian judges condemned him; Atherian soldiers killed him.

Jirahar legal memory does not absolve those elders. It disputes the later claim that their guilt passes through blood to every Jirahar.

## The Carpenter We Mourn

Mainstream Jirahar teaching rejects Isfir as Mesakha and divine Redeemer. It nevertheless remembers his Jirahar birth, his execution by foreign power, his mercy toward the condemned, and the catastrophes that followed. Some families mourn him as a wrong son of the towers; others avoid his name.

## The Scribe We Cannot Forgive

Sha'il bar Qoreth, later called Aquilan, took the dispute beyond Jirahar courts and proclaimed the Path fulfilled in Isfir. His writings became a foundation for the Redeemer churches and were repeatedly quoted to justify dispossession. Jirahar memory therefore treats him more bitterly than Isfir.

## The Silent Acceptance

After the Resurrection, sacrifice continued at Qiryath but its authenticated sign of divine acceptance ceased. Jirahar schools attribute this to defilement, coming destruction, hidden judgement, exile, priestly failure, or a test. Redeemer churches treat it as confirmation that sacrifice had been fulfilled. The silence is an observed historical fact; its public interpretation remains contested.

## Collective Blame

Church polemic turned a named council's real act into hereditary guilt. Later rulers used that accusation to cancel debts, seize property, dissolve charters, and sanctify violence. Reliable authorial history must name the responsible institution without repeating the persecutors' claim.
