# The Path of the Chosen

The **Orakh Bekhirin**, usually shortened to the **Orakha**, is the Jirahar covenant of worship, law, memory, and communal obligation. It claims continuity with the Great Prophet but recognizes that applying an ancient road-law across towers, ships, foreign quarters, and changing empires requires argument.

## The Three Scrolls

- **Sifra d'Dinai:** commands, prohibitions, court forms, and household obligations.
- **Sifra d'Masai:** Exodus, journeys, camp lists, disasters, and contested history.
- **Sifra d'Nevai:** prophetic warnings, restoration promises, and tests for claimants.

No single Scroll stands alone. Law without journey becomes domination; journey without law dissolves memory; prophecy without both produces ruin.

## The Peshara

The Peshara is the accumulated tradition of interpretation: majority rulings, preserved dissents, legal queries, exemplary disputes, narratives, proverbs, and local precedents. A later ruling may govern practice without altering the words of a Scroll. Minority opinions remain recorded because circumstances may change and because a victorious court can still be wrong.

## Worship

Jirahar worship addresses the Lord without images. Homes, caravans, ships, and study halls keep fixed hours of prayer, shared readings, blessing over food, remembrance of the Exodus, and care for the dead. The destruction of Qiryath ended lawful sacrifice, not prayer or covenant life.

## Law and Mercy

The Orakha governs marriage, descent, conversion, contracts, food, burial, charity, education, testimony, restitution, and communal discipline. It contains severe penalties alongside elaborate requirements of warning, evidence, jurisdiction, and lawful court. Argument over whether a sentence is theoretically correct but presently impossible is a central feature of Jirahar jurisprudence.

## Chosenness

Mainstream teaching treats chosenness as inherited obligation to keep the Path. Prideful schools turn it into superiority; penitential schools emphasize that election increases judgement rather than reducing it.
