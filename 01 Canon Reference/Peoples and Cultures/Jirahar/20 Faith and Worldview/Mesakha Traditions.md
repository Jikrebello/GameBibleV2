# Mesakha Traditions

The **Mesakha**, the Awaited One, is expected to gather the dispersed, restore Qiryath, vindicate the Path, establish truthful judgement, and settle the relation between Jirahar and the nations.

## Keeper Tests

A claimant must demonstrate command of the Scrolls without rehearsed trickery, restore divided households, judge without purchased testimony, refuse patron power, and produce signs connected to Qiryath that cannot be staged by warlock script, contract, Shade, relic fraud, or Idol pressure. No claimant has passed.

## Competing Readings

- **Orthodox Keepers** expect a personal ruler and lawful court.
- **Restorationists** emphasize return, sovereignty, and rebuilding.
- **Mercy jurists** emphasize justice and the near-impossibility of condemning informed outsiders without coercion.
- **Dominion zealots** emphasize subjugation of hostile nations and preparation by force.
- **Hidden Words mystics** allow personal, communal, and cosmic meanings to overlap.

## Qiryath

The Empty City's perpetual charter forbids rebuilding *before the Mesakha*, words Emperor Vestian wrote in his own hand c. 396 AR; Jirahar jurists gloss them as "before recognition of the Mesakha", and the charter names no one to do the recognising. Pilgrims may enter marked courts, mourn, study, and remove nothing. Its silent Way-Gate is treated as the most dangerous possible sign.

## Meshalem

Meshalem was a road-clerk of Migdal Melach. In 1287 a Black Wind ride burned a caravan camp on the Iqashar road and passed his wagons by, and in 1288 he proclaimed himself the Mesakha because the Wind had turned aside for him. Families of half the tower towns sold or mortgaged their goods and took the return road to the Empty City, on roads the Black Wind had emptied of Covenant garrisons and at a price per wagon Qarayel himself had named. Behind them the Covenant's courts declared the charters of every family that had left forfeit.

By 1291 tens of thousands were camped outside Qiryath Reqa, and the Eclessian lords of the riverlands were threatening to clear the camp. To save the people, the Keepers asked Meshalem to do the one thing the Keeper Tests allow a claimant to do at Qiryath: go down the stair to the silent gate before the Keepers and the whole return, and lay his hand on it. Twice he went down. Twice he came back up without touching it. One account says he said *not with these hands*; most say he said nothing. That is why he is called **the Twice-Turned**.

The return broke up within the month. Its families had no charters to go home to, and Sestaran creditors foreclosed on what they had mortgaged; many died on the roads, and many settled landless in Eclessian river towns. Meshalem stayed at the Empty City as a mourner. The Keepers let him sweep the outer court for a generation as a living warning, and their rule against claimants hardened while he was there to be pointed at. He died there about 1316. A few households still hold that his turning proved him true, because the Mesakha would never submit to a test set by fear; the Keepers call them the Sweepers and do not argue with them. Since Meshalem, no claimant may go down the gate stair until the Keepers have examined him, presumption against claimants has become nearly absolute, and the Keepers prohibit any attempt to wake the gate.
