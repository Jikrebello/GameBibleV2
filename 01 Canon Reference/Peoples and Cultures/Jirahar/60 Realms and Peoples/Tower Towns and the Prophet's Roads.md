# Tower Towns and the Prophet's Roads

The Jirahar hold no territory on any map — they hold **verticals and vectors**: the tower towns, and the ways between them. This note closes the geography thread and grounds the canon "magic-infused transportation" in the World Egg's own physics.

## How a Tower Town Works

A tower town is a **vertical city under its own law** (canon): one to five great towers with their skirt-walls, built narrow because the Jirahar buy no more land than they must and defend all of it. Interior logic, base to crown: gates and caravanserai (where foreign law ends); markets and workshops; the banking floors; dwelling tiers by family and lineage; the study-halls of the Peshara; and at the crown, the **scroll-vault and the Way-Gate**, the two things a tower exists to protect. Every tower keeps the Orakha's law within its walls by ancient charter, renewed with each host kingdom at ruinous rates — the recurring tragedy being that charters are parchment and pogroms are not.

## The Prophet's Roads (the spine's ruling on the Way-Gates)

**the Way-Gates follow the Exodus.** When the Great Prophet led humanity across the Great Glass Desert and the scattering routes beyond, the crossing laid down **metaphysical mass** — the holiest freight ever moved, moved on foot — and where the exodus camped, the mass pooled. Those campsites became stable, rent-free corridors through the Astral shallows: **the Prophet's Roads**, anchored not by any patron's hoard but by the Exodus itself. The tower towns stand where they stand because *the towers are built on the campsites* — every one of them. The Jirahar did not choose defensible ground; they settled their god's own footprints, and the Way-Gates simply walk the roads their ancestors walked, out of the world and back into it a hundred leagues on.

**What this means, and who must never learn it:** the network pays no astral rent — the only long-range transport in the world the Concord of Weights cannot price, which the patrons find intolerable and inexplicable. The Keepers understand exactly what they hold and encode it in the driest possible liturgical language ("the Ways of our fathers are sufficient for us"). The Church's scholars suspect; the Covenant's jurists suspect; the Medori would pay anything; and a Way-Gate has never once opened for an outsider, because the Roads themselves refuse — you walk them by inheritance or not at all. *(Whether a sincere convert can walk them is a question the Keepers have never tested in public, and the answer is a story waiting to happen.)*

## The Great Towers (the network's anchors)

- **Migdal Qesheth** ("Tower of the Bow") — the sacred capital, in the desert margin near Zanthir under the emir's old charter: seat of **High Keeper Zerahiah**, the great scroll-vault, and the master Way-Gate. The bow is the covenant-sign: bent, not broken.
- **Migdal Dahava** ("Gold Tower") — the banking anchor, in the Velian belt: counterweight to the Medori, correspondent to everyone, trusted by no one, indispensable to all.
- **Migdal Yamma** ("Sea Tower") — the water-ark port on the Thalassian's eastern shore near Halmenth: where the caravan-fleets winter and the Sermoran factors pretend not to count the sails.
- **Migdal Melach** ("Salt Tower") — the Covenant-facing caravan gate on the Iqashar road, protected by Qarayel's old flat peace-price, which the Four Hordes still honor.
- **Migdal Talga** ("Snow Tower") — the far southern anchor in the Eclessian marches, serving the frontier trade; the tower nearest Grenzburg's world, and the one whose charter is thinnest.
- **Qiryath Reqa, the Empty City** — the mother-tower of the old eastern homeland, razed in the persecutions (c. 300–400 AR) and **never rebuilt by law**: the Jirahar hold its ruin in perpetual charter (the one clause no Eclessian crown has dared revoke) and keep it empty for the Mesakha, who is prophesied to appear there. A Jirahar holy void in the middle of Eclessian Christendom — pilgrims walk years to stand in an absence. Its dead Way-Gate is the network's missing anchor, and the Keepers' quietest article of faith is that it is not dead but *waiting*.

## The Caravans Between

The land-arks and water-arks (canon) move where the Roads don't reach and carry what gates can't: bulk goods, brides, the dead going home, and the yearly **circuit of the Keepers** that stitches the diaspora into one people. Caravan law is the Orakha in miniature — a rolling tower.

## Story Use

Charter-renewal crises (a crown that wants the Talga tower's gate more than its taxes); the first outsider to walk a Road (or die refusing); the Empty City's gate flickering — and every power on the board learning of it within the season; the patrons' standing bounty for an explanation of the rent-free network; and the Grenzburg-adjacent hook: when winter closes every pass, Migdal Talga's gate still opens, and everyone besieged knows it.
