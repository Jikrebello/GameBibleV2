# Qiryath Reqa

**Qiryath Reqa**, the Empty City, contains the remains of both sacred tower cities. It stands in Eclessian territory under a perpetual charter that permits Jirahar custody and pilgrimage while forbidding reconstruction *before the Mesakha*, words Emperor Vestian wrote into the chancery draft in his own hand about 396 AR in place of "in perpetuity".

Qiryathi identity once included residents, neighbourhoods and sanctuary households of the living city. It also survived among people deported from First Qiryath and among families returning to build the second city. Qiryath remained one centre within a dispersed Jirahar people rather than the source of every Jirahar community.

## Archaeological Layers

- early caravan court and First Tower foundations;
- Arqeshite siege cuts, destruction fill, and deportation records;
- Second Tower courts, expanded sanctuary, cisterns, schools, and dwellings;
- charter-period works: the governor's pilgrimage tribunal outside the sanctuary gate, and the Qoreth archive where the sanctuary copy of the charter was kept;
- siege works of 64–67 AR, the broken cistern galleries of the river quarter, sanctuary burn layers, and garrison works;
- diminished settlement under direct Atherian administration after the annulment of the old charter;
- survey and clearance works authorised by Astrion in 349–350 AR and abandoned when the Jirahar crews refused to work under the Red Star's wardens;
- Vestian clearance (c. 396 AR), sealed entrances, and later pilgrim paths.

The city was always larger below ground than above. Most deep halls are collapsed, flooded, sealed, or legally forbidden. Excavation is both archaeology and sacrilege.

## The Sanctuary

The altar's sign of acceptance could be touched: an accepted offering burned without smoke and left the stones cool under the hands of the custodial houses. It was first withheld in the first winter of the Arqeshite siege, and it returned about 654 BR at the rebuilt altar, under an offering carried by mixed households of returners and settlers.

Every appointed offering went up the sanctuary stair. In the first city the guardian of the walls carried the basket, and the crowd in the paved yard cheered him beside the cool stones as if the sign had confirmed him. Yedran bar Aqev, a carrier of altar wood, became guardian around 953 BR by catching the basket when a fevered guardian fell on the steps. Around 940 two parties fought for the basket on those steps, and it reached the altar in the hands of a dying lamp-filler. His widow, Rava bat Horel, climbed them at the next festival ahead of the offering and asked the custodians whom the stones had answered, and from then on the bearer was chosen by lot. Around 826 soldiers killed the wife and brother of the custodian Tobel bar Yamin at his door, for the witness-staff he had carried away rather than lay his hand on the altar while an Arqeshite royal image stood on the stair. The image stood there, the offering burned without smoke, the day was written with a blank, and eighty years later King Neshkar II cited that morning when he demanded a place for his own.

Sacrifice continued from the Second Tower's consecration until 67 AR. From the late Serrat years the sanctuary stood under an Atherian charter that kept every image out of its courts and required a daily offering on the emperor's behalf; the tower houses remembered its making as the Governor's Question. After Isfir's resurrection, ritual fire and procedure continued but the authenticated sign of divine acceptance never returned. The offering for the emperor was made every morning to the last, and the altar's vessels left through the city's gate before the legions reached the precinct.

## The burial slope

Isfir was executed beside the river road and buried outside Second Qiryath. The destruction of 67 AR cut off access to the slope and dispersed households that knew the approach. Redeemer pilgrims come to the ruined city in memory of the Resurrection. The original chamber has no authenticated location in 1360 AR. Pilgrims use paths maintained by the Jirahar custodians under the Empty Charter. [[The Rise of the Redeemer]]

## Custody

Jirahar custodians maintain paths, stabilise masonry, receive pilgrims, bury discovered remains, and prevent removal. Eclessian officers guard the outer boundary and repeatedly dispute what counts as preservation rather than rebuilding.

## Silent Gate

The gate chamber lies beneath the Second Tower ruin, at the foot of its own stair. It last opened in 67 AR, when the Keeper Elqan bar Sered sent the sanctuary's vessels and the custodians' books through it, before the legions broke in, to a bench the Keepers have never named. It has not opened since, and tower talk says it is still on the road with the vessels. The gate of Migdal Shahar, two days east on the Dawn Road, had closed three years earlier for a plainer reason: its keeper sent the tower's households through to Vasharan country and broke the scroll-keys before the legions reached her.

From 1288 the claimant Meshalem led families from half the tower towns to the Empty City, and by 1291 tens of thousands were camped outside the ruin while the riverland lords threatened to clear them. The Keepers asked him to go down the gate stair before the whole return and lay his hand on the gate. Twice he went down, and twice he came back up without touching it; one account gives him the words *not with these hands*. The return broke up within the month. Meshalem stayed, swept the outer court as a mourner and died there in 1303, and a few households the Keepers call the Sweepers still hold that his turning proved him true.

Since Meshalem no claimant may go down the gate stair until the Keepers have examined him, and the Keepers prohibit any attempt to wake the gate. A true response would be treated as possible Mesakha evidence and an international emergency. The charter names no one to recognise the Mesakha. The Church claims the power to say which signs are real, inherited from the augur colleges in 383; the Keepers hold the Mesakha tests. A sign recognised by either would be claimed to lift the ban on rebuilding.

## Present Conflict

Pilgrim numbers are rising after reports of warmth beneath the gate stair. Old custodial families note that by the ancient sign warm stones meant an offering refused, and the Book of the Unanswered Years says so. Eclessian clergy want an investigation; Jirahar custodians suspect staged prophecy or an attempt to void the charter.
