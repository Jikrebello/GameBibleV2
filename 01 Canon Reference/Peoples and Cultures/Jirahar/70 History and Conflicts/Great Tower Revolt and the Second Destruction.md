# Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction

The Great Tower Revolt began in 66 AR against Atherian rule. In 70 AR the legions took Second Qiryath and burned its sanctuary. The last rebel fortress fell in 73 AR. Atheria abolished the charter under which Qiryath had governed its communal affairs and placed the city and its approaches under direct military and provincial authority. Captivity and flight scattered the city's people. Many displaced Jirahar sought refuge beyond direct Atherian rule, where older communities already had households, courts and agreements with local rulers.

## Causes

After Isfir's death, the silence of sacrificial acceptance divided Qiryath. Militants claimed the sign would return when foreign rule ended. They cited the Trial before the Stars, when the sign had stayed silent before an Atherian governor's augur. Older houses answered that the Withholding had ended under a Vasharan king, at an offering carried by households the severe faction of that day wanted expelled. Cautious houses feared another Arqeshite ruin and tried to preserve the charter. In 66 AR the governor's officers took money from the sanctuary treasury on the pretext of arrears, under the charter's clause that made the sanctuary's dues surety for the tribute. When people protested, soldiers killed demonstrators near the sanctuary. Intermediaries tried to arrange payment and secure the withdrawal of troops. Militant priests stopped the offering made on behalf of the emperor, and the garrison lost its local supply and protection.

## Revolt, 66 AR

Rebels drove the garrison from its posts and broke the first relief force as it withdrew through the river approaches. They took its siege equipment and stores. Qiryath appointed commanders and collected food and money in its own name. Towns and travelling companies divided over whether to answer its summons. Priestly houses, popular captains and aristocratic moderates competed over the city's defences. Armed factions killed rivals, including people trying to negotiate a surrender.

From 67 to 69 AR Atherian commanders recovered crossings, fortified towns and storehouses before marching on Qiryath. The campaign left burned villages and captives and drove people towards the city. Some households crossed the frontier before the siege closed. Others chose the tower's cisterns and walls over flight, and brought what food they could carry.

## Siege and Destruction, 70 AR

The Atherian army arrived while the city still held pilgrims and refugees. The legions breached the outer defences, built a siege line around the remaining city and cut off its river landings. Rival factions had burned or seized stores to deny them to one another. Hunger and disease spread through the refuge halls. Soldiers killed or displayed some people who tried to escape. The ordinary sacrifice ceased during the siege as the priests ran out of people and provisions to continue it.

In the final assault, fighting reached the sanctuary precinct. Fire spread into the sanctuary and upper tower, and the legions killed defenders and civilians inside. The commanders' accounts later blamed the first flame on soldiers acting without orders. Survivors remembered a systematic destruction of the precinct once it was taken. The first ignition remains disputed. Imperial officers stripped the sanctuary, took its vessels to Caleran for a triumph, and demolished defences after the fire. Captives were sorted for sale, forced service and ransom.

The siege works and destruction cut off access to the burial slope where Isfir had been laid. The households that knew the approach dispersed, and later pilgrims could no longer establish which chamber had held him.

## Last Fortress, 73 AR

A final rebel community held a cliff fortress after Qiryath fell. Imperial troops enclosed it with camps and built a ramp to reach the wall. Its end survives in competing accounts of heroic refusal, killings ordered by its own leaders and imperial massacre. The ramp, assault and mass death are established. The order in which people died and the speeches attributed to their leaders remain disputed.

## Direct rule and exile

The Atherian governor annulled the sanctuary charter after the city's capture. Imperial officers took the courts, cisterns, roads and records of land and tribute. A permanent garrison held the crossings and the surviving approaches. The governor assessed confiscated sanctuary and rebel estates as imperial property and levied a tribute in place of the old sanctuary contribution within the empire. The receipts and garrison registers remained instruments of control long after the army left the siege lines. A diminished settlement of labourers, returning families and households granted leave to remain lived under direct provincial rule. Vestian's clearance, about 396 AR, ended that settlement and left the city empty under a charter forbidding rebuilding "before the Mesakha". [[Vestian Persecutions and the Long Diaspora]]

The free exile from Qiryath went chiefly beyond direct Atherian control. Across the eastern frontier, households sought kin in communities under Vasharan authority. Others took dry-margin routes through independent well towns and territories where caravan leaders could negotiate protection. Hosts bargained over water, pasture, taxes, burial ground and responsibility for newcomers. Some accepted a family while refusing its armed escort. Older settlements absorbed relatives, teachers and craftspeople as well as people with few resources to offer. Captives sent into the empire and Jirahar communities already living there had a different experience of the same defeat.

The sanctuary priesthood lost its place of service. Lawful sacrifice at Qiryath never resumed. Study halls, households, travelling courts and teachers of the Peshara took on work previously coordinated through the sanctuary. They preserved names, judged disputed vows, found money for ransoms and kept feast days across different jurisdictions. Older diaspora institutions supplied places and people capable of receiving the exiles.
