# Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction

## Causes

After Isfir's death, the silence of sacrificial acceptance divided Qiryath. Imperial taxation, corrupt governors, seizures from pilgrims, factional killings, and repeated insults to the sanctuary turned dispute into revolt. Militants claimed the silence would end when foreign rule ended; cautious houses feared another Arqeshite ruin.

## Revolt, 66 AR

Rebels expelled the imperial garrison and defeated the first relief force. Tower and road communities divided over participation. Early victory produced rival councils rather than unity: priestly houses, popular captains, aristocratic moderates, and uncompromising zealots fought over command and supplies.

## Siege and Destruction, 70 AR

Atherian legions isolated the river approaches, built siege lines, and starved the city while factions inside destroyed one another's stores. The upper tower and sanctuary burned during the final assault. Sacred vessels were carried into imperial triumph; captives were enslaved, impressed, ransomed, or dispersed.

The sacrificial fire had continued until the siege. Its sign of acceptance had not appeared since 0 AR.

## Last Fortress, 73 AR

A final rebel community held a cliff fortress after Qiryath fell. Its end became competing memory: heroic refusal, murder-suicide under fanatic command, imperial massacre, or all three in sequence. Authorial history confirms mass death but not every later speech attributed to its leaders.

## Consequences

The priestly order loses its functioning sanctuary. Study halls, households, travelling courts, and the Peshara become the main instruments of continuity. Qiryath remains inhabited in diminished form until later persecution, but lawful sacrifice is never restored.
