# Vestian Persecutions and the Long Diaspora

## Before Vestian

The destruction of 70 AR had already scattered the people. During the Underground Centuries, Jirahar and Redeemer congregations sometimes sheltered one another and sometimes denounced one another. Local violence existed, but imperial policy remained inconsistent.

## From Riot to Policy

After the Redeemer's faith gained imperial power, theologians and courtiers weaponized the role of Ezrahel's council into inherited accusation. Property desire, debt cancellation, prophecy panic, and religious zeal converged. Vestian's reign transformed those pressures into coordinated restrictions, confiscation, expulsion, and destruction.

Between 377 and 395 AR, most surviving eastern towers were seized or razed. Qiryath's diminished settlement was cleared and placed under a perpetual empty charter. Children, scrolls, tools, and the dead moved through road and sea networks built long before the crisis.

## The Long Diaspora

Refugees established or enlarged tower communities, host-city quarters, Sea Fleets, and Turning Companies. Charters became central political instruments. Maternal registers protected identity across separated households, while conversion courts prevented the covenant from becoming entirely closed by ancestry.

## Inherited Wound

Church cultures remember the Jirahar through Isfir, suspicion, and collective blame. The Jirahar remember that a persecuted faith became an imperial persecutor. Sincere repentance and genuine protection exist, but neither erases the legal and liturgical inheritance of the violence.
