# First Qiryath and the Arqeshite Exile

The first destruction of Qiryath occurred in 742 BR, when the Arqeshite River Empire conquered the city after its rulers refused royal worship and the surrender of priestly hostages. Arqesh broke the upper tower, stripped the sanctuary and deported much of the city's learned and skilled population. The altar's sign of acceptance had already failed in the siege's first winter, and it stayed withheld through the exile and the rebuilding. The exile dispersed Jirahar law and scholarship among the eastern river cities and taught the Jirahar to judge, pray and keep their calendar without the sign, so that Qiryath could later be rebuilt without recovering its former sovereignty.

## The Arqeshite River Empire

Arqesh was an early mortal empire of levees, walled river capitals, deportation armies, court astronomers, and royal estates in the eastern lands later absorbed by Atheria. It conquered through water control as much as battle: a rebellious city could lose canals before it lost walls.

## Destruction, 742 BR

At Qiryath an accepted offering burned without smoke and left the altar stones cool to the hand, and Arqeshite canal districts had begun bringing their disputes up the road to be judged over those stones. An Arqeshite royal image had stood on the sanctuary stair once before, around 826 BR, when the guardian Seram ben Teth sent soldiers to take a witness-staff from a custodian who would not stand beside it. The custodian escaped, and his wife and brother were killed at his door. The offering burned without smoke that morning. The river court remembered the clean fire; Qiryath's custodians remembered that the day had been written with a blank where the third witness belonged. King Ilbarun II demanded that the royal image stand in the upper sanctuary beside the altar and that the priestly houses send hostages. Qiryath refused. In the first winter of the siege that followed, the council declared the city's foreign residents unhoused to save grain, so that no household within the walls might feed them, and they died through the winter in the caravan yard below the sanctuary. At the next appointed offering the smoke rose black and the stones were hot. The council split, and households began leaving by the burial passages. Arqesh entered the city in 742, stripped the sanctuary, broke the upper tower, and deported judges, engineers, artisans, singers, and leading families to imperial river cities.

Road companies carried surviving scrolls away. Rural households remained around the ruin. Other tower towns became independent centres.

## Exile

Deported Jirahar served as scribes, metalworkers, canal engineers, musicians, traders, and court dependants. Some prospered without forgetting captivity. The Peshara's first great exile collections date from disputes over worship, food, marriage, and law far from Qiryath. Exile judges ruled on witnesses alone, prayer turned towards the ruined city, and at each appointed festival a line was written in the Book of the Unanswered Years: the year, and "no answer".

## Return, 690–660 BR

Arqesh fell in 690 BR after House Ubar's succession war brought provincial revolts, failed water government and two foreign campaigns into the same struggle. Arezan Tiravan's Vashari coalition held the eastern roads and rebellious provinces. Eryndor III of Ortheia brought the principal siege army and allied fleet from the west. The capital's own dredging gangs and deportee quarters opened its gates.

After Eryndor died in 684, Arezan issued the Decree of Roads and Houses. It restored the sanctuary site and permitted selected deported households and sacred objects to return, while requiring property registration, road service and acceptance of Vasharan supremacy. The crown paid for the platform and the first courses of the rebuilding, wagering that the sign would return under its protection, and let the grant lapse when the Wars of the Inheritance took its road gangs. Returners, stayers and road families contested fields, appointments and membership throughout those wars, and finished the sanctuary themselves. The Second Qiryath was consecrated around 660 without recreating the former kingdom. The consecration offering was made in the king's name, and it smoked.

A severe faction blamed the silence on the spouses and children of Arqeshite settlers and demanded their expulsion. The cistern keeper Yaelith bat Coren refused, and several old houses left the city. Around 654, at the next appointed festival, those mixed households carried the offering, and the stones went cool. The departing houses called the witnesses partisans; Vasharan inscriptions credited the king.

## Legacy

The first exile established a permanent principle: the Path can be kept without sovereignty or one sacred city. The sign had come back to a city with no king, under an offering carried by households a faction had wanted gone, and Jirahar who wait for acceptance to return still cite that morning. The exile also established the recurring wound that host rulers may value Jirahar skill while treating Jirahar households as movable assets.
