# Jirahar Economy and Trade

Jirahar economic life is diverse because dispersion requires communities to survive under incompatible laws and ecologies. No hidden council coordinates all trade, and most households are not wealthy.

## Transport and Brokerage

Road Companies carry goods between minor markets the great houses ignore. Sea Fleets connect ports without requiring settlement. Turning Companies bridge road and river systems. Caravan brokers know tariffs, seasonal routes, customary courts, animal care, and which promises survive a change of ruler.

## Skilled Work

Common trades include wheel and metal repair, animal dealing, music, performance, medicine, textiles, dyeing, salvage, navigation, translation, book repair, copying, river engineering, fishing, seasonal harvest work, ship ownership, and warehousing.

## Credit

Host restrictions on landholding, guild entry, and military office pushed some Jirahar into lending, brokerage, insurance, remittance, and portable wealth. Migdal Dahava became a major clearing and credit center. This is an institution and historical niche, not an ethnic instinct or universal conspiracy.

Letters issued by one recognized court can be authenticated at another, but solvency depends on named houses, reserves, reputation, and local enforcement. The Medori compete with Dahava while using Jirahar correspondents where neither can operate directly.

## Way-Gates

Way-Gates can move complete caravans between fixed tower anchors. Keeper control, preparation, destination consent, and political secrecy prevent routine commercial use. Gates cannot reach most customers, ports, farms, fairs, or roads. They redistribute people and urgent cargo; they do not replace transport economies.

## Arms and Powder

Jirahar companies purchase and use ordinary weapons according to local law. They possess no unique black-powder formula or monopoly. Defensive advantage comes from movement, intelligence, prepared refuge, disciplined convoy practice, and occasional gate access.

## Exploitation and Poverty

Host rulers levy protection taxes, revoke licenses, confine quarters, seize collateral, and compel middleman roles. Internal lenders can also exploit desperate households. Peshara law limits interest, foreclosure, subsistence seizure, and sale of communal obligations, but enforcement varies.
