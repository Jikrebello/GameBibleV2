# Social and Political Structure

The Jirahar possess common law and institutions without a common sovereign. Households provide the first setting for worship, descent, education and care; travelling companies and protected urban quarters govern shared work; tower councils maintain settled communities. Keepers and the High Keeper coordinate learning and difficult rulings across these bodies, but none can command the diaspora as a state.

## Household

The household is the first unit of worship, descent, education, care, and property. Maternal registers establish identity, but spouses, converts, apprentices, dependants, and adopted children make lived households broader than one bloodline.

## Company and Quarter

Road, Sea, and Turning companies elect or recognize practical officers for route, stores, defence, worship, and dispute. Host-city quarters maintain councils that negotiate taxes, gates, burial, schooling, fire safety, and relations with local authorities.

## Tower Councils

Each tower has its own council of household delegates, guild representatives, judges, and Keepers. No tower council governs the others. Migdal Qesheth carries sacred precedence, while Dahava, Yamma, Melach, and Talga can reject its political requests.

## Keepers of the Path

Keepers are trained judges, teachers, archivists, and ritual authorities. High Keeper Zerahiah is first in dignity and coordinates rulings of common concern, but he is neither king nor universal legislator. See [[Keepers, Courts, and Communal Government]].

## Wealth and Status

Old tower houses, successful fleet owners, famous judges, caravan captains, artisans, and patrons possess different forms of standing. Wealth buys education and advocacy but does not automatically create legal authority. Poverty can make formal rights difficult to exercise.

## Internal Tensions

- tower stability versus road independence;
- wealthy charter patrons versus ordinary households;
- strict courts versus Mercy jurists;
- old maternal houses versus converts;
- local civic loyalty versus transregional obligation;
- Keeper secrecy versus a community's right to know why a gate remains closed.
