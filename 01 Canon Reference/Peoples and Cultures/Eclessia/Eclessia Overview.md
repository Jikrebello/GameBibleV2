# Eclessia Overview

Eclessia is the eastern inheritance of the Atherian world: a group of kingdoms, duchies, free cities and frontier marches lying east of Thalmyria. Its peoples share the historical influence of the Eclessian Church, while their governments, languages of daily life and local customs reflect different mixtures of provincial, Folk, Markish and Vesari ancestry.

Cathedrals, parish churches, monasteries, universities and guilds remain prominent institutions. Their authority is now divided by the Freeflame Reformation and the territorial settlement of the Korrheim Accord. Religious allegiance consequently affects households, travel and government throughout the region.

## Historical formation

Atherian provincial settlement joined older populations through conquest, military service, town life and intermarriage. In the eastern riverlands, imperial builders reused canals and cities inherited from the earlier Arqeshite Empire. Folk communities entered provincial society on differing terms, while others retained independent kindreds and institutions.

Markish communities developed along the military frontier through the mixture of garrison settlers and Folk households. Farther east, the Vesari formed through river settlement, warfare, marriage and the incorporation of several populations. Their princely and assembly traditions later contributed to Virelia's elective monarchy.

The Fall of 905, refugee movements and the rise of regional powers altered these provincial societies. The Eclessian Church provided a shared institutional inheritance through the formation of separate crowns. The Great Split of 1015 divided it from the Thalmyric Church; exchanges of refugees and religious exiles continued across the border. ## Regions and their differences

[[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/60 Realms and Peoples/Kingdom of Aurelia/The Kingdom of Aurelia Overview.md|Aurelia]] is the centre of the Primarchal crown, major pilgrimage and the institutional old Church. Neighbouring [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/60 Realms and Peoples/Duchy of Veridancia/The Duchy of Veridancia Overview.md|Veridancia]] combines vineyards with universities, seminaries, printing and theological debate.

[[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/60 Realms and Peoples/Duchy of Caldria/The Duchy of Caldria Overview.md|Caldria]] supports knightly orders, war industries and frontier armies. In [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/60 Realms and Peoples/Kingdom of Virelia/The Kingdom of Virelia Overview.md|Virelia]], grain estates and an elective sabre-right nobility shape power over the Vesari-derived population. Household piety there retains practices that differ from its official Church allegiance.

The [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/60 Realms and Peoples/Ironvale Dominion/The Ironvale Dominion Overview.md|Ironvale Dominion]] is a centre of foundries, guild government, black powder and Freeflame power. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/60 Realms and Peoples/Confederation of Korrheim/The Confederation of Korrheim Overview.md|Korrheim]] is organised through cantons and is known for engineering and hired companies. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/60 Realms and Peoples/Duchy of Brauvalis/The Duchy of Brauvalis Overview.md|Brauvalis]] combines brewing towns, monastic crafts and arrangements for confessional compromise.

The [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/60 Realms and Peoples/Sylvan Marches/The Sylvan Marches Overview.md|Sylvan Marches]] lie along the colder southern frontier, where parish government becomes less continuous and Folk institutions remain visible. Southern coastal communities maintain watches and beacons against seaborne raiding. Their history is distinct from the Saltborn settlements of western Kedrava in Thalmyria.

## Government and public institutions

Authority is distributed among crowns, hereditary lords, cities, guilds and Church bodies. Their relationships differ by territory. Universities train clergy and scholars; monasteries preserve learning and productive estates; guilds regulate trades and entry into urban occupations. Military orders hold their own responsibilities and possessions.

Parish life brings the wider religious order into baptism, marriage, burial, teaching and seasonal observance. Cathedral and relic institutions draw pilgrims, wealth and legal disputes. Reform movements challenge the use of these powers, while rulers seek religious support for local independence or wider claims.

Citizenship, estate, confession and ancestry affect a person's position in different ways. A common Church inheritance has not prevented regional competition, noble coercion or disputes over the powers of towns and villages. The [[Historical Atlas of Eclessia]] traces how these arrangements developed.

## Work and the household year

Regional production shapes food and exchange. Markish communities favour rye and barley bread, oats, pork, preserved cabbage, river fish and beer. Wheat, oil, wine, fruit and cheese are prominent in the southern provincial lands. Vesari households use buckwheat, beets, smoked fish and honey-spirits. Coastal fisheries supply preserved fish to inland markets.

The Church's fasting calendar has helped sustain that fish trade. Feasts and seasonal work organise marriages, slaughter, salting, grave-tending and the replenishment of household stores. At Kindlenight, families relight their hearths from the church lamp. Children receive clay candle pots at Saint Solana's feast. Freeflame communities observe much of the same year while interpreting saints' days as memorials.

Marriage involves public banns, family property and vows at the church door. Guild apprenticeships and service carry young people into other households. Funerals gather mourners around lamps, graves and shared food. Songs and jokes about clergy, landlords and bankers accompany these institutions, giving public expression to grievances that formal proceedings cannot always settle.

## Faith, reform and neighbouring peoples

The [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/20 Faith and Worldview/Eclessian Church Overview.md|Eclessian Church]] maintains a visible hierarchy of doctrine, sacraments, councils and religious institutions. The [[Freeflame Reformation]] challenged its monopoly of authority while continuing devotion to the Redeemer. The [[The Korrheim Accord|Korrheim Accord]] settled confession territorially, leaving mixed families and border parishes to negotiate matters the agreement did not resolve.

Jirahar communities occupy sharply different positions under these local governments. Eclessia contains [[Qiryath Reqa]], [[Migdal Talga]], protected quarters and sites of earlier persecution. Conversion pressure, confiscation, restitution and pilgrimage remain disputed. The guilt of the elders involved in Isfir's death has repeatedly been used to assign false hereditary blame to later Jirahar communities.

Relations with Thalmyria combine shared religious ancestry, institutional rivalry and continuing exchanges of people and learning. Along the Folk frontier, parish expansion meets surviving clan-land and hearth-law.

## Eclessia in 1360

The effects of the [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|Grey Sweat]] remain visible in labour and landholding. Shortages of workers weakened serfdom across much of Eclessia, while Virelia's nobles imposed tighter obligations on survivors. Guild towns, estates and competing confessions inherited different advantages from the same disaster.

The Accord provides a political settlement without ending religious conflict. Princes, reforming cities and the Sanctum continue to contest its application. Old debts, peasant flight, guild privileges and the expense of frontier defence shape government alongside doctrine.
