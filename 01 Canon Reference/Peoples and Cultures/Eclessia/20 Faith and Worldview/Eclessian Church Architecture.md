# Eclessian Church Architecture

Sacred buildings in Eclessia record political history in stone. Their plans distinguish old-Church sacramental worship from Freeflame congregational practice, while plague additions and confiscated properties reveal the violence between them.

## Old-Church Churches

An old-Church parish normally has a public nave, a screened or raised sanctuary, side chapels, a font near the entrance, and a sacristy containing registers and licensed relics. Cathedrals add chapter houses, relic courts, schools, guest cloisters, and processional space. Stained glass and painted cycles teach scripture, saints' lives, and local history.

The altar is not merely a speaking platform. It is the focus of sacramental action and therefore the building's legal as well as sacred center. Ownership of an altar can determine burial rights, market privileges, and which archive validates a marriage.

## Plague Architecture

The Grey Sweat added exterior pulpits, wash courts, ventilation towers, charnel galleries, and detached plague chapels. Hospitals grew beside monasteries and city gates. Walls of names compensate for missing parish books; ossuaries often contain sealed rooms whose dead were never individually identified.

Bruckmoor changed relic security. Post-1243 shrines use witness grilles, separated offering chests, inspection doors, and court seals intended to prevent an apparition or custodian from controlling both evidence and income.

## Freeflame Meetinghouses

Freeflame congregations place scripture, preaching, and communal song at the center. Meetinghouses use broad halls, visible reading desks, plain kindling lamps, good acoustics, and attached schools or print rooms. Burial grounds favor named stones and communal memorial walls over relic chapels.

Some meetinghouses are converted churches. Their removed screens, whitewashed images, and emptied shrines remain political wounds. Others retain tested art and architecture, especially where guild congregations reject icon-breaking as another form of false certainty.

## Confiscated and Shared Buildings

The Reform Wars turned abbeys into hospitals, guild halls, arsenals, schools, noble estates, and ruins. Under the Accord, a building follows territorial settlement, not the private confession of its former clergy. Korrheim and border towns sometimes divide time, entrances, or chapels between rites; every such arrangement produces disputes over bells, burial soil, and who holds the keys.

## Regional Forms

- Aurelia builds monumental basilicas, court complexes, and pilgrimage hospitals.
- Veridancia joins cathedrals to universities, seminaries, theaters, and print districts.
- Caldria favors fortified churches and order compounds.
- Virelia preserves icon corners, standing-vigil space, and timber traditions beneath western chancels.
- Ironvale converts industrial halls and guild courts into Freeflame civic worship.
- Brauvalis joins abbeys to infirmaries, breweries, and guest houses.
- The Marches use defensible stone chapels, timber meetinghouses, and shrines raised over older Folk boundaries.
