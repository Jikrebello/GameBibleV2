# Eclessian Church Overview

The Eclessian Church is the western institutional inheritance of the Redeemer's faith: sacramental, juridical, relic-bearing, and governed from the Sanctum Divinitatis in Aurelium. Its priests are celibate, its bishops hold territorial sees, and its Lord Primarch claims the first teaching seat and custody of the Crown of Embers.

The Church is not spiritually empty. Its sacraments work, its saints can be tested, and many of its hospitals, schools, monasteries, and parishes preserve real goods. Its gravest error is the recurring belief that stewardship of grace permits ownership of it.

## Authority and Offices

- **Lord Primarch Alistair III** governs from Aurelium through the Primarchal chancery and the High Council.
- **Bishops** supervise dioceses, ordain priests, judge clerical discipline, and negotiate constantly with territorial rulers after the [[The Korrheim Accord|Korrheim Accord]].
- **Relic courts** test claims of sainthood, apparitions, and sanctified objects. Their modern procedure descends from the Bruckmoor scandal during [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|The Grey Sweat]].
- **Parish priests** keep the registers by which Eclessians prove birth, marriage, burial, legitimacy, and title.
- **Monastic houses** maintain worship, hospitals, libraries, estates, and specialist crafts. Their property and exemptions remain central confessional disputes.
- **The Order of the Lantern** trains clergy, investigates false sanctity, conducts missions, and supplies the Counter-Reform's quiet intelligence.

## Doctrine

The Church teaches that the Lord's White Flame restores rather than merely empowers; that Isfir the Redeemer opened a true road through death; that saints may intercede without replacing the Lord; and that relics can bear the impress of genuine sanctity. It rejects the Thalmyric claim that imperial suffering and the Eternal Ember grant Veridun an equal or superior universal authority.

The Church also teaches that ordered office protects the faithful from private revelation, false relics, predatory patrons, and rulers who would make doctrine a household possession. The [[Freeflame Reformation]] attacks the point where this defense became monopoly.

## The Assurance Scandal

Intercession and burial rites can aid a soul. The old assurance letters failed their buyers during the Grey Sweat, when priests died faster than the dead could be sent. In 1252 Lord Primarch Gelasin II answered with the **Chancery of Sendings**: endowed Sending Houses sang named rites for named dead, families bought sealed letters promising them, and where the rites were sung the dead lay still. The trade spread on that evidence. Lord Primarch Alessior II, an ascetic whose Bull of the Sufficient Seal (1283) taught that the sealed letter itself placed a soul in the Church's keeping before any rite was sung, removed every limit on the trade and gave sealed letters to the poor by the hundred thousand, so that parishes stopped singing for their own dead. His Cardinal-Treasurer Silvio Quarne turned the trade into an industry to pay the Primarch's election debt: printed letters priced by age, blank letters with the name left open, and Saint Gerlind's face on the seal. In 1285 an apparition traced to the treasurer's office set a riot burning Aurelium's Saint-Leon quarter, whose singers sent the dead for nothing. From about 1288 letters nobody would ever sing were sold to brokers as dead paper. Ansgar of Brenholt, a Sending House chaplain, counted more than ten letters sold for every rite he was given time to sing, and when his notes were read aloud over forty-one drowned miners in 1297 the Weiss country was ready to hear them.

The Council of Veridance (1319–1324), which sat with the reformers' own author, Lucian Ferro, in the hall as its witness, repudiated the Sufficient Seal and abolished assurance sales. That made every unsung letter a debt, so it ordered the Great Arrears sung for every letter sealed and never performed; it codified relic testing, rebuilt seminary education, and chartered as the Order of the Lantern the night-singers of Aurelium's Saint-Leon quarter, whom the Sanctum had hunted for forty years, to walk the haunted parishes singing names out of the old registers. The levy that paid for the Arrears turned the princes of the Weiss against the Sanctum within three years of its first collection. Gray assurance markets survive through euphemism, private chaplaincies, and forged certificates, particularly around Aurelia.

## The Confessional Settlement

Since 1343 AR, the Church no longer governs every Eclessian territory. The Korrheim Accord gave every parish yard to the rite that had kept its dead sent, and every ruler the public rite of his land's yards. No prince chose his confession, and none may change it by decree. The Church retains Aurelia, Veridancia, Caldria, Virelia, Brauvalis, the Sylvan Marches, and Grenzburg in law, though actual practice varies sharply.

The Accord grants no individual liberty. Old-Church households in Ironvale, Freeflame households in Aurelia, mixed marriages, wandering preachers, and unaffiliated radicals survive through local toleration, concealment, or exile.

## Present Divisions

- **Primarchal traditionalists** defend broad clerical privilege and the reunification mandate.
- **Veridancian reformers** accept the Council's discipline and seek institutional renewal without abandoning hierarchy.
- **Relic rigorists** fear that reform has weakened necessary defenses against real supernatural fraud.
- **Temporal clerics** use office, land, and law as instruments of family power.
- **Parish clergy** often keep communities alive while their superiors argue over jurisdiction.
