# The Confederation of Korrheim Overview

Korrheim is a central Markish confederation of mountain cantons, free towns, fortified valleys, engineering guilds, and hired companies. The Council of Heldenheim governs common defense and treaty obligations while each canton guards its confession, courts, and militia.

## Historical Formation

Korrheim's valleys belonged nominally to Gervin's inheritance but resisted durable princely rule. Mutual defense, toll compacts, and siege obligations hardened into a confederation. Its soldiers became valuable abroad because canton discipline survived where noble levies did not.

## The Grey Sweat

Communal burial, shared granaries, and valley quarantine strengthened civic oaths. Abandoned estates passed more often to communes and guilds than to magnates. The Siege Engineers' Uprising of 1330 grew from the descendants of plague laborers resisting hereditary council monopolies.

## Reformation and Accord

Some cantons accepted Freeflame preaching; others remained old Church. Repeated attempts to impose a uniform rite nearly broke the confederation. Korrheim hosted the 1343 settlement because neither confession could dismiss its soldiers, roads, or internal example.

The Accord's rule applies canton by canton. Heldenheim seats old-Church and Freeflame delegates together but does not guarantee private conscience within either jurisdiction.

## Government and Faith

The Council of Heldenheim handles foreign policy, common defense, inter-canton roads, and treaty enforcement. Cantons send delegates under binding instructions. Joint civic oaths avoid disputed theology while maintaining public peace.

## Economy and Society

Engineering, transit tolls, livestock, metalwork, protected credit, and military contracts support Korrheim. Service abroad finances farms and political advancement but also imports foreign feuds. Guild citizenship and landholding determine voice more often than abstract equality.

## Warfare

Korrheim fields pike formations, crossbow companies, engineers, mountain riders, and contract captains. Fortresses control passes rather than merely decorate borders. Service articles regulate payment, confession, burial, and prohibited employers.

## Present Tensions

- Whether confessional peace can survive a canton succession or manipulated riot.
- Rich captain-families converting military fame into oligarchy.
- Engineers and smaller communes demanding broader representation.
- Foreign rulers testing the Accord through deniable contracts.
