# The Ironvale Dominion Overview

Ironvale is the northern Markish foundry state where the Freeflame Reformation became a constitution. Coal, iron, wage labor, printing, bombards, and guild government distinguish it from the landed realms around it. Grand Minister Aldric Weyn governs through the Council of Iron.

## Historical Formation

Ironvale began as one of Gervin's marcher duchies. Its mines and fortified guild towns grew steadily, but the ducal house remained supreme until the Reform Wars.

## The Grey Sweat

Labor scarcity allowed miners, smiths, and carriers to demand wages and movement. Guild burial funds and neighborhood congregations replaced failed estate and parish structures. The social power that later sustained reform was forged during plague reconstruction.

## Reformation and Accord

The Reformation began in Ironvale's pits. In 1290 the Blackwater at Brenholt drowned forty-one miners and the Sending House's chaplain, Ansgar, and the widows buried them by reading his margins aloud. The synod that tried his book in 1299 sat in Duke Ortwin's bridge-town of Aldersbruck. From 1306 the Ashenmen held the duchy's pits and starved its forges, and Ortwin's son Rainald fought them for twenty years as an old-Church duke. In 1329 the Council of Veridance's auditors brought him a levy for the Great Arrears and a roll showing that his own father had never been sung. Rainald seized the chests of every Sending House in his duchy and burned their sealed letters in Brenholt market square; the Ashenmen bands of the upper Weiss held their oath fulfilled and came up out of the pits into his service. He governed by Doctor Lucian Ferro's *Order of the Open Flame*, and Ferro himself refused to come to Ironvale. The princes' war that followed exhausted his house in blood, debt, and legitimacy. His last son died at Falkendorf in 1340, and Rainald died without an heir in 1341. The guild ministries assumed government and dated the Grand Ministry from that transfer. That winter the new Ministry banked the furnaces and sold iron to neither confession until the princes would talk, and the Iron Fast is the Dominion's proudest claim on the peace of 1343.

The Schedule of Yards lists every churchyard in Ironvale as Freeflame, so the Accord recognizes the whole Dominion as Freeflame territory. Old-Church households may remain only under local toleration, have no general right to public worship, and carry their dead across the border for burial. The river congregations of the upper Weiss, who keep no yard, have no standing under the Accord or with the Ministry's church.

## Government and Faith

The Council of Iron represents the principal guilds, foundry districts, chartered congregations, and military offices. Weyn is elected from within that structure rather than crowned. Tested elders share civic authority but do not form a separate priestly estate.

Freeflame discipline supports literacy, mutual aid, and public accounting. It can also become moral surveillance, with employment and reputation tied to congregational judgment.

## Economy and Society

Coal, iron, tools, plate, artillery, scripts, and pamphlets drive the Dominion. Workers are free in law and exposed to injury, debt, and employer power in fact. Medori credit reaches the Council through equipment finance and export contracts.

Three permanent Shell works amplify that industry: **Furnace Crown** supplies forge-charge, **Blackwall Compression Yard** produces weight-seed, and **Thunder Draw** recovers stormglass. Ironvale possesses less theoretical breadth than Vorthara but excels at converting a narrow anchor base into standardized guns, armor, fortifications, and [[Ironbound Frames]]. Access is a state and guild secret; their products are visible throughout the Dominion.

## Warfare

The Blackened Guard protects the state; the Ironclad Siege Corps operates bombards and warlock-assisted engines; Smelterborn Brigades combine disciplined infantry with industrial logistics. Ironvale sells arms to governments whose theology it condemns.

## Present Tensions

- The Greystone labor conflict tests whether guild citizenship protects workers or only masters.
- Black powder and Shell scripts threaten old military and religious limits.
- Old ducal loyalists, displaced Church interests, and foreign financiers seek leverage.
- Reformist certainty risks reproducing the coercion it overthrew.
