# The Duchy of Veridancia Overview

Veridancia is a south-central Latinate duchy of vineyard valleys, court cities, universities, printers, and ecclesiastical argument. Under Duke Adrien III, it is the old Church's intellectual reform center: loyal to the Sanctum, suspicious of Primarchal corruption, and unwilling to surrender hierarchy to the Freeflame.

## Historical Formation

Veridancia's Atherian towns survived the Fall through episcopal archives, merchant councils, and landed courts. It entered the middle inheritance at Drossfeld and consolidated as a duchy while preserving unusually strong municipal and scholarly institutions.

The Veridancian Succession War of 1278-1282 ended with a strengthened Council of Verdissia and formal roles for lay jurists in ducal government.

## The Grey Sweat

The plague emptied vineyard villages and shattered parish titles. Surviving workers won wages and movement rights. Universities trained lay notaries to reconstruct inheritance, while hospitals and charitable houses accumulated land left without clear heirs.

## Reformation and Accord

Veridancian scholars circulated Ansgar's arguments even while condemning revolt. The Council of Veridance, 1337-1352, abolished assurances, codified relic tests, rebuilt seminaries, and founded the Order of the Lantern. The Accord fixes the duchy as old-Church territory but cannot prevent its presses from supplying every side.

## Government and Faith

Adrien III rules with the Council of Verdissia, court nobility, municipalities, bishops, universities, and merchant syndics. Lantern seminaries teach Freeflame texts accurately so clergy can answer them. A small but steady number of students convert through the very curriculum designed to resist conversion.

## Society and Economy

Wine, silk, books, trained administrators, sacred drama, and legal education sustain the duchy. Court patronage can elevate talent, but access remains brokered through family, examination, and sponsorship. Smuggling thrives because every censor's index is also a catalog of valuable books.

## Warfare

Veridancia hires troops and contributes trained officers rather than maintaining Caldria's military society. Fortified river towns and diplomatic marriages are its preferred defenses. During the Reform Wars, its jurists were as important as its soldiers.

## Court Annals

**Rene of Montreval**, called the Vine King, consolidated the great vineyard syndicates before the succession crisis. **Eleanor Desrosiers**, the Silk Duchess, turned merchant patronage into recognized court power. **Sir Alaric de Calvienne** commanded the ducal coalition that ended the succession war and then accepted civilian oversight.

- **War of the Vine Lords, 1279-1281 AR:** the commercial theater of the Veridancian Succession War; rival houses burned presses, seized cellars, and attempted to control vineyard title.
- **Smuggler's Gambit, 1320 AR:** book-runners used wine routes to break a Reform War censorship cordon and exposed the complicity of ducal customs officers.
- **Merchant Revolt of Verdissia, 1330 AR:** guilds forced representation on tax and printing councils, creating the civic machinery later used by the Council of Veridance.

## Present Tensions

- Whether honest scholarship can remain inside an institution that fears its conclusions.
- Whether Lantern intelligence serves reform or merely more competent control.
- Rivalry between noble courts, municipal law, and university privilege.
- The continuing wine-and-ale tariff struggle with Brauvalis.

## Story Uses

Book-running, university disputation, relic forensics, court scandals, false examination places, inheritance trials, and missions whose investigators begin to agree with the people they examine.
