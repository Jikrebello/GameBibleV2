# Legends, Ruins, and Conflicts

## The Ruin Register

Marcher tradition once attributed every ruin to one forgotten civilization. The surviving evidence distinguishes several layers:

- **Valtorheim:** an Atherian archive-settlement abandoned during late imperial retreat.
- **Black Gate of Dornwald:** a sealed warlock-script installation whose purpose is still disputed.
- **Weeping Stones of Uldrekh:** a Folk memorial landscape altered by later Weeping Court activity.
- **Temple of the Rooted God:** a Menhir pact-site renamed by hostile Eclessian chroniclers.
- **Maw of Unmaking:** a confirmed Left Hand breach, not a natural cave.
- **Drowned Keep of Kael'Ryth:** a fortress destroyed after its garrison attempted to bind a local soul-route.

The Sunken Spires are the flooded upper works of Valtorheim rather than a separate lost empire.

## Wardens and Keepers

The **Wardens of the Deepwood** are ranger companies chartered by Marchburg but dependent on Folk guides and village support. Eldric "Ghost of the Glen" is remembered for evacuation and concealment, not for defeating an entire people alone. Sister Ysolde hunted predatory relics during the Reform Wars. Garrik the Hollow survived Kael'Ryth and returned with memories no witness can verify.

The **Hollowborn** are secretive human keeper-families descended from refugees assigned to watch the ruin seals. They are unrelated to Pale-Skins or Hollow-Kings. Their initiation trials test memory, route knowledge, and resistance to ruin influence.

## Preserved Conflicts

- **Battle of Raven's Hollow, 1240 AR:** plague flight, settler seizure, and a broken Folk sanctuary pact produced five days of battle. Neither side's heroic account includes the displaced villagers who began it.
- **Bloodmoon Massacre, 1315 AR:** Baron Viktor Dreymann's troops destroyed a surrounded Folk warband and noncombatant camp during the Reform Wars. Retaliation continues around its anniversary.
- **Ghostwood Raids:** an ongoing label covering different Folk reprisals, criminal bands, and staged attacks by land speculators.

## Frontier Rule

Folk communities have real land claims and internal laws. They are not morally interchangeable with predatory raiders, and their sovereignty does not make every act of vengeance just. Marcher settlers can be lawful descendants of plague refugees while still living on land acquired through atrocity.
