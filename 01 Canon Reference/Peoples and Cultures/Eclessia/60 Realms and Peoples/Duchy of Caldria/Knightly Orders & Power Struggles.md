# Knightly Orders & Power Struggles

## Chartered Orders

**Order of the Silver Cross** holds old Third Crusade fortresses and considers itself the duchy's doctrinal shield. It resists secular inspection of its relic armories.

**Brotherhood of Stagfort** guards forest roads and southern approaches. Its practical cooperation with Folk guides makes it suspect to court traditionalists.

**Iron Banner Knights** provide heavy cavalry to the Grand Duke. Their landed families fear artillery and military rents will make their social order obsolete.

**Lance of Dornheim** trains scouts, messengers, and mobile frontier companies. It contains the highest proportion of tolerated Freeflame soldiers.

These are Caldrian orders and do not replace the Eclessia-wide institutions in [[Eclessian Military Orders]].

## Preserved Campaigns

- **Great Pagan War, 1210-1216 AR:** a Caldrian name for coordinated campaigns against frontier shrine leagues. Its destruction of neutral sanctuaries made later Folk alliances harder.
- **Schism Wars, 1310-1324 AR:** the local Reform War theater. Old Church chronicles preserve the older name, but it was not a second Great Split.
- **War of the Broken Lance, 1335 AR:** officers of the Lance of Dornheim refused an order to purge Freeflame soldiers. The resulting mutiny ended with mixed-confession service articles and Alric IV's rise as mediator.

## Legendary Soldiers

Sir Reinhardt von Varendor, "the Bastion," is remembered for holding Caldros Gate during the Schism Wars. Dame Isolde Rensfeld developed the mobile reserve doctrine Alric now favors. Sir Wilhelm the Black remains disputed: hero to Iron Banner houses, pillager to the villages through which he supplied himself.

## Present Power Struggle

Alric IV wants a unified ducal command, licensed warlock corps, and artillery independent of order estates. The Silver Cross defends sacred privilege; the Iron Banner defends mounted nobility; Stagfort insists local alliances cannot be directed from court; Dornheim wants confessional service protected.
