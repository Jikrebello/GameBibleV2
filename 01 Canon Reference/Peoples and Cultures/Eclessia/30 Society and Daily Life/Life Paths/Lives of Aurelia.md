# Lives of Aurelia

The Primarchal State: the only place in the world where the Church is also the landlord, the government, the largest employer, and the town gossip. Living here is living *inside* the institution everyone else argues about.

## The Farmer of the Church-Estates

Born a tenant of a chapter, abbey, or curial office — and therefore the best-documented farmer alive: the tithe-barn's ledgers run back centuries, which cuts both ways. His obligations are enforced to the letter; so are his *rights* — commons, gleaning, fixed entry-fines — because the Church's own courts must be seen honoring its own parchment. He pays more reliably and starves more rarely than a lay lord's man. The proverb: *"A hard lord, the crozier — but it cannot die and it cannot gamble."* His decisions are the western farmer's usual (marriage, the vineyard risk, the pilgrimage year) plus one local one: whether a clever child goes to the **choir school**, the estate's one golden door.

## The Curial Clerk (the city's defining road)

Half of Aurelium works for the Sanctum in some capacity; the clerk is its citizen-archetype. Two ladders climb the same wall: the **tonsured** (minor orders, benefice-fed, celibate on paper) and the **lay notaries** from the university — rivals for the same desks in the Registry of Causes, the Datary of Benefices, and the relic courts' evidence rooms. The defining decisions: which cardinal's household to attach to (patronage is oxygen); whether to touch **assurance-adjacent work** (abolished at Veridance, alive in the gray market, career-making and soul-staining); and what to do the day a file crosses the desk that the office above wants lost. The city's saying: *"In Aurelium even the ink takes sides."*

## The Pilgrimage Trades

The holy city's second economy: inn-keepers who sleep forty strangers a night in Highsun season, badge-casters and candle-molders, licensed guides ("this very stone, good pilgrims—"), professional witnesses for relic authentications, and the letter-writers at the Sanctum gates who put the whole continent's petitions into curial Latinate for a copper. Honest work, mostly; the Weeping Courts' Postulants recruit at its desperate edges, and everyone knows a guide who vanished after selling visions he shouldn't have.

## The Priest and the Bishop, at the Summit

An Aurelian parish is a career posting, not a life — the ambitious rotate through on their way up, and the parishioners, connoisseurs of clergy the way Sermorans are of sailors, grade them accordingly. Above, the bishop's road is at its most political: every see in the Primarchal State is a curial appointment, every appointment a faction's move in the standing war between Vrenne's traditionalists, the Primarch's reformists, and the Phoenix Grandmaster's sword-party. The one universally respected clerical road: the **Lantern teachers**, who answer only to Alistair and are therefore trusted by people who trust nothing else here.

## The Criminal

Aurelium's underworld is *paperwork with teeth*: relic-fraud ateliers (bone-carvers with saints' price-lists), the gray assurance market (Veridance abolished the trade; grief did not abolish the demand), pilgrimage-season cutpurse guilds with seasonal migration patterns like birds, and the counterfeit-dispensation trade the Inquisition actually prioritizes. Violent crime is rare and theatrically punished; the real career criminals here have excellent handwriting.

## The Strange-Dose

Highest ambient in Eclessia: Aurelium sees authenticated miracles the way capitals see royalty — occasionally, at a distance, with queues. The city's people are therefore the world's most *discerning* faithful: they believe absolutely and are impressed by nothing, and the phrase "an Aurelium shrug" means exactly that everywhere else.

## Hooks

The vault under the Sanctum (Unburnt Psalter, Vessing file, Crown of Embers) as the world's most consequential room; a Registry clerk who has found an uncatalogued entry-shadow with no named owner; the choir-school child whose voice makes hymn-lamps behave.
