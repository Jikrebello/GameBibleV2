# Lives of Virelia

The hard file. East of the Orzva watershed the Grey Sweat's aftermath ran backward: where the west freed its farmers, the sabre-right nobility bound theirs — and the binding tightens every generation as the grain barges go north. Virelia is the granary of Eclessia, and the granary has a price nobody upriver asks about.

## The Serf (most Virelian lives)

**Born** on the estate's roll — literally: the demesne estate ledger records the birth before the parish register does, and which book matters more is the whole story of his life. Childhood is field work by six; letters almost never (the sabre-right nobility closed the winter schools two generations back — literate serfs write petitions). **The decisions — and their absence:** he cannot leave the land, marry off the estate, or take a town apprenticeship without the lord's letter; what remains is the interior economy of dignity — the icon corner kept bright, the standing vigils sung whole (the eastern-hearted piety the western priests pretend is local custom), the garden plot worked by moonlight, and the one enormous decision that defines the road: **stay or run.** Flight is the east's open secret: south to the Sylvan Marches, further south to Grenzburg's Funnel (autumn's tide of "seasonal laborers" includes every year's crop of the vanished), or east to the river mouths where the **Unwritten** take anyone. Caught runaways are branded on the second offense; the third offense is not a branding. Every serf family has a name they don't say and a letter they can't read from somewhere free.

**Death:** in the parish yard — the one ground the lord doesn't own — which is why eastern funerals are *loud*: it is the single ceremony where a bound family's full worth can be publicly spent.

## The Sabre-Right Noble

**Born** into the noble nation — perhaps one Virelian in twelve carries the sabre-right, from magnate palaces to noble families plowing their own fields with a sword on the wall ("barefoot sabre-right nobility," poor as their serfs and legally worlds above them). The identity is intoxicating: legal equality of all nobles, the *district noble moot* moot-democracy, election fever every royal death, golden-liberty rhetoric — a republic of lords sitting on a nation of the bound, and genuinely unable to see the contradiction the west's pamphlets keep pointing at. **The forks:** magnate clientage (whose ante-chamber to stand in); the hussar road (the winged lancers, the class's glory and its cavalry-poor-cousin economics: a season's harvest for the armor); the election bet (which candidate, whose gold, the whispered "eastern candidate" the Sanctum always kills); and the marriage market where grain acreage weds pedigree.

## The Hussar

The soldier's road in its eastern key: recruited only from the sabre-right, horse and wings self-funded, glory real — the charge that broke the steppe raiders at the fords is sung in three languages — and the account books grim: most hussars are mortgaged to a magnate or the Medori by their second campaign. The fork every winged rider faces by forty: the magnate's household (retainer-captain, comfortable, owned) or the small estate and the slow decline into sabre-on-the-wall gentility.

## The Priest of the East

A western hierarchy over an eastern flock (the Choosing's long tail): the priest is Sanctum-formed and Latinate-rited, his parish keeps standing vigils, icon corners, and feast-dates the calendar quarrel officially settled centuries ago. The wise priest learns which battles are doctrine and which are his own grandmother; the unwise one writes to his bishop, who writes to Veridancia, and the village goes quiet for a decade. Freeflame preachers do poorly here — not for the sabre-right nobility's policing but because the serfs already have a hidden church; they need another one like a second lord.

## The Criminal — and the Unwritten

Estate theft is discipline, not law; real crime lives on the rivers (grain-barge piracy — the Orzva's floating toll) and at the map's edge, where the **Unwritten** ride: runaway-serf brotherhoods gone semi-nomadic on the steppe fringe, part bandit, part border militia, electing elected camp captains, raiding Tarkhan and sabre-right nobility with fine impartiality, and appearing on no register anywhere — which to men erased from ledgers at birth is not an inconvenience but the entire point. The magnates hire them, hunt them, and pretend both less than they do. The Ash Horde knows their camps. So, lately, do Freeflame riders, who find "the Fire keeps no landlord" preaches *extremely* well out there.

## The Strange-Dose

Higher than the west's: steppe-edge thin places, the Tarkhan wind-omens crossing the border with the horse-trades, and the eastern piety's older furniture — a Virelian serf's grandmother knows charms the Inquisition would call by other names, and the priest has decided not to hear them.
