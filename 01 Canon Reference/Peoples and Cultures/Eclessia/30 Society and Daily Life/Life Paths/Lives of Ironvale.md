# Lives of Ironvale

The different world: no duke, no serfs, no north yard — a guild-state running on wages, coal, and the Freeflame, where the Reformation isn't a controversy but the constitution. Everyone else's roads bend here; some invert.

## The Miner and the Foundryman (the Dominion's farmer-equivalent)

**Born** in a company row-house under the tips; presented to the congregation (no font politics — one public confession here) and to the **tally-office** within the same week, because the second registration is the one that feeds him. Boys go down or go to the frames at ten; girls to the sorting belts and the wash-houses. **The decisions:** which seam (coal pays steady and kills slow; iron pays better and kills fast; the deep silver kills strangely — Highlake's cousins know); whether to take the **Syndicate oath** (the Miners' Syndicate is chapel, insurer, and shadow-parliament in one, and its dues are the best money a pit family spends); and the ladder-fork at twenty: stay hewing, or the **Brigade** (the Smelterborn offer militia pay on top of pit pay, and the knowledge that the last riot they suppressed was their own street's). Black lung is the road's toll; the Syndicate's widow-and-orphan fund is the road's dignity; and the oath sworn "by the Knot" (of Menos — the relic the Council of Iron keeps from both churches) is the road's whole theology in three words.

## The Guild Apprentice

The real ladder: seven years indentured to the Forgekeepers, the pattern-shops, or the new bombard-works. In the Red Anvil yards, stolen Jirahar powder knowledge enters one bribed formula at a time. Journeyman years traveling the Markish towns; the masterpiece; the vote. A master's daughter is the Dominion's best marriage and knows it. The fork that defines the age: **frames versus knights** — the pro-mechanization yards pay double and the traditionalist shops swear the Church will shut it all down someday, and both are right to worry.

## The Freeflame Elder (the priest-road, inverted)

No celibate father here: the congregation **tests and elects** its elders — working men and women examined in doctrine and life by neighbors who have known them forty years, unpaid beyond honor, serving beside their trade. Menos is patron; Ansgar's Grievances are read aloud each year on the burning's anniversary; the dead are buried in one equal yard with psalms and no lamp — *the Fire needs no wick of ours* — which visiting old-Church relatives find either heretical or, at certain gravesides, quietly enviable. The elder's hard forks: discipline cases (the congregation judges its own, and small towns make that mercy or that cruelty); and the standing vigilance the Eclipsis have taught them without their knowing why — *jealousy in the doctrine* is the tell they test for, per principles they'd be alarmed to find in an Inquisition manual.

## The Grand Ministry Clerk and the Overseer-Noble

The bureaucrat's road runs through the **Council of Iron's** tally-offices: production ledgers, safety rolls revised after Greystone in blood, and the Weyn ministry's contract desks. The noble's road survives as a rump: Houses Blackridge and Greystone serve as salaried marshals and mine-lords under charter — aristocrats on wages, proud, resentful, and one bad grievance away from inviting a bishop back in. **There is no bishop:** the see of Ironvale has sat vacant since the Wars of Reform, its claimant "in exile" at Veridancia issuing pastoral letters no one here reads — the Dominion's favorite running joke and the Sanctum's least favorite precedent.

## The Criminal

Organized crime does poorly (the Syndicate polices its own; the guilds audit; the Under-Ledger's two attempts on the tally-offices ended with cell-names posted on chapel doors, which is Ironvale for excommunication). What thrives: formula-theft (powder secrets both directions), tip-scavenging gangs, wage-paper forgery, and the smuggling of *ideas* — this is where the banned pamphlets get printed, and the pressmen's night-runs are half the east's underground library.

## The Strange-Dose

Low and industrial: Shell-scar seams the deep miners map and avoid, hymn-lamps in the pit-head chapels that the whole shift watches like a canary (they gutter before firedamp — no one knows why and no one goes down when they do), and the Knot itself — touched once, at oath-taking, by every miner in the Dominion, and warmer than iron should be.

## Hooks

A deep gallery breaks into worked stone nobody dug; the exiled bishop announces a pastoral *visit*; a Red Anvil formula surfaces in a Covenant siege-train, and the trail runs through a chapel deacon.
