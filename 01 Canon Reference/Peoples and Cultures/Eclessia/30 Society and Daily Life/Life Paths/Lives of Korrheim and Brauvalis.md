# Lives of Korrheim and Brauvalis

The central Markish pair: the cantons that hire out their sons and the duchy that brews its politics. Between them runs the Accord's main seam — which means this is also where the Inquisition is *active* rather than ambient, and everyone's manners show it.

## Korrheim: the Canton Citizen

**Born** into a commune, not under a lord: the canton rolls are the birthright, and the yearly **muster** is its price — every male citizen shoots the crossbow court at sixteen and marches the drill-meadow till fifty. Citizenship is jealously narrow (the resident non-citizen *Zugewandte* class does the scut work and resents it by charter). **The decisions:** trade or pike — the canton's two exports are craftwork and *war* — and the pike-fork is the famous one: the **canton companies** (canon: mercenaries serving everyone) recruit at every muster, offering triple wages, plunder-shares, and the statistically excellent odds nobody's mother believes. A Korrheim man who serves two contracts comes home propertied or not at all; the cantons' quiet arithmetic is that the companies export exactly the young men whose land-hunger would otherwise break the communes. **The shame nobody says at the Heldenheim council:** canton pikes have faced canton pikes on foreign fields, paid by opposing churches — post-Falkendorf (canon) there is a standing rule against taking both sides of one war, honored the way rules like that are.

**Confession:** by canton, per the Accord — the council chamber at Heldenheim seats old-Church and Freeflame delegates at one table with the practiced blandness of men whose relatives killed each other within living memory. The joint civic feasts are triumphs of scheduling.

## Brauvalis: the Burgher and the Brewer-Monk

The duchy runs on beer the way Ironvale runs on coal: monastic breweries (canon) anchor the trade, guild maltsters and coppersmiths fatten on it, and the **Grand Tapping** (canon) is the civic year's crown. **The burgher's road:** guild apprenticeship, the widow-remarriage economy (a master's widow holds the license — Brauvalis marriages are brutally practical), the town council ladder, and the standing three-way tug between duke, Church, and guilds that the Tax Revolt of Dornfeld (canon) settled in the guilds' favor five years ago and everyone expects to reopen. **The brewer-monk's road:** the abbeys take second sons and clever poor boys into a life that is genuinely prayer *and* genuinely industry — cellarer is a career, the abbey's beer pays for its charity, and the memory of Duke Reinhardt's excommunicated tax collectors (canon) makes every abbot a constitutional lawyer. The reformed critique — *monks brewing fortunes behind walls while widows tithe* — lands hard here precisely because the abbeys also run the hospitals, and both facts are true.

**The seam:** Brauvalis is "one succession from flipping" (canon), and daily life prices it in: mixed-confession marriages are contracts with *escape clauses*; the cathedral and the meeting-house watch each other's attendance; and the duke's health is the market's favorite commodity. The Inquisition keeps a standing office here — the black wagon is *seen* in Brauvalis, not merely feared — and the Red Tally keeps its ripening Brauvalis succession account (per the Left Hand), which no one in the duchy knows and everyone in the duchy feeds.

## Shared Roads

**The farmer:** free tenantry at its most comfortable — hop-gardens, barley, dairy; the Sweat's labor-price legacy at full strength. **The priest/elder pair:** the seam's clergy are the Accord's daily mechanics, negotiating funerals for mixed families and market-day truces; the good ones are the region's real diplomats. **The criminal:** salt- and toll-smuggling on the canton roads, beer-fraud (a hanging matter in Brauvalis, half-seriously), and the seam's dark trade — *people*: heresy-flight in both directions, moved by guides who take either coin.

## The Strange-Dose

Modest: a wonder-crucifix here, a hymn-lamp there, and the seam's specialty — **competing miracles**, where both confessions' shrines answer the same drought, and the pamphlet war afterward does more damage than the drought did.

## Hooks

A canton company comes home *converted* (whole, together, mid-contract) and the council must decide what that means; the Grand Tapping barrel runs red — prank, omen, or Weeping Courts theater; the ducal physician is offered three retainers in one week and recognizes none of the hands.
