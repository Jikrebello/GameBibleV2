# Eclessian Daily Life and the Year of the Flame

Commandment 8's second half: how Eclessians actually eat, marry, mourn, sing, and lie to each other. Regional registers follow [[The Making of the Eclessians]]; confessional practice follows the Reformation settlement.

## The Year of the Flame (the twelve liturgical months)

The Church's calendar overlays the old Atherian civic grid: twelve months, a seven-day week ending in the **Lord's Day**, feast days fixed. The months, from the year's start at the Binding-anniversary:

1. **Bindingtide** (the Binding and the three dark days; the year begins in grief)
2. **Risenmonth** (resurrection feasts; eggs, bells, debts traditionally forgiven between neighbors — not, notoriously, by banks)
3. **Emberrise** (Saint Solana's feast: children receive *Solana jars*, a candle in a clay pot; lamps relit from the parish flame)
4. **Greenveil** (spring labor; Rogation-style field processions; the Folk's Bright-Fire falls nearby, which makes borderland priests nervous every single year)
5. **Highsun** (midsummer; Saint Dorn's feast — the roads' saint — when pilgrimage season opens and every crossroads shrine gets flowers)
6. **Harvestmonth** (first fruits; grain tithes; the month of weddings, because the larders can bear a feast)
7. **Aquilantide** (Saint Aquilan's feast, when the *whole* verse of the Tower-Born epistle must be read aloud — root and branch both — a rubric written in black ink after dark centuries)
8. **Wanefall** (autumn slaughter and salting; Saint Myra's feast: foundling processions, alms for mothers)
9. **Souls-month** (the month of the dead: grave-tending, the Rite of the Empty Seat, Ischar's name spoken once and refused; sextons bless the cemetery bounds because those wards materially discourage ghouls)
10. **Emberfast** (the great fast; no meat, dim lamps, catechism season)
11. **Kindlenight** (midwinter: the Vigil of the Last Ember, every hearth relit from the church lamp; gift-small, light-large)
12. **Thawwait** (the mud month; nothing happens in Thawwait, an Eclessian proverb for futility: *"as busy as Thawwait"*)

**Confessional friction:** the Freeflame keep the same year but strip the saints' feasts to "memorials," which in practice means everyone attends the same bonfires and argues about what they mean. The Korrheim Accord's silence on mixed-confession feast days is filled by local custom and annual fistfights.

## Food (by register)

**Markish marches** (Grenzburg country): rye and barley bread, pot-oats, pork and preserved cabbage, river fish, mushroom-hunting as near-religion; beer is food, brewed dark; the autumn slaughter-sausage (*wanewurst*) hangs in every rafter. **Latinate south:** wheat bread, oil, wine, orchard fruit, cheese pride bordering on heraldry; the Sanctum's kitchens set fashions the marches mock and copy. **Vesari east:** buckwheat, beets, smoked lake-fish, honey-spirits, dumplings whose regional variants have started duels. **Southern coasts:** herring in twenty forms, salt-cod, and stockfish Lent-fare the whole coast pretends to enjoy (the mead-wedding, sea-totem culture belongs to the Thalmyric [[The Saltborn Coast Overview|Saltborn]], not here). Fast-days shape all of it: the Church's meatless calendar built Eclessia's fish trade, and the Medori hold shares in Lent.

## Weddings

Banns cried three Lord's Days running; the vows spoken **at the church door**, in public, before the couple enters for the blessing — marriage is the town's business first and heaven's second. The ring-hand differs by confession (old Church left, Freeflame right — a detail spies actually use). Markish weddings feature the *bride-ale* and a mock bride-theft the priests deplore; Vesari weddings need three days and a sworn-witness count. Dowry, jointure, and the widow's third are law everywhere and litigation everywhere.

## Funerals (the serious business)

Because soul-routing is real, Eclessian death-custom is metaphysical infrastructure: confession if death gives warning; the **viaticum lamp** lit at the deathbed to "show the road"; burial in consecrated ground within three days; the sexton's watch against ghoul-fray; and the month's mind mass. Suicides, the excommunicate, and the unshriven go in the north yard — a cruelty reformers attack and tradition defends with ghoul-statistics. Mourners eat **soul-cakes**; children are told the Boatman takes no coin, *"only truth,"* which is why Eclessian corpses carry no obols and Eclessian folktales are full of people who tried anyway.

**The funeral keen (Markish, sung by the women at the graveside):**
> *Low the lamp and long the road, / truth the only toll.*
> *What you gave returns to God, / what you kept, the whole.*
> *Walk you well past mere and mire, / heed no voice but One —*
> *till the yolks are set in order / and the Weighing's done.*

("Heed no voice but One" is older than the Church admits — it is a warning about the leftward road, and the Folk have the same line in their own keens, which both peoples pretend not to notice.)

## Songs, Proverbs, Jokes

**Drinking song (the marches' standard, endless verses):**
> *The Duke he taxed the barley, / the Bank it taxed the malt,*
> *the Church she taxed the drinking, / and called the headache fault —*
> *so here's to honest thirsty men / who pay the triple fee:*
> *we'll drink the field, the loan, the sin, / and still stand — mostly — free!*

**Proverbs:** *"As busy as Thawwait."* — *"Every relic pays a rent."* (cynical-southern) — *"The Flame keeps no landlord"* (now a dangerous thing to say in the wrong parish, and everyone knows exactly how it lands before they say it) — *"Kneel surprised"* (to profit while feigning innocence; from Adalmar's coronation chronicle, used exactly as cynically as you'd hope).

**The joke the whole east tells (confessions swapped by teller):** A priest, a Freeflame elder, and a Medori factor find one coin in the road. The priest says it must go to the Church, for all things return to God. The elder says it must go to the poor, for the Fire keeps no landlord. The factor says nothing, and the coin is never seen again — *but its interest is.*

## Class, Hypocrisy, and the Texture of Grievance

The tithe-barn stands next to the toll-bridge and the debtor's board, and every peasant can rank the three by hatred (the order varies by season). Guild towns despise field villages and marry into them; cathedral canons preach Emberfast on capon; reformed magistrates ban feast-day dancing and their sons pay the fines personally. The reliable social glue is the feast calendar itself — enemies who won't share doctrine will share a Kindlenight fire, and the year's rhythm does the reconciling that sermons can't.

## In Grenzburg

All of it compressed and sharpened: Wanefall slaughter decides who winters inside the walls; Souls-month grave-watch is a militia duty (the Southwood teaches why); Kindlenight's relit hearths are counted by the Castellan as a census; the bride-ale doubles as the Folk-truce venue; and the drinking song above has a local verse about the Medori that the Bank's factor has heard, priced, and let live.
