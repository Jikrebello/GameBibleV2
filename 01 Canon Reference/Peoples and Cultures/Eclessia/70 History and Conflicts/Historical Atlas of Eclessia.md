# Historical Atlas of Eclessia

Eclessia is a family of eastern successor kingdoms and duchies whose peoples formed from provincial Atherian, Folk, Markish, Vesari and other regional communities. Its common civilisation grew through the Church, local lordship and the memory of Adalmar's crown, while political authority remained divided. The Great Split, the Grey Sweat and the Reformation left the Eclessian world of 1360 with rival rulers, contested rites and a strong shared historical identity.

## Historical Atlas — wider inheritance

Eclessians are a later Braided people formed from provincial Atherian, Folk, Markish, Vesari and other regional layers. Church institutions connected them while warfare, landholding and dynastic loyalty kept their political worlds plural.

During the [[Historical Atlas of the Atherian Age of Glory|Age of Glory]], eastern river cities and mountain corridors belonged among the empire's richest and most politically important provinces. The Veloran and Serrat houses reached imperial rule through eastern commands and municipal alliances. Raveth's famine estates and Arcelon's military road left agricultural and transport inheritances later absorbed into Eclessian regions.

The east's bishops were governing it long before the Fall. In 653 a squall sank the fleet carrying the eastern tax grain, and the court let the bishops keep the revenue at home "for one year", a year nobody ever ended. Bishops who kept the revenue whatever a relic test showed could afford to test honestly, and Aurelium entered the results in its register of answered fire. In 711 Bishop Simeon recorded a Caleran claimant's standard silent and held the eastern governors' oaths "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful". When Caleran fell in 905 the bishops simply went on, keeping a single register of land and oath and the governors' oaths at Aurelium. In 974 a march banner taken up by a miller's widow, Hedwen, kindled at the ford of Kaltfurt as she stood between Grass-Tarkhan raiders and a refugee column, and the marcher lords acclaimed the commander on the bank, Adalmar. He joined the bishops' principalities by war, marriage and the register, and in 1006 the relic court of Lord Primarch Silvane II credited the Kaltfurt banner's kindling to him, though Hedwen testified that it had never burned in his hand. Silvane would not crown a man over a living witness, and the matter stayed open until both of them were dead.

Forced conversion during the Folk Wars (985–994) remains part of the inheritance. Adalmar's registers granted frontier land the clans held by seer-oath. In 993 he called their oath-keepers and seers to Wolfsmere under safe-conduct to recite the grove boundaries for his clerks, and when the recital contradicted his grants he had the four thousand drowned in their own lake. The next winter the lake rose and took his shore-fort. The Folk chief Weylan Oath-Bent, baptized six years before the Drowning, sued the king in the bishops' court for the broken safe-conduct and won the Peace of the Bent Oak (999), which struck the grants beyond the lake from the register. Eclessia's southern frontier has stopped at the lake country ever since.

The Great Split of 1015 began with the Sanctum's demand that Veridun lift the bronze hood from its Great Lamp and let the lamp be examined; the Patriarch nailed the hood shut. Two years later Lord Primarch Bonifac III, who had struck Hedwen's testimony through, crowned Adalmar Emperor of the Atherians at the ford of Kaltfurt, where the millers shut their sluices and left him standing in mud. He was the only emperor the east ever had. His son Ludovar the Mild was recorded silent at every examination and was never crowned; Ludovar's sons and lords bought examined banners from rival bishops, and at Drossfeld in 1041 they agreed to let a lot choose which of them the Sanctum should examine. The lot came up blank, and each kept what he held that day. In 1061 the cold hearth-lamp that the Vesari prince Radomir had sent west for a light from the Ember lit itself in his fire-keeper's hands in Veridun's cathedral; Veridun kept it, and under Vesari hearth-law that made Radomir Veridun's client. He took his fire from the Sanctum's altar instead, and in 1064 the kingless lords of the eastern march chose him king beside the Vesari princes, which made Virelia. In the Two Winters of 1101–1103 King Ermbold of Aurelia refused to carry the unworn Crown of Embers to its examination and sent it to the Primarch in a grain cart, Virelia's crown became elective, and in 1104 the crown was left in the Sanctum's trust "until the realm is worthy of one head".

The High Noon that followed was Eclessia's crowded century. The Primarchs ruled Aurelia directly from 1166. Escaped mine slaves founded Korrheim's first cantons with the Oath of the Shafts in 1124. Marcher lords and the Knights of Saint Aveline waged the Great Southern Purge against Folk seers in 1158–1163 until riders without breath ended it. Around 1180 children in three duchies walked west toward Caleran, and forty never came home. The Sanctum defined the Unkindled Conception in 1183 and the Rising of the Mother in 1204, and in 1217 Caldrian captains took three Thalmyric valleys while Thalmyria fought its civil war. On the eve of the Grey Sweat in 1232 the realms were full of people and short of land.

The Grey Sweat of 1232–1247 killed about a third of eastern Eclessia and left the dead walking where priests had died. In the west survivors bargained for wages; in Virelia the magnates sealed priestless villages and bound their survivors to the demesne in exchange for a priest and a grave. The Sanctum sold sealed letters promising that Sending Houses would sing for the dead, and under Alessior II it sold far more than it sang. Ansgar of Brenholt counted the letters against the rites in 1296 and sang his grievances over drowned miners in 1297; at Aldersbruck in 1305 his psalter would not burn, and the spring flood took him from the bridge-tower where he was held. Miners and farmers swore in the Weiss where he was lost and fought out of their pits for twenty-three years, until Duke Rainald burned the seals; the princes' war that followed ended in 1343, when old-Church and reformed rulers put their hands in the same river and swore the Korrheim Accord. In 1360 Eclessia holds Sanctum realms, Accord-protected reformed duchies and a schism with Veridun three and a half centuries old, and its myths remember friendships broken by institutions and poor households whose lives no settlement restored.
