# Eclessia During the Invasion

Eclessia's cities, dioceses, roads, estates, and peoples were old provincial societies when Caleran fell in 905 AR. The invasion transformed their relationship to empire: a distant eastern inheritance became a self-governing world. The east survived because it had already been governing itself for two and a half centuries, through bishops who kept its revenue, a see at Aurelium that examined relics before it trusted them, and charters the Covenant's courts could not void.

## Before the Fall

Immunity sales after 398 made the great eastern monasteries landlords, and in the Thin Floods of 546–552 their bishops handed out grain where governors had none. In 653 a Thalassian squall sank the fleet carrying the eastern tax grain to Caleran. The court could not replace it and let the eastern bishops collect and spend the revenue at home "for one year". The year was never ended.

That accident made honesty cheap. Caleran had ruled in the 640s that a relic estate kept its tax freedom only while its relic answered a test, and in the capital the tests were bought. In the east the bishops kept the revenue whichever way a test fell, so they tested honestly. Aurelium, a see of readers and copyists since the Underground Centuries, kept the results in its register of answered fire: which relics answered, which never had and which clergy kindled. Bishops began to promote from it.

In 700, during the War of the Two Oaths, the claimant in Caleran sent the Velos Standard east to rally the provinces. Bishop Oswin of Aurelium set it up in his cathedral, examined it before the people for three days and recorded it silent. He refused the summons and held the eastern governors' oaths "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful". In 701 Corvenas I confirmed Aurelium's right to examine relics presented in the east for imperial purposes, and Eclessians date the Examiner's primacy from that grant. Through the eighth and ninth centuries bishops ran the east's grain, walls, courts and register. Markish lords held the Tarkhan and Folk frontiers and took their oaths from bishops in trust, and Ironvale's forges worked for the east. In 874 Aurelium refused to join the Reception unless it could examine the Standard again, and it stayed home.

## The Fall Reaches the East, 905 AR

The Qahirid resurgence struck the imperial heart and northern approaches before turning toward the eastern provinces. News arrived through refugees, broken tax convoys, and contradictory Church letters. No examined summons came from Caleran in 903–905, and the east did not march; its revenue and soldiers were its own. Some governors waited for orders that would never come; others converted imperial authority into hereditary rule.

Caleran's loss severed military pay, grain schedules, episcopal appeals, and the legal archive on which provincial titles depended. Refugees brought word that on the last day Corvenas III had carried the Velos Standard onto the river dyke and called on the Fire before both armies, and that nothing came; the Standard Aurelium had recorded silent in 700 had failed in public a third time. A copy of the Instrument of the Ash Hall, by which he surrendered himself and his relics, reached Aurelium within the year. Eastern preachers read it aloud for a generation as the end of a court that had never let its relics be examined.

## The Stand at Crown Gate, 906-908 AR

The eastern charters had been re-sealed before witnesses Aurelium had tested, and the Covenant's courts could not void them, so the Qahirid army had to fight its way east. The advance was stopped at Caldria by provincial troops, refugee formations, knightly confraternities, local levies, and controversial Vortharan specialists. At [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)]], Caldrian forces held Crown Gate Pass while Vortharan counter-scripts destroyed one Depth-Core and one Slave-Soul Covenant golem and crippled a second Depth-Core. Later Eclessian chronicles call the entire defence the Stand at Crown Gate; contemporary records describe a desperate coalition with no single commander.

Caldria's fortress culture and the political authority of its orders date from this defence. The halt protected the eastern heartland but did not recover the empire.

## Local Government

Bishops authenticated records, abbeys stored grain, towns repaired walls, and landholders raised permanent households. Coastal Aratheos and the valley district of Fareth survive in the record as local leagues: Aratheos maintained beacon and storm roads, while Fareth organised vineyard militias and refugee farms.

Their relic brands, militia oaths, and saint banners were absorbed by successor realms.

## Refuge and Estrangement

Refugees carried Caleran's manuscripts, relics, trades, and competing accounts of failure east. Others fled south and helped create the Thalmyric restoration. The two refugee streams remembered the same disaster differently: Eclessia learned that institutions must survive without the capital, and that unexamined wonders hide fraud; Thalmyria learned that the empire must be restored around the Eternal Ember, and that the Fire comes to the refuge and not to the palace.

The difference became political before it became schism.

## Toward Adalmar

Between 908 and 961 AR, emergency lordships hardened into principalities, and none could claim to be the whole empire. Grass-Tarkhan riders on the scattered imperial horse raided their roads. In 960 a march banner taken up by the miller's widow Hedwen kindled at Kaltfurt while she shielded a refugee column from one such raid, and the commander watching from the far bank, Adalmar, was acclaimed king of the eastern marches the next year. He conquered and married the principalities into a crown. When a Primarch crowned him emperor in 1017, standing in the ford where Hedwen had stood and two years after the churches had split, much of the east read it as the old episcopal trust over oaths made visible, and Veridun read it as usurpation.
