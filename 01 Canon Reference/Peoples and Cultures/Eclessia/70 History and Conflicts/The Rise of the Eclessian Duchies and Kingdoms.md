# The Rise of the Eclessian Duchies and Kingdoms

The present Eclessian realms descend from Atherian provinces, post-Fall emergency lordships, Adalmar's united crown, and the truce lines on which his dynasty's wars stopped. Several acquired their modern form centuries after their names first appeared. Their founding stories are full of objects that decided things: a kindled banner at a ford, a lake that rose against a fort, a blank wax tablet drawn from a helmet, a cold hearth-lamp that lit itself and never came home, a crown sent away in a grain cart as "a hat", and a canton oath sworn at a pithead.

## The Bishops' East

When Caleran fell in 905 the eastern provinces were already used to governing themselves. Their bishops had collected and spent the eastern revenue since the seventh century, kept a single register of land, oath and relic, and held the provincial governors' oaths at Aurelium "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful", a formula dating from Bishop Simeon's examination of a court relic in 711. Markish lords held the frontier marches and took their oaths from the bishops. After the Fall emergency lordships hardened into principalities, but none could claim to be the empire. ## Adalmar's Crown, 975-1019 AR

By the 970s Grass-Tarkhan riders on horses bred from the fallen empire's own studs were reaching the eastern roads, and the bishops' levies could not catch them. In 974 a raid caught a column of refugees and grain carts at the ford of **Kaltfurt**. The march chaplain ran. **Hedwen**, a miller's widow, took up the march banner from the mud and stood with it in the water between the riders and the children, and the banner took the Flame. The raid broke on the ford. The commander on the bank was **Adalmar**, a Markish margrave's son, and in 975 the marcher lords acclaimed him king of the eastern marches.

Adalmar the Great united the region through war, marriage, Church law, and forced conversion. The bishops gave him one examined law and one register; he gave them a sword that could reach the raiders. Then he turned the register south. It listed the imperial frontier forts and estates abandoned after 905, which he granted to his Markish lords, and the clans who had held that country for three generations by seer-oath fought for it. In 987 the Green March war-leader Weylan took baptism and had his clans' fields entered in the register under his own name, to hold them by the settlers' law; the clans called him Oath-Bent. The **Folk Wars** (985–994) carried parish and noble rule into the southern borderlands with settlement drives, baptisms at spear-point and clan set against clan. In 993 Adalmar called the clans' oath-keepers and seers to Wolfsmere under safe-conduct to recite their boundaries for his clerks. The recital covered everything he had granted away, and at the **Drowning at Wolfsmere** he had the four thousand drowned in their own sacred lake, which remains the crown's deepest atrocity. The next winter the lake rose on a windless night and swallowed the fort Adalmar had built on its shore, and his frontier stopped at the lake country. Weylan sued his king in the bishops' court for the broken safe-conduct, and in 999 the **Peace of the Bent Oak** struck the grants beyond the lake from the register and wrote the march charters to keep bells, boats and iron away from it. [[Historical Spine of the Folk]]

His lords now called him the Drowner. In 1006 Adalmar asked the Sanctum to examine the Kaltfurt banner, and the relic court of **Lord Primarch Silvane II** recorded it answering and credited the sign to the king. Hedwen walked to Aurelium and testified that the banner had never burned in Adalmar's hand. Silvane entered her testimony and would not crown a man over a living witness. She died around 1010 and he in 1012. His successor **Bonifac III** had her testimony struck through, offered Veridun the chance to prove its own emperor by lifting the hood from its Great Lamp, and was answered with four nails and an anathema. In 1017 Bonifac crowned Adalmar **Emperor of the Atherians** at the ford of Kaltfurt itself, with the new **Crown of Embers**. The Kaltfurt millers shut their sluices that morning, by their own account, and the emperor was crowned standing in mud. He wore the crown two years. It offered common law and defence, but it also created the claim that Eclessia could inherit Atheria without Veridun. Hedwen's millers keep her feast, and Markish reform preachers call her struck-through line the first protest against the Sanctum's examinations.

## Split, Silence and the Blank Lot

The Great Split of 1015 separated the Sanctum and Veridun when Lord Primarch Bonifac III demanded that Veridun's hooded Great Lamp be uncovered and examined. [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]]

The Sanctum had told Veridun that an emperor is the man who proves lawful, and after Adalmar's death in 1019 it had to hold his heir to the same rule. **Ludovar the Mild** was devout, and the Sanctum recorded his banner silent at every examination it held for him. He reigned thirty-one years as king and was never crowned emperor; the Crown of Embers lay unworn in the royal chapel at Aurelium. His sons Aldwin, Lotharic and Gervin, and several of his greater lords, found other bishops willing to examine their banners and banners willing to answer, some of them with warlock fire scripted into the staff-head. From 1027 they fought one another under them. Their war reached the mountain fortress of Grauwacht around 1040, where porters and prisoners of both armies say an early military saint came to the burning refuse gate for the captives both sides had abandoned.

At **Drossfeld** in the spring of 1041, with their armies drawn up and nobody willing to fight, the brothers accepted the bishops' proposal of a lot. Each brother's name was cut on a wax tablet and sealed in Adalmar's helmet; the one drawn would send his banner to Aurelium for the Sanctum's own examination, and rule them all if it answered. Old Ludovar asked for a fourth tablet, left blank, "for the emperor who proves lawful". The legate drew the blank. Under the terms each man kept what he held that day until a banner answered, and none has:

- **Aldwin** held the south: the Kingdom of Aurelia, the lands around the Sanctum and the keeping of the unworn crown.
- **Lotharic** held the eastern march. Grass-Tarkhan riders killed him at the Orzva fords in 1047, and he left no heir.
- **Gervin** held the middle march: the existing Duchy of Caldria, which had held the line since 906, and the lands that became Brauvalis, Veridancia, Korrheim, Ironvale and the Sylvan Marches. Its dukes, abbots and order-captains carried examined banners of their own and soon stopped asking his leave.

The holdings were written down on the field on wax tablets, and the tablet for the middle march was smudged along its eastern line before anyone copied it. Every modern border claim interprets Drossfeld differently, because every Eclessian border began as that afternoon's truce line, and the middle march's claims all begin with what the smudge covered. Ludovar died in 1050.

## Founders and Their Chronicles

Older regional chronicles preserve these figures and inflate their offices:

- **Queen-Matron Seraphyne** was Aurelia's battle-abbess and emergency protector c. 930-949 AR; the chronicles make her the founder of a post-Fall kingdom.
- **Abbess Elyenne of Verdissia** and **Rhalien Veris** built Veridancia's first post-Drossfeld ducal compact in the 1050s. Rhalien was acclaimed as war-chaplain and protector before his descendants regularized the title.
- **Juran the Stalwart** was a Vesari elected war-prince before the Choosing; the chronicles remember him as the first king of a fully Eclessian Virelia.

## The Choosing of Virelia, 1061 AR

The Vesari were an older Braided river people with their own towns, moots and rites, formed from remnant populations, arriving Ark-derived groups and later local unions. Their gods lived at the hearth: a house's eldest woman kept its fire, and a house whose fire died had to relight it from a greater fire or become the client of whoever lent the light. In 1059 Grass-Tarkhan riders burned the hall of Prince **Radomir I** at Strazgorod and his house-fire went out. Rather than borrow from a neighbouring prince, he sent his aunt **Zdena**, the house's fire-keeper, west with sworn hearth-envoys to ask for a light from the Ember that had relit Thalmyria. In Veridun's cathedral in 1061 Patriarch Makarios the Adamant refused to light an unbaptised hearth from it, and while the envoys argued the cold lamp in Zdena's hands took the Flame, lit by nobody, before witnesses of both churches. Makarios refused to move what the Fire had chosen. Zdena answered, *"It chose my hand. I carried it,"* and stayed in Veridun to tend it. A house whose fire burned in Veridun's hall was Veridun's client, and Radomir would not be. He chose the Sanctum: its baptism, Eclessian swords against the Grass-Tarkhan, and a fire from its own altar, carried east in an iron pot, to light his new hall. A house lit from a church's fire was that church's client, and Radomir judged a church two months' ride away a lighter lord than any neighbour. In 1064 the Markish and Latinate lords of the eastern march, kingless since Lotharic's death, met the Vesari princes in a moot of sabres and took Radomir for their king. This created Virelia's durable contradiction: a Sanctum hierarchy above village piety that still looks to Veridun, with the Virelian Lamp still burning in Veridun's cathedral.

## The Lintels and the Sabre Crown, 1068-1103 AR

Radomir II won his throne in a war with his own kin. The houses that lost were old Vesari maternal households that painted their ancestors' faces above their door lintels and kept the district grain, and they demanded restitution through the duties of Strazgorod's ancient protector image, which had recognised those houses and opened a famine store in bad years; among them grew a rival image, the Elder Face. Between 1068 and 1102 Radomir II's officers burned the faces above the lintels, executed keepers and witnesses, took and renamed children and forbade one kin-name. The protector image returned with a harder face, and its famine store never opened again. Survivors refuse to paint a face above a lintel to this day. Virelian chroniclers call the purge resistance to pagan sedition; Vesari songs make every victim an ancestral saint. [[The Hunger Wars]]

When the Two Winters came in 1101, the districts the purge had emptied could not feed themselves, and Radomir II's sons died of the famine-fever. In 1103 the **Council of Sabres**, whose fathers had chosen the house in 1064, chose again and elected a king, arguing that a house whose first fire burned in another's hall could not pass its seat by blood alone. Veridun's envoys offered to send the Virelian Lamp home if the candidate of the old Vesari east won; he lost by three sabres. Virelia has elected its kings ever since, and the offer is made at every election.

## The Crown in Trust, 1101-1104 AR

The blank lot ended any practical imperial monarchy. The kings of Aldwin's line kept the Crown of Embers for eighty-three years without wearing it, each examined at his accession and each recorded silent. In the first of the **Two Winters**, under ash-grey skies and with no summer to follow, the lords of the east met at Drossfeld in the ash-snow of 1102 to find one head for the famine, and asked King **Ermbold** of Aurelia to carry the crown barefoot to the Sanctum and stand his examination. He refused: "It is a hat, not a relic." He sent it to the Primarch in a grain cart, under the sacks, and kept his kingdom; Aurelia remembers him as Ermbold the Idle. In 1103 came the finest harvest in a century, which farmers called the Apology. In 1104 the kings and dukes swore to leave the Crown of Embers in the Sanctum's custody "until the realm is worthy of one head", and the Primarchy's partisans dated the Apology from that day. Eclessia remained a family of realms held together by Church institutions, shared law, roads, dynastic memory, and an unrealized promise of reunion. Kings who petitioned the Sanctum to bring the crown out were told the same thing for a century: reunion first.

## How the Modern Realms Emerged

**Aurelia's** kings kept their title after Ermbold gave the crown away, until the last of Aldwin's line died in 1166. The Primarchs then took the Aurelian estates into the same trust as the Crown of Embers and ruled the kingdom directly; the Primarchal State dates from that year.

**Virelia** was made by a moot of sabres in 1064 and has filled its throne by election since 1103: one crown, chosen by the Council of Sabres.

**Caldria** consolidated as a territorial duchy of fortress-orders and frontier captains. In 1217, while Thalmyria fought its post-Qarath civil wars, Caldrian captains crossed the watershed and took three rite-changing valleys.

**Brauvalis** grew around monastic towns and guild estates. In the Two Winters its abbeys fed their famished neighbours and took their land in return.

**Veridancia** consolidated as a territorial duchy around the compact of the 1050s, its towns and schools. It bought Sestaran grain in the Two Winters and has owed Sestaran houses money since.

**Korrheim's** cantons began in the Heldenberg silver mines, worked by convicts, bread-rioters and the children of Folk captives. A slave courier led a revolt around 1116, and in the winter of 1123 the rebels broke the counts' army by a broken passage under the mountain called the Cold Way, paid for by condemned prisoners sent through it first. In 1124 the free valleys swore the **Oath of the Shafts**. Their liberator confessed what the passage had cost, went into exile and died in 1146; his cult calls him the Saint of Ashen Wings. Korrheim's cantons resisted permanent princely rule from that oath onward, and keep their liberation feast with a second morning on which the passage's victims are named.

**Ironvale** remained ducal until the Reform Wars destroyed its house and elevated the guild ministries.

**The Sylvan Marches** never achieved comparable centralization. The First Southern Crusade (1060–1067) carried marcher rule south around the lake country, and the Great Southern Purge (1158–1163) ended in a winter when riders without breath went through the marcher camps.

Around 1180, after a local famine, children in Brauvalis, Caldria and Veridancia walked west toward Caleran and were turned back at the watershed; forty never came home and the records hold no trace of them. The Sanctum defined the Unkindled Conception in 1183 and the Rising of the Mother in 1204. By 1232 the realms were crowded, rents were rising and younger sons had nowhere to go. The Grey Sweat, Freeflame Reformation, and Korrheim Accord later changed these realms as profoundly as Drossfeld had frozen them.
