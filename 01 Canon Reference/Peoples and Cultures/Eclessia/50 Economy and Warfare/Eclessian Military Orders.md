# Eclessian Military Orders

Eclessian orders are chartered institutions, not a single army. Some guard roads or shrines; others hold estates, maintain hospitals, investigate supernatural threats, or serve frontier vows. The Reform Wars reduced their universal reach and forced each to declare how it relates to territorial confession.

## Order of the Radiant Path

The Radiant Path maintains pilgrim roads, bridges, and fortified hospices. Its mounted brethren escort high-value processions; its Pathwardens patrol on foot with spear and crossbow. The order lost houses in Freeflame territory but retains cross-border passage through carefully secular service contracts.

## Inquisitors of Saint Malveris

Saint Malveris's inquisitors specialize in forbidden objects, fraudulent relics, and cult networks. The Council of Veridance curtailed their independent prisons and placed their supernatural investigations under Lantern review in 1324, nine years after the Lantern's first priest died in one of those prisons. Traditionalists resent the oversight; reformers doubt it goes far enough.

## Knights of Saint Aveline

The Avelines defend the southern marches and maintain a penitential memory of earlier campaigns against the Folk. It dates from the midwinter of 1163, at the end of the Great Southern Purge, when riders whose breath made no smoke went through the marcher camps after the boundary groves were cut and took a whole Aveline chapter with the Folk households nearest the groves. Their charter now distinguishes armed raiders, Grobi warbands, and lawful Folk sanctuaries. Chapters that ignore the distinction risk both secular prosecution and spiritual corruption.

## Order of the Blessed Watch

The Blessed Watch protects pilgrims, plague cemeteries, hospitals, and endangered parish archives. Its Shield Brethren train in warding and evacuation rather than conquest. It is the order most often trusted across confessional borders.

## Order of the Vigilant Flame

The Vigilant Flame once acted openly against sedition and heresy. Its burnings and property seizures during the Reform Wars made it a symbol of coercive old-Church power. The order survives under strict episcopal warrants, smaller and angrier, with several former chapters operating as unauthorised companies.

## Order of the Lantern

Chartered by Lord Primarch Celestin IX during the Council of Veridance in 1324, the Lantern is older than the Reformation it was chartered to answer. It began as an outlaw brotherhood: the lay singers of Aurelium's Saint-Leon quarter, who sang the dead out for nothing under Saint Gerlind's rule of a priest present, and who went on doing it at night and by lantern-light after the quarter was burned in 1285. Their priest, Father Anselin of Saint-Leon, had been carried out of that fire as a child and had studied under Lucian Ferro. The Inquisitors of Saint Malveris took him at a night funeral in 1314 and he died in one of their prison-houses the next winter, untried; the Order has never been told which house. The Council needed singers for the Great Arrears and took the brotherhood it had hunted for forty years. The Lantern now trains clergy, investigators, teachers, linguists, and field theologians. It carries arms when evidence requires protection but is not principally a knightly order. Its first rectors were Ferro's students, and they built it on his habit of understanding an opponent's doctrine accurately before judging it.

## Confessional Limits

Recognized Freeflame realms do not admit an armed clerical order merely because the Sanctum chartered it. Orders therefore travel under diplomatic safe-conducts, hospital licenses, mercenary articles, or local invitations. This creates fertile ground for forged warrants and deniable intervention.
