# Lives of Caldria and the Sylvan Marches

The frontier pair — one frontier armored, one feral. Caldria is what Eclessia looks like when it braces against the outside; the Marches are what it looks like when it stops pretending to govern.

## Caldria: the Order-Tenant and the Squire

The Third Crusade's battlefield (canon) never demobilized: the **Knights of Saint Aveline** and their sister-orders hold commanderies across the duchy, and a Caldrian farmer is likely an **order-tenant** — rent paid to a preceptory, protection real (the beacon-towers work; the sally actually comes), and the tithe-and-rent stacked heavier than a western tenant's for the privilege. The bargain is honest and old: *the wall costs.* His children's forks bend order-ward: the boy to the commandery as **squire-sergeant** (the poor road into the fighting orders — knighthood needs blood, but sergeants carry half the lances), the girl perhaps to the hospitaller sisters. **The knightly road itself:** vowed, celibate, professional — the closest thing old-Church Eclessia has to Thalmyria's soldier-culture, with the Grand Duke Alric IV (canon) pressing modernization (bombards on the walls) against zealot preceptors who call powder cowardice. The order pews hold the duchy's tension: crusade-liturgy every feast, and a border that has been *quiet* for two generations — Caldria is an instrument tuned for a war that keeps not coming, which is its own slow crisis.

**The strange-dose:** liturgically saturated, practically moderate — relic-processions on the walls, warded fords, and the veterans' certainty that the *last* war's stories were not all stories.

## The Sylvan Marches: the Settler, the Warden, and the Free

"Technically Eclessia" (canon). **Who comes here:** Virelian runaways (the serf-road's escape valve empties into these woods), reformed families two steps ahead of a flipped succession, debt-fugitives, order-deserters, second sons with axes — and the Folk, who were here first and remain, watching the newcomers repeat the newcomers' mistakes. **The settler's road:** clear, plant, fortify, negotiate — with High Warden Dreymann's wardens if lucky, with Baroness Wolfsden's knights if near Grimmholt, with the local clan's Speaker always (the wise settler learns hearth-law before church law; the graves of the unwise make good boundary markers). No tithe-collector's circuit survives three winters (local proverb, statistically supported); the parish here is a missionary post, and the **Elsin-tradition missioners** — gentle, unarmed, half-native by their third year — are the only Church presence the clans tolerate, to the standing fury of the Aveline tradition one duchy north.

**The warden's road:** Dreymann's rangers — trackers, arbiters, hangmen when it comes to it — recruited from exactly the fugitives they now police, which is the system's cynicism and its genius. **The free road:** Blackbriar (canon) runs the outlaw economy — fence-market, bounty-board, no questions — and the Marches' bandit is as likely a farmer-in-feud-season as a professional; the Red Tally does its best frontier recruiting here, and the wardens know the difference between a raid and a *presented bill* even when the law doesn't.

**The strange-dose: HIGH.** This is the one Eclessian territory where the thin world is a neighbor rather than a rumor — Folk seers on market days, wells that answer, the Nine-Springs' healing circuit, barrow-fields everyone routes around, and the settled dead not always settling. Marchers develop the frontier theology: precise etiquette, few questions, salt in the doorway, and a working relationship with two or three kinds of truth.

## Hooks

A Virelian magnate's catchers arrive at a Marches village with legal paper and armed men, and the runaway they've come for is now the blacksmith, the deacon, and the militia captain; a Caldrian preceptor requests wardens' help tracking something that crossed *out* of the quiet border; Blackbriar's bounty-board posts a name that belongs to a saint's day.
