# Velian Culture Overview

The Velians are an Atherian-descended people of the desert-margin towns, river districts and cultivated estates of the [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Velian/60 Realms and Peoples/Velian States/The Velian States Overview.md|Velian States]] and Zanthir. Their communities took shape around a distinctive communion of the Redeemer's faith, and developed through six centuries of life between Covenant rulers and the Atherian and Thalmyric empires. Town, congregation, craft and family memory remain important sources of belonging.

Their present country contains churches, irrigation works, vineyards, orchards, forts and several kinds of local government. Its towns connect societies that use different languages and worship in different ways. Velian physicians, translators and copyists have consequently held an important place in frontier life.

## Historical formation

The condemnation of Bishop Nasriel of Qabir at the Council of Halmenth in 411 AR separated his communion from the imperial Church. Its followers survived in desert-margin cities, where local congregations preserved their worship and teaching. During the First Unsealing they negotiated protection from the Banu Mizan. Tribute, disarmament and restrictions on testimony secured the continued possession of churches and property, the appointment of clergy and the use of communal courts.

These arrangements helped communities endure conquest, while placing them in an unequal relationship with their protectors. Later Qahirid governments increased tribute, garrisons, bondage and custody over personal names. Town institutions persisted through these changes. Municipal officers, clergy, craftsmen and families continued to organise work and education under successive rulers.

The failed Second Crusade and the Long Reconquest brought renewed danger. Thalmyric authorities frequently treated Velians as collaborators and heretics, although local choices had been made under widely differing pressures. Zanthir and the Velian States remained outside secure Thalmyric control. The [[Historical Atlas of the Velians]] follows these changes and the traditions associated with the frontier.

## Towns, households and work

Velian households preserve family papers alongside knowledge of a trade, a congregation and the places from which their relatives came. Multilingual schooling supports the work of translators, physicians, irrigation officers and copyists. The preservation of Atherian learning has depended in part on these ordinary professions and the families and schools that sustained them.

Rural communities grow grain, vines and orchard crops, and keep sheep. Shared water makes cooperation between neighbouring households essential, including households of different faiths. The same towns contain Thalmyric-rite parishes, Covenant converts, Jirahar residents and mixed families. Religious disagreement therefore accompanies practical relationships in farming, medicine, trade and education.

The border economy also includes ransom, slavery and compulsory payments. Protection is experienced unevenly: a prosperous town can bargain through its usefulness, while poorer households bear levies and restrictions with fewer means of resisting them.

## Faith and communal authority

The [[The Velian Church|Velian Church]] follows Nasriel's teaching that Amaryah bore the perfect human vessel, upon whom the uncreated Fire descended at the opening of the Redeemer's ministry. Thalmyric and Eclessian theologians condemn this account as a division of the Redeemer's person. Velian theologians defend it as an affirmation of the Fire's transcendence.

The Catholicos of Qabir heads the Church. Its clergy may marry; its churches are whitewashed, image-free and lit by a single lamp. The liturgy preserves the Jirahar dialect associated with the Redeemer's earthly life. These practices give Velian congregations a recognisable character across political borders.

Church membership and ancestry overlap without being identical. Some members of the communion come from other peoples, and Velian families differ in observance and religious allegiance. Under Covenant rule, recognised congregations belong to [[The People of the Lamp]]. This status protects worship and communal institutions while imposing legal inferiority, levies and restrictions. Periodic persecution and changes of ruler can undermine its guarantees.

## Government and the frontier in 1360

The Velian States have several centres of authority. Princes, city councils, bishops, estate houses, Covenant governors, Zanthiri patrons, Thalmyric-backed claimants and militias control different districts and roads. Some towns pay two powers. Others maintain a degree of autonomy through the services they provide to both.

In 1360, Emir Badis and the client-state compacts protect much of the Church's institutional heartland. The region also faces conversion pressure, competing military claims and disputes over bondage and water. Families with relatives on opposing sides must live with accusations of collaboration and with the possibility of another change of government.
