# Peoples and Cultures — Gateway Article Editorial Review

This review records the gateway-article pilot completed on 13 September 2026. It covers the reader-facing [[Cultures MOC|Peoples and Cultures]] entrance and the eleven principal overview articles reached from it. The pass applied the [[World Egg Encyclopedia - Editorial Style Guide]] without changing established history, ancestry, theology, government or creaturely continuity.

## Authority and scope

The [[Peoples and Cultures - Authorial Navigation|authorial culture guide]] supplied the required coverage for culture overviews. The [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Ethnic Groups Overview.md|Peoples and Ancestry Register]] and [[01 Canon Reference/History/Peoples in the World Historical Atlas.md|Peoples through History]] remain the cross-cultural controls. Culture registers, historical atlases and formation articles continue to control the facts summarised by each overview.

This was an information-architecture and copy-editing pass. It preserved the factual propositions already present in the reviewed articles. New links point to existing canonical explanations; they do not promote draft material or create new relationships.

## Findings and decisions

- The principal culture entrance was marked `author-only` even though the reader guide and site navigation treated it as the public entrance. Its visibility is now `public`.
- Every overview already had an unheaded, self-contained lead and remained between approximately 850 and 1,525 body words. No overview required division by size.
- The leads distinguish culture or civilisation from ancestry, religion and political allegiance where that distinction matters most. Regional variation, ordinary work, authority, faith, neighbours and the circumstances of 1360 receive substantive summaries rather than link-only treatment.
- Important first-use links were strengthened in the Ahl-e-Ahd, Eclessian, Nsizwa, Tarkhan, Thalmyric, Velian and Atherian entrances. The Nsizwa and Velian overviews received the largest proportional improvement because their useful child articles were least visible from the body.
- Every overview now ends with purpose-labelled routes for history, ordinary life or institutions, faith or neighbours and comparison with other cultures. The common comparison route returns a direct-search visitor to the public culture entrance.
- Headings that contain the present-day account now identify 1360 explicitly where the older wording obscured it. A vault-wide check found no incoming links to the retired heading anchors.
- The two Atherian reference portraits now have descriptive alternative text. The illustrations remain visual references rather than evidence for a named historical person.

## Historical-civilisation entrance expansion

The culture entrance originally presented Atheria as its only historical civilisation. The expanded reader route now introduces ten extinct political worlds in two clear groups:

- **Giant-age kingdoms and crownlands:** Arsuvan, Vathra and Nhaldur;
- **early human cities and empires:** ancient Qahur, the Gateway Republic, Qiryath, Arqesh, Vashar, Ortheia and the Atherian city-state and imperial sequence.

Each entry identifies the form of society, its place in the wider chronology and one reader-ready starting article. Nhaldur enters through the public [[01 Canon Reference/History/30 Exodus and First Civilizations/Historical Atlas of the Southern Cold World.md|Historical Atlas of the Southern Cold World]] rather than making its author-only detailed control the beginner destination. Ancient histories belonging to still-living peoples remain attached to the Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Ahl-e-Ahd entries, then reappear in the longer regional routes. This prevents “historical civilisation” from implying that every ancient population formed a single state.

## Validation and next boundary

The technical pass checks frontmatter, new links, heading anchors, rendered lists, image alternatives and the public route from the reader guide through Peoples and Cultures to each overview. The whole-vault compatibility report remains the final link and rendering check.

The next editorial boundary is the set of child articles behind these gateways. Priority should follow reader traffic and explanatory dependence: formation and ordinary-life articles first, then polity and faith overviews, then specialised histories. Link count remains a review signal rather than a quota.
