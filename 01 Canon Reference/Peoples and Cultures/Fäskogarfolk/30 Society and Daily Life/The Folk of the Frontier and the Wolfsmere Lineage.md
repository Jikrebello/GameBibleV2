# The Folk of the Frontier and the Wolfsmere Lineage

"The Folk" is an exonym — the settled Eclessians' word for the unconverted, mostly Enaidfae-descended (and mixed Markish-Enaidfae) clan peoples of the southern forest frontiers. The Folk call themselves by clan; the collective name they accept, when they must, is *the Old Blood*. Gaelic/Celtic-coded: clan-land held from ancestors not kings, seers over priests, oaths over documents, rivers and groves as legal persons.

## Clan Structure

A clan is a kinship bloc holding named territory under three offices: the **Speaker** (outward voice, treaty-holder), the **War-Keeper** (raid and defense), and the **Seer** (grove-law, omens, the dead). Clans federate seasonally and feud generationally. Known clans of the Grenzburg frontier: the **Moss-Crown**, the **Shattered-Tusk**, the **Ghost-Foot**, the **River-Clans** (Speaker Bran), the **Ash-Spear** (Warlord Cenn), and the **Chapel-Folk** — the converted, despised and growing. The broader frontier includes: the **Elk-Ford** (drovers and ferry-right holders), the **Grey-Withy** (basket-traders, famous neutrals, informal diplomatic channel for everyone), the **Nine-Springs** (healers, guardians of a thin-space complex of holy wells), and the **Owl-Mark** (night-hunters and secret-keepers — it is the Owl-Mark who are said to keep the location of Saint Elsin's grave).

## The House of the Bent Oak

The line of **Weylan Oath-Bent**, who fought Adalmar for twenty years and then took baptism (c. 1005), endures among the Chapel-Folk as their great lineage — the **Bent Oak** house. Every Chapel-Folk community shelters under its precedent ("if Weylan could bend..."), and every unconverted clan uses its name as the word for honorable surrender, which is not a compliment. Bent Oak elders sit in a unique misery: the Church parades them; the clans pity them; both need them.

## Wolfsmere and the Lakekeepers

At the **Drowning at Wolfsmere (993 AR)**, Adalmar's soldiers drowned four thousand captive oath-keepers and clan-seers in their own sacred lake. Nine seers were held under and did not drown — pulled out breathing at dusk, marked ever after: pale eyes, cold hands, and dreams that come up from the water. From those nine descends the **Lakekeeper line**, the Folk's most sacred and most feared office.

**What the Lakekeepers keep:**

- **The Roll of the Drowned.** All four thousand names, recited in order once a year at the lake's edge, one breath per name, across three nights. (The Folk's dark mirror of the Church's Rite of the Empty Seat — one tradition keeps a seat, the other keeps a lake.)
- **The lake-law.** No Church bell may sound within hearing of the water; no boat crosses; no iron enters; the drowned are spoken of as *held*, never as dead.
- **The watch.** Wolfsmere is a standing leftward thin space — "the Lake That Remembers." A mass soul-route atrocity at a sacred site does not close. Things surface: memories that are not the dreamer's own, voices in the reed-beds naming debts, and in the worst years, the water standing up. The Lakekeepers do not worship what is in the lake. They keep it *asleep*, and they have never told anyone — Church, clan, or crown — exactly what that costs.

**The present Lakekeeper connection:** the **Thorn-Seer Mael** of the Grenzburg factions is a Lakekeeper-line seer. This explains both the Thorn-Seer's unifying authority across clans and the "prophetic opportunism" the faction notes describe: a Lakekeeper bargaining with settled powers is not seeking advantage but *paying the lake's costs* by other means. This single link wires Grenzburg's Folk politics directly into 350 years of canon wound.

## The Folk and the Three Fires

- **The old Church** took the lake and keeps the marches' cathedrals; Aveline-tradition crusaders and Elsin-tradition missioners fight over Folk policy to this day.
- **The Freeflame** fascinates and unsettles the clans: preachers with no hierarchy, no relics, and no bells — some clans hear them gladly ("a fire with no landlord — that we know"), and the Sanctum has noticed with alarm.
- **The seers' verdict** on all of it, given by a Grey-Withy speaker and repeated everywhere: *"Three hearths quarrel over one fire, and none of them asked the wood."*

## Story Use

The Roll of the Drowned as a living quest-calendar (something goes wrong at name two-thousand-and-something); Lakekeeper succession crises; the Bent Oak dilemma at every conversion plotline; Owl-Mark secret-keeping versus Church relic-hunters seeking Elsin's grave; and the standing Grenzburg question of what happens to the lake's sleep if the frontier's wars reach the water.
