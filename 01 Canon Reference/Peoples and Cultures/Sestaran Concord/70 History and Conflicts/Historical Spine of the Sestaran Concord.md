# Historical Spine of the Sestaran Concord

The Sestaran Concord is a league of seven maritime cities whose Braided populations arose through local survival, migration, Atherian settlement, commerce, captivity and intermarriage. Its members developed separate civic histories before joining for mutual defence after the Fall of Caleran. In the century after the Grey Sweat, Fiorenzia's Medori bank grew rich clearing the estates of the plague dead and the payments of the living for them, the Black Wind's tolls pushed the cities' trade to the sea, and Castara found a way across the Boiling Sea. By 1360 rival banking houses, shipyards, archives, mercenary companies and sea routes sustain a prosperous, divided Concord that lends to every power on the board.

## Braided Harbor Peoples and City Worlds

Across the late Scattering and Hearth ages, local Orphaned communities, southern war-settlers, other Ark-derived migrants, island pilots, refugees, captives, river traders and inland fair populations braided differently at each site. Early contact included negotiated quarters and shared defense as well as armed occupation, grain seizure, slave markets and expulsions. Harbor communities, river markets, island stations, inland fairs and mountain refuges developed independently. Fiorenzia, Sermora and Vardessa became cities before later Atherian state conquest.

## Atherian Incorporation

Atherian expansion conquered or chartered the peninsula. Aversia and Orsena were imperial foundations; Vorthara remained incompletely contained. Roads, citizenship, archives, and conversion bound the region to the empire while preserving local councils useful to imperial rule.

During the [[Historical Atlas of the Atherian Age of Glory|Age of Glory]], harbour governments occupied a changing range from direct provincial rule to treaty alliance and commercial privilege. Fleets required their pilots, shipyards and stores. Municipal families used that need to preserve local authority, while imperial commanders compelled crews and timber when negotiation failed. These distinct arrangements contributed to the later independence of the cities.

## Drift, c. 400-540 AR

As the imperial court's revenues and rites failed, provincial commands kept their own revenue, and the harbour cities kept their taxes, hired their own forces and negotiated directly with neighbours. In the succession war of 447–452 the court first hired cohorts of Sestaran warlocks to throw Shell-fire for it, and the trade outlived the war. Under the Saldane emperors the cities dug out charters older than the empire, Vardessa's the famous case, and paid Caleran in ships and warlock contracts in place of tax. Independence existed before anyone declared it.

## Fall and Defensive Compact, 905-909 AR

The Fall cut imperial command and opened the peninsula to conquest. Vortharan and Caldrian forces broke a Covenant army in 907 at [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)]]. Fiorenzia, Sermora, Aversia, Vardessa, and Orsena sealed the first defensive compact in 908. Vorthara added its seal in 909 while rejecting domestic oversight.

## Competitive Independence, 910-1231 AR

With the Covenant's northern advance broken, the six cities turned on one another. They fought private wars over tolls and river mouths, seized each other's convoys, hired troops abroad and repeatedly tested the line between rivalry and existential danger. Refugee wealth from the Fall, including the crewless ships of the Thirty Sisters, was repainted and resold for generations. Famine in the Two Winters of 1101–1103 taught the harbour cities to keep grain under seal. In 1102 Sermora closed its harbour chain against famine ships from the Eclessian coast. A foreign-born chain-tender lowered it, refugees and harbour workers died in the crush at the gap, and the city hanged him as a traitor. In 1166 a rival's fleet and hired Ättar keels besieged Sermora with a leased storm-engine, and defenders and attackers alike saw a figure in a chain-tender's apron above the harbour wall; the engine broke and the siege failed. The same figure was seen to rebuke the Sermoran soldiers who then murdered foreign residents blamed for the breach. Since then the city's harbour law lowers the chain for one foreign or refugee vessel at every feast. In Aversia's high mines, from about 1110 to 1170, miners kept a compact with the small grey people at the edge of the lamplight, the Knockers, until Shield-House officials turned it into production law, faked its warning knock and sent strikers and prisoners down to die under a cut rib. Aversian miners still never answer the middle of three knocks. No member succeeded in turning wartime leadership into permanent sovereignty.

## The Sweat and the Roll of Faces, 1232-1264 AR

The Grey Sweat reached the Sestaran ports by sea and killed through every quarter. In Fiorenzia the heads of the patrician houses carried the city's biers by old privilege, and the fever followed the biers. By 1238 nearly half the houses entered in the Roll of Faces had no living head, and the senate could not keep a quorum. It sold seats. The Medori, a counting-house family with a reputation for unusual clauses, bought one with a genealogy their rivals still call counterfeit.

The plague also gave Fiorenzia its faces. The dead were burned on the river banks, and families with no grave to visit paid the mask-makers for a wax likeness of each of their dead, hung it on the house wall and carried it through the streets at the Feast of Souls. Within a generation a house was known by its wall, and the city's painters learned their trade copying wax. The old houses said the Medori wall held faces that nobody remembered seeing alive.

**Bartolo Medori the Clearer** made the family's fortune in the years that followed. Every city had estates whose owners and heirs had died in the same month, and his house cleared them: settling debts, finding claimants, selling what no one claimed. From 1252, when the Sanctum founded its Chancery of Sendings, his letters of credit carried the payments of families across a dozen jurisdictions to the Sending Houses that sang rites for their dead. His ledger-maxim, "lend to both, collect from the winner", is quoted as cynicism and practised as policy.

## The Black Wind and the turn to the sea, 1265-1300 AR

Qarayel's Black Wind made Sestaran caravans on the northern routes pay the grass-toll from 1265 to 1300, proving the compact defended walls better than trade beyond them. Merchants answered by moving freight to ships. Sermora's and Orsena's fleets carried what the roads had, and Sestaran hulls went further along the Boiling Sea coast than before, putting in at a minor salt-and-turtle port called Castara.

In 1274 Bartolo's credit bought the conclave's votes for Alessior II, a Malvora of Sestara orphaned by the Sweat, an ascetic who had never asked for them. Bartolo had read Alessior's preaching correctly: in 1283 the new Primarch's Bull of the Sufficient Seal taught that a sealed letter placed a soul in the Church's keeping before any rite was sung, and assurance letters were sold without limit from then on, with every payment passing through Medori hands. The election debt was paid out of that trade. When Alessior died in 1289 owing more than the Primarchal State could pay, Bartolo took the Chancery of Sendings' master registers as surety: every letter sold, every name, every House assigned to sing it. The Sanctum has never redeemed them. Bartolo died in 1301.

## The Medori Flowering, 1301-1345 AR

Bartolo had outlived his sons. The bank passed to his granddaughter **Ginevra**, who kept its inner books for half a century and is said never to have signed anything in her own name. In 1311 she married one of her own factors. **Lauro Medori of the Open Vault** (1289–1353) was a mask-maker's son from the river quarter, apprenticed to the counting-floors at twelve, and he took his wife's name. He spent the family's money on academies, galleries and the painters he had grown up among, the masters of the Fiorenzian flowering, and the bank lent to both sides of the Reform Wars. Sermoran and Medori loans financed Caedros Aetherion's Crimson Pilgrimage and the siege of Zehrim (1319–1325) against Thalmyria's customs and antiquities concessions. The war-loans also broke men. Taddeo Vetti, a Sermoran-born factor, had kept the bank's desks at Aurelium, Saronika and Qabir and had written its factors a private guide to reading a sovereign before lending to one. His last desk failed in the Reform Wars defaults, and in 1336 the bank sold him into Fiorenzia's Debtors' Corps.

## Castaran Expansion, 1328-1334 AR

In 1328 the Castaran salt-hauler *Naris's Hand*, under the pilot Ottavia Sele, was caught at sea on a Breath day, when the hurricane belt falls flat calm, and crossed to the Shattered Isles. She returned with green crystal, Expanse hardwood and a wrecked ship's chart-case of old Viscaran surveys, and with a log that disagreed with the calendar. Castara's new University rebuilt the Viscaran storm-keel from the wreck's lines, and by the early 1330s its ships crossed in hurricane-season windows of six to ten weeks.

A boom port without a fleet is a prize, and Castara joined the Concord as the seventh seal in 1332 in exchange for ships, scouts and overseas provisions. In 1333 its surveyors staked wells across Ash-Horde pasture for an inland road towards Vardessa and Aversia, and the Ash banners burned the survey camps. In 1334 the city concluded the first [[Castaran Long Road|Ash Road Peace]]. The annual escort payment secured registered caravans but never extended Castaran sovereignty across Tarkhan country.

## The Bank of the Dead, 1345-1349 AR

The Korrheim Accord of 1343 ended the war-loans that had carried the bank through the Reformation, and two years later a man in a grey coat nearly ended the bank. Vetti had worked nine years on the river dikes. In 1345 he sold a Sermoran printer what he had copied of the Fiorenzian sheets of the Chancery registers, for the price of his own paper. The pamphlet named Fiorenzian dead whose Sendings had been paid for and never sung, and called the Medori vault **the Bank of the Dead**. Vetti was found drowned the next spring in the dike he had been set to mend. The printer brought out his factors' guide in 1347 as *Of Princes as Borrowers*.

That winter the faces of the named dead began to sweat on their walls, and then to speak. Families queued in their own halls to hear their mothers. The voices knew what the dead had known, and what they said named debts, bastards and forged wills in half the houses of the Roll of Faces. Frate Severin, a Flame-preacher, told the city that the faces had become doors for the Hungers, that the thing speaking through them was nobody's mother, and that a door is shut by naming and singing the dead behind it, never by fire.

The senate chose fire. At the Feast of Souls in 1346, when the city walks its faces, the senators ordered every speaking face surrendered at the cathedral square, and guildsmen went from house to house to take down the ones that were withheld. Severin begged the city not to light the pyre. It was lit, and witnesses still argue about what they saw standing in the **Pyre of Masks**. The crowd went from the square to the Medori palace to take the names by force.

Lauro met them on the steps. Against the ruling of his wife and the bank's board, he had the Fiorenzian sheets of the registers carried out of the vault, read the first of them aloud himself, and gave them to the friar. Severin took no office. The city followed him into its churches for the **Long Vigil**: four years of singing every name on the sheets, house by house, day and night. The painters painted the burned faces back from the memories of the living, so that each name sung had a face again, and the panels of those years are the best work Fiorenzia has done. Crowds drowned a dozen mask-makers in the river as door-makers, and Severin preached against the drownings and was not obeyed. In 1348 the appraisers' guild hanged a master painter whose panels showed faces nobody had described to him, and locked away what was left in his workshop.

The last sheet was sung in the summer of 1349. That autumn the citizen households, voting over the heads of the senate that had burned their dead, made the ducal chair hereditary in the Medori line, and Lauro sat in it. Severin preached against the gift. The city had sung its dead out of pawn, he said, and was pawning the living. On the eve of the Feast of Souls he went alone into the cellar of the mask-makers' hall, where the moulds of the burned faces were kept, and was found in the morning sitting upright, wearing a wax face that no family in Fiorenzia would own and that nobody could lift from his. He was buried in it outside the walls, and many Fiorenzians believe the Medori had a hand in it. Lauro died in 1353, and his son Arrigo followed him as duke.

## The Concord in 1360

The Medori lend to Veridun, the Sanctum, the reformed duchies, Castara and the Ash road, and they still hold the registers the Order of the Lantern needs to sing the Sanctum's unpaid rites. Every city but Fiorenzia has asked for its own sheets and been quoted a price. Sermora's fleets and papers watch them. Castara's crossings bring back things the University catalogues behind locked doors. By 1360 the Concord finances, arms, transports, and spies upon every major power while remaining unable to agree on what any of that is for.

## Historical Atlas — wider inheritance

Sestaran harbour populations are Braided peoples. Coastal refuge, occupation, commerce, intermarriage and locally defeated conquests produced different ports. The tradition of a harbour founded twice belongs to one such community.

Atherian influence left institutions and techniques. The defensive compact of 908–909 joined distinct powers under the pressure of invasion. Later banking, craft and maritime expansion strengthened the cities without giving them uniform government.

The Medori rise began in the plague years, when the Roll of Faces lost its houses and the dead left estates to settle, and the merchant-humanist flowering came two generations later. The same counting-houses endowed plague hospitals, paid mercenary lances and insured slave cargoes, often in one ledger.
