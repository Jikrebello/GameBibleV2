# Sestara and the Wider World

The Sestaran Concord occupies the ports and overland approaches through which many of the known world's peoples trade, borrow, hire soldiers and recover captives. Its member cities pursue these relationships separately, using their own fleets, fairs, banks and foreign quarters. The Concord's wealth depends upon preserving access among rivals, but the same commerce carries arms, exploitative concessions, enslaved people and political debts across its borders.

Most of the debts are recent. In and after the Grey Sweat, Fiorenzia's Medori cleared the estates of the plague dead and carried the payments of the living to the Sending Houses; in 1296 they took the Sanctum's assurance registers as surety for a Primarch's debts. Sermoran and Medori loans paid for Thalmyria's frontier campaigns of 1319–1325. The Black Wind's tolls pushed Sestaran trade to the sea, and in the 1330s Castara opened the only road across the Boiling Sea. In 1360 nearly every court on the board owes a Sestaran house for a war, for its dead or for passage.

## Jirahar

Migdal Dahava competes with Medori credit while clearing obligations the Bank cannot safely hold. Migdal Yamma and the Sea Fleets work through Sermoran shipping. Orsena and Vardessa maintain protected Jirahar quarters whose sanctuary, food law, schools, and burial rights predate many citizen houses; Turning Companies use their river yards and fairs. Cities value Jirahar routes and repeatedly resent the independence that makes them useful. When Meshalem's return movement collapsed in 1291, Sestaran creditors foreclosed on tower-town property sold for the journey, and Jirahar houses remember which cities bought cheap.

## The Covenant

Sermoran and Orsenan merchants carry Covenant goods; Fiorenzian scholars study contract law; Mazrakan hosts Sestaran factors; Aversian arms reach both legal and embargoed buyers. Iqashar's escort fees to the Black Wind (1265–1300) and its later hire of Iron-Horde companies were partly paid through Sestaran bills. Public politics treats the Medori Bank as Fiorenzian, though rivals circulate unproved stories about the family's foreign legal inheritance and its uncanny clauses.

## Tarkhan

Vardessa's horse fairs and northern roads depend on negotiated word-prices, a custom settled during the Black Wind's grass-toll. Sermora hires Ash Horde lances and transports horses by sea. Castara purchases the annual [[Castaran Long Road|Ash Road Peace]] for declared caravans crossing northeastern Tarkhan country, a peace bought in 1334 after Castaran surveyors staked wells on Ash pasture and the Ash banners burned their camps; the route remains outside Castaran territory and ordinary Seven-Seal protection. Aversia sells tools and arms while demanding security, and Fiorenzian insurers price both the payment and its failure. Tarkhan guests judge Sestaran contracts by hospitality and declared word-price, not notarial elegance.

## Folk

Sestaran factors seek timber, amber, medicines, guides, resin, rare plants, and access to place-bound materials. Fair exchange exists alongside fraudulent concessions, destructive logging, relic theft, and contracts signed by people with no authority over communal land. Vortharan buyers are especially dangerous because they value living and metaphysical specimens.

## Ättar

Ättar crews raid Sestaran shipping, serve as mercenary sailors, sell timber, furs, whale products, iron, and cold-water navigation, and ransom one another through Orsena. Sermoran letters can turn the same captain from pirate to privateer without changing the last voyage's dead. Vorthara's arenas employ both free champions and enslaved or debt-bound Ättar captives; Lion-Mane jarls treat the distinction as a reason for vengeance only when it affects their own hall.

## Thalmyria and Eclessia

Both empires borrow money, buy arms, hire ships, contest church jurisdiction, and fear a rival controlling the peninsula. Vorthara's role in 907 binds it to Caldrian history.

Thalmyria remembers Sestaran aid, wartime profiteering and debt in the same breath. The Crimson Pilgrimage and the siege of Zehrim were financed against Saronikan customs and antiquities concessions, and Veridun's chancellery has been paying for them for thirty-five years.

Eclessia owes Sestara in a stranger currency. The Medori hold the Chancery of Sendings' registers, the record of every assurance letter sold under Alessior VI, and the Council of Veridance had to buy copies sheet by sheet so that the Order of the Lantern could sing the rites those letters promised. The Sanctum, the reformed duchies and the Freeflame princes all borrowed from the same houses during the Reform Wars. In 1345 a Sermoran pamphlet calling the Medori vault "the Bank of the Dead" brought down the family in Fiorenzia for three years, and the Vortharan brokers who once bought unsung letters from the Sanctum's own treasurer have never been found.

Neither empire can conquer the Concord cheaply without driving the other cities into automatic muster.
