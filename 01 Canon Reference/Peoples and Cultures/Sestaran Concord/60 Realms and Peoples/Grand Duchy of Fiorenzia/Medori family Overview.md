# Medori Family Overview

The Medori are Fiorenzia's ruling ducal and banking house. Their public legitimacy rests on credit, patronage, infrastructure, charity, art, diplomacy, and admission to the Roll of Faces in 1238 AR.

## Rise

**Bartolo Medori the Clearer** (c. 1215–1301) expanded a regional bank into an international clearing and lending house. When the Grey Sweat left nearly half the houses in Fiorenzia's Roll of Faces without a living head, the senate sold seats to keep a quorum, and in 1238 Bartolo bought one with a genealogy rivals still call counterfeit. His house cleared the estates of the plague dead and, from 1252, carried the payments of the living to the Sending Houses that sang rites for them. In 1274 his credit bought the conclave for Alessior II, an ascetic whose doctrine of the sealed letter multiplied the Sending payments passing through Medori hands. When Alessior died in 1289 owing more than the Primarchal State could pay, Bartolo took the master registers of the Chancery of Sendings as surety, and the Sanctum has never redeemed them.

Bartolo outlived his sons, and the bank passed to his granddaughter **Ginevra**, who kept its inner books for half a century. Her husband **Lauro of the Open Vault** (1289–1353) was a mask-maker's son who had come up through the counting-floors and took the Medori name at their marriage in 1311. He made Medori culture fashionable enough that rivals learned to imitate the family they despised. In 1345 a Sermoran pamphlet printed extracts from the registers in the Medori vault, sold to the printer by the bank's own disgraced factor Taddeo Vetti, naming Fiorenzian dead whose Sendings had been paid for and never sung, and called the vault the Bank of the Dead. That winter the wax faces of the named dead began to speak on Fiorenzian walls, and at the Feast of Souls in 1346 the senate burned them in the Pyre of Masks, which Frate Severin begged the city not to light. When the crowd came from the fire to the palace for the names, Lauro opened the vault against the ruling of his wife and the board and gave the city its sheets. Severin led the Long Vigil for four years without holding office. When the last sheet was sung in 1349 the citizen households made the ducal chair hereditary in the Medori line, over the senate's head, and Lauro was the first hereditary duke. Severin preached against the gift and was found dead in a wax face that autumn. Lauro's son **Arrigo** succeeded him in 1353.

## Present House

- **Duke Gaius Medori** heads the family and duchy.
- **Lady Valeria Medori** manages diplomacy and political marriage.
- **Lord Matteo Medori** directs major investments.
- **Lord Fabrizio Medori** oversees logistics, shipping exposure, and protected routes.
- **Lady Isabella Medori** manages private archives, internal discipline, and sensitive instruments.

The family is not one mind. Gaius values dynastic stability, Matteo seeks returns, Fabrizio needs functioning routes, Valeria avoids coalitions against Fiorenzia, and Isabella protects secrets even when disclosure would help the Bank.

## Public Power

Medori loans reach courts, towns, guilds, armies, churches, and foreign houses. Patronage creates genuine art, hospitals, schools, and public works while binding recipients into political obligation. The family's power is greatest when clients continue functioning and weakest where war, repudiation, revolution, or rival credit destroys enforceability.

## Present Tensions

Old houses dispute purchased ancestry and have never forgiven a crown voted over the senate's head; Severin's memory stains that crown, because he preached against it and died within the season, and many Fiorenzians believe the family had a hand in the face he was found in; the family itself has never agreed whether Lauro saved the bank or gave away its best asset; debtors organize; foreign defaults rise; and younger Medori disagree over whether the family is a dynasty with a bank or a bank burdened by a duchy.
