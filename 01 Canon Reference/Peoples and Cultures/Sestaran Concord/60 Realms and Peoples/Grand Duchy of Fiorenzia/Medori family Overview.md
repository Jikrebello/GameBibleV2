# Medori Family Overview

The Medori are Fiorenzia's ruling ducal and banking house. Their public legitimacy rests on credit, patronage, infrastructure, charity, art, diplomacy, and admission to the Golden Book in 1238 AR.

## Rise

**Bartolo Medori the Elder** (c. 1215–1301) expanded a regional bank into an international clearing and lending house. When the Grey Sweat emptied about a third of the houses in Fiorenzia's Golden Book, the senate sold seats to keep a quorum, and in 1238 Bartolo bought one with a genealogy rivals still call counterfeit. His house cleared the estates of the plague dead and, from 1252, carried the payments of the living to the Sending Houses that sang rites for them. In 1281 his credit bought the conclave for Alessior II, an ascetic whose doctrine of the sealed letter multiplied the Sending payments passing through Medori hands. When Alessior died in 1296 owing more than the Primarchal State could pay, Bartolo took the master registers of the Chancery of Sendings as surety, and the Sanctum has never redeemed them.

His grandson **Lauro the Magnificent** (1289–1341) made Medori culture fashionable enough that rivals learned to imitate the family they despised. In 1345 a Sermoran pamphlet printed extracts from the registers in the Medori vault, naming Fiorenzian dead whose Sendings had been paid for and never sung, and called the vault the Bank of the Dead. Lauro's son **Arrigo** answered at the carnival of 1346 with a Medori-paid Pyre of Masks that Frate Severin begged the city not to light; when the crowd turned from the fire to the palace, Arrigo fled to Orsena with the registers. Severin led the city's Long Vigil for four years without holding office and was found dead in a mask in 1349. The bankrupt senate then invited the family home and paid for the city's register sheets with the ducal chair, hereditary in the Medori line. Arrigo was the first hereditary duke.

## Present House

- **Duke Gaius Medori** heads the family and duchy.
- **Lady Valeria Medori** manages diplomacy and political marriage.
- **Lord Matteo Medori** directs major investments.
- **Lord Fabrizio Medori** oversees logistics, shipping exposure, and protected routes.
- **Lady Isabella Medori** manages private archives, internal discipline, and sensitive instruments.

The family is not one mind. Gaius values dynastic stability, Matteo seeks returns, Fabrizio needs functioning routes, Valeria avoids coalitions against Fiorenzia, and Isabella protects secrets even when disclosure would help the Bank.

## Public Power

Medori loans reach courts, towns, guilds, armies, churches, and foreign houses. Patronage creates genuine art, hospitals, schools, and public works while binding recipients into political obligation. The family's power is greatest when clients continue functioning and weakest where war, repudiation, revolution, or rival credit destroys enforceability.

## Present Tensions

Old houses dispute purchased ancestry; Severin's memory stains a crown the city sold to get its dead back, and many Fiorenzians believe the family had a hand in his mask; debtors organize; foreign defaults rise; and younger Medori disagree over whether the family is a dynasty with a bank or a bank burdened by a duchy.
