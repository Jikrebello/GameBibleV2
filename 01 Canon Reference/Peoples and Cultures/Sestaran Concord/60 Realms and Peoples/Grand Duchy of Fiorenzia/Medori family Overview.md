# Medori Family Overview

The Medori are Fiorenzia's ruling ducal and banking house. Their public legitimacy rests on credit, patronage, infrastructure, charity, art, diplomacy, and admission to the Golden Book in 1238 AR.

## Rise

Bartolo Medori the Elder expanded a regional bank into an international clearing and lending house. The family used wealth, marriage, patronage, and a controversial payment to enter Fiorenzia's patriciate. Lauro the Magnificent made Medori culture fashionable enough that rivals learned to imitate the family they despised.

## Present House

- **Duke Gaius Medori** heads the family and duchy.
- **Lady Valeria Medori** manages diplomacy and political marriage.
- **Lord Matteo Medori** directs major investments.
- **Lord Fabrizio Medori** oversees logistics, shipping exposure, and protected routes.
- **Lady Isabella Medori** manages private archives, internal discipline, and sensitive instruments.

The family is not one mind. Gaius values dynastic stability, Matteo seeks returns, Fabrizio needs functioning routes, Valeria avoids coalitions against Fiorenzia, and Isabella protects secrets even when disclosure would help the Bank.

## Public Power

Medori loans reach courts, towns, guilds, armies, churches, and foreign houses. Patronage creates genuine art, hospitals, schools, and public works while binding recipients into political obligation. The family's power is greatest when clients continue functioning and weakest where war, repudiation, revolution, or rival credit destroys enforceability.

## Present Tensions

Old houses dispute purchased ancestry; Severin's memory stains the restored regime; debtors organize; foreign defaults rise; and younger Medori disagree over whether the family is a dynasty with a bank or a bank burdened by a duchy.
