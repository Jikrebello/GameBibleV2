# The Grand Duchy of Fiorenzia Overview

Fiorenzia is an inland highland duchy whose capital controls fertile river valleys, vineyards, estate towns, and the peninsula's deepest credit networks. It is a state with a real countryside, not merely a wealthy city wall.

## Formation

The river city predates Atheria. Imperial rule preserved its municipal houses while turning the surrounding valleys into taxable estates. During the Decadence, local families retained revenue and troops. Fiorenzia sealed the first defensive compact in 908 AR.

## Government and Citizenship

Duke **Gaius Medori** rules beside the patrician senate. Registered citizen households cast one vote through a recognized head of either sex. Senior office belongs to families in the Golden Book. The Medori converted financial dominance into formal ancestry by purchasing admission in 1238, a legal transaction older houses still call a counterfeit genealogy.

## Society and Faith

The capital's artists, scholars, factors, guilds, servants, laborers, and migrants depend on contado tenants and estate workers. Old-Church worship is public, splendid, and heavily patronized. Frate Severin's rule from 1346 to 1349 proved that Fiorenzian devotion cannot be dismissed as decoration.

## Economy and Defence

The [[Medori Bank]] connects small loans, sovereign debt, insurance, patronage, intelligence, and foreign infrastructure. Fiorenzia prefers hired companies, covert agents, and the [[Debtors’ Corps]] to a large standing army. During a Concord muster it supplies emergency credit, intelligence, and contracted troops.

The Bank has clients on opposing sides of wars, but it does not control every conflict or uniformly seek endless war. Different Medori offices profit from stability, reconstruction, victory, and prolonged debt in competing ways.

## Present Tensions

Golden Book resentment, Severin's memory, debts that never amortize, tenant volatility, foreign defaults, Medori succession, and the question of whether culture remains patronage when one family owns the patrons.
