# The Grand Duchy of Fiorenzia Overview

Fiorenzia is an inland highland duchy whose capital controls fertile river valleys, vineyards, estate towns, and the peninsula's deepest credit networks. It is a state with a real countryside, not merely a wealthy city wall.

## Formation

The river city predates Atheria. Imperial rule preserved its municipal houses while turning the surrounding valleys into taxable estates. During the Decadence, local families retained revenue and troops. Fiorenzia sealed the first defensive compact in 908 AR.

## Government and Citizenship

Duke **Gaius Medori** rules beside the patrician senate. Registered citizen households cast one vote through a recognized head of either sex. Senior office belongs to families in the Golden Book. The Medori converted financial dominance into formal ancestry by purchasing admission in 1238, when the Grey Sweat had emptied a third of the Book and the senate sold seats to keep a quorum; older houses still call the transaction a counterfeit genealogy.

## Society and Faith

The capital's artists, scholars, factors, guilds, servants, laborers, and migrants depend on contado tenants and estate workers. Old-Church worship is public, splendid, and heavily patronized. In 1345 a Sermoran pamphlet printed the names of Fiorenzian dead whose paid-for Sendings had never been sung, from the Chancery registers in the Medori vault. Frate Severin preached that the Medori held the city's dead in pawn and that the carnival masks had become doors for the Hungers, which are shut by naming, never by fire. The Medori lit the Pyre of Masks against his warning in 1346 and fled with the registers when the crowd turned on them. Severin never held office, but his Long Vigil from 1346 to 1349, when the whole city sang every name it could remember, proved that Fiorenzian devotion cannot be dismissed as decoration. It ended with Severin dead in a mask nobody could lift, and with the senate buying the city's register sheets back by making the ducal chair hereditary in the Medori line.

## Economy and Defence

The [[Medori Bank]] connects small loans, sovereign debt, insurance, patronage, intelligence, and foreign infrastructure. Fiorenzia prefers hired companies, covert agents, and the [[Debtors’ Corps]] to a large standing army. During a Concord muster it supplies emergency credit, intelligence, and contracted troops.

The Bank has clients on opposing sides of wars, but it does not control every conflict or uniformly seek endless war. Different Medori offices profit from stability, reconstruction, victory, and prolonged debt in competing ways.

## Present Tensions

Golden Book resentment, Severin's memory, debts that never amortize, tenant volatility, foreign defaults, Medori succession, and the question of whether culture remains patronage when one family owns the patrons.
