# The Free Port of Orsena

Orsena is a tiny neutral port at the eastern foot of the Vortharan Range, where the Eclessian road descends to the Thalassian. Its territory extends little beyond walls, docks, cistern ridge, signal forts, market gardens, and the road stations required to keep them alive.

## Formation

Atheria founded Orsena as a customs and naval station serving the border roads. Its registrars, pilots, and warehouse houses retained control during the Decadence. Independence came through administrative continuity rather than rebellion. Orsena sealed the defensive compact in 908.

## Government and Citizenship

**Harbor Rector Sabina Valeris** governs with the Harbor Book houses, water board, pilots, warehouse guilds, and recognized foreign quarters. Each registered household casts one vote through its head, regardless of sex. Three registered generations are required for rector, harbor judge, cistern board, and senior customs office.

## Neutrality

Orsena hosts prisoner exchanges, ransoms, covert negotiations, diplomatic residences, religious communities, and cargoes too politically dangerous for direct delivery. Neutrality is not moral approval: the port enforces declared sanctuary and exchange terms while refusing to ask why yesterday's enemies need the same warehouse today.

## Society and Economy

Foreign residents and transient crews outnumber citizens. Jirahar, Eclessian, Thalmyric, Covenant, Sermoran, Ättar, and other communities maintain compacted quarters. Ship repair, transshipment, storage, translation, exchange, pilotage, water, and intelligence sustain the city.

Food and fresh water are strategic vulnerabilities. Cistern law, grain reserves, waste control, fire watches, and harbor health receive harsher enforcement than doctrine or private vice.

## Defence and Present Tensions

Orsena maintains a small professional harbor militia, fixed rune batteries, chains, signal towers, and fortified cisterns. During a muster it supplies harbor access, repair, diplomacy, prisoner exchange, and intelligence.

Present conflicts include foreign pressure, citizen exclusion, water sabotage, smuggling through diplomatic houses, refugees denied permanent status, and whether neutrality protects peace or merely launders violence.
