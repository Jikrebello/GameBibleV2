# House of Soran

House Soran is Aversia's new princely dynasty, born from the marriage of a contract-captain family into a weakened Shield House and completed by civil war. Its authority rests on control of fortress roads and regular companies, but it must still bargain with mine charters, guild benches, service citizens, and old houses.

## Rise

Gaius Bellori built the disciplined Iron Wolves company. His son Valerius married Lady Elara Soran and took her older name, combining soldiers with a lawful claim to office. During Aversia's civil war, the new Sorans defeated Houses Verati and Carvonius, seized the princely office, and converted portions of the company into a regular force.

The victory was not clean. Executions, confiscations, and disputed service grants created exiles and martyrs. The Sorans won government; they did not erase the institutions that make Aversia function.

## Present House

- **Duke Marcus Soran** balances industry, defence, and the fear that his dynasty still looks temporary.
- **General Aurelia Soran** commands the regular field forces and prioritizes readiness over commercial convenience.
- **Lord Valerius Soran II**, Master of Contracts, manages company agreements, supply, and foreign clients.
- **Lady Octavia Soran** handles diplomacy and intelligence, often learning truths the family would rather negotiate without.
- **Lady Elena Soran** maintains alliances among civic houses and mining interests.

## Rule and Limits

The duke appoints commanders and directs foreign policy. The Shield Houses retain senior magistracies, while guild and service benches control revenue, technical labor, and portions of the franchise. A Soran order that closes mines, breaks contracts, or ignores earned citizenship can still provoke lawful resistance.

## Present Tensions

Marcus needs legitimacy; Aurelia needs dependable forces; Valerius needs profitable contracts; Octavia needs freedom to manage dangers before they become public. Their interests overlap without making the family a single mind. Verati and Carvonius survivors, real or invented, remain useful banners for opposition.
