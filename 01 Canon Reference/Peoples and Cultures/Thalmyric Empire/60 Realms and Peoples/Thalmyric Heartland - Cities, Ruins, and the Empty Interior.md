# Thalmyric Heartland - Cities, Ruins, and the Empty Interior

Thalmyria is simultaneously the most urbanized old country in the known world and one of its emptiest settled interiors. Population gathers around surviving walls, restored aqueducts, imperial roads, monasteries, ports, and garrison markets. Between them lie terraces without villages, ghost roads, ruined estates, and valleys whose legal owners have been dead for centuries.

## Why the Cities Endured

The invasion drove urban populations into rural and mountain refuge. Occupation authorities then concentrated grain, water, law, and military protection around selected towns. Reconquest governments repeated the pattern because defended cities could absorb returnees whose former villages were occupied, destroyed, or legally inaccessible.

Cities offer guild work, Church charity, military pay, courts, hospitals, and reliable water. A rural family may retain an ancestral field while spending most of the year in a city district built from refugees of the same province.

## The Empty Interior

The interior is not wholly deserted. Shepherds, monastery estates, charcoal burners, seasonal farmers, veteran colonies, and stubborn villages remain. What has vanished is the dense Atherian and early imperial settlement web.

Abandonment follows broken aqueduct branches, unsafe roads, disputed title, old siege belts, failed occupation colonies, and land too exposed to levy or raid. Qarath's Well and later civil wars emptied additional districts but deepened an older pattern.

## Ruins in Ordinary Life

A farmer may turn up a corroded sword, sling bullet, arrowhead, coin, roof tile, grave marker, or piece of star-temple mosaic in an ordinary season. Builders routinely quarry old walls. Parish churches keep baskets for objects found in fields until an assessor visits.

Intact relics, sealed crypts, working mechanisms, and supernatural sites are rare. Their rarity gives them value; their presence beneath nearly every old settlement gives stories somewhere to begin.

## City Layers

Caleran reuses Atherian forums, converted temples, occupation courts, Reconquest walls, and modern pilgrimage districts. Veridun overlays refuge caves, early churches, imperial palaces, and dense living neighborhoods. Virelos preserves rebellion tunnels and coronation houses. Regional capitals display the same layering at smaller scale.

## Land and Memory

An empty village can have several living claimants: a return family, an occupation-descended household, a monastery holding an emergency charter, a veteran line, and the Crown. Rural decline cannot be reversed merely by ordering people home because no authority agrees where home legally is.

## Story Use

Use the heartland for excavation beneath occupied houses, inheritance disputes around ghost villages, restored aqueduct journeys, illegal quarrying, veteran colonies, monastery archives, and urban districts whose residents still identify with a village none has seen.
