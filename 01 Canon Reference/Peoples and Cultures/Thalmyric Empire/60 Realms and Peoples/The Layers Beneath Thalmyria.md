# The Layers Beneath Thalmyria

Thalmyric ruins belong to identifiable historical strata. A ruin should reveal who built it, who reused it, why it was abandoned, and which later people altered the site.

## First Layer: Hill Cities

Pre-Atherian walls, ancestor chambers, cisterns, and shrines lie beneath later cities and isolated heights. Their law and religion predate the Star synthesis. Menhir influence, Giant survivals, and early warlock experiments appear here but must be established site by site.

## Second Layer: Republic and Stars

Republican roads, senate houses, legion forts, observatories, baths, aqueducts, and Star Pantheon temples form the most visible ancient layer. Many were reused continuously. Sealed temple basements may preserve Idol architecture, forbidden augury, or civic archives rather than treasure.

## Third Layer: Converted Empire

Early churches occupy former temples; martyr crypts run beneath public squares; saint houses, hospitals, and icon workshops fill converted districts. Pagan and Redeemer-era masonry often share one wall.

## Fourth Layer: Fall and Occupation

Burned gates, collapsed siege tunnels, contract courts, golem magazines, name vaults, impounded shrines, and occupation housing belong to 905 onward. Some systems remain legally or metaphysically active because their terminating contracts were never executed.

## Fifth Layer: Reconquest

Mountain redoubts, hidden lamp chambers, return forts, siege works, execution pits, amnesty records, and icon caches belong to the Long Reconquest. A site can preserve both liberation and atrocity in adjacent rooms.

## Sixth Layer: Qarath and Civil War

Abandoned estates, blindings, faction crypts, emergency mints, and fortified monasteries mark the 1214-1225 collapse and its aftermath. These sites are younger but often better armed and more politically dangerous.

## Antiquities Law

The Crown claims state archives, imperial arms, mints, strategic works, and anything affecting public defense. The Church claims relics, martyr sites, consecrated burials, and liturgical objects. Landholders claim ordinary material. Licensed finders receive declared shares after assessment.

No category is clean. A saint may have died in a state prison; a military archive may sit beneath a church; an ordinary coin may carry an active contract. Crown assessors, bishops, landowners, cities, and finders therefore litigate constantly.

## Field Standard

Every ruin dossier should record strata, visible signs, present owner, legal claimants, known hazards, prior excavation, recoverable knowledge, supernatural status, and at least three story uses.
