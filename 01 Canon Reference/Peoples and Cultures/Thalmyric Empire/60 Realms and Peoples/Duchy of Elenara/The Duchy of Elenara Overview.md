# The Duchy of Elenara Overview

Elenara is the central river and monastery duchy: vineyards, grain terraces, market towns, hospitals, archive houses, and abandoned estates beneath green hills. Its living culture grew from river-valley villages, pastoral uplands, monastery estates, and Atherian provincial law.

## Historical Formation

Rural and monastic refuges absorbed urban families after 905. Occupation rule concentrated around river estates and irrigation works. Return claimants later found both surviving villages and imposed populations in possession of their land.

Elenara remains a duchy because its prince's census was stopped. Around 1171–1173 the prince ordered a count of his own, announced as grain relief and meant to seize dependants for road labour, list refugees for tax and choose hostages, since a complete register would prove him fit for a royal crown. Clerks and soldiers broke down doors and dragged families to the counting stations. A mortality followed the census roads, and witnesses saw a dark raptor filled with white fire above the leading edge of each road. The prince confessed what the census had been for, released the hostages, burned the schedule and came barefoot to a bereaved household's threshing floor at **Kremna**, and the dying stopped there. Court histories call it plague and devotional Lives a commissioned angel. Thalmyric law still requires a census to state its purpose, a limit jurists call "the floor beyond the count", and after communal threshing in the river country one circle of grain is left uncounted and handed out by hand. ## Government

Duchess Mirena Ielen rules with town councils, landed houses, bishops, and powerful monasteries. Imperial return law and Church charters give almost every major estate several plausible histories.

## Settlement and Economy

Cities and monastery towns grow while high terraces empty. Wine, grain, fruit, medicine, manuscripts, wool, mills, and pilgrimage hospitality support the duchy. Abandoned aqueduct branches make some fertile valleys unusable.

## Faith and Ruins

Monasteries preserve icons, seed, occupation testimony, and records that can overturn noble title. Beneath churches lie Atherian villas, martyr rooms, and hidden lamp caches from the Breaking and the Impounding.

## Present Conflicts

Archive access, monastery taxation, cityward migration, ruin ownership, noble debt, and whether empty estates should go to veterans, return claimants, or current tenants.
