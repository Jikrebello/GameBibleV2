# The Kingdom of Veridun Overview

The Kingdom of Veridun is the imperial core containing three centers no ruler has successfully collapsed into one: the court at Veridun, the Patriarchate and Eternal Ember at Caleran, and the coronation city of Virelos.

## Historical Layers

The district contains pre-Atherian hill sites, the old imperial capital, converted temples, martyr crypts, occupation works, mountain refuges, Reconquest roads, and living cities. No other region possesses comparable density.

Caleran fell in 905 and returned in 957. Veridun survived as refuge and became the permanent imperial capital. Virelos gave the resistance a crown.

## Government

Caedros IV rules from Veridun through Grand Chancellor Prokopia and the imperial ministries. Patriarch Sophron governs the Church from Caleran. Each city has old privileges, landed houses, guilds, monasteries, and archives capable of resisting both.

## Settlement

Veridun is crowded with court households, officials, soldiers, students, petitioners, and refuge-descended districts. Caleran combines pilgrimage, Church government, archaeology, ports, hospitals, and old-city neighborhoods. Rural belts between restored roads contain abandoned terraces and disputed return villages.

## Economy

Administration, pilgrimage, education, antiquities, vineyards, grain, metalwork, construction, and military supply dominate. Ancient aqueducts make dense settlement possible and expensive to maintain.

## Faith

The imperial court, Holy Synod, monasteries, and local cults possess overlapping authority. The Great Lamp and Virelian Lamp remain at Veridun; the Eternal Ember and Pillar remain at Caleran.

## Present Conflicts

- The succession of Alexane, Sophios, or an adopted military heir.
- Whether Sophron will bless a Zanthir campaign.
- Old Caleran houses seeking control over the refuge-born court.
- Archaeological claims beneath occupied neighborhoods.
- Rural districts demanding investment from cities built on their taxes.

## Story Uses

Court intrigue, sacred archaeology, city underlayers, return law, imperial ceremonies, relic disputes, aqueduct failures, and succession conspiracies.
