# Caleran and the Eternal Ember

Caleran is the old capital of Atheria and the present seat of the Patriarch of Veridun. Its sacred precinct grew around the memory of the early martyrs and the Eternal Ember, a sanctuary flame kindled from the lamp of Amaryah's household at Veridun Minor.

## The Recovered City

Caleran lies on the northwestern lowland coast at the Aurin's mouth. Atherian forums, baths, palaces, temples, aqueducts, and road monuments remain embedded in the modern city. Converted churches, occupation courts, Reconquest walls, and pilgrimage districts occupy the same ground.

The city fell in 905 and returned in 957. It never again became the ordinary imperial court because Veridun's refuge government had become indispensable and the return remained incomplete.

Present-day Caleran has approximately 350,000 permanent residents. Major pilgrimages can raise the population toward 450,000. Fourteen civic districts spread across the old river channels, sacred core, occupation quarter, working wards, ports, walls, and expanding suburbs.

## The Eternal Ember

After the gift of the Holy Flame at Second Qiryath, Solana took a live coal from the first congregation's hearth to Veridun Minor. Amaryah lit her lamp from it. Her house gave travellers shelter and food, and Calira died defending it in 29 AR. Neighbouring households took turns tending the lamp after Amaryah's death. Each keeper lit a fresh wick from the old one before taking it away.

After Cassian legalised the faith in 292 AR, the congregation at Caleran could publicly commemorate Jareth, who died in the Celestial Forum in 41 AR, and the other martyrs of the capital. Cassian granted the former forum precinct to the congregation and paid for a basilica. Keepers from Veridun Minor brought a daughter flame and the vessel venerated as Solana's jar, while the household lamp remained at Veridun Minor. The Caleran flame became known as the Eternal Ember. The Velian Church claims an older daughter of Amaryah's lamp: Qabir's flame, carried from the bread oven at Veridun Minor in the winter of 248, which its deacons refused to relight from the Ember's daughter at Halmenth in 411. The Ember is a tended flame with a continuous line of kindling. Its keepers supply fuel and replace wicks. The White Flame can act through the relic by commission. Custody gives the Patriarch responsibility for the lamp and its worship, while the divine gift remains free.

Idris impounded the Ember in 905 AR, and it burned under Covenant guard for fifty-two years.

### Under Covenant guard, 905–957

In the first week of the occupation, soldiers tried to put the Ember out. The keepers' ledger records water, sand and a wet cloak, and the flame burning on. Idris had never come to Caleran; his ruling arrived by courier from Iqashar weeks later. The lamp was neither holy nor his own but undecided. Under the Covenant law of deposit, a thing whose ownership no mortal court can settle is held by a keeper until a competent judge decides, and Idris named the final audit, the judgement at the end of days, as that judge. The keepers pledged their own names as surety that the lamp would be neither divided, removed nor put out. A deposit is kept where it stands and open to view, so the basilica around it was never closed; its clergy sang the liturgy for fifty-two years with a Covenant guard at the door.

Their ledger runs day by day for fifty-two years: oil, wicks, the watch, and every attempt on the lamp. It records a drunken sergeant with a bucket, a zealot who tried to carry the lamp off to the north, Caleran's own faithful, who tried more than once to take a coal from it, and in its last spring the amir himself. Because the instrument was addressed to the final audit and not to Idris, it was the one instrument of his that still held in 931, when his heir had refused his instruments, he himself had died and his army's oaths lapsed. Three generations of keepers answered to it rather than to the amir who governed the city around them. When Amir Durayd offered in 948 to return the Ember for payment of its account, Caedros I's court refused to acknowledge any Covenant custody.

No lamp could lawfully be lit from the Ember while the deposit stood. A Thalmyric tradition says one keeper nonetheless let a deacon light a single lamp from it every Feast of the Mother, and the ledger shows a second measure of oil on each of those feasts with no entry to explain it. Covenant jurists deny that any lamp was lit.

The ledger's last entries are the fullest. In the spring of 957, with the occupied quarters in revolt, Amir Durayd sent his household guard to carry the lamp to his ships as the price of a welcome at Iqashar. The keepers, Covenant men of the third generation, barred the basilica against their own amir. The guard broke in, and eleven keepers died at the lamp before the funeral crowds and the deacons drove the guard out; the ledger names each of them and records that the oil was replenished that night as usual. The dockers burned Durayd's ships that night, and he was taken in the harbour fort nineteen days later. The surviving keepers held the forum precinct until the Thalmyric heralds had heard their terms, which named the garrison families their amir had shut out and the surrendered quarters under protection and asked payment of the Ember's account.

Veridun's payment in 957 discharged the deposit and released the keepers' names. Covenant jurists cite it as proof that even the People of the Lamp honour the law of deposit. Thalmyric memory holds that the payment acknowledged preservation and nothing more, though Caleran's clergy pray each spring for the eleven keepers by name. The ledger itself is shown in Caleran's Impounded Ledger Gallery.

## The Pillar of Concordance

Atherian officers once read judgements at the Pillar in the Celestial Forum. The nails from Isfir's execution, sent to Caleran in the governor's case chest as proof of the sentence, were leaded into the Pillar by the Purge commission in the year it burned Jareth there (c. 41 AR), as a monument to a sentence carried out; Cassian's grant of the precinct (c. 300–310) carried Pillar and nails to the Church. The church kept the monument within the converted precinct, where pilgrims remember the officials who condemned the first witnesses and the people who died there. Caedros I died at the Pillar during the recovery of Caleran. Thalmyric witnesses reported that the White Flame descended on him. Eclessian relic courts continue to dispute the evidence for his canonization.

## The Patriarchal Seat

Patriarch Sophron and the Holy Synod govern from the Cathedral of the Eternal Ember. The title “of Veridun” preserves the refuge succession. Major synods alternate between Caleran and Veridun when crown and Church require mutual presence.

## Below Caleran

Every building project risks opening an older city. Known underlayers include hill-city walls, senate tunnels, martyr crypts, Star temples, name vaults, siege works, sealed occupation courts, and forgotten aqueduct branches. They lie in pockets and broken runs, each dug for its own age.

Most of what diggers find is masonry, records, graves and broken tools. An intact relic or a working ancient system is a rare and dangerous find.
