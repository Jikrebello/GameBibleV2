# Historical Atlas of Thalmyria

Thalmyria is the western successor empire formed by Atherian heartland survivors, mountain refugees, occupation-born families and the armies of the Long Reconquest. Its history centres on the recovery of Caleran, the creation of a new imperial house and later rivalry with Eclessia and the Ahl-e-Ahd. The empire of 1360 governs the oldest concentration of Atherian infrastructure while carrying unresolved memories of occupation, religious division and the violence of restoration.

## Historical Atlas — wider inheritance

Thalmyrics inherit Atherian heartland and maritime traditions through refugees, provincial survivors, occupation-born populations and Reconquest settlers. Mountain refuge and the founding wars made a new imperial identity from these layers.

Caedros I's coronation in 922, the Burning Banners and recovery of Caleran in 957 stood at the centre of the restoration. The Long Reconquest continued to 1052; Zanthir and the Velian States remained outside. Returning households encountered real inhabitants whose rights could not be erased merely by calling the conquest holy.

Victory put the Fire back into endowed office, and the war-lamps that had burned before Caleran guttered again. In 982 a bought chaplain's lamp stayed dark on the gate of Anavra while Meliton's riders burned the town, and the survivors broke it on their bishop's doorstep. The Breakers went from parish to parish asking every lamp to burn for them and smashing the ones that did not; the soldiers sent against them broke their own war-lamps and acclaimed their commander, Tavoran III (984), who hooded the Great Lamp of Veridun in bronze to keep it from the question (986). Households hid their lamps in wells and ovens; the Reconquest slowed; reconquered valleys lost the monks who buried their dead; and the potter-monk Dovrin stamped the answer on the feet of clay lamps at Keramion. The Breaking faded after 1009, when a monk whose lamp had kindled in a hospice door became Patriarch Makarios and taught the Breakers that there is no test. In 1015 Aurelium demanded that the hood be lifted and the Great Lamp examined; Makarios nailed it shut, and the churches split. Tavoran IV opened crown lamp-houses to shelter lamps from the last Breakers, and made them compulsory after one lamp burned to death the officer who forced it before Meliton (1029). Their keepers sold the oil. In the dry summers of 1046–1048 families lifted hidden lamps from their sunken wells, some still burning, and went to the lamp-houses to ask for the rest; the houses were cold (1049). Families walked to Caleran for a light from the Ember and were robbed and killed on the amirate roads, and when the last southern amirate surrendered in 1052 every lamp in the empire was relit from one flame carried on foot from it, the old emperor walking the first mile barefoot. That night Makarios drew his nails and the Great Lamp was found burning under its hood. The Feast of the Rekindling keeps that night. In 1061 the cold hearth-lamp of a burned Vesari hall lit itself in Veridun's cathedral in its fire-keeper's hands, and Patriarch Makarios would not let it go home; Virelia chose the Sanctum, and the Virelian Lamp still hangs beside the Great Lamp.

The Two Winters of 1101–1103 emptied the northern frontier. Caedros III resettled it as military districts, each holding owing a lance and a door-lamp lit from the Rekindling relay, and the district lamps kindled when their families held their houses against the Covenant's mercy towers in the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139). Caedros spent the Covenant's ransom and then Sestaran loans on the golden age that followed. To bar his own son, who had cut down surrendered men at Vresna, he left his succession to the Fire; at his funeral in 1159 no claimant's lamp kindled, the son's flared with bought script-fire and the court acclaimed him on it, a district widow's lamp took the Flame in the nave, and after three years of war the district armies made their own commander emperor and set the son to keep the graves at Vresna. A census meant to make Elenara's prince a king was stopped at a threshing floor at Kremna in 1171–1173. At Qarath's Well in 1214 Markellos's army drank from a sworn well and was routed, and at sunset the well named its own price in wet letters on the curb: a well for every cup. What followed was a domestic crime after an external defeat. The emperor came home too sick to hold a pen and would not give up the aqueduct the Covenant claimed for his cup; his uncle Sergian pressed the emperor's seal to a decree taking the farms of every district holder who had drunk; Markellos left his own palace for a Kedravan monastery; and the Covenant held the debt against the Throne and took the aqueduct anyway. The War of the Seal ran until 1225, when Markellos poured out a cup of water at Virelos and gave his claim to Nikandros, the Kedravan who had read the well-curb aloud and brought his contingents home.

In 1227 the Sanctum offered Nikandros the Restorer the Crown of Embers, Eclessian soldiers and the valleys Caldria had seized if he would let its relic court try the Great Lamp; he refused, and a decade later won back half the lost districts when plague-stricken Covenant garrisons, their oaths left without witnesses, walked home. The Grey Sweat reached Thalmyria by sea around 1234. Where priests died, villagers sang their own dead out, and the empire came through with fewer haunted villages than Eclessia and a hardened belief that the Flame answers the singer, not the office. Evandros the Sower resettled the emptied land with Kedravan mountain families, and Stavren the Patient, who came late to the throne, kept twenty years of peace on Sestaran credit. When Covenant night riders on Iron Horde horses struck the chapel-forts, a provincial Aetherion commander named Caedros held Orinel (1318), led the Crimson Pilgrimage and took Zehrim (1325). The summer flux of 1327 killed Stavren's sons, and the old emperor adopted the hero of Zehrim. In 1360 Caedros IV is widowed and has buried his elder son; his daughter, his scholar son and the adoption party divide the court; and he kneels for a day at a time before the Great Lamp while the court watches the flame.
