# Historical Atlas of Thalmyria

Thalmyria is the western successor empire formed by Atherian heartland survivors, mountain refugees, occupation-born families and the armies of the Long Reconquest. Its history centres on the recovery of Caleran, the creation of a new imperial house and later rivalry with Eclessia and the Ahl-e-Ahd. The empire of 1360 governs the oldest concentration of Atherian infrastructure while carrying unresolved memories of occupation, religious division and the violence of restoration.

## Historical Atlas — wider inheritance

Thalmyrics inherit Atherian heartland and maritime traditions through refugees, provincial survivors, occupation-born populations and Reconquest settlers. Mountain refuge and the founding wars made a new imperial identity from these layers.

Caedros I's coronation in 922, the Burning Banners and recovery of Caleran in 957 stood at the centre of the restoration. The Long Reconquest continued to 1052; Zanthir and the Velian States remained outside. Returning households encountered real inhabitants whose rights could not be erased merely by calling the conquest holy.

Victory put the Fire back into endowed office, and the war-lamps that had burned before Caleran guttered again. After the Ashfall Omen of 983–984, when Mount Askre buried Veridun in ash and the Great Lamp shrank to a bead for forty days, the soldier-emperor Leovar III broke the empire's lamps (987). Households hid them in wells and ovens; the Reconquest slowed; reconquered valleys lost the monks who buried their dead; and the exiled monk Ioan stamped the answer on the feet of clay lamps in Velian kilns. In the dry summers after 1008 families deepening their wells lifted the hidden lamps, some still burning, and Leovar III died of the flux from a drought well (1011). His son hesitated, and in 1013 the households carried a thousand lamps into Veridun and took the hood off the Great Lamp themselves. Within two years Aurelium's demand to examine the restored lamps produced the Great Split (1015). Leovar IV, who had let the lamps back in, locked every one in crown lamp-houses after one burned to death the officer who forced it before Meliton (1029). Their keepers sold the oil, and when the houses were opened at his funeral in 1049 the empire's household fires were dead. Families walked to Caleran for a light from the Ember, and when the last southern amirate surrendered in 1052 Theodosia had every lamp in the empire relit from one flame carried on foot from it. The Feast of the Rekindling keeps that night. In 1061 the cold hearth-lamp of a burned Vesari hall lit itself in Veridun's cathedral in its fire-keeper's hands, and Patriarch Makarios would not let it go home; Virelia chose the Sanctum, and the Virelian Lamp still hangs beside the Great Lamp.

The Two Winters of 1101–1103 emptied the northern frontier. Caedros III resettled it as military districts, each holding owing a lance and a door-lamp lit from the Rekindling relay, and the district lamps kindled when their families held their houses against the Covenant's mercy towers in the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139). Caedros spent the Covenant's ransom and then Sestaran loans on the golden age that followed. To bar his own son, who had cut down surrendered men at Vresna, he left his succession to the Fire; at his funeral in 1167 no claimant's lamp kindled, the son's flared with bought script-fire and his rivals blinded him, a district widow's lamp took the Flame in the nave, and the district armies made their own commander emperor. A census meant to make Elenara's prince a king was stopped at a threshing floor at Kremna in 1171–1173. At Qarath's Well in 1214 Markellos's army drank from a sworn well and was routed, and the emperor who drank last with his men was sent home as a guest with a bill for the water: the Southern Deeds districts. What followed was a domestic crime after an external defeat. His uncle Sergian deposed him to make the bill his private debt and took the farms of every district holder who had drunk, and the Covenant held the bill against the Throne anyway. Civil war ran until 1225, when Markellos poured out a cup of water at Virelos and gave his claim to Nikandros, the Kedravan who had read the well-curb aloud and brought his contingents home.

Nikandros the Restorer refused in 1227 to let the Sanctum try the Great Lamp as the price of Eclessian soldiers, and a decade later won back half the lost districts when plague-stricken Covenant garrisons, their oaths left without witnesses, walked home. The Grey Sweat reached Thalmyria by sea around 1234. Where priests died, villagers sang their own dead out, and the empire came through with fewer haunted villages than Eclessia and a hardened belief that the Flame answers the singer, not the office. Evandros the Sower resettled the emptied land with Kedravan mountain families, and Stavren the Patient kept thirty years of peace on Sestaran credit. When Covenant night riders on Iron Horde horses struck the chapel-forts, a provincial Aetherion commander named Caedros held Orinel (1318), led the Crimson Pilgrimage and took Zehrim (1325). The summer flux of 1327 killed Stavren's sons, and the old emperor adopted the hero of Zehrim. In 1360 Caedros IV is widowed and has buried his elder son; his daughter, his scholar son and the adoption party divide the court; and he kneels for a day at a time before the Great Lamp while the court watches the flame.
