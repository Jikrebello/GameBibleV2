# The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)

The Long Reconquest was the 144-year struggle by which the mountain refuges became the Thalmyric Empire and recovered the core Atherian heartland. Caleran's return in 957 was its sacred midpoint. The refugee league won it against an enemy whose own history turned against it: the Qahirid army's oaths lapsed when Idris's heir refused them and Idris died, the occupied south passed to an amir who swore it to himself and ended as Thalmyria's prisoner, and from 951 Iqashar was fighting Tarkhan riders on its northern wells. It won much of the heartland at the canal heads in its own hills.

## Flight and Occupation, 905-908 AR

The fall of Caleran scattered the heartland. Urban households fled toward mountain monasteries, rural estates, fortified valleys, eastern ports, and Eclessia. Covenant authorities secured roads, ports, irrigation works, and archives before attempting complete territorial control. The last emperor, Corvenas III, had surrendered himself and his relics by the Instrument of the Ash Hall and lived on in Caleran as a prisoner, and the refuge councils governed in his name until he died in the winter of 908.

Occupation repopulation mixed garrison households, rewarded clients, contract-bound survivors, imported labourers, and forcibly transplanted subjects. Land and water rights were rewritten into Covenant instruments. This policy created functioning occupied districts while ensuring that later return would never be a simple restoration.

The occupation had weak foundations from its first winter. It lay a full season's march from Iqashar and was meant to pay for itself from estates worked by bound survivors whose names sat in local name vaults. The Atherian remount studs its clerks had listed for seizure were driven north into the Tarkhan grass by their handlers, and the occupation stayed an army of camels and foot.

## Mountain Rebellion, 908-921 AR

The captive emperor's death freed the refuge to raise a standard of its own. That summer Caedros Aetherion joined surviving soldiers, refuge councils, monasteries, village militias, and dispossessed houses. The resistance initially fought through ambush, evacuation, road denial, and the preservation of people rather than territory. Two years earlier the refuge had survived its first siege when the hearth-keepers of Veridun Minor baked the city's flour instead of burning it and the smoke of their ovens lay over the valley for five days ([[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun.md|the Mother's Veil]]).

The Covenant army's defeat at [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)]] had already ended its Eclessian advance, destroyed two of five great engines, crippled a third, and forced occupation forces to cover too many roads with too few machines. Knowledge recovered from a released human name-cage became part of the rebels' later counter-engine practice.

The rebels' amnesty did as much damage as their ambushes. Anyone who came up to the mountains without a bound instrument was received, and the labour that paid the garrisons drained into the hills a family at a time. Some occupation governors answered with terror. At one mountain signal house, about 910–913, the devotional tradition says a governor's warlocks trapped and maimed a Herald of heaven sent to proclaim a protected surrender; from the failed rescue came the horn players' rule of naming the protected before dividing spoils ([[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun.md|The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun]]).

[[The Battle of Caelmount]] in the winter of 917–918 proved a Covenant field force could be destroyed in the mountains. Amir Qazem's column reached the valley's store caves and lay down in them out of the snow; the cave-wardens had shut the vents that kept the mountain's bad air out of the galleries, and the column died there of the air, the cold and the desertion of its levies. The valley could never store bread in those caves again. Caedros promised to feed it until it had granaries, and in 921 he took Virelos and its granaries to keep the promise, giving the rebellion a defensible political centre as well.

## Crown and Burning Banners, 922-949 AR

Caedros was crowned at Virelos in 922. The [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The War of the Burning Banners.md|War of the Burning Banners]] brought independent refuge armies, monastery leagues, and noble claimants under one crown. The result was a shared command, tax, and return policy.

The enemy changed during these years. Idris al-Fattah had bound his army and the surrendered cities to himself by name, with his heir written in as the next holder. The heir refused to take the instruments up in 929 and Idris died in 931, and every one of those oaths lapsed together. The Covenant rebuilt its throne as the Sublime Threshold, but the occupied south went its own way: Durayd ibn Sahl, amir of Caleran, swore its cities and garrisons to himself, and Iqashar refused him engines until he swore them back. For the rest of the Banners war Thalmyria faced a regime with walls, garrison families and no reinforcements. [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]] gives the Covenant's side.

## The Caleran Campaign, 950-962 AR

The Caleran campaign produced the first large body of disputed reports concerning White Flame manifestations in battle. Church authorities distinguish these rare events from the rune-script Shell-fire used by imperial warlocks. Caleran was recovered in 957. Caedros I died at the Pillar of Concordance as witnesses reported the White Flame descending; Caedros II completed the immediate campaigns by 962.

Iqashar's army was absent throughout. In 951 Grass-Tarkhan riders on the hymn-blessed descendants of the lost Atherian studs broke through the Covenant's hound-guarded northern wells, and the field army spent the campaign on the grass. Iqashar's one contribution, its last Slave-Soul engine, came south by sea in 955; Thalmyric war-lamps opened its name-cage before Caleran's outer works in 956. In 957 a funeral riot spread through the occupied districts. Durayd, certain that no relief was coming, sent his guard to carry the Ember to his ships; its Covenant keepers held the lamp against him at the cost of eleven of their own, the dockers burned his ships, and he was taken in the harbour fort nineteen days later. The parishes opened the river gate, and the surviving keepers opened the forum precinct only on terms that named the garrison families he had shut out and the surrendered quarters under protection.

The Eternal Ember had remained lit under Covenant guard and property law. Veridun paid the occupation ledger in full, preserving both the Ember and the humiliating fact that its enemy had kept the account.

## Province by Province, 962-1052 AR

The next ninety years were fought through roads, irrigation districts, walled towns, marriage settlements, amnesties, sieges, and competing land registers. Recovery of the Kedravan Frost Gate and the Arkenan Basalt Draw, beside the Virelos Wind Stair held since 921, gave Thalmyria the storm signals, cold-pressure, forge-charge and fortification work of three Shell anchors. Many returned families found their ancestral villages inhabited by other families with legal claims of their own. Some settlements negotiated continuity; others suffered expulsion, massacre, or repeated changes of ruler.

The war took this shape because the enemy had come apart and Thalmyria held the man who could finish taking it apart. The other occupied districts were sworn to Durayd by name, and Caedros II kept him in a walled house at Veridun until his death about 971 and had him sign their releases. An amir who took a release and terms opened his gates lawfully; an amir who refused swore his garrison to himself. Iqashar claimed them all and armed none of them. Most of the fields of those that held out drank from canals that came down from the hills, and Caedros II's engineers took the canal heads and set water-courts there; farmers who registered got their water, and amirs who would not register their districts were left holding walls above dry fields. River citadels and anchor works had to be besieged, and one hill citadel held out for eleven years on its cisterns.

The First and Second Breakings weakened the return by turning imperial force inward. The First Breaking took the lamps from the army and the funeral monks from the reconquered valleys; the Impounding sealed them in crown lamp-houses, where their keepers sold the oil and let them die; Theodosia's Rekindling relit them all from the Ember when the last amirate surrendered in 1052. The Covenant could not exploit the first. Its jurists let the Breaking run, sheltering exiled flame-monks in the Velian belt, where Ioan of Velia stamped the sayings that ended it on the feet of clay lamps; and from 1006 to 1011 its army could not march at all, because a lending house at Iqashar held the pledged true name of the Custodian of the Vault of Names, and through it the names of the Covenant's bound soldiers. During the second, Sultan-Caliph Tamim the Auditor wrote off the south: in 1033 his treasury declared the amirates claimed but no longer defended. A last relief army, sent in 1041 to the wrong well by a copyist's error, dispersed for want of water. In 1044, with the coast closed to its barges by Thalmyric squadrons, Zanthir withdrew its grain from the last amirates and kept its own autonomy.

Empress Theodosia inherited the cold lamp-houses in 1049 and secured the surviving core road network in 1052. The last amirate surrendered to her armies the same year. The Feast of the Rekindling commemorates religious restoration and the practical reconnection of the heartland.

## What Was Not Recovered

The Velian States remained fragmented beneath Covenant-aligned rulers, local churches, mixed communities, and resistance networks. Zanthir survived as an autonomous emirate through tribute and diplomacy. Its bargain of 1044, tribute to Veridun while the sea is open and to Iqashar while the caravans run, still governs its payments. Thalmyric maps call both unfinished provinces; their inhabitants do not agree.

## The Demographic Inheritance

Four generations of flight and return concentrated population around walls, markets, monasteries, garrisons, and restored aqueducts. Many villages never revived. Others carried occupation-descended populations that return law refused to recognise. Those who rejected return law altogether went north in 1052 as the Covenant's Southern Houses, carrying deeds that became the War of the Southern Deeds in 1131. The modern empty interior is therefore a political landscape.
