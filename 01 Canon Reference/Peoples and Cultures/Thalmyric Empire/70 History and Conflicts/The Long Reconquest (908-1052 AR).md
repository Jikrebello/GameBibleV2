# The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)

The Long Reconquest was the 144-year struggle by which the mountain refuges became the Thalmyric Empire and recovered the core Atherian heartland. Caleran's return in 957 was its sacred midpoint. The refugee league won it against an enemy whose own history turned against it: the Qahirid army's oaths lapsed with the deaths of Idris and his heir, the occupied south passed to an amir who swore it to himself, and from 951 Iqashar was fighting Tarkhan riders on its northern wells.

## Flight and Occupation, 905-908 AR

The fall of Caleran scattered the heartland. Urban households fled toward mountain monasteries, rural estates, fortified valleys, eastern ports, and Eclessia. Covenant authorities secured roads, ports, irrigation works, and archives before attempting complete territorial control.

Occupation repopulation mixed garrison households, rewarded clients, contract-bound survivors, imported labourers, and forcibly transplanted subjects. Land and water rights were rewritten into Covenant instruments. This policy created functioning occupied districts while ensuring that later return would never be a simple restoration.

The occupation had weak foundations from its first winter. It lay a full season's march from Iqashar and was meant to pay for itself from estates worked by bound survivors whose names sat in local name vaults. The Atherian remount studs its clerks had listed for seizure were driven north into the Tarkhan grass by their handlers, and the occupation stayed an army of camels and foot.

## Mountain Rebellion, 908-921 AR

Caedros Aetherion joined surviving soldiers, refuge councils, monasteries, village militias, and dispossessed houses. The resistance initially fought through ambush, evacuation, road denial, and the preservation of people rather than territory.

The Covenant army's defeat at [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)]] had already ended its Eclessian advance, destroyed two of five great engines, crippled a third, and forced occupation forces to cover too many roads with too few machines. Knowledge recovered from a released human name-cage became part of the rebels' later counter-engine practice.

The rebels' amnesty did as much damage as their ambushes. Anyone who came up to the mountains without a bound instrument was received, and the labour that paid the garrisons drained into the hills a family at a time. Some occupation governors answered with terror. At one mountain signal house, about 910–913, the devotional tradition says a governor's warlocks trapped and maimed a Herald of heaven sent to proclaim a protected surrender; from the failed rescue came the horn players' rule of naming the protected before dividing spoils ([[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun.md|The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun]]).

[[The Battle of Caelmount]] in 917 proved a Covenant field force could be defeated in the mountains. Virelos was secured in 921, giving the rebellion a defensible political centre.

## Crown and Burning Banners, 922-949 AR

Caedros was crowned at Virelos in 922. The [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The War of the Burning Banners.md|War of the Burning Banners]] brought independent refuge armies, monastery leagues, and noble claimants under one crown. The result was a shared command, tax, and return policy.

The enemy changed during these years. Idris al-Fattah had bound his army and the surrendered cities to himself by name, with his heir written in as the next holder. The heir drowned in 929 and Idris died in 931, and every one of those oaths lapsed together. The Covenant rebuilt its throne as the Sublime Threshold, but the occupied south went its own way: Durayd ibn Sahl, amir of Caleran, swore its cities and garrisons to himself, and Iqashar refused him engines until he swore them back. For the rest of the Banners war Thalmyria faced a regime with walls, garrison families and no reinforcements. [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]] gives the Covenant's side.

## The Caleran Campaign, 950-962 AR

The Caleran campaign produced the first large body of disputed reports concerning White Flame manifestations in battle. Church authorities distinguish these rare events from the rune-script Shell-fire used by imperial warlocks. Caleran was recovered in 957. Caedros I died at the Pillar of Concordance as witnesses reported the White Flame descending; Caedros II completed the immediate campaigns by 962.

Iqashar's army was absent throughout. In 951 Grass-Tarkhan riders on the hymn-blessed descendants of the lost Atherian studs broke through the Covenant's hound-guarded northern wells, and the field army spent the campaign on the grass. Iqashar's one contribution, its last Slave-Soul engine, came south by sea in 955; Thalmyric war-lamps opened its name-cage before Caleran's outer works in 956. In 957 a funeral riot in the occupied districts and the certainty that no relief was coming led Durayd to surrender Caleran in his own name, with the garrison families and the surrendered quarters named under protection.

The Eternal Ember had remained lit under Covenant guard and property law. Veridun paid the occupation ledger in full, preserving both the Ember and the humiliating fact that its enemy had kept the account.

## Province by Province, 962-1052 AR

The next ninety years were fought through roads, irrigation districts, walled towns, marriage settlements, amnesties, sieges, and competing land registers. Recovery of the Kedravan Frost Gate and the Arkenan Basalt Draw, beside the Virelos Wind Stair held since 921, gave Thalmyria the storm signals, cold-pressure, forge-charge and fortification work of three Shell anchors. Many returned families found their ancestral villages inhabited by other families with legal claims of their own. Some settlements negotiated continuity; others suffered expulsion, massacre, or repeated changes of ruler.

The war took this shape because the enemy had come apart. Durayd's surrender released Caleran and left the other occupied districts sworn to nobody; each amir re-swore his own garrison, and by 962 Thalmyria faced a dozen amirates, each with its own walls and its own price. Iqashar claimed them all and armed none of them. Some held out for a decade, some sold their towns for safe passage north, and a few changed sides more than once.

The First and Second Breakings weakened the return by turning imperial force inward. The First Breaking took the lamps from the army and the funeral monks from the reconquered valleys; the Impounding sealed them in crown lamp-houses; Theodosia's Rekindling released them when the last amirate surrendered in 1052. The Covenant could not exploit the first. Its jurists let the Breaking run, sheltering exiled flame-monks in the Velian belt, where Ioan of Velia wrote the formula that ended it; and from 1006 to 1011 its army could not march at all, because a lending house at Iqashar held the pledged true name of the Custodian of the Vault of Names, and through it the names of the Covenant's bound soldiers. During the second, Sultan-Caliph Tamim the Auditor wrote off the south: in 1033 his treasury declared the amirates claimed but no longer defended. A last relief army, sent in 1041 to the wrong well by a copyist's error, dispersed for want of water. In 1044 Zanthir withheld its fleet and grain from the last amirates and bought its own autonomy.

Empress Theodosia ended the Second Breaking and secured the surviving core road network in 1052. The last amirate surrendered to her armies the same year. The Feast of the Rekindling commemorates religious restoration and the practical reconnection of the heartland.

## What Was Not Recovered

The Velian States remained fragmented beneath Covenant-aligned rulers, local churches, mixed communities, and resistance networks. Zanthir survived as an autonomous emirate through tribute and diplomacy. Its bargain of 1044, tribute to Veridun and Iqashar in alternate seasons, still governs its payments. Thalmyric maps call both unfinished provinces; their inhabitants do not agree.

## The Demographic Inheritance

Four generations of flight and return concentrated population around walls, markets, monasteries, garrisons, and restored aqueducts. Many villages never revived. Others carried occupation-descended populations that return law refused to recognise. Those who rejected return law altogether went north in 1052 as the Covenant's Southern Houses, carrying deeds that became the War of the Southern Deeds in 1131. The modern empty interior is therefore a political landscape.
