# Challenges and Future Tensions

Thalmyria's strength and its dangers grow from the same inheritance: old institutions, sacred memory, recovered territory, a permanent frontier, and the visible remains of a greater empire beneath nearly every settled district. Most of its present quarrels can be dated. They come from the defeat at Qarath's Well in 1214 and the Restorer's refusal of 1227, from the Grey Sweat and the open gates that followed it, from the Crimson Pilgrimage and Zehrim, and from a summer fever in 1327 that ended one imperial line and made a frontier commander emperor.

## The Unsettled Crown

Caedros IV came to the throne by adoption. In 1327 the summer flux at Veridun killed both sons of the old emperor Stavren the Patient within a month, and in 1328 Stavren adopted the hero of Zehrim, a commander from a provincial branch of House Aetherion whose grandmother was a daughter of Nikandros the Restorer. The Caleran houses objected then and have not forgotten.

Caedros now has no living accepted heir. His wife Vasilia died in 1357, and his elder son Prince Nikandros, named for the Restorer, died in a skirmish in the Velian marches in 1358. Princess Alexane, who has governed a Vlastravan military district for a decade, Prince Sophios, the seminary-trained scholar, and an adopted commander each promise a different empire. The officers who favour adoption point out that it is how the present emperor was made. Since Vasilia's fortieth-day, Caedros has kept long silences before the Great Lamp of Veridun, and courtiers who say the flame stands still while he kneels are already arguing about what an heir named after such a silence would be worth. Older courtiers remember that the last emperor who sat up with a flame, Caedros III, left his succession to it and started the War of the Seven Lamps. Provincial rulers are turning appointments, marriages and military budgets into succession positions.

## Zanthir and the War Party

Nikandros's death gave renewed force to those demanding a final campaign. The frontier has had no treaty since Zehrim: Thalmyric emperors will not put their names into a Covenant instrument, and the Covenant makes no peace without one, so the peace is kept by fear, raids and arithmetic. Iron-Horde horsemen ride with Covenant companies on the far side; the Zanthiri emir pays tribute to Veridun and Iqashar in alternating seasons. Veterans who know the cost, frontier communities that would become the battlefield, and treasury officials who cannot finance it form an unstable peace coalition.

## The Empty Interior

The interior never recovered from the War of the Seal and the Sweat, which arrived by sea around 1234. Evandros the Sower spent his reign (1263–1290) resettling the districts Nikandros recovered and the villages the fever emptied, with veterans and Kedravan mountain families, and the work was never finished. Cities and fortified corridors grow while many villages shrink. Abandoned terraces, broken aqueduct branches, and unsafe roads surrender land to smugglers, local strongmen, and the dead. Resettlement schemes repeatedly underestimate how much infrastructure a village needs before families will stay.

## The Buried Empire

Imperial, Church, landholder, and finder claims overlap whenever antiquities emerge. Most finds are mundane; the rare archive, relic, weapon cache, or functioning system can redraw a title, embarrass a saint cult, bankrupt a noble house, or begin a dungeon expedition. The concessions pledged to Sestaran lenders since the Crimson Pilgrimage mean that some of what is dug up already belongs to a bank.

## Debt and Dependence

Sermoran and Medori loans paid for the Crimson Pilgrimage (1319–1322) and the siege of Zehrim (1324–1325), secured on Saronikan customs and antiquities concessions. Sestaran credit still maintains fleets, granaries, and reconstruction. It prevents immediate collapse while moving future customs revenue beyond imperial control. Prokopia's chancellery seeks solvency without allowing creditors to own the recovery.

## Crown and Church

The Holy Synod and emperor require one another but dispute appointments, military preaching, relic law, monastic land, and Caleran's influence over Veridun. Neither side is united: contemplatives, administrators, militants, provincial bishops, and court clergy form changing coalitions. Both remember 1227, when Nikandros the Restorer refused the Sanctum's offer of the Crown of Embers and Eclessian soldiers for a trial of the Great Lamp, and both took the Council of Veridance's five tests of 1324, none of which can be applied to a saint who left no remains, as Eclessia's answer to Caedros I. Any request for Eclessian help now has to be made past that history. The Synod has refused to authenticate the vision of a lamp carried out of a burning archive that every succession faction claims.

## Return and Belonging

Mixed occupation-descended communities remain vulnerable to purist politics. Among them are the people of Zehrim and the districts the Crimson Pilgrimage reached, whose oath-ledgers Caedros burned on the fort's altar in 1325. The Covenant's Court of Breaches ruled in 1326 that they remain bound in its registers at Iqashar, so any who cross north can be seized. Thalmyria calls them freed and has not decided whether they belong. The state must decide whether loyalty, law, ancestry, or suffering defines belonging, and Caedros's frontier amnesties are the first answer his successors will be asked to keep or tear up.
