# Cultural Identity and Rivalry

Thalmyric identity is built from faith, urban memory, imperial inheritance, mountain survival, and the conviction that old Atheria did not die: it retreated, endured, and returned. That story is powerful enough to unite very different regions and broad enough for each to tell it in its own favor.

## Who Counts as Thalmyric

A court family in Veridun, a vine grower in Elenara, a Vlastravan veteran, an Arkenan councillor, a Kedravan monastery village, and a Saltborn pilot may speak differently and remember different ancestors. They are joined by the imperial calendar, Church communion, Atherian legal inheritance, the memory of the Fall, and participation in the long work of return.

Mixed Velian families and descendants of occupation-era settlers remain the hardest test. Law can recognize their loyalty while neighbors still question their belonging. Some become fierce imperial patriots; others reject both empires' demand that they choose a single ancestry.

## Class and Place

- **Urban citizens** take pride in councils, guilds, schools, waterworks, and the ability to name every historical layer beneath their street.
- **Rural households** measure government by road safety, irrigation, tax collectors, and whether a distant claimant can seize a field because of an ancient title.
- **Veteran communities** see the empire as a compact earned through service and often distrust hereditary courtiers.
- **Monasteries** preserve local memory but may compete bitterly over relics, land, and the interpretation of return.
- **Old houses** treat genealogy as evidence; displaced families treat survival as evidence; archivists charge both for copies.

## Rivalries

### Eclessia

Eclessians are fellow heirs of the Redeemer but are stereotyped as fragmented, doctrinally argumentative, and too willing to let princes divide the Church. Eclessians answer that Thalmyria mistakes imperial custom for universal truth. Trade, pilgrimage, scholarship, and intermarriage make the rivalry intimate rather than foreign.

### Ahl-e-Ahd

The Covenant is remembered as conqueror, slaver, and architect of imposed metaphysical debt. This enmity is not invented. It becomes dangerous when officials transfer guilt from institutions and perpetrators to every northern merchant, convert, mixed family, or occupation-descended civilian.

### Sestara

Sestarans are admired for shipping, art, finance, and archival cunning, then accused of selling each back to its original owner at interest. Thalmyric rulers depend on Sestaran credit and resent the dependence. Sestarans consider Thalmyric pride an excellent collateral substitute until the treasury misses payment.

### Internal Regional Rivalry

Veridun calls itself practical; Caleran calls it spiritually provincial. Saronika mocks inland solemnity. Vlastrava calls the coast soft. Elenara considers both noisy. Arkena believes every serious argument requires a council. Kedrava remembers when all of them needed mountain shelter.

## Everyday Expression

Regional food ranges from oil, fish, greens, wine, and filled breads in the old cities to smoked meat, maize-like grains, beans, sour dairy, orchard fruit, and heavy stews in the uplands. Dress favors layered linen and wool, embroidered household or district patterns, bright feast garments, and practical dark outerwear. Public culture is expressive: argument, lament, hospitality, procession, and song are communal arts rather than breaches of dignity.
