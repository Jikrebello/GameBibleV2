# Thalmyric Canon Register

This is the creator-facing control sheet for Thalmyria. Authority descends through [[Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)]], [[Gazetteer of the Known World]], [[Present-Day Courts and Crowns (1360 AR)]], [[Named Figures of the Known World]], [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]], [[White Flame Doctrine]], this register, and then regional dossiers.

## Three Sacred Cities

- **Veridun** is the living imperial capital, refuge-city, coronation cathedral, and seat of [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/60 Realms and Peoples/Kingdom of Veridun/Emperor Caedros IV Aetherion.md|Emperor Caedros IV Aetherion]]. The Great Lamp and Virelian Lamp remain there. The Virelian Lamp is the dead hearth-lamp of Radomir I's burned hall at Strazgorod, which lit itself in the hands of his fire-keeper Zdena before Patriarch Makarios (1061); her descendants tend it in the cathedral close, and under Vesari hearth-law whoever keeps a house's fire is its lord. In 1227 the Sanctum offered Nikandros I the Crown of Embers, Eclessian soldiers and the three Caldrian valleys for a trial of the Great Lamp in its relic court; he refused: "a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned." The Great Lamp stood under a bronze hood from 986 to 1052, nailed shut by Patriarch Makarios against the Sanctum's examiners in 1015, and was found burning when the hood was lifted.
- **Caleran** is the recovered Atherian capital, site of Jareth's martyrdom, the Pillar of Concordance and the Eternal Ember, and present seat of Patriarch Sophron. The Ember was kindled from Amaryah's lamp at Veridun Minor. Idris impounded it in 905 as a deposit held for the final audit; soldiers could not put it out, its Covenant keepers guarded it, ledgered its fuel and in 957 held it against their own amir, and Veridun paid the invoice in full on the recovery of 957. The Rekindling (1052) relit every lamp in the empire from it, so Thalmyria's whole fire "burns on a receipt". The Redeemer's execution and Resurrection took place at Second Qiryath.
- **Virelos** is the mountain rebellion's cradle and the coronation city of the Aetherion line.

The title **Patriarch of Veridun** survives from the exile years. The Patriarchate returned to Caleran after the Reconquest without surrendering the title that legitimized the refuge Church.

## Fixed Historical Chain

Thalmyria's military provinces and soldier-tenure descend from the kindled marches of 612–905, each held under one charter-oath renewed by the emperor in person before a frontier witness on whom the Fire came. Caleran falls in 905, taken in Idris's name by the engine-master Barzin and the breach-court train after Corvenas III's public call on the Fire fails on the river dyke, and Corvenas III surrenders his person and relics and dies a captive in 908; Veridun survives the Veil in 906, the smoke of ovens baked against the council's order; the Mountain Rebellion runs 908-921; [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/70 History and Conflicts/The Battle of Caelmount.md|Caelmount]] (the Breath of Askre, 917–918) proves a Covenant field army can be destroyed in the mountains, at the price of the valley's granary; Caedros I takes Virelos in 921 to feed Caelmount and is crowned there in 922; the Burning Banners unify the refuges in 922-949; Caleran is recovered in 957, when the Ember's Covenant keepers hold it against Amir Durayd and he is taken alive, and Caedros I dies at the Pillar of Concordance; Caedros II completes the first coastal campaigns by 962; and [[The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)|the Long Reconquest]] takes the separately sworn amirates, some by releases signed in the captive Durayd's hand and most of the rest at their canal heads, until the Rekindling in 1052.

The restored church's lamps gutter again. The Breaking begins from below at Anavra (982): Breakers ask every lamp to burn for them and smash the ones that stay dark. Leovar III (984–1011) breaks his own war-lamp, takes back the sees' estates and hoods the Great Lamp to keep it from the question (986); Ioan of Velia's *Sayings for the Foot of a Lamp*, stamped on clay lamps at Keramion inside Thalmyria, survive in the rubble. The Breaking fades after Makarios, whose lamp kindled at Sarmene (1009), becomes Patriarch and teaches that there is no test. Leovar IV (1011–1055) opens crown lamp-houses as a refuge (1012) and makes them compulsory after his Master of Lamps is burned to death forcing a wayhouse lamp before Meliton (1029); the keepers sell the oil; in the dry years of 1046–1048 families lift hidden lamps from sunken wells, and the houses are opened and found cold (1049). Every lamp is relit from the Ember (1052), Leovar IV walking the first mile barefoot; the hood is lifted and the Great Lamp found burning. Theodosia the Kindler reigns 1055–1076. There is no First or Second Breaking, no Night of the Thousand Lamps and no eruption omen.

Caedros III (1121–1159), married and godfather to the districts, settles frontier land emptied by the Two Winters as military districts owing a lance and a lamp, and wins the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139) when the district lamps kindle in defence of their homes. To bar his son Orestan after the killings at Vresna, he leaves his succession to the Fire (the Kolonna Declaration, 1139); at the Unanswered Funeral (1159) no claimant's lamp kindles, the court acclaims Orestan on a counterfeit flare, and the district armies take Despina of Kolonna's kindled lamp as their warrant and beat him by 1162. Nobody is blinded; Orestan keeps a chapel at Vresna. At Qarath's Well (1214) Markellos's army drinks from a sworn well whose guest-peace turns the water in men who take up arms against their host; at sunset the well's bound warden names the price, a well for every cup, which Suhrab cannot lower. The Throne's cup is held to be owed the aqueduct head at Kolonna, and Markellos refuses it. Sergian, the chancellor, presses the sick emperor's seal to the Forfeiture of the Drinkers; Markellos leaves his palace for Kedra and is never deposed; the Court of Breaches answers that the Throne drank, does not die and did not leave the palace. No emperor has signed a Covenant instrument since 1215. The war of 1215–1225 is the War of the Seal.

Markellos pours out the cup at Virelos (1225) and gives his claim to Nikandros, who had read the well-curb at Qarath and did not drink; every emperor since pours out a cup at his coronation. Nikandros I, of a Kedravan cadet branch of House Aetherion, refuses the Sanctum's unasked offer of the Crown of Embers for a test of the Great Lamp (1227) and recovers half the Qarath districts when plague-widowed Covenant garrisons march home (1238–1246); Evandros the Sower (1263–1290) and Stavren the Patient (1290–1329) follow; Caedros IV, a provincial Aetherion adopted by Stavren in 1328, is crowned c. 1329.

The Velian States and Zanthir remain outside secure imperial rule. The defeat at Qarath's Well and the War of the Seal deepen the older demographic wound without creating it.

## Moral and Theological Rule

The Covenant occupation imposed predatory contract rule, forced movement, and legal bondage. Its evil does not make every occupation-descended family culpable. Thalmyric defense and liberation can be righteous; expulsion, massacre, or treating ancestry as guilt remains evil.

The White Flame sanctifies and restores. Pain does not buy salvation. Thalmyric asceticism is meant to strip self-deception and train attention; suffering sought as proof of holiness is spiritual vanity.

## Geographic Rule

Thalmyria lies west-central. The Covenant presses from the north and northwest; Eclessia lies east; the Thalassian shore lies east and southeast. Because the world is southern-hemisphere oriented, Kedrava and the deeper southern highlands are colder than the northern lowlands.
