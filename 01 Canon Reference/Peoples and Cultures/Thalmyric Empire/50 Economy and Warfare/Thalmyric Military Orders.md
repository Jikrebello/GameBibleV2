# Thalmyric Military Orders

Military orders combine vows, property, hospitals, archives, and specialist service. They supplement the imperial army and remain answerable to both Church discipline and secular law.

## Vows, Households, and Office

Thalmyric military orders distinguish lay vows from ordination. A knight, healer, scout, engineer, or archivist may live under an order's rule without becoming clergy or gaining authority to administer sacraments.

Poverty and obedience are common but not identical across orders:

- **poverty** means that issued arms, armour, rooms, transport, and stores belong to the house or mission rather than the member;
- a declared devotional object, family letter, marriage token, or freely given personal keepsake may remain personal under the local rule;
- **obedience** is bounded by the order's charter, Church discipline, and secular law. It does not sanctify a knowingly evil command;
- marriage is permitted in many lay branches, including the Long Watch. These branches impose no universal chastity vow;
- married members may live in order houses, attached households, or road stations according to assignment;
- dismissal ends office and access to common property. It does not erase civil liability, create clerical disgrace, or make former members apostates.

These vows are voluntary disciplines with real costs. They are not ownership of a person, and an order that prevents lawful departure has corrupted its own rule.

## Order of the Returning Standard

The Returning Standard preserves Reconquest roads, recovered archives, imperial memorials, and disputed return sites. Its members include mounted escorts, archivists, engineers, and relic guards. It inherits the useful service traditions formerly divided among Phoenix and Ashen-Star orders.

Its temptation is to treat every ancestral claim as superior to every living community.

## Order of the Long Watch

The Long Watch maintains frontier chapels, evacuation routes, scouts, signal towers, hospitals, hospices, and prisoner-exchange stations. It absorbs the legitimate functions of the former Eternal Vigil. Kedravan houses such as the [[Hospice of the Lantern Road]] are known for receiving orphans, road families, wounded soldiers, and winter evacuees without requiring later enlistment.

Its members spend more time preventing raids and moving civilians than fighting battles. War parties distrust its negotiated relationships across the border.

Lay knights normally take poverty and obedience while retaining the right to marry. Their arms and armour are issued, repaired, and returned through the house. Prayer ropes are common personal devotional objects, but they neither confer ordination nor mechanically command the White Flame.

Long Watch discipline treats stores as a moral and practical responsibility. Relief inventories require witnesses, reserves require declared release conditions, and concealment from the vulnerable is judged more severely than an honest shortage. [[The Froststep Evacuation]] records one fair application of that rule in the history of [[Bernard Valeon]].

The order permits lay members to offer ordinary prayer beside the dying. A member who is not ordained must say so plainly and may not imitate sacramental office. Bernard's Last Ember words follow this tradition: companionship in danger, not a private claim to priesthood.

## Iron Martyrs

The Iron Martyrs began among maimed veterans and hospital clergy. They guard corrupted battlefields, dangerous relics, ossuaries, and weapons that cannot safely return to ordinary arsenals.

They reject the claim that injury proves sanctity. Their rule requires veterans to receive care before being permitted to take permanent vows.

## Ash Guard

The Ash Guard is not a Church order. It is the emperor's household guard, recruited from Saltborn and baptized Ättar oath-families whose loyalty is sworn to the sovereign's person.
