# Saints and Relics

Thalmyria glorifies saints by recognizing a life already received by the faithful and confirmed through synodal discernment. It does not teach that suffering, flame, military service, or imperial rank creates sainthood.

## Glorification

Local veneration normally begins around remembered holiness, reliable counsel, mercy, martyrdom, healing, or an incorrupt witness after death. Elders and bishops investigate doctrine, life, miracles, pride, coercion, and the effects of the cult before the Holy Synod adds a name to the diptychs.

The process is less juridical than Eclessian canonization and no less vulnerable to politics.

## Anchored Saints

- **Amaryah the Fire-Bearer:** Mother of refuge memory and the Church's supreme model of faithful custody.
- **Caedros I the Returning:** founder-emperor glorified after his death at the Pillar; his status remains the central dispute with Eclessia.
- **Ioan of Velia:** defender of the vessels and theologian of the Uncreated Kindling.
- **Theodosia the Kindler:** restorer of icons and founder of the Rekindling feast.
- **Saint Naris of Halmenth:** drowned coast saint whose cult serves sailors and families of the lost.
- **The Twelve Companions:** shared foundation of the faith, interpreted through Thalmyric patronage and liturgy.

## Relics

Relics are bodily remains, personal objects, texts, icons, lamps, or places marked by a holy life. They may bear real sanctity but are not batteries. A relic can become silent when used for vanity, coercion, or profit.

The Church distinguishes relics from imperial trophies, Atherian antiquities, battlefield objects, and ordinary memorials. An object may fall under several categories and therefore several legal claims.

## The Saint-Emperor Problem

Thalmyria holds that Caedros I was received by the Flame. Eclessia rejects the evidence because no body, relic anchor, or independent test remains. His hymns kindle according to the righteousness of the singer, not institutional allegiance, though neither Church knows that authorial truth.

## False Kindling

Thalmyric discernment fears holy appearance without humble fruit. A claimant who demands worship, flatters national pride, consumes devotees, or makes restoration contingent on obedience is treated as dangerous even when miracles occur.
