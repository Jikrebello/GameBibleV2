# Icons, Churches, and Sacred Space

Thalmyric sacred architecture is layered rather than stylistically uniform. A church may be an Atherian basilica, a converted Star temple, a mountain refuge chapel, a Reconquest fortress, an occupation-era prayer hall, or a new urban parish built from all five.

## Icons

Icons present the Redeemer, Amaryah, companions, saints, and scriptural events as witnesses within worship. They are honored, carried, kissed, and illuminated; they are not worshiped as gods or treated as objects that compel the White Flame.

The First and Second Breakings taught households to hide icons and lamps inside wells, ovens, walls, and granaries. Rediscovered caches remain common sources of local conflict.

## The Church Interior

A typical church has a domed or vaulted gathering space, screened sanctuary, icon wall, standing room, lamps, incense, and resonant surfaces for communal chant. Side chapels hold local saints and family memorials. Parish books and declared antiquities occupy secured rooms.

Statues are uncommon in worship, though ancient civic sculpture may survive outside or within converted complexes.

## Regional Forms

- Caleran reuses monumental Atherian basilicas, martyr crypts, and converted forums.
- Veridun favors volcanic stone, refuge caves, domes, and palace churches.
- Saronika and the Saltborn coast use lighthouse chapels, maritime mosaics, and storm-resistant low profiles.
- Vlastrava builds fortified churches, veteran ossuaries, and frontier monasteries.
- Elenara joins churches to vineyards, hospitals, guest houses, and archive cellars.
- Arkena preserves island basilicas, academies, and processional harbors.
- Kedrava uses timber, mountain stone, painted interiors, and refuge tunnels.

## Sacred Fire

Lamps are tended human objects that may become vessels of grace. Braziers, hot oil, and manufactured ordeal are not required for ordinary worship. Heated floors, smoke vents, and fire channels are practical architecture before they are symbolism.

## Ruin Integration

Churches often sit above pagan temples, martyr prisons, occupation courts, and older graves. Excavation requires both Church and Crown authority when sacred and imperial claims overlap.
