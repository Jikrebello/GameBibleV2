# Theological Schism with Eclessia

The Great Split of 1015 AR is a dispute over imperial inheritance, primacy, sanctity, and who may speak publicly for the White Flame. Cultural differences sharpen it but did not cause it alone.

## Shared Faith

Both Churches confess the Lord, the Redeemer, resurrection, judgment, sacraments, saints, moral truth, and the reality of the White Flame. Their rites remain mutually recognizable even where their clergy dispute validity or jurisdiction.

## The Thalmyric Claim

Thalmyria holds the old heartland, Caleran, the Pillar, the Eternal Ember, and an imperial line founded by the Returning. It argues that the Church survived through Veridun and that lived theophany cannot be reduced to an eastern court's permission.

## The Eclessian Claim

Eclessia holds the Commission of the Pen, institutional continuity, relic courts, and the Crown of Embers in trust. It argues that a self-canonizing empire cannot make its own military manifestations proof of universal authority.

## The Kindling Question

Veridun teaches that uncreated Fire consents to dwell in sanctified vessels. Eclessia teaches that grace given to mortals is governable through apostolic office and test. Both preserve real goods and both are tempted to possess what they can only serve.

## Daily Difference

Thalmyric parish priests may marry; bishops arise from celibate clergy. Icons and household lamps carry stronger everyday weight. The Churches compute important feasts differently, maintain separate saint registers, and dispute marriage, baptism, and relic recognition along the border.

## The Rupture

Cardinal Hubald nailed Bonifac III's anathema to Veridun's Great Lamp with four nails. Patriarch Makarios answered before the nails were cold. Neither anathema has been lifted.

## Present Relations

Pilgrims cross the same roads, scholars exchange texts, and both Churches oppose genuine predatory cults. Their reunification parties nevertheless imagine submission more often than reconciliation. Freeflame doctrine threatens both by denying that either institution owns the Fire.
