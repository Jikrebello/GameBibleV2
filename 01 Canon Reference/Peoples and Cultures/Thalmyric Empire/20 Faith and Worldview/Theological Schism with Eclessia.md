# Theological Schism with Eclessia

The Great Split of 1015 AR is a dispute over imperial inheritance, primacy, sanctity, and who may speak publicly for the White Flame. Cultural differences sharpen it but did not cause it alone.

## Shared Faith

Both Churches confess the Lord, the Redeemer, resurrection, judgment, sacraments, saints, moral truth, and the reality of the White Flame. Their rites remain mutually recognizable even where their clergy dispute validity or jurisdiction.

## The Thalmyric Claim

Thalmyria holds the old heartland, Caleran, the Pillar, the Eternal Ember, and an imperial line founded by the Returning. It argues that the Church survived through Veridun and that lived theophany cannot be reduced to an eastern court's permission.

## The Eclessian Claim

Eclessia holds the Commission of the Pen, institutional continuity, relic courts, and the Crown of Embers in trust. It argues that a self-canonizing empire cannot make its own military manifestations proof of universal authority.

## The Kindling Question

Veridun teaches that uncreated Fire consents to dwell in sanctified vessels. Eclessia teaches that grace given to mortals is governable through apostolic office and test. Both preserve real goods and both are tempted to possess what they can only serve.

## Roots in the Guttering

Each church traces its method to the centuries before the Fall that Thalmyric refuge chroniclers named the Guttering. Thalmyric preachers tell it from the passes. The palace's hymns stopped kindling while the frontier hermits' went on; the court called its failures "postponed", hired Shell-fire to fill its ranks and at last to finish its accession rites; and when the end came, the Fire let the capital with the most relics fall and saved the refuge with the fewest. Theophany, in their telling, is what the refuge learned.

Eclessian canonists tell it from Aurelium's register of answered fire. After the eastern tax fleet sank in 653, the eastern bishops kept the revenue whichever way a relic test fell, so they could afford to test honestly. In 711 Bishop Oswin examined the Velos Standard before the people for three days and recorded it silent, and in 905 the capital that would not be examined fell to a Covenant siege train that kept its own file on Caleran's counterfeit fire. Protocol, in their telling, is what the east learned. A summary attributed to a Veridancian lecturer is repeated in both seminaries, usually to score a point: the refuge learned that the Fire comes where it will, and the east learned that a fire nobody examines is a fire somebody fakes.

## Daily Difference

Thalmyric parish priests may marry; bishops arise from celibate clergy. Icons and household lamps carry stronger everyday weight. The Churches compute important feasts differently, maintain separate saint registers, and dispute marriage, baptism, and relic recognition along the border.

## The Rupture

In 1015 Lord Primarch Octavin III demanded that Veridun acknowledge the Examiner's primacy by lifting the bronze hood under which the Great Lamp had stood since 986 and submitting the lamp to Aurelium's examination. If it burned and answered, the Sanctum would record Veridun's emperor as the one who proved lawful under the trust of 711; if Veridun refused, the trust would pass to Adalmar. Patriarch Makarios, who had spent six years teaching the Breakers that the Fire is not to be questioned, nailed the hood to the lamp's stand with four nails while Cardinal Wigand waited on the cathedral steps. Wigand read Octavin's anathema to a shut door, and Makarios answered from the doorway with the hammer in his hand. Neither anathema has been lifted. Two years later Octavin crowned Adalmar emperor at the ford of Kaltfurt.

## Present Relations

Pilgrims cross the same roads, scholars exchange texts, and both Churches oppose genuine predatory cults. Their reunification parties nevertheless imagine submission more often than reconciliation. Freeflame doctrine threatens both by denying that either institution owns the Fire.
