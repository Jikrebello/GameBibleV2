# Apocrypha, Hymns, and Theologies of the Flame-Born Redeemer

This collection records non-scriptural miracle acts, hymns, sermons, and disputed theological poetry used in Thalmyria. Inclusion records cultural importance, not historical truth or Church approval.

## The Fire at Dolmiran

The Redeemer enters a town whose rulers burn debtors' records and people together. He restores the names of the dead without restoring the creditors' claims. Return jurists quote the story; the oldest surviving manuscript postdates the Long Reconquest.

## The Seven Curses Broken

Seven predatory bindings confront the Redeemer and fail when their victims speak truth without accepting the powers' definitions. The tale resembles later contract theology and may preserve a genuinely early exorcism tradition.

## The Blaze on the Black Sea

A storm-fire surrounds a boat without consuming it and guides refugees to shore. Saltborn singers claim the coast; Saronikan monasteries claim the manuscript; neither claim is early.

## Swordless, Yet Slaying

This military hymn describes the Redeemer defeating the Adversary without becoming an earthly conqueror. Frontier orders sing shortened verses that omit its warning against glory.

## Flame and Mercy

One school says the Fire's severity is mercy because it destroys corruption. Another warns that persons are not corruption and cannot be purified by being destroyed. The Holy Synod receives the latter distinction: the White Flame restores persons while burning what deforms them.

## Eschatological Hope

The Redeemer will return and restoration will be completed. No emperor, patriarch, vision, victory, or recovered province can declare the hour. Claims that the fall of Zanthir automatically begins the final age are popular frontier apocalypticism, not doctrine.
