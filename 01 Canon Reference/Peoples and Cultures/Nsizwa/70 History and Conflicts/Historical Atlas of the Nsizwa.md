# Historical Atlas of the Nsizwa

## Historical Atlas — wider inheritance

Nsizwa ancestors survived Giant collapse through southwestern plateaus, cattle communities, burial refuges and war bands. Their Orphaned reassembly preserved bodies and praise while other parts of the old infrastructure failed. Mzalu's legendary unification therefore belongs to the joining of several survivor populations, not a secret Ark migration.

War-regiments, Horn-Captains and Spirit-Keepers built a durable order around defence, cattle, craft and the material presence of the dead. Mahlezi's wrapping traditions and Mzikhona's repeated appearances remain received histories whose supernatural claims require individual identification. No human soul is annihilated when a preserved body is destroyed, and no speaking tomb automatically contains its named ancestor.

Their regional power has ordinary foundations: farming, seasonal pasture, plateaus, metalwork and neighbouring trade. It also creates coercion, demands upon households and disputes over who may speak for the honoured dead. These are not generic features borrowed from an undifferentiated Africa. The Nsizwa remain separate from the far-distant Last Houses, whose mortuary government arose independently and has no hidden teaching lineage here.
