# Al-Ahdiya Overview

**Al-Ahdiya**, the Way of the Covenant, began with Mujalib's revelations from Malak al-Ahd in 541 AR. It teaches that creation possesses an intelligible moral order and that witnessed obligation can restrain mortal and supernatural power.

## Shared Doctrine

- The Lord is the source of rightful order; no mortal ruler stands above obligation.
- Mujalib is the final lawgiver of the Covenant, not the Great Prophet of the Exodus.
- The Great Covenant orders creation, but human instruments remain fallible.
- Deliberate breach wounds trust, community, and sometimes the soul.
- Names, witnesses, consent, capacity, terms, and completion determine whether an instrument can bind.
- Spirits may be bound only under strict law, although imperial practice routinely exceeds that claim.

## Worship

Daily devotion combines recited clauses, praise, confession of breaches, almsgiving, and petitions for sound judgement. Important agreements require purification, competent witnesses, and public reading. Pilgrimage to Al-Qahur recalls Mujalib and the Covenant of the 4,444. Neighborhood halls provide teaching, charity, mediation, and registers of birth, marriage, release, and death.

## Law and Conscience

Mainstream jurists distinguish legal enforceability from righteousness. An instrument may be valid and wicked, as Bir Qassim demonstrated. Formalists resist that distinction; reformers argue that coercion, concealed metaphysical terms, or permanent ownership destroy meaningful consent.

## The Two Great Traditions

[[The Witnessed Seal]] grounds authority in public attestation and the continuing community of jurists. [[The Sealed Blood]] grounds succession in Mujalib's household through Ruqaiya and the Veiled Wasiyin. They share scripture, pilgrimage, basic worship, and much law while disagreeing over legitimate authority and sacred history.

## Institutions

There is no single universal priesthood. Aqidin teach, preach, witness, and administer communities; jurists interpret law; arbiters judge; shrine custodians maintain pilgrimage sites; communal foundations support water, education, sanctuary, and burial. Political rulers repeatedly attempt to control all five.

## Moral Boundary

Al-Ahdiya contains sincere worshippers, honest judges, charity, sanctuary, and real protection. Its imperial institutions also defend slavery, conquest, name custody, and coerced service. Those goods do not redeem those evils, and those evils do not make every adherent knowingly corrupt.
