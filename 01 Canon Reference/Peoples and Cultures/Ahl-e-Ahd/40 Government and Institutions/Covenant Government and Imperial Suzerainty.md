# Covenant Government and Imperial Suzerainty

The Covenant is governed through an imperial court whose commands pass through provincial rulers, military households, jurist colleges, municipal councils and local compacts. Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III possesses the highest recognised authority in 1360 AR, but his power depends upon institutions that collect revenue, maintain water and roads, supply armies and translate decrees into local practice. Covenant government consequently combines a strong claim to universal suzerainty with extensive negotiation among offices, communities and hereditary interests.

## The Sublime Threshold

Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III claims to stand at the threshold between sacred law and temporal government. Decrees require his seal, treasury registration, and competent witnesses. In practice the palace household, Guard, jurist colleges, provincial patrons, and tax contractors negotiate every command.

## Layers of Rule

- Imperial governors command strategic provinces, roads, arsenals, and tribute.
- Hereditary rulers retain local courts in exchange for revenue, troops, and formal acknowledgment.
- Military households control estates and garrisons, especially in Al-Qahur.
- Municipal councils administer water, markets, walls, sanitation, and guilds.
- Communal courts govern recognized religious and foreign communities within defined limits.
- Tribal and caravan compacts remain authoritative where imperial enforcement is thin.

## Office and Accountability

Office is conceived as a bundle of named duties rather than personal ownership. Petitions can remove a corrupt minor official; removing a powerful one requires patrons, troops, or proof the court cannot afford to ignore. Provincial appointments commonly become hereditary in practice while remaining revocable in theory.

## Succession

The Qahirids avoid a new seal dispute through brutal clarity: one prince succeeds and rivals are killed, confined, blinded, or permanently bound. The Cull of Brothers is political murder defended as prevention of civil war. Marzuq's sons Kavus and Ardavan already maintain competing households.

## How the Threshold and the Cull Began

The first Qahirid rulers bound their soldiers to themselves by name, with the heir written into each oath as the next holder. In 929 Idris al-Fattah's heir drowned in a palace cistern, and in 931 Idris died before the empire could re-swear. Every oath that named the two dead men lapsed. Levies went home, southern garrisons found nobody to obey, and bound beasts slipped their feeding names; at Iqashar a great Depth-Core engine walked out of the Chain Furnace yard and killed nineteen people in the dyers' quarter. A feeder named Gulnar stopped it from the threshold stone of the Dyers' Hall by giving its feeding name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir, whoever stands in it".

The Regency of the Bench, jurists and the Custodian of the Vault who ruled for the eight-year-old Mahdar until 940, made her formula law. Soldiers, officers and engine crews swore to the Sublime Threshold, an office that cannot die, and the oaths were witnessed at Al-Qahur. Al-Qahur's acknowledgment of each new Sultan-Caliph dates from that arrangement.

The Cull began in the same crisis, as a mercy. A cousin of Idris claimed the lapsed oaths as the nearest grown man of the house. The Regency **lodged** his name in the Vault: it took his true name into custody, so that nobody could swear to him, pray for him by name or hold him to anything he signed. He lived thirty years in a walled garden at Al-Qahur. For the next three centuries lodging was the usual Qahirid answer to rival princes, and the classical age added a gentler one: younger sons could enter the colleges and take the jurist's oath renouncing the throne.

Both protections proved breakable. In 1190 the Court of Breaches voided Suhrab's renunciation as coerced, and he took the throne his oath had given up. Late in his own reign Suhrab lodged his most gifted son on the Guard's evidence that the prince had taken soldiers' oaths in his own name; the prince died in the Deep Vault's cloister within the year. Iqashari jurists date the Cull's turn from the lodge to the knife and the blinding needle from the reigns that followed, when princes had learned that a court could undo a renunciation and the Guard could reach a lodged man.
