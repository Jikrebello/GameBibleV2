# Covenant Government and Imperial Suzerainty

## The Sublime Threshold

Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III claims to stand at the threshold between sacred law and temporal government. Decrees require his seal, treasury registration, and competent witnesses. In practice the palace household, Guard, jurist colleges, provincial patrons, and tax contractors negotiate every command.

## Layers of Rule

- Imperial governors command strategic provinces, roads, arsenals, and tribute.
- Hereditary rulers retain local courts in exchange for revenue, troops, and formal acknowledgment.
- Military households control estates and garrisons, especially in Al-Qahur.
- Municipal councils administer water, markets, walls, sanitation, and guilds.
- Communal courts govern recognized religious and foreign communities within defined limits.
- Tribal and caravan compacts remain authoritative where imperial enforcement is thin.

## Office and Accountability

Office is conceived as a bundle of named duties rather than personal ownership. Petitions can remove a corrupt minor official; removing a powerful one requires patrons, troops, or proof the court cannot afford to ignore. Provincial appointments commonly become hereditary in practice while remaining revocable in theory.

## Succession

The Qahirids avoid a new seal dispute through brutal clarity: one prince succeeds and rivals are killed, confined, blinded, or permanently bound. The Cull of Brothers is political murder defended as prevention of civil war. Marzuq's sons Kavus and Ardavan already maintain competing households.
