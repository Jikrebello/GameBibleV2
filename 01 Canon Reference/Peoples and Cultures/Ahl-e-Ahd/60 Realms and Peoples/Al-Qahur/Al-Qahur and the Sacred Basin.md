# Al-Qahur and the Sacred Basin

Al-Qahur is the Covenant's sacred and agricultural heart, a river-and-oasis basin whose waterworks, grain and pilgrimage sustain lands far beyond its borders. Caliph Uwais guards its holy sites and the Fourth Seal, while Sword-Regent Nadir ibn Haraz and the military households exercise most daily political power. Their dependence upon Qahuri cultivators and canal councils gives village institutions influence beneath an unequal estate system.

## Ancient inheritance

The basin supported [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns|the civilisation of ancient Qahur]] long before Al-Qahur received its present name. Orphaned river communities restored canals after Ishkatar's fall and built competing crowns around Tenaret, Qemaret and Sakhret. Reunification, foreign rule, temple property and the labour of specialised towns changed the system repeatedly. Modern irrigation follows portions of that ancient work alongside later Covenant foundations.

Ancient burial districts remain part of the inhabited country. Some belong to the [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]], whose most successful founders bound their souls to embalmed bodies and small estates in the Egg White. Basin authorities seal, tax, bargain with or deny these houses according to circumstance. A responding tomb may hold a genuine Patron, a constructed office or an Idol Mask; age and splendour cannot distinguish them.

## The Bread of the Bound

The Covenant's hold on the Basin began in famine. In the Thin Floods of 546–552 the river failed in five summers of seven and the yoke-sickness killed the plough oxen. Through the worst years the 4,444 bound offices dug the oasis and channels at Al-Qahur day and night while children starved in the canal towns below, and grain went to those who sealed their names into the Covenant, one household at a time. Qahuri villages remember both halves of that bargain.

## The Basin and the Threshold, 931–1190

Al-Qahur's present weight in Qahirid politics dates largely from the Unsworn Years. When Idris al-Fattah died in 931, two years after his heir Ruzbeh had refused to take up his instruments, the oaths that bound the Qahirid army to the two of them lapsed. The Regency at Iqashar rebuilt the throne as the Sublime Threshold and had every new oath witnessed at Al-Qahur, so that the throne's continuity rested on the Basin's sacred custody. Ruzbeh was the first prince to be lodged. His true name was taken into custody so that nobody could swear to him, and he lived out thirty years in a walled garden in the Basin.

The Basin captains would not re-swear without land. In 938 they took estates in the Sacred Basin on fixed-term leases, with the Basin engine yard, and became the military households. Qahuri canal councils have pointed out ever since that the leases had end dates. "Armed tenants who forgot the lease had an end" began as a lawyer's phrase and became a village insult.

In 1059 Sultan-Caliph Tamim divided the Vault of Names. Its old halls at Al-Qahur, which already held the founding instruments and the Fourth Seal, became the **Deep Vault**. The names of the dead and the surrendered names of the Unnamed Custodians came south from Iqashar to join them in a train of sealed chests that took eleven days on the Basin road, while villagers covered their wells as it passed. Al-Qahur has held the name of Iqashar's chief registrar ever since. The Basin starved in the Two Winters of 1101–1103, when the grain failed under ash-grey skies, and its farmers were saved from ruin by the Court of Breaches' grey-sky ruling that a contract which cannot be performed is suspended rather than voided.

## The Sweat and the Pageless Prince, 1236–1252

The Grey Sweat fell heavily on the Basin. Its witnesses died faster than the bound, the Deep Vault's registers of the dead stopped balancing against Iqashar's living ones, and the military households' fixed-term leases of 938 ran out while their sons died holding frontier forts whose oaths had lost their witnesses. In 1249 **Prince Qaydar**, a Qahirid cousin lodged in the walled garden at Al-Qahur, walked out: the register volume that held his lodged name had been burned with plague bedding, and the Court of Breaches ruled *no page, no lodging*. The Basin's military households rose for him. The Ahdanite Guard, whose officer bench had witnessed the reigning Sultan-Caliph's accession, fought for the throne it had witnessed, and the Pageless Prince was taken and killed in 1252. Since then the Cull of Brothers has used the knife, the canal councils have contested estates held on leases long expired, and every accession has been witnessed by the Guard bench at Iqashar as well as acknowledged at Al-Qahur.

## Sacred Authority

Caliph Uwais, Custodian-of-the-Wells, guards the pilgrimage sites, the remembered Covenant of the 4,444, and the Vault containing the Fourth Seal. His office is the oldest in the Covenant: it descends from the Seal of Water, the first of the three instruments under which Mujalib gave his authority away in 563. His acknowledgment gives Qahirid rulers sacred legitimacy; his silence can wound them.

## Military-Household Rule

Effective government belongs to Sword-Regent **Nadir ibn Haraz** and competing military households descended from Ahdanite commanders. They control cavalry estates, tax districts, garrisons and succession appointments. Their soldiers defend the Basin and keep canals open, but their wealth rests on coerced recruitment, estate tenants, enslaved labour and offices treated as inheritance.

## Qahuri Basin

The [[The Qahuri|Qahuri]] majority sustains irrigation councils, village shrines, fisheries, orchards, grain fields, pilgrimage hostels, and river markets. Military rulers who ignore canal elders discover that command cannot make water climb a neglected gate.

## Confession and Minorities

Al-Qahur is strongly Witnessed-Seal. Sealed-Blood pilgrims may enter under supervision and gather quietly for the Mourning of the Wells. People of the Lamp maintain old urban congregations but face strict building and procession law.

## Present Conflict

Uwais fears becoming a ceremonial seal for Nadir and Marzuq. Nadir needs Uwais alive, compliant, and childless. Qahuri councils want fixed limits on military estates, while younger officers argue that only their houses prevent famine and invasion.

## Glass Frontier

[[Qasr Ilyun]] anchors the basin's northern Glass frontier. Its wells, patrols, breach court, and mixed Qahuri-Jirahar expedition economy make it the last dependable gateway before the Great Glass Desert's moving ruin country.

Qahuri grave-watch cautions against following erased names or empty funeral trains into the Glass. The stories call their destination **the Hollow City**, but basin authorities treat it as a family of warnings rather than a mapped settlement.
