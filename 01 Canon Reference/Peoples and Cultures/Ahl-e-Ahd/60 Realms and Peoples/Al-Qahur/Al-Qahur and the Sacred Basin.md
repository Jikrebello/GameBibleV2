# Al-Qahur and the Sacred Basin

Al-Qahur is the Covenant's sacred and agricultural heart, a river-and-oasis basin whose waterworks, grain and pilgrimage sustain lands far beyond its borders. Caliph Uwais guards its holy sites and the Fourth Seal, while Sword-Regent Nadir ibn Haraz and the military households exercise most daily political power. Their dependence upon Qahuri cultivators and canal councils gives village institutions influence beneath an unequal estate system.

## Sacred Authority

Caliph Uwais, Custodian-of-the-Wells, guards the pilgrimage sites, the remembered Covenant of the 4,444, and the Vault containing the Fourth Seal. His acknowledgment gives Qahirid rulers sacred legitimacy; his silence can wound them.

## Military-Household Rule

Effective government belongs to Sword-Regent **Nadir ibn Haraz** and competing military households descended from Ahdanite commanders. They control cavalry estates, tax districts, garrisons, and succession appointments. Their soldiers defend the basin and keep canals open, but their wealth rests on coerced recruitment, estate tenants, enslaved labor, and offices treated as inheritance.

## Qahuri Basin

The [[The Qahuri|Qahuri]] majority sustains irrigation councils, village shrines, fisheries, orchards, grain fields, pilgrimage hostels, and river markets. Military rulers who ignore canal elders discover that command cannot make water climb a neglected gate.

## Confession and Minorities

Al-Qahur is strongly Witnessed-Seal. Sealed-Blood pilgrims may enter under supervision and gather quietly for the Mourning of the Wells. People of the Lamp maintain old urban congregations but face strict building and procession law.

## Present Conflict

Uwais fears becoming a ceremonial seal for Nadir and Marzuq. Nadir needs Uwais alive, compliant, and childless. Qahuri councils want fixed limits on military estates, while younger officers argue that only their houses prevent famine and invasion.

## Glass Frontier

[[Qasr Ilyun]] anchors the basin's northern Glass frontier. Its wells, patrols, breach court, and mixed Qahuri-Jirahar expedition economy make it the last dependable gateway before the Great Glass Desert's moving ruin country.

Qahuri grave-watch cautions against following erased names or empty funeral trains into the Glass. The stories call their destination **the Hollow City**, but basin authorities treat it as a family of warnings rather than a mapped settlement.
