# Qazmarid and the Sealed Blood

Qazmarid occupies the western strait on a separate landmass. It is not a Thalmyric border province. Its ports, hill fortresses, orchard valleys, and naval stations make it an independent counterweight to Iqashar.

## Rule and Faith

Caliph-Wasi Zahran claims descent from Rumaitha bint Mujalib through her son Hilal, and custody of the Counter-Instrument. A hierarchy of Wasiyin, lineage courts, shrine foundations, and fortress governors supports him. The Sealed Blood is the state confession and the Reading of the Wells its central public rite.

## Society and Economy

Qazmari clans, maritime houses, orchard towns, sailors, artisans, and soldiers provide the state's base. Strait tolls, shipping, citrus, oil, horses, salt, and fortified trade sustain it. The court sponsors exiles and dissidents from Iqashar while suppressing Qazmari critics who ask why sacred descent should escape public witness.

## Minorities

Witnessed-Seal subjects retain courts and worship but are excluded from the highest offices and periodically accused of Qahirid allegiance. Redeemer-faithful communities are smaller than in Zanthir and receive narrower protections.

## The Proclamation of 933

Qazmarid's rulers took the title Caliph-Wasi during Iqashar's Unsworn Years. When Sultan-Caliph Idris died in 931, two years after his heir had refused to take up his instruments, the oaths his army had sworn to the two of them by name lapsed together. Qazmarid had been the Sealed Blood's stronghold since the seventh century, when Hilal and the thirty-six of his household who lived through Bir Qassim came over the strait with the escort that had refused to disarm them, and the households that mourned with them gathered there. Hilal lived veiled at Qazmarid until 631 and is buried in its upper town. Under Qahir the lords of Qazmarid had paid Iqashar for peace in the strait. In 933 they stopped paying, and the lord of Qazmarid's jurists drew the lesson in public: if oaths died with the man, the Qahirid claim had only ever been a man's, and the Fourth Seal had been widowed with Zuhayr ibn Qays; only blood continued, and the blood was Rumaitha's. Witnessed-Seal jurists answered that the witness of a living community cannot be widowed. The argument has not moved since.

For the rest of the century Qazmari naval stations tolled every Covenant convoy that used the western strait, including the ship that carried Iqashar's last Slave-Soul engine south to Caleran in 955, and sheltered fugitives from every Qahirid succession. Qazmari chroniclers boast that Qazmarid never fought Iqashar in those years. It charged it.

## Foreign Policy

Zahran denies Marzuq's universal title, competes for routes through the western strait, and uses Sealed-Blood minorities as influence networks. Qahirid invasion is limited by sea, distance, and the risk of turning Zahran into the martyr he claims already to be. Qazmari captains alternately fight, hire, ransom, and enslave Ättar crews moving between the Lion-Mane Coast and western Kedrava.
