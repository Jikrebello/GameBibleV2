# The Qahirid Suzerainty and Iqashar

Iqashar is the Covenant's imperial capital: a northern escarpment metropolis commanding the best roads to the desert, Al-Qahur, and the Tarkhan approaches. It is the seat of Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III and the Sublime Threshold.

## Court

Marzuq rules with Sayyida Nurhan, who controls household finance and marriage; Guard-Master Temuz, who commands the Ahdanite Guard; and the unnamed Custodian of the Vault of Names. Princes Kavus and Ardavan lead rival succession households.

## Society

Iqashari administrators and merchants dominate the city, but Ahdari clans, Qahuri pilgrims, Sealed-Blood minorities, Jirahar, Tarkhan envoys, People of the Lamp, and foreign factors maintain quarters and institutions. Imperial culture is deliberately Ahdari in ceremony and Iqashari in administration.

## Economy and Power

Customs, caravan tolls, textiles, metalwork, weapons, court consumption, provincial revenue, and military contracts sustain the capital. Its weakness is logistical: authority travels by road and water station, and distant rulers can obey the wording of an order while defeating its purpose.

## Northern Frontier

Iqashar faces the Tarkhan through wells rather than a clean border. The [[Nine Wells of Tergai]] remain ancestral Tarkhan ground held by Qahirid forts. Iron Horde companies, hired since 1304 because theirs are the only horses that will stand beside the Covenant's bound wardens and engines, guard routes and increasingly occupy frontier offices; White Horde and dispossessed bands raid the approaches. Horse warfare dominates the grass, while ancient Well-Tarkhan camel traditions control the driest country. [[Mazrakan, Market of the Nine Measures]] converts part of that conflict into trade.

## The City of Registers, 931–1190

Iqashar had been the marshals' road-head since Qatada's day, and it became the seat of an army in 878, when Qahir ibn Ruzbeh marched nine thousand unransomed crusade prisoners into it and set them to dig the cisterns and furnace pits of the Chain Furnace yard. It became the Covenant's working capital in the century after it lost the south. In 931, when Idris al-Fattah's death voided the oaths sworn to him, the great Depth-Core engine later called the Elder walked out of the Chain Furnace yard and killed nineteen people in the dyers' quarter before a feeder named Gulnar stopped it with the formula that named the Sublime Threshold. The dyers still credit her.

The city's other founding story is a debt. In 1003 the Custodian of the Vault of Names pledged his own true name to a lending house below the Ruzbeh Weight House to ransom a son taken by Tarkhan riders. By 1006 the house held the Vault through him, and the army could not march because no commander knew whose instrument his soldiers stood under. In 1011 the Custodian surrendered his name into the Vault's deepest custody, the bond was left with nobody on one side, and the Vault's guards burned the lending house and its books. Every Custodian since has given up his name at investiture.

After the southern amirates were lost in 1052, registrars, auditors and jurists took over the business of government from the commanders, and Iqashar acquired its reputation for working drains and honest granaries. Tamim the Auditor split the Vault in 1059, keeping the names of the living bound at Iqashar as the Living Register and sending the names of the dead south to Al-Qahur. The breach-courts that had ridden with the Unsealing armies were given a building in the city as the standing Court of Breaches, which heard the frontier's quarrels and, in 1190, voided the renunciation that had kept Suhrab from the throne.

## The Guard's Bench, 1236–1360

The Grey Sweat struck the Iqashar barracks harder than the dry country. The Covenant calls those years the Emptying of the Benches: the witnesses needed to release the names of the dead died faster than the bound, and the Living Register has never balanced since. With the jurist colleges gutted, Suhrab's last book of the *Ledger* (1243) named the officer bench of the Ahdanite Guard a competent witness for the Threshold's own instruments. He wrote it so that his son Mihrzad, dead of the Sweat at a frontier fort in 1241 with only his Guard escort to see it, could be released from the Living Register; his surviving son Tahmur acceded before the bench. When the lodged Prince Qaydar walked free in 1249 because his register page had burned, the Guard bench fought for the throne it had witnessed and killed him in 1252. Every accession since has passed before the bench; the Guard placed Marzuq III in 1349 while the Cull killed or blinded his brothers. The bench is also why the Guard will not march far from Iqashar while the Threshold could fall vacant.

Iqashar paid the Tarkhan between 1265 and 1300, when Qarayel's Black Wind rode horses its bound well-wardens could not frighten, and hired Iron Horde companies from 1304 for the same quality in their horses.

## Present Crisis

Marzuq is clever, indolent, and dependent upon the Guard that placed him. The coming succession may trigger a Cull of Brothers, Sealed-Blood revolt, military intervention, or Tarkhan opportunism.
