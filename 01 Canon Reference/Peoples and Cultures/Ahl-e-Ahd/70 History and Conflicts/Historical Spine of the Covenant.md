# Historical Spine of the Covenant

The Covenant is a religious and political civilisation formed among Ahdari and neighbouring peoples after Mujalib's revelation in 541 AR. Its history grew from much older societies of defended wells, canals, markets, guest-law and witnessed agreement, and later encompassed conquest, schism, translation, imperial government and frontier exchange. In 1360 the Qahirid state remains its largest power, alongside independent Qazmari, Zanthiri, Iqashari and other communities with distinct ancestry and interests.

## Before Mujalib

After the Exodus, ancient Ahdari communities preserved water-right, guest protection, blood-price, sanctuary and witnessed oath. They developed in the Near-Glass and western well country through relationships with independent Orphaned societies. The Qahuri of the Sacred Basin belonged to one of those older populations. They rebuilt riverworks after Ishkatar's fall and passed through the [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns|Senareth, Yasharet, Aramen and later Twin-Reed ages]].

Qahuri kings, temple households, canal councils and craft towns had already created a populous river civilisation by the time of Mujalib. Their [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]] also produced a small class of bound human dead who acted as Egg White Patrons. Iqashari towns followed their own development. Mujalib preached among settled societies with long memories of water, exchange and government.

## Revelation and Unification, 541-567 AR

Mujalib grew up in a well-country broken by the Ubar Toll War of 514–523, when Atheria and Vashar both stopped the stipends that had kept the Ahdari well-houses as clients. He received the revelation of Malak al-Ahd in 541 AR. Five years later the Thin Floods began: the Basin's flood failed in five summers of seven, and the yoke-sickness killed the settled world's oxen while sparing the desert's camels. Through the worst years the 4,444 bound offices dug the oasis and channels at Al-Qahur, and grain went to those who sealed their names into the Covenant. Alliances, preaching, arbitration, war and that bread united Ahdari clans and settled allies. Al-Qahur became the sacred centre.

Vashar ended in the same famine. With the Dawn Road's relay stations emptied, Covenant jurists declared the last King at Dawn forsworn in 555; road commanders and the horse-lords of Ruzhan sealed with the Covenant for bread, and resisting valleys were reduced by 560.

In 558 AR, in the worst year of the dry-country drought that outlasted the floods, Mujalib's coalition violently seized the [[Nine Wells of Tergai]], ancestral Tarkhan ground and the northern frontier's oldest wound. Later Witnessed-Seal tradition recast the conquest as voluntary submission. Qahirid forts and pilgrimages still depend upon that false public memory.

## First Unsealing and Schism, 568-612 AR

Qatada Sayf-al-Ahd led the First Unsealing with breach-courts marching beside his armies; half the Atherian north was served, not fought. The disputed succession of 597 and massacre at Bir Qassim in 603 divided Witnessed Seal from Sealed Blood. Atherian towns learned to plead the disputed seal, Sealed-Blood jurists refused to sit in Uthar's name, and in 598 Uthar dismissed Qatada. Zanthir was established in 601; Banu Mizan client states emerged across the Velian belt around 610. After the standoff at Tamsar in 611, the Banu Mizan brokered the instrument of 612 that Covenant law calls the Suspension.

## Long Frontier, 612-884 AR

Banu Mizan rule produced translation houses, water courts, protected Redeemer communions, trade, bondage, and mixed frontier societies. Covenant breach-assessors kept a file of counterfeit fire in Atherian rites from the Copper Lamp of 683 onward, and Banu Mizan scholars mapped routes into Hell. Qazmari support gave the Sealed Blood a durable territorial base. Imperial unity repeatedly fractured among dynasties and military households. When the Banu Mizan emir died in 872 leaving a child, Caleran called the regent's oath void and launched the Second Crusade; the young officer Qahir ibn Ruzbeh, of a Ruzhan house, made his name defending the Velian belt in 874–878, and in 884 obtained the ruling that Atheria had broken the peace of 612.

## Qahirid Resurgence and Caleran, 885-929 AR

In 885 Qahir's officers ended Banu Mizan rule in the Velian belt, and in 885–886 the college at Al-Qahur ruled that the Clause of the Given binds parties, not powers. Qahir rebuilt central forces through Ahdanite mass levies, capture companies, name-cage foundries and provincial integration. A soldier without the old clans' sacred standing, he bound his captains, foundry masters and engine crews to himself by name, with his son written into every instrument as the next holder. Sultan-Caliph Idris al-Fattah kept the practice and took Caleran in 905. Vortharan warlocks broke three of his five great engines at Crown Gate in 907, the Atherian remount studs he had counted on escaped north into the Tarkhan grass, and from 908 the mountain rebels drew off the occupation's bound labour with their amnesties.

## The Unsworn Years and the Lost South, 929-1052 AR

Idris's heir drowned in a palace cistern in 929, and Idris died in 931 before the empire could re-swear to anyone else. Every instrument naming the two dead men was **widowed**. Levies went home; southern garrisons found nobody to obey; the silt hounds bound into the northern well-shafts slipped their feeding names and hunted the escarpment towns; at Iqashar the great Depth-Core engine later called the Elder broke out of the Chain Furnace yard and killed nineteen people in the dye-yards before a feeder named Gulnar stopped it by giving its feeding name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir". The Regency of the Bench, ruling for the boy Mahdar, made that formula the law of the throne: instruments have been sworn to the **Sublime Threshold** ever since, witnessed at Al-Qahur. The Regency lodged a rival cousin's name in the Vault rather than kill him, the first form of the Cull of Brothers. In 933 Qazmarid's lord took the title Caliph-Wasi; in 938 the Basin captains re-swore for estates on fixed-term leases and became Al-Qahur's military households.

Durayd ibn Sahl, amir of occupied Caleran, swore the south to himself and ruled it for twenty-six years. In 951 Grass-Tarkhan riders on hymn-blessed Atherian horses, which did not shy from bound things, broke through the hound-guarded northern wells in the Long Reach and held Iqashar's army on the grass. Thalmyric war-lamps opened the last Slave-Soul engine's name-cage before Caleran in 956, and in 957 Durayd surrendered the city in his own name. The occupied heartland then split into a dozen separately sworn amirates that Thalmyria took one at a time. Iqashar sheltered Ioan of Velia during the First Breaking; lost the use of its army from 1006 to 1011, while a lending house held the pledged name of the Custodian of the Vault; cut the south loose in Tamim's Audit of 1033; and lost its last relief army to a copyist's error at the Wrong Well in 1041. Zanthir bought its autonomy in 1044 by paying tribute in alternate seasons. In 1052 the last amirate fell and the Southern Houses carried their deeds north. See [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]].

## Iqashar's Classical Age, 1052-1190 AR

Defeat put registrars and jurists in charge at Iqashar. In 1059 Tamim divided the Vault of Names: the Living Register stayed at Iqashar under the Unnamed Custodian, while the names of the dead and the Custodians' surrendered names went south to the Deep Vault at Al-Qahur, beside the founding instruments and the Fourth Seal. Mazrakan was chartered in 1067 to turn the horse frontier into a market, and Tarkhan companies served on word-price outside the Register. The Velian belt became the southern frontier. The Two Winters of 1101–1103 starved the Basin and idled the foundry. Khurzad lost the War of the Southern Deeds, 1131–1139, when the mercy towers he sent to enforce the Southern Houses' claims misread settlers who could not surrender without forfeiting their land. In 1190 the Court of Breaches voided the renunciation that had kept Suhrab, a younger son, from the throne.

## Suhrab and the Black Wind, 1190-1303 AR

Competition with Thalmyria hardened the frontier. Suhrab the Lawbinder ruled from 1190 to 1243 and compiled the *Ledger of Ledgers*, harmonising the provincial codes that had failed on the Day of Two Readings. In 1212 his Court of Breaches revived the 1126 ruling as the Unburdened Deeds, offering the Thalmyric district families confirmation of their land under Covenant deed with no levy owed, and some registered at the frontier bench beside Qarath's Well. In the drought summer of 1214 the Thalmyric army that marched to stop them found the outer wells along its road fouled with carcasses by Tarkhan companies in Covenant pay, and drank at dusk from Qarath's sworn well, whose curb carried a guest's peace in three scripts; at dawn the water turned in the men who lifted arms against their host, the Tarkhan companies on blessed lines rode through what was left, and Suhrab took the emperor captive and ransomed him for the districts. When the emperor's own court blinded him and argued that an unfit emperor widowed every instrument he had sworn, the Court of Breaches answered that the ransom was sworn to the Throne, which does not widow, and cited the Sublime Threshold and the Ember deposit of 957. Covenant garrisons marched into the districts in 1215.

The Grey Sweat reached Covenant lands about 1236 by the Velian fairs, the Zanthiri ports and Mazrakan. It fell lightly on the dry country and heavily on the Basin and the Iqashar barracks. Every bound death needed a witnessed release in the Living Register, and deaths outran the witnesses; the Covenant calls those years **the Emptying of the Benches**, after the empty witness benches of the courts, and its registers of the living and the dead have never balanced since. Garrison instruments needed living witnesses too. On the Qarath frontier and at the Tarkhan wells, commanders held their men's bonds widowed, as the army had in 931, and marched them home; between 1238 and 1246 Thalmyria walked back through open gates into half the districts it had ransomed away. Suhrab's most gifted son, commanding on that frontier, swore the wavering garrisons to himself to hold them, and the Ledger's own book against armies sworn to a living man was turned on him. Guard officers testified to it. Suhrab lodged his son's name in the Deep Vault, and the prince died in its cloister within the year, of fever according to the Vault and by the Guard's hand according to the Iqashar bazaar. Foreign chroniclers, who could not tell a lodging from an execution, recorded that Suhrab executed his son on the Guard's evidence. Dying in 1243, Suhrab added a last book to the *Ledger*. It let a sealed register stand as witness where no competent witness survived, which bound the widowed garrisons again, and it named the officer bench of the Ahdanite Guard a competent witness for the Threshold's own instruments, because the plague had emptied the jurist colleges. His surviving son **Tahmur** acceded before that bench.

In 1249 a cousin lodged under Suhrab, **Prince Qaydar**, walked out of his walled garden at Al-Qahur. The register volume that held his lodged name had been burned with plague bedding, and the Court of Breaches held what the law said: *no page, no lodging*. The Basin's military households, whose leases of 938 had run out and whose sons had died holding forts for nobody, rose for him. The Guard bench fought for the Threshold it had witnessed, and the Pageless Prince was taken and killed in 1252. A Guard-Master's sentence passed into law in practice, *the Vault can lose a page; the grave cannot*, and from then on the Cull of Brothers killed, blinded or lodged, lodging being the mildest of the three. Thinned levies and the debts of the unreleased dead left Iqashar poorer when Qarayel's Black Wind came out of the grass. Its horses walked up to the bound wardens of the northern wells as Grey Sister's daughters once had. Tahmur began paying the Tarkhan in 1265, and **Marzuq I** (1268–1301) kept paying and called it escort until the horses' grace thinned and the wardens held again in 1300, exposing the limits of settled power on the frontier where the seizure of the Nine Wells had begun.

## Present Settlement, 1304-1360 AR

The Tarkhan herds were partitioned among four Hordes in 1304, and in the same year **Marzuq II** (1301–1349) hired Iron Horde companies on word-price, because theirs were the only horses on the frontier that would stand beside the Covenant's devil-mantled riders and small contract engines. From about 1310 Iron-mounted companies raided Thalmyric chapel-forts on the Velian frontier by night. The raids brought on Orinel (1318), the Thalmyric Crimson Pilgrimage (1319–1322) and the loss of Zehrim (1324–1325), where the fort's oath-ledgers were burned on its altar. In 1326 the Court of Breaches ruled that the people those ledgers had bound remained bound in the Living Register at Iqashar; Thalmyria calls them freed, and Iqashar calls them absconded. No treaty ended the war. No Thalmyric emperor has signed a Covenant instrument since the Court's answer of 1215, and the frontier has kept an armed peace with nothing written to keep or break. When Marzuq II died in 1349 the Guard bench witnessed **Marzuq III** while the Cull killed or blinded his brothers. Qazmarid remains independent, Al-Qahur indispensable but internally divided, and Zanthir the expected first battlefield of the next imperial war.

## Historical Atlas — wider inheritance

The Ahdari predecessors of the Covenant peoples formed during the later northern fission, when defended wells, territorial oaths and enforceable restitution offered an alternative to more mobile Near-Glass institutions. Tarkhan neighbours traded, married, raided and negotiated on their own behalf.

Mujalib's revelation in 541 AR transformed that inheritance. Conquest, Al-Qahur's foundation and the First Unsealing made an imperial-confessional identity capable of admitting several peoples. The Long Frontier allowed translation, irrigation and mixed households to flourish alongside captivity, ransom and legal coercion.

The Qahirid resurgence and Fall of Caleran enlarged the empire. The widowing of Idris's instruments in 931, the Long Reach of the Tarkhan horse and the loss of the south then turned the Covenant inward, towards Iqashar's registers and the Threshold that cannot die; later prosperity under Suhrab did not prevent fragmentation. In 1360, Qahuri, Iqashari, Qazmari, Ahdari, Zanthiri and other communities possess different ancestries and recognise no uncontested ruler.
