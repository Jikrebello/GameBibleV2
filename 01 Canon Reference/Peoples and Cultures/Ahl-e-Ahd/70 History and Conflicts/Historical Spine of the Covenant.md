# Historical Spine of the Covenant

The Covenant is a religious and political civilisation formed among Ahdari and neighbouring peoples after Mujalib's revelation in 541 AR. Its history grew from much older societies of defended wells, canals, markets, guest-law and witnessed agreement, and later encompassed conquest, schism, translation, imperial government and frontier exchange. In 1360 the Qahirid state remains its largest power, alongside independent Qazmari, Zanthiri, Iqashari and other communities with distinct ancestry and interests.

## Before Mujalib

After the Exodus, ancient Ahdari communities preserved water-right, guest protection, blood-price, sanctuary and witnessed oath. They developed in the Near-Glass and western well country through relationships with independent Orphaned societies. The Qahuri of the Sacred Basin belonged to one of those older populations. They rebuilt riverworks after Ishkatar's fall and passed through the [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns|Senareth, Yasharet, Aramen and later Twin-Reed ages]].

Qahuri kings, temple households, canal councils and craft towns had already created a populous river civilisation by the time of Mujalib. Their [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]] also produced a small class of bound human dead who acted as Egg White Patrons. Iqashari towns followed their own development. Mujalib preached among settled societies with long memories of water, exchange and government.

## Revelation and Unification, 541-567 AR

Mujalib grew up in a well-country broken by the Ubar Toll War of 514–523, in which Atheria and Vashar each voided the other's well-awards and granted the same wells twice, so that every well of any size had two lawful holders. He received the revelation of Malak al-Ahd in 541 AR beside such a well, and for eight years he sealed the shares of disputed wells on clay tablets; some sixty kept wells, which nobody dared foul, were the Covenant's first country. Five years after the revelation the Thin Floods began: the Basin's flood failed in five summers of seven, and the yoke-sickness killed the settled world's oxen while sparing the desert's camels. Through the worst years the 4,444 bound offices dug the oasis and channels at Al-Qahur, and grain went to those who sealed their names into the Covenant. Preaching, arbitration, war and that bread joined Ahdari clans and settled allies under one roll, and every name on it was owed bread in a failed year. Al-Qahur became the sacred centre.

Vashar ended in the same famine. With the Dawn Road's relay stations emptied, Covenant jurists declared the last King at Dawn forsworn in 555; road commanders and the horse-lords of Ruzhan sealed with the Covenant for bread, and resisting valleys were reduced by 560.

In 558 AR, in the worst year of the dry-country drought that outlasted the floods, the guard of the oasis wells under its captain Uthar ibn Qays violently seized the [[Nine Wells of Tergai]], ancestral Tarkhan ground and the northern frontier's oldest wound. Later Witnessed-Seal tradition recast the conquest as voluntary submission. Qahirid forts and pilgrimages still depend upon that false public memory.

Mujalib took no ruler's title. In 563 he divided his authority under three sealed instruments: the Seal of Water to Uthar and the guard of the wells, the Seal of Names to the college of scribes, and the Seal of the Roads to the marshal Qatada Sayf-al-Ahd. He lived until 596 in a house beside the first channel and set none of the three above the others.

## First Unsealing and Schism, 568-612 AR

By 567 the roll had outgrown the Basin's bread. The Covenant's bound carriers would lift only grain that had been given, so from 568 breach-courts went into the Atherian north under the escort of Qatada's horsemen to turn town granaries into gifts. A served town kept its walls and altars, entered its householders on the roll, owed a share of each harvest and received a bound keeper at its well; half the north was served, not fought. At the Dry Ford in 575 the northern field army beat Qatada and hanged a court, then broke up for want of water at wells that drew only for the roll.

Mujalib died in 596. The college's Fourth Seal named Uthar, who became the first caliph, and the Counter-Instrument of the Prophet's daughter Ruqaiya named her son Husan. Atherian towns learned to plead the disputed seal, Sealed-Blood jurists refused to sit in Uthar's name, and in 598 Qatada halted the northern army until the seals could be tried. In 603 Husan, travelling to arbitration under Qatada's safe conduct, was pushed off the named road by a flood, and the bound keeper of the station well at Bir Qassim would not draw for his household. Sixteen died of thirst, Ruqaiya among them. The escort refused to disarm the rest and carried them to the strait, and Qatada rode after it; the caliph's column killed the dying and the drovers who had given them water. Husan lived, veiled, at Qazmarid, and the Covenant divided into Witnessed Seal and Sealed Blood. Zanthir was established in 601; Banu Mizan client states emerged across the Velian belt around 610. After the standoff at Tamsar in 611, the Banu Mizan brokered the instrument of 612 that Covenant law calls the Suspension.

## Long Frontier, 612-884 AR

Banu Mizan rule produced charter-houses, where the old empire's deeds were read and its learning copied, water courts, protected Redeemer communions, trade, bondage, and mixed frontier societies. Covenant breach-assessors kept a file of counterfeit fire in Atherian rites from the Copper Lamp of 683 onward, and Banu Mizan scholars mapped routes into Hell. Qazmari support gave the Sealed Blood a durable territorial base. Imperial unity repeatedly fractured among dynasties and military households, and the caliphs of Uthar's line at Al-Qahur kept their wells and little else. When the Banu Mizan emir died in 872 leaving a child, Caleran called the regent's oath void and launched the Second Crusade. Qahir ibn Ruzbeh, of a Ruzhan house that kept the weigh-house at Iqashar, made his name defending the Velian belt in 874–878. Nobody could pay his riders, so he took the crusade's nine thousand unransomed prisoners instead, entered them under his own name and paid his army from their hire. In 881 the caliph gave him the Seal of the Roads, unheld since Qatada, with the new title of Sultan, and in 884 he obtained the ruling that Atheria had broken the peace of 612.

## Qahirid Resurgence and Caleran, 885-929 AR

In 885 Qahir's officers ended Banu Mizan rule in the Velian belt, and in 885–886 the college at Al-Qahur ruled that the Clause of the Given binds parties, not powers. Qahir rebuilt central forces through Ahdanite mass levies, capture companies, name-cage foundries and provincial integration. A soldier without the old clans' sacred standing, he bound his captains, foundry masters and engine crews to himself by name, with his son written into every instrument as the next holder. Idris al-Fattah, the first of the house to be styled Sultan-Caliph, kept the practice. He was thirty-one in 902 and had daughters and no son, so every oath in the army named him and nobody after him, and his jurists kept him at Iqashar while Qahir's engine-master Barzin and the breach-court train took Caleran in his name in 905. Vortharan warlocks broke three of his five great engines at Crown Gate in 907, the Atherian remount studs he had counted on escaped north into the Tarkhan grass, and from 908 the mountain rebels drew off the occupation's bound labour with their amnesties.

## The Unsworn Years and the Lost South, 929-1052 AR

In 929 Idris's heir Ruzbeh, fourteen and taught by a jurist of the people of the Clause, refused before the foundry masters to take up his father's instruments, and Idris died in 931 before the empire could re-swear to another son. Every instrument now named a dead man and a boy who had refused it, and jurists called such instruments **widowed**. Levies went home; southern garrisons found nobody to obey; the silt hounds bound into the northern well-shafts slipped their feeding names and hunted the escarpment towns; at Iqashar the great Depth-Core engine later called the Elder broke out of the Chain Furnace yard and killed nineteen people in the dye-yards before a feeder named Gulnar stopped it by giving its feeding name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir". The Regency of the Bench, ruling for the boy Mahdar, made that formula the law of the throne: instruments have been sworn to the **Sublime Threshold** ever since, witnessed at Al-Qahur. When Basin captains tried to make Ruzbeh take the instruments up after all, the Regency lodged his name in the Vault rather than kill him, the first form of the Cull of Brothers. In 933 Qazmarid's lord took the title Caliph-Wasi; in 938 the Basin captains re-swore for estates on fixed-term leases and became Al-Qahur's military households.

Durayd ibn Sahl, amir of occupied Caleran, swore the south to himself and ruled it for twenty-six years. In 951 Grass-Tarkhan riders on hymn-blessed Atherian horses, which did not shy from bound things, broke through the hound-guarded northern wells in the Long Reach and held Iqashar's army on the grass. Thalmyric war-lamps opened the last Slave-Soul engine's name-cage before Caleran in 956. In 957, with the occupied quarters in revolt, Durayd tried to carry the Ember off to buy his way home; its keepers, Covenant men whose names were pledged to it, held the lamp against his guard, the dockers burned his ships, and he was taken alive in the harbour fort. The parishes opened the gates, and the keepers made the terms that named the garrison families protected. Thalmyria kept Durayd at Veridun for fourteen years, because every garrison beyond Caleran was still sworn to him by name, and had him sign their releases. A town whose amir took a release and terms opened its gates; an amir who refused swore his garrison to himself. Thalmyria took most of those that held out by seizing the canal heads in the hills and registering the farmers' water, until the amirs held walls above dry fields. Iqashar sheltered Ioan of Velia during the First Breaking; lost the use of its army from 1006 to 1011, while a lending house held the pledged name of the Custodian of the Vault; cut the south loose in Tamim's Audit of 1033; and lost its last relief army to a copyist's error at the Wrong Well in 1041. Zanthir kept its autonomy from 1044 by paying each empire in the season that empire could close: Veridun while the sea was open, Iqashar while the caravans ran. In 1052 the last amirate fell and the Southern Houses carried their deeds north. See [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]].

## Iqashar's Classical Age, 1052-1190 AR

Defeat put registrars and jurists in charge at Iqashar. In 1059 Tamim divided the Vault of Names: the Living Register stayed at Iqashar under the Unnamed Custodian, while the names of the dead and the Custodians' surrendered names went south to the Deep Vault at Al-Qahur, beside the founding instruments and the Fourth Seal. Mazrakan was chartered in 1067 to turn the horse frontier into a market, and Tarkhan companies served on word-price outside the Register. The Velian belt became the southern frontier. The Two Winters of 1101–1103 starved the Basin and idled the foundry. Khurzad lost the War of the Southern Deeds, 1131–1139, when the mercy towers he sent to enforce the Southern Houses' claims misread settlers who could not surrender without forfeiting their land. In 1190 the Court of Breaches voided the renunciation that had kept Suhrab, a younger son, from the throne.

## Suhrab and the Black Wind, 1190-1303 AR

Competition with Thalmyria hardened the frontier. Suhrab the Debtor ruled from 1190 to 1243. His father's commission had spent forty years failing to decide which provincial code stood highest; Suhrab abandoned the question and wrote the *Ledger of Ledgers*, a book of the occasions on which the Covenant's law had killed its own people, each counted like a debt and closed with the clause owed, to which every code must yield. He read its first book, Bir Qassim, at the dry well in 1194, where Sealed-Blood mourners answered every clause with *And yet the well was dry* and his diggers brought up dust from eighty fathoms. In 1212 his Court of Breaches closed the *Ledger*'s book of the Southern Deeds by reviving the 1126 ruling as the Unburdened Deeds, offering the Thalmyric district families confirmation of their land under Covenant deed with no levy owed, and some registered at the frontier bench beside Qarath's Well. In the drought summer of 1214 the Thalmyric army that marched to stop them found the outer wells along its road fouled with carcasses by Tarkhan companies in Covenant pay, and drank at dusk from Qarath's sworn well, whose curb carried a guest's peace in three scripts; at dawn the water turned in the men who lifted arms against their host, the Tarkhan companies on blessed lines rode through what was left, and Suhrab, rather than take the emperor captive, kept him three days as a guest and sent him home with the bill the curb allowed, the price of the water, which was the districts. When the emperor's own court deposed him and argued that the bill was his private debt, the Court of Breaches answered that he had drunk under his standard as the Throne, which does not die and is not deposed, and cited the Sublime Threshold and the Ember deposit of 957. Covenant garrisons marched into the districts in 1215.

The Grey Sweat reached Covenant lands about 1236 by the Velian fairs, the Zanthiri ports and Mazrakan. It fell lightly on the dry country and heavily on the Basin and the Iqashar barracks. Every bound death needed a witnessed release in the Living Register, and deaths outran the witnesses; the Covenant calls those years **the Emptying of the Benches**, after the empty witness benches of the courts, and its registers of the living and the dead have never balanced since. Garrison instruments needed living witnesses too. On the Qarath frontier and at the Tarkhan wells, commanders held their men's bonds widowed, as the army had in 931, and marched them home; between 1238 and 1246 Thalmyria walked back through open gates into half the districts the bill for the water had taken. Suhrab's most gifted son, **Prince Mihrzad**, commanded on that frontier. A prince of the house was a competent witness under the *Ledger*, so he rode from fort to fort through the plague country with a Guard escort and stood witness himself while each garrison re-swore to the Threshold. The forts he reached held; the forts he did not reach opened their gates. His escort also carried the Sweat into three forts that had been clean. In the winter of 1241 he died of it at a well-fort where every jurist and every competent witness was already dead, and only the Guard officers of his escort saw him die. With no competent witness, his name could not be released, and the Living Register carried him as an officer still owing service. Frontier villages remember him as the rider who brought the Sweat; the Iqashar bazaar sings him as the Rider of the Benches.

Suhrab's last book of the *Ledger*, the book of the Benches, was written in 1243 for his son. It let a sealed register stand as witness where no competent witness survived, which bound the widowed garrisons again, and it named the officer bench of the Ahdanite Guard a competent witness for the Threshold's own instruments, because the plague had emptied the jurist colleges and the Guard's officers had been the only witnesses of the prince's death. The first release entered under it was Mihrzad's. Suhrab died that year, and his surviving son **Tahmur** acceded before the Guard bench.

In 1249 a cousin lodged under Suhrab, **Prince Qaydar**, walked out of his walled garden at Al-Qahur. The register volume that held his lodged name had been burned with plague bedding, and the Court of Breaches held what the law said: *no page, no lodging*. The Basin's military households, whose leases of 938 had run out and whose sons had died holding forts for nobody, rose for him. The Guard bench fought for the Threshold it had witnessed, and the Pageless Prince was taken and killed in 1252. A Guard-Master's sentence passed into law in practice, *the Vault can lose a page; the grave cannot*, and from then on the Cull of Brothers killed, blinded or lodged, lodging being the mildest of the three. Thinned levies and the debts of the unreleased dead left Iqashar poorer when Qarayel's Black Wind came out of the grass. Its horses walked up to the bound wardens of the northern wells as Grey Sister's daughters once had. Tahmur began paying the Tarkhan in 1265, and **Marzuq I** (1268–1301) kept paying and called it escort until the horses' grace thinned and the wardens held again in 1300, exposing the limits of settled power on the frontier where the seizure of the Nine Wells had begun.

## Present Settlement, 1304-1360 AR

The Tarkhan herds were partitioned among four Hordes in 1304, and in the same year **Marzuq II** (1301–1349) hired Iron Horde companies on word-price, because theirs were the only horses on the frontier that would stand beside the Covenant's devil-mantled riders and small contract engines. From about 1310 Iron-mounted companies raided Thalmyric chapel-forts on the Velian frontier by night. The raids brought on Orinel (1318), the Thalmyric Crimson Pilgrimage (1319–1322) and the loss of Zehrim (1324–1325), where the fort's oath-ledgers were burned on its altar. In 1326 the Court of Breaches ruled that the people those ledgers had bound remained bound in the Living Register at Iqashar; Thalmyria calls them freed, and Iqashar calls them absconded. No treaty ended the war. No Thalmyric emperor has signed a Covenant instrument since the Court's answer of 1215, and the frontier has kept an armed peace with nothing written to keep or break. When Marzuq II died in 1349 the Guard bench witnessed **Marzuq III** while the Cull killed or blinded his brothers. Qazmarid remains independent, Al-Qahur indispensable but internally divided, and Zanthir the expected first battlefield of the next imperial war.

## Historical Atlas — wider inheritance

The Ahdari predecessors of the Covenant peoples formed during the later northern fission, when defended wells, territorial oaths and enforceable restitution offered an alternative to more mobile Near-Glass institutions. Tarkhan neighbours traded, married, raided and negotiated on their own behalf.

Mujalib's revelation in 541 AR transformed that inheritance. Conquest, Al-Qahur's foundation and the First Unsealing made an imperial-confessional identity capable of admitting several peoples. The Long Frontier allowed translation, irrigation and mixed households to flourish alongside captivity, ransom and legal coercion.

The Qahirid resurgence and Fall of Caleran enlarged the empire. The widowing of Idris's instruments in 931, the Long Reach of the Tarkhan horse and the loss of the south then turned the Covenant inward, towards Iqashar's registers and the Threshold that cannot die; later prosperity under Suhrab did not prevent fragmentation. In 1360, Qahuri, Iqashari, Qazmari, Ahdari, Zanthiri and other communities possess different ancestries and recognise no uncontested ruler.
