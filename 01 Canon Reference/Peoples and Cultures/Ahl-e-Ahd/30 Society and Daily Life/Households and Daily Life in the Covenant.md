# Households and Daily Life in the Covenant

Most Covenant subjects encounter law through ordinary necessities: a water turn, marriage portion, apprenticeship, rent, caravan wage, inheritance, or neighborhood surety. Few practice metaphysical contract magic.

## Household

Households may include several generations, apprentices, servants, freed dependants, and business partners. Marriage instruments specify property, residence, support, children, and dissolution. Practice differs by people and class: Ahdari route shares, Iqashari professional alliances, Qahuri land and water, Qazmari shrine ties, and Zanthiri mixed-law households all produce different families.

## Work and Learning

Children learn recitation, sums, signatures, and the danger of signing unread text. Worship occurs in Covenant halls, neighborhood courts, shrines, schools, and the household. Craftspeople organize through guild instruments, cultivators through water councils, and travelers through caravan compacts.

## Food and Festival

Daily food follows geography: flatbread and herd products on desert roads; grain, fish, dates, and lentils in Al-Qahur; orchard foods and seafood around Qazmarid and Zanthir. Public life includes night markets, poetry, wrestling, horse and camel fairs, pilgrim feasts, weddings, funerals, and the solemn Mourning of the Wells.

## The Unequal Burden

Wealth buys better witnesses, advocates, and escape clauses. Poor households depend on neighborhood sureties, sanctuary foundations, honest jurists, and communal memory. Enslaved and name-bound people live inside households while lacking the legal power to leave them.
