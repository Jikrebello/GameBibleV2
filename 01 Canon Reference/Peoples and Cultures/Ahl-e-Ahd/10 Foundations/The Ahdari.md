# The Ahdari

The Ahdari are an ancient people of the western well country and Near-Glass roads. Their ancestry is principally Glass-derived and includes durable Orphaned inheritance from older oasis, Narashi and Tarkhan communities. Well keepers, caravan houses, herders, poets, craftspeople and sanctuary guardians shared an Ahdari identity centuries before the Covenant.

Mujalib was Ahdari. His preaching and the rise of Al-Ahdiya made formal Ahdari the language of scripture and imperial law. The Ahdari consequently became a founding people of the Covenant, while Qahuri, Iqashari, Qazmari, Zanthiri and other peoples entered that civilisation with their own ancestry and history.

Their society prizes remembered obligation, hospitality, endurance, poetry, and the ability to move people and water safely. Urban elites imitate Ahdari robes and genealogies, sometimes to the amusement of clans who still live beyond city walls. Kin groups negotiate marriages through reciprocal protection and property clauses; women of established caravan houses commonly control route shares, wells, and household capital.

Most Ahdari are not rulers. Some remain pastoral or caravan peoples; others are soldiers, scribes, drovers, leatherworkers, guides, or urban poor. Imperial claims to represent pure Ahdari custom are political inventions built from real ancestry.
