## Locked Peoples

**Ahl-e-Ahd** is an imperial-confessional identity containing several ancestries. The Ahdari supplied its founding language and a large Glass-descended core. Ahdari communities had already braided with Narashi, Tarkhan, Jirahar, oasis, Qahuri, Gateway and other populations in different regions before and during Covenant expansion. Ahdari, Iqashari, Qahuri, Qazmari, Zanthiri and Velian remain meaningful identities in 1360 AR. Tarkhan and Jirahar remain distinct peoples, although individuals and communities from both can enter Al-Ahdiya, serve Covenant states and identify as Ahl-e-Ahd in the confessional or imperial sense.

The ancient Ahdari formed in the western well country between approximately 1950 and 1400 BR. They were a durable people long before Mujalib. Their protected wells, markets, camel roads, hospitality, raiding and plural cults belong to pre-Covenant history. No ancient office inherited the Great Prophet's commission. Ark-derived law founded no public office on a bargain: ancient Ahdari wells were held by armed protectors, oath and blood-price, unlike the Narashi crown-mothers' sealed wells (c. 2255 BR). Covenant jurists call the Unpaid Well, the Prophet held to his own law, their first case.

The ancient Qahuri are principally Orphaned descendants of Sacred Basin communities which survived Ishkatar's collapse. They did not descend from the Glass host. The Gateway people were a Braided ancient population formed from older shore communities, Ahdari migrants, maritime settlers, captives and freed households. The House of Nerethan inherited a junior Qahuri maternal connection through Tazirun Zaheret, Lemara, Hesara and Hadrima; that relationship conveyed no claim over Qahur.

## Locked Geography

Iqashar lies in the far north at the Tarkhan approaches. Al-Qahur occupies the fertile basin south of it. Qazmarid lies across the western strait. Zanthir and the Velian belt lie southeast toward the Thalassian and Thalmyric frontier. South grows colder.

The [[Nine Wells of Tergai]] are ancestral Well-Tarkhan works seized by Covenant forces in 558 AR. The public First Submission tradition is historically false. White-Horde raids are Iqashar's immediate danger; Iron-Horde military settlement is its slower institutional danger.

## Locked Metaphysical Truth

The Creditor wears the mask of Malak al-Ahd and profits from Covenant rent architecture. Its interference kept the Fourth Seal dispute permanently unresolved but does not establish which succession instrument began genuine. Sincere belief, honest judgement, and lawful protection remain possible inside the corrupted system.

The [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]] predates the Covenant. A successful rite binds one complete human soul to its embalmed body and a small Egg White estate; founders able to lease access, protection or power act as Patrons. This delays the Boatman's road and final judgement without destroying or dividing the soul. Failed rites may leave infrastructure offices or copied roles, and dependent Greed Masks may parasitise those remains. The present reachability of Meresha, Khaseret, Tamekha and other Second-House Patrons is unresolved.

## Locked History, 514–905

- **The Ubar Toll War** (514–523) between Atheria and Vashar stops the stipends that kept the Ahdari well-houses as clients; the well-country falls into feud. Mujalib (b. c. 510) grows up in it and receives the revelation in 541.
- **The Thin Floods** (546–552) and the **yoke-sickness** (548–551), an ox murrain that spares camels, break the settled economy. In **the Bread of the Bound** (c. 549) the 4,444 dig the oasis and channels at Al-Qahur, and grain goes to those who seal their names. The Covenant's founding proof is food. The claim that the Vault of Names began as the famine ration roll is one later jurist's; the two records are separate.
- **The end of Vashar** (555–560): famine empties the Dawn Road's relay stations, and Covenant jurists declare the last King at Dawn forsworn on the crown's own promise to keep the roads. Ruzhan horse-lords seal for bread and become Covenant road and cavalry officers. The Qahirids descend from one of their houses: a converted Vasharan line without Ahdari sacred lineage, which is why they legitimised themselves by personal instrument.
- **The Nine Wells** (558) had no sealed instrument to serve and were taken by force. The pattern holds for the Covenant's whole history: legal stalemate with peoples who hold charters, permanent war with peoples who do not.
- **The First Unsealing** (568–612): breach-courts void northern charters whose witnesses were the augur colleges Vestian dissolved (383–384), and half the north is "served, not fought". The advance stops where hermit-bishops had re-sealed charters in the Fire, after the Fourth Seal is disputed (597) and Uthar dismisses Qatada (598). At **Tamsar** (611) a breach-court's ruling and a Caleran reliquary both fail before two armies; the **Suspension** of 612, brokered by the Banu Mizan, holds every disputed charter "neither void nor in force". The Velian charters wait in fireproof chests on it still.
- **The Clause of the Given** (translation convention; the native form belongs to the locked Covenant naming register and is not yet fixed): the recited covenant binds the 4,444 to "take nothing that is not given". Witnessed-Seal jurists read it for three centuries as barring the bound offices from conquest, so from 568 to 905 the Covenant fought with human armies, contract logistics, pure-pattern siege engines and breach-courts.
- **The Copper Lamp** (681–683) begins the Covenant's file of counterfeit fire in Atherian rites, which reaches its fullest form in the record of Corvenas III's accession (884).
- **The Ruling of the Broken Suspension** (884): the Second Crusade breached the instrument of 612, so the Suspension is void. In 885 Qahir's officers take the Velian client courts from the Banu Mizan regency; a Banu Mizan marriage line keeps Zanthir as tributary emirs.
- **The Ruling on Parties** (885–886): the college at Al-Qahur rules that the Clause binds parties, not powers; a captured Depth beast, a condemned man's forfeited name and a pure construct are party to nothing. Name-cage foundries and capture companies follow. The dissenting **people of the Clause** hold that a covenant of provision cannot be honoured by conquering with what it did not name; they have been outvoted, never answered.

## Locked History, 905–1190

- **Sovereigns:** Idris al-Fattah 902–931; Mahdar ibn Idris 931–979 (Regency of the Bench to 940); Mahdar's son 979–c. 1018; a short reign c. 1018–1029; Tamim the Auditor 1029–1068; one or two reigns 1068–1112; Khurzad 1112–1142; Khurzad's son 1142–1190; Suhrab 1190–1243. Unnamed reigns remain open.
- **The Sublime Threshold** dates from the Widowed Seals (929–938): Idris bound army and conquered cities to himself by name with his heir written in; the heir drowned in 929 and Idris died in 931 before the re-swearing, widowing every instrument. Oaths are since sworn to the Threshold, an office that cannot die, and witnessed at Al-Qahur. The formula is credited to the feeder Gulnar (931). Idris's Ember deposit, addressed to the final audit, survived the widowing; Veridun paid its invoice in 957.
- **Lodging** — taking a living person's true name into Vault custody so that no oath to him binds and no instrument he signs holds — is the Cull of Brothers' original form (931). The jurist's renunciation is a classical-age protection for younger sons, voided in Suhrab's case (1190). Killing and blinding enter after the Pageless Prince (1252).
- **The Vault of Names** has two houses since 1059: the Living Register at Iqashar under the Unnamed Custodian, and the Deep Vault at Al-Qahur holding the dead, the founding instruments, the Fourth Seal and the Custodians' surrendered names. Every Custodian since 1011 surrenders his name at investiture, after the first pledged his to an Iqashari lending house (1003–1011) and the army could not march.
- **Al-Qahur's military households** hold Basin estates on the fixed-term leases of 938.
- **Qazmarid** has been the Sealed Blood's base since the seventh century; its lords paid Qahir for peace in the strait, stopped paying in 933 and took the title Caliph-Wasi in that year's proclamation.
- **Zanthir's** alternate-season tribute dates from 1044.
- **The Southern Houses** are occupation-born families who went north in 1052 with Covenant deeds; their claims caused the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139).
- **Great engines:** five (905); two after Crown Gate (907); the first great mercy tower c. 944; the last great Slave-Soul engine lost before Caleran (956); second and third towers c. 1000 and in the 1080s; one tower lost 1131–1139, leaving the Elder (Depth-Core) and two mercy towers in 1190. Only Iqashar's foundry builds great frames from c. 960.
- **Tarkhan companies** have served on word-price outside the Living Register since 1067.

## Locked History, 1190–1360

- **Sovereigns:** Suhrab the Lawbinder (1190–1243), Tahmur (1243–1268), Marzuq I (1268–1301), Marzuq II (1301–1349), Marzuq III (1349–).
- **Qarath's Well** (1214) was won on a sworn well-peace; when Thalmyria blinded the ransomed emperor to "widow" the ransom, the Court of Breaches answered that an instrument sworn to the Throne cannot widow.
- **Suhrab's lodged son** (c. 1240): Guard officers swore that the prince had taken soldiers' oaths in his own name; Suhrab lodged his name in the Deep Vault and he died in its cloister within the year.
- **The Emptying of the Benches** (c. 1236–1243): in the Grey Sweat, deaths outran the witnesses needed to release bound names and hold garrison oaths; frontier garrisons marched home (the Open Gates, 1238–1246), and the living and dead registers have never balanced since. Suhrab's last book (1243) lets a sealed register stand where no witness survives and names the Guard's officer bench a competent witness for the Threshold.
- **Since 1243** the Ahdanite Guard's officer bench witnesses every accession at Iqashar; since the Pageless Prince (1249–1252), whose register page was lost in the plague, the Cull kills or blinds.
- **The Black Wind** (1265–1300): Iqashar paid Qarayel's escort fees because its bound wardens could not frighten the black colt's get. From 1304 it hires Iron Horde companies, whose horses stand beside bound things.
- **Zehrim's people** remain bound in the Living Register by the Court of Breaches' ruling of 1326.
