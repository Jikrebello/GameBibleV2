## Locked Peoples

**Ahl-e-Ahd** is an imperial-confessional identity containing several ancestries. The Ahdari supplied its founding language and a large Glass-descended core. Ahdari communities had already braided with Narashi, Tarkhan, Jirahar, oasis, Qahuri, Gateway and other populations in different regions before and during Covenant expansion. Ahdari, Iqashari, Qahuri, Qazmari, Zanthiri and Velian remain meaningful identities in 1360 AR. Tarkhan and Jirahar remain distinct peoples, although individuals and communities from both can enter Al-Ahdiya, serve Covenant states and identify as Ahl-e-Ahd in the confessional or imperial sense.

The ancient Ahdari formed in the western well country between approximately 1950 and 1400 BR. They were a durable people long before Mujalib. Their protected wells, markets, camel roads, hospitality, raiding and plural cults belong to pre-Covenant history. No ancient office inherited the Great Prophet's commission. Ark-derived law founded no public office on a bargain: ancient Ahdari wells were held by armed protectors, oath and blood-price, unlike the Narashi crown-mothers' sealed wells (c. 2255 BR). Covenant jurists call the Unpaid Well, the Prophet held to his own law, their first case.

The ancient Qahuri are principally Orphaned descendants of Sacred Basin communities which survived Ishkatar's collapse. They did not descend from the Glass host. The Gateway people were a Braided ancient population formed from older shore communities, Ahdari migrants, maritime settlers, captives and freed households. The House of Nerethan inherited a junior Qahuri maternal connection through Tazirun Zaheret, Lemara, Hesara and Hadrima; that relationship conveyed no claim over Qahur.

## Locked Geography

Iqashar lies in the far north at the Tarkhan approaches. Al-Qahur occupies the fertile basin south of it. Qazmarid lies across the western strait. Zanthir and the Velian belt lie southeast toward the Thalassian and Thalmyric frontier. South grows colder.

The [[Nine Wells of Tergai]] are ancestral Well-Tarkhan works seized by Covenant forces in 558 AR. The public First Submission tradition is historically false. White-Horde raids are Iqashar's immediate danger; Iron-Horde military settlement is its slower institutional danger.

## Locked Metaphysical Truth

The Creditor wears the mask of Malak al-Ahd and profits from Covenant rent architecture. Its interference kept the Fourth Seal dispute permanently unresolved but does not establish which succession instrument began genuine. Sincere belief, honest judgement, and lawful protection remain possible inside the corrupted system.

The [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]] predates the Covenant. A successful rite binds one complete human soul to its embalmed body and a small Egg White estate; founders able to lease access, protection or power act as Patrons. This delays the Boatman's road and final judgement without destroying or dividing the soul. Failed rites may leave infrastructure offices or copied roles, and dependent Greed Masks may parasitise those remains. The present reachability of Meresha, Khaseret, Tamekha, Deshren and other Second-House Patrons is unresolved.

Khaseret's House of the Returning Cargo went on lending after her death. Its factor Aramen of Sakhret took Qemaret around 1076 BR to recover the Yasharet crown's defaulted pledge, with garrisons the House had fed, and the last Yasharet king, Iddar, was walled alive into its harbour vault; Aramen married Iddar's sister, the "southern restoration" is his court poetry, and his crown's conquests paid the dead. Hesren III set the gilded servants of a buried Giant estate, the Single Radiance, on the tombs (c. 840–829 BR) and hung the estate's seal on his son Deshren. The Radiance was the estate's Processional Coil with its guards and Tally Hands, constructs without moral agency; the Pride Mask formerly ruled under that name is withdrawn. Deshren of Meheret took the rite and died c. 829 BR: he is a fourth successful founder, a prince who was never crowned, and his Court at Noon holds the stripped metal and the allotment tallies inside Meheret's sealed palace. Khaseret's House also lent the grain that fed Eryndor III's army in 695 BR against the right of her masters to prepare his body; the failed rite of 684 followed from that pledge.

## Locked History, 514–905

- **The Ubar Toll War** (514–523) between Atheria and Vashar leaves every well of any size in the dry country with two lawful holders: each crown voids the other's well-awards and grants the same wells again, and neither withdraws a grant at the peace. The well-country falls into feud. Mujalib (b. c. 510) grows up in it, receives the revelation in 541 beside a well with two masters and seals the shares of some sixty **kept wells** by 549.
- **The Thin Floods** (546–552) and the **yoke-sickness** (548–551), an ox murrain that spares camels, break the settled economy. In **the Bread of the Bound** (c. 549) the 4,444 dig the oasis and channels at Al-Qahur, and grain goes to those who seal their names; every name on the roll is owed bread in a failed year. The Covenant's founding proof is food. The claim that the Vault of Names began as the famine ration roll is one later jurist's; the two records are separate.
- **The end of Vashar** (555–560): famine empties the Dawn Road's relay stations, and Covenant jurists declare the last King at Dawn forsworn on the crown's own promise to keep the roads. Ruzhan horse-lords seal for bread and become Covenant road and cavalry officers. The Qahirids descend from one of their houses: a converted Vasharan line without Ahdari sacred lineage, which is why they legitimised themselves by personal instrument.
- **The Nine Wells** (558) had no sealed instrument to serve and were taken by force by the guard of the oasis wells under Zuhayr ibn Qays. The pattern holds for the Covenant's whole history: legal stalemate with peoples who hold charters, permanent war with peoples who do not.
- **The Three Seals** (563): Mujalib (d. 596) never held office and gave his authority away under three sealed instruments: Water (the guard of the wells, Zuhayr ibn Qays), Names (the college of scribes at Al-Qahur) and the Roads (the marshal Qatada Hafiz-al-Turuq). "The Hand-Sealed" are these three first holders. There was no caliph before 597, and the sequence of four Hand-Sealed Caliphs (572–601) is retired. Their heirs in 1360 are the caliph of the wells at Al-Qahur, the Vault of Names and the Sultan-Caliph's roads.
- **The First Unsealing** (568–612): the roll has outgrown the Basin's bread and the bound carriers lift only what is given, so breach-courts go north to make grain a lawful gift. They void northern charters whose witnesses were the augur colleges Vestian dissolved (383–384); served towns enter their people on the roll, owe a share of each harvest (**the Given**) and receive a bound keeper at the town well, and half the north is "served, not fought". At the **Dry Ford** (575) the northern field army beats Qatada and breaks up at wells that draw only for the roll. The advance stops where hermit-bishops had re-sealed charters in the Fire, when the 4,444 are all placed, and after the Fourth Seal is disputed (597): **Zuhayr** becomes the first caliph on the college's instrument, and **Qatada** halts the northern army (598) until the seals can be tried. At **Tamsar** (611) a breach-court's ruling and a Caleran reliquary both fail before two armies; the **Suspension** of 612, brokered by the Banu Mizan, holds every disputed charter "neither void nor in force". The Velian charters wait in fireproof chests on it still.
- **Bir Qassim** (603): after a flood pushes Hilal's household off the road named in Qatada's safe conduct, the bound keeper of the station well will not draw for the fifty-eight named, and Jarud ibn Sulayt's court at Qafra rules the protection void. Sixteen die of thirst, Rumaitha bint Mujalib among them. The escort refuses to disarm the rest and carries thirty-seven to the strait; Qatada, ordered to arrest it, rides after it and dies on the road back in 604, and the Seal of the Roads passes to the college. A column of the guard of the wells kills the five still alive at the well and fourteen drovers who had given them water. Witnessed registers count five dead, mourning houses thirty-five. Hilal lives veiled at Qazmarid until 631. The Reading of the Wells repeats his reading at the well-mouth. Sealed-Blood wells are drawn by hand. The keeper is a bound devil that took the cruellest reading its instrument allowed before any court spoke. The well ran dry within the year because Zuhayr had the keeper recalled and the shaft silted ([[The Fourth Seal - Authorial Truth]]).
- **The Clause of the Given** (translation convention; the native form belongs to the locked Covenant naming register and is not yet fixed): the recited covenant binds the 4,444 to "take nothing that is not given". Witnessed-Seal jurists read it for three centuries as barring the bound offices from conquest, so from 568 to 905 the Covenant fought with human armies, contract logistics, pure-pattern siege engines and breach-courts.
- **The Copper Lamp** (681–683) begins the Covenant's file of counterfeit fire in Atherian rites, which reaches its fullest form in the record of Corvenas III's accession (884).
- **Rab Yequtiel of Zanthir** (c. 748–831), a Jirahar physician, served fifty years as release-witness at bound deathbeds in Zanthir's bond-hospitals and salt pans and wrote ***What the Register Keeps***: a register holds the name and lets the person go. Custodians quoted it at bedsides, Keepers of the Path called it a licence for bondage, and Velian copyists carried it into the belt's churches. It is authorially right, since custody of a name never touches soul-routing; after 885 the foundry's name-cages made it false in cases nobody outside the foundry could identify, which is why it could not quiet occupied Caleran's funerals.
- **Qahir's rise** (878–881): he pays the unpaid northern horse from the labour of nine thousand unransomed crusade prisoners entered under his own name at Iqashar; in 881 the caliph at Al-Qahur gives him the Seal of the Roads, in the college's keeping since 604, and the new title of Sultan. Idris is the first styled Sultan-Caliph.
- **The Ruling of the Broken Suspension** (884): the Reception breached the instrument of 612, so the Suspension is void. In 885 Qahir's officers take the Velian client courts from the Banu Mizan regency; a Banu Mizan marriage line keeps Zanthir as tributary emirs.
- **The Ruling on Parties** (885–886): the college at Al-Qahur rules that the Clause binds parties, not powers; a captured Depth beast, a condemned man's forfeited name and a pure construct are party to nothing. Name-cage foundries and capture companies follow. The dissenting **people of the Clause** hold that a covenant of provision cannot be honoured by conquering with what it did not name; they have been outvoted, never answered.

## Locked History, 905–1190

- **Sovereigns:** Idris al-Qari 902–931; Mahdar ibn Idris 931–979 (Regency of the Bench to 940); Mahdar's son 979–c. 1018; a short reign c. 1018–1029; Tamim the Auditor 1029–1068; one or two reigns 1068–1112; Khurzad 1112–1142; Khurzad's son 1142–1190; Suhrab 1190–1243. Unnamed reigns remain open.
- **The Fall of Caleran** (905) was conducted by Qahir's engine-master **Barzin** and the chief jurist of the breach-court train. Idris, thirty-one and sonless at his accession, was kept at Iqashar because every oath in the army named him and nobody after him; he never saw the city, and *al-Qari* is a jurist's "Reader". The great engines came to the Aurin dyke; the Depth-Core frames waded the flood to lean on its Star's share, and the dyke was broken at dusk on the last day, drowning the poor quarters. Thalmyric tradition says the Red Star showed it and Covenant engineers say a dissolved augur college's archive did; both are true. On the last day Corvenas III's public call on the Fire failed before both armies and the holding quarters took terms. The breach-court accepted only his person and relics (the **Instrument of the Ash Hall**), and he died their captive keeper in 908. Idris's ruling on the Ember came by courier; a deposit is kept where it stands, so the basilica stayed open under guard for fifty-two years.
- **The Sublime Threshold** dates from the Widowed Seals (929–938): Idris bound army and conquered cities to himself by name (sonless until 915, he was kept from the Caleran campaign), with his heir written in from 915–917; the heir, Ruzbeh, refused at fourteen to take the instruments up (929) and Idris died in 931 before the re-swearing, widowing every instrument. A successor written in as a child holds nothing until he takes up with consent. Oaths are since sworn to the Threshold, an office that cannot die, and witnessed at Al-Qahur. The formula is credited to the feeder Gulnar (931). Idris's Ember deposit, addressed to the final audit, survived the widowing; Veridun paid its invoice in 957.
- **Lodging** — taking a living person's true name into Vault custody so that no oath to him binds and no instrument he signs holds — is the Cull of Brothers' original form; the first lodged name was Ruzbeh's (932). The jurist's renunciation is a classical-age protection for younger sons, voided in Suhrab's case (1190). Killing and blinding enter after the Pageless Prince (1252).
- **The Vault of Names** has two houses since 1059: the Living Register at Iqashar under the Unnamed Custodian, and the Deep Vault at Al-Qahur holding the dead, the founding instruments, the Fourth Seal and the Custodians' surrendered names. Every Custodian since 1011 surrenders his name at investiture, after the first pledged his to an Iqashari lending house (1003–1011) and the army could not march.
- **Al-Qahur's military households** hold Basin estates on the fixed-term leases of 938.
- **Qazmarid** has been the Sealed Blood's base since Hilal ibn Raiq was carried there from Bir Qassim in 603; its lords paid Qahir for peace in the strait, stopped paying in 933 and took the title Caliph-Wasi in that year's proclamation.
- **Zanthir's** alternate-season tribute dates from 1044: Veridun is paid while the sea is open and Iqashar while the caravans run, after Thalmyric squadrons closed the coast to the grain barges that fed the last amirates.
- **The Southern Houses** are occupation-born families who went north in 1052 with Covenant deeds and the bronze water-tokens of the occupation's canal courts; their claims caused the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139).
- **Caleran (957)**: Durayd tried to carry off the Ember; its keepers held it (eleven dead), the dockers burned his ships and he was taken alive in the harbour fort. The keepers made the protected-first terms. Caedros II kept him at Veridun until c. 971, signing the releases of the garrisons sworn to him; amirs who refused release swore their garrisons to themselves, and Thalmyria took most of those at their canal heads. No tribute passed between amir and emperor in either direction.
- **Great engines:** five (905); two after Crown Gate (907); the first great mercy tower c. 944; the last great Slave-Soul engine lost before Caleran (956); second and third towers c. 1000 and in the 1080s; one tower lost 1131–1139, leaving the Elder (Depth-Core) and two mercy towers in 1190. Only Iqashar's foundry builds great frames from c. 960.
- **Tarkhan companies** have served on word-price outside the Living Register since 1067.

## Locked History, 1190–1360

- **Sovereigns:** Suhrab the Debtor (1190–1243), Tahmur (1243–1268), Marzuq I (1268–1301), Marzuq II (1301–1349), Marzuq III (1349–).
- **The *Ledger of Ledgers*** (1190s–1243) is a book of the occasions on which Covenant law killed the Covenant's own people, entered as debts and closed with the clause owed; its first book is Bir Qassim, read at the well in 1194. Suhrab wrote it after the codes commission of 1151 had sat forty years without ranking the provincial codes. Its book of the Southern Deeds was closed by the Unburdened Deeds of 1212, which led to Qarath's Well.
- **Qarath's Well** (1214) was won on a sworn well-peace whose curb also named a debt, "the price of the water", for any guest who broke it. The well's bound warden named the price at sunset, a well for every cup, and Suhrab, who had wanted a bounded cession of the Southern Deeds districts, could not lower it: the Sultan-Caliph is not the house. He never met the emperor. When Thalmyria's chancellor argued that the debt had left the palace with the sick emperor, the Court of Breaches answered that the Throne had drunk, does not die and had not left.
- **Prince Mihrzad** (d. 1241), Suhrab's most gifted son, held the Qarath-frontier forts in the Emptying as a living witness, carried the Sweat into three and died where only his Guard escort survived; his name could not be released until Suhrab's last book (1243).
- **The Emptying of the Benches** (c. 1236–1243): in the Grey Sweat, deaths outran the witnesses needed to release bound names and hold garrison oaths; frontier garrisons marched home (the Open Gates, 1238–1246), and the living and dead registers have never balanced since. Suhrab's last book (1243) lets a sealed register stand where no witness survives and names the Guard's officer bench a competent witness for the Threshold, written so that Mihrzad could be released.
- **Since 1243** the Ahdanite Guard's officer bench witnesses every accession at Iqashar; since the Pageless Prince (1249–1252), whose register page was lost in the plague, the Cull kills or blinds.
- **The Black Wind** (1265–1300): Iqashar paid Qarayel's escort fees because its bound wardens could not frighten the black colt's get. From 1304 it hires Iron Horde companies, whose horses stand beside bound things.
- **Zehrim's people** remain bound in the Living Register by the Court of Breaches' ruling of 1326.
