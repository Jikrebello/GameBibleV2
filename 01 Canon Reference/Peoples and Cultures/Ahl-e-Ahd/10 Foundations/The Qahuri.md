# The Qahuri

The Qahuri are the cultivators, water engineers, shrine communities, boat households and market towns of the Sacred Basin. They descend principally from Orphaned human communities that survived the fall of the Serpent Kingdom of Ishkatar and rebuilt damaged riverworks under their own local councils. Their irrigation customs and seasonal labour obligations have histories extending back more than three millennia. Covenant law now attributes many surviving channels to the Covenant of the 4,444, although several of them follow much older alignments.

Qahuri life follows flood, planting, pilgrimage and harvest. Extended households pool fields and water shares; village matrons and canal elders hold practical authority that military landlords ignore at their peril. Food centres on river grain, dates, lentils, fish, herbs and festival sweets sold to pilgrims. Linen, reed work, painted ceramics and devotional recitation distinguish the Basin.

Ancient Qahur passed through several royal reunifications and divisions remembered as the **Twin-Reed Crowns**. Tenaret, Qemaret and Sakhret ranked among its enduring centres. Royal houses built canals, organised armies and supported craft settlements; they also imposed forced labour, executed rivals and enlarged mortuary estates at the expense of living households. The failed Single Radiance cult and periods of Arqeshite and High-Road rule remain visible in local ceremonies, damaged sanctuaries and competing royal histories.

A wealthy minority followed [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|the White Measure]], an occult funerary discipline which allowed a few founders to bind their human souls to embalmed bodies and private Egg White estates. These dead patrons influenced commerce and burial for centuries. They remain distinct from Ishkatari Giants, estate offices, copied household roles and Idol Masks which sometimes imitated them.

The military houses consider themselves guardians of Qahuri order. Many Qahuri consider them armed tenants who forgot the lease had an end.

At the basin's desert margin, Qahuri scouts, water families, garrison households, and ruin workers sustain [[Qasr Ilyun]]. Its breach court is a frontier institution, not a model for all Qahuri law.
