# Tarkhan Warfare and the Open Country

Tarkhan warfare is a contest over movement, water, remounts, information, and the enemy's confidence in its own road. A wall matters only after an army reaches it with food.

## Well Warfare

Well-Tarkhan forces combine camel archers, mounted scouts, curved sabres, light lances, foot bowmen, and mixed horse detachments. They conceal approach behind ridges, foul or bypass lesser water, seize draw gear, and force a heavier army to choose between a bad march and a defended well.

## Grass Warfare

Grass-Tarkhan disperse for forage and gather at signals. Mounted archery, false retreat, night movement, remount strings, falcon reconnaissance, grass fire, and attacks on baggage make pursuit expensive. Hunting clans excel at reading mass movement; pastoral clans can sustain larger cavalry forces.

## Command

A raid belongs to a recognized male leader who promises division of risk and reward. Banner-lords command larger forces through reputation and gift. Khans coordinate campaigns but must negotiate with clan leaders whose followers can leave. Wind-speakers advise; they do not ordinarily hold formal command.

## Limits

Tarkhan armies struggle against defended ports, deep forests, prolonged siege, epidemic among concentrated animals, and campaigns that cannot live from dispersed supply. Covenant forts at the Nine Wells and Aversian engines can stop them when fully supplied. Their answer is usually isolation, bribery, starvation, or waiting rather than direct assault.

## Foreign Service

Iron companies sell integrated horse and camel units to Iqashar. Ash lances guard or extort the Castaran road and serve abroad. Individual Tarkhan work as mercenaries, falconers, scouts, caravan guards, and arena fighters. Contracted service can become settlement, hostage policy, or political capture.
