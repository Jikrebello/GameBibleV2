# First Catch and Grey Sister

The **First Catch** names the grassland horse revolution from 905 to 920 AR. As Atherian frontier commands collapsed, remount studs escaped, were abandoned, or were driven north by surviving handlers. Eastern Tarkhan clans captured and bred them. A generation later the riders of those herds broke the Covenant's northern frontier in the raid season Tarkhan memory calls **the Long Reach**.

Tarkhan memory gives the first willing capture to **Grey Sister**, a pale mare who drank from a hunter's hand rather than fleeing. Later genealogies connect the finest sacred lines to her. The underlying Atherian stock had been hymn-blessed for imperial war; the blessing persists weakly and unevenly in descendants.

## The studs the Covenant expected

Sultan-Caliph Idris's clerks listed the Atherian border studs among the estates the occupation would take over after Caleran fell. The lay brothers and handlers who kept these march studs drove the herds north into the grass instead, and the occupation of the old heartland stayed an army of camels and foot for its whole half-century. Iqashari registers record the studs as an estate lost in the first winter. Tarkhan memory records them as horses that came looking for riders.

The stud brothers had sung over their foals for generations, and the blessing they left in the stock was practical: a horse of the early lines does not shy from bound things. It stands before a Depth beast, a devil's mantle or a speaking engine as it would before a dog. Tarkhan tellers say the steadiness covers bound things only; a grey horse will still bolt from a grass fire or a stampede of great-horns.

## Consequences

Within three generations cart and foot migration had become mounted migration. Mounted hunting and remount wealth reordered prestige in the camps, and eastern raids reached settled roads that had always been out of range. Western clans bought horses but kept their camels for the dry country, where the Well-Tarkhan camel civilisation was already ancient by 905. Horse genealogy became sacred record and high finance at once, and a foal accepted as Grey Sister's blood could seal a peace as solemnly as the translation of a relic.

## The Long Reach, 951

In 951 the first raid season reached the Covenant's north-eastern caravan roads and the outer wells of the escarpment towns below Iqashar. The riders came for their own reasons. Remount wealth had made horses the measure of a household, and young riders needed captives and animals to found their own. The seizure of the [[Nine Wells of Tergai]] in 558 gave raiding a grievance that every camp remembered. And the mounted camps were following the great-horn herds into range their grandparents had crossed on foot.

The Covenant's northern defence had been built against camel-borne Well-Tarkhan. It rested on silt hounds bound into the well-shafts, on hound-handlers riding with the road escorts, and on the panic a bound beast causes in ordinary animals. Against the grey lines all three failed. The hounds came up the shafts spitting silt; the grey horses stood; the riders lanced the hounds at the well-lip while the garrison's own horses screamed and tore their tethers. That season Grass-Tarkhan riders on Grey Sister's daughters watered their horses at the central well of Tergai without asking any fort's leave, joining western water memory to eastern horse memory.

The Long Reach held Iqashar's army on the grass from 951 to 957, the years when the Thalmyric armies came down on Caleran. A Thalmyric chronicle claims that Caedros I sent envoys to the khans. Tarkhan genealogists answer that the horses were Atherian, the choice was theirs, and no envoy drank at their fires.

## The Horse War and the fading grace

Iqashari registers call the raiding at its height, from about 975 to 1010, **the Horse War**. No khan united the banners, and the same riders struck Eclessian and Sestaran roads in other seasons. In the 990s Iqashar sent a great mercy tower north, an engine built to besiege cities by predicting where their people would run and proclaiming terms in their own titles. The camps simply moved. For a season the tower stood on a rise announcing surrender terms to empty pasture, and the Tarkhan called it **the Talking Hill**. Iqashar decided the north was cheaper to buy than to beat; in 1067 it chartered [[Mazrakan, Market of the Nine Measures|Mazrakan]] and began buying blessed lines and Tarkhan companies on word-price.

The blessing thins with each generation, so the danger to the Covenant was greatest while the herds were young, between about 960 and 1020, and declined slowly afterwards. Tarkhan genealogy measures that decline exactly. That is why the oldest sacred lines are priced above provinces, and why buyers at Mazrakan still ask how many generations stand between a foal and Grey Sister before they ask its price.

## The Thinning and the black colt

Tarkhan companies on the last strong lines still rode through a Thalmyric army at Qarath's Well in 1214, but by the 1230s the wind-speakers' counts showed the high lines failing. Clans had priced dowries, debts and word-prices in sacred foals for three centuries, and the steppe calls the decades when those foals lost their worth **the Thinning**.

The grace came back in a single line. In the late winter of 1249 a grey mare of Grey Sister's line dropped a black colt too weak to stand in the camp of a minor Grass-Tarkhan herd-master. **Gavrin**, a Velian farrier taken from a Covenant well-fort that the plague had emptied, knelt in the snow and sang over it the old remount hymn that Velian villages kept as a blessing for sick animals. The colt stood that night. It grew into a stallion that walked up to a bound well-warden as if it were a fencepost, and its foals did the same. The herd-master took the horse's name, Qarayel, and built the Black Wind on its sons; see [[Qarayel and the Black Wind]]. The renewed grace thins by generation as Grey Sister's did, and by 1300 the black colt's grandchildren were thinning too.
