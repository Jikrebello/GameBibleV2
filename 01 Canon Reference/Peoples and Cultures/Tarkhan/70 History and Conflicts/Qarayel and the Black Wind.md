# Qarayel and the Black Wind

**Qarayel, the Black Wind** (d. 1303 AR) was the herd-master of a minor Grass-Tarkhan clan who united the Tarkhan banners at the Kurultai of the Long Grass in 1261 and for thirty-five years made the Covenant, the Velian fairs and the Sestaran roads pay for passage through country he never tried to occupy. His name was first a horse's. *Qarayel*, black wind, was the stallion at the centre of his power, a colt from Grey Sister's line whose foals stood before the Covenant's bound things when every other horse on the frontier bolted. The rider was called by his horse's name for the rest of his life, and each Horde now remembers a different birth-name for him.

His empire rose with a renewed horse-grace and ended as it thinned. The four Hordes of 1360 are its partition.

## The Thinning

The horses the Tarkhan caught after Atheria's fall carried the old remount blessing of the imperial studs: they did not panic before bound things, devil-mantled riders or the Covenant's warded wells. Each generation carried it more faintly. Wind-speakers keep the sacred genealogies, and by the 1230s their counts showed the high lines failing. Clans had priced dowries, debts and word-prices in sacred foals for three centuries, and a foal of Grey Sister's line was worth less every year. The steppe calls those decades **the Thinning**. Feuds over unpayable debts in horseflesh broke the larger coalitions apart, and the Covenant's bound well-wardens began to hold the Nine Wells frontier again.

## The Grey Uncle and the black colt

**Gavrin** (c. 1215–1281) was a Velian farrier and church deacon from the Covenant's client belt, entered in the Covenant's registers as a bound craftsman and sent north around 1235 to shoe and doctor horses at a well-fort on Iqashar's Tarkhan frontier. About 1241, in the plague the Covenant calls the Emptying of the Benches, the garrison's oaths were held to have lost their witnesses and the soldiers marched home. The craftsmen, bound to the fort, stayed behind. Tarkhan raiders took them, and Gavrin was traded twice before he came to the herd-master of a small clan in the central grass.

In the late winter of 1249 a grey mare of Grey Sister's line dropped a black colt too weak to stand, and the herd-master's small son wept over it. Gavrin knelt in the snow, laid his hands on the colt and sang the old remount hymn that Velian villages kept as a blessing for sick animals. The colt stood that night. It grew into a stallion that would walk up to a warded well-post as if it were a fence, and its foals did the same. The wind-speakers said the old grace had come back into the grass.

Gavrin blessed sick foals and sick children and refused to bless anything for war. The grace went into the black colt's line regardless. The herd-master offered to fire-name him into the clan; Gavrin answered that he would accept when the other slaves of the camp were freed, and stayed a slave. The camp called him **the Grey Uncle**. He doctored captives, buried the dead of three faiths and kept the feasts of his own church in knots on a bridle.

In 1279 envoys of the Thalmyric emperor Evandros reached the camp to buy him for the imperial studs. He refused to leave. In 1281 he was found in the horse lines with his throat cut. Qarayel killed the envoys and sent their heads to Veridun in a feed-bag. The Moon circuit raised a cairn over the farrier.

Who killed him is still argued:
- The Tarkhan say the Thalmyric envoys, who could not take him home.
- Thalmyria, which canonised him in the 1290s as Saint Gavrin of the Halter, says Covenant name-hunters, who could find any man whose name sat in their registers. Covenant records do list him as a bound craftsman never released.
- Some steppe tellers say Qarayel himself, to keep the grace from being sold.

Velian congregations, whose fairs the Black Wind burned, call him the **Wind-Saddler** and keep no feast for him. Tarkhan mothers still say the Grey Uncle's name over a weak foal.

## The Kurultai of the Long Grass

Qarayel built his coalition by stud-right before he built it by the sword. Any clan that came to his fire under guest-right could put its mares to the black colt's sons, and every foal of that line carried the grace. By 1261 the colt had sired nine proven stallions, and the banners that gathered at the Kurultai of the Long Grass came to keep that grace in their herds. Qarayel raised a standard braided from the manes of the nine, the **Nine-Mane Standard**, and the assembly acclaimed him.

## The Black Wind years, 1265–1300

Qarayel's host moved between horse and camel country and refused any campaign whose glory depended on a fixed siege; the grace won battles in the open and nothing at a wall. He rewarded scouts, hostage keepers, interpreters, herd masters and negotiators as carefully as warriors.

In 1265 his first long ride burned the largest of the Velian fairs, where Covenant name-brokers sold Tarkhan captives taken on the Nine Wells frontier. The other fairs paid after that. Iqashar's frontier depended on bound well-wardens whose terror worked on horses that panicked; the black colt's get did not, and from 1265 the Sultan-Caliphs paid what their registers called escort fees. Sestaran caravans on the northern routes accepted the grass-toll, and Sestaran trade began to turn to the sea.

He was generous under guest-right and ruthless outside it. Captive sale, punitive grazing, hostage-taking and public devastation paid for the peace he offered afterwards. Wind-speakers kept counting the grace through each generation of foals, and they told him the window would close.

By 1300 it was closing. The third generation from the colt was thinning, Iqashar's wardens held against Tarkhan horses again, and the escort fees stopped.

## Death and grave

Qarayel died in his tent in the spring of 1303, undefeated and without an accepted successor, while preparing a war against Iqashar. His sons' lines were still riding in when, on the first night, his old stallion, a grandson of the black colt, broke its tether and came to the tent. The watchers say they saw it go out into the grass with the dead man across its back and no one at its head. Every grace-mare in the great camp pulled her pickets and followed it into the dark. Nobody followed them; the wind-speakers forbade it, and the riders' own horses would not take the trail. On the eleventh day the herds came home, lean and quiet, without the stallion and without the body. They did not come home together. They came in four bands, to four pastures a week's ride apart, and grazed there as if they had never left.

Where he lies is the steppe's holiest secret, and the Moon Horde forbids any search for it, because discovery would become a claim to universal rule. Each Horde tells the night differently. White tellers say the stallion was taking him to the sky; Iron tellers say the mares knew where the old grace came from and took him back to it; Moon wind-speakers say only that the herds came home, and that is all a living person needs to know. The grave has never been found.

## The Partition of the Herds

In 1304 his descendants met to divide what he had left, and found it divided. The most numerous herds had come home to the western grass, where the sons of his eldest line were camped. The black colt's daughters' lines had gone to the wells of his second line. The oldest mares had walked back to the assembly ground of the Long Grass, where the Nine-Mane Standard stood, and the poorest lines had drifted to the eastern roads. The assembly argued through a summer and then named what the horses had done, grace-line by grace-line:
- **White**, his eldest line through Temuchar, kept the most numerous herds and a paternity quarrel.
- **Iron**, his second line, kept the black colt's daughters' lines and bred them back carefully. Its horses would stand beside the Covenant's own bound things, and Iqashar began hiring them that year.
- **Moon** kept the Nine-Mane Standard, the oldest mares and the wind-speakers, and with them the right to call a kurultai.
- **Ash** kept the eastern grass, the poorest lines and the roads.

Nobody at the assembly chose his share, and every Horde has an account of that. White tellers say the horses knew the eldest line. Iron tellers say a mare goes to her own blood's water and there is nothing to explain. Ash tellers say the wind-speakers cut the herds in the dark and left the east what nobody wanted. Moon wind-speakers say nothing about it at all.

## Legacy

Every Horde invokes a different Qarayel: lawful ancestor, conqueror, protector of movement, or warning against settled dependency. Every Horde also counts its foals. The grace that made him is thinning again, and the wind-speakers do not agree about what the steppe will be when its horses are ordinary.
