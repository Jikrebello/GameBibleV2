# Historical Spine of the Tarkhan

The Tarkhan are Orphaned peoples of the northern dry belt and eastern grasslands. Western camel-and-well societies and eastern hunting and herding communities developed long before the capture of Atherian horses transformed regional warfare after 905 AR. Their later history follows those horses: a blessing carried in their blood that let them stand before the Covenant's bound things, its slow thinning, its renewal in one black colt in 1249, Qarayel's Black Wind and the parting of his herds into four great Horde coalitions, each of which follows its own leadership and routes in 1360.

## Orphaned Reassembly in the Dry Belt

Tarkhan ancestors descend principally from Vathran subject communities, fugitives, freed captives, client-state survivors and mobile groups left outside the Glass Ark. The Great Unyoking destroyed the Giant crown's upper roads and scattered its human population among the northern desert, seasonal lake country and eastern grasslands. Western clans developed camel circuits, well custom and caravan brokerage. Eastern communities combined hunting, small herds and movement by foot, cart and sledge. Taleri craft towns survived around grounded Vathran works.

The broad Tarkhan name entered common use during the Hearth-Crown centuries. Fosterage, marriage, assemblies and related dialects joined Tergai well houses, Elkari grass families, Taleri towns and Narashi caravan households without placing them under one government. [[The Tarkhan before the Horse Hordes]] gives the ancient history through 350 BR.

## The defence of Rethmar, 392 BR

[[Lethren Ostar#The disaster at Rethmar|Ostar's northern expedition]] threatened western well communities near the approaches later associated with Mazrakan. Kharuz of the Stone Wells organised resistance, while the water keeper Tavisha helped move supplies beyond the army's reach. Camel transport, foot archers and defended water enabled local forces to defeat separated Atherian columns. Captivity and ransom followed.

## The Nine Wells, 558 AR

During Mujalib's unification, Covenant forces captured the [[Nine Wells of Tergai]]. Fighting, expulsion, and imposed access instruments replaced Tarkhan seasonal tenure. Later Covenant histories renamed the seizure the First Submission and described it as voluntary. White-Horde reclamation and Iron-Horde service both grew from this unresolved wound.

## First Catch, 905-920 AR

The Atherian remount studs broke up after Caleran fell. Eastern Tarkhan captured and bred the scattered stock. [[First Catch and Grey Sister]] transformed travel, hunting, war, and status within three generations while western camel life continued.

## Horse Centuries, 920-1249 AR

The imperial studs had been blessed with hymns, and their horses did not panic before bound things. The Covenant's northern frontier was built on the opposite expectation: bound well-wardens, devil-mantled escorts and warded wells whose terror broke enemy horses. Against the young herds of the First Catch that terror failed. In 951, the first raid season of **the Long Reach**, Grass-Tarkhan riders broke the Covenant's hound-guarded northern wells and watered their horses at Tergai, and from then on they reached caravan roads and outer wells that had been out of range. The raiding peaked between about 975 and 1010, the years Iqashar calls the Horse War. Clans expanded their circuits, traded horses, raided border provinces and hired out as scouts and cavalry. In 1067 Iqashar chartered Mazrakan to buy what it could not beat and turn the horse frontier into customs. Tarkhan companies in Covenant pay, on the last strong lines, fouled the outer wells on the road to Qarath's Well in 1214 and rode through the Thalmyric army that drank there. No ruler kept more than a regional coalition for long. The Nine Wells hardened as the western frontier; the northern Sestaran routes developed recurring grass-tolls.

The blessing thinned with every generation. Wind-speakers tracked it in the sacred genealogies, and the price of Grey Sister's lines tracked the wind-speakers. By the 1230s the high lines were failing. Clans that had priced dowries, debts and word-prices in sacred foals could not pay them, feuds broke the larger coalitions apart, and the Covenant's bound wardens began to hold the wells again. The steppe calls this the Thinning.

## Black Wind, 1249-1303 AR

In 1249 a Velian slave farrier, Gavrin, whom the camps called the Grey Uncle, sang an old remount hymn over a dying black colt of Grey Sister's line. The colt lived, and it and its get stood before bound things as the First Catch horses had. Its herd-master took the horse's name, [[Qarayel and the Black Wind|Qarayel]], and built a coalition by offering stud-right under guest-right. At the Kurultai of the Long Grass in 1261 the banners acclaimed him under the Nine-Mane Standard, braided from the manes of the colt's nine proven sons.

Between 1265 and 1300 the Black Wind made Iqashar, the Velian fairs and the Sestaran routes pay without attempting permanent occupation. Iqashar's escort fees bought off a frontier whose wardens could not frighten Qarayel's horses. The first long ride, in 1265, burned the great Velian fair where Covenant brokers sold Tarkhan captives. Gavrin was killed in Qarayel's camp in 1281; who killed him is disputed. By 1300 the grace was thinning again, Iqashar's wardens held, and the payments stopped. Qarayel died undefeated in 1303. On the night of his death his stallion carried his body out of the camp unled, the grace-mares followed it, and the herds came home on the eleventh day without him, in four bands to four pastures. The grave remains undisclosed by policy and ritual.

## Partition and Road Peace, 1304-1360 AR

The Partition of the Herds in 1304 was the assembly at which his children's lines accepted what the horses had done. Each band had come home to one line's grass, each band was a grace-line, and the White, Iron, Moon and Ash coalitions are named for them. White kept the most numerous herds and raided; Iron kept the best-kept lines, which Iqashar hired at once because they would stand beside the Covenant's own bound things; Moon kept the Standard and the right to call a kurultai; Ash kept the eastern grass, the poorest lines and the roads. Iron-mounted companies rode with Covenant night raiders on the Thalmyric frontier in the 1310s, and Iron households have entered Covenant frontier offices since.

In 1333 Castaran surveyors staked wells across Ash pasture for an inland road, which every Tarkhan read as the Nine Wells again; the Ash banners burned the survey camps. In 1334 Castara bought the first [[Castaran Long Road|Ash Road Peace]]. By 1360 White raids threaten Covenant wells, Iron companies occupy imperial contracts, Sorqai's age makes succession dangerous, Chilgir's debt makes the Castaran peace unstable, and every Horde's wind-speakers report the grace thinning in its foals.

## Historical Atlas — wider inheritance

Western camel-and-well circuits and eastern hunting and herding developed through different ecologies. Their relations with Near-Glass peoples included marriage, ransom, trade, raids and contested water long before either the Covenant or Atheria's Fall.

The Nine Wells seizure in 558 AR violently altered western life. The First Catch, c. 905–920, supplied horses from scattered imperial studs to already-skilled eastern communities. Before it, the Tarkhan possessed a mature civilisation of wells, camel roads, grass circuits, assemblies and craft towns.

Qarayel's 1261 unification and the Black Wind years changed the wider world's costs of movement. Extraction without occupation still meant burned fairs, threatened dependants and coercion. After his death in 1303, successor hordes developed distinct policies from the herds each inherited. The grave remains protected.
