# Tarkhan and the Wider World

Tarkhan societies connect the grasslands and dry belt to surrounding empires through seasonal movement, warfare, trade and military service. Wells and declared passage prices govern their most important frontiers, while horses, camels, hides, escorts and captives move through Covenant and Sestaran markets. Abroad, Tarkhan people appear under many conditions—as clan envoys, hired cavalry, caravan guards, traders, slaves and exiles.

Most of these relationships were set in the century after 1261. Qarayel's Black Wind taught every neighbour what blessed horses could do to a frontier, and the Partition of his herds in 1304 decided which Horde each neighbour would have to deal with.

## Covenant

The [[Nine Wells of Tergai]] make the frontier sacred, economic, and unresolved. Iqashar paid the Black Wind escort fees from 1265 to 1300 because its bound well-wardens could not frighten Qarayel's horses, and stopped when the horses' grace thinned. White-Horde raids are the immediate threat to Iqashar. Iron-Horde companies are the slower danger: since 1304 Iqashar has hired them because their carefully bred horses are the only mounts on the frontier that will stand beside the Covenant's own bound things, and their armed households are entering frontier administration. Mazrakan trades animals, hides, escorts, captives and intelligence while claiming neutrality at its gates.

## Sestaran Concord

The Black Wind's grass-toll on the northern roads pushed Sestaran trade to the sea from the 1260s, and that trade made Castara. Castara now buys the annual Ash Road Peace, concluded in 1334 after Castaran surveyors staked wells across Ash pasture and the Ash banners burned their camps. Vardessa hosts horse fairs and protected fires. Aversia sells arms and demands secure freight. Sermora transports mercenaries and remounts. Fiorenzian insurers profit from both the toll and the fear of it, and Fiorenzian intermediaries carry Khan Chilgir's debts. Outside recognized member territory, the Seven-Seal Muster does not make a caravan war collective.

## Jirahar

Migdal Melach and Road Companies negotiate passage through old word-law; the flat peace-price they pay goes back to Qarayel's word. Jirahar caravans pay declared prices, hire escorts, trade animals, and sometimes shelter defeated camps.

## Eclessia and Thalmyria

Eclessian eastern roads meet Ash traders, raiders, mercenaries, and displaced camps. Thalmyric contact is more distant and commonly mediated through Covenant or Sestaran markets, and it carries an old injury on both sides. In 1279 Thalmyric envoys came to Qarayel's camp to buy the Velian farrier Gavrin, whose blessing had renewed the steppe's horse-grace; in 1281 Gavrin was found murdered and Qarayel sent the envoys' heads to Veridun. Thalmyria venerates Gavrin as a saint, and the Moon Horde keeps his cairn closed. Both churches maintain small missions; converts remain Tarkhan.

## Folk and Ättar

Folk and Tarkhan exchange animals, metal, guides, medicines, and feud stories at the edge of forest and grass. Ättar contacts bring horses, mercenary work, distant goods, and occasional slave exchange.

## Abroad

Tarkhan beyond the dry belt appear as hired cavalry, caravan guards, falconers, slaves, gladiators, scouts, traders, hostages, and exiles. Their legal status matters more than outsiders' assumption that every rider represents a Horde. Foreign buyers pay most for a horse with a wind-speaker's genealogy, and least attention to the fact that every genealogy now shows the grace thinning.
