# Word-Price, Guest-Right, and Clan Law

Tarkhan law is carried through witnesses, genealogy, remembered settlements and the public declaration of consequences. Clan assemblies recognise marriages, successions and pasture rights, while an oath's word-price establishes the compensation owed for breach. These practices travel with camps and coalitions and therefore require no permanent court building, although accepted judges and assemblies remain essential to their force.

## Word-Price

An oath names the price of its breach when sworn: animals, silver, service, exile, blood, surrender of a claim, or another measurable loss. A low price signals a limited promise. A great price advertises confidence and creates hostages to reputation.

Payment resolves the stated breach only where the act itself is compensable. Murder under guest-right, knowing sale of a free clan member, betrayal of a common muster, and some desecrations may make a person **fireless** rather than merely indebted.

## Guest-Right

Any traveler admitted to a lawful fire receives food, water, and protection for three days. The guest must disclose active pursuit, avoid theft and seduction within the host household, and refrain from mapping stores or defenses. A truthful enemy may be safer at the fire than a lying cousin.

## Assembly and Judgement

Clan assemblies recognize marriages, fosterage, adoption, succession, word-price, and pasture settlements. Disputes begin with witnesses and material proof. Champions may answer injuries but cannot turn every private quarrel into a killing feud without exposing their kin to compensation.

## Rule

Banner-lords lead by gifts, protection, and accepted judgement. Khans hold wider coalitions only while subordinate banners continue to follow. A kurultai can recognize a common khan, settle succession, or declare a campaign, but it cannot manufacture resources or erase old claims by proclamation.

## Women and Office

Women speak through household standing, property, lineage testimony, wind-speaking, and regency. Permanent khanship and formal war leadership remain male offices. Sorqai's custody of the Standard gives her exceptional constitutional weight without abolishing this rule.
