# Tarkhan Camps, Households, and Daily Life

The household is the durable Tarkhan institution. Camps gather several related households around animals, carts, tents, fires, stores, and a shared watch. A banner may contain hundreds of such units and still dissolve when pasture, feud, or a khan's poverty makes departure sensible.

## Work

Women commonly control tents, preserved food, textiles, milk economies, household goods, lineage memory, and significant herd shares. Men dominate formal raiding and war command. Both sexes herd, ride, trade, repair, hunt, treat animals, and move camp when necessity ignores custom.

Well-Tarkhan homes use camel hair, felt, reed matting, leather water gear, and low shade structures. Grass-Tarkhan camps favor felted wool, wheeled frames, horse lines, drying racks, and windbreaks. Wealth is visible in animals, metal fittings, dyed cloth, followers, and the ability to feed guests without exposing winter stores.

## Family and Fosterage

Marriage joins routes and obligations as much as individuals. Bridewealth, reciprocal gifts, herd shares, and guaranteed access to seasonal pasture are negotiated publicly. Fosterage can end a feud, train a future banner-lord, secure a hostage, or make strangers into kin. It can be affectionate and coercive at once.

## Food and Movement

Milk, dried meat, grain, roots, onions, fermented drinks, blood drawn without killing an animal, hunted game, and purchased fruit sustain travel. Well clans calculate life by water days. Grass clans calculate by pasture recovery, foaling, and herd movement. A camp that remains too long destroys the ground it needs next year.

## Status

Free clan members possess assembly voice, compensation rights, and recognized fire. Dependants and clients receive protection for service. Captives, slaves, and unacknowledged slave-born children lack full standing. Adoption and manumission can change status but require witnesses and public obligation.
