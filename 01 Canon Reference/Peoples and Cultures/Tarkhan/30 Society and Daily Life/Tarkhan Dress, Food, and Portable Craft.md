# Tarkhan Dress, Food, and Portable Craft

Tarkhan material culture assumes that a household may have to move. Wealth must travel, protect the body, feed a guest, repair an animal, or prove a remembered obligation.

## Dress

Layered coats, wrapped shirts, loose riding trousers, boots, belts, veils, caps, and weather cloaks vary by ground and season. Well-Tarkhan favor light woven layers, face protection, leather water gear, and camel-hair cloth. Grass-Tarkhan use felt, wool, fur edging, rain capes, and riding coats reinforced at seat and knee.

Color and trim identify household, marriage connection, mourning, or banner service more reliably than a universal Horde uniform. Metal plaques, horn toggles, embroidery, beadwork, feather cases, and inherited belts carry portable status. Warriors may own curved sabres or composite bows, but most adults also carry knives and repair tools.

## Food

Milk, curds, hard cheese, dried or boiled meat, rendered fat, grain porridge, flat bread, onions, roots, fermented drinks, and purchased fruit form the ordinary table. Blood may be drawn from living herd animals during severe travel. Great-horn hunting adds preserved meat and fat to some northeastern camps without defining every diet.

Hospitality food is deliberately better than travel food. A poor household may borrow stores to honor three-day guest-right and then pursue compensation through kin.

## Portable Craft

Felt-making, weaving, leatherwork, saddle construction, bow-making, horn carving, rope, water gear, cart repair, metal fitting, and animal medicine are high-status practical arts. Falcon hoods, jesses, travel perches, and weather cases are specialist work. A beautifully made object earns prestige because it survives use, not because it escaped it.
