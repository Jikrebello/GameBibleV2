# Raiding, Slavery, and Manumission

Raiding supplies portable wealth, reputation, captives, foreign weapons, and followers. Assemblies distinguish raids against declared enemies, private revenge, licensed foreign service, and unlawful attacks on protected guests, but victims rarely find those distinctions meaningful.

## Thralls

Captives work farms, halls, workshops, ships, fisheries, and mines and may be sold abroad. Sexual coercion and captive concubinage are common. The institution is materially central and objectively evil.

Thralls possess no full weregild. Owners may punish, sell, separate, or exploit families within broad customary limits. Killing another person's thrall is commonly judged as property damage; killing one's own may go unpunished unless it violates sanctuary or an oath.

A thrall-born child follows the mother's status. An Ättar father may publicly acknowledge, free, and name the child into his hall; doing so does not free the mother. Unacknowledged mixed children therefore remain among the enslaved population despite obvious kinship.

## Routes Out

Freedom may come through self-purchase, a master's grant, adoption, ransom, escape, military service, or public naming. Freed people retain reduced compensation rights for at least one generation in many halls and may remain economically bound to former owners.

Foreign markets buy and sell Ättar as well. Captured raiders appear as oarsmen, household slaves, mercenaries under coercive contracts, and gladiators in Covenant and Vortharan arenas. The Ättar are practitioners of slavery, not immune beneficiaries of it.
