# War of the Empty Halls

The **War of the Empty Halls**, fought from approximately 1608 to 1596 BR, was the greatest ancient Grobi invasion of the Ättar hall country. Gharuk Eight-Bowls united nine drum companies to seize northern settlements and establish a permanent food road. The first defeats exposed the inability of rival halls to coordinate their stores and defences. Four coalitions eventually broke the invasion after their chiefs accepted [[Mother Moon's Bargain]] and attacked the Grobi ration system.

The war preserved the Ättar ranges but bound their ruling houses to the firstborn exchange. It did not end the annual Grobi war season or create one Ättar state.

## The Nine-Drum Host

Grobi packs breed and feed through the warmer months, then move north when winter closes the southern hunting grounds. Most hosts break after one or two raids. Three abundant summers before 1608 BR supported unusually large herds, many orcs and several ogres south of the Grey-Wolf approaches. Early freezes then killed grazing animals and trapped stored carcasses beyond broken river ice.

**Gharuk Eight-Bowls**, an orc-stage commander, proposed conquest instead of dispersal and bodily reversion. He stood between seven and nine feet tall and possessed at least the strength of five human men. Food, breeding access and the promise of human settlements brought nine large companies under his drums. He defeated or consumed the leaders who resisted.

The hobgoblin **Varka Red-Ladle** made the gathering mobile. She organised rough ration sledges, scouts, breeding hearths and repair crews. Human prisoners carried loads, translated warnings and opened roads until the Grobi ate them. Varka reserved part of the first captured stores for the young because their survival would preserve the host's future numbers.

Wrath speakers named the invasion the opening of the **Warm Road**. Gharuk sought northern halls, livestock and granaries able to maintain large stages through winter.

## Urzha's betrayal

The Narrow-Bowl Hearths resisted Gharuk after his orcs consumed stores reserved for their young. Gharuk seized two camps, ate their leaders and placed the surviving brood on Varka's sledges. When food declined, he began feeding those young to his ogres.

**Urzha**, the strongest surviving Narrow-Bowl leader, withdrew with her scouts. She later sold Gharuk's ration route to the Ättar coalitions so that human fighters would destroy the stronger predator above her. The humans promised a marked eastern corridor and agreed to withhold attack while her pack escaped. Urzha preserved her own breeding strength; she did not join the human cause or become their friend.

## The opening defeats

The Nine-Drum Host entered the north-eastern passes with preserved meat, sledges, captured animals and human captives kept as marching food. Scouts followed old Nhalduran routes which the hall defences considered impassable. A beacon line failed after one chief withheld replacement watchers during a feud.

Human messengers found no common command. Wolf-affiliated halls demanded immediate reinforcement. Ox chiefs wanted the drove gates held. Lion crews refused to beach their vessels before the autumn fish catch. Bear holds feared leaving inner settlements exposed. Gharuk defeated them separately.

The Grobi took **Hall Arven** with its roof, granary and livestock intact. They ate the defenders and noncombatants during the victory feast. **Keld Varn** burned after its defenders spoiled the food and withdrew. At **Morn Gate**, captives being kept for later consumption were forced to reopen a collapsed road while Grobi breeding hearths occupied the warm animal rooms. Other settlements emptied before attack, and people without carts or fodder died during flight.

The name *Empty Halls* first described the captured and abandoned settlements. Later songs claimed that every inhabitant died, but refugee registers and later marriages record survivors.

## Four coalitions

The remaining halls gathered at the closed road above Hornwall. Four chiefs brought different resources:

- **Savar of Red Shoal** commanded Lion-affiliated boat crews and supplied dried fish and fast coastal fighters.
- **Torvek of Hornwall** held the Ox drove gate and brought cattle, heavy shields, carts and engineers.
- **Brennar of Stoneden** supplied Bear infantry, charcoal, iron and the largest refuge stores.
- **Eldran of Grey Pass** led Wolf scouts and survivors of the broken beacon line. His following possessed the best route knowledge and the least food.

**Hedra Store-Keeper** supervised the common granary. Each coalition surrendered its keys and recorded withdrawals publicly. The rule prevented fighter rewards and chiefly feasts from consuming the shares needed by dependants, scouts and boat crews.

Urzha gave this assembly the Nine-Drum ration route and the position of her captured brood. Her information made an attack upon Varka's sledges possible.

## Mother Moon's price

The four chiefs still lacked the force to confront Gharuk before another hall was consumed. They climbed to a lunar thin place above the closed road and asked the Pale Household to place the beasts of their standards in their bodies and the beasts' fury in every spear sworn beneath them.

The Court divided the words into two gifts. Savar received a Lion hybrid, Torvek an Ox, Brennar a Bear and Eldran a Wolf. Each monstrous form attached to the named chief's paternal office line. The wider sworn population received the lesser rage inheritance, which later intermarriage spread through the Ättar.

Each ruling line owed the first living child of its future pact-holder. The Court would take the child during sleep beneath open moonlight and leave a changeling in the bed. The substitute would grow at a human pace and depart at adolescence. The original would then return at the same apparent age, still bearing a human soul but lacking an uninterrupted memory of the stolen years.

Hedra opposed the bargain and demanded that parents and halls carrying the future cost be heard. The chiefs argued that the Nine-Drum Host would consume more settlements before another All-Thing could assemble. They accepted the terms.

## Breaking the Warm Road

The counteroffensive attacked supply before massed strength. Wolf scouts and Urzha's guides reached Varka's sledge route. Lion boats carried grain and casualties between surviving halls. Ox engineers closed the drove road behind one Grobi division. Bear infantry held Morn Gate while the ration camp was attacked.

The transformed chiefs fought at separate positions. Savar opened a captured gate from a burning roof. Torvek overturned a barricade and nearly died after remaining too long in hybrid form. Brennar held Morn Gate while refugees crossed. Eldran pursued the runners carrying Gharuk's reserve food order.

Varka shortened fighter rations to preserve the young. Gharuk instead opened brood sledges to feed his ogres. Several companies abandoned him when their leaders began to shrink or could no longer promise food.

Gharuk was wounded near Hall Arven and lost the mass which had made his command visible. His own orc guard dragged him down and ate him alive, each competitor trying to claim the former chief's strength through his flesh. Ättar songs give the killing to the four chiefs, but accounts of their positions place them elsewhere. They broke his command; the Grobi completed the succession feast.

Varka withdrew several hearths. Urzha recovered part of her brood and escaped through the promised corridor. A later human company attacked Grobi stragglers after the marker fell, and victors killed wounded Grobi and young in two captured camps. They also enslaved human prisoners whom the host had kept as food, accusing them of service without distinguishing coercion from willing aid.

## Consequences

The war ended through broken companies and guarded withdrawals by c. 1596 BR. Annual Grobi invasions continued.

The victory made the four pact chiefs powerful and gave their coalitions lasting names. Assemblies required future old-rite chiefs to descend from an eligible paternal line, win selection by free hall men and transform publicly. The coalitions became the Lion, Ox, Bear and Wolf pact-kindreds rather than four states.

The firstborn price began with the next generation. Returned children became Moon-Seers. Hedra's store rules also endured: major musters required named keepers, public withdrawal marks and protected shares for the people sustaining the fighters.
