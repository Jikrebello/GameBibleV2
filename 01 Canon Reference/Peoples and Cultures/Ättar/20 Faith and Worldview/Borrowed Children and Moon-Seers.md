# Borrowed Children and Moon-Seers

Every traditional ruling line produces two children from one absence: the **borrowed child**, a changeling raised in the hall, and the **returned child**, who comes home at adolescence to become its Moon-Seer.

## Borrowed Children

Changelings are lunar courtiers in a mortal childhood rather than disguised demons. They eat, learn, quarrel, fear punishment, and can love their foster household. Their differences appear in small ways: wrong responses to daylight, perfect recall of tides, animal unease, and dreams shared with other borrowed children.

They remain compelled to leave when called. A hall that cages one risks fog-tides, failed births, uncontrolled transformations, and the attention of senior courtiers. Some families grieve the changeling as deeply as the child first taken.

## Returned Children

The returned child remembers the Court in fragments: long hunts beneath unmoving moons, lessons in names and scent, and years that do not fit mortal time. They may return affectionate, cold, frightened, devout, or furious. Difference is not proof of corruption.

Returned children of any sex become Moon-Seers and stand outside the ordinary division of male and female offices. They interpret lunar omens, witness installations, identify early moon-loss, maintain exchange histories, and advise the hall on bargains. They may testify in a berserker challenge but cannot decide it.

When a Moon-Seer dies or becomes unable to serve, the next returned child inherits the office. In gaps between returns, senior women preserve ordinary rites but cannot speak with the full authority of one taught by the Court.
