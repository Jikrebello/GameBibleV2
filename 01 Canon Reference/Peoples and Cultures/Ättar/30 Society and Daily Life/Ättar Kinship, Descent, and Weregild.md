# Ättar Kinship, Descent, and Weregild

## Hall and Descent

The acknowledged father transmits totem, pact lineage, and ordinary hall membership. A child of an Ättar father and foreign mother can be fully Ättar after public naming, though moon-blood and rage are often weaker or unreliable. A child of an Ättar woman and foreign father belongs to the mother's household but requires adoption and naming by an Ättar paternal line to enter a totem kindred.

Children of two Ättar parents usually carry the strongest moon-blood. Appearance does not determine inheritance. A hall's genealogy matters because it identifies responsibility, privilege, and the lines from which a skinwalker chief may be selected.

## Household Power

Men hold chiefly, raiding, and formal warrior offices. Women ordinarily control stores, keys, textile wealth, household land-use, marriage negotiation, fosterage, genealogy, healing, and domestic ritual. A chief who loses the support of the women controlling his winter stores discovers the limit of transformed strength.

Moon-Seers of either sex are exempt from this division. Returned children cannot be enslaved, married without consent, or used as compensation.

## Fosterage and Marriage

Fosterage creates political kin stronger than ordinary friendship. Marriages join halls but do not erase the wife's birth genealogy or property claims. Captive concubinage and forced unions exist and are coercive regardless of later acknowledgment of children.

## Weregild

Compensation depends on legal status, household standing, public office, and circumstances. Free hallfolk possess full blood-price; dependants and freed people possess reduced but enforceable prices; thralls are usually treated as property loss rather than equal persons. Killing under sanctuary, murdering a guest, violating a truce, or concealing kinship sharply increases payment and may make compensation impossible.

Weregild limits feuds but does not make the hierarchy just. Refusal, inability to pay, or disagreement over status can begin generations of retaliation.
