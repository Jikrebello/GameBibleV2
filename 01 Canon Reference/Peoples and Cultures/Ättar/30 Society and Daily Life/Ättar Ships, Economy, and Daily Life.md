# Ättar Ships, Economy, and Daily Life

Most Ättar do not spend the whole year raiding. Hall economies combine cold-grain farming, cattle and sheep, fishing, whaling, forestry, charcoal, bog and mountain iron, fur trapping, weaving, metalwork, shipbuilding, and seasonal trade.

## Taking the Keel

To **take the keel** is to join a seasonal voyage. A crew may raid, trade, escort, explore, carry mercenaries, hunt whales, or accept foreign privateering terms. The phrase describes an occupation and risk, not a single legal category. A captain can be a licensed ally in one port and a murderer in the next.

## The Hall Year

Spring repairs ships, fences, fields, and roof turf. Summer favors voyages, distant pasture, construction, and markets. Autumn brings crews home, settles shares, judges quarrels, and fills stores. Winter turns halls into crowded workshops, feast-houses, courts, hospitals, and military refuges.

Long hair and braids carry family and campaign meanings. Bronze, silver, and gold torcs display gifts received and loyalties owed. Cutting an oath-torc or shaving a captive can be a legal humiliation rather than fashion.

## Built World

Coastal halls combine feast-house, boathouse, palisade, smokehouse, raised granaries, and protected wells. Inland halls favor turf insulation, attached livestock space, ringworks, beacon ridges, hidden food stores, and narrow approaches suitable for fighting Grobi. Chiefly doors and high seats are built to accommodate a hybrid ruler.

Skalds preserve genealogy, compensation judgements, voyages, insults, and losses. A beautiful song can establish a claim; a deliberately false genealogy can begin a feud.
