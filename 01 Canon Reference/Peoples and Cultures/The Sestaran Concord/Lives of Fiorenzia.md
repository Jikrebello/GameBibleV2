# Lives of Fiorenzia

The Concord's salon and its counting-house — where the money is oldest, the art is newest, and the one Sestaran institution of bondage wears grey.

## The Contado Tenant

Born in the Fiorenzian Highlands under a short commercial lease: vines, olives, mulberry for the silk-reels, all priced by the city's exchanges. His fork is the **mezzadria bargain** — the half-shares contract with an urban landlord that trades independence for capital (vines take four years to pay; the landlord's ledger waits, at interest). Good years buy his daughter a guild-dowry in town; two bad harvests in sequence put him on the road every highland family fears: the one that ends at the **Corps assessor's** table.

## The Debtor and the Grey Corps (Fiorenzia's institution)

Insolvency here is not prison — it is *conscription*: the **Debtors' Corps** (canon) takes the formally bankrupt into uniformed labor-service — roadworks, flood-dikes, siege-lines when the city fights — wages garnished to the ledger, term set by the debt's arithmetic. The grey coats are called **walking ledgers**; their column drills on feast-days as civic theater (creditors watch with satisfaction, the portside with arithmetic of its own). The road's texture: social death survivable by numbers — Corpsmen who work out clean re-enter citizenship scrubbed (the books say *paid*, and in Fiorenzia the books are the soul); the broken sign again within a year; and the Corps' recruiting sergeants know to the week when the mezzadria harvests fail. **The whisper the Bank hates:** some Corps paper never amortizes no matter the payments — and the men who hold such paper have started comparing clauses. (The Under-Ledger cell inside somebody's ledgers is a Left-Hand canon hook; Fiorenzia is where it would sing.)

## The Factor (the Medori's man abroad)

The city's proudest road: apprenticed at twelve to the counting-floors, cipher-trained, posted at twenty to a foreign desk — Grenzburg, Qabir, Strazgorod — where he is the family's eyes, the branch's judge, and utterly alone. The forks: whose paper to refuse (a factor's real art is the loan *not* made); how to report a partner's son's losses; and the career-summit choice when the family offers the **inner books** — the ones (per the Medori's hidden-lineage canon) that are said to be written in two hands. Factors who see the inner books do not retire abroad.

## The Artist and the Humanist

Patronage is the ladder: workshop apprenticeship, guild mastery, then the competition for commissions that are always also politics (a Medori altarpiece is a balance-sheet statement). The city still works in **Severin's shadow** — fourteen years since the Pyre of Masks, and every studio keeps one drawer of things it would burn first *this* time, just in case. The humanist track runs through the academies Lauro built: Vetti's heirs, careful now, teaching princes' sons rhetoric and keeping their sharpest manuscripts in cipher. The road's paradox: Fiorenzia pays genius lavishly and remembers, vividly, that it once hanged it in the square.

## Priest, Bishop, Noble, Criminal

The bishop is always a great family's second son and usually a competent administrator (the see is a rung, not a calling, and the city prefers it that way — *called* bishops make bonfires). Parish clergy run rich confraternities — the lay brotherhoods that do the actual charity, bury the poor, and march magnificently. The nobility IS the money, aged: Medori satellites, marriage-brokered, Diadem-courted (the Left Hand's beautiful invitations do well here). The signature crime is **forgery** — art, attribution, letters of credit, provenance; the guild of appraisers is the real police, and the best forgers are failed masters, which everyone finds almost unbearably poignant.

## Hooks

A grey-coat column refuses to march on a feast-day, in perfect discipline, holding its paper in the air; the inner-books offer made to a factor the party knows; Severin's confiscated drawer resurfaces at auction, uncatalogued.
