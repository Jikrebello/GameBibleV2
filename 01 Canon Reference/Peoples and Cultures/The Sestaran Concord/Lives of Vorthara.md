# Lives of Vorthara

The mill-town of the uncanny. Forget the tower on the hill: Vorthara is a *working city* whose heavy industry happens to be the arcane — priced, policed, guild-regulated, and leaking. The Black Spire is its blast furnace; the districts below live downwind.

## The Ordinary Vortharan (most of the city)

Landlords who rent to apprentices (with damage clauses three pages long), chandlers selling candles that must *not* be tallow near the Elixirium quarter, arena vendors, reagent-porters, laundresses who charge triple for robes ("you know why"), and the two civic roles no other city has: the **bone-cart men** (licensed collectors of the industry's failures, human and otherwise — their guild's silence is contractual and absolute) and the **leak-wardens** — the district watch that walks the gutters with brass rods and canary-lamps, chalking the hazard-signs every Vortharan child reads before letters: *don't touch, don't drink, don't answer if it greets you.* On **leak days** (declared by horn) the affected district shutters, the wardens walk, and the liability courts convene within the week, because in Vorthara even the impossible has a tortfeasor.

**The strange-dose here breaks the continental rule** — a Vortharan porter has seen more of the uncanny by twenty than a marches priest sees ever — and the result is the city's famous temperament: not wonder but *competence*. The Vortharan shrug is different from the Aurelium shrug: Aurelium believes and is unimpressed; Vorthara is impressed and bills for it.

## The Apprentice (the tuition-bond road)

Entry to the schools (canon: the Crucible, the Elixirium, the Ironbound, the Fleshweavers, the Nightveil, and the rest) is by talent-test and **tuition-bond**: the school fronts the ruinous fees against years of post-mastery service. The forks: which school (the Crucible is glory and export; the Elixirium is steady money; the Fleshweavers pay best and cost most, in every sense; the Nightveil doesn't recruit, it *notices* you); and the washout question, which is the road's real texture — most who test in never master, and the city is staffed by its own failures: half-trained scriveners, wardens, proof-assistants, arena-surgeons, all still paying their bonds. Vorthara wastes nothing, including its disappointments.

## The Crucible Warlock (the export)

The war college's product (canon: gauntlet-casters, elemental specializations, sub-faction rivalries): arena-proven, contract-exported, the Concord's answer to knighthood. The career: cadet years under the sub-factions (Emberblades, Frostguard, and their rivals), the **arena ladder** — Vorthara's blood-sport circuit is examination, advertisement, and settlement of institutional disputes in one — then the export contracts: siegework for Aversian companies, ducal retainers in Eclessia, the standing offers from Iqashar nobody officially takes. The forks: specialization (the gauntlet's rotating bands reward focus and punish it — a pure Pyromancer is magnificent until it rains); whose war to decline (Crucible paper forbids contracts against Vorthara itself and is silent about everything else, and every graduate learns what that silence costs someone eventually); and the veteran's fork — teaching bench, arena mastery, or the quiet third door into the Pale Hand's brokerage, where the real money and the [[The Concord of Weights|actual landlords]] are.

## The Gladiator

The Flesh Pits (canon) run two rosters: the **made** (Fleshweaver constructs and rebuilt volunteers — the line between those two categories is a liability-court question with case law) and the **free blades** — debt-driven, glory-driven, or Crucible cadets making rent. Careers are short, celebrity is real (pit-champions endorse reagent brands), and the surgeons' row behind the arena is the best trauma medicine in the world, for exactly the reasons you'd guess. The road's fork is always the same one, offered in a back room with excellent manners: *we can make you faster.* Everything after that fork is a different road.

## Priest, Noble, Criminal

The interdict means no bishop: chapels cluster *outside* the walls (the Lamplighters' Gate row), served by clergy officially "ministering to travelers" for three centuries of wink. The Council of Magi (canon) is the nobility — school-masters and faction-heads whose feuds are the city's politics and occasionally its weather. Crime is inventory-shaped: reagent theft, script-piracy, unlicensed castings (the liability courts' criminal docket), grave-goods for the Fleshweavers, and the standing quiet trade the leak-wardens are really watched for — *selling what leaks* before reporting it.

## Hooks

A leak day that doesn't end; a bone-cart man breaks guild silence to the party, once, in five words; the arena's newest champion doesn't sleep, and her endorsements have started appearing in districts she's never visited.
