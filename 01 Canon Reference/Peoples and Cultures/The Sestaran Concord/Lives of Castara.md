# Lives of Castara

The gold-rush city: the Concord's youngest life, loudest streets, and longest odds — last safe port before the Boiling Sea, first claimant on whatever the Emerald Expanse turns out to be.

## The Emigrant-Colonist (the road everyone came to walk)

They arrive on every tide: Virelian runaways at the end of their long road, Eclessian second sons, Fiorenzian bankrupts one step ahead of the grey coat, veterans, dreamers, and the merely hungry. The road: work the docks or the yards to raise the **passage-share** (or sign for it against Expanse wages — ordinary debt here, not Fiorenzia's institution, though the interest is frontier-honest); survive the **crossing** — six to ten weeks, hurricane season permitting, through the Shattered Isles' pirate politics (canon) with a Naris-token in every fist; then the Expanse itself: claim-stakes, company camps, and a land that is (per the Age-of-Chains canon) *already someone's grave* — every strike is also an excavation, and the veterans' first rule is the region's whole theology: **if the ground is worked stone, work elsewhere.** The forks: company wage versus own-claim gamble; what to do with the *other* kind of find (the University pays well, the smugglers better, and some finds pay you); and the return question — Castara's streets are salted with men who struck it rich and came back, men who didn't and came back, and the unsettling third kind: the ones who came back *different*, about whom the dockside has songs it sings only sober.

## The Shipwright and the University Scholar

The respectable roads: the yards (galleon-frames, storm-keels, the Viscaran legacy — canon) where a master shipwright outranks most nobles in practical honor; and the **University** (canon: Astronomer's and Navigation Guilds, artifact research) — the Concord's youngest and hungriest school, where a talented nobody can rise on mathematics alone, chart-work is state secret, and the **artifact wing** does the world's most dangerous cataloguing behind the world's most cheerful facade. The scholar's fork: publish, patent, or *report quietly* — because some charts and some catalogue entries get bought before they're finished, and the buyers' agents include (the careful have noticed) parties whose interest in Giant-land archaeology is not academic. (The Left Hand and the Concord of Weights both shop here; the University pretends otherwise and locks the good vault twice.)

## The Merchant Prince and the Displaced

The new elite (canon): expedition-financiers whose fortunes are three voyages old, building marble on streets that were fish-sheds a generation back — and the **old families** (canon: the pushed-aside lesser nobility), whose road is the city's quiet bitterness: rents from land they're forced to sell, daughters married to money they despise, and a genuine grievance the Princes keep purchasable but never purchased. Their salons are where Castara's memory lives, and where its sabotage gets funded when the bitterness curdles.

## Priest, Soldier, Criminal

Religion is departure-shaped: the **Blessing of Fleets**, chapels-of-departure along the quays (Naris everywhere; Dorn for the landward roads), and a frontier clergy that has quietly adopted the missioner's flexibility about what colonists bring back in their nightmares. The soldiery is naval and private — Prince-funded escort squadrons, University marines for the survey ships, and the wall-militia that watches the pirate isles' weather. Crime is claim-shaped: jumped stakes, salted finds, forged assay-marks, artifact-smuggling past the University registry (the good stuff moves in fish-barrels, which every inspector knows and some retire on), and the crimps who sign drunks onto crossings they never agreed to — the Gold Rush's oldest and ugliest trade.

## Hooks

A returned colonist speaks fluent court-Atherian he never learned; the University's locked vault requests — by name — an appraiser the party knows; an old-family salon offers to fund the expedition nobody sane would, to the site their grandfather's charts marked with a bird.
