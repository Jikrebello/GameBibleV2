### **I. Shared Hope, Divided Sight**

Both the **Thalmyric** and **Eclessian Churches** hold fast to the foundational hope of all faithful: that the **Redeemer will return**, bringing about the final victory over the Adversary, the end of suffering, and the dawn of a **New Kingdom**—one without pain, corruption, or decay.

This belief is rooted in the shared holy text known as the **Scroll of the End** (parallel to the Book of Revelation), written by the apostle-seer **John of the Burning Isle**, whose final vision was said to be **transcribed by fire into ash-parchment**. Though both Churches uphold the Scroll as sacred, their interpretations have diverged over time.

Despite their differences, both acknowledge the core truth: **no soul shall know the hour**, and **the kingdom that is coming is not of this world—but already unfolding within it**.

Mainstream theology across both traditions affirms that:

- No one knows the time of the Redeemer’s return.
- There will be no singular prophet who arises to declare the end.
- The faithful are called to live **in constant spiritual readiness**, trusting in divine timing.
- Signs—such as wars, false prophets, disasters, and upheaval—will precede the end, but these are **symbolic and open to interpretation**, not roadmaps.

---

### **II. The Eclessian Interpretation**

The Eclessian Church emphasizes the Redeemer's **cosmic kingship** and the divine order that His return will restore.

#### **Living Models of Faithfulness**

- Some Eclessians speak of historical or future figures who may exemplify **virtue, wisdom, and spiritual leadership**, especially in times of chaos.
- These are **not heralds of the end**, but **reflections of the Redeemer’s light**—living reminders to remain vigilant.
- They are held up as teachers and reformers who **strengthen the Church**, not as agents of divine conclusion.

#### **The Kingdom Now and to Come**

- The present world, when rightly ordered, can **foreshadow the coming Kingdom**.
- The Church must labor to **bring moral clarity, education, and discipline**, forming citizens ready for divine rule.
- Revelation is viewed as an **allegorical book**, rich in symbols to be interpreted carefully and rationally.

> _“The Logos will return as Judge and King. Let the mind be sharpened, that the soul may be ready.”_ — From the Aureliate Commentary on the End

---

### **III. The Thalmyric Interpretation**

The Thalmyric Church views the end not as a time of political realignment, but of **cosmic purgation**—a final reckoning through fire, suffering, and transcendent glory.

#### **The Burning Saint as Metaphor**

- Some speak in poetic or visionary language of a "Burning Saint"—not a specific foretold individual, but a **symbol of the sanctified life**, lived amid fire and hardship.
- Such imagery represents the **call to spiritual endurance**, not the arrival of a final figure who will usher in the end.

#### **The Kingdom Now and to Come**

- The Kingdom has already begun, hidden in the hearts of the faithful, **burning like coals beneath ash**.
- Its full revelation will come only when the world is **purified by divine fire**.
- The Scroll of the End is **read aloud during holy vigils**, believed to stir the Flame-Spirit in the faithful.

> _“The Redeemer will come not through gates, but through flame. And His fire will not be stopped.”_ — From the Ashbound Canticles

---

### **IV. Prophecy, Tension, and Unity in Mystery**

While the Eclessians prepare their minds and cities, and the Thalmyrics prepare their souls and sanctuaries, both know this: the Redeemer’s return will be **beyond all knowing**, and no flame, scroll, or vision can fully grasp the hour.

Figures of reform, sanctity, or inspiration may arise in each generation, but these are not eschatological agents. They are **witnesses to the faith**, not signs of the end.

There are rare joint readings of the Scroll of the End, particularly during eclipses or celestial alignments, where both traditions chant their interpretations side by side—not to debate, but to **wait together**.

---

### **V. Conclusion: One Flame, One Return**

Though divided in emphasis—one seeking clarity, the other communion—both Churches are bound in the deeper truth:

- That the Redeemer shall return.
- That the world groans in preparation.
- That His Kingdom is both **now** and **not yet**.

And as the final lines of the Scroll declare:

> _“He comes not by mind alone, nor flame alone, but by both. Prepare the altar of the soul, for the fire is near.”_
