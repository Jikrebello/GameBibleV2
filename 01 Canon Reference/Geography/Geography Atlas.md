# Places and Geography

The known world lies in the southern hemisphere. Its northern drylands give way to temperate inner-sea countries and then to the increasingly cold forests, mountains and seas of the south. Rivers, harbours, wells, roads and winter passes shape the reach of states and the lives of the communities between them.

The present map records useful relationships rather than a finished cartographic settlement. Named countries, cities and routes are established, while many exact coastlines, borders and travel distances remain subject to later survey. The [[01 Canon Reference/Geography/Gazetteer of the Known World.md|Gazetteer of the Known World]] provides the fullest written account.

## The inner sea and imperial heartlands

The Thalassian Sea joins the western Atherian heartland, the Sestaran peninsula and the Eclessian coast. Its harbours carry grain, timber, metal, pilgrims and armies. Islands and narrow approaches give small cities influence far beyond their cultivated land.

Thalmyria occupies the greatest surviving concentration of Atherian roads, aqueducts and sacred sites. Veridun is the imperial capital; Caleran stands near the Aurin's mouth as the recovered old capital. Eastward, Eclessia extends through kingdoms, duchies, free cities and marches. Between them, the seven Sestaran cities command ports, passes, workshops and commercial routes without forming one territorial state.

## Northern drylands and the Glass

The Covenant countries occupy a broad northern crescent of deserts, cultivated basins, straits and maritime districts. Iqashar, Al-Qahur, Qazmarid and Zanthir possess separate governments and regional histories. The Velian States form a disputed belt between Zanthir and Thalmyria.

Beyond and between the settled states lie the western wells and northeastern grasslands of the Tarkhan. The Nine Wells of Tergai and the Castaran Long Road are fixed points in a country whose horde ranges and seasonal movements cannot be represented as ordinary borders. The Great Glass Desert occupies the north-central approaches and preserves the route of the ancient refuge amid a wider field of ruin.

## Southern forests, ranges and cold coasts

South of the principal empires, political borders thin into Folk customary country. The Green March Kindreds meet Eclessian parishes and frontier settlements; the Rootbound Heartwood contains old forest confederacies; the Gale-Crown Ranges command passes and mines; and the Tidewood Coast faces the cold outer sea.

The Ättar ranges lie to the southwest. Their many halls share no single government, although voyages, marriage and war connect them. Farther southeast, Grobi movement and winter hunger place sustained pressure upon the ranges, forests and Grenzburg frontier. Campaigns in this country depend on stored food, frozen roads, shelter and the brief southern summer.

## Roads, towers and dispersed settlement

Some peoples occupy routes and separated settlements rather than continuous territory. Five living Jirahar tower-citadels, host-city quarters, road companies, sea fleets and Turning Companies connect communities under many rulers. Fixed Prophet's Roads and local Underways support parts of this movement.

The major states likewise depend on corridors that cross political boundaries. Pilgrimage roads carry traders, messengers and armies; maritime routes join ports that may be enemies on land; and winter can close the southern passes entirely.

## Distant lands

The Nsizwa inhabit a separate island-continent in the far southwestern ocean, reached only by a difficult voyage from the Ättar coasts or Qazmarid. The Emerald Expanse lies across the northern Boiling Sea, where Castaran expeditions enter countries marked by the collapse of Giant rule. Other distant peoples and sovereignties remain beyond regular contact with the core setting.

## Cartographic controls

Future maps must preserve the southern-hemisphere climate rule and give physical causes to borders, campaigns and settlements. Mountains, rivers, roads, ports, granaries, holy sites and supernatural hazards determine where people can live and how armies move. The [[01 Canon Reference/Geography/Geography Atlas - Cartographic Control.md|Cartographic Control]] records map layers, unresolved survey work and sites that should remain hidden.
