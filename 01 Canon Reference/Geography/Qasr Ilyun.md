# Qasr Ilyun

Qasr Ilyun is a fortified Qahuri breach-court town at the cultivated edge of Al-Qahur and the northern margin of the [[Great Glass Desert]]. It is the last dependable place to obtain water, witnesses, repair labor, and recognized recovery papers before entering the shifting ruin country.

## Town and People

The town grew around three deep wells, a caravan wall, and an old frontier fort. Qahuri water families, garrison households, glass scouts, drovers, shrine jurists, salvagers, and Jirahar road companies live or season there. Its permanent population is modest, but storm season fills its yards with expeditions waiting for safe wind.

No single community owns the town's identity. Qahuri institutions govern the wells and fields. Al-Qahur's military households maintain the walls and patrols. Jirahar companies keep independent records and memorial routes. Foreign scholars and salvagers must work through local hosts or accept that no one will search for them cheaply.

## The Breach Court

Qasr Ilyun's breach court handles ruins too dangerous or unstable for ordinary property proceedings. It can close exposed sites, register witnesses, quarantine artifacts, record deaths, and issue temporary custody. Its orders govern people living under Al-Qahur's authority; they do not make Giant claims morally valid or ancient mechanisms safe.

The court is valued because an inventory can return a dead worker's share to family, prevent two expeditions killing each other over the same find, and keep a dangerous object out of an open market. It is distrusted because officials and military houses can turn temporary custody into permanent seizure.

## Roads Beyond

- the main Glass road follows marked wells toward active caravan country;
- memorial paths used by Jirahar companies reach older graves and Exodus sites;
- the abandoned **Black Salt Track** runs into bitter-well country and is omitted from most commercial itineraries;
- glass scouts maintain temporary storm routes that may vanish after one season.

Public maps end these roads where dependable water and patrols end. Rumors of deeper palaces, estates, and buried cities are not reliable geography.

## Grave Legends

Glass scouts sometimes call an unnamed eastern ruin **the Hollow City**, while Jirahar memorial companies warn of **the City After the Last Name**. Stories agree only that stolen names, opened graves, and a city beneath a salt mirror belong together. No dependable route, distance, or surviving witness confirms that the two names describe one place.

## Story Uses

- a breach hearing must decide whether an object can safely remain in private hands;
- a returned pack animal is the only evidence that an expedition found anything;
- two route records disagree because a glass storm moved an entire landmark;
- a military household wants a site reopened before memorial witnesses finish recording its dead.
