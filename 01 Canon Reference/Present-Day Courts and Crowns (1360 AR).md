# Present-Day Courts and Crowns (1360 AR)

Who rules where, right now. Integrates existing canon rulers (marked ✓) with newly invented courts. Companion to [[Present-Day Political Board]] and [[Named Figures of the Known World]].

## Eclessia

### The Primarchal State of Aurelia

**Lord Primarch Alistair III** ✓ rules Aurelia directly — the Primarchy is also a crown (the Papal-State arrangement), which is why the **Crown of Embers** vault, the Sanctum Divinitatis, and the reunification mandate all sit in one man's keeping. Elected 1354 in succession to Celestin IX, he is a **reformist**: enforcing the Council of Veridance's abolition of routing assurances against a thriving gray market, curbing noble corruption, and quietly reading Freeflame pamphlets to understand rather than merely burn them. Against him: **Cardinal Marcellus Vrenne** ✓ and the Council of the Eternal Creed (traditionalists who consider Veridance a surrender already); **Grandmaster Henri d'Montesse** ✓ of the Order of the Phoenix (faith by sword; wants the Crown of Embers on a head, preferably one he chooses); **Archbishop Ysabel de Braxford** ✓ (pacifist scholar, Great Library). The **Inquisition of Saint Malveris** ✓ is weaponized by all sides, and the **Order of the Lantern** answers, inconveniently, to the Primarch alone. Alistair's private nightmare has a name: the Unburnt Psalter is in *his* vault.

### The Kingdom of Virelia

**King Gregor IV Radomir** ✓, elected by the Szlachta Council, former Grand Hetman. Confession: old Church, but the elective system means every royal death is a confessional auction — reformed nobles hold perhaps a third of the Council, and the Korrheim Accord gives each magnate's estates his own rite. Virelia is where the Accord goes to be stress-tested.

### The Ironvale Dominion

**Grand Minister Aldric Weyn** ✓ and the Council of Iron. The Dominion is the Reformation's child: the last duke, **Rainald**, sheltered Doctor Ferro, armed the Ashenmen's heirs, and broke his house doing it — blood, debt, and Church embargo — leaving the guilds to fill the vacuum (the Grand Ministry dates its charter to the house's extinction). Ironvale is the **Freeflame stronghold**: Saint Menos the Chain-Breaker is its patron, congregational hymn-casting its norm, and the Church's regulation of its black-powder experiments is read there as the old enemy's newest leash. *(Reconciliation note: fold this heritage into the Ironvale Overview and its Conflicts note.)*

### The Rest of the Confessional Map (canon rulers integrated ✓)

**Caldria** — **Grand Duke Alric IV von Caldria** ✓: staunchly old Church (the Third Crusade's battlefield does not reform), pushing military modernization against his own zealot old guard. **Veridancia** — **Duke Adrien III de Veridancia** ✓: counter-reform hub (the Council met there; Lantern seminaries), its charming duke balancing Church obligation against merchant money. **Brauvalis** — **Duke Reinhardt von Brauvalis** ✓: old Church but bruised by it (excommunicated tax collectors over the monastic-brewery levy), Accord-frozen, one succession from flipping. **Korrheim** — the **Council of Heldenheim** ✓ (rotating canton representatives): the Accord's birthplace, professionally and profitably neutral, still scarred by the Falkendorf Rebellion. **The Sylvan Marches** — **High Warden Elias Dreymann** ✓ with frontier lords like **Baroness Katarina Wolfsden** ✓: nominally old Church, practically half-pagan (Enaidfae substrate barely under the paint), wholly ungovernable. **Grenzburg's duchy**: officially Sanctum-loyal (Bishop Konrad, Prelate Caestus), with Folk practice under the floorboards and Freeflame preachers among the winter labor camps — all three fires in one small room.

**Marker of the age:** in Markish reform country the register runs Ansgar's way; in the Latinate south the Sanctum holds; Virelia's folk piety leans east under a western crown (see [[The Making of the Eclessians]]).

## Thalmyria

### The Imperial Throne

**Emperor Caedros IV Aetherion** ✓ in Veridun, claiming the Nikandran restoration line. *(Character per existing canon note — not re-characterized here.)*

### The Patriarchate

**Patriarch Sophron the Unsleeping** *(new)*, enthroned 1351 after surviving the seven days in the Cavern of Smoke and Flame — allegedly without sleeping, hence the style. An Ember-Visionary by formation, which unsettles the Hierophants (doctrine men prefer a doctrine man) and delights the monasteries. His Council of Flame is split three ways: the **Flame-Marshals** press for a Zanthir crusade while the Qahirid suzerainty stumbles; the **Hierophants** want the Codex Ignis defended against a new wave of Eclessian polemic on the Kindling Question; and the **Ember-Visionaries** keep transcribing the same troubling omen from three separate trance-dormitories — *a lamp carried out of a burning archive* — which no one has interpreted and everyone has an interpretation for.

### The Court's Standing Wounds

The ransom-shame of Markellos (never again negotiate); the Nikandran grievance (never again ask the East); and the empirical embarrassment nobody speaks of: the Saint-Emperor's hymns kindle for some officers and not for others, and the pattern follows no rank the court can control.

## The Covenant (Qahirid Suzerainty)

### The Sublime Threshold at Iqashar

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III** *(new)*, r. 1349–present: clever, indolent, throne bought by the Cull of Brothers and held by balance. The real government:

- **Sayyida Nurhan, "the Veiled Ledger"** *(new)* — the Sultan-Caliph's mother, who controls the household treasury, the marriage market, and, through both, half the court (the Valide-Sultan analog).
- **The Unnamed Custodian** *(new)* — chief of the Vault of Names, who surrendered his own true name into custody upon investiture; the only officer the Guard cannot bind and the court cannot blackmail.
- **Guard-Master Temuz** *(new)* — commander of the Ahdanite Guard, king-broker, veteran of the last frontier war; loyal to the institution of the Guard, which is not the same as the throne, and everyone knows it.
- **Princes Kavus and Ardavan** *(new)* — the two surviving sons. The Cull awaits Marzuq's death; both princes are recruiting; every court appointment is secretly a wager on which brother survives the other.

### The Vassal Thrones

- **Al-Qahur** — **Caliph Uwais the Custodian-of-the-Wells** *(new)*: spiritual precedence, holy city, oasis of the 4,444; politically weak, liturgically indispensable (the Sharifian analog). His acknowledgment legitimizes Marzuq; his silence would wound him.
- **Qazmarid** — **Caliph-Imam Zahran of the Sealed Blood** *(new)*: denies the Qahirid universal caliphate outright, claims descent from Ruqaiya bint Mujalib (see [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]]), and keeps what he says is the Counter-Instrument beneath his throne.
- **Zanthir** — **Emir Badis al-Zanthiri** *(new)*, capital Qabir: the Granada-remnant, last pearl of the Banu Mizan inheritance, paying tribute toward Iqashar and Veridun in alternating seasons, sheltering Jirahar quarters and Thalmyric exile-monks alike, universally assumed to be the next war's first battlefield and playing every court against that day.

## Sestara (light pass — expand with the Concord's own notes)

The Medori bank under **Duke Gaius Medori** ✓, Chairman and head of the family, with **Lord Matteo Medori** ✓ over investments (and Grenzburg canon fielding **Factor Gianni** ✓ and **Agent Livia** ✓ downstream). Canon holds a loaded secret: the Medori carry hidden **Ahl-e-Ahd lineage and contract-magic** — which retroactively explains both the bank's uncanny instruments and what exactly the family bought when it purchased Alessior VI's Primarchy. Sermora's Doge and Admiralty ✓, Aversia's House of Soran ✓, Vorthara's Council of Magi ✓ per existing canon. Fiorenzia still dreams uneasily of Frate Severin.

## The Jirahar

**High Keeper Zerahiah** *(new)*, first among the Keepers of the Path in the most sacred Tower Town: post-Meshalem orthodoxy incarnate — ferocious gatekeeping of prophecy, quiet finance, quieter memory. His standing ruling: any claimant to Al-Muntazar is presumed false until the Keepers rule otherwise, and the Keepers have never ruled otherwise. The irony canon (that this is exactly how a true one would be missed) stands.
