# Origins of Warlock Rune Magic

Warlock rune magic predates formal imperial war colleges. It begins as practical elemental engineering among early settlements that need fire, stone, water, ice, wind, and pressure to survive.

## Proto-Scripts

The first scripts are not elegant spells. They are fixed rune sequences used for:

- kiln heat,
- irrigation cuts,
- well clearing,
- frost hardening,
- firebreaks,
- quarry splitting,
- road compaction,
- mine ventilation,
- ward posts,
- signal fires,
- river locks,
- siege repairs.

## Source

The scripts draw elemental force from the [[Elemental Shell and the Four Planes]]. Early practitioners do not always understand the Shell as later metaphysicians do. They learn repeatable formulae first and theory later.

## City-State Formalization

During the [[Age of City-States]], urban powers begin standardizing these scripts:

- Atherian cities attach them to roads, aqueducts, siege engines, and military engineers.
- Sestaran cities monetize them through guilds, foundries, shipyards, insurance, and mercenary contracts.
- frontier forts adapt them for cold, palisades, bridge repair, and Grobi defense.
- later Vortharan schools turn them into fast battlefield spellcraft.

## Failure Modes

Early rune magic is dangerous because formula can outrun wisdom:

- Shell burn,
- runaway fire,
- frozen blood,
- lightning grounding failures,
- acidic seepage,
- cracked walls,
- dead fields,
- unstable instruments,
- accidental thin spaces.

## Future Consequence

By the time warlocks become recognizable as battlefield specialists, the world has already depended on elemental scripts for centuries. War magic is the militarized child of survival engineering.
