# Peoples through History

The peoples of the known world formed across several different histories. Ancestry, language, religious inheritance, imperial citizenship and present political identity changed at different speeds. Some communities passed through the refuge in the Great Glass Desert; others survived the fall of Giant rule in their own lands; later peoples arose as conquest, migration, slavery, conversion, fosterage and marriage joined older populations.

Three terms provide a broad guide. **Ark-derived** traditions descend substantially from the One Glass People formed during the refuge. **Orphaned** peoples descend principally from communities that survived outside it. **Braided** peoples emerged through later joining. These terms describe historical formation. They establish no rank of civilisation, moral worth or human nature, and they do not determine an individual's political or religious allegiance.

For a direct directory of the living cultures, use [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Cultures MOC.md|Peoples and Cultures]].

## Giant rule and the Unbinding

Before c. 2300 BR, human societies lived under and between a many-state Giant world. Captive households cultivated land, kept workshops, served armies and preserved forms of kinship and worship beneath rulers of differing descent and power. The Great Unbinding broke these states through slave revolt, Giant civil war, failed systems of recurrence and heavenly severance.

The followers gathered by the Great Prophet escaped into the Glass. Numerous other communities remained in their own countries or fled through different routes. The ancestors of the Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Nsizwa belong principally to these Orphaned histories, although each people formed in a different region and under different pressures.

## The Glass refuge and the Scattering

The desert refuge turned captives from different Giant dominions into the One Glass People. Shared provision, worship, labour and the Prophet's teaching gave them a common life over generations. The Prophet left no inheritable commission. After his death, disputes over leadership and renewed contact with the world beyond the desert contributed to gradual separation.

Southern war-settlement leagues became the ancestors of the Atherian world. Communities remaining nearer the Glass later divided under the pressures of water, distance, travel and Tarkhan contact. Portable courts and route memory became important to the Jirahar, while defended wells, territorial coalitions and guest protection contributed to ancient Ahdari society. Families and institutions remained connected across these divisions for generations.

The ancient sources did not use the retrospective terms *proto-Atherian*, *proto-Jirahar* and *proto-Ahdari*. Southern migrants named captains, camps, occupations and destinations. Near-Glass communities named roads, towers, wells and households. Broader people names acquired meaning as these local groups formed durable relationships.

## Hearths, first kingdoms and city worlds

Between c. 1900 and 800 BR, rebuilding produced halls, defended wells, tower circuits, cultivated river states, ports and maritime leagues. Orphaned societies developed without waiting for the Glass descendants. Settlers entered inhabited lands and established themselves through alliance, trade, marriage, slavery and war.

The Folk formed through many southern clan worlds rather than a single ancestral kingdom. Related Ättar societies concentrated greater authority in halls while sustained Grobi warfare changed their institutions. Tarkhan communities rebuilt the northern ground after Vathra's flying cities fell, maintaining deep wells, camel roads, craft towns and seasonal grass routes. Ahdari communities joined western waters through markets and shared protection. Jirahar tower societies connected settled centres with travelling households. In the south, Atherian maritime cultures grew through their encounters with the Enathi, Serathi, Vardeni and the survivors of Giant palace kingdoms.

The eastern river and highland peoples also formed independent states. House Ubar joined the Ubar canal cities into Arqesh, while House Tiravan emerged among the Vashari highlands. The Jirahar built Qiryath between these powers and the Near-Glass roads. Ortheian kings later imposed a temporary hegemony upon Atherian cities, and Eryndor III's eastern conquest created Successor kingdoms without absorbing these peoples into one ancestry.

## Republic, empire and provincial peoples

From the Celestial Compact and Caleran republic arose an Atherian political identity increasingly defined by citizenship, military service, cities and law. Expansion joined populations of many ancestries on unequal terms. Roads and markets connected provinces while conquest brought enslavement, land seizure and compulsory service.

The empire became the setting of the Redeemer's ministry, persecution of his followers and the later conversion of imperial government. Provincial peoples adapted inherited Atherian institutions to local languages, households and religious life. Markish, Vesari, Velian and other regional formations grew during these centuries, while Folk, Ättar, Jirahar and Tarkhan communities maintained distinct histories both within and beyond imperial power.

## Covenant expansion and the Long Frontier

Mujalib's preaching began in 541 AR among societies whose wells, caravan routes, cities and local cults had much older histories. The Covenant joined several ancestries and countries through a shared religious and political civilisation. Its expansion incorporated Qahuri river lands, Gateway communities, Jirahar settlements, Tarkhan clients, Velians and former Atherian provinces under different arrangements.

The Long Frontier created roads, markets, garrisons and mixed towns while also imposing conquest, tribute, slavery and religious restrictions. The Ahl-e-Ahd emerged as a civilisation embracing Ahdari, Iqashari, Qahuri, Qazmari and Zanthiri identities rather than as a single biological people.

## Fall, recovery and successor societies

The fall of Caleran in 905 AR broke the remaining unity of the Atherian state. Refugees, mountain communities, soldiers and clergy sustained the resistance that became Thalmyria. Other provinces developed through their own cities, churches, ruling houses and mixed populations. The Long Reconquest restored much of the old heartland while displacing occupation-born families and leaving several frontier societies outside secure Thalmyric rule.

The Great Split divided the Thalmyric and Eclessian churches. Sestaran cities preserved their independence through a defensive compact; Velians remained divided among border states and foreign rulers; Eclessian and Thalmyric identities continued to develop through regional mixtures inherited from the imperial world. The political map of 1360 therefore contains ancient peoples, later braided societies and states whose subjects belong to several cultures.
