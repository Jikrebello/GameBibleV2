# Authorial Notes from Reader Articles — Redeemer and Conversion

Safeguards, rulings and prompts moved out of the public Redeemer and Conversion and Jirahar history articles during the November 2026 editorial cleanup; material already held in the Chronology, the Jirahar Canon Register and other control files was removed without duplication, and each item below keeps its original force.

## [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]

- Real-history analogs removed from reader prose: the Spring at Sarnwell (1311 AR) is modelled on Lourdes ("*(Lourdes.)*"), and the Eclessian processions that crown her images were described as "May-analog processions". (The Halmenth 411 / Ephesus analog is already held in [[Named Figures of the Known World]].)

## [[The Age of the Redeemer]]

- Companion roll: "The called Companions remain the established company: thirteen called, one struck, with their own fixed histories. The later conversion of Aquilan, the martyrdoms and the Falling-Asleep of Amaryah do not open a vacancy for another central apostle. Lesser guides, soldiers, criminals, householders and failed followers can nevertheless enter the history without crowding that company." Local Underground histories feed the Lives "without creating a second apostolic generation". The same safeguard was removed from [[The First Church - From Qiryath to the Tower Revolt|The First Church]]: Aquilan's "mission is distinct from the Companion roll. The empty seat stays empty."
- Ontology stated as fact, now attributed to believers in the articles: Isfir's death and Resurrection "transformed the human soul-road and established the central truth around which later churches, empires and persecutions developed"; [[The Rise of the Redeemer]] likewise stated "The Resurrection restored the human soul-road."
- Rulings removed from "What the age leaves": "A Saint's earthly repentance can be genuine while people harmed by that life refuse to celebrate it"; "A miracle answers its commission, not every proposition written beneath the icon"; the move to [[The Conversion of the Empire]] "does not remove the need to distinguish sanctity from political success"; "Calling every survivor a martyr or every recanter a monster destroys the historical difference."

## [[The Conversion of the Empire]]

- Campaign prompts removed from reader prose (no other copy found):
  - **Ashes of the Covenant:** a Jirahar scholar rescues household registers from a confiscated eastern tower without surrendering them to a patron bargain.
  - **The Closed Road:** a Great Trek must choose between a hostile physical pass and a distant gate bench unwilling to receive another tower's danger.
  - **The Last Bell:** a Redeemer monk tries to save a Jirahar child marked for death, and together they flee an empire turning zealot.
- Authorial truth stated as fact, now attributed or removed in the article: the era's "central wound is that genuine miracles and genuine sanctity eventually became instruments of imperial coercion"; some temple images "had fed genuine Masks", and destroying a statue "could expose theft without ending the appetite which had used it"; "Authentic relics were no less dangerous to people who treated them as imperial equipment. Their response proved only the thing actually done."
- Penitent Governor: "His repentance was real" (now "The tradition holds his repentance real"); "The death did not make his earlier victims less important."
- Preacher of the Second Gospel: his charitable work was "genuine", he was "justly removed from teaching", and captive sacrifice, the child possession trial and the murder of a former companion "were personally authorized acts, not cruelties supplied only by later followers". The article keeps his personal authorization of all three.

## [[The Ember Underground]]

- The article previously ruled that the Ashway Bridge coal test was a practical safety signal (wind, rain or movement on the bridge) which "later tradition described" as the Flame refusing entry, and that the monks' ceremony "does not claim that every successful crossing was miraculous". The article now leaves both readings open. **Ruling fixed 14 November 2026:** some extinguished coals were weather and some were not, and the couriers wrote them so that no one could tell; see [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth#3. The Great Purge (c. 36–45 AR)|Signs and Proof §3]].

## [[The Forty Flames]]

- "Judgements about the conduct of the rising or the sanctity of individual relics require their own evidence." (The furnace sign itself is already fixed as fact in the [[Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)|Chronology]].)

## [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]]

- Aquilan's "fulfilled, not abolished" was called "the single most consequential theological claim in history, and the hinge on which everything the Jirahar suffered afterward would swing."

The following reader statements were superseded and removed; none is the sole account of a victim or a crime.

- **Dates and sequence.** Vestian "377–395", compulsion "in 380", colleges dissolved "380" → accession 377, Dissolution at Caleran 383 (provinces by 384), the Profession 385, death 398. - **Savel Orren.** "Gave up two road forts for water and safe passage … died on the journey back" → Tellan's water trains save the column; the forts are abandoned in the withdrawal; Savel abdicates at the depot line in 352 and dies on an estate.
- **Astrion's last words.** The reader articles keep the Church's "You have won, Carpenter" as a later attribution and add the field record's question about the baggage.

- **Legacy AE files.** [[The Temple That Would Not Burn]] ("~447–450 AE", "Conversion-era") → c. 245–247 AR under Tervan's edict; "Monk Audaris, 2nd Century" → "Audaris the Reader, late 3rd Century". [[Saintmaker’s Forge]] ("~463 AE", "Redeemer nun", "first canonized saint-icons", "His story is the foundation of Redeemer iconography" as narration) → 289–291 AR at Marvesh, a consecrated widow of a burial fraternity, the foundation claim attributed to Thalmyric smiths. [[The Apostle’s Bones]] ("~475 AE", "~540–560 AE", "Council of Concord Flame (546 AE)", the hilt "melted and recast into two relics", the disputants "Eclessian reformers, Thalmyric militant orders, and remnants of the Imperial priesthood") → found in the 320s under Sevrin; the 320s dispute is the four sees against the Star college; the hearing follows the 305 model; the hilt survives at Veridun Minor; the Eclessian and Thalmyric claims move after 1015, where the joint-custody settlement now sits. - **The Forty Flames.** "Its date and institutional setting remain uncertain" (Council of Second Flame) → c. 383–384, the first bishops' sign hearing.

## Causality Pass 10C revisions (14 November 2026)

[[Causality Pass - 10C Bending Kellovar, Halmenth and the Last Fortress|Pass 10C]] bent five sequences that still followed their analogues. The truths are in [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]] §5, §8, §9, §12 and §15. Superseded reader statements are listed here; every victim and atrocity in them survives in the new telling.

- **The last fortress (68 AR).** "A final rebel community held a cliff fortress … built a ramp to reach the wall … competing accounts of heroic refusal, killings ordered by its own leaders and imperial massacre … speeches attributed to their leaders" ([[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]]) → Migdal Shahar on the Dawn Road: a siege dug downwards, gallery roofs dropped on refuge halls, a gate bench that takes households and not fighters, nine days of openings, the keys broken and the killing in the lower halls. The account that the rebels killed their own survives as the legate's claim.
- **Tervan's edict (245 AR).** "Watched each household make its offering and handed over a written certificate"; "they looked for the households with no certificate"; "buy a certificate"; "a person who bought a certificate"; "how long penance should last and what weight to give a confessor's intercession"; "the offices that issued the certificates" ([[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization|The Church after Qiryath]], [[Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian - Emperors Wars and Revolts|Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian]], [[The Ember Underground]], [[The Temple That Would Not Burn]], [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]], [[The Age of the Redeemer]], [[The Conversion of the Empire]]) → lamps relit at the market altar and burning green on the colleges' oil, night watches on the graves, tinkers' pot-green, the colleges' oil books, and a reconciliation quarrel over who may be given fire.
- **Astrion's death (351 AR).** "Struck by a spear whose thrower no witness saw"; "a spear struck him in the side" → the untried span at the Emperor's Cut, under which he insisted on standing. The field record's question and the later Church attribution "You have won, Carpenter" are kept.
- **Kellovar (377 AR).** "Folk households sought admission across the southern fort line … Lucar admitted them in 371–372 and recruited fighters"; "armed resistance spread from 372 among the admitted households"; "Lucar marched south before reserves and civilian transport were in place"; "Lucar died there" → the confiscation of the guides' and traders' tokens from c. 368; children pledged for relief grain; the households' escape across the fords (372–377); the army drowned at dawn; Lucar carried dry on a stolen token, three days at the Otter house's fire, and killed by his own rearguard on the near bank. Vestian is recognised while Lucar is still alive.
- **Halmenth (411 AR).** "Qedrel's communion fled north beyond the empire's reach"; "It fled north into the desert cities beyond imperial reach"; "northern refuge" → the relic exemptions, the charge under the augurs' old law, the lamps lit from the Ember's daughter, the escape by Sea Fleet ship, the gateway sees struck off the roll and left alone as taxpayers.

## Causality Pass 11C revisions (14 November 2026)

[[Causality Pass - 11C Second Bend - Tower Revolt to Vestian|Pass 11C]] broke the skeletons that survived Pass 10C: the same people meeting the same fates in the same order. The truths are in [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]] §5, §7, §8, §9 and §15 to §18. Superseded reader statements are listed here. Every victim and atrocity in them survives in the new telling, some under a new cause, and where 11C supersedes a 04 or 10C entry above, 11C governs.

- **Dates.** The Second Destruction "65 AR" → 72 AR. Migdal Shahar "68 AR" (siege "65–68") → 64 AR (siege 62–64). The revolt "61–68" → 61–72. The bones at Veridun Minor "in the 320s" → c. 338. Garen Vardun "147–171" → 147–162; Marek Vessan "171–185" → 162–189 (d. c. 204).
- **The Tower Revolt's opening.** "The governor's officers took money from the sanctuary treasury on the pretext of arrears"; "Militant priests stopped the offering made on behalf of the emperor" → the Nine Mornings: the emperor's offering unburnt on the altar, the augur's entry of fraud under the Purge edict, and the killing of the custodians and two hundred others on the sanctuary stair. The Keeper keeps the emperor's offering to the last morning.
- **The first relief force.** "Rebels … broke the first relief force as it withdrew through the river approaches. They took its siege equipment and stores" → no army comes against Qiryath for eight years; the legate goes round it to the Dawn Road.
- **The order of the towers.** "After Qiryath fell, the surviving war party and several thousand refugees went down into Shahar"; "the last rebel stronghold" → Shahar is besieged first (62–64) and falls eight years before Qiryath. The collapses, the nine days, Yaltah, Eshkar and the killing in the lower halls are unchanged.
- **The siege.** "The Atherian army arrived while the city still held pilgrims and refugees"; "Rival factions had burned or seized stores to deny them to one another"; "The ordinary sacrifice ceased during the siege" → the pilgrim roads shut since 61, a city of villagers driven in from the burned country, cistern galleries dropped by the Red Star's survey, and an offering that never lapsed. The factional killings, the hunger and disease in the refuge halls, and the killing and display of people who tried to escape are kept.
- **The fire and the vessels.** "The commanders' accounts later blamed the first flame on soldiers acting without orders … The first ignition remains disputed"; "took its vessels to Caleran for a triumph" → the legate's augur lights the emperor's bull on the altar and the fire takes the house; the vessels go through Qiryath's gate before the breach, and the triumph carries empty litters.
- **The tribute.** "Levied a tribute in place of the old sanctuary contribution"; "the old tribute rolls" (all owned articles) → the Litter Debt, charged by the head for the missing vessels, and its rolls.
- **78–189 AR.** The four-row table "Four reigns remembered for public works and order", Dorvan "adopted Daven Talar", Rovan "withdrew the exposed garrisons left by Daven" and "adopted Garen", Garen "brought his son into command early", Marek "sold appointments" and "Guards eventually killed Marek" → Ysane Dascen the Paymistress ruling with Daven Talar, the garrisons struck from the ledger in his lifetime, Garen killed in the south in 162, the Vessan regency, and Marek seated at the spared towns' table in 189 and living as their guest. The seizures in the southern treaty belt, the recaptured and resold rebels of Sarqen and the armed escorts of the following years are kept.
- **248–292 AR.** "The eastern road command and the southern army … Each started acting in the emperor's name"; "the court brought taxes and appointments back under one authority"; "seizing their books of signs as counter-augury"; "arrested the elders who kept the letters" → the sold and voided campaign seasons, and the colleges' condemnation of congregations' halls and burial yards as cursed ground, with the dead carted to common pits.
- **Velos, 292.** "He saw the White Flame stand upon the Aurin and heard, 'By this Flame, hold.' He won under its sign"; "After his victory at the Stone Bridge" → the night on the ice: no battle, no words, and the court's army crossing the bridge to him.
- **Halmenth, 305.** "An elder in Halmenth handed over a chest … its book of signs … two couriers were arrested"; "They found that he had handed over the chest" → a hearing on Halmenth's refusal of the Concord, signed "as a record of ice". Two couriers arrested at Halmenth survive in the account of the condemned ground.
- **Cassian's household.** "He executed his eldest son and the High Augur"; "the empress, whom rumour had made the heir's ally, lived"; "the sealed answer that had killed his brother" → the High Augur executed; Torian ordained by force and made chaplain of the bridge chapel; Cassian baptised by him in 307. The empress is cut from the episode.
- **The bones.** "In the 320s builders … found a woman's bones" → c. 338, uncovered when the wardens of the Red Star loaded the ruined shrine.
- **Astrion.** "Tied publicly paid posts in rhetoric, calendar teaching and civic history to a teacher's willingness to honour the rites"; "directed endowed temples to reserve grain for travellers and burial money"; "A wall gave way during clearance and injured workers"; "He died at thirty-four"; "Church tradition later gave him the words 'You have won, Carpenter'" → the Entering; the Jirahar crews' refusal of the Red Star's wardens and the flogging of forty; an emperor crushed at the Emperor's Cut who lives, loses Caleran to a false report and reigns east of the depot line under the Accord until c. 372. The carpenter line is withdrawn.
- **Savel, Corlan and Lucar.** "Officers acclaimed Savel Orren"; "Two exposed road forts were abandoned"; "gave the crown back at the depot line"; "Corlan held Caleran and the west. He named his brother Lucar co-emperor" → Savel refuses the acclamation; Lucar Aster, Sevrin's son-in-law, is sole emperor at Caleran from 352; Corlan is his marshal and dies c. 354.
- **Kellovar.** "The survivors … were fed on terms: one child from each household pledged to the crown estates"; "children pledged for relief grain" → children of the allied fort-district households delivered to the river-courts as the fords' toll, some sold by stewards and written down as delivered. Nobody is admitted at the forts.

## [[Historical Spine of the Jirahar]]

- Formation safeguards: the proto-Jirahar "do not emerge as a complete people at the end of the crossing"; they inherit the One Glass People's formation and the Near-Glass fission, "not an isolated original tribe which walked out already finished".
- The displacements of 72 AR (dated 65 before Pass 11C and 67 until the anchor dates moved) and 377–398 AR "cannot be collapsed into one timeless exile".
- The Long Frontier forms of Jirahar public life (translation, medicine, trade, household learning): "none defines every Jirahar person".
