# Authorial Notes from Reader Articles — Redeemer and Conversion

Safeguards, rulings and prompts moved out of the public Redeemer and Conversion and Jirahar history articles during the November 2026 editorial cleanup; material already held in the Chronology, the Jirahar Canon Register and other control files was removed without duplication, and each item below keeps its original force.

## [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]

- Real-history analogs removed from reader prose: the Spring at Sarnwell (1311 AR) is modelled on Lourdes ("*(Lourdes.)*"), and the Eclessian processions that crown her images were described as "May-analog processions". (The Halmenth 411 / Ephesus analog is already held in [[Named Figures of the Known World]].)

## [[The Age of the Redeemer]]

- Companion roll: "The called Companions remain the established company: thirteen called, one struck, with their own fixed histories. The later conversion of Aquilan, the martyrdoms and the Dormition of Amaryah do not open a vacancy for another central apostle. Lesser guides, soldiers, criminals, householders and failed followers can nevertheless enter the history without crowding that company." Local Underground histories feed the Lives "without creating a second apostolic generation". The same safeguard was removed from [[The First Church - From Qiryath to the Tower Revolt|The First Church]]: Aquilan's "mission is distinct from the Companion roll. The empty seat stays empty."
- Ontology stated as fact, now attributed to believers in the articles: Isfir's death and Resurrection "transformed the human soul-road and established the central truth around which later churches, empires and persecutions developed"; [[The Rise of the Redeemer]] likewise stated "The Resurrection restored the human soul-road."
- Rulings removed from "What the age leaves": "A Saint's earthly repentance can be genuine while people harmed by that life refuse to celebrate it"; "A miracle answers its commission, not every proposition written beneath the icon"; the move to [[The Conversion of the Empire]] "does not remove the need to distinguish sanctity from political success"; "Calling every survivor a martyr or every recanter a monster destroys the historical difference."

## [[The Conversion of the Empire]]

- Campaign prompts removed from reader prose (no other copy found):
  - **Ashes of the Covenant:** a Jirahar scholar rescues household registers from a confiscated eastern tower without surrendering them to a patron bargain.
  - **The Closed Road:** a Great Trek must choose between a hostile physical pass and a distant gate bench unwilling to receive another tower's danger.
  - **The Last Bell:** a Redeemer monk tries to save a Jirahar child marked for death, and together they flee an empire turning zealot.
- Authorial truth stated as fact, now attributed or removed in the article: the era's "central wound is that genuine miracles and genuine sanctity eventually became instruments of imperial coercion"; some temple images "had fed genuine Masks", and destroying a statue "could expose theft without ending the appetite which had used it"; "Authentic relics were no less dangerous to people who treated them as imperial equipment. Their response proved only the thing actually done."
- Penitent Governor: "His repentance was real" (now "The tradition holds his repentance real"); "The death did not make his earlier victims less important."
- Preacher of the Second Gospel: his charitable work was "genuine", he was "justly removed from teaching", and captive sacrifice, the child possession trial and the murder of a former companion "were personally authorized acts, not cruelties supplied only by later followers". The article keeps his personal authorization of all three.

## [[The Ember Underground]]

- The article previously ruled that the Ashway Bridge coal test was a practical safety signal (wind, rain or movement on the bridge) which "later tradition described" as the Flame refusing entry, and that the monks' ceremony "does not claim that every successful crossing was miraculous". The article now leaves both readings open. **Ruling fixed 14 November 2026:** some extinguished coals were weather and some were not, and the couriers wrote them so that no one could tell; see [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth#3. The Great Purge (c. 36–45 AR)|Signs and Proof §3]].

## [[The Forty Flames]]

- "Judgements about the conduct of the rising or the sanctity of individual relics require their own evidence." (The furnace sign itself is already fixed as fact in the [[Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)|Chronology]].)

## [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]]

- Aquilan's "fulfilled, not abolished" was called "the single most consequential theological claim in history, and the hinge on which everything the Jirahar suffered afterward would swing."

## Causality Pass 04 revisions (14 November 2026)

The reader articles of 0–411 AR were revised under [[Causality Pass - 04 Who May Authenticate a Sign, 0 to 411 AR|Causality Pass 04]]. The authorial truths behind them (the Commission Rule, the Three Silent Days, the Kept Lamps Mask, the Second Silence, the Kellovar ford, the Man-Bearer ruling, the Nails' provenance) are in [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]]. The following reader statements were superseded and removed; none is the sole account of a victim or a crime.

- **Dates and sequence.** Vestian "377–395", compulsion "in 380", colleges dissolved "380" → accession 377, Dissolution at Caleran 381 (provinces by 384), the Profession 385, death 398. Restitution "in 293" → c. 296 (the Velos Concord). Corlan's death "375" → c. 366. The Folk admission "376" → 371–372, war 372–377. The challenge "by a kinsman of Astrion" in 355–357, "defeated" by Lucar → Tellan's stand-off ending in the Accord of the Two Standards, c. 357. Cassian "r. 288–316" → acclaimed 288, reigned 292–316.
- **Cassian's household.** "Executed his eldest son and empress on unpublished treason evidence" (Conversion, Cassian, Sevrin) → c. 309, the son and the High Augur of Astaroth over a sealed succession answer; the empress lives. The content of the answer stays unruled.
- **Savel Orren.** "Gave up two road forts for water and safe passage … died on the journey back" → Tellan's water trains save the column; the forts are abandoned in the withdrawal; Savel abdicates at the depot line in 352 and dies on an estate.
- **Astrion's last words.** The reader articles keep the Church's "You have won, Carpenter" as a later attribution and add the field record's question about the baggage.
- **Restitution tables.** The four-row table "Who gets the confiscated property" was moved from [[Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR|Cassian and the Four Sees]] into [[After Cassian - The Succession of 316 and the Forty Flames|After Cassian]] verbatim; the reader article keeps a prose summary.
- **Legacy AE files.** [[The Temple That Would Not Burn]] ("~447–450 AE", "Conversion-era") → c. 249–251 AR under Tervan's edict; "Monk Audaris, 2nd Century" → "Audaris the Reader, late 3rd Century". [[Saintmaker’s Forge]] ("~463 AE", "Redeemer nun", "first canonized saint-icons", "His story is the foundation of Redeemer iconography" as narration) → 289–291 AR at Marvesh, a consecrated widow of a burial fraternity, the foundation claim attributed to Thalmyric smiths. [[The Apostle’s Bones]] ("~475 AE", "~540–560 AE", "Council of Concord Flame (546 AE)", the hilt "melted and recast into two relics", the disputants "Eclessian reformers, Thalmyric militant orders, and remnants of the Imperial priesthood") → found in the 320s under Sevrin; the 320s dispute is the four sees against the Star college; the hearing follows the 297 model; the hilt survives at Veridun Minor; the Eclessian and Thalmyric claims move after 1015, where the joint-custody settlement now sits. [[Codex of The Era of Blood and Ash]] keeps its in-world text whole beneath a new reader introduction.
- **The Forty Flames.** "Its date and institutional setting remain uncertain" (Council of Second Flame) → c. 382–383, the first bishops' sign hearing.

## [[Historical Spine of the Jirahar]]

- Formation safeguards: the proto-Jirahar "do not emerge as a complete people at the end of the crossing"; they inherit the One Glass People's formation and the Near-Glass fission, "not an isolated original tribe which walked out already finished".
- The displacements of 70 AR and 377–395 AR "cannot be collapsed into one timeless exile".
- The Long Frontier forms of Jirahar public life (translation, medicine, trade, household learning): "none defines every Jirahar person".
