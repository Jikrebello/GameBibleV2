# The Historical Seed Garden

This is the nursery for human-scale stories that may later grow into history. It is not a second chronology and it does not make every listed adventure true. Its job is to ensure that every age contains ordinary people whose local decisions could later be remembered as dynastic origins, saints' lives, outlaw cycles, military traditions, cautionary tales, relic disputes, patron cults, or unfinished mysteries.

The preferred shape is the **ascending company**: strangers, relatives, rivals, fugitives, hired hands, pilgrims, thieves, soldiers, or failed students begin with a small need and discover only afterward that they have stepped into history. They do not assemble because prophecy has labelled them important. History becomes interested in them after they have already become interested in one another.

The seventeen beds below remain the nursery's stable framework; approved cards should deepen them before creating duplicate signature companies.

## The Garden Rule

Plant lightly and harvest late.

Every seed receives only three firm roots at first:

1. **Humble ground:** the ordinary problem that first brings the people together.
2. **Durable trace:** one thing unquestionably left in the present: a custom, object, grave, road, legal phrase, bloodline, feast, ruin, song, or enemy.
3. **Historical pressure:** the larger event their story eventually touches without necessarily causing by itself.

Everything else remains loose: the full membership, the order of adventures, who betrayed whom, who survived, whether supernatural claims are true, and whether later traditions have merged several companies into one.

This protects future stories. A seed can support a tavern anecdote, a relic hunt, a novel, a campaign, or nothing more than an old name on a map without the surrounding world depending upon its resolution.

## The Human-Ascent Pattern

The favored legendary career has six movements:

1. **Misfit life:** thief, caravan guard, kitchen slave, failed novice, debtor, hunter, washerwoman, junior clerk, displaced child, or unwanted heir.
2. **Accidental fellowship:** the company meets through debt, escape, employment, kinship, disaster, mistaken identity, or a job too small for official attention.
3. **Local competence:** they solve something immediate. They are not yet saving the world.
4. **Widening consequence:** their enemy, discovery, oath, rescued person, or stolen object connects the local story to a larger historical pressure.
5. **Divergent ends:** members do not receive one moral verdict. One may become a ruler, one a saint, one a sinner, one a respectable founder, one an outlaw, one may later bear a Patron role or enter the Severed Exalt register with continuity stated separately, and one simply goes home.
6. **Broken remembrance:** later cultures remember different members, reorder the adventures, suppress embarrassing origins, and argue about what the company was called.

No created being becomes God. Human ascent may nevertheless be astonishing: kingship, sainthood, damnation, preservation in the Egg White, Astral patronage through an anchor, survival of residue or a separate legend-persona, or historical influence so great that later people mistake consequence for divinity. None of those outcomes changes the human soul into an office, Patron persona, Mask, or different kind of being.

## Planting Budget by Era

An era normally needs no more than:

- **one signature company** capable of carrying a full heroic cycle;
- **two or three loose figures** who might have crossed its path;
- **one material remainder** that can be encountered in play;
- **one disputed ending** that different communities use differently.

The signature company should not cause every major event of its century. Its story crosses history at one or two pressure points, while most of the chronology remains the work of nations, institutions, weather, economics, revelation, war, and millions of unrecorded people.

## First Planting Across the Historical Spine

All titles and company names below are provisional folk handles.

### Deep Giant Time — The People Beneath the Bowl

**Humble ground:** household slaves from unrelated peoples hide inside the service passages of a Giant palace after being accidentally recorded as dead.

**Durable trace:** several distant cultures possess a children's hiding game called *Beneath the Bowl*, always involving one place at the table that must remain empty.

**Historical pressure:** the first durable human refuge-networks within Giant civilization. Later rebels may inherit routes whose makers never imagined general liberation.

**Loose growth:** the refuge becomes a long Ansharan resistance ecology of hidden food, falsified registers, service routes, burial work, and care rather than one secret army. Its people eventually divide: some join Exodus columns, some remain behind, and some disappear during the Drinking Wars. The company may never have had a leader. The empty place may commemorate a traitor, sacrifice, rescuer, or somebody who was never caught.

### Late Age of Chains — The Company of the Broken Measure

**Humble ground:** a mill sleeper, a ledger-girl, a giant-blooded human born outside the recognized household line, an elderly cook, and a condemned hunter become responsible for moving one sick child between estates. Every companion and the child possesses a complete `human-soul`.

**Durable trace:** a broken Giant measuring rod is displayed in three incompatible shrines. The phrase *the measure broke first* means that an unjust system has invalidated its own laws.

**Historical pressure:** during the warning and plague years, local slave escapes, monster-clearing, and one estate measure's mutually impossible commands open a bounded route toward the gathering. The victory does not reveal a universal weakness in Giant law.

**Loose growth:** the Sleeper may solve impossible physical tasks but fail at ordinary life. The ledger-girl, not he, may become the true architect of the escape roads. Later stories disagree over which member first heard the Great Prophet.

### Exodus — The Last Cart

**Humble ground:** refugees assigned to a broken provision cart fall behind the Glass Ark's vulnerable rear and must carry the elderly, injured, pregnant, and unwanted beyond the front camp's most visible signs. Their work joins provisioning, cart repair, medicine, route-and-name memory, and defense.

**Durable trace:** cultures across the Scattering place one damaged wheel or wheel-shaped loaf at certain funerals, departures, and winter feasts.

**Historical pressure:** the Exodus succeeds not only because of the Prophet at its head and high-sign provision at impossible bottlenecks, but because obscure people ration, repair, bury, remember, and keep its vulnerable rear alive.

**Loose growth:** every people claims a different member as its ancestor. One version says the company arrived after the Prophet's death; another says they reached the threshold first and voluntarily waited for the rest.

### Age of Scattering — The Seven Who Took the Wrong Road

**Humble ground:** seven scouts searching for water misread a road sign, lose the migrating host, and spend years attempting to return.

**Durable trace:** seven widely separated wells, cairns, towers, or ferry stones claim to be one of their stopping places, though the geography cannot form a single route.

**Historical pressure:** the formation of the post-Exodus peoples and their different fragments of inherited teaching.

**Loose growth:** the seven may have become ancestors among different peoples, or later singers may have gathered unrelated founders into one travelling company. Their apparent failure to return becomes the beginning of several homes.

### Hearths and First Kings — The Crownless Company

**Humble ground:** village hirelings are paid to recover stolen winter cattle and discover that the supposed thieves are refugees from a king who has begun taking children as tribute.

**Durable trace:** a plain iron cooking ring used in several coronations, always carried ahead of the crown and never explained consistently.

**Historical pressure:** the birth of protective kingship and its recurring temptation to reproduce Giant dominion.

**Loose growth:** the person eventually acclaimed as king may begin as the company's cook, hostage, paymaster, or least successful fighter. One companion refuses office and becomes the ancestor claimed by later outlaw fraternities.

### Hearths, Forests, and Early Menhir Pacts — The Wolf and the Lantern

**Humble ground:** two confidence tricksters pretend to be qualified spirit-hunters in order to earn a meal, then encounter a household genuinely haunted by a violated place-law.

**Durable trace:** paired carvings of a long-legged wanderer and a hooded lamp appear on southern bridges; travellers leave jokes rather than prayers.

**Historical pressure:** humans learning that the wilderness is inhabited jurisdiction, and that survival depends upon etiquette, bargaining, audacity, and occasionally violence.

**Loose growth:** some tales make them brothers, some lovers, some rivals, and some one person speaking to his own shadow. Their cycle can absorb comic adventures indefinitely without requiring a final biography.

### Age of City-States — The Bent-Key Fellowship

**Humble ground:** a locksmith's apprentice, disgraced athlete, foreign interpreter, mortuary singer, dock thief, and junior augur are hired to steal an unimportant municipal debt tablet.

**Durable trace:** keys bent deliberately out of shape are left at civic amnesties and failed revolutions. At least one major archive contains a sealed door whose lock accepts no straight key.

**Historical pressure:** the struggle among archive law, debt, citizenship, erased crimes, and the first urban alliances.

**Loose growth:** one member may become a tyrant whose respectable dynasty deletes the theft; another may found a court of appeal; a third disappears while pursuing the edge of the [[The Erasure of Orthea|Orthean crime]] without explaining the Unwritten Weird itself.

### Age of Stars — The Road Without Milestones

**Humble ground:** a survey party is commissioned to settle a boundary quarrel between two forgettable towns.

**Durable trace:** a surviving road changes width, paving style, and legal ownership repeatedly but bears no milestones for nearly two hundred leagues.

**Historical pressure:** the Atherian synthesis of roads, astronomy, civic law, military movement, and imperial imagination.

**Loose growth:** the surveyors may become conquerors only after discovering how easily measurement becomes sovereignty. One refuses the emerging empire and is remembered alternately as the first road-saint, first highwayman, or first rebel against Atherian unity.

### Atherian Age of Glory — The Low Door Companions

**Humble ground:** provincial auxiliaries, actors, debtors, and an imperial clerk shelter together during a riot by crawling through a servants' entrance beneath a star theatre.

**Durable trace:** the Low Door remains part of imperial military folklore: officers entering a new command stoop beneath a deliberately low lintel before taking the high gate in ceremony.

**Historical pressure:** frontier campaigning, class movement, provincial incorporation, and the transformation of talented outsiders into servants or masters of empire.

**Loose growth:** the clerk may rise into the imperial household; the best soldier may become a king beyond the frontier; the actor may create the public version of all their adventures. Their celebrated unity should conceal at least one unforgiven betrayal.

### The Redeemer and Underground Centuries — The Empty-Purse Road

**Humble ground:** smugglers and hired guides agree to move one proscribed family through an imperial checkpoint for money they never receive.

**Durable trace:** an empty purse hangs beside the entrance of certain old pilgrim houses, signifying that refuge precedes payment.

**Historical pressure:** the Redeemer's teaching moving through ordinary criminal, commercial, familial, and military networks alongside the named Companions and martyrs.

**Loose growth:** members may include a future bishop, a recanted believer, a saint never formally recognized, a thief who remains a thief, and someone later condemned as a Sinner. Their adventures should show that proximity to holy history does not erase personality or guarantee holiness.

### Conversion and Decadence — The Children of the Closed Temple

**Humble ground:** former star-temple servants lose their livelihood when an imperial decree closes their sanctuary and take work retrieving objects from abandoned sacred sites.

**Durable trace:** contradictory catalogues of rescued, stolen, destroyed, and converted objects circulate in Church and Sestaran archives.

**Historical pressure:** the coercive edge of conversion, the true created power behind parts of the old religion, and the question of what can be purified rather than destroyed.

**Loose growth:** one becomes a sincere saint; one attempts restoration of a Menhir cult; one sells dangerous objects; one spends a lifetime returning relics to communities that hate them. Later polemic pretends they were never companions.

### Covenant Rise and the Long Frontier — The Four Who Brought Water

**Humble ground:** prisoners from four communities are promised release if they can reopen a poisoned or contract-sealed well no official engineer will touch.

**Durable trace:** four cups are set at certain frontier negotiations, but Covenant, Tarkhan, Jirahar, and Velian tellings disagree over who is entitled to drink first.

**Historical pressure:** the transformation of water-right, oath, portable law, and contract metaphysics into a civilizational system capable of both justice and conquest.

**Loose growth:** one prisoner may become a renowned jurist, another a frontier ruler, another the first great opponent of the breach-courts, and another may later bear a Patron role—or leave behind a separate Patron persona—whose bargains reproduce the prison in subtler form. The continuity must be stated before either version is retained.

### The Fall of Atheria — The Thirty-First Crew

**Humble ground:** dock thieves, deserters, a widowed pilot, two children, and a junior archivist steal a condemned vessel to escape Caleran.

**Durable trace:** a ship's bell bearing an impossible thirty-first mark survives in a mountain monastery far from navigable water.

**Historical pressure:** the refugee crisis, the preservation and loss of archives, and the human beginnings of later resistance networks.

**Loose growth:** their story may brush against [[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register|the Thirty Sisters]] but must never explain it. Some traditions say they witnessed the empty fleet; others identify their stolen ship as one of it. The archivist's missing bundle can seed later political or religious disputes without containing a master key to the Fall.

### Mountain Rebellion and Reconquest — The Six at the Cinder Inn

**Humble ground:** before anyone speaks of restoration, six refugees share the last room in a burned roadside inn and cooperate to recover stolen food, boots, and medicine.

**Durable trace:** the inn's rebuilt table is claimed by a monastery, a military order, and a village tavern; all three have plausible provenance.

**Historical pressure:** the survival of Veridun, the Mountain Rebellion, Caedros I's rise, the Burning Banners, and the moral compromises of reconquest.

**Loose growth:** this is the strongest candidate for a full ascending-company epic. Its eventual members may include the future emperor, a smuggler, a displaced Covenant subject, a Vortharan warlock, a monk or camp healer, and a person later branded collaborator. They begin by wanting breakfast and safety. By the end, one wears a crown, one rejects the new empire, one becomes a saint, one becomes rich, one is deliberately erased, and one may remain active in the Egg White.

The company must not secretly cause every victory. Its power lies in becoming one human thread through a mass historical upheaval.

### Two Crowns and the Great Split — The Last Common Road

**Humble ground:** couriers, minor clergy, caravan guards, and translators are separately hired to carry letters between courts during a routine jurisdictional quarrel.

**Durable trace:** fragments of an unsigned common rule survive in both churches, each accusing the other of removing the final page.

**Historical pressure:** the Lamp Controversy, the Two Emperors, and the Great Split.

**Loose growth:** the company tries to prevent a disaster it does not yet understand. Members later become polemicists, abbots, diplomats, outlaws, and founders on both sides. Their failure should not reduce the Split to one missed delivery, but their friendships make the institutional rupture personal.

### High Noon and Grey Sweat — The Forty-First Ferry

**Humble ground:** ferrymen, gravediggers, an assurance clerk, two thieves, and an exhausted novice keep one illegal burial route operating during the plague.

**Durable trace:** a ferry token accepted nowhere in ordinary commerce remains a sign that a corpse, fugitive, or debtor will be carried without questions.

**Historical pressure:** the Grey Sweat, the queued soul-road, routing assurances, Brannoc's outlaw cycle, and the eventual reform crisis.

**Loose growth:** members may encounter [[Folk Heroes and Legendary Adventurers|Brannoc]] without becoming his formal band. One becomes respectable by inventing the official version of what they did. Another is blamed for selling names to the Undertow. The forty-first ferry may be a boat, a route, a password, or a person.

### Reformation and the Age of Held Proofs — The Company with No Charter

**Humble ground:** failed students, discharged soldiers, a printer, relic-test assistant, itinerant actor, and minor noble's unwanted child are hired for deniable work around a disputed apparition.

**Durable trace:** none yet. This is a deliberately unharvested present-age bed.

**Historical pressure:** every member becomes entangled with a different held proof, and their continued friendship becomes politically impossible before any of them understand why.

**Loose growth:** this is suitable for a campaign-facing or novel-facing company. Do not assign ultimate fates in the historical spine. Their scale may remain local, or one may become a ruler, saint, Sinner, Patron-role bearer, reformer, or historical embarrassment. Any later Patron persona remains separately classified. The world must be able to continue if they die unknown.

## Recurring Cross-Age Seeds

These can be planted around any company without fixing its whole plot.

- **The empty chair:** one member is omitted, disputed, unnamed, or remembered only by a role.
- **The embarrassing profession:** later royal or saintly accounts suppress that a founder began as thief, prostitute, deserter, confidence artist, butcher, debt collector, actor, or collaborator.
- **The small first job:** the famous fellowship first assembled over stolen livestock, unpaid rent, a missing child, bad ale, a broken bridge, or an ordinary burial.
- **The member who went home:** not every survivor seeks greatness. A figure who retires into family life may leave the most consequential descendants.
- **The respectable betrayer:** the member who compromises becomes institutionally successful and writes the surviving account.
- **The false final adventure:** later singers add a satisfying ending to people whose actual fates are unknown.
- **The inherited enemy:** the company defeats an immediate foe but leaves a legal claim, orphan, cult, machine, bloodline, route, or Patron obligation that survives.
- **The reusable relic:** an object associated with the company has a mundane original purpose and only later becomes politically or spiritually charged.
- **The company name that came later:** companions rarely call themselves what history calls them.

## Promotion Test

Before moving a seed into canon, answer:

1. Does it arise from this world's metaphysics, geography, culture, and historical pressure rather than merely restaging a source myth?
2. Can the beginning be told without mentioning the grand fate?
3. Does more than one member possess a meaningful trajectory?
4. Does the company change history without replacing mass history?
5. Does it leave at least one useful trace even if no future story explains the whole legend?
6. Do later cultures disagree for intelligible reasons rather than arbitrary mystery?
7. Is at least one outcome neither triumph nor tragedy, but ordinary human continuation?
8. If every supernatural claim is false, does the human story still work? If the claims are true, do they remain consistent with the Lord's unique uncreated divinity?
