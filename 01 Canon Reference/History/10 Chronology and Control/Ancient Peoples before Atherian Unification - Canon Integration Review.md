# Ancient Peoples before Atherian Unification — Canon Integration Review

## Ruling

The synchronised ancient identity package was approved and promoted on **11 September 2026**. It covers Atherian, Package A and Package B populations through 350 BR. Its public articles describe identities available during the period and do not project later confessional or national names backwards.

The promotion fixes ordinary rules of belonging, the widening uses of several people names and the distinction between ancestry, language, religion, citizenship, dynasty and political allegiance. It introduces no new major war, migration or sovereign figure.

## Canonical distinctions

- **Atherian** can denote shared language and culture, citizenship in a particular Atherian city or political standing within the Caleran republic. Context must identify the intended meaning.
- **Enathi**, **Serathi** and **Vardeni** remain Orphaned peoples. Atherian speech, Common Sky worship and Caleran citizenship do not alter that population history.
- **Ortheian** is an Atherian-speaking regional and political identity outside Caleran civic membership.
- **Qahuri** names the broad river people and civilisation. Tenareti, Qemareti and Sakhreti are regional and crown affiliations, not separate ancestries.
- **Gateway** names an ancient Braided regional population. **Qarzethi**, **Esharati** and **Sarqeni** name civic or town identities within that country. **Spared Towns** names a memorial and political relationship. **Hadrathi** names a related border people.
- Qarzethi identity survives after 520 BR through refugees and descendants. It conveys neither the abolished republic's offices nor collective guilt for its sacrificial government.
- **Jirahar** names a dispersed people maintained through descent, conversion and mutual recognition. **Qiryathi** names relationships with one city. Deportees may acquire Arqeshite language and office without automatic genealogical assimilation.
- **Arqeshite** names a civic and imperial culture centred on Arqesh. House Ubar and the Arqeshite River Empire are a dynasty and polity.
- **Vashari** names the related highland people, including Kavreshi, Ruzhani and Daryami regions. **Vasharan** names the multi-people imperial order of the Crown of Roads and Dawns.
- **Delkosi** names the mixed civic, fleet and court culture formed around Delkos. Its members need not possess Ortheian ancestry.
- Tasarian, Ilyessan and Aveshi remain separate civic populations. No common eastern-coastal people name has been created.

## Samyra Nerethan

Samyra's inheritances are four distinct relationships. She belonged to the old Braided Gateway country and its Sarqeni crown; Hadrima supplied Esharati kin; Tazirun Zaheret supplied a junior Qahuri maternal connection; and House Nerethan carried Delkosi fleet-and-court culture. These relationships strengthened different political claims. They did not create one ancestry, confer a Qahuri crown or make the Nerethans descendants of Eryndor III.

## Scope locks

- Wider proto-Ahdari and Tarkhan self-understanding remains assigned to Package C.
- Folk and Ättar self-understanding remains assigned to Package D.
- Harbour populations beyond the established Atherian and eastern ports remain assigned to Package E.
- Developments after 350 BR remain outside this integration. The global 350 BR identity audit follows completion of Packages C–G.
- Ark-derived, Orphaned and Braided remain population histories rather than ranks of civilisation, moral character or individual purity.
- Every human population retains complete human souls. No ethnic account creates Giant continuity, a Mask identity, a Patron role or supernatural office.

## Canon changes

The integration created [[Peoples before Atherian Unification]] and ten linked public articles. It expanded [[Enathi]], [[Serathi]], [[Vardeni]], [[The Qahuri]] and [[The Making of the Jirahar]]. It added identity routes to the World Historical Atlas, Peoples and Cultures, Peoples through History, the principal Atherian and eastern atlases, selected polities, Qiryath Reqa and Samyra Nerethan.

The five author-only source dossiers remain preserved as the development record. Their promotion status has been synchronised below the source text rather than deleting their alternatives and reasoning.
