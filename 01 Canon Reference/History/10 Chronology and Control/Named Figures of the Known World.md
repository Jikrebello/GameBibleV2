# Named Figures of the Known World

The people behind the [[Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)]]. Each entry records a figure's period, motivation, legacy, and the wound or story hook left behind. Historical design analogues are kept separately in [[Real-History Concordance]].

**Map ruling:** Eclessia sits east of Thalmyria in the mirrored map logic; older western-province phrasing should be read as superseded unless it refers to a local western subregion inside Eclessia.

## Atherian foundations and the republic

[[Avren and Caleth]] (c. 1150–1090 BR) led the estuary coalition associated with Caleran's foundation. Avren killed Caleth during a dispute over command and provisions. Their mother Meral, Caleth's wife Sareth and the surviving children preserved relationships and memories which the reigning household could not control. [[Caleran Foundations - Authorial History]] records Ravessa's actual fosterage and its limited purpose.

[[Ilaron and Tarsenna]] (c. 1000–950 BR) joined maritime resources and Serathi religious learning in a later royal generation. Their Common Sky settlement established shared public ceremonies while preserving rival interests and practices. Both were responsible for the captive sacrifice at a campaign celebration. Their daughter Ilessa maintained festival resources through subsequent royal disputes.

[[Caleran - Foundation and Royal Households]] also places Esvan's waterfront works and Senavar's coercive supply policy in the generations between foundation and Compact. [[Arsuvan - The Giant Kingdom of the Aurin|Arsuvan]] introduces the earlier Giant rulers Veskar, Tarvessa and Arveth, and Lethra's human boat rescue during the kingdom's collapse.

[[Arkenan and Saronikan Cities before the Empire]] introduces Kassilon's loss of command after Erenost's grain seizure, Doreva's grain ventures at Pyrassa and Mesthra's observational teaching at Irenai. They belong to independent eastern histories, without a shared royal genealogy or later supernatural office.

[[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] — The magistrate **Eshren Damar** led the Ilyessa veterans to the Noon Sighting at Sarven (488 BR) and was killed in a bread crowd in 487. **Vespen Harod** accepted the Red Field against the rebel captains of the war of the closed chains in 466, brought troops into Caleran in 448 and died in office around 441. The senator **Arest Corven** followed Veyr into sanctuary at Ilamar and began there the history of the republic's last years, which Casvilar's son Amesar finished on the island and Casvilar's heirs had reason to fear.

## Ancient Qahur and the Gateway country

[[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]] — **Neshara Senareth** began the First Long Crown around 1880 BR; **Talmera of Isethar**, a widowed boat-owner, carried grain for both rival courts during the famine of about 1468 BR, and each later condemned her dealings with the other; and **Khevar II** reunited the basin in 1412 through war, marriage and the execution of rival claimants. **Talzara Yasharet** founded the mixed lower-fan dynasty at Qemaret around 1248. **Iddar Yasharet**, its last king, defaulted on the crown's pledge to Khaseret's House of the Returning Cargo after the low floods of c. 1084 and was walled alive into its harbour vault. **Aramen of Sakhret**, the House's factor, took Qemaret around 1076 with garrisons the House had paid; his court poets called the foreclosure a southern restoration, and his empire's conquests paid the dead. **Ithren III** imposed the Single Radiance (c. 842–821), confiscating the estates of the dead for his garrisons and building Aseret, where no one might be buried. His son **Deshren** took the White Measure regimen from the Copper Orchard's masters and died c. 815 with the confiscated deeds sealed in his palace at Aseret, the only crowned founder of a Second House; his stewards collected rent in a dead king's name. **Zaheret of Tenaret**, a boat-owner's daughter and an allotment captain's widow, led the allotment households against those stewards and was crowned at Tenaret in 814; her soldiers kept the temple fields.

[[The White Measure and the Second Houses]] — **Meresha of Isethar** (d. c. 1439 BR) developed the White Measure and became the first well-attested Second-House Patron. Her freed scribe **Hesenu** entered the Court of Unspent Lamps as a captive human soul. **Khaseret of Qemaret** (d. 1126) opened the House of the Returning Cargo, which went on lending after her death. Its stewards took the Yasharet crown's grain fleet, store courts and gate tolls in pledge and foreclosed on Qemaret around 1076, when its cellars admitted clients with clear accounts and shut out families carrying inherited debt; in 695 it fed Eryndor III's army against the right of her masters to prepare his body. **Tamekha Rasen** (d. 916) built the Copper Orchard through medicine, royal contracts and experiments upon prisoners; demands from her dead estate helped provoke the Tenaret stoppage of 903. **Nemeret the Pure** failed to preserve his soul. A dependent Greed Mask later learned to speak through the estate-office built around his memory.

[[The Gateway Wars]] — Harbour Judge **Qelmar Suthar** lost Tammur to the Atherian commander **Aster Vey** in 649 BR. **Zamaret Ordan**, hereditary captain of Oresh, invaded Caleran's allied country with gifts of grain (c. 612–601). The magistrate **Dema Varin** presided over the Lustration of the Field in 609, and **Celor Marath**, first Warden of the Red Field, held the approaches below Thesra in 606. In the Rope Camp stoppage (c. 536–531) **Damen** was seized carrying the council's terms and **Yasra of Belqara** spoke for the stripped districts; **Meleq Ordan** broke the camp with **King Harzun of Hadrath** and married Harzun's daughter **Sapha**.

[[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth]] — **Ilya of Qarzeth** carried evidence of the child prisons to Esharat. The midwife **Sapha of Esharat**, unrelated to the Hadrathi princess, organised a rescue route; collectors killed her husband and sacrificed their daughter **Neral**. The elderly senator **Orsen Valeor** demanded Qarzeth's destruction, while **Mera Castren** proposed enforced closure and inspection before the final policy was chosen. Sapha of Esharat set the rules of the survivors' meal at Sarqen's landing, which became the spared towns' Table Oath.

[[Qahur and the Gateway before Atheria]] — **Prince Tazirun Zaheret**, a younger Qahuri son, married **Lemara of Esharat** around 548 BR. Their daughter **Hesara** helped conceal children during the siege of Qarzeth and became Hadrima's mother. This maternal relationship brought Qahuri kin and ceremony into the later Nerethan household without conveying rule over the Sacred Basin.

## Qiryath and the eastern empires

[[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/70 History and Conflicts/Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria.md]] — **Mareh bat Sivan**, the Mother of Measures, preserved three road camps during famine and fever around 1800 BR. **Tovan bar Rameq**, **Tirzah bat Oren**, **Hador bar Mavin**, **Othan bar Raqel** and **Barakhel of the Reed Cloak** belong to separate protector cycles. Their victories include recovered captives and saved harvests; their histories also preserve slaughtered prisoners, drowned pledge-children, a protector who took service with the fortress he had fought, a daughter killed as a truce's surety and the destruction of people who never chose the heroes' wars. The shelf's water answered Tovan's household bargain and passed at his death to the children of his sister **Imra**; four of his sons drowned trying to seize it.

**Amariah bat Qoreb** joined rival pilgrimage calendars around 1020 BR. **Yedran bar Aqev**, an altar wood-carrier, caught the offering when the guardian **Shaqel Oram** fell with fever on the sanctuary stair (c. 959 BR), and a crowd that read the cool stones as his confirmation made him Guardian of Qiryath. Around 945 he left **Hanel bar Sem**'s ford garrison to die so that he could carry the spring offering; Hanel's widow **Rava bat Horel** asked the custodians publicly whom the stones answered, and Shaqel's daughter **Mikalet bat Oram** answered that they answered only the offering. The bearer was chosen by lot thereafter, and the grain-room war among Yedran's sons broke the protector house after his death (c. 934).

**Qoram ben Teth**, guardian, married **Zimra bat Keresh** of an Arqeshite river house (c. 829 BR) and had the custodian **Tobel bar Yamin** killed for refusing to witness beside her royal image on the sanctuary stair (c. 826). The stones were cool, as they were at every appointed offering, and Neshkar II's demand of 746 relied on that morning. In the drought that followed, **Haviel of the Lower Stair** had Zimra's diviners thrown down the cistern they had drained to wash the image; the road judges unhoused him for it, and around 817 six women lifted the swollen gate for the families shut outside the walls, Haviel among them.

**Elior bar Hadan**, chief guardian in 746 BR, refused Neshkar II's image and hostages; **Maraiah bat Qesem** argued for hostages to save the city and gained hearers when the sign was withheld. The widow **Yaelith bat Coren**, keeper of Second Qiryath's surviving cistern, married the exile official **Neram bar Elior** and refused to expel the settler-spouses and children who worked her water; the offering carried by her households c. 654 BR ended the Withholding.

[[01 Canon Reference/History/30 Exodus and First Civilizations/Arqesh - River Cities, Kings and Deportation.md]] — **Saruq of Teluq** is the disputed river founder later claimed by House Ubar. **Merod I Ubar** seized Arqesh around 958 BR and established the first durable canal monarchy. **Kassur I Ubar** developed its provincial and deportation system. **Merod II Ubar** sent his son **Ashkur** to have the dead king of Aseret's fled stewards confirmed in their fields; Ashkur entered the sealed palace at Aseret in 814 and was lost, and Merod used the loss to intensify an existing intervention in Qahur. **Neshkar II Ubar** destroyed First Qiryath in 742. His sister **Iltara Ubar** governed Khalbar and supervised deportation and resettlement. **Ishara of the Soot Court**, a smith, led the forced workers who opened Khalbar's gate to the coalition before Arqesh fell in 690. **Kassur III**, the last River-King, lost his portable images in 688 when they began to answer in Eryndor's voice and their bearers carried them over; he surrendered to Arezan, sealed the Gate House's water books to him at Durqesh and died a guarded guest at Kavresh around 676.

[[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]] — **Arezan I Tiravan** united Vashari highland houses against Arqesh and founded the Crown of Roads and Dawns. His sister **Mithara Tiravan** organised food, marriages and communication among the coalition. Her daughter **Parena Tiravan** married Eryndor III and acted between the two courts; after his death she bore **Mehrava** at Kavresh (684), whose paternity Ortheia and Delkos denied. Daryav I married Mehrava around 668, and every Tiravan king from Kavran I descended from her and could claim the Far Road through her. Arezan's heirs **Daryav I**, **Kavran I**, **Mithara I**, **Xarun I**, **Orvaz I**, **Tiraz III** and **Orvaz II** developed the road commands and governed Vashar to the threshold of Atherian empire. **Mazhara** of Daryam refused to call the Witness of Dawn a sign and was made keeper of the royal flame, which edited her carved sayings; **Rathava** condemned Orvaz I's perjury and recanted in the garden to save his labourers. Orvaz I was struck down at the harvest acclamation of 431 BR and died days later.

[[Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest]] — **Valeron II** imposed Ortheian hegemony upon several Compact cities, struck the first coins to pay his standing army and was killed by its soldiers at the Pelatha pay table in 706, when Olyssar's withheld silver left the count short. **Eryndor III**, the Far-Road King, paid his father's killers with the sack of Olyssar, crossed the eastern sea, fed his army in Qahur on a dead financier's loan, joined Arezan in the capture of Arqesh, lost a road to a wood whose price he refused and died in 684 aboard ship at Nemeru, sailing for home, during a White Measure rite he had consented to. His courage, curiosity and skill accompanied massacre, enslavement and the killing of two intimates, an officer who refused to bow before Kassur III's images and a companion who mocked them in the king's own voice. The separate wonders of his campaign became [[The Five Limits of Eryndor]].

[[Wars of the Inheritance]] — **Demeran Kavos**, Eryndor's regent in the west from 704, kept Ortheia and the home army, killed the king's half-brother after the speaking tomb at Delkos made the heirs worthless, and sent after Parena and her daughter agents whom Arezan's road guards hanged at Durqesh. **Serevan of Delkos**, on whose flagship the king died, paid for Eryndor's deathbed rite, sailed his body, Kassur's images and the Terys families to Delkos and governed in the name of the voice in the tomb; his officer houses included the ancestors of Nerethan. **Neresh of Qemaret**, the House of the Returning Cargo's man in the camp, brought the White Measure masters to Eryndor's deathbed under the pledge of 695, held selected Qahuri crown towns through garrison and marriage, and c. 657 had the masters testify that their rite had failed. Their wars destroyed the nominal succession and divided Eryndor's conquests.

[[The Eastern Sea Wars]] — **Liora of Tasar** led the harbour revolt of 514 BR after a Vasharan governor attempted to impose a magistrate and enlarge naval service. **Xarun I Tiravan** retook the city in 508, executed its leaders and sold inhabitants or deported them to the Dawn Road stations before launching the great western invasion of 497; in 493 he claimed the sea in his title and lost his granary fleet at anchor off Ilyessa, where the grain spoiled in sound hulls. **Ebris of Ilyessa**, the pilot who gave his fleet the terms of the Narrows, was drowned by his marines. **Nerica Aven**, who directed the League of the Eastern Oar's fleet, kept Velathene's refuge fire lit for his deserting crews. **Ardash Aresh**, governor of Avesh, held the overland grain until Xarun promised the western command to his house. Liora's fate remains disputed.

## Vathra and the northern ground

[[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]] — **Ozhur IV of Urzavad** tried to keep Urzavad aloft through requisitions of food, ballast, workers and children. His cousin **Varakesh of Dazhur** began the War of Altitude and discarded dependants while claiming to reform the kingdom. The Dynastic Giant **Kelvara** first served Ozhur, then used her knowledge of the descent grounds to help human communities ground Kel-Orun. Her assistance saved thousands and did not undo her earlier participation in captive transfers.

**Seyra Vatan** organised tether crews; **Dorun of Tal-Kesh**, whom the mine and cistern crews he brought into the rising called Dorun of the Draw-Gangs, changed Urzavad's route notices; **Meya of Narash** brought camel houses and western wells into the Great Unyoking. Meya had previously supplied Vathra with captives taken in local wars. In the first winters after the war she sealed the Hundred Wells by a bargain at the dead anchor field; every Narashi crown-mother since has walked the round of the wells at accession, and succession stayed maternal. Their constituencies cooperated without becoming one permanent company or state.

[[The Tarkhan before the Horse Hordes]] — **Boran Tall-Hand** built a chariot house at Daram Ford and seized children from households that refused animals for his teams; when his salvage-copper convoy stuck in the mud and he ordered the grain dumped to save the copper, his own porters killed him (the Feast of the Copper Wheels). The foster cousins **Savan Uruz** and **Demer Khol** founded rival foreign-backed client courts; Savan's soldiers killed him when Arqeshite payment failed, and former captives defeated Demer. **Yalun of the White Camel** and **Marat Two-Bows** joined the coalition against Arqesh for different purposes and treated captives differently after victory.

[[The Ahdari before the Covenant]] — **Harun Savel** violated the disarmed market at Saqara around 1280 BR and caused the trade which supported it to move to Mazar. At Rethmar in 381 BR, the water keeper **Tavisha** removed stores from Ostar's expected route while **Kharuz of the Stone Wells** commanded camel scouts and foot archers. Their coalition dispersed after defeating the Atherian army.

## The imperial-founding generation

[[Teren Casvilar]] (425–370 BR) conquered southern districts, accepted the Red Field in 376 and governed through personal supremacy; seven challengers entered in the muster ground's arms register, kept by his own quartermaster, killed him on the muster ground in 370 before the contest opened. His assassination began the succession wars, in which no commander dared disband an army while any of the seven lived. [[Marovan Veyr]] (435–371), his former partner and principal rival, refused a table laid with an empty seat at Sarqen in 373 and died of fever in sanctuary at Ilamar; his son Ardel inherited his following. [[Lethren Ostar]] (442–381) financed the partnership, sold the defeated rebels of 399 north in lots to the well trade and died during the failed northern expedition at Rethmar.

[[Aurelius Thalion]] (390–318 BR) was Casvilar's nephew and designated heir. He refused the Red Field in 370, governed from the store-house above the muster ground, never commanded in a battle and sent painted boards of his face to war in his place; one of them became the accession portrait that spoke in 247. Around 350, with all seven of Casvilar's challengers dead, he stood on the muster ground and the Red Star's priests declared the Field closed into his command. He had Sarqen only by eating at the spared towns' unranked table and swearing their oath. [[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]] follows his government to his death at the spring meal on Sarqen's landing in 318, the death of his heir Caelor after an untested treasury hall fell on him in 326, and the succession of his younger son Meren. [[Darion Velsar]] (408–350) devised the Writing-In, won at Darsil in 363, accepted the Salt Field in 353 and was killed on it by Melvo. [[Seluc Varroth]] (born 414) ruled the Red Field open and unclaimed in 367, lost his magazines when his own garrisons burned them in 364, and refused to declare the Field closed in 350 while retaining the office of First Celebrant of the Common Sky. [[Istran Melvo]] (born 391) organised the fleets which burned Marelith and blockaded Sarqen, and met Velsar on the Salt Field in person, against Thalion's wish.

[[Samyra Nerethan]] (392–333) ruled from Sarqen. [[The House of Nerethan]] fixes the incoming commander Nerethan, Hadrima of Esharat, Arenes, Tavren and Olymes as the preceding royal generations. Samyra's elder sister Merath, Thalion's second wife, was executed at her request in 358, and Merath's son Meren became emperor. Samyra swore the spared towns' Table Oath at accession; the towns restored her in 373 after Vardek laid an empty seat for Veyr, and put her house out in 350 after her son Amesar, Casvilar's son, killed Mavren Seneth at their table. She sat down at that table as a stranger, where the oath forbade anyone to take her, and lived at Esharat as the towns' guest with Talven and Lethia; Amesar lived in sanctuary on Ilamar until about 320.

[[Households of the Imperial Founding]] records Lysara's support for her son, Evara's management of his household, Ilvara Deneth's death in 361, Merath Nerethan's marriage and execution, Calvena's marriage to Veyr, her public refusal of divorce in 377, her mediation of the 357 agreement with Ardel and her plea for Amesar in 350, Miretha Ardel's grain carts and death at Darsil, and Tobal of Hazreth, the rower seated in the queen's place. The same account preserves the roles of the assassins Deceran Tolm, Mavren Seneth and Corial Draven, the southern organiser Velcara and the speaker Belisar Venn, one of the Four at the Stores, who was written into the register at Velsar's demand with Thalion's consent and killed on the muster ground. Deceran Tolm kept the arms register in which the seven entered their names; Iren Sallas, another of the seven, walked out of Ilamar's sanctuary to his death in 359; Draven died aboard Marelith in 355 and Seneth, the last, at the towns' table in 350. Drenoc and Morveth belong to independent Folk coalitions; Kharuz and Tavisha to the northern well resistance. None is a member of an existing signature company.

## The Age of Glory

[[The House of Thalion]] — **Meren I** (r. 318–286 BR), Thalion's younger son by Merath Nerethan, born at Esharat in 358 and hidden there in a grain bin; he swore the Table Oath on Sarqen's landing at his accession. **Valeran I** (r. 286–263), Meren's adopted son and the husband of Cassara, expanded the roads, stores and waterworks and kept the wardens' test after the quarry strike of 274. **Olarion** (r. 263–247), their son, left the court divided by purges sworn before the accession portrait and died suddenly, precipitating the [[The Year of Four Eagles|Four Eagles crisis]].

**Avaren Mott** (d. 247 BR), senior magistrate at Caleran, governed through the speaking accession portrait during the Four Eagles. Its assurance that no imperial granary was empty sent soldiers behind it into the poor districts. He was killed at the threshold of the audience hall.

[[The House of Veloran]] — **Samor I** (r. 246–229 BR) won the succession war, covered the speaking portrait with a soldier's cloak and kept it veiled by law. **Aren I** (r. 229–203), his adopted son and Tessa's husband, built municipal works and returned the Crown of Olveth. **Orent I** (r. 203–174) reigned through the Sleepwalkers' Summer and took the Arcelon corridor. **Orent II** (r. 174–151) received Raveth into the empire and died in the fever summer of 151 without a named heir.

[[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]] — **Elian I Serrat** (r. 151–126 BR) won a negotiated succession and paid for it with citizenship sworn before the emperor's image. **Elian II** (r. 126–101) authorised famine relief and the resulting crown estates, which passed to his adopted heir **Cassen I** (r. 101–76), in whose last years Atheria took Qiryath. **Velen I** (r. 76–61) unveiled the Face at his spectacles and fell to his guards. **Doran I** (r. 61–42) took power with stores held by the Low Door Companions, re-veiled the Face and had his officers enter by the low door; his son-in-law **Othran I** (r. 42–24) succeeded him. **Othran II** (r. from 24 BR) was emperor at the Redeemer boundary.

**Neriah bar Sorek** (fl. 119–113 BR) — the [[The Keeper of Borrowed Grain|Jirahar administrator of the Raveth hunger]]. His grain system saved towns, transferred defaulting farms to the imperial household and made their former owners dependent labourers. He resigned rather than administer the oath before the emperor's image that estate officers substituted for the neighbours' countersignature.

[[The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door - Imperial History|The Low Door Companions]] (fl. c. 96–54 BR) — Sena Varris, Oren Tal, Brana Keth, Thessa Qor, Miro Fen and Helon Var rose from service work after saving a magistrate. Their later advancement involved hostages, an unlawful raid and betrayal of a family refuge. Helon was executed after freeing condemned hostage children. In 61 BR Miro and Brana supplied Doran's march, and his officers adopted the companions' low door.

## The Age of the Redeemer


**Lord-Scribe Ezrahel bar Qoreth** (fl. 0 AR), keeper of the sanctuary copy of the Qiryath charter his grandfather had drafted, led the senior elders who referred Isfir to the Atherian governor at Second Qiryath. He feared imperial retaliation against the sanctuary and the other towers. He sought a capital sentence over the objections of an elder who challenged the testimony. Jirahar and Redeemer accounts preserve different judgements of his decision. [[Jirahar Memory of Isfir]]

**High Augur Severan of Astaroth** (fl. 0 AR) accompanied the provincial governor to Second Qiryath and urged a public punishment. The governor passed the sentence. Severan's last recorded augural reading, three days after the execution, says the stars did not answer; every other college's book is blank for the same three days. His college recorded that he "laid down the staff". Sestaran humanists pay fortunes for alleged pages of the Severan Fragments.

**The Companions** — thirteen called, one struck (full roll, deaths, and patronages in [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]]). **Ischar the Silvered** — sold him and was struck; the seat is kept empty at every commemorative table, and his refused silver ("Ischar's coin," money no one will admit to owning) circulates still: thirty coins that always come back. The two primacies descend from this table: Eclessia claims the **Commission of the Pen** through **Saint Jareth of the Bound Eye**; Veridun claims the **Ember Succession** through **Amaryah's hearth** at Veridun Minor — Solana carried the ember, Amaryah kept it, Calira died for it.

**Amaryah the Fire-Bearer** (d. c. 55 AR) — the faith's great theological dispute (see [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]): Fire-Bearer or Man-Bearer (settled at Halmenth 411 AR — whose losers, struck off the regency's roll and left alone as taxpayers, survive as the [[The Velian Church|Velian Church]] under Covenant contract); Dormition (Veridun) against defined Unkindled Conception and bodily Rising (Sanctum); honored-not-invoked (Freeflame); and a pending apparition case at Vessing (1352 AR) the Sanctum dares not rule on.

**Saint Aquilan the Turned** (d. c. 45 AR) — of Jirahar blood and training. Born **Sha'il bar Qoreth**, kinsman of the Lord-Scribe who referred Isfir to the governor, Keeper-trained, an Atherian citizen by his house's veiled oath under Elian I's grant; as "Aquilan the Hound" a prosecutor of the early assemblies before the Great Purge, struck blind on the Virelos road and baptised by the congregation he came to arrest. Apostle to the nations; his **Epistles** ("fulfilled, not abolished") are the hinge of supersession and thus of centuries of pogroms he never saw. The Freeflame quote his seatless commission against both hierarchies; the Jirahar keep the exact and unforgiving formula: *"The carpenter we mourn. The scribe we cannot forgive."*

**Yaltah bat Ozer** (d. 68 AR), gate keeper of Migdal Shahar, the deep tower on the Dawn Road where the Tower Revolt ended, opened its Way-Gate on the receiving bench's terms (households, and no one who had borne arms) against her captain's refusal, sent the households through over nine days and broke the scroll-keys before the legions reached the chamber. **Eshkar bar Hanan** (d. 68 AR), the war captain, refused terms that abandoned his fighters and died holding the stairs. Gate benches still cite Shahar when they weigh another tower's danger against its people. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]]

## The Conversion Era

[[Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian - Emperors Wars and Revolts|Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian]] follows these reigns and the provincial wars.

**Emperor Tervan Marcellan** (r. 241–248 AR) rose through the provincial commands. After mutinies and failures to supply the garrisons, he issued the **Edict of Public Sacrifice** in 245, in answer to the Kept Lamps, a grave-lamp cult that had fed mutinies in the unpaid eastern garrisons. Every household had to put out the lamps it kept for its dead at a market altar, relight them from the Stars' college fire and keep them burning green on the colleges' salted oil, and night watches walked the graves for yellow flames. The order widened persecution from cases against individual congregations to a test imposed across directly governed provinces. His successor suspended it in 248, and the congregations' quarrel over who might be given fire spread the daughter-flame custom. [[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization]]

**Emperor Cassian the Baptised** (acclaimed 288; r. 292–316 AR) saw the White Flame at the **Stone Bridge of Velos** in 292, won the civil war and legalised the Redeemer's faith. He kept the augur colleges because they entered the unfreezing reach as his sign, and c. 296 traded a crown warden at the bridge for empire-wide restitution (the Velos Concord). He convened the four sees at Halmenth, granted the Celestial Forum with its Pillar and Nails to Caleran's church and funded basilicas. c. 309 he executed his eldest son and the High Augur of Astaroth over a sealed succession answer. He was baptised, dying, in the unfrozen reach in 316, which gave rise to the Cassian Wager. The vision was real. The content of the sealed answer and the outcome of the Wager remain unresolved. [[Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR|Cassian and the Four Sees]]

**Brask** (fl. 290–291 AR), the Ashforges' enslaved script-keeper, was freed by Cassian's officers so that his oath to the cold furnace and the flame on the Forty would stand: the first sign sworn before crown officers outside the augurs' power. [[The Forty Flames]]

**Emperor Sevrin** (r. 316–348 AR), Cassian's younger son, commanded the southern reserve and gained recognition by guaranteeing the eastern command's pay and revenue. He confirmed restitution and renewed market treaties with southern Folk communities. He refused to transfer the wardenship at Velos and never opened the sealed succession answer. His intended heir died c. 343. **Adran**, Cassian's nephew, accepted command in the east under Sevrin. Adran's son Astrion succeeded Sevrin. [[The Reign of Sevrin]]

**Astrion the Apostate** (r. 348–351 AR), Adran's son and Cassian's great-nephew, was raised by Flame tutors and studied the old Stars through the eastern archives. He commanded the northern desert approaches before his accession. He restored privileges to the Star cults, had the augurs re-enter the Velos reach as Hecatara's answer, and in 350 obtained the Red Star's single answer to his public test of legitimacy. He died at thirty-four on the retreat from a punitive campaign against northern desert communities, under an untried timber span at a dry wash (the Emperor's Cut) that he insisted on standing beneath, as the opener of a work, while the wagons crossed. The field record gives no last words, only his question whether the baggage with the register had crossed. The Church later quoted *"You have won, Carpenter,"* as surrender; army songs give the words to the master carpenter who offered to stand in his place, and Sestaran humanists remember them as tragedy. [[The Reign of Sevrin]] · [[The Conversion of the Empire]]

**Savel Orren** (r. 351–352 AR) was acclaimed during Astrion's retreat, restored church stipends and abdicated at the depot line when Caleran declared for Corlan. He died on his estate.

**Tellan the Water-Bringer** (fl. 351–c. 390 AR), Adran's younger son and Astrion's brother, saved the retreating army with water trains under the bronze standard of the 350 test, the Answered Standard, and held the eastern command under the Accord of the Two Standards (c. 357). He never claimed the throne. He lost the War of the Two Standards (383–384) and died a pensioned captive above the Aurin.

**Corlan Aster** (r. 352–c. 366 AR) held Caleran and the west; **Lucar Aster** (r. 352–377 AR) held the south and north and ruled alone from c. 366. Lucar's chaplains confiscated as idols the ford-tokens of the Folk guides and traders of the southern fort districts, and his officers took children in pledge for relief grain and sold some of them. In 377 his army followed the escaping households into the Kellovar ford unpaid and drowned. Lucar crossed dry on a confiscated silver token, spent three days as a guest at the fire of the house it belonged to, and was killed by his own rearguard when he rode back across. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]]

**Cathra of the Otter house** (fl. 369–377 AR), mother of a chief's house of the southern fort districts whose grandson was sold off the crown estates, kept Lucar three days at her fire under hearth-law after the Kellovar ford carried him across on her house's confiscated token, and sent him home. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]]

**Emperor Vestian the Pious** (r. 377–398 AR) held the inner depots after the Kellovar drowning and was recognised at Caleran while Lucar was still across the river, giving the Velos register to the bishops for that recognition; he tried no one for Lucar's death. He dissolved the augur colleges (383–384), won the War of the Two Standards and made the faith compulsory through the Profession of 385. When the Profession's lamps failed to kindle, his court blamed hidden rites and the Jirahar, and under him the persecution of the Jirahar turned from riot to imperial policy. He wrote *before the Mesakha* into Qiryath's Empty Charter (c. 396) and died in 398, leaving the throne to a son still in the schoolroom.

**Ilios Marrant** (fl. 383 AR), last High Augur of Caleran, made the colleges' final public petition to the Red Star at the Dissolution. Nothing answered.

**Bishop Nasriel of Qabir** (c. 362–c. 440 AR), a reader from a baptised family of Jirahar descent, kept Qabir's count of the unkindled professions (385–388) and carried copies to Raveth. He was elected bishop c. 402 over Caleran's objection and refused to certify relic estates for the regency's tax exemptions. Condemned at Halmenth in 411 and charged under the augurs' old law against publishing an unentered sign, he was rowed out to a Sea Fleet ship by Halmenth's ferry crews and died at Qabir, bishop of cities the regency had struck off its roll and left alone because they still paid their land tax. His deacons kept Qabir's own flame, a daughter of Amaryah's lamp, and would not relight it from the Ember's daughter. His communion survives as the [[The Velian Church|Velian Church]].

**Bishop Ostrel of Halmenth** (fl. 411 AR) hosted the Council of Halmenth and voted to condemn the Man-Bearer teaching; his ferry crews rowed Nasriel out to a Sea Fleet ship the same night. [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]

## The Atherian emperors after Vestian

**The Atherian emperors after Vestian** (398–905 AR) belonged to four houses, each raised by a different way of paying the army. [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

- **The Vestianic house** (398–447). Vestian's son reigned through the court bishops and paymasters who had made his father, selling relic estates freedom from tax and audit. His only son and heir drowned at the Aurin mouth in 446.
- **The Saldane house** (452–587). Founded by the commander of the southern forts, acclaimed by the great sees in the war of 447–452 and paid in immunities. **Teomar Saldane** (r. 509–531) fought the Ubar Toll War (514–523). The emperor of the Thin Floods, remembered in the north as the Granary Thief, kept Caleran's dole by requisitioning northern stores. The house was deposed in 587 after the loss of the north.
- **The Leovarid house** (587–709). **Leovar I** (r. 587–624), a frontier general who travelled with a hermit-bishop, led the crusade of seals and signed the peace of 612. **Leovar II** (r. 681–709), whose accession lamp was the Copper Lamp, died with his heir in the Fever Week of 709.
- **The Corvenid house** (713–905). **Corvenas I** (r. 713–746), commander of the march facing the Velian belt and husband of Leovar II's daughter, won the War of the Two Oaths by riding the marches to renew their oaths in person. **Corvenas II** (r. 861–884) launched the Second Crusade and was forced to abdicate.

**Corvenas III** (r. 884–905 AR; d. 908) — The last Atherian emperor. Sincerely devout, he could reach every remedy only through a public test: riding to the marches to kindle a summons, striking the lost north off the registers, or submitting his relics to Aurelium. He chose rites inside Caleran and turned the palace into the Ash Hall. Covenant files held that his accession rite was finished with warlock fire. On the last day of the siege he carried the Velos Standard onto the land wall and called on the Fire before both armies, and nothing came. The breach-court accepted from him only his person and his relics, by the **Instrument of the Ash Hall**; he lived as their captive keeper and died among them in the winter of 908, and the refuge raised its own standard that summer. Veridun struck his name from its prayers for the dead. [[Prelude to Collapse]] · [[The Invasion and the Fall]]

**Simeon of Aurelium** (bishop c. 694–731 AR) — In 711 examined the Velos Standard, sent east by the Caleran claimant, recorded it silent and held the eastern governors' oaths "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful". Corvenas I's confirmation of Aurelium's right of examination in 713 is "Simeon's primacy".

**Sarion the Pale** (d. 903 or 904 AR) — A march monk stoned in Caleran during Corvenas III's Jubilee for preaching *"The inked ones are coming. They walk with chains and offers of peace. The crown will burn in silence."* His words were cut on a memorial wall after 957.

## The Covenant

**Mujalib, the Final Prophet** (c. 510–572 AR) — Received the contract cosmology from Malak al-Ahd in 541; unified the desert tribes; founded Al-Qahur. Authorial truth: his revelation was the Creditor's masterpiece, while Mujalib himself was wholly sincere.

**The Four Rightly-Sealed Caliphs** (572–601 AR) — Mujalib's first successors, each confirmed by a sealed succession contract said to bear the Prophet's own witness-mark. The fourth seal is disputed to this day — the Covenant's permanent succession fault line, detailed in [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]].

**Qatada Sayf-al-Ahd, "Sword of the Covenant"** (d. 604 AR) — The Wave One general who never lost a field battle; conqueror of the northern provinces, behind armies that marched with breach-courts. Dismissed in 598 at the height of his fame by Caliph Uthar, whose own seal was disputed and who feared the army loved the sword more than the seal — and who obeyed the dismissal without a word, which frightened the caliph more.

**Husan ibn Raiq** (d. 603 AR) — Mujalib's grandson through Ruqaiya, named heir by the Counter-Instrument. After six years among the well-clans of the dry country he went north to arbitration under a safe conduct that named fifty-eight travellers and one road. A flood pushed them off it, and at Bir Qassim the well's bound keeper would not draw for his household; seven died of thirst. On the third night he read the instrument aloud at the well-mouth clause by clause, the origin of the Mourning of the Wells (*And yet the well was dry*). The escort killed him and thirty-three of his household at dawn; the seventeen survivors went west with the drovers and seeded the Sealed-Blood households at Qazmarid. [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]]

**Jarud ibn Sulayt** (fl. 603 AR) — Presiding jurist of the travelling court at Qafra who ruled Husan's safe conduct void because the route was an essential term. The Sealed Blood speaks his name only to curse it; penitential Witnessed-Seal schools teach his ruling as the type of *lawful and wrong*.

**The Banu Mizan, "House of the Scales"** (c. 610–885 AR) — The dynasty of the Velian golden age: translation houses, water-courts, the great libraries where Atherian learning survived in the conqueror's custody. They brokered the peace of 612. Their courts hosted Jirahar philosophers, and their surviving compacts later sheltered Thalmyric exile monks. Their rule in the Velian belt ended in 885, when Qahir's officers took the client courts from a regency; a Banu Mizan marriage line kept Zanthir at Qabir as tributary emirs, and their compacts outlived the dynasty. Every present-day power is embarrassed by how much it owes them.

**Qahir ibn Ruzbeh** (c. 855–902 AR) — Founder of the Qahirid dynasty, of a Ruzhan horse-lord house that sealed with the Covenant for bread when Vashar fell (555–560). Made his name defending the Velian belt against the Second Crusade (874–878); obtained the rulings that the crusade had broken the peace of 612 (884) and that the Clause of the Given binds parties, not powers (885–886); rebuilt the war machine around mass Ahdanite levies, capture companies and siege-golem foundries.

**Sultan-Caliph Idris al-Fattah, "the Unsealer of Gates"** (r. 902–931 AR), Qahir's successor. Sonless at his accession, with every oath in the army naming him and nobody after him, he was kept at Iqashar by his jurists while Qahir's engine-master Barzin and the breach-court train took Caleran in his name in 905; he never saw the city. Covenant jurists render *al-Fattah* "the Opener", for a city opened by reading; Thalmyric chroniclers render it "the Conqueror". By courier he ruled the Eternal Ember a deposit held for the final audit, which kept the basilica open under guard; his officers preserved the Ember and the Pillar of Concordance until the Thalmyric recovery. Like his father he bound army and conquered cities to himself by name; his eldest son Ruzbeh, born 915, was written in as successor-holder. Ruzbeh drowned in 929 and Idris died of fever in 931 before anyone re-swore, widowing every instrument at once.

**Barzin** (fl. 885–905 AR) — Qahir's engine-master, a Ruzhan horse-lord's son who built the name-cage foundries and, in his sixties, took Caleran in Idris's name with the chief jurist of the breach-court train. Thalmyric tradition says the Red Star showed his engineers the Star's share of the land wall, the one span its builders had made to break in the wardens' test; his engineers said they bought its place in a dead augur college's archive.

**Sultan-Caliph Mahdar ibn Idris** (r. 931–979 AR) — Eight at accession; the Regency of the Bench ruled for him to 940 and made the throne the Sublime Threshold. He refused to arm Durayd's south, lost Caleran in 957 while his army was held on the northern wells by the Long Reach, and armed none of the amirates that followed until his death; his son spent the Horse War buying horses and building walls in the north.

**Gulnar** (fl. 931 AR) — Vashari-born engine feeder at Iqashar who stopped the walking Depth-Core engine, later the Elder, in the dye-yards by giving its feeding name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir": the formula behind the Sublime Threshold. Foundry masters said the beast drank itself quiet; the dyers' quarter credits her.

**Durayd ibn Sahl, Amir of the Southern Threshold** (fl. 931–957 AR; d. c. 971) — Amir of occupied Caleran who swore the south to himself when Idris's instruments widowed and offered to sell the Ember back in 948. In the funeral revolt of 957 he sent his guard to carry the Ember to his ships; its Covenant keepers, the third generation of them and born in the occupation, held it against him, eleven of them dying at the lamp, and he fled by sea to Qabir, leaving the garrison families behind. The surviving keepers made the terms on which the city opened. The southern garrisons, sworn to an absent man, split into separately sworn amirates. He grew old as the guest of Qabir's emir. Iqashari chroniclers say he ran from what he had stolen and tried to steal the one thing that was not his.

**The first Unnamed Custodian** (fl. 1003–1011 AR) — Custodian of the Vault of Names who pledged his true name to an Iqashari lending house to ransom a son taken by Tarkhan riders, leaving the army unable to march from 1006, then freed the Vault in 1011 by surrendering his name into its deepest custody. Registrars give him three former names. Every Custodian since has surrendered his name at investiture.

**Sultan-Caliph Tamim the Auditor** (r. 1029–1068 AR) — Wrote off the southern amirates in the Audit of the South (1033), divided the Vault of Names into the Living Register and the Deep Vault (1059), and chartered Mazrakan (1067).

**Sultan-Caliph Khurzad** (r. 1112–1142 AR) — Enforced the Court of Breaches' 1126 ruling for the Southern Houses with mercy towers and lost the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139) to Caedros III; his heir died of dysentery in the siege camp in 1135.

**Sultan-Caliph Suhrab the Debtor** (r. 1190–1243 AR) — A younger son who had taken the jurist's oath renouncing the throne and spent his youth as a clerk of his father's failed commission on the codes; the Court of Breaches voided his renunciation as coerced after the heir died at the Mazrakan fair in 1189. The apex. Instead of ranking the provincial codes he wrote the **Ledger of Ledgers**, a book of the occasions on which Covenant law had killed the Covenant's own people, each entered as a debt and closed by the clause owed; he read its first book, Bir Qassim, at the dry well in 1194, where Sealed-Blood mourners answered every clause *And yet the well was dry*, and the Iqashar crowds named him the Debtor. Its book of the Southern Deeds produced the Unburdened Deeds (1212) and so the **Battle of Qarath's Well** (1214), which he won on a sworn well's guest-peace; he kept the beaten emperor three days as a guest and sent him home with the bill for the water, the Southern Deeds districts. His most gifted son, Prince Mihrzad, died of the Sweat in 1241 holding the frontier forts as a living witness, with only his Guard escort to see it; Suhrab's last book (1243), written so that the prince's name could be released, named the Guard's officer bench a competent witness for the Threshold, and every accession since has been made before it. The dynasty has been living off his capital ever since.

**Prince Mihrzad** (d. 1241 AR) — Suhrab's most gifted son. In the Emptying of the Benches he rode the Qarath-frontier forts as a living witness while their garrisons re-swore to the Threshold; the forts he reached held. His Guard escort carried the Sweat into three forts that had been clean, and he died of it at a well-fort where only that escort survived, so that his name could not be released until his father's last book. Frontier villages remember the rider who brought the Sweat; the Iqashar bazaar sings the Rider of the Benches.

**Sultan-Caliph Tahmur** (r. 1243–1268 AR) — Suhrab's surviving son, the first sovereign to accede before the Guard's officer bench; put down the Pageless Prince (1249–1252) and began paying the Black Wind in 1265.

**Prince Qaydar, the Pageless** (d. 1252 AR) — A lodged Qahirid prince whose register page burned in the plague; freed by the Court of Breaches' "no page, no lodging", he raised the Basin and was killed. "The Vault can lose a page; the grave cannot": after him the Cull of Brothers turned from the lodge to the knife and the blinding needle.

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq I** (r. 1268–1301 AR) — Paid Qarayel's "escort fees" until the horses' grace thinned in 1300; his courts voided the charters of Meshalem's returners (1289–1291).

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq II** (r. 1301–1349 AR) — Hired the Iron Horde after the Partition; his night raids brought on Orinel, the Crimson Pilgrimage and the loss of Zehrim. His death opened the Cull that made Marzuq III.

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III** (r. 1349 AR-present) — Reigns from Iqashar as Qahirid suzerain over a layered imperial order. Al-Qahur supplies sacred legitimacy, independent Qazmarid rejects his universal claim, and autonomous Zanthir pays contested tribute. Clever and indolent, he depends on Ahdanite officer-brokers and the Vault of Names. The Guard bench witnessed his accession in 1349 while the Cull killed or blinded his brothers; it costs him sleep. See [[Present-Day Courts and Crowns (1360 AR)]].

**Caliph Uwais, Custodian-of-the-Wells** — Sacred ruler of Al-Qahur whose acknowledgment legitimises the Qahirid throne. Politically constrained by Sword-Regent Nadir and the military households.

**Sword-Regent Nadir ibn Haraz** — Effective governor of Al-Qahur, balancing Ahdanite-descended military houses, irrigation councils, pilgrimage, and dependence on Uwais's sacred authority.

**Caliph-Wasi Zahran** — Sealed-Blood ruler of independent Qazmarid, claimant through Ruqaiya bint Mujalib and asserted custodian of the Counter-Instrument.

**The Emirate of Zanthir** (601 AR–present) — First-wave conquest emirate turned last autonomous remnant: survivor of the Reconquest by timely tribute and timelier treachery (in 1044 it starved the last Covenant amirates of its fleet and grain and agreed with Veridun to pay tribute to each empire in alternate seasons), still standing as "the last pearl of the Banu Mizan." Under **Emir Badis al-Zanthiri** it pays tribute toward Iqashar and Veridun in alternating seasons, insures with the Medori, shelters Jirahar quarters, and is universally assumed to be the next war's first battlefield.

## Thalmyria

**Caedros I the Unbowed** (r. 922–957 AR) — founder-saint and rebel general. A commander of the mountain rebellion who promised to feed Caelmount after its road-menders broke their road under Amir Qazem's Covenant column (917), took Virelos and its granaries to keep the promise (921) and was crowned there in 922, then brought the rival refuge armies under one crown in the War of the Burning Banners (922–949). He died at the Pillar of Concordance in 957, on the day the Ember's Covenant keepers opened the forum precinct to his army after Amir Durayd fled. Thalmyric witnesses said the White Flame took his body, leaving armor and sword; Eclessian relic courts reject the canonisation for want of remains and independent examination. The event completed the sacred objective of his reign but not the [[The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)|Long Reconquest]].

**Caedros II the Road-Maker** (r. 957–979 AR) — secured Caleran, rebuilt its walls, protected surrendered quarters, and opened the roads required for the next generations of province-by-province return. His engineers took the canal heads in the hills above the separately sworn amirates and set water-courts there, so that farmers who registered got their water and most amirs were left with walls above dry fields. He inherited a sacred victory and an unfinished war. He paid for both by restoring the Church's estates and filling its sees from the families who had paid for the return, and under him the war-lamps that had kindled before Caleran's walls kindled less and less often.

**Ilarion Aetherion** (r. 979–984 AR) — Caedros II's son. Died of the lung-fever that followed the eruption of Mount Askre; his death put a soldier on the throne.

**Leovar III the Lampbreaker** (r. 984–1011 AR) — Vlastravan march commander and Caedros II's son-in-law, acclaimed by the army after Ilarion's death, taking the regnal name of the old oath-holding emperors. As a young man he carried a war-lamp before Caleran and saw it kindle; in the decades after, he watched it go dark. In the **Ashfall Omen** (983–984) the Great Lamp of Veridun shrank to a bead for forty days, and he concluded that the Fire refused the vessels men had made and sold. The **First Breaking** (987) smashed icon-lamps, melted relic-braziers, broke the army's war-lamps and exiled the flame-monks; he hooded the Great Lamp rather than break it. Soldiers loved him; monks recorded him as a demon; the reconquered valleys he left without funeral monks remember the dead who were not buried. In the dry years after 1008 his own soldiers refused to break the lamps families lifted from their sunken wells; in 1011 he went to break one himself at Dryma, drank from the drought well and died of the flux within the week.

**Ioan of Velia, "the Potter"** (c. 950–1024 AR) — A Velian potter's son who took vows in a Thalmyric march monastery and was driven home by the First Breaking to his father's kilns at Keramion, in the Velian belt under the old Banu Mizan compacts and Covenant protection. He and the exiled monks made cheap clay lamps stamped on the foot with forty sayings, the *Sayings for the Foot of a Lamp*, which Velian traders sold north under Covenant customs seals; the Covenant taxed the argument that ended the Breaking, which everyone found embarrassing. When the Breakers smashed the lamps the feet survived; households mortared them into their walls, children traded them to make up the forty, and the Synod received them in 1013. The best known states the position that eventually won: the Flame is uncreated but *consents* to dwell in what sanctity prepares, for "the Redeemer had a body; the Fire may have a lamp."

**Anthe** (d. 1027 AR) — Caedros II's daughter, Leovar III's widow and Leovar IV's mother, the Breaking's last defender. After her husband's death she held the palace guard and kept the bronze hood on the Great Lamp; on the Night of the Thousand Lamps (1013) she ordered the guard to hold the cathedral doors, and its captain opened them. She lived out her life in palace rooms without a lamp and died there in the dark. Eclessian apologists excused Adalmar's coronation of 1002 by calling her husband's seat "the hooded throne".

**Arsen** (fl. 1013 AR) — Captain of the palace guard who opened the cathedral doors to the Night of the Thousand Lamps against the dowager's order; his mother was in the square. The households lifted the hood off the Great Lamp with their own hands, and the palace guard still swears its captains at that door.

**Leovar IV** (r. 1011–1049 AR) — Leovar III's son, acclaimed at thirty-five. Through his father's reign he carried his own war-lamp hidden in his saddle-bag; it never kindled in his hand. He temporised for two years while his mother held the hood, then knelt when the households carried a thousand lamps into Veridun (1013), recalled the monks and created the office of Master of Lamps to keep the restored lamps. After his Master of Lamps, his old companion Kallion, was burned to death forcing a wayhouse lamp before Meliton (1029), he sealed every lamp but the Great Lamp and the Ember in crown lamp-houses to keep the Fire out of men's hands: **the Second Breaking**, or Impounding. The keepers sold the oil. When he died of a fall on the cathedral ice the houses were opened for his bier and found cold.

**Kallion, Master of Lamps** (d. 1029 AR) — Leovar IV's companion of the saddle-bag years and the first Master of Lamps. Seized the wayhouse lamp of Pelin's Bridge to prove that a restored lamp could serve an army, forced it against the name-bound walls of Meliton before both armies, and was burned to death.

**Empress Theodosia the Kindler** (r. 1049–1076 AR) — Leovar IV's daughter, empress in her own right at twenty-four. Inherited lamp-houses found cold at her father's bier and an empire whose household fires were dead, while families walked to Caleran for a light from the Ember. Took the last southern amirate's surrender, joined the roads and instituted the **Feast of the Rekindling** (1052), still Thalmyria's greatest liturgical day: every lamp in the empire relit from one flame carried on foot from the Ember at Caleran. She walked the first mile barefoot, in penance for her father's houses.

**Caedros III, "the Wall of the World"** (r. 1121–1167 AR) — Raised in the frontier monasteries and a garrison captain before his crowning; a big, convivial man who danced at district weddings and stood godfather to more than four hundred district children. Married Chrysanthe, a Saronikan admiral's daughter, whose father's ships carried the settlers' grain; they had a son, Orestan, and three daughters. Resettled the frontier the Two Winters had emptied as military districts owing a lance and a lamp, and won the **War of the Southern Deeds** (1131–39) when the district lamps kindled in defence of their homes, his own among them at Kolonna (1134). After Orestan cut down surrendered men at Vresna (1137), he declared at Kolonna (1139) that his heir would be the claimant whose lamp the Fire kindled at his bier. Spent the Covenant's ransom and then Sestaran loans on High Noon's roads, towns and monasteries, leaving the treasury in debt. In his last years he sat up at night with the lamp that had kindled at Kolonna; it never kindled again. At his bier no claimant's lamp kindled.

**Orestan** (c. 1114–c. 1187 AR) — Caedros III's son, commander of the district horse, who had some four hundred surrendered Covenant levies cut down at the ford of Vresna (1137). Barred without being named by the Kolonna Declaration, he brought a lamp scripted with Shell-fire to his father's bier in 1167; an old warlock smelled it, and the other claimants had him blinded on the cathedral steps, the first imperial blinding in Thalmyric memory. He lived twenty years more in a Kedravan monastery.

**Despina of Kolonna** (fl. 1167 AR) — A district soldier's widow whose lamp kindled in the nave at Caedros III's funeral while the seven claimants', the emperor's own son among them, stayed dark or burned false. The court suppressed it; the district armies made it their warrant. She refused to return to court.

**Andrion, "the Widow's Emperor"** (r. 1169–1198 AR) — Commander of the military districts, acclaimed by their armies after the War of the Seven Lamps. Inherited Caedros III's Sestaran debts and extended the district levy to pay them; his reign saw the Threshing Floor in Elenara.

**Markellos the Guest** (r. 1209–1215 AR; d. 1231) — Andrion's grandson, raised in the districts. Marched in a drought summer to stop his frontier families accepting the Covenant's Unburdened Deeds. At Qarath's Well he heard Nikandros read the guest-peace on the curb aloud and drank anyway, last, from his men's bucket; the army sickened at dawn when it took up arms. He stayed with the men who drank, was kept three days as Suhrab's guest with elaborate contract-courtesy and sent home with the bill for the water: the Southern Deeds districts. He refused to pay. His uncle Sergian took the farms of every district holder who had drunk and deposed him to make the bill his private debt; the Covenant's Court answered that the bill was the Throne's. He sheltered with the Kedravan monasteries, and in 1225 at Virelos poured out a cup of water untasted and gave his claim to Nikandros. He died a monk at Kedra. The shame lies not only in defeat but in an empire proving it feared humiliation more than injustice.

**Sergian** (r. 1215–1217 AR) — Markellos's uncle, the Caesar who kept the empty treasury. Declared the Forfeiture of the Drinkers (1214), giving the farms of every district holder who had drunk at Qarath to hired companies, deposed his nephew to make the bill for the water a private debt, and took the throne. Killed in the Crown Hall by drinkers' families who had walked to Veridun with their door-lamps.

**Nikandros I the Restorer** (r. 1225–1263 AR) — Of a cadet Aetherion branch in Kedrava, schooled in a refuge monastery; commanded the Kedravan monastic contingents at Qarath, read the well's guest-peace aloud to the emperor's council and was overruled, kept the refuge rule against drinking sworn water and withdrew in order with the wounded. Sheltered the deposed Markellos, who in 1225 at Virelos poured out a cup of water untasted and gave him his claim for an oath to return the drinkers' farms; he kept half of it by evicting Sergian's companies, violently. Every emperor crowned at Virelos since has poured out a cup. Crowned with the monasteries and Vlastrava behind him, he stabilised the rump. Swallowed two centuries of schism to ask Eclessia for soldiers (1227); the Sanctum's price was Veridun's submission, the Great Lamp carried east and tried by its relic court. He refused — *"a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned"* — fought on alone, and won back half the loss when Covenant garrisons marched home in the plague (1238–1246). Thalmyric liturgy still includes a line widely understood to mean *the East sold us for a doctrine.* Present-day Caedros IV claims the restoration line.

**Evandros the Sower** (r. 1263–1290 AR) — Nikandros I's son. Resettled the recovered districts and the plague-emptied interior with veterans and Kedravan families; sent the embassy that failed to buy Gavrin the Farrier.

**Stavren the Patient** (r. 1290–1329 AR) — Evandros's son. Kept the peace on Sestaran credit, founded the war college at Vatrin, lost both sons to the summer flux of 1327 and adopted Caedros, the hero of Zehrim, who reigns in 1360 as Caedros IV.

## Eclessia

**Hedwen of Kaltfurt** (d. c. 1010 AR) — Markish miller's widow who took up the march banner at the ford of Kaltfurt (974) and held it between Grass-Tarkhan riders and a refugee column; the banner kindled and the raid broke. In 1001 she testified at Aurelium that it had never burned in Adalmar's hand; her testimony is in the register, struck through. Refused to carry the banner into the Folk Wars. Venerated by millers and in Markish reform country.

**Adalmar the Great** (r. 975–1019 AR; crowned Emperor of the Atherians 1002) — Markish margrave's son, acclaimed king of the eastern marches after Kaltfurt. Joined the bishops' principalities by war, marriage and a single register. His **Folk Wars** (982–1008) granted frontier land held by Folk seer-oath to his Markish lords; at the **Drowning at Wolfsmere** (993) he had four thousand captive oath-keepers and clan-seers drowned in their own lake "to drown the oath with its keepers". The next winter the lake stood up and took his shore-fort, and his frontier stopped there. In 1001 the Sanctum's relic court credited the Kaltfurt banner's kindling to him; in 1002, amid Leovar III's First Breaking, **Lord Primarch Silvane II** crowned him in the Sanctum Divinitatis, declaring discharged the eastern oaths held "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful" since 711. The chronicle says he *"knelt surprised"*; no historian in or out of world believes it. Eclessia's greatest hero and its deepest sin, in one man. (Authorial note: Wolfsmere is a standing leftward thin space, "the Lake That Remembers", and a live Grenzburg-adjacent hook.)

**Weylan Oath-Bent** (c. 950-1012 AR) - The Folk war-leader who fought Adalmar for twenty years, disappeared after Wolfsmere, and accepted baptism with his surviving clans around 1005 AR; his baptism bought the Peace of the Bent Oak, which wrote the lake-law (no bell, no boat, no iron) into the march charters. To Eclessia, a trophy convert; to the Folk, "the Bent Oak," spoken with pity, contempt, and unbearable understanding in equal measure. Whether his baptism was submission, strategy, sincerity, or all three remains disputed among his descendants and rivals.

**Ludovar the Mild** (r. 1019–1046 AR) — Adalmar's surviving son: devout, earnest and recorded silent at every examination the Sanctum held for him. His sons bought examinations from rival sees and carried banners some of which had Shell-fire in the staff-head.

**The Partition of Drossfeld** (1049 AR) — Adalmar's grandsons **Aldwin**, **Lotharic** and **Gervin**, unable to win, drew lots for three portions sealed in Adalmar's helmet. Aldwin drew the south with Aurelium and the imperial title; Lotharic the east, later joined to Virelia by his daughter's marriage to Radomir I; Gervin the middle march, which held the existing Duchy of Caldria and fragmented within two generations into Brauvalis, Veridancia, the Korrheim valleys, Ironvale and the Sylvan Marches. The wax of Gervin's lot was smudged along its eastern line; every present border dispute in Eclessia cites Drossfeld the way lawyers cite scripture, and the middle march's disputes begin with the smudge.

**Radomir I of Strazgorod** (fl. 1059–1061 AR) — Vesari prince gathering the river princedoms. When Grass-Tarkhan riders burned his hall in 1059 and its house-fire died, he sent his fire-keeper west to relight it from the Ember rather than become a neighbour's client. The lamp lit itself in her hands in Veridun and Makarios the Adamant kept it; unwilling to be Veridun's client under hearth-law, Radomir took baptism from the Sanctum, married Lotharic's heiress and lit his new hall from Lotharic's hearth. Their son ruled Virelia.

**Zdena of Strazgorod** (fl. 1061 AR) — Radomir I's aunt and the fire-keeper of his house. Carried its dead hearth-lamp to Veridun to ask for a light from the Ember; when Makarios refused to light an unbaptised hearth, the cold lamp took the Flame in her hands before witnesses of both churches. She told him, "It chose my hand. I carried it," and stayed to tend it; her descendants still keep the Virelian Lamp in the cathedral close. Venerated in Virelian villages; on neither church's calendar.

**Ermbold the Idle** (deposed 1102 AR) — King of Aurelia of Aldwin's line and last Adalring emperor. In the first of the Two Winters he refused to carry the Crown of Embers in a penitential procession ("It is a hat, not a relic"); the other kings deposed him at the winter diet of Drossfeld. In 1104 the Sanctum took custody of the **Crown of Embers** "in trust, until the realm is worthy of one head." That vault is the reunification mandate made physical: the Church literally keeps the empire in a box, and every king in Eclessia knows the Primarch could, in principle, take it out.

**Lord Primarch Bonifac III & Cardinal Hubald of Caldria** (fl. 1015 AR) — Bonifac demanded that Veridun acknowledge Simeon's primacy — Aurelium's right to examine the restored lamps, the Great Lamp first — and the Sanctum's trusteeship of the imperial title; his legate Hubald, received with calculated insult, nailed the bull of anathema **to the Great Lamp's stand** in Veridun's cathedral — four nails, and both sides understood the reference to the Four Nails of the Pillar instantly. Patriarch **Makarios the Adamant**, a returning flame-monk whom the lamp-carriers had acclaimed on the Night of the Thousand Lamps (1013) and who would not let an eastern court examine a lamp the households had unhooded with their own hands, answered with a counter-anathema before the nails were cold. The Great Split, 1015. Neither anathema has ever been lifted.

**Lord Primarch Gelasin II** (r. 1242–c. 1266 AR) — Elected by both colleges of the Schism of the Twin Cardinals; confirmed the Ferry Rule for the plague years only, kept the usurper's bishops and refused to rule on him; reorganised the old assurance letters into the Chancery of Sendings (1252), whose Sending Houses sang named rites for named dead.

**Aubert of Corvel, the Ferryman Primarch** (d. 1251 AR) — Cardinal and twin of **Aimery of Corvel**, who was elected Primarch in 1240 and died of the Sweat that night unsung. Aubert buried him secretly, wore his ring for twenty months and issued the **Ferry Rule** (1241), letting any baptised person sing the funeral where no priest lived. Exposed in 1242 when his brother's unsent figure was seen at the conclave garden wall; the colleges' quarrel over his acts is the **Schism of the Twin Cardinals**. Aurelian canonists call him the Usurper of the Ring; Brauvalis abbeys and the Freeflame, the Ferryman Primarch. [[The Grey Sweat]]

**Saint Gerlind of Lindenfurt** (c. 1228–1279 AR) — Miller's daughter who sang the plague dead out for bread (*"the dead are named, not bought"*); acquitted by a relic court in 1266; canonised by Alessior II, whose Chancery then printed her face on assurance letters. Claimed by the Sanctum and the Freeflame alike. [[The Grey Sweat]]

**Lord Primarch Alessior II** (r. 1281–1296 AR) — A Malvora of Sestara orphaned by the Sweat, raised by the chapter that sang for his family, and as bishop known for singing haunted villages quiet; an ascetic who slept on a board. Bartolo Medori's credit bought his election without his asking, and he paid the bill. His **Bull of the Sufficient Seal** (1283) taught that a letter sealed by the Chancery placed a soul in the Church's keeping before any rite was sung, which removed every limit on **routing assurances**; he signed letters by night with his own hand and rebuilt the Sanctum's dome to carry the names of the letter-bought dead in gold. His treasurer Silvio Quarne ran the trade. Within a month of his death in 1296 painters reported him walking beneath the dome; the Sanctum denies it. The direct, proximate cause of the Reformation: Ansgar's First Grievance answers his bull, and the Grievances name him eleven times.

**Silvio Quarne, the Bridge-Seller** (c. 1238–1299 AR) — Alessior II's Cardinal-Treasurer; industrialised the assurance trade, staged the Saint-Leon apparition (1285), after which Gerlind's free singers were burned out of their quarter in Aurelium, and sold unsung letters to brokers as "dead paper". Died in Vorthara; his ledgers vanished.

## The Reformation

**Ansgar of Brenholt** (1264–1305 AR) — A marches friar and Vestibule-doctrine lecturer who, after counting more than ten letters sold for every rite sung in his own parish, sang the **Hundred Grievances** (1297) as a litany at the Blackwater pithead in Brenholt over forty-one drowned miners, the crowd answering each with Gerlind's *named, not bought*: *"No seal of wax weighs anything at the Weighing. You have sold the poor a bridge that is not yours."* Because soul-routing is objectively real, this was not rhetoric but an accusation of metaphysical fraud — and everyone, including his judges, half-knew it. The Grievances went down the Weiss as a song, families buried their dead the Brenholt way, and the upper Weiss stopped buying letters. The **Synod of Aldersbruck** (1305), under the legate Tiburce Vaudrey, took up his Hundredth Grievance and put his psalter to the brazier; it did not burn. The legate voided the test and held Ansgar in the Duke of Ironvale's toll-tower on the bridge, and the spring flood took the tower; his body was never found. The reach below the broken bridge is the Preacher's Water. **The Unburnt Psalter** sits in a lead vault under the Sanctum; its existence is officially denied and universally known — and in a world with a proof-of-sainthood protocol, both churches understand exactly why no one dares test it. A permanently loaded gun.

**The Ashenmen Risings** (1306–1329 AR) — Ansgar's home region swore in the Weiss at the broken bridge within a year of the flood and fought out of the pits and drifts of the upper Weiss for twenty-three years without one oath breaking: burning seals in the deep galleries, hanging Sending House chaplains from the winding gear, starving the iron country, and surviving the Caldrian drowning of the Brenholt drifts (1314). Rainald burned the seals in 1329, and the bands came up into his army. The risings sit in and around the Ironvale Dominion, explaining both its existing underground-movement canon and the later guild-industrial vacuum that Grand Minister Aldric Weyn inherits.

**Doctor Lucian Ferro** (1279–1352 AR) — the movement's true architect, and never its member. A southern jurist, junior notary at the Synod of Aldersbruck, whose honest record of the unburnt psalter and the bridge-tower was copied before anyone could stop it. As a Sanctum canonist at Veridance he was commissioned c. 1312 to set out the Brenholt teaching and refute it; the first half, **The Order of the Open Flame** (c. 1316), systematised it so fairly that the reformers adopted it whole: the Flame kindles wherever sanctity gathers — *"the Lord's Fire keeps no landlord"* — therefore no hierarchical mint, no assurance trade, no relic-court monopoly, congregational hymn-casting under tested elders. The refutation never came. Suspended and never tried, he refused Rainald's invitation to Ironvale (*"I wrote their case. I did not promise to plead it."*) and sat as the Council of Veridance's unvoting witness from 1337 until his death in 1352. Reformed duchies built on his book are famously orderly, literate, and merciless. **Duke Rainald of Ironvale** (duke 1309–1341), son of the **Duke Ortwin** whose tower held Ansgar, watched the flood take it at fourteen and in 1329 burned Ironvale's Sending letters at Brenholt, ending the Risings. Rainald was the *last* Duke of Ironvale: the [[Freeflame Reformation|Wars of Reform]] cost his house its blood (his last son died at Falkendorf in 1340), its credit, and its Church standing, and the guilds filled the vacuum (the present Grand Ministry dates its charter to the house's extinction). Ferro is dead eight years at present day; his book governs Ironvale, and his students teach on both sides.

**Lord Primarch Celestin IX** (r. 1330–1354 AR) — convened the **Council of Veridance** (1337–1352), summoned Ferro to deliver his refutation and kept him fifteen years as the Council's witness, so that the Council answered his book line by line: it abolished assurance sales (too late), repudiated the Sufficient Seal and so ordered the Great Arrears, codified the five sainthood tests and rebuilt seminary education. In 1338 it chartered **the Order of the Lantern** — teaching, missions, and quiet intelligence — from the outlaw night-singers of **Father Ivo of Saint-Leon** (c. 1283–1350), carried as a child out of the quarter Quarne's riot burned in 1285 and later Ferro's student. The lanterns were first carried because by day the singing was forbidden; the old Church kept the name as a claim that the Flame belongs in a vessel, carried where the Church wills.

**The Korrheim Accord** (1343 AR) — *Every roof under its lord's lamp*, sworn in the plain speech of the river country and copied by Sanctum canonists into their law-books as *whose the realm, his the rite*: each recognised ruler, whether duke, ministry, canton or chapter, fixes his territory's confession. Forced by the Grand Ministry's Iron Fast (1341–1342) and sworn with hands in the Weiss below the broken bridge at Aldersbruck, it excludes the radical Freeflame congregations, the Unsworn, who refused to swear on a relic; it binds no one's conscience and does not so much as mention the reunification mandate — a cold peace everyone signed and no one believes. Present-day Eclessia lives inside its silences.

## The Sestaran Merchant Age

**Bartolo Medori the Elder** (c. 1215–1301 AR) — The Medori bought their seat in Fiorenzia's Golden Book in 1238, when the Grey Sweat had emptied a third of it. Bartolo built the bank into the peninsula's dominant credit house: he cleared the plague dead's estates, carried Sending payments, bought Alessior II's election (1281), unasked, because he had read the bishop's preaching on the seal, and took the Chancery registers as surety (1296). His ledger-maxim “lend to both, collect from the winner” is quoted as cynicism and practiced as policy.

**Lauro Medori the Magnificent** (1289–1341 AR) — The patronage apex: academies, galleries, the Fiorenzian flowering — heir to his grandfather Bartolo's purchase of Alessior II's election, which makes the Medori co-authors of the Reformation, and creditor of Nicanor Vetti, whose *Mirror* is dedicated to him. The family does not discuss this.

**Arrigo Medori** (fl. 1341–1349 AR) — Lauro's son, who inherited the bank as the Korrheim Accord ended the war-loans. He paid for the Pyre of Masks at the carnival of 1346 to answer Frate Severin, fled down the river to Orsena with the master registers when the crowd turned from the fire to the palace, and came home in 1349 at the bankrupt senate's invitation, giving the city's churches the Fiorenzian sheets of the registers in return for the ducal chair, hereditary in his line. The first hereditary Duke of Fiorenzia; Fiorenzians say the city bought back its dead with its liberty.

**Frate Severin of Fiorenzia** (1301–1349 AR) — Flame-preacher who answered the Sermoran "Bank of the Dead" pamphlet of 1345 by preaching that the Medori held the city's dead in pawn and that the carnival masks had become doors for the Hungers, which are shut by naming, never by fire. In 1346 he begged the city not to light the Medori-paid **Pyre of Masks**; it was lit, witnesses still argue about what they saw standing in that fire, and the Medori fled with the registers. Holding no office, he led the city's **Long Vigil** for four years. At the carnival of 1349 he was found in the mask-makers' cellar wearing a gilded mask nobody could lift from his face, and was buried masked outside the walls; weeks later the senate bought the city's dead back from the Medori with a hereditary ducal chair. In a world where the Flame is real, the question *was Severin kindled, proud or murdered?* has teeth: the Freeflame claim him, the Lantern studies him, Fiorenzia dreams about him.

**Nicanor Vetti** (1289–1347 AR) — Sermoran-born Medori factor whose foreign desk failed in the Reform Wars defaults; sold into Fiorenzia's Debtors' Corps (1336), he wrote **The Mirror for Sovereigns** in its grey coat about 1340: statecraft stripped of every sacred pretension, dedicated to Lauro Medori, who held his paper, as an offer to pay the rest of the debt in counsel. Freed when the city tore up Medori paper in 1346, he died the next winter. Banned by both churches, condemned by the Covenant, and found in the private shelves of every ruler on the board. Selections survive in [[The Mirror for Sovereigns — Selected Maxims]].

**Ottavia Sele** (fl. 1328 AR) — Castaran salt-hauler pilot whose *Naris's Hand* crossed the Boiling Sea on a Breath day, bringing back the Viscaran chart-case that opened the route and Castara's rush of the 1330s.

**Duke Gaius Medori** — Present ruler of Fiorenzia and head of the Medori family, governing beside a senate whose old houses resent the purchased ancestry that made his title possible.

**Doge Terenzo Vescari** — Present compromise ruler of Sermora, balancing Admiralty houses, guilds, privateer captains, and the secrets accumulated by the Veiled Office.

**Duke Marcus Soran and General Aurelia Soran** — Present princely and military heads of Aversia. Their mercenary dynasty seized an office older Shield Houses still consider stolen.

**Pass-Keeper Salvi Corren** — Keeper of Vorthara's roads, gates, shared water stations, and external Concord seal. He is not ruler of the anarchic city.

**First Speaker Eliana Corvesi** — Head of Castara's Merchant Princes and advocate of continued Boiling Sea expansion despite labor unrest and contested foreign claims.

**Harbor Rector Sabina Valeris** — Present rector of Orsena, responsible for neutrality, cistern security, prisoner exchange, and the Harbor Book's exclusions.

**First Burgess Tomas Bellafor** — Presiding officer of Vardessa's guild benches and custodian of the neutral muster hall.

## The Steppe

**Qarayel, the Black Wind** (d. 1303 AR) — Herd-master of a minor Grass-Tarkhan clan, called by the name of his black stallion; united the banners in 1261 by stud-right under the Nine-Mane Standard; extracted escort fees, fair-ransoms and the grass-toll 1265–1300, and in 1289 gave Meshalem's return his word for a flat price per wagon; died undefeated; his old stallion, a grandson of the black colt, carried his body out of the camp unled, the grace-mares followed, and the herds came home on the eleventh day without him; grave unknown by policy. His herds were partitioned by grace-line into the four Hordes in 1304. [[Qarayel and the Black Wind]]

**Gavrin the Farrier, the Grey Uncle** (c. 1215–1281 AR) — Velian bound craftsman taken from a plague-emptied Covenant well-fort; his blessing on a dying black colt (1249) renewed the horse-grace that made the Black Wind; refused freedom unless the camp's slaves were freed; murdered in 1281 (disputed). Saint Gavrin of the Halter in Thalmyria; the Wind-Saddler to Velian congregations.

## The Jirahar Thread

**The last River-King of Arqesh** (d. c. 676 BR) — Kassur III in the river records, remembered under incompatible names in Jirahar and Atherian ones. His empire had destroyed First Qiryath in 742 BR; his succession war and failed flood regime opened the return that produced Second Qiryath. He surrendered to Arezan in 688 and sealed the canal books to him.

**Rab Yequtiel of Zanthir, "the Witness at the Bed"** (c. 748–831 AR) — Jirahar physician who for fifty years witnessed the deathbeds of bound labourers in the bond-hospitals and salt pans of Zanthir's dry margin, where Covenant law required a witness before a name could be released. His *What the Register Keeps* argued from those beds that a register holds the name and lets the person go. Custodians quoted it to quiet the dying, Keepers of the Path denounced it as a licence for bondage, Velian copyists carried it into the belt's churches, and after the foundry's name-cages nobody in occupied Caleran could trust it.

**Meshalem the Twice-Turned** (c. 1260–1303 AR) — A road-clerk of Migdal Melach who proclaimed himself the Mesakha in 1288 because a Black Wind ride had passed his wagons by; half the diaspora's tower towns sold their goods and took the return road under Qarayel's word, and Marzuq I's courts declared their charters forfeit behind them. At the Empty City in 1291 the Keepers asked him to lay his hand on the silent gate; twice he went down the stair before the whole return and twice came back without touching it. The return broke up with no homes to go to, and he swept the outer court as a mourner until his death. The catastrophe — worse than any pogrom, because self-inflicted — explains the present-day Keepers' ferocious gatekeeping of prophecy and the resulting political doctrine: after Meshalem, every claimant is presumed false and none may approach the gate before examination, which is exactly the environment in which a true one would be missed. That irony remains central to Jirahar claimant stories.

**High Keeper Zerahiah** (present day) — First in dignity among independent Jirahar courts at Migdal Qesheth. He coordinates claimant scrutiny and gate cooperation without ruling the other towers. His greatest fear is another Meshalem; his greatest possible failure is refusing the true claimant for exactly that reason.
