# Named Figures of the Known World

The people behind the [[Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)]]. Each entry records a figure's period, motivation, legacy, and the wound or story hook left behind. Historical design analogues are kept separately in [[Real-History Concordance]].

**Map ruling:** Eclessia sits east of Thalmyria in the mirrored map logic; older western-province phrasing should be read as superseded unless it refers to a local western subregion inside Eclessia.

## Atherian foundations and the republic

[[Avren and Caleth]] (c. 1150–1090 BR) led the estuary coalition associated with Caleran's foundation. Avren killed Caleth during a dispute over command and provisions. Their mother Meral, Caleth's wife Sareth and the surviving children preserved relationships and memories which the reigning household could not control. [[Caleran Foundations - Authorial History]] records Ravessa's actual fosterage and its limited purpose.

[[Ilaron and Tarsenna]] (c. 1000–950 BR) joined maritime resources and Serathi religious learning in a later royal generation. Their Common Sky settlement established shared public ceremonies while preserving rival interests and practices. Both were responsible for the captive sacrifice at a campaign celebration. Their daughter Ilessa maintained festival resources through subsequent royal disputes.

[[Caleran - Foundation and Royal Households]] also places Esvan's waterfront works and Senavar's coercive supply policy in the generations between foundation and Compact. [[Arsuvan - The Giant Kingdom of the Aurin|Arsuvan]] introduces the earlier Giant rulers Veskar, Tarvessa and Arveth, and Lethra's human boat rescue during the kingdom's collapse.

[[Arkenan and Saronikan Cities before the Empire]] introduces Kassilon's loss of command after Erenost's grain seizure, Doreva's grain ventures at Pyrassa and Mesthra's observational teaching at Irenai. They belong to independent eastern histories, without a shared royal genealogy or later supernatural office.

[[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] — The magistrate **Eshren Damar** led the Ilyessa veterans to the Noon Sighting at Sarven (488 BR) and was killed in a bread crowd in 487. **Vespen Harod** accepted the Red Field against the rebel captains of the war of the closed chains in 466, brought troops into Caleran in 448 and died in office around 441. The senator **Arest Corven** followed Veyr into sanctuary at Ilamar and wrote there the history later used against Casvilar's heirs.

## Ancient Qahur and the Gateway country

[[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]] — **Neshara Senareth** began the First Long Crown around 1880 BR; **Talmera of Isethar**, a widowed boat-owner, carried grain for both rival courts during the famine of about 1468 BR, and each later condemned her dealings with the other; and **Khevar II** reunited the basin in 1412 through war, marriage and the execution of rival claimants. **Talzara Yasharet** founded the mixed Delta dynasty at Qemaret around 1248. **Aramen of Sakhret** destroyed that crown in 1076 and established the great southern restoration. **Ithren III** imposed the exclusive Single Radiance cult; **Queen Zaheret** restored the older temples after his death while inheriting the victims, confiscations and damaged households of his purges.

[[The White Measure and the Second Houses]] — **Meresha of Isethar** (d. c. 1439 BR) developed the White Measure and became the first well-attested Second-House Patron. Her freed scribe **Hesenu** entered the Court of Unspent Lamps as a captive human soul. **Khaseret of Qemaret** (d. 1126) opened the House of the Returning Cargo, which protected some refugees and refused others according to inherited debt. **Tamekha Rasen** (d. 916) built the Copper Orchard through medicine, royal contracts and experiments upon prisoners; demands from her dead estate helped provoke the Tenaret stoppage of 903. **Nemeret the Pure** failed to preserve his soul. A dependent Greed Mask later learned to speak through the estate-office built around his memory.

[[The Gateway Wars]] — Harbour Judge **Qelmar Suthar** lost Tammur to the Atherian commander **Aster Vey** in 649 BR. **Zamaret Ordan**, hereditary captain of Oresh, invaded Caleran's allied country with gifts of grain (c. 612–601). The magistrate **Dema Varin** presided over the Lustration of the Field in 609, and **Celor Marath**, first Warden of the Red Field, held the approaches below Thesra in 606. In the Rope Camp stoppage (c. 536–531) **Damen** was seized carrying the council's terms and **Yasra of Belqara** spoke for the stripped districts; **Meleq Ordan** broke the camp with **King Harzun of Hadrath** and married Harzun's daughter **Sapha**.

[[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth]] — **Ilya of Qarzeth** carried evidence of the child prisons to Esharat. The midwife **Sapha of Esharat**, unrelated to the Hadrathi princess, organised a rescue route; collectors killed her husband and sacrificed their daughter **Neral**. The elderly senator **Orsen Valeor** demanded Qarzeth's destruction, while **Mera Castren** proposed enforced closure and inspection before the final policy was chosen. Sapha of Esharat set the rules of the survivors' meal at Sarqen's landing, which became the spared towns' Table Oath.

[[Qahur and the Gateway before Atheria]] — **Prince Tazirun Zaheret**, a younger Qahuri son, married **Lemara of Esharat** around 548 BR. Their daughter **Hesara** helped conceal children during the siege of Qarzeth and became Hadrima's mother. This maternal relationship brought Qahuri kin and ceremony into the later Nerethan household without conveying rule over the Sacred Basin.

## Qiryath and the eastern empires

[[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/70 History and Conflicts/Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria.md]] — **Mareh bat Sivan**, the Mother of Measures, preserved three road camps during famine and fever around 1800 BR. **Tovan bar Rameq**, **Tirzah bat Oren**, **Hador bar Mavin**, **Othan bar Raqel** and **Barakhel of the Reed Cloak** belong to separate protector cycles. Their victories include recovered captives and saved harvests; their histories also preserve slaughtered prisoners, dynastic violence, a lethal vow and the destruction of people who never chose the heroes' wars.

**Amariah bat Qoreb** joined rival pilgrimage calendars around 1020 BR. **Yedran bar Aqev** rose from sanctuary service and outlawry to become Guardian of Qiryath, then kept relief grain in Qiryath to feast his eldest son's marriage to a river house while **Uriad bar Sem**'s post starved. Uriad's widow **Rava bat Horel** later accused him publicly at a pilgrimage feast; her lament ends with the once-hungry man eating from a golden bowl. Their descendants' struggle broke the protector dynasty.

**Elior bar Hadan**, chief guardian in 746 BR, refused Neshkar II's image and hostages; **Maraiah bat Qesem** argued for hostages to save the city and gained hearers when the sign was withheld. The widow **Yaelith bat Coren**, keeper of Second Qiryath's surviving cistern, married the exile official **Neram bar Elior** and refused to expel the settler-spouses and children who worked her water; the offering carried by her households c. 654 BR ended the Withholding.

[[01 Canon Reference/History/30 Exodus and First Civilizations/Arqesh - River Cities, Kings and Deportation.md]] — **Saruq of Teluq** is the disputed river founder later claimed by House Ubar. **Merod I Ubar** seized Arqesh around 958 BR and established the first durable canal monarchy. **Kassur I Ubar** developed its provincial and deportation system. **Merod II Ubar** sent his son **Ashkur** to restore Radiance exiles to their temple fields; Ashkur's murder in 814 let him intensify an existing intervention in Qahur. **Neshkar II Ubar** destroyed First Qiryath in 742. His sister **Iltara Ubar** governed Khalbar and supervised deportation and resettlement. **Ishara of the Soot Court**, a smith, led the forced workers who opened Khalbar's gate to the coalition before Arqesh fell in 690. **Kassur III**, the last River-King, was murdered during flight in 688 and received a royal burial from the conqueror who had defeated him. Eryndor kept his portable images as regalia.

[[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]] — **Arezan I Tiravan** united Vashari highland houses against Arqesh and founded the Crown of Roads and Dawns. His sister **Mithara Tiravan** organised food, marriages and communication among the coalition. Her daughter **Parena Tiravan** married Eryndor III and acted between the two courts before she and her child were murdered in the succession. Arezan's heirs **Daryav I**, **Kavran I**, **Mithara I**, **Xarun I**, **Orvaz I**, **Tiraz III** and **Orvaz II** developed the road commands and governed Vashar to the threshold of Atherian empire. **Mazhara** of Daryam refused to call the Witness of Dawn a sign and was made keeper of the royal flame, which edited her carved sayings; **Rathava** condemned Orvaz I's perjury and recanted in the garden to save his labourers. Orvaz I was struck down at the harvest acclamation of 431 BR and died days later.

[[Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest]] — **Valeron II** imposed Ortheian hegemony upon several Compact cities and created the army inherited by his son. **Eryndor III**, the Far-Road King, destroyed Olyssar, crossed the eastern sea, joined Arezan in the capture of Arqesh and died there in 684 during a White Measure rite he had consented to. His courage, curiosity and skill accompanied massacre, enslavement and the killing of two intimates, an officer and a companion who refused to bow before Kassur III's images. The separate wonders of his campaign became [[The Five Limits of Eryndor]].

[[Wars of the Inheritance]] — **Demeran Kavos** took Ortheia and the western army and ordered the deaths of Parena and her child after the speaking tomb at Delkos made the heirs worthless. **Serevan of Delkos** paid for Eryndor's deathbed rite, diverted his body and Kassur's images to Delkos and governed in the name of the voice in the tomb; his officer houses included the ancestors of Nerethan. **Neresh of Qemaret** brought the White Measure masters to Eryndor's deathbed, held selected Qahuri crown towns through garrison and marriage, and c. 657 had the masters testify that their rite had failed. Their wars destroyed the nominal succession and divided Eryndor's conquests.

[[The Eastern Sea Wars]] — **Liora of Tasar** led the harbour revolt of 514 BR after a Vasharan governor attempted to impose a magistrate and enlarge naval service. **Xarun I Tiravan** retook the city, executed its leaders and sold or deported inhabitants before launching the great western invasion of 497. Liora's fate remains disputed.

## Vathra and the northern ground

[[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]] — **Ozhur IV of Urzavad** tried to keep Urzavad aloft through requisitions of food, ballast, workers and children. His cousin **Varakesh of Dazhur** began the War of Altitude and discarded dependants while claiming to reform the kingdom. The Dynastic Giant **Kelvara** first served Ozhur, then used her knowledge of the descent grounds to help human communities ground Kel-Orun. Her assistance saved thousands and did not undo her earlier participation in captive transfers.

**Seyra Vatan** organised tether crews; **Dorun of Tal-Kesh**, whom the mine and cistern crews he brought into the rising called Dorun of the Draw-Gangs, changed Urzavad's route notices; **Meya of Narash** brought camel houses and western wells into the Great Unyoking. Meya had previously supplied Vathra with captives taken in local wars. In the first winters after the war she sealed the Hundred Wells by a bargain at the dead anchor field; every Narashi crown-mother since has walked the round of the wells at accession, and succession stayed maternal. Their constituencies cooperated without becoming one permanent company or state.

[[The Tarkhan before the Horse Hordes]] — **Boran Tall-Hand** built a chariot house at Daram Ford and seized children from households that refused animals for his teams; when his salvage-copper convoy stuck in the mud and he ordered the grain dumped to save the copper, his own porters killed him (the Feast of the Copper Wheels). The foster cousins **Savan Uruz** and **Demer Khol** founded rival foreign-backed client courts; Savan's soldiers killed him when Arqeshite payment failed, and former captives defeated Demer. **Yalun of the White Camel** and **Marat Two-Bows** joined the coalition against Arqesh for different purposes and treated captives differently after victory.

[[The Ahdari before the Covenant]] — **Harun Savel** violated the disarmed market at Saqara around 1280 BR and caused the trade which supported it to move to Mazar. At Rethmar in 381 BR, the water keeper **Tavisha** removed stores from Ostar's expected route while **Kharuz of the Stone Wells** commanded camel scouts and foot archers. Their coalition dispersed after defeating the Atherian army.

## The imperial-founding generation

[[Teren Casvilar]] (425–370 BR) conquered southern districts, accepted the Red Field in 376 and governed through personal supremacy; challengers entered in the muster ground's arms register, kept by his own quartermaster, killed him on the muster ground in 370. His assassination began the succession wars. [[Marovan Veyr]] (435–371), his former partner and principal rival, refused a table laid with an empty seat at Sarqen in 373 and died of fever in sanctuary at Ilamar; his son Ardel inherited his following. [[Lethren Ostar]] (442–381) financed the partnership, sold the defeated rebels of 399 north in lots to the well trade and died during the failed northern expedition at Rethmar.

[[Aurelius Thalion]] (390–318 BR) was Casvilar's nephew and designated heir. Around 350 he stood on the muster ground with no named challenger left alive, the augurs declared the Red Field closed into his command, and his supremacy began imperial rule. [[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]] follows his government to 318, the death of his heir Caelor after an untested treasury hall fell on him in 326, and the adoption of Meren. [[Darion Velsar]] (408–350) supplied much of the coalition's eastern military strength before becoming Thalion's rival. [[Seluc Varroth]] (born 414) ruled the Red Field open and unclaimed in 367 and lost his military command in 355 while retaining the office of First Celebrant of the Common Sky. [[Istran Melvo]] (born 391) organised the fleets which defeated the sea-heir and the royal coalition.

[[Samyra Nerethan]] (392–350) ruled from Sarqen. [[The House of Nerethan]] fixes the incoming commander Nerethan, Hadrima of Esharat, Arenes, Tavren and Olymes as the preceding royal generations. Samyra's sister Merath was executed in 358, her son Amesar in 350; Talven and Lethia survived in Evara Thalion's custody. She swore the spared towns' Table Oath at accession; the towns restored her in 373 after Vardek laid an empty seat for Veyr.

[[Households of the Imperial Founding]] records Lysara's support for her son, Calvena's marriage to Veyr, her public refusal of divorce in 377 and her mediation of the 357 agreement with Ardel, Miretha Ardel's resistance at Darsil, and Evara's and Ilvara's distinct household work. The same account preserves the roles of the assassins Deceran Tolm, Mavren Seneth and Corial Draven, the betrayed speaker Belisar Venn and the southern organiser Velcara. Deceran Tolm kept the arms register in which the assassins entered their names. Drenoc and Morveth belong to independent Folk coalitions; Kharuz and Tavisha to the northern well resistance. None is a member of an existing signature company.

## The Age of Glory

[[The House of Thalion]] — **Meren I** (r. 318–286 BR), son of Evara and Velsar and adopted heir of Thalion. **Valeran I** (r. 286–263), Meren's adopted son and the husband of Cassara, expanded the roads, stores and waterworks and kept the wardens' test after the quarry strike of 274. **Olarion** (r. 263–247), their son, left the court divided by purges sworn before the accession portrait and died suddenly, precipitating the [[The Year of Four Eagles|Four Eagles crisis]].

**Avaren Mott** (d. 247 BR), senior magistrate at Caleran, governed through the speaking accession portrait during the Four Eagles. Its assurance that no imperial granary was empty sent soldiers behind it into the poor districts. He was killed at the threshold of the audience hall.

[[The House of Veloran]] — **Samor I** (r. 246–229 BR) won the succession war, covered the speaking portrait with a soldier's cloak and kept it veiled by law. **Aren I** (r. 229–203), his adopted son and Tessa's husband, built municipal works and returned the Crown of Olveth. **Orent I** (r. 203–174) reigned through the Sleepwalkers' Summer and took the Arcelon corridor. **Orent II** (r. 174–151) received Raveth into the empire and died in the fever summer of 151 without a named heir.

[[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]] — **Elian I Serrat** (r. 151–126 BR) won a negotiated succession and paid for it with citizenship sworn before the emperor's image. **Elian II** (r. 126–101) authorised famine relief and the resulting crown estates, which passed to his adopted heir **Cassen I** (r. 101–76), in whose last years Atheria took Qiryath. **Velen I** (r. 76–61) unveiled the Face at his spectacles and fell to his guards. **Doran I** (r. 61–42) took power with stores held by the Low Door Companions, re-veiled the Face and had his officers enter by the low door; his son-in-law **Othran I** (r. 42–24) succeeded him. **Othran II** (r. from 24 BR) was emperor at the Redeemer boundary.

**Neriah bar Sorek** (fl. 119–113 BR) — the [[The Keeper of Borrowed Grain|Jirahar administrator of the Raveth hunger]]. His grain system saved towns, transferred defaulting farms to the imperial household and made their former owners dependent labourers. He resigned rather than administer the oath before the emperor's image that estate officers substituted for the neighbours' countersignature.

[[The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door - Imperial History|The Low Door Companions]] (fl. c. 96–54 BR) — Sena Varris, Oren Tal, Brana Keth, Thessa Qor, Miro Fen and Helon Var rose from service work after saving a magistrate. Their later advancement involved hostages, an unlawful raid and betrayal of a family refuge. Helon was executed after freeing condemned hostage children. In 61 BR Miro and Brana supplied Doran's march, and his officers adopted the companions' low door.

## The Age of the Redeemer


**Lord-Scribe Ezrahel bar Qoreth** (fl. 0 AR), keeper of the sanctuary copy of the Qiryath charter his grandfather had drafted, led the senior elders who referred Isfir to the Atherian governor at Second Qiryath. He feared imperial retaliation against the sanctuary and the other towers. He sought a capital sentence over the objections of an elder who challenged the testimony. Jirahar and Redeemer accounts preserve different judgements of his decision. [[Jirahar Memory of Isfir]]

**High Augur Severan of Astaroth** (fl. 0 AR) accompanied the provincial governor to Second Qiryath and urged a public punishment. The governor passed the sentence. Severan's last recorded augural reading, three days after the execution, says the stars did not answer; every other college's book is blank for the same three days. His college recorded that he "laid down the staff". Sestaran humanists pay fortunes for alleged pages of the Severan Fragments.

**The Companions** — thirteen called, one struck (full roll, deaths, and patronages in [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]]). **Ischar the Silvered** — sold him and was struck; the seat is kept empty at every commemorative table, and his refused silver ("Ischar's coin," money no one will admit to owning) circulates still: thirty coins that always come back. The two primacies descend from this table: Eclessia claims the **Commission of the Pen** through **Saint Jareth of the Bound Eye**; Veridun claims the **Ember Succession** through **Amaryah's hearth** at Veridun Minor — Solana carried the ember, Amaryah kept it, Calira died for it.

**Amaryah the Fire-Bearer** (d. c. 55 AR) — the faith's great theological dispute (see [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]): Fire-Bearer or Man-Bearer (the Ephesus fight, settled at Halmenth 411 AR — whose losers survive as the [[The Velian Church|Velian Church]] under Covenant contract); Dormition (Veridun) against defined Unkindled Conception and bodily Rising (Sanctum); honored-not-invoked (Freeflame); and a pending apparition case at Vessing (1352 AR) the Sanctum dares not rule on.

**Saint Aquilan the Turned** (d. c. 45 AR) — of Jirahar blood and training. Born **Sha'il bar Qoreth**, kinsman of the Lord-Scribe who referred Isfir to the governor, Keeper-trained, an Atherian citizen by his house's veiled oath under Elian I's grant; as "Aquilan the Hound" a prosecutor of the early assemblies before the Great Purge, struck blind on the Virelos road and baptised by the congregation he came to arrest. Apostle to the nations; his **Epistles** ("fulfilled, not abolished") are the hinge of supersession and thus of centuries of pogroms he never saw. The Freeflame quote his seatless commission against both hierarchies; the Jirahar keep the exact and unforgiving formula: *"The carpenter we mourn. The scribe we cannot forgive."*

[[Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian - Emperors Wars and Revolts|Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian]] follows these reigns and the provincial wars.

**Emperor Tervan Marcellan** (r. 241–248 AR) rose through the provincial commands. After mutinies and failures to supply the garrisons, he issued the **Edict of Public Sacrifice** in 245, in answer to the Kept Lamps, a grave-lamp cult that had fed mutinies in the unpaid eastern garrisons; the edict also licensed every household's graves and lamps. Local commissioners witnessed offerings to the Stars and certified compliance. The order widened persecution from cases against individual congregations to a test imposed across directly governed provinces. His successor suspended it in 248. [[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization]]

**Emperor Cassian the Baptised** (acclaimed 288; r. 292–316 AR) saw the White Flame at the **Stone Bridge of Velos** in 292, won the civil war and legalised the Redeemer's faith. He kept the augur colleges because they entered the unfreezing reach as his sign, and c. 296 traded a crown warden at the bridge for empire-wide restitution (the Velos Concord). He convened the four sees at Halmenth, granted the Celestial Forum with its Pillar and Nails to Caleran's church and funded basilicas. c. 309 he executed his eldest son and the High Augur of Astaroth over a sealed succession answer. He was baptised, dying, in the unfrozen reach in 316, which gave rise to the Cassian Wager. The vision was real. The content of the sealed answer and the outcome of the Wager remain unresolved. [[Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR|Cassian and the Four Sees]]

**Brask** (fl. 290–291 AR), the Ashforges' enslaved script-keeper, was freed by Cassian's officers so that his oath to the cold furnace and the flame on the Forty would stand: the first sign sworn before crown officers outside the augurs' power. [[The Forty Flames]]

**Emperor Sevrin** (r. 316–348 AR), Cassian's younger son, commanded the southern reserve and gained recognition by guaranteeing the eastern command's pay and revenue. He confirmed restitution and renewed market treaties with southern Folk communities. He refused to transfer the wardenship at Velos and never opened the sealed succession answer. His intended heir died c. 343. **Adran**, Cassian's nephew, accepted command in the east under Sevrin. Adran's son Astrion succeeded Sevrin. [[The Reign of Sevrin]]

**Astrion the Apostate** (r. 348–351 AR), Adran's son and Cassian's great-nephew, was raised by Flame tutors and studied the old Stars through the eastern archives. He commanded the northern desert approaches before his accession. He restored privileges to the Star cults, had the augurs re-enter the Velos reach as Hecatara's answer, and in 350 obtained the Red Star's single answer to his public test of legitimacy. He died at thirty-four on a punitive campaign against northern desert communities, struck by a spear no witness saw thrown. The field record gives no last words, only his question whether the baggage with the register had crossed. The Church later quoted *"You have won, Carpenter,"* as surrender; Sestaran humanists remember the words as tragedy. [[The Reign of Sevrin]] · [[The Conversion of the Empire]]

**Savel Orren** (r. 351–352 AR) was acclaimed during Astrion's retreat, restored church stipends and abdicated at the depot line when Caleran declared for Corlan. He died on his estate.

**Tellan the Water-Bringer** (fl. 351–c. 390 AR), Adran's younger son and Astrion's brother, saved the retreating army with water trains under the bronze standard of the 350 test, the Answered Standard, and held the eastern command under the Accord of the Two Standards (c. 357). He never claimed the throne. He lost the War of the Two Standards (383–384) and died a pensioned captive above the Aurin.

**Corlan Aster** (r. 352–c. 366 AR) held Caleran and the west; **Lucar Aster** (r. 352–377 AR) held the south and north and ruled alone from c. 366. Lucar's officers sold admitted Folk dependants and his chaplains seized their ford-tokens. His army crossed the Kellovar ford unpaid in 377 and broke, and he died there. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]]

**Emperor Vestian the Pious** (r. 377–398 AR) held the inner depots after Lucar's defeat and gave the Velos register to the bishops for Caleran's recognition. He dissolved the augur colleges (383–384), won the War of the Two Standards and made the faith compulsory through the Profession of 385. When the Profession's lamps failed to kindle, his court blamed hidden rites and the Jirahar, and under him the persecution of the Jirahar turned from riot to imperial policy. He wrote *before the Mesakha* into Qiryath's Empty Charter (c. 396) and died in 398, leaving the throne to a son still in the schoolroom.

**Ilios Marrant** (fl. 383 AR), last High Augur of Caleran, made the colleges' final public petition to the Red Star at the Dissolution. Nothing answered.

**Bishop Nasriel of Qabir** (c. 362–after 411 AR), a reader from a baptised family of Jirahar descent, kept Qabir's count of the unkindled professions (385–388) and carried copies to Raveth. He was elected bishop c. 402 over Caleran's objection and condemned at Halmenth in 411; his communion survives as the [[The Velian Church|Velian Church]].

## The Atherian emperors after Vestian

**The Atherian emperors after Vestian** (398–905 AR) belonged to four houses, each raised by a different way of paying the army. [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

- **The Vestianic house** (398–447). Vestian's son reigned through the court bishops and paymasters who had made his father, selling relic estates freedom from tax and audit. His only son and heir drowned at the Aurin mouth in 446.
- **The Saldane house** (452–587). Founded by the commander of the southern forts, acclaimed by the great sees in the war of 447–452 and paid in immunities. **Teomar Saldane** (r. 509–531) fought the Ubar Toll War (514–523). The emperor of the Thin Floods, remembered in the north as the Granary Thief, kept Caleran's dole by requisitioning northern stores. The house was deposed in 587 after the loss of the north.
- **The Leovarid house** (587–709). **Leovar I** (r. 587–624), a frontier general who travelled with a hermit-bishop, led the crusade of seals and signed the peace of 612. **Leovar II** (r. 681–709), whose accession lamp was the Copper Lamp, died with his heir in the Fever Week of 709.
- **The Corvenid house** (713–905). **Corvenas I** (r. 713–746), commander of the march facing the Velian belt and husband of Leovar II's daughter, won the War of the Two Oaths by riding the marches to renew their oaths in person. **Corvenas II** (r. 861–884) launched the Second Crusade and was forced to abdicate.

**Corvenas III** (r. 884–905 AR) — The last Atherian emperor. Sincerely devout, he could reach every remedy only through a public test: riding to the marches to kindle a summons, striking the lost north off the registers, or submitting his relics to Aurelium. He chose rites inside Caleran and turned the palace into the Ash Hall. Covenant files held that his accession rite was finished with warlock fire. His death is told three ways. [[Prelude to Collapse]]

**Simeon of Aurelium** (bishop c. 694–731 AR) — In 711 examined the Velos Standard, sent east by the Caleran claimant, recorded it silent and held the eastern governors' oaths "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful". Corvenas I's confirmation of Aurelium's right of examination in 713 is "Simeon's primacy".

**Sarion the Pale** (d. 903 or 904 AR) — A march monk stoned in Caleran during Corvenas III's Jubilee for preaching *"The inked ones are coming. They walk with chains and offers of peace. The crown will burn in silence."* His words were cut on a memorial wall after 957.

## The Covenant

**Mujalib, the Final Prophet** (c. 510–572 AR) — Received the contract cosmology from Malak al-Ahd in 541; unified the desert tribes; founded Al-Qahur. Authorial truth: his revelation was the Creditor's masterpiece, while Mujalib himself was wholly sincere.

**The Four Rightly-Sealed Caliphs** (572–601 AR) — Mujalib's first successors, each confirmed by a sealed succession contract said to bear the Prophet's own witness-mark. The fourth seal is disputed to this day — the Covenant's permanent succession fault line, detailed in [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]].

**Qatada Sayf-al-Ahd, "Sword of the Covenant"** (d. 604 AR) — The Wave One general who never lost a field battle; conqueror of the northern provinces, behind armies that marched with breach-courts. Dismissed in 598 at the height of his fame by Caliph Uthar, whose own seal was disputed and who feared the army loved the sword more than the seal — and who obeyed the dismissal without a word, which frightened the caliph more.

**The Banu Mizan, "House of the Scales"** (c. 610–885 AR) — The dynasty of the Velian golden age: translation houses, water-courts, the great libraries where Atherian learning survived in the conqueror's custody. They brokered the peace of 612. Their courts hosted Jirahar philosophers, and their surviving compacts later sheltered Thalmyric exile monks. Their rule in the Velian belt ended in 885, when Qahir's officers took the client courts from a regency; a Banu Mizan marriage line kept Zanthir at Qabir as tributary emirs, and their compacts outlived the dynasty. Every present-day power is embarrassed by how much it owes them.

**Qahir ibn Ruzbeh** (c. 855–902 AR) — Founder of the Qahirid dynasty, of a Ruzhan horse-lord house that sealed with the Covenant for bread when Vashar fell (555–560). Made his name defending the Velian belt against the Second Crusade (874–878); obtained the rulings that the crusade had broken the peace of 612 (884) and that the Clause of the Given binds parties, not powers (885–886); rebuilt the war machine around mass Ahdanite levies, capture companies and siege-golem foundries.

**Sultan-Caliph Idris al-Fattah, "the Unsealer of Gates"** (r. 902–931 AR), Qahir's successor, took Caleran in 905 at twenty-six. He sealed the martyrs' precinct in the Celestial Forum under glass as “evidence held for the final audit” and impounded the Eternal Ember. His guards preserved the Ember and the Pillar of Concordance until the Thalmyric recovery. Like his father he bound army and conquered cities to himself by name, with his eldest son Ruzbeh written in as successor-holder. Ruzbeh drowned in 929 and Idris died of fever in 931 before anyone re-swore, widowing every instrument at once.

**Sultan-Caliph Mahdar ibn Idris** (r. 931–979 AR) — Eight at accession; the Regency of the Bench ruled for him to 940 and made the throne the Sublime Threshold. He refused to arm Durayd's south, lost Caleran in 957 while his army was held on the northern wells by the Long Reach, and armed none of the amirates that followed until his death; his son spent the Horse War buying horses and building walls in the north.

**Gulnar** (fl. 931 AR) — Vashari-born engine feeder at Iqashar who stopped the walking Depth-Core engine, later the Elder, in the dye-yards by giving its feeding name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir": the formula behind the Sublime Threshold. Foundry masters said the beast drank itself quiet; the dyers' quarter credits her.

**Durayd ibn Sahl, Amir of the Southern Threshold** (fl. 931–957 AR) — Amir of occupied Caleran who swore the south to himself when Idris's instruments widowed, offered to sell the Ember back in 948, and surrendered Caleran in his own name in 957. Iqashari chroniclers say he surrendered what he had stolen; tradition divides between his death in the forum and old age in Qabir.

**The first Unnamed Custodian** (fl. 1003–1011 AR) — Custodian of the Vault of Names who pledged his true name to an Iqashari lending house to ransom a son taken by Tarkhan riders, leaving the army unable to march from 1006, then freed the Vault in 1011 by surrendering his name into its deepest custody. Registrars give him three former names. Every Custodian since has surrendered his name at investiture.

**Sultan-Caliph Tamim the Auditor** (r. 1029–1068 AR) — Wrote off the southern amirates in the Audit of the South (1033), divided the Vault of Names into the Living Register and the Deep Vault (1059), and chartered Mazrakan (1067).

**Sultan-Caliph Khurzad** (r. 1112–1142 AR) — Enforced the Court of Breaches' 1126 ruling for the Southern Houses with mercy towers and lost the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139) to Caedros III; his heir died of dysentery in the siege camp in 1135.

**Sultan-Caliph Suhrab the Debtor** (r. 1190–1243 AR) — A younger son who had taken the jurist's oath renouncing the throne; the Court of Breaches voided it as coerced after the heir died at the Mazrakan fair in 1189. The apex. Codified the **Ledger of Ledgers**, harmonising every provincial contract code; won the **Battle of Qarath's Well** (1214) and took an emperor captive. Late in his reign he lodged his most gifted son's name in the Deep Vault on the Guard's evidence that the prince had taken soldiers' oaths in his own name; the prince died in the Vault's cloister within the year, and foreign chroniclers recorded an execution. His last book (1243) named the Guard's officer bench a competent witness for the Threshold, and every accession since has been made before it. The dynasty has been living off his capital ever since.

**Sultan-Caliph Tahmur** (r. 1243–1268 AR) — Suhrab's surviving son, the first sovereign to accede before the Guard's officer bench; put down the Pageless Prince (1249–1252) and began paying the Black Wind in 1265.

**Prince Qaydar, the Pageless** (d. 1252 AR) — A lodged Qahirid prince whose register page burned in the plague; freed by the Court of Breaches' "no page, no lodging", he raised the Basin and was killed. "The Vault can lose a page; the grave cannot": after him the Cull of Brothers turned from the lodge to the knife and the blinding needle.

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq I** (r. 1268–1301 AR) — Paid Qarayel's "escort fees" until the horses' grace thinned in 1300; ruled when Meshalem converted (1291).

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq II** (r. 1301–1349 AR) — Hired the Iron Horde after the Partition; his night raids brought on Orinel, the Crimson Pilgrimage and the loss of Zehrim. His death opened the Cull that made Marzuq III.

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III** (r. 1349 AR-present) — Reigns from Iqashar as Qahirid suzerain over a layered imperial order. Al-Qahur supplies sacred legitimacy, independent Qazmarid rejects his universal claim, and autonomous Zanthir pays contested tribute. Clever and indolent, he depends on Ahdanite officer-brokers and the Vault of Names. The Guard bench witnessed his accession in 1349 while the Cull killed or blinded his brothers; it costs him sleep. See [[Present-Day Courts and Crowns (1360 AR)]].

**Caliph Uwais, Custodian-of-the-Wells** — Sacred ruler of Al-Qahur whose acknowledgment legitimises the Qahirid throne. Politically constrained by Sword-Regent Nadir and the military households.

**Sword-Regent Nadir ibn Haraz** — Effective governor of Al-Qahur, balancing Ahdanite-descended military houses, irrigation councils, pilgrimage, and dependence on Uwais's sacred authority.

**Caliph-Wasi Zahran** — Sealed-Blood ruler of independent Qazmarid, claimant through Ruqaiya bint Mujalib and asserted custodian of the Counter-Instrument.

**The Emirate of Zanthir** (601 AR–present) — First-wave conquest emirate turned last autonomous remnant: survivor of the Reconquest by timely tribute and timelier treachery (in 1044 it starved the last Covenant amirates of its fleet and grain and agreed with Veridun to pay tribute to each empire in alternate seasons), still standing as "the last pearl of the Banu Mizan." Under **Emir Badis al-Zanthiri** it pays tribute toward Iqashar and Veridun in alternating seasons, insures with the Medori, shelters Jirahar quarters, and is universally assumed to be the next war's first battlefield.

## Thalmyria

**Caedros I the Unbowed** (r. 922–957 AR) — founder-saint and rebel general. A commander of the mountain rebellion, crowned at Virelos in 922, then brought the rival refuge armies under one crown in the War of the Burning Banners (922–949). He died at the Pillar of Concordance in 957, after Amir Durayd surrendered Caleran in his own name. Thalmyric witnesses said the White Flame took his body, leaving armor and sword; Eclessian relic courts reject the canonisation for want of remains and independent examination. The event completed the sacred objective of his reign but not the [[The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)|Long Reconquest]].

**Caedros II the Road-Maker** (r. 957–979 AR) — secured Caleran, rebuilt its walls, protected surrendered quarters, and opened the roads required for the next generations of province-by-province return. He inherited a sacred victory and an unfinished war. He paid for both by restoring the Church's estates and filling its sees from the families who had paid for the return, and under him the war-lamps that had kindled before Caleran's walls kindled less and less often.

**Ilarion Aetherion** (r. 979–984 AR) — Caedros II's son. Died of the lung-fever that followed the eruption of Mount Askre; his death put a soldier on the throne.

**Leovar III the Lampbreaker** (r. 984–1011 AR) — Vlastravan march commander and Caedros II's son-in-law, acclaimed by the army after Ilarion's death, taking the regnal name of the old oath-holding emperors. As a young man he carried a war-lamp before Caleran and saw it kindle; in the decades after, he watched it go dark. In the **Ashfall Omen** (983–984) the Great Lamp of Veridun shrank to a bead for forty days, and he concluded that the Fire refused the vessels men had made and sold. The **First Breaking** (987) smashed icon-lamps, melted relic-braziers, broke the army's war-lamps and exiled the flame-monks; he hooded the Great Lamp rather than break it. Soldiers loved him; monks recorded him as a demon; the reconquered valleys he left without funeral monks remember the dead who were not buried.

**Ioan of Velia, "the Lamplit"** (c. 950–1024 AR) — The great defender of the vessels, writing safely from the Velian belt, where the old Banu Mizan compacts still sheltered monasteries under Covenant rule and the emperor's writ could not reach — a Thalmyric monk under Covenant protection, which everyone found embarrassing. His *Three Orations on the Housed Fire* argue the position that eventually won: the Flame is uncreated, but *consents* to dwell in what sanctity prepares — "the Redeemer had a body; the Fire may have a lamp."

**Empress-Regent Anthe** (regency 1011–1019 AR) — Leovar III's widow, regent for Leovar IV. Restored the lamps at the **Council of the Thousand Lamps** (1013) because she needed an army whose lamps kindled, monks to bury its dead and the monasteries' grain, after the Covenant's army was freed to move again in 1011. Eclessian apologetics backdated her regency to excuse Adalmar's coronation of 1002.

**Leovar IV** (r. 1011–1049 AR) — Raised by the empress who restored the lamps; impounded them all. After his Master of Lamps was burned to death forcing a wayhouse lamp before Meliton (1029), he sealed every lamp but the Great Lamp and the Ember in crown lamp-houses: **the Second Breaking**, or Impounding. Died of a fall on the cathedral ice.

**Empress Theodosia the Kindler** (r. 1049–1071 AR) — Leovar IV's daughter. Took the last southern amirate's surrender, opened the lamp-houses and instituted the **Feast of the Rekindling** (1052), still Thalmyria's greatest liturgical day: every lamp in the empire relit from one flame carried on foot from the Ember at Caleran. She walked the first mile.

**Caedros III, "the Wall of the World"** (r. 1118–1167 AR) — Raised in the frontier monasteries. Resettled the frontier the Two Winters had emptied as military districts owing a lance and a lamp; won the **War of the Southern Deeds** (1131–39) when the district lamps kindled in defence of their homes, his own among them at Kolonna (1134); refilled the treasury with the Covenant's ransom for its custodians. Never married. Believing the Fire watched the throne, he left his succession to it, and at his bier no claimant's lamp kindled.

**Despina of Kolonna** (fl. 1167 AR) — A district soldier's widow whose lamp kindled in the nave at Caedros III's funeral while seven claimants' lamps stayed dark. The court suppressed it; the district armies made it their warrant. She refused to return to court.

**Andrion, "the Widow's Emperor"** (r. 1169–1198 AR) — Commander of the military districts, acclaimed by their armies after the War of the Seven Lamps. Extended the district levy; his reign saw the Threshing Floor in Elenara.

**Markellos the Captive** (r. 1209–1214 AR) — Andrion's grandson, raised in the districts. Marched in a drought summer to stop his frontier families accepting the Covenant's Unburdened Deeds; his army drank from the sworn well at **Qarath** and sickened at dawn when it took up arms. He stayed with the men who drank and was taken, treated with elaborate contract-courtesy, and ransomed for the military districts. His uncle and regent Sergian had him blinded to "widow" the ransom; the Covenant's Court answered that an instrument sworn to the Throne cannot widow. He died of the wound-fever within the month. The shame lies not only in defeat but in an empire proving it feared humiliation more than injustice.

**Sergian the Regent** (d. 1217 AR) — Markellos's uncle. Blinded the returned emperor on a Covenant doctrine; killed by a Veridun crowd in the Crown Hall.

**Nikandros I the Restorer** (r. 1225–1263 AR) — Of a cadet Aetherion branch in Kedrava; commanded the Kedravan monastic contingents at Qarath, which kept the refuge rule against drinking sworn water and withdrew in order with the wounded. Won the post-Qarath civil wars with the monasteries and Vlastrava behind him and stabilised the rump. Swallowed two centuries of schism to ask Eclessia for soldiers (1227); the Sanctum's price was Veridun's submission, the Great Lamp carried east and tried by its relic court. He refused — *"a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned"* — fought on alone, and won back half the loss when Covenant garrisons marched home in the plague (1238–1246). Thalmyric liturgy still includes a line widely understood to mean *the East sold us for a doctrine.* Present-day Caedros IV claims the restoration line.

**Evandros the Sower** (r. 1263–1290 AR) — Nikandros I's son. Resettled the recovered districts and the plague-emptied interior with veterans and Kedravan families; sent the embassy that failed to buy Gavrin the Farrier.

**Stavren the Patient** (r. 1290–1329 AR) — Evandros's son. Kept the peace on Sestaran credit, founded the war college at Vatrin, lost both sons to the summer flux of 1327 and adopted Caedros, the hero of Zehrim, who reigns in 1360 as Caedros IV.

## Eclessia

**Hedwen of Kaltfurt** (d. c. 1010 AR) — Markish miller's widow who took up the march banner at the ford of Kaltfurt (974) and held it between Grass-Tarkhan riders and a refugee column; the banner kindled and the raid broke. In 1001 she testified at Aurelium that it had never burned in Adalmar's hand; her testimony is in the register, struck through. Refused to carry the banner into the Folk Wars. Venerated by millers and in Markish reform country.

**Adalmar the Great** (r. 975–1019 AR; crowned Emperor of the Atherians 1002) — Markish margrave's son, acclaimed king of the eastern marches after Kaltfurt. Joined the bishops' principalities by war, marriage and a single register. His **Folk Wars** (982–1008) granted frontier land held by Folk seer-oath to his Markish lords; at the **Drowning at Wolfsmere** (993) he had four thousand captive oath-keepers and clan-seers drowned in their own lake "to drown the oath with its keepers". The next winter the lake stood up and took his shore-fort, and his frontier stopped there. In 1001 the Sanctum's relic court credited the Kaltfurt banner's kindling to him; in 1002, amid Leovar III's First Breaking, **Lord Primarch Silvane II** crowned him in the Sanctum Divinitatis, declaring discharged the eastern oaths held "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful" since 711. The chronicle says he *"knelt surprised"*; no historian in or out of world believes it. Eclessia's greatest hero and its deepest sin, in one man. (Authorial note: Wolfsmere is a standing leftward thin space, "the Lake That Remembers", and a live Grenzburg-adjacent hook.)

**Weylan Oath-Bent** (c. 950-1012 AR) - The Folk war-leader who fought Adalmar for twenty years, disappeared after Wolfsmere, and accepted baptism with his surviving clans around 1005 AR; his baptism bought the Peace of the Bent Oak, which wrote the lake-law (no bell, no boat, no iron) into the march charters. To Eclessia, a trophy convert; to the Folk, "the Bent Oak," spoken with pity, contempt, and unbearable understanding in equal measure. Whether his baptism was submission, strategy, sincerity, or all three remains disputed among his descendants and rivals.

**Ludovar the Mild** (r. 1019–1046 AR) — Adalmar's surviving son: devout, earnest and recorded silent at every examination the Sanctum held for him. His sons bought examinations from rival sees and carried banners some of which had Shell-fire in the staff-head.

**The Partition of Drossfeld** (1049 AR) — Adalmar's grandsons **Aldwin**, **Lotharic** and **Gervin**, unable to win, drew lots for three portions sealed in Adalmar's helmet. Aldwin drew the south with Aurelium and the imperial title; Lotharic the east, later joined to Virelia by his daughter's marriage to Radomir I; Gervin the middle march, which held the existing Duchy of Caldria and fragmented within two generations into Brauvalis, Veridancia, the Korrheim valleys, Ironvale and the Sylvan Marches. The wax of Gervin's lot was smudged along its eastern line; every present border dispute in Eclessia cites Drossfeld the way lawyers cite scripture, and the middle march's disputes begin with the smudge.

**Radomir I of Strazgorod** (fl. 1061 AR) — Vesari prince who tested four faiths. His envoys' lamp kindled unbidden in Veridun and Makarios the Adamant would not let it leave; Radomir chose the Sanctum, married Lotharic's heiress, and their son ruled Virelia.

**Ermbold the Idle** (deposed 1102 AR) — King of Aurelia of Aldwin's line and last Adalring emperor. In the first of the Two Winters he refused to carry the Crown of Embers in a penitential procession ("It is a hat, not a relic"); the other kings deposed him at the winter diet of Drossfeld. In 1104 the Sanctum took custody of the **Crown of Embers** "in trust, until the realm is worthy of one head." That vault is the reunification mandate made physical: the Church literally keeps the empire in a box, and every king in Eclessia knows the Primarch could, in principle, take it out.

**Lord Primarch Bonifac III & Cardinal Hubald of Caldria** (fl. 1015 AR) — Bonifac demanded that Veridun acknowledge Simeon's primacy — Aurelium's right to examine the restored lamps, the Great Lamp first — and the Sanctum's trusteeship of the imperial title; his legate Hubald, received with calculated insult, nailed the bull of anathema **to the Great Lamp's stand** in Veridun's cathedral — four nails, and both sides understood the reference to the Four Nails of the Pillar instantly. Patriarch **Makarios the Adamant** answered with a counter-anathema before the nails were cold. The Great Split, 1015. Neither anathema has ever been lifted.

**Lord Primarch Gelasin II** (r. 1242–c. 1266 AR) — Closed the Schism of the Twin Cardinals and reorganised the old assurance letters into the Chancery of Sendings (1252), whose Sending Houses sang named rites for named dead.

**Saint Gerlind of Lindenfurt** (c. 1228–1279 AR) — Miller's daughter who sang the plague dead out for bread (*"the dead are named, not bought"*); acquitted by a relic court in 1266; canonised by Alessior II, whose Chancery then printed her face on assurance letters. Claimed by the Sanctum and the Freeflame alike. [[The Grey Sweat]]

**Lord Primarch Alessior II** (r. 1281–1296 AR) — A Malvora of Sestara who bought the Primarchy with Bartolo Medori's credit and ruled it like a principality: nepotism, poisonings alleged and probable, and — fatally — the industrial expansion of **routing assurances** to fund his wars and the rebuilding of the Sanctum Divinitatis in monumental splendor. His treasurer Silvio Quarne ran the trade. The direct, proximate cause of the Reformation: Ansgar's grievances name him eleven times.

**Silvio Quarne, the Bridge-Seller** (c. 1238–1299 AR) — Alessior II's Cardinal-Treasurer; industrialised the assurance trade, staged the Saint-Leon apparition (1285) and sold unsung letters to brokers as "dead paper". Died in Vorthara; his ledgers vanished.

## The Reformation

**Ansgar of Brenholt** (1264–1305 AR) — A marches friar and Vestibule-doctrine lecturer who posted the **Hundred Grievances** (1297), after counting more than ten letters sold for every rite sung in his own parish, against the sale of routing assurances: *"No seal of wax weighs anything at the Weighing. You have sold the poor a bridge that is not yours."* Because soul-routing is objectively real, this was not rhetoric but an accusation of metaphysical fraud — and everyone, including his judges, half-knew it. Tried at the **Synod of Aldersbruck** (1305), he refused to recant and was burned; his ashes were shoveled into the River Weiss to prevent relics. His psalter did not burn. **The Unburnt Psalter** sits in a lead vault under the Sanctum; its existence is officially denied and universally known — and in a world with a proof-of-sainthood protocol, both churches understand exactly why no one dares test it. A permanently loaded gun.

**The Ashenmen Risings** (1306–1329 AR) — Ansgar's home region rose within a year of the burning. Bands sworn in the Weiss fought twenty-three years of wagon-fort war without one oath breaking, until Rainald burned the seals in 1329 and the bands became his army. The risings sit in and around the Ironvale Dominion, explaining both its existing underground-movement canon and the later guild-industrial vacuum that Grand Minister Aldric Weyn inherits.

**Doctor Lucian Ferro** (1279–1352 AR) — the movement's true architect. A southern jurist-theologian, junior notary at the Synod of Aldersbruck, whose record he carried to Ironvale, he systematised Ansgar's fire into **The Order of the Open Flame**: the Flame kindles wherever sanctity gathers — *"the Lord's Fire keeps no landlord"* — therefore no hierarchical mint, no assurance trade, no relic-court monopoly, congregational hymn-casting under tested elders. Reformed duchies under his discipline are famously orderly, literate, and merciless. Sheltered and armed by **Duke Rainald of Ironvale** (duke c. 1309–1341), who had watched Ansgar's burning as a boy and in 1329 burned Ironvale's Sending letters at Brenholt, ending the Risings. Rainald was the *last* Duke of Ironvale: the [[Freeflame Reformation|Wars of Reform]] cost his house its blood (his last son died at Falkendorf in 1340), its credit, and its Church standing, and the guilds filled the vacuum (the present Grand Ministry dates its charter to the house's extinction). Ferro is dead eight years at present day; his students govern.

**Lord Primarch Celestin IX** (r. 1330–1354 AR) — convened the **Council of Veridance** (1337–1352): abolished assurance sales (too late), codified the five sainthood tests, rebuilt seminary education, and chartered **the Order of the Lantern** — teaching, missions, and quiet intelligence, founded by **Father Ivo the Lamed**, a crippled veteran of the Zanthiri border wars turned mystic. The name is a deliberate provocation: the Flame *belongs* in a vessel, carried where the Church wills.

**The Korrheim Accord** (1343 AR) — *Every roof under its lord's lamp*, sworn in the plain speech of the river country and copied by Sanctum canonists into their law-books as *whose the realm, his the rite*: each recognised ruler, whether duke, ministry, canton or chapter, fixes his territory's confession. Sworn with hands in the Weiss, it excludes the radical Freeflame congregations, the Unsworn, who refused to swear on a relic; it binds no one's conscience and does not so much as mention the reunification mandate — a cold peace everyone signed and no one believes. Present-day Eclessia lives inside its silences.

## The Sestaran Merchant Age

**Bartolo Medori the Elder** (c. 1215–1301 AR) — The Medori bought their seat in Fiorenzia's Golden Book in 1238, when the Grey Sweat had emptied a third of it. Bartolo built the bank into the peninsula's dominant credit house: he cleared the plague dead's estates, carried Sending payments, bought Alessior II's Primarchy (1281) and took the Chancery registers as surety (1296). His ledger-maxim “lend to both, collect from the winner” is quoted as cynicism and practiced as policy.

**Lauro Medori the Magnificent** (1289–1341 AR) — The patronage apex: academies, galleries, the Fiorenzian flowering — and heir to his grandfather Bartolo's purchase of Alessior II's Primarchy, which makes the Medori co-authors of the Reformation. The family does not discuss this.

**Frate Severin of Fiorenzia** (1301–1349 AR) — Flame-preacher who came to power after the Sermoran "Bank of the Dead" pamphlet of 1345 and seized Fiorenzia's soul for four years; his **Pyre of Masks** (1346) burned the vanities of the merchant age in the cathedral square — and witnesses still argue about what they saw standing in that fire. Hanged and burned by a Medori-restored government in 1349. In a world where the Flame is real, the question *was Severin kindled or mad?* has teeth: the Freeflame claim him, the Lantern studies him, Fiorenzia dreams about him.

**Nicanor Vetti** (1289–1347 AR) — Dismissed Sermoran under-secretary; wrote **The Mirror for Sovereigns** in exile: statecraft stripped of every sacred pretension, dedicated — with what must be irony — to a Medori. Banned by both churches, condemned by the Covenant, and found in the private shelves of every ruler on the board. Selections survive in [[The Mirror for Sovereigns — Selected Maxims]].

**Ottavia Sele** (fl. 1328 AR) — Castaran salt-hauler pilot whose *Naris's Hand* crossed the Boiling Sea on a Breath day, bringing back the Viscaran chart-case that opened the route and Castara's rush of the 1330s.

**Duke Gaius Medori** — Present ruler of Fiorenzia and head of the Medori family, governing beside a senate whose old houses resent the purchased ancestry that made his title possible.

**Doge Terenzo Vescari** — Present compromise ruler of Sermora, balancing Admiralty houses, guilds, privateer captains, and the secrets accumulated by the Veiled Office.

**Duke Marcus Soran and General Aurelia Soran** — Present princely and military heads of Aversia. Their mercenary dynasty seized an office older Shield Houses still consider stolen.

**Pass-Keeper Salvi Corren** — Keeper of Vorthara's roads, gates, shared water stations, and external Concord seal. He is not ruler of the anarchic city.

**First Speaker Eliana Corvesi** — Head of Castara's Merchant Princes and advocate of continued Boiling Sea expansion despite labor unrest and contested foreign claims.

**Harbor Rector Sabina Valeris** — Present rector of Orsena, responsible for neutrality, cistern security, prisoner exchange, and the Harbor Book's exclusions.

**First Burgess Tomas Bellafor** — Presiding officer of Vardessa's guild benches and custodian of the neutral muster hall.

## The Steppe

**Qarayel, the Black Wind** (d. 1303 AR) — Herd-master of a minor Grass-Tarkhan clan, called by the name of his black stallion; united the banners in 1261 by stud-right under the Nine-Mane Standard; extracted escort fees, fair-ransoms and the grass-toll 1265–1300; died undefeated; grave unknown by policy. His herds were partitioned by grace-line into the four Hordes in 1304. [[Qarayel and the Black Wind]]

**Gavrin the Farrier, the Grey Uncle** (c. 1215–1281 AR) — Velian bound craftsman taken from a plague-emptied Covenant well-fort; his blessing on a dying black colt (1249) renewed the horse-grace that made the Black Wind; refused freedom unless the camp's slaves were freed; murdered in 1281 (disputed). Saint Gavrin of the Halter in Thalmyria; the Wind-Saddler to Velian congregations.

## The Jirahar Thread

**The last River-King of Arqesh** (d. 688 BR) — Kassur III in the river records, remembered under incompatible names in Jirahar and Atherian ones. His empire had destroyed First Qiryath in 742 BR; his succession war and failed flood regime opened the return that produced Second Qiryath.

**Rab Yequtiel of Zanthir, "the Lamp of the Exile"** (c. 748–831 AR) — Physician to a Banu Mizan emir and the Path's greatest philosopher; his *Guide for the Wandering* reconciled Scroll legalism with Atherian philosophy and is studied, denounced, and depended upon in equal measure by every Keeper of the Scrolls since.

**Meshalem the Twice-Turned** (c. 1260–1303 AR) — Proclaimed himself the Mesakha in 1288; half the diaspora's tower towns sold their goods and began the return. Arrested by Qahirid authorities under Marzuq I in 1291 and offered the choice of proof or conversion, he converted. The catastrophe — worse than any pogrom, because self-inflicted — explains the present-day Keepers' ferocious gatekeeping of prophecy and the resulting political doctrine: after Meshalem, every claimant is presumed false, which is exactly the environment in which a true one would be missed. That irony remains central to Jirahar claimant stories.

**High Keeper Zerahiah** (present day) — First in dignity among independent Jirahar courts at Migdal Qesheth. He coordinates claimant scrutiny and gate cooperation without ruling the other towers. His greatest fear is another Meshalem; his greatest possible failure is refusing the true claimant for exactly that reason.

Several of the fourteen now carry their history openly in the era articles. The Saint of Ashen Wings is the Heldenberg slave courier whose revolt (c. 1116–1124) ended in the Oath of the Shafts and Korrheim's first cantons; he confessed the Cold Way experiment c. 1137 and died in exile in 1146 ([[High Noon and the Qarath Disaster]]). The Judge Beneath the Black Eaves ran warrants between Dvorna's two courts, and the trade in his forecasts hardened the Split ([[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]]). The Clerk of a Hundred Wells left frontier courts "the Clerk's ending", the date after which an emergency order must justify itself again, and Banu Mizan jurists claim him for their water law. The Preacher of the Second Gospel's counter-church sharpened the question of who may authenticate a sign and made bishops readier to let the emperor answer it.
