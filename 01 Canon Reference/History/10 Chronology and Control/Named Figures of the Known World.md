# Named Figures of the Known World

The people behind the [[Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)]]. Each entry records a figure's period, motivation, legacy, and the wound or story hook left behind. Historical design analogues are kept separately in [[Real-History Concordance]].

**Map ruling:** Eclessia sits east of Thalmyria in the mirrored map logic; older western-province phrasing should be read as superseded unless it refers to a local western subregion inside Eclessia.

## Atherian foundations and the republic

[[Avren and Caleth]] (c. 1150–1090 BR) led the estuary coalition associated with Caleran's foundation. Avren killed Caleth during a dispute over command and provisions. Their mother Meral, Caleth's wife Sareth and the surviving children preserved relationships and memories which the reigning household could not control. [[Caleran Foundations - Authorial History]] records Ravessa's actual fosterage and its limited purpose.

[[Ilaron and Tarsenna]] (c. 1000–950 BR) joined maritime resources and Serathi religious learning in a later royal generation. Their Common Sky settlement established shared public ceremonies while preserving rival interests and practices. Both were responsible for the captive sacrifice at a campaign celebration. Their daughter Ilessa maintained festival resources through subsequent royal disputes.

[[Caleran - Foundation and Royal Households]] also places Esvan's waterfront works and Senavar's coercive supply policy in the generations between foundation and Compact. [[Arsuvan - The Giant Kingdom of the Aurin|Arsuvan]] introduces the earlier Giant rulers Veskar, Tarvessa and Arveth, and Lethra's human boat rescue during the kingdom's collapse.

[[Arkenan and Saronikan Cities before the Empire]] introduces Kassilon's loss of command after Erenost's grain seizure, Doreva's grain ventures at Pyrassa and Mesthra's observational teaching at Irenai. They belong to independent eastern histories, without a shared royal genealogy or later supernatural office.

[[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] — The magistrate **Eshren Damar** led the Ilyessa veterans to the Noon Sighting at Sarven (488 BR), saw the land they occupied bought back for bread in the hungry spring of 487, and worked his own plot bareheaded until about 457. **Vespen Harod** accepted the Red Field against the rebel captains of the war of the closed chains in 466, lived twenty-five years in a tent on the muster ground, killed two of the senators who tried to replace him and died in office around 441. The senator **Arest Corven** followed Veyr into sanctuary at Ilamar and began there the history of the republic's last years, which Casvilar's son Hadram finished on the island and Casvilar's heirs had reason to fear.

## Ancient Qahur and the Gateway country

[[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]] — **Neshara Senareth** began the First Long Crown around 1880 BR; **Talmera of Isethar**, a widowed boat-owner, carried grain for both rival courts during the famine of about 1468 BR, and each later condemned her dealings with the other; and **Khevar II**, a Senareth cadet raised among the tomb priests of Tenaret, was named king by three statues of the Answering Kings in 1412 and reunited the basin through marriage, treaty and the execution of rival claimants. **Talzara Yasharet** founded the mixed lower-fan dynasty at Qemaret around 1248; her heirs began the empire on White Measure credit. **Iddar Yasharet** defaulted on the crown's pledge to Khaseret's House of the Returning Cargo after the low floods of c. 1084 and was walled alive into its harbour vault. **Aramen of Sakhret**, the House's factor, took Qemaret around 1076 with garrisons the House had paid and married Iddar's sister; Sakhret's king-list makes him the founder of a new house, and Qemaret's makes him a factor ruling for a Yasharet heir. **Hesren III**, king of the middle crown from c. 842, dug up the seal of a buried Giant estate at Meheret around 840 and set its gilded procession, the Single Radiance, on the Basin's tombs, giving the cleared fields to his garrisons. He hung the seal on his six-year-old son **Deshren**, whom the estate wasted. Deshren took the White Measure rite around 829 and died the landlord of everything his father had taken, a prince who was never crowned; Hesren paid him rent until his own death around 821. **Zaheret of Tenaret**, a boat-owner's daughter and an allotment captain's widow, turned the boat households against the dead prince's stewards and was crowned on a grain barge at Tenaret in 814.

[[The White Measure and the Second Houses]] — **Meresha of Isethar** (d. c. 1439 BR) developed the White Measure and became the first well-attested Second-House Patron. Her freed scribe **Hesenu** entered the Court of Unspent Lamps as a captive human soul. **Khaseret of Qemaret** (d. 1126) opened the House of the Returning Cargo, which went on lending after her death. Its stewards took the Yasharet crown's grain fleet, store courts and gate tolls in pledge and foreclosed on Qemaret around 1076, when its cellars admitted clients with clear accounts and shut out families carrying inherited debt; in 695 it fed Eryndor III's army against the right of her masters to prepare his body. **Tamekha Rasen** (d. 916) built the Copper Orchard through medicine, royal contracts and experiments upon prisoners; demands from her dead estate helped provoke the Tenaret stoppage of 903. **Nemeret the Pure** failed to preserve his soul. A dependent Greed Mask later learned to speak through the estate-office built around his memory.

[[The Gateway Wars]] — Quay Elder **Qedran Suthar** lost Tammur to the Atherian commander **Aster Vey** in 663 BR. **Tashqar Ordan**, hereditary captain of Oresh, invaded Caleran's allied country with gifts of grain (c. 612–604) while his own city disowned him, and was killed at the store doors of Erenost by people he had fed. The magistrate **Dema Varin** presided over the Lustration of the Field in 609, and **Celor Marath**, first Warden of the Red Field, held the approaches below Thesra in 606. In the Rope Camp stoppage (c. 536–531) **Damen** carried the council's terms, was saved from the camp by **Yasra of Belqara**, who spoke for the stripped districts, and was killed by his own council after it broke the oath he had sworn. **Teshun Ordan**, husband of **King Harzun of Hadrath**'s daughter **Sapha**, took the collectors back into the towns with Harzun's riders.

[[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth]] — **Ilya of Qarzeth** carried evidence of the child prisons to Esharat. The midwife **Sapha of Esharat**, unrelated to the Hadrathi princess, organised a rescue route; collectors killed her husband and sacrificed their daughter **Neral**. The senator **Orsen Valeor** demanded Qarzeth's destruction, while **Mera Castren** proposed enforced closure and inspection. Castren's policy was tried first and received the collectors' books from Qarzeth's assembly. Valeor lived to see the sack, ransomed forty-one of the children his soldiers sold and died about 514. Sapha of Esharat set the rules of the survivors' meal at Sarqen's landing, which became the spared towns' Table Oath.

[[Qahur and the Gateway before Atheria]] — **Prince Tazirun Zaheret**, a younger Qahuri son, married **Lemara of Esharat** around 548 BR. Their daughter **Hesara** helped conceal children during the siege of Qarzeth and became Hadrima's mother. This maternal relationship brought Qahuri kin and ceremony into the later Nerethan household without conveying rule over the Sacred Basin.

## Qiryath and the eastern empires

[[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/70 History and Conflicts/Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria.md]] — **Mareh bath Sivan**, the Mother of Measures, preserved three road camps during famine and fever around 1800 BR. **Menakhel of the Reed Cloak** (c. 1335 BR) broke a raiding household at three wells and executed the men who surrendered to him; his sons' toll house became House Sereq. **Tovan bar Rameq**, a herd boy hidden in an old cistern from Sereq's collectors (c. 1290), bargained with the shelf's water, which drowned the Sereq camp with the pledged children in its pens. The road judges bound his line to call the streams as a debt and barred it from office; the rite passed to the children of his sister **Imra**, and four of his sons drowned trying to seize it. **Tirzah bath Oren** exposed a cistern poisoning around 1210. At the toll fortress of Gedran (c. 1171) the weigher **Dovan**, who gave true weight in secret, was killed under the fortress's weights on his own scale; his daughter **Nareh bath Dovan** carried his honest stones through the villages, and **Hador bar Mavin**, unhoused for his private war, became Gedran's gate captain and sold his prisoners east. **Othan bar Raqel** broke a harvest truce to take Gedran around 1128; two sureties were killed, and Hador carried Othan's daughter **Naveth**, a child of the cistern line, down the river road.

**Amariah bath Qoreb** joined rival pilgrimage calendars around 1020 BR. **Zeran Oram**, guardian and head of a custodial house, hanged four women of the lower town around 962 BR after they killed his brother, the keeper of the lower grain rooms, whose guards had taken women drawing their portions. **Yedran bar Aqev**, an altar wood-carrier, caught the offering when Zeran fell with fever on the sanctuary stair (c. 953), and a crowd that read the cool stones as his confirmation made him Guardian of Qiryath while Zeran lived on in the upper house. Around 940 Zeran's sons and the lower town fought for the basket on the stair; the lamp-filler **Hanel bar Sem** carried it to the altar and died of his injuries. His widow **Rava bath Horel** asked the custodians publicly whom the stones had answered, and Zeran's daughter **Avileth bath Oram** answered that they answered only the offering. The bearer was chosen by lot thereafter. Yedran's command lapsed, and he went back to the wood road.

**Seram bar Teth**, guardian, married **Tilqa bath Keresh** of an Arqeshite river house (c. 829 BR). His levy stripped the villages that fed the cistern house, the rite of Tovan's line lapsed and the shelf's streams ran astray for four autumns. Around 826 his soldiers killed the wife and brother of the custodian **Tobel bar Yamin**, who escaped with his house's witness-staff rather than witness beside Tilqa's royal image; the image stood on the sanctuary stair, the offering burned without smoke and the day was written with a blank. Ilbarun II's demand of 746 relied on that morning. Around 824 Tilqa, descended through her mothers from Naveth, went down Tovan's cistern and the streams answered her. **Haviel of the Lower Stair** then had her diviners drowned in the lower town's cistern; the road judges unhoused him for it, and around 817 six women lifted the swollen gate for the families shut outside the walls, Haviel among them.

**Elior bar Hadan**, chief guardian in 746 BR, refused Ilbarun II's image and hostages; **Yavreth bath Qesem** argued for hostages to save the city and gained hearers when the sign was withheld. The widow **Yaelith bath Coren**, keeper of Second Qiryath's surviving cistern, married the exile official **Neram bar Elior** and refused to expel the settler-spouses and children who worked her water; the offering carried by her households c. 654 BR ended the Withholding.

[[01 Canon Reference/History/30 Exodus and First Civilizations/Arqesh - River Cities, Kings and Deportation.md]] — **Irduq of Teluq**, found as a child on the regulator beam the night Teluq's head gate opened unbidden, kept that gate and never ruled; Arqesh's Gate House later held the beam and sealed its water books with his sleeping child. **Merod I Ubar** seized Arqesh around 958 BR and established the first durable canal monarchy. **Kassur I Ubar** developed its provincial and deportation system. **Merod II Ubar** sent his son **Ashkur** to have the dead prince of Meheret's fled stewards confirmed in their fields; around 810 Ashkur entered the sealed palace with three of them and came out alone with a tally naming House Ubar the dead prince's steward east of the river, and Merod occupied the eastern canal towns as his collector. **Ilbarun II Ubar** destroyed First Qiryath in 742. His sister **Iltara Ubar** governed Khalbar and supervised deportation and resettlement. **Ishara of the Soot Court**, a smith, led the forced workers who opened Khalbar's gate to the coalition. In 690 the dredging gangs and deportee quarters of Arqesh, their rations cut by Kassur III, opened the capital. **Kassur III**, the last River-King, lost his portable images in 688 when they began to answer in Eryndor's voice and their bearers carried them over; he surrendered to Arezan, sealed the Gate House's water books to him at Durqesh and died a guarded guest at Kavresh around 676.

[[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]] — **Arezan I Tiravan** united Vashari highland houses against Arqesh and founded the Crown of Roads and Dawns. His sister **Mithara Tiravan** organised food, marriages and communication among the coalition. Her daughter **Parena Tiravan** married Eryndor III and acted between the two courts; she bore him **Mehrava** in the camp before Arqesh (691), whom Ortheia and Delkos refused to count his heir. Mithara also founded the relay stations that became the Dawn Road, and around 670 married Mehrava to Arezan's younger son **Vahrad**; every Tiravan king from their son Kavran I claimed the Far Road through her. **Tavrad I** (671–638) sent the consecration offering that smoked at Qiryath, put down Nemeru's rising and built the Terrace of Mirrors. **Tiraz I** (638–629) drowned with his tax camp when a canal district cut its embankment, and the keepers of Daryam carried the royal flame past his infant sons to **Kavran I** (629–594). **Mithara I** (594–552) followed him. **Tiraz II** (552–528) built the western grain stations and gave the Avesh road command to the Aresh house. **Kazren I** (528–491), **Orvaz I** (491–439), **Tiraz III** (439–409) and **Orvaz II** (from 409) governed Vashar to the threshold of Atherian empire. **Mazhara** of Daryam refused to call the Witness of Dawn a sign and was made keeper of the royal flame, which edited her carved sayings; **Rathava** condemned Orvaz I's perjury and recanted in the garden to save his labourers. At the harvest audience of 444 BR nobody on the terrace could speak Orvaz I's new praise, and the mirrors went dark.

[[Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest]] — **Valeron II** imposed Ortheian hegemony upon several Compact cities, struck the first coins to pay his standing army and was killed by its soldiers at the Pelatha pay table in 709, when Marenthe's withheld silver left the count short. **Eryndor III**, the Far-Road King, left his father's killers in Ortheia and confirmed their sack of Marenthe by letter, crossed the eastern sea with newer men, fed his army in Qahur on a dead financier's loan, joined Arezan in the capture of Arqesh, lost a road to a wood whose price he refused and died in 673 aboard ship at Nemeru, sailing for home, during a White Measure rite he had consented to. His courage, curiosity and skill accompanied massacre, enslavement and two deaths he permitted in his own hall: an officer executed for refusing the images' order to bow, and a companion strangled by their bearers for mocking them, whose voice the images then took. The separate wonders of his campaign became [[The Five Limits of Eryndor]].

[[Wars of the Inheritance]] — **Demeran Kavos**, speaker of the standing companies Eryndor left in Ortheia, kept the home army and the mints, killed the king's half-brother, whom the upland assembly had acclaimed, once the speaking tomb at Delkos made him a usurper, and sent after Parena and her daughter agents whom Arezan's road guards hanged at Durqesh. **Serevan of Delkos**, on whose flagship the king died, paid for Eryndor's deathbed rite, received his body, Kassur's images and the Belyra families when the flagship's Belyra crews sailed them to Delkos, and governed in the name of the voice in the tomb; his officer houses included the ancestors of Nerethan. **Neresh of Qemaret**, the House of the Returning Cargo's man in the camp, brought the White Measure masters to Eryndor's deathbed under the pledge of 695, held selected Qahuri crown towns through garrison and marriage, and c. 657 had the masters testify that their rite had failed. Their wars destroyed the nominal succession and divided Eryndor's conquests.

[[The Eastern Sea Wars]] — **Liora of Tasar** led the harbour revolt of 514 BR after the Vasharan governor **Mazdan Aresh**, killed there after surrendering, tried to impose a magistrate and enlarge naval service; refused by the Caleran Senate, she hired ships under **Eryon Telmar**. **Ardash Aresh** retook the city in 511, executed its leaders and sold inhabitants or deported them to the Dawn Road stations. **Zadran Vezh** lost a fleet at its moorings off Ilyessa in 502. **Kazren I Tiravan** hired Ilyessa's pilots, launched the western invasion of 497, claimed the sea in his title in 493, lost his granary fleet at anchor and died of the flux before Velanthe. **Ebris of Ilyessa**, the pilot who gave his fleet the terms of the Narrows, was drowned by his marines. **Nerica Aven**, who directed the League of the Eastern Oar's fleet, kept Velanthe's refuge fire lit for his deserting crews. Ardash Aresh burned lower Avesh to hold its granaries and sold the young **Orvaz I** his road home for the western command. Liora's fate remains disputed.

## Vathra and the northern ground

[[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]] — **Ozhur IV of Urzavad** tried to keep Urzavad aloft through requisitions of food, ballast, workers and children. His cousin **Varakesh of Dazhur** began the War of Altitude and discarded dependants while claiming to reform the kingdom. The Dynastic Giant **Kelvara** first served Ozhur, then used her knowledge of the descent grounds to help human communities ground Kel-Orun. Her assistance saved thousands and did not undo her earlier participation in captive transfers.

**Seyra Vatan** organised tether crews; **Dorun of Tal-Kesh**, whom the mine and cistern crews he brought into the rising called Dorun of the Draw-Gangs, changed Urzavad's route notices; **Meya of Narash** brought camel houses and western wells into the Great Unyoking. Meya had previously supplied Vathra with captives taken in local wars. In the first winters after the war she sealed the Hundred Wells by a bargain at the dead anchor field; every Narashi crown-mother since has walked the round of the wells at accession, and succession stayed maternal. Their constituencies cooperated without becoming one permanent company or state.

[[The Tarkhan before the Horse Hordes]] — **Boran Tall-Hand** built a chariot house at Daram Ford and seized children from households that refused animals for his teams; when his salvage-copper convoy stuck in the mud and he ordered the grain dumped to save the copper, his own porters killed him (the Feast of the Copper Wheels). The foster cousins **Savan Uruz** and **Demer Khol** founded rival foreign-backed client courts; Savan's soldiers killed him when Arqeshite payment failed, and former captives defeated Demer. **Yalun of the White Camel** and **Marat Two-Bows** joined the coalition against Arqesh for different purposes and treated captives differently after victory.

[[The Ahdari before the Covenant]] — **Harun Savel** violated the disarmed market at Saqara around 1280 BR and caused the trade which supported it to move to Mazar. At Rethmar in 402 BR, the water keeper **Tavisha** removed stores from the route of the expedition Lethren Ostar had sent under his son Pel while **Kharuz of the Stone Wells** commanded camel scouts and foot archers. Their coalition dispersed after defeating the Atherian army.

## The imperial-founding generation

[[Teren Casvilar]] (431–370 BR) conquered southern districts and accepted the Red Field at Kellovar in 383 against the Folk chief Drenoc, who vanished into a river ford. The Field he could not close carried him through civil war to personal supremacy. Seven challengers entered in the muster ground's arms register, one of them as Drenoc's son, killed him on the muster ground in 370 before the contest opened. His assassination began the succession wars, in which no commander dared disband an army while any of the seven lived. [[Marovan Veyr]] (435–371), his son-in-law, former partner and principal rival, never met him in the field; he refused a table laid with an empty seat at Sarqen in 373 and died of fever in sanctuary at Ilamar, and his son Ardel inherited his following, lost it at Marelith in 355 and lived out his life as a guest under refuge law at Pyrassa. [[Lethren Ostar]] (446–c. 374) financed the partnership, sold the defeated rebels of 410 north in lots to the well trade, lost his son Pel to the wells in the failed expedition to Rethmar (402), was entered as a contestant on Casvilar's Field in 379 and died a fugitive at Vorthara.

[[Aurelius Thalion]] (390–318 BR) was Casvilar's nephew and designated heir. He refused the Red Field in 370, governed from the store-house above the muster ground, never commanded in a battle and sent painted boards of his face to war in his place; one of them became the accession portrait that spoke in 247. Around 350, with all seven of Casvilar's challengers dead, he stood on the muster ground and the Red Star's priests declared the Field closed into his command. He had Sarqen only by eating at the spared towns' unranked table and swearing their oath. [[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]] follows his government to his death at the spring meal on Sarqen's landing in 318, the death of his heir Caelor after an untested treasury hall fell on him in 326, and the succession of his younger son Meren. [[Darion Velsar]] (408–350) won at Darsil in 369, devised the Writing-In, was outrun on Vashar's Dawn Road in 354, accepted the Salt Field in 353 and was killed on it by Melvo. [[Seluc Varroth]] (414–350), First Celebrant of the Common Sky, ruled the Red Field fouled and unclaimed in 367, lost his magazines when his own garrisons burned them in 364, carried the register of challengers to Velsar's Salt Field in 353 and was killed there; the register was lost. [[Istran Melvo]] (born 391) organised the fleets which burned Marelith and blockaded Sarqen, refused to take Ardel Veyr from the refuge of his own home harbour, and met Velsar on the Salt Field in person, against Thalion's wish.

[[Samyra Nerethan]] (392–333) ruled from Sarqen. [[The House of Nerethan]] fixes the incoming commander Nerethan, Hadrima of Esharat, Arenes, Tavren and Olymes as the preceding royal generations. Samyra's elder sister Merath, Thalion's second wife, was held in her palace from 358 with her newborn son Meren, whom the towns inspected at the landing every spring; Merath died there in the blockade winter of 351, and Meren became emperor. Samyra swore the spared towns' Table Oath at accession; the towns restored her in 373 after Vardek laid an empty seat for Veyr, and put her house out in 350 after her son Hadram, Casvilar's son, killed Mavren Seneth at their table. She sat down at that table as a stranger, where the oath forbade anyone to take her, and lived at Esharat as the towns' guest with Talven and Lethia; Hadram lived in sanctuary on Ilamar until about 320.

[[Households of the Imperial Founding]] records Lysara's support for her son, Evara's management of his household, Ilmara Deneth's death in 361, Merath Nerethan's marriage, her seven years in her sister's palace and her death in 351, Selvena's marriage to Veyr, her public refusal of divorce in 377, the terms she made for Ardel at Pyrassa in 353 and her plea for Hadram in 350, Miretha Ardel's ten years of grain carts and her death at Darsil in 356, and Tobal of Hazreth, the rower seated in the queen's place. The same account preserves the roles of Mavren Seneth, who had watched Drenoc enter the ford at Kellovar, the ruined ship-owner Corial Draven and Dervan Tolm, quartermaster of the muster ground, whose complicity is disputed, the southern organiser Velcara and the speaker Besral Venn (c. 434–348), one of the Four at the Stores, who was written into the register at Velsar's demand with Thalion's consent in 366, stood on the muster ground with a practice sword and was left alive by the soldiers sent to kill him. Dervan Tolm kept the arms register in which the seven entered their names; Iren Sallas, another of the seven, walked out of Ilamar's sanctuary to his death in 359; Draven died aboard Marelith in 355, another at a relay station on the Dawn Road by 352, and Seneth, the last, at the towns' table in 350. Drenoc and Morveth belong to independent Folk coalitions; Kharuz and Tavisha to the northern well resistance. None is a member of an existing signature company.

## The Age of Glory

[[The House of Thalion]] — **Meren I** (r. 318–286 BR), Thalion's younger son by Merath Nerethan, born at Esharat in 358 and kept in Samyra's household until 350; he swore the Table Oath on Sarqen's landing at his accession. **Cassara** (c. 310–266), his daughter, was refused by two commands when her face was painted on the heir's board and was seated by the spared towns at Sarqen's table from 286. **Varelan I** (r. 286–263), her husband and the face on the board, expanded the roads, stores and waterworks and kept the wardens' test after the quarry strike of 274. **Olarion** (r. 263–247), their son, left the court divided by purges sworn before the accession portrait, allowed no heir to be painted and was found dead before the portrait, precipitating the [[The Painted Reign|Painted Reign]].

**Avaren Mott** (d. 245 BR), senior magistrate at Caleran, governed in the name of the speaking accession portrait for two winters. Clerks in its sight read out full granaries, and its assurance that no imperial granary was empty sent soldiers behind it into the poor districts. He was the first man executed under Samor's law against questioning the veiled Face.

[[The House of Veloran]] — **Samor I** (r. 245–229 BR), a warden-general of the river army, brought grain into Caleran, covered the speaking portrait with his cloak, refused to be painted and kept the Face veiled by law; from his reign emperors were agreed by the river commands and made under an arch. **Aren I** (Aren Soreth, r. 229–203), the last child of the old house and Samor's hostage, was chosen over Samor's grandson, built municipal works and returned the Crown of Olveth. **Orent I** (r. 203–174), Samor's grandson, reigned through the Sleepwalkers' Summer and took the Arcelon corridor. **Orent II** (r. 174–151) received Raveth into the empire and died in the fever summer of 151 without an agreed successor.

[[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]] — **Elian I Halreth** (r. 151–126 BR), governor of Sarqen, was seated by the spared towns in the emperor's empty place and paid the eastern cities with citizenship sworn before the emperor's image. **Elian II** (r. 126–101) approved the crown estate his sister **Lethara**'s stewards made of Raveth's famine debts; it passed to her son **Kelsen I** (r. 101–76), in whose last years Atheria took Qiryath. **Velen I** (r. 76–61) unveiled the Face at his spectacles and drowned under his own untested banks. **Doran I** (r. 61–42) took power with stores held by the Low Door Companions, re-veiled the Face and had his officers enter by the low door; those officers chose his son-in-law **Odrun I** (r. 42–24) to follow him. **Odrun II** (r. from 24 BR) was emperor at the Redeemer boundary.

**Neriah bar Sorek** (fl. 119–113 BR) — the [[The Keeper of Borrowed Grain|Jirahar administrator of the Raveth hunger]]. His grain system saved towns. He issued relief as village debts on countersigned tallies, and the court turned them into a crown estate against his advice. He resigned rather than administer the oath before the emperor's image that estate officers substituted for the tallies, and left the villages their copies.

[[The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door - Imperial History|The Low Door Companions]] (fl. c. 96–54 BR) — Sena Varris, Oren Tal, Brana Keth, Thessa Qor, Miro Fen and Helon Var rose from service work after saving a magistrate. Their later advancement involved hostages, an unlawful raid and betrayal of a family refuge. Helon was executed after freeing condemned hostage children. In 61 BR Miro and Brana supplied Doran's march, and his officers adopted the companions' low door.

## The Age of the Redeemer


**Lord-Scribe Ezrahel bar Qoreth** (fl. 0 AR), keeper of the sanctuary copy of the Qiryath charter his grandfather had drafted, led the senior elders who referred Isfir to the Atherian governor at Second Qiryath. He feared imperial retaliation against the sanctuary and the other towers. He sought a capital sentence over the objections of an elder who challenged the testimony. Jirahar and Redeemer accounts preserve different judgements of his decision. [[Jirahar Memory of Isfir]]

**High Augur Severan of Astaroth** (fl. 0 AR) accompanied the provincial governor to Second Qiryath and urged a public punishment. The governor passed the sentence. Severan's last recorded augural reading, three days after the execution, says the stars did not answer; every other college's book is blank for the same three days. His college recorded that he "laid down the staff". Sestaran humanists pay fortunes for alleged pages of the Severan Fragments.

**The Companions** — thirteen called, one struck (full roll, deaths, and patronages in [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]]). **Ischar the Silvered** — sold him and was struck; the seat is kept empty at every commemorative table, and his refused silver ("Ischar's coin," money no one will admit to owning) circulates still: thirty coins that always come back. The two primacies descend from this table: Eclessia claims the **Commission of the Pen** through **Saint Jareth of the Bound Eye**; Veridun claims the **Ember Succession** through **Amaryah's hearth** at Veridun Minor — Solana carried the ember, Amaryah kept it, Calira died for it.

**Amaryah the Fire-Bearer** (d. c. 55 AR) — the faith's great theological dispute (see [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]): Fire-Bearer or Man-Bearer (settled at Halmenth 411 AR — whose losers, struck off the regency's roll and left alone as taxpayers, survive as the [[The Velian Church|Velian Church]] under Covenant contract); Falling-Asleep (Veridun) against defined Unkindled Conception and bodily Rising (Sanctum); honored-not-invoked (Freeflame); and a pending apparition case at Vessing (1352 AR) the Sanctum dares not rule on.

**Saint Aquilan the Turned** (d. c. 45 AR) — of Jirahar blood and training. Born **Sha'il bar Qoreth**, kinsman of the Lord-Scribe who referred Isfir to the governor, Keeper-trained, an Atherian citizen by his house's veiled oath under Elian I's grant; as "Aquilan the Hound" a prosecutor of the early assemblies before the Great Purge, struck blind on the Virelos road and baptised by the congregation he came to arrest. Apostle to the nations; his **Epistles** ("fulfilled, not abolished") are the hinge of supersession and thus of centuries of pogroms he never saw. The Freeflame quote his seatless commission against both hierarchies; the Jirahar keep the exact and unforgiving formula: *"The carpenter we mourn. The scribe we cannot forgive."*

**Yaltah bath Ozer** (d. 64 AR), gate keeper of Migdal Shahar, the deep tower on the Dawn Road that the legions besieged before Qiryath, opened its Way-Gate on the receiving bench's terms (households, and no one who had borne arms) against her captain's refusal, sent the households through over nine days and broke the scroll-keys before the legions reached the chamber. **Eshkar bar Hanan** (d. 64 AR), the war captain, refused terms that abandoned his fighters and died holding the stairs. Gate benches still cite Shahar when they weigh another tower's danger against its people. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]]

**Elqan bar Sered** (d. winter 72–73 AR), Keeper of the sanctuary through the Tower Revolt, went on making the emperor's offering every morning after the governor's soldiers killed the custodians on the stair, sent the sanctuary's vessels through Qiryath's Way-Gate before the breach and died under torture at Arcelon without naming the bench that received them. **Gedor bar Ammi** (d. 62 AR), a carrier of the emperor's wood, confessed to soaking it during the Nine Mornings of 61 and was killed by the men of the war captain **Zevad bar Tirzan**. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]]

**Karsen Maruth** (fl. 62–78 AR), the legate who dug down into Migdal Shahar, broke Qiryath's cisterns and burned its sanctuary, held a triumph of empty litters in 76 and lost the succession war of 78 to Dorvan Dascen. **Dessan Vole**, the governor's augur of the Red Star, entered the Nine Mornings as fraud in 61 and lit the emperor's offering on Qiryath's altar in 72. **Halen Corvath** was the governor who sent soldiers up the sanctuary stair.

[[Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian - Emperors Wars and Revolts|Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian]] follows these reigns and the provincial wars.

**Melqa of Belqara** (fl. 187–189 AR), a tally-woman of Sarqen's port yards, led some three hundred runaways of the rising onto the landing on the morning of the governor's oath and sat them at the spared towns' table, from which no stranger can be sold.

**Emperor Tervan Marcellan** (r. 233–248 AR) rose through the provincial commands. After a decade of mutinies and failures to supply the garrisons, he issued the **Edict of Relit Lamps** in 245, in answer to the Kept Lamps, a grave-lamp cult that had fed mutinies in the unpaid eastern garrisons. Every household had to put out the lamps it kept for its dead at a market altar, relight them from the Stars' college fire and keep them burning green on the colleges' salted oil, and night watches walked the graves for yellow flames. The order widened persecution from cases against individual congregations to a test imposed across directly governed provinces. The oil's green was verdigris scraped from the temple bronzes. Tervan died in the spring of 248, and his successor suspended the order that summer, after the High Augur of Caleran refused to open the year before a scraped image. The congregations' quarrel over who might be given fire spread the daughter-flame custom. [[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization]]

**Emperor Cassian of the Reach** (acclaimed 288; r. 292–307 AR) was acclaimed by a frontier army whose campaign season the capital's augurs had voided. In the winter of 292 his vanguard was crossing the frozen Aurin by night when the White Flame stood on the river under the **Stone Bridge of Velos** and the ice went out. No battle was fought; the court's army crossed the bridge to him, and he legalised the Redeemer's faith. He kept the augur colleges because they entered the unfreezing reach as his sign, and c. 302 traded a crown warden at the bridge for empire-wide restitution (the Velos Concord), which Halmenth signed last, in 305, "as a record of ice". He granted the Celestial Forum with its Pillar and Nails to Caleran's church and funded basilicas. c. 304 he executed the High Augur of Astaroth over a sealed succession answer and had his eldest son Torian, who had asked for it, ordained and confined as chaplain of the bridge chapel. Torian baptised him, dying, in the unfrozen reach in 307, which gave rise to the Cassian Wager. The vision was real. The content of the sealed answer and the outcome of the Wager remain unresolved. [[Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR|Cassian and the Four Sees]]

**Torian** (c. 272–c. 341 AR), Cassian's eldest son, led the vanguard onto the ice at Velos in 292. About 304 he asked the High Augur of Astaroth for an answer on the succession. His father had him ordained by the bishop of Caleran and shut into the bridge chapel at Velos as its crown chaplain, where he baptised Cassian in 307 and served until his death. Whether a man ordained against his will was a chaplain is part of the Cassian Wager.

**Brask** (fl. 290–291 AR), the Ashforges' enslaved script-keeper, was freed by Cassian's officers so that his oath to the cold furnace and the flame on the Forty would stand: the first sign sworn before crown officers outside the augurs' power. [[The Forty Flames]]

**Emperor Sevrin** (r. 307–340 AR), Cassian's younger son, commanded the southern reserve and gained recognition by guaranteeing the eastern command's pay and revenue. He confirmed restitution and renewed market treaties with southern Folk communities. He refused to transfer the wardenship at Velos, where his disinherited elder brother Torian was chaplain until c. 341, and never opened the sealed succession answer. His only son died c. 335. **Adran**, Cassian's nephew, accepted command in the east under Sevrin on terms that put his line next in the succession, and Adran's son Astrion succeeded Sevrin under them. [[The Reign of Sevrin]]

**Astrion the Carried** (c. 317–c. 372 AR; r. 340–352 at Caleran; styled emperor east of the depot line from c. 357), Adran's son and Cassian's great-nephew, called the Unanswered by Church writers, was raised by Flame tutors and studied the old Stars through the eastern archives. He commanded the northern desert approaches before his accession and succeeded Sevrin under the terms of 307. He restored privileges to the Star cults, revived the Purge's edict against unentered wonders (the Entering), had the augurs re-enter the Velos reach as Hecatara's answer, and in 350 obtained the Red Star's single answer to his public test of legitimacy. On the retreat from a punitive campaign against northern desert communities in 351 he insisted on standing, as the opener of a work, beneath an untried timber span at a dry wash (the Emperor's Cut). It broke under the wagons of wounded and crushed his hips and legs. The field record gives only his question whether the baggage with the register had crossed. He lived. Caleran, told that he was dead, made Lucar Aster emperor and kept him. Under the Accord of the Two Standards (c. 357) Astrion kept the name of emperor in the east for life, with no heir to it, and was carried to every muster under the Answered Standard. The Red Star never answered him again, and his augurs wrote that down every spring. Sestaran humanists remember him as tragedy. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]] · [[The Conversion of the Empire]]

**Savel Orren** (fl. 351 AR), commander of Astrion's guard, was offered the throne beside the wreck at the Emperor's Cut and refused it while the emperor breathed. He had a litter made from the broken span and served in Astrion's eastern household until he died. He was never emperor.

**Tellan the Water-Bringer** (fl. 351–c. 390 AR), Adran's younger son and Astrion's brother, saved the retreating army with water trains under the bronze standard of the 350 test, the Answered Standard. He commanded the east in the field for his crippled brother under the Accord of the Two Standards (c. 357), and in his own right from Astrion's death c. 372. He never claimed the throne. He lost the War of the Two Standards (383–384) and died a pensioned captive above the Aurin.

**Lucar Aster** (r. 352–377 AR), Sevrin's son-in-law and commander of the southern reserve, was made emperor at Caleran on a false report of Astrion's death and kept when it was corrected. His elder brother **Corlan Aster** (d. c. 354) held the Aurin approaches for him and ran the southern defences as his marshal; he was never emperor. With his marshal dead Lucar could not hold both the Aurin approaches and the south, and the stand-off with the eastern command (355–357) followed. From c. 368, fourteen years after Corlan's death, Lucar's own chaplains confiscated as idols the ford-tokens of the Folk guides and traders of the southern fort districts, and his officers crossed the fords by the older toll, delivering children of the allied households to the far banks; stewards sold some of the children. In 377 his army followed the escaping households into the Kellovar ford with no token and drowned. Lucar crossed dry on a confiscated silver token, spent three days as a guest at the fire of the house it belonged to, and was killed by his own rearguard when he rode back across. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]]

**Cathra of the Otter house** (fl. 369–377 AR), mother of a chief's house of the southern fort districts whose grandson a fort steward entered as delivered to the ford and sold, kept Lucar three days at her fire under hearth-law after the Kellovar ford carried him across on her house's confiscated token, and sent him home. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]]

**Emperor Vestian the Pious** (r. 377–398 AR) held the inner depots after the Kellovar drowning and was recognised at Caleran while Lucar was still across the river, giving the Velos register to the bishops for that recognition; he tried no one for Lucar's death. He dissolved the augur colleges (383–384), won the War of the Two Standards and made the faith compulsory through the Profession of 385. When the Profession's lamps failed to kindle, his court blamed hidden rites and the Jirahar, and under him the persecution of the Jirahar turned from riot to imperial policy. He wrote *before the Mesakha* into Qiryath's Empty Charter (c. 396) and died in 398, leaving the throne to a son still in the schoolroom.

**Ilios Marrant** (fl. 383 AR), last High Augur of Caleran, made the colleges' final public petition to the Red Star at the Dissolution. Nothing answered.

**Bishop Qedrel of Qabir** (c. 362–c. 440 AR), a reader from a baptised family of Jirahar descent, kept Qabir's count of the unkindled professions (385–388) and carried copies to Raveth. He was elected bishop c. 402 over Caleran's objection and refused to certify relic estates for the regency's tax exemptions. Condemned at Halmenth in 411 and charged under the augurs' old law against publishing an unentered sign, he was rowed out to a Sea Fleet ship by Halmenth's ferry crews and died at Qabir, bishop of cities the regency had struck off its roll and left alone because they still paid their land tax. His deacons kept Qabir's own flame, a daughter of Amaryah's lamp, and would not relight it from the Ember's daughter. His communion survives as the [[The Velian Church|Velian Church]].

**Bishop Ostrel of Halmenth** (fl. 411 AR) hosted the Council of Halmenth and voted to condemn the Man-Bearer teaching; his ferry crews rowed Qedrel out to a Sea Fleet ship the same night. [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]

## The Atherian emperors after Vestian

**The Atherian emperors after Vestian** (398–905 AR) belonged to four houses, each raised by a different way of paying the army. [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

- **The Vestianic house** (398–447). Vestian's son reigned through the court bishops and paymasters who had made his father, selling relic estates freedom from tax and audit. His only son had been tonsured at eleven by the regency's bishops and would not leave his abbey when the emperor died in 447.
- **The Saldane house** (452–587). Founded by the commander of the southern forts, acclaimed by the great sees in the war of 447–452 and paid in immunities. **Teomar Saldane** (r. 509–531) fought the Ubar Toll War (514–523). The emperor of the Thin Floods, remembered in the north as the Granary Thief, kept Caleran's dole by requisitioning northern stores. The house was deposed in 587 after the loss of the north.
- **The Tavoranid house** (587–698). **Tavoran I** (r. 587–624), a frontier general who travelled with a hermit-bishop, led the crusade of seals and signed the peace of 612. **Tavoran II** (r. 681–698), whose accession lamp was the Copper Lamp, died in the Fever Week of 698, which left his son too weak to ride the marches; his son-in-law Corvenas rode them instead.
- **The Corvenid house** (701–905). **Corvenas I** (r. 701–746), commander of the march facing the Velian belt and husband of Tavoran II's daughter, won the War of the Two Oaths by riding the marches to renew their oaths in person. **Corvenas II** (r. 861–884) launched the Reception and was forced to abdicate.

**Corvenas III** (r. 884–905 AR; d. 908) — The last Atherian emperor. Sincerely devout, he could reach every remedy only through a public test: riding to the marches to kindle a summons, striking the lost north off the registers, or submitting his relics to Aurelium. He chose rites inside Caleran and turned the palace into the Ash Hall. Covenant files held that his accession rite was finished with warlock fire. On the last day of the siege he carried the Velos Standard onto the river dyke and called on the Fire before both armies, and nothing came. The breach-court accepted from him only his person and his relics, by the **Instrument of the Ash Hall**; he lived as their captive keeper and died among them in the winter of 908, and the refuge raised its own standard that summer. Veridun struck his name from its prayers for the dead. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/70 History and Conflicts/Prelude to Collapse.md|Prelude to Collapse]] · [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Invasion and the Fall.md|The Invasion and the Fall]]

**Oswin of Aurelium** (bishop c. 694–731 AR) — In 700 examined the Velos Standard, sent east by the Caleran claimant, recorded it silent and held the eastern governors' oaths "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful". Corvenas I's confirmation of Aurelium's right of examination in 701 is "the Examiner's primacy".

**Sarion the Pale** (d. 903 or 904 AR) — A march monk stoned in Caleran during Corvenas III's Jubilee for preaching *"The inked ones are coming. They walk with chains and offers of peace. The crown will burn in silence."* His words were cut on a memorial wall after 957.

## The Covenant

**Mujalib, the Final Prophet** (c. 510–596 AR) — Received the contract cosmology from Malak al-Ahd in 541, beside a well with two masters; sealed the shares of the kept wells; came down to the Basin in the Thin Floods, where the 4,444 dug Al-Qahur and bread went to those who sealed their names. He took no ruler's title, gave his authority away in 563 under three Seals (Water, Names and the Roads) and lived another thirty-three years beside the first channel while the Covenant took half an empire in his name. Authorial truth: his revelation was the Creditor's masterpiece, while Mujalib himself was wholly sincere.

**The Hand-Sealed** (563 AR) — The three first holders of Mujalib's Seals, sealed by the Prophet's own hand: Zuhayr ibn Qays and the guard of the wells (Water), the college of scribes at Al-Qahur (Names), and the marshal Qatada Hafiz-al-Turuq (the Roads). There was no caliph while Mujalib lived. The fourth Seal, which would set one holder over the other three, is disputed to this day — the Covenant's permanent succession fault line, detailed in [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]].

**Zuhayr ibn Qays** (c. 533–609 AR) — Captain of the guard of the oasis wells, who took the Nine Wells by force in 558, held the Seal of Water from 563 and was named by the college's Fourth Seal in 597. The first to bear the title of caliph. He could not dismiss Qatada, did not order the killing at Bir Qassim and punished nobody for it; within the year the college that had named him recalled the well's keeper to stop the mourners' pilgrimage.

**Qatada Hafiz-al-Turuq, "Keeper of the Roads"** (c. 535–604 AR) — A caravan-master's son who held the Seal of the Roads from 563 and escorted the breach-courts into the Atherian north. He lost the one pitched battle of the conquest, at the Dry Ford in 586, and took the province anyway when the victorious army broke up at wells that would not draw for it. In 598 he halted his own army because he would serve no notice under a disputed seal, and Zuhayr could not dismiss a marshal sealed by the Prophet's hand. He gave Hilal the safe conduct of 603. When it failed at Bir Qassim and the caliph ordered him to arrest the escort that had refused to disarm the travellers, he read the order, answered nothing and rode west to see them to the strait. He died on the road back in 604.

**Hilal ibn Raiq** (c. 565–631 AR) — Mujalib's grandson through Rumaitha, named heir by the Counter-Instrument. After six years among the well-houses of the dry country he went north to arbitration under a safe conduct that named fifty-eight travellers and one road. A flood pushed them off it, and at Bir Qassim the well's bound keeper would not draw for his household. On the sixth night he read the instrument aloud at the well-mouth clause by clause, the origin of the Reading of the Wells (*And yet the well was dry*). The escort refused to disarm the household and carried him west with thirty-six others; his mother, left with the dying at her own order, died of thirst. He lived veiled at Qazmarid until 631, the first of the Veiled Wasiyin. [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]]

**Rumaitha bint Mujalib** (c. 538–603 AR) — The Prophet's daughter, whose household produced the Counter-Instrument naming her son Hilal. Too weak to ride from Bir Qassim, she ordered him to go and died of thirst beside a full well her father's bound servant would not draw from. Hers is the first name read at the Reading of the Wells.

**Jarud ibn Sulayt** (fl. 603 AR) — Presiding jurist of the travelling court at Qafra who ruled Hilal's safe conduct void because the route was an essential term. The Sealed Blood speaks his name only to curse it; penitential Witnessed-Seal schools teach his ruling as the type of *lawful and wrong*.

**The Banu Mizan, "House of the Scales"** (c. 610–885 AR) — The dynasty of the Velian golden age: charter-houses, water-courts, the great libraries where Atherian learning survived in the conqueror's custody. They brokered the peace of 612. Their courts hosted Jirahar philosophers, and their surviving compacts later sheltered Thalmyric exile monks. Their rule in the Velian belt ended in 885, when Qahir's officers took the client courts from a regency; a Banu Mizan marriage line kept Zanthir at Qabir as tributary emirs, and their compacts outlived the dynasty. Every present-day power is embarrassed by how much it owes them.

**Qahir ibn Ruzbeh** (c. 848–902 AR) — Founder of the Qahirid dynasty, of a Ruzhan horse-lord house that sealed with the Covenant for bread when Vashar fell (555–560). Made his name defending the Velian belt against the Reception (874–878); paid his unpaid riders from the labour of the crusade's nine thousand unransomed prisoners, entered under his own name, and was given the Seal of the Roads and the new title of Sultan in 881; obtained the rulings that the crusade had broken the peace of 612 (884) and that the Clause of the Given binds parties, not powers (885–886); rebuilt the war machine around mass Ahdanite levies, capture companies and siege-golem foundries.

**Sultan-Caliph Idris al-Qari, "the Unsealer of Gates"** (r. 902–931 AR), Qahir's successor. Thirty-one and sonless at his accession, with every oath in the army naming him and nobody after him, he was kept at Iqashar by his jurists while Qahir's engine-master Barzin and the breach-court train took Caleran in his name in 905; he never saw the city. Covenant jurists gave him *al-Qari*, "the Reader", for a city opened by reading; Thalmyric chroniclers, who never saw him, call him the Letter-Caliph. He was the first of his house styled Sultan-Caliph. By courier he ruled the Eternal Ember a deposit held for the final audit, which kept the basilica open under guard; his officers preserved the Ember and the Pillar of Concordance until the Thalmyric recovery. Like his father he bound army and conquered cities to himself by name; his eldest son Ruzbeh, born 915, was written in as successor-holder. In 929 Ruzbeh, fourteen, refused to take the instruments up, and Idris died of fever in 931 before anyone re-swore, widowing every instrument at once.

**Ruzbeh ibn Idris** (915–c. 962 AR) — Idris's eldest son, written into every instrument as successor-holder. Taught by a jurist of the people of the Clause, he refused at fourteen to take the instruments up at the Chain Furnace yard when he was told what the second engine held, and refused again when Basin captains tried to force him in 932. The Regency lodged his name, the first lodging, and he lived thirty years in a walled garden at Al-Qahur. Iqashari jurists lay the dead of the Unsworn Years at his door; the people of the Clause keep his refusal as the one day their argument was obeyed.

**Barzin** (fl. 885–905 AR) — Qahir's engine-master, a Ruzhan horse-lord's son who built the name-cage foundries and, in his sixties, took Caleran in Idris's name with the chief jurist of the breach-court train. Thalmyric tradition says the Red Star showed his engineers the Star's share of the Aurin dyke, the one length its builders had made to break in the wardens' test, and his Depth-Core engines waded the flooded river to lean on it; his engineers said they bought its place in a dead augur college's archive.

**Sultan-Caliph Mahdar ibn Idris** (r. 931–979 AR) — Eight at accession; the Regency of the Bench ruled for him to 940 and made the throne the Sublime Threshold. He refused to arm Durayd's south, lost Caleran in 957 while his army was held on the northern wells by the Long Reach, and armed none of the amirates that followed until his death; his son spent the Horse War buying horses and building walls in the north.

**Gulnar** (fl. 931 AR) — Vashari-born engine feeder at Iqashar who stopped the walking Depth-Core engine, later the Elder, in the dye-yards by giving its feeding name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir": the formula behind the Sublime Threshold. Foundry masters said the beast drank itself quiet; the dyers' quarter credits her.

**Durayd ibn Sahl, Amir of the Southern Threshold** (fl. 931–957 AR; d. c. 971) — Amir of occupied Caleran who swore the south to himself when Idris's instruments widowed and offered to sell the Ember back in 948. In the funeral revolt of 957 he sent his guard to carry the Ember to his ships; its Covenant keepers, the third generation of them and born in the occupation, held it against him, eleven of them dying at the lamp. The dockers burned his ships, and he was taken alive in the harbour fort nineteen days later. The surviving keepers made the terms on which the city opened and left him outside them as the thief of a deposit. Caedros II kept him in a walled house at Veridun until his death, signing the releases of the southern garrisons that were sworn to him by name. Iqashari chroniclers say he tried to steal the one thing in Caleran that was not his and then signed away everything that was.

**The first Unnamed Custodian** (fl. 1003–1011 AR) — Custodian of the Vault of Names who pledged his true name to an Iqashari lending house to ransom a son taken by Tarkhan riders, leaving the army unable to march from 1006, then freed the Vault in 1011 by surrendering his name into its deepest custody. Registrars give him three former names. Every Custodian since has surrendered his name at investiture.

**Sultan-Caliph Tamim the Auditor** (r. 1029–1068 AR) — Wrote off the southern amirates in the Audit of the South (1033), divided the Vault of Names into the Living Register and the Deep Vault (1059), and chartered Mazrakan (1067).

**Sultan-Caliph Khurzad** (r. 1112–1142 AR) — Enforced the Court of Breaches' 1126 ruling for the Southern Houses with mercy towers and lost the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139) to Caedros III; his heir died of dysentery in the siege camp in 1135.

**Sultan-Caliph Suhrab the Debtor** (r. 1190–1243 AR) — A younger son who had taken the jurist's oath renouncing the throne and spent his youth as a clerk of his father's failed commission on the codes; the Court of Breaches voided his renunciation as coerced after the heir died at the Mazrakan fair in 1189. The apex. Instead of ranking the provincial codes he wrote the **Ledger of Ledgers**, a book of the occasions on which Covenant law had killed the Covenant's own people, each entered as a debt and closed by the clause owed; he read its first book, Bir Qassim, at the dry well in 1194, where Sealed-Blood mourners answered every clause *And yet the well was dry*, and the Iqashar crowds named him the Debtor. Its book of the Southern Deeds produced the Unburdened Deeds (1212) and so the **Battle of Qarath's Well** (1214), which was won on a sworn well's guest-peace while he sat at Iqashar; the well's bound warden named the price, a well for every cup, and the Court of Breaches told him that the Sultan-Caliph is not the house and could not lower it. His most gifted son, Prince Mihrzad, died of the Sweat in 1241 holding the frontier forts as a living witness, with only his Guard escort to see it; Suhrab's last book (1243), written so that the prince's name could be released, named the Guard's officer bench a competent witness for the Threshold, and every accession since has been made before it. The dynasty has been living off his capital ever since.

**Prince Mihrzad** (d. 1241 AR) — Suhrab's most gifted son. In the Emptying of the Benches he rode the Qarath-frontier forts as a living witness while their garrisons re-swore to the Threshold; the forts he reached held. His Guard escort carried the Sweat into three forts that had been clean, and he died of it at a well-fort where only that escort survived, so that his name could not be released until his father's last book. Frontier villages remember the rider who brought the Sweat; the Iqashar bazaar sings the Rider of the Benches.

**Sultan-Caliph Tahmur** (r. 1243–1268 AR) — Suhrab's surviving son, the first sovereign to accede before the Guard's officer bench; put down the Pageless Prince (1249–1252) and began paying the Black Wind in 1265.

**Prince Qaydar, the Pageless** (d. 1252 AR) — A lodged Qahirid prince whose register page burned in the plague; freed by the Court of Breaches' "no page, no lodging", he raised the Basin and was killed. "The Vault can lose a page; the grave cannot": after him the Cull of Brothers turned from the lodge to the knife and the blinding needle.

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq I** (r. 1268–1301 AR) — Paid Qarayel's "escort fees" until the horses' grace thinned in 1300; his courts voided the charters of Meshalem's returners (1289–1291).

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq II** (r. 1301–1349 AR) — Hired the Iron Horde after the Partition; his night raids brought on Orinel, the Crimson Pilgrimage and the loss of Zehrim. His death opened the Cull that made Marzuq III.

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III** (r. 1349 AR-present) — Reigns from Iqashar as Qahirid suzerain over a layered imperial order. Al-Qahur supplies sacred legitimacy, independent Qazmarid rejects his universal claim, and autonomous Zanthir pays contested tribute. Clever and indolent, he depends on Ahdanite officer-brokers and the Vault of Names. The Guard bench witnessed his accession in 1349 while the Cull killed two of his brothers and blinded a third. The youngest was lodged at Al-Qahur's insistence and lives in the walled garden there, his page in the Deep Vault. It costs him sleep. See [[Present-Day Courts and Crowns (1360 AR)]].

**Caliph Uwais, Custodian-of-the-Wells** — Sacred ruler of Al-Qahur whose acknowledgment legitimises the Qahirid throne. Politically constrained by Sword-Regent Nadir and the military households.

**Sword-Regent Nadir ibn Haraz** — Effective governor of Al-Qahur, balancing Ahdanite-descended military houses, irrigation councils, pilgrimage, and dependence on Uwais's sacred authority.

**Caliph-Wasi Zahran** — Sealed-Blood ruler of independent Qazmarid, claimant through Rumaitha bint Mujalib and asserted custodian of the Counter-Instrument.

**The Emirate of Zanthir** (601 AR–present) — First-wave conquest emirate turned last autonomous remnant: survivor of the Reconquest by timely tribute and timelier treachery (in 1044, with the coast closed to its barges by Thalmyric squadrons, it withdrew its grain from the last Covenant amirates and agreed to pay tribute to Veridun while the sea is open and to Iqashar while the caravans run), still standing as "the last pearl of the Banu Mizan." Under **Emir Badis al-Zanthiri** it pays tribute toward Iqashar and Veridun in alternating seasons, insures with the Medori, shelters Jirahar quarters, and is universally assumed to be the next war's first battlefield.

## Thalmyria

**Caedros I the Unbowed** (r. 922–957 AR) — founder-saint and rebel general. A commander of the mountain rebellion who promised to feed Caelmount after its cave-wardens turned their own granary into the grave of Amir Qazem's Covenant column (917–918), took Virelos and its granaries to keep the promise (921) and was crowned there in 922, then brought the rival refuge armies under one crown in the War of the Burning Banners (922–949). He died at the Pillar of Concordance in 957, on the day the Ember's Covenant keepers opened the forum precinct to his army after Amir Durayd's guard was driven from the lamp. Thalmyric witnesses said the White Flame took his body, leaving armor and sword; Eclessian relic courts reject the canonisation for want of remains and independent examination. The event completed the sacred objective of his reign but not the [[The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)|Long Reconquest]].

**Caedros II the Road-Maker** (r. 957–973 AR) — secured Caleran, rebuilt its walls, protected surrendered quarters, and opened the roads required for the next generations of province-by-province return. His engineers took the canal heads in the hills above the separately sworn amirates and set water-courts there, so that farmers who registered got their water and most amirs were left with walls above dry fields. He kept the captured amir Durayd at Veridun and had him sign the releases of the southern garrisons. He inherited a sacred victory and an unfinished war. He paid for both by restoring the Church's estates and filling its sees from the families who had paid for the return, and under him the war-lamps that had kindled before Caleran's walls kindled less and less often.

**Ilarion Aetherion** (r. 973–978 AR) — Caedros II's son, who sold the sees his father had endowed. The Breaking began in his reign, at Anavra (975). Died of a lung-fever at twenty-six; the army he had sent to protect the bishops acclaimed its commander.

**Varkas of Anavra** (fl. 975 AR) — A lamp-sergeant who had carried a kindled lamp before Caleran. When a bought chaplain's war-lamp stayed dark and Meliton's riders burned Anavra, he led the survivors forty miles to break it on the bishop's doorstep: "It did not burn for us. Show us one that does." The first Breaker.

**Tavoran III the Lampbreaker** (r. 978–1011 AR) — Vlastravan march commander and Caedros II's son-in-law, acclaimed at forty-three by an army that had just broken its own war-lamps, taking the regnal name of the old oath-holding emperors. As a young man he carried a war-lamp before Caleran and saw it kindle; in the decades after, he watched it go dark, and he broke it before his soldiers. He let the Breakers work and took back the sees' estates. When they came for the Great Lamp of Veridun (986) he had it hooded in bronze and told them the emperor had put out its eye. Soldiers loved him; monks recorded him as a demon; the reconquered valleys he left without funeral monks remember the dead who were not buried. He died in a room without a lamp, having asked to see under the hood and been refused.

**Dovrin of Velia, "the Potter"** (c. 950–1032 AR) — A Velian potter's son whose father followed the armies of the return north to the clay-pits of Keramion in the reconquered Aurin valley. He took vows in a march monastery, and when the Breakers emptied it he went home to the wheel and made cheap clay lamps stamped on the foot with forty sayings, the *Sayings for the Foot of a Lamp*. When the Breakers smashed the lamps the feet survived; households mortared them into their walls, children traded them to make up the forty, and the Synod received them in 1012. The Breakers pulled down his kilns twice, and he outlived the last of their bands. The best known saying answers their test: "nobody breaks a door because the guest is late."

**Anthe** (fl. 986 AR) — Caedros II's daughter and Tavoran III's wife. She had the bronze hood for the Great Lamp cast from the melted lamps of her father's court clergy.

**Arsen** (fl. 1049 AR) — Captain of the guard on the crown lamp-house in Veridun. Ordered to hold the door against a square full of households carrying lamps lifted from their wells, his mother among them, he broke the seal himself. The house was cold. The palace guard still swears its captains at that door.

**Tavoran IV** (r. 1011–1055 AR) — Tavoran III's son, acclaimed at thirty-five. Through the Breaking he carried his own war-lamp hidden in his saddle-bag; it never kindled in his hand. He opened crown lamp-houses where households could leave their lamps out of the Breakers' reach and created the office of Master of Lamps to keep them. After his Master of Lamps, his old companion Kallion, was burned to death forcing a wayhouse lamp before Meliton (1036), he made the houses compulsory and sealed every lamp but the hooded Great Lamp and the Ember in them: **the Impounding**. The keepers sold the oil. In 1049 he went into the opened house in Veridun and found his own lamp dead on the first shelf. He walked the first mile of the Rekindling barefoot (1052).

**Kallion, Master of Lamps** (d. 1036 AR) — Tavoran IV's companion of the saddle-bag years and the first Master of Lamps. Seized the wayhouse lamp of Pelin's Bridge to prove that a kept lamp could serve an army, forced it against the name-bound walls of Meliton before both armies, and was burned to death.

**Empress Korinna the Kindler** (r. 1055–1076 AR) — Tavoran IV's daughter. At twenty-four she walked to Caleran in a pilgrim's coat with the families whose lamps had died in the crown's houses, and came back with a lit lamp and a list of the people she had seen killed on the amirate roads. Her father gave her the southern armies; she took the last amirate's surrender and joined the roads for the **Rekindling** (1052), still Thalmyria's greatest liturgical day. The households called her the Kindler before she was empress.

**Caedros III, "the Wall of the World"** (r. 1121–1159 AR) — Raised in the frontier monasteries and a garrison captain before his crowning; a big, convivial man who danced at district weddings and stood godfather to more than four hundred district children. Married Chrysanthe, a Saronikan admiral's daughter, whose father's ships carried the settlers' grain; they had a son, Orestan, and three daughters. Resettled the frontier the Two Winters had emptied as military districts owing a lance and a lamp, and won the **War of the Southern Deeds** (1131–39) when the district lamps kindled in defence of their homes, his own among them at Kolonna (1134). After Orestan cut down surrendered men at Vresna (1137), he declared at Kolonna (1139) that his heir would be the claimant whose lamp the Fire kindled at his bier. Spent the Covenant's ransom and then Sestaran loans on High Noon's roads, towns and monasteries, leaving the treasury in debt. In his last years he sat up at night with the lamp that had kindled at Kolonna; it never kindled again. At his bier no claimant's lamp kindled, and the court acclaimed his son on a counterfeit flare.

**Orestan, "Hot-Coin"** (c. 1114–c. 1182 AR; acclaimed 1159, beaten 1162) — Caedros III's son, commander of the district horse, who had some four hundred surrendered Covenant levies cut down at the ford of Vresna (1137). Barred without being named by the Kolonna Declaration, he brought a lamp scripted with Shell-fire to his father's bier; an old warlock smelled it and was walked out, and the court acclaimed him. The districts fought him for three years and broke his companies under Kolonna. Asked what should be done with him, Despina of Kolonna said he should keep a grave, and Andrion set him to build and keep a chapel over the dead of Vresna, which he did for twenty years.

**Despina of Kolonna** (fl. 1159–1162 AR) — A district soldier's widow whose lamp kindled in the nave at Caedros III's funeral while the seven claimants' stayed dark or burned false. The court suppressed it; the district armies made it their warrant. She refused to return to court, and her one judgement was on Orestan.

**Andrion, "the Widow's Emperor"** (r. 1162–1198 AR) — Commander of the military districts, acclaimed by their armies after the War of the Seven Lamps. Inherited Caedros III's Sestaran debts and extended the district levy to pay them; his reign saw the Threshing Floor in Elenara.

**Markellos the Drinker** (r. 1209–1225 AR; d. 1231) — Andrion's grandson, raised in the districts. Marched in a drought summer to stop his frontier families accepting the Covenant's Unburdened Deeds. At Qarath's Well he heard Nikandros read the guest-peace on the curb aloud and drank anyway, last, from his men's bucket; the army sickened at dawn when it took up arms. He stayed with the men who drank. At sunset the well named its price, a well for every cup, and the Covenant's Court held that his cup was owed the Throne's well, the aqueduct head at Kolonna. He would not give it, and kept the water-sickness to his death. While he could not grip a pen his uncle Sergian pressed his seal to the Forfeiture of the Drinkers; he had himself carried out of the palace to Kedra and never sat in the Crown Hall again. In 1225 at Virelos he poured out a cup of water untasted and gave his claim to Nikandros. He died a monk at Kedra. The shame lies not only in defeat but in an empire proving it feared a debt more than an injustice.

**Sergian** (d. 1217 AR) — Markellos's uncle, the chancellor who kept the empty treasury and the seals. Pressed the sick emperor's seal to the Forfeiture of the Drinkers (1214), giving the farms of every district holder who had drunk at Qarath to hired companies, and after the emperor left the palace governed in his name and told Iqashar the debt had gone with him. Killed in the Crown Hall by drinkers' families who had walked to Veridun with their door-lamps.

**Nikandros I the Restorer** (r. 1225–1249 AR) — Of a cadet Aetherion branch in Kedrava, schooled in a refuge monastery; commanded the Kedravan monastic contingents at Qarath, read the well's guest-peace aloud to the emperor's council and was overruled, kept the refuge rule against drinking sworn water and withdrew in order with the wounded. Sheltered Markellos when he left his palace; in 1225 at Virelos Markellos poured out a cup of water untasted and gave him his claim for an oath to return the drinkers' farms, and he kept half of it by evicting Sergian's companies, violently. Every emperor crowned at Virelos since has poured out a cup. Crowned with the monasteries and Vlastrava behind him, he stabilised the rump. He asked nobody for help. In 1227 the Sanctum offered it unasked: the Crown of Embers on his head, Eclessian lances and the three Caldrian valleys, if the Great Lamp were carried east and tried by its relic court. He refused — *"a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned"* — fought on alone, and won back half the loss when Covenant garrisons marched home in the plague (1238–1246). He died three years after the last of them had gone, with the recovered districts still empty. Thalmyric liturgy still includes a line widely understood to mean *the East sold us for a doctrine.* Present-day Caedros IV claims the restoration line.

**Evandros the Sower** (r. 1249–1296 AR) — Nikandros I's son. Resettled the recovered districts and the plague-emptied interior with veterans and Kedravan families, the work of two generations; sent the embassy that failed to buy Gavrin the Farrier.

**Stavren the Patient** (r. 1296–1329 AR) — Evandros's son, who came late to the throne. Kept the peace on Sestaran credit, founded the war college at Vatrin, lost both sons to the summer flux of 1327 and adopted Caedros, the hero of Zehrim, who reigns in 1360 as Caedros IV.

## Eclessia

**Hedwen of Kaltfurt** (d. c. 1010 AR) — Markish miller's widow who took up the march banner at the ford of Kaltfurt (960) and held it between Grass-Tarkhan riders and a refugee column; the banner kindled and the raid broke. In 1006 she testified at Aurelium that it had never burned in Adalmar's hand; her testimony is in the register, struck through. Refused to carry the banner into the Folk Wars. Venerated by millers and in Markish reform country. The Primarch who heard her would not crown Adalmar while she lived; his successor's chancery struck her line through in 1013. The millers of Kaltfurt say they shut their sluices on the coronation of 1017.

**Adalmar the Great** (r. 961–1019 AR; crowned Emperor of the Atherians 1017) — Markish margrave's son, acclaimed king of the eastern marches after Kaltfurt. Joined the bishops' principalities by war, marriage and a single register, and granted frontier land held by Folk seer-oath to his Markish lords (the **Folk Wars**, 985–994). At the **Drowning at Wolfsmere** (993) he called the clans' oath-keepers and seers to their lake under safe-conduct to recite the grove boundaries, and when the recital contradicted his grants he had the four thousand drowned "to drown the oath with its keepers". The next winter the lake stood up and took his shore-fort, and his frontier stopped there; his lords called him the Drowner. In 1006 he asked the Sanctum to examine the Kaltfurt banner, and its court, under **Lord Primarch Valerin II**, credited the kindling to him over Hedwen's testimony; Valerin would not crown him. In 1017, two years after Veridun refused to unhood its Great Lamp, **Lord Primarch Octavin III** crowned him at the ford of Kaltfurt, seventy-six years old and standing in mud. He was the only emperor the east ever crowned. Eclessia's greatest hero and its deepest sin, in one man. (Authorial note: Wolfsmere is a standing leftward thin space, "the Lake That Remembers", and a live Grenzburg-adjacent hook.)

**Weylan Oath-Bent** (c. 950–1012 AR) — The Green March war-leader who took baptism in 987 and entered his clans' fields in the bishops' register to hold them by the settlers' law. In 993 he carried Adalmar's safe-conduct to the clans and watched under guard as the four thousand who trusted it were drowned. He neither renounced his baptism nor forgave: he sued the king in the bishops' own court, and the **Peace of the Bent Oak** (999) struck the grants south of the lake from the register and wrote the lake-law (no bell, no boat, no iron) into the march charters. To Eclessia, a trophy convert; to the Folk, "the Bent Oak," spoken with pity, contempt, and unbearable understanding in equal measure. Whether his baptism was submission, strategy, sincerity, or all three remains disputed among his descendants and rivals.

**Wolmar the Silent** (r. 1019–1034 AR) — Adalmar's surviving son, past fifty at his accession: devout, earnest and recorded silent at every examination the Sanctum held for him, so that he reigned as king and was never crowned emperor. His sons and lords bought examinations from rival sees and carried banners some of which had Shell-fire in the staff-head. At Drossfeld he asked for a blank tablet among the lots, and he died seven years later, a guest in Aldwin's house.

**The Blank Lot of Drossfeld** (1027 AR) — Wolmar's sons **Aldwin**, **Hartwin** and **Gervin**, unable to win, agreed that one name drawn from Adalmar's helmet should go to the Sanctum for examination and rule if his banner answered. A fourth tablet was left blank at Wolmar's asking, and the legate drew it. Each kept what he held that day: Aldwin the south with Aurelium and the keeping of the Crown of Embers; Hartwin the eastern march, kingless after his death in 1047 and joined to Virelia in 1064; Gervin the middle march, which held the existing Duchy of Caldria and fragmented within two generations into Brauvalis, Veridancia, the Korrheim valleys, Ironvale and the Sylvan Marches. The tablet listing the middle march's holdings was smudged along its eastern line; every present border dispute in Eclessia cites Drossfeld the way lawyers cite scripture, and the middle march's disputes begin with the smudge.

**Radomir I of Strazgorod** (fl. 1059–1064 AR) — Vesari prince gathering the river princedoms. When Grass-Tarkhan riders burned his hall in 1059 and its house-fire died, he sent his fire-keeper west to relight it from the Ember rather than become a neighbour's client. The lamp lit itself in her hands in Veridun and Makarios the Adamant kept it; unwilling to be Veridun's client under hearth-law, Radomir took baptism from the Sanctum and lit his new hall from a fire carried from its altar. In 1064 the kingless lords of the eastern march and the Vesari princes chose him king in a moot of sabres, the first king of Virelia.

**Zdena of Strazgorod** (fl. 1061 AR) — Radomir I's aunt and the fire-keeper of his house. Carried its dead hearth-lamp to Veridun to ask for a light from the Ember; when Makarios refused to light an unbaptised hearth, the cold lamp took the Flame in her hands before witnesses of both churches. She told him, "It chose my hand. I carried it," and stayed to tend it; her descendants still keep the Virelian Lamp in the cathedral close. Venerated in Virelian villages; on neither church's calendar.

**Ermbold the Idle** (fl. 1102 AR) — King of Aurelia of Aldwin's line and last Adalmarid keeper of the unworn Crown of Embers. In the first of the Two Winters he refused to carry it barefoot to the Sanctum and stand his examination ("It is a hat, not a relic") and sent it to the Primarch in a grain cart. He kept his kingdom. In 1104 the kings and dukes left the **Crown of Embers** with the Sanctum "in trust, until the realm is worthy of one head." That vault is the reunification mandate made physical: the Church literally keeps the empire in a box, and every king in Eclessia knows the Primarch could, in principle, take it out.

**Lord Primarch Octavin III & Cardinal Wigand of Caldria** (fl. 1013–1017 AR) — Octavin had Hedwen's testimony struck through, then offered Veridun the proof Oswin's trust required: lift the hood from the Great Lamp and let Aurelium examine it. Patriarch **Makarios the Adamant**, a flame-monk whose lamp had kindled in a hospice door at Sarmene (1009) and whom the Breakers had followed, would allow no test of the Fire by anyone. While the legate Wigand waited on the cathedral steps, Makarios nailed the hood to the lamp's stand with four nails, and both sides understood the reference to the Four Nails of the Pillar instantly. Wigand read the anathema to a shut door; Makarios answered from the doorway. The Great Split, 1015. Neither anathema has ever been lifted. Two years later Octavin crowned Adalmar. In 1052 Makarios drew his own nails and lifted the hood, and the Great Lamp was burning.

**Lord Primarch Gelasin II** (r. 1242–c. 1266 AR) — Elected by both colleges of the Schism of the Twin Cardinals; confirmed the Ferry Rule for the plague years only, kept the usurper's bishops and refused to rule on him; reorganised the old assurance letters into the Chancery of Sendings (1252), whose Sending Houses sang named rites for named dead.

**Aubert of Corvel, the Ferryman Primarch** (d. 1251 AR) — Cardinal and twin of **Aimery of Corvel**, who was elected Primarch in 1240 and died of the Sweat that night unsung. Aubert buried him secretly, wore his ring for twenty months and issued the **Ferry Rule** (1241), letting any baptised person sing the funeral where no priest lived. Exposed in 1242 when his brother's unsent figure was seen at the conclave garden wall; the colleges' quarrel over his acts is the **Schism of the Twin Cardinals**. Aurelian canonists call him the Usurper of the Ring; Brauvalis abbeys and the Freeflame, the Ferryman Primarch. [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|The Grey Sweat]]

**Saint Gerlind of Lindenfurt** (c. 1228–1279 AR) — Miller's daughter who sang the plague dead out for bread (*"the dead are named, not bought"*); acquitted by a relic court in 1266 on condition that she sing only with a priest present, the ruling from which the Lantern's city choirs and the Freeflame's lone country chaplains both descend; canonised by Alessior II, whose Chancery then printed her face on assurance letters. Claimed by the Sanctum and the Freeflame alike. [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|The Grey Sweat]]

**Lord Primarch Alessior II** (r. 1274–1289 AR) — A Malvora of Sestara orphaned by the Sweat, raised by the chapter that sang for his family, and as bishop known for singing haunted villages quiet; an ascetic who slept on a board. Bartolo Medori's credit bought his election without his asking, and he paid the bill. Told in 1282 that the Sending Houses could never sing what had been sold, he could not believe the Church had been allowed to promise what it could not perform, and his **Bull of the Sufficient Seal** (1283) taught that a letter sealed by the Chancery placed a soul in the Church's keeping before any rite was sung. That removed every limit on **routing assurances**. He signed letters by night with his own hand and gave them away as alms: in 1287 he had some four hundred thousand sealed without charge for Aurelia's plague dead, and parishes that had sung for their poor stopped singing. His treasurer Silvio Quarne ran the trade. Within a month of his death in 1289 the night clerks reported him still at his signing desk; the Sanctum denies it. The direct, proximate cause of the Reformation: Ansgar's First Grievance answers his bull, and the Grievances name him eleven times.

**Silvio Quarne, the Bridge-Seller** (c. 1238–1299 AR) — Alessior II's Cardinal-Treasurer; industrialised the assurance trade, staged the Saint-Leon apparition (1285), after which Gerlind's free singers were burned out of their quarter in Aurelium, and sold unsung letters to brokers as "dead paper". Died in Vorthara; his ledgers vanished.

## The Reformation

**Ansgar of Brenholt** (1260–1290 AR) — A marches friar and Vestibule-doctrine lecturer who came home as chaplain of the Brenholt Sending House, where his father sold the letters. From about 1284 he tallied in the margins of his psalter more than ten letters sold for every rite he was given time to sing (**Ansgar's Count**), sang the unsung names at night for nothing, and wrote beside them the notes he never sent: *"No seal of wax weighs anything at the Weighing. You have sold the poor a bridge that is not yours."* Because soul-routing is objectively real, this was not rhetoric but an accusation of metaphysical fraud — and everyone, including his judges, half-knew it. He drowned in the Blackwater pit in 1290 with forty-one miners, gone down to sing for the trapped. At their funeral the tally-keeper Irmel read his margins aloud in place of the rite, the widows answering each with Gerlind's *named, not bought*, and the hundred notes she chalked on the shift board are the **Brenholt Margins**. They went down the Weiss as a song, families buried their dead the Brenholt way, and the upper Weiss stopped buying letters. The **Synod of Aldersbruck** (1299), under the legate Tiburce Vaudrey, could not try a drowned man and tried his book: it put the psalter to the brazier, and it did not burn. The legate voided the test, vaulted the book and charged the Weiss country a second fee for its funerals. Ansgar was never tried or condemned. The reach below the broken bridge, where the Blackwater's drowned went into the river, is the Preacher's Water. **The Unburnt Psalter** sits in a lead vault under the Sanctum; its existence is officially denied and universally known — and in a world with a proof-of-sainthood protocol, both churches understand exactly why no one dares test it. A permanently loaded gun.

**Irmel of the Blackwater** (c. 1269–1344 AR) — Tally-keeper at the Blackwater pithead, to whom Ansgar gave his psalter before he went down. She read his margins over the drowned in 1290 and so led the first unbought funeral, was flogged at Aldersbruck in 1299 for keeping the book from his superiors, kept the Brenholt board through the Risings and never took Ferro's book for her rule. Her people give their dead to the Weiss and keep no churchyard, so the Night Count of 1342 had nothing to count, and they are the first of the **Unsworn**. *"The dead are named, not counted."* Reformed elders honour her and do not let her followers preach. [[Freeflame Reformation]]

**The Ashenmen Risings** (1306–1329 AR) — After five winters of distraint for the second fee, and the sale of the unpaid fees to Vortharan brokers, Ansgar's home region swore in the Weiss at the broken bridge and fought out of the pits and drifts of the upper Weiss for twenty-three years without one oath breaking: burning seals in the deep galleries, hanging collectors and Sending House chaplains from the winding gear, starving the iron country, and surviving the Caldrian drowning of the Brenholt drifts (1314). Eleven of them swore to the Mother's appearance at Sarnwell (1311) and went back to the war. Rainald burned the seals in 1329, and the bands came up into his army. The risings sit in and around the Ironvale Dominion, explaining both its existing underground-movement canon and the later guild-industrial vacuum that Grand Minister Aldric Weyn inherits.

**Doctor Lucian Ferro** (1279–1339 AR) — the movement's true architect, and never its member. A southern law clerk, junior notary at the Synod of Aldersbruck (1299), whose honest record of the unburnt psalter, the void ruling and the second fee was copied before anyone could stop it. As a Sanctum canonist at Veridance he was commissioned c. 1309 to set out the teaching of the unbought funerals, which nobody had yet written down, and refute it; the first half, **The Order of the Open Flame** (c. 1313), systematised it so fairly that the reformed towns adopted it whole: the Flame kindles wherever sanctity gathers — *"the Lord's Fire keeps no landlord"* — therefore no hierarchical mint, no assurance trade, no relic-court monopoly, congregational hymn-casting under tested elders. The refutation never came. Suspended and never tried, he sat as the Council of Veridance's unvoting witness for its five years (1319–1324), refused Rainald's invitation to Ironvale in 1330 (*"I wrote their case. I did not promise to plead it."*) and died at Veridance in 1339, buried in the university's own yard. Reformed duchies built on his book are famously orderly, literate, and merciless. **Duke Rainald of Ironvale** (duke 1309–1341), son of the **Duke Ortwin** in whose bridge-town the psalter was tried, fought the Ashenmen in his own mines for twenty years as an old-Church prince. In 1329, handed the Council's Arrears-levy and a roll showing his own father unsung, he burned Ironvale's Sending letters at Brenholt, ending the Risings. Rainald was the *last* Duke of Ironvale: the [[Freeflame Reformation|Wars of Reform]] cost his house its blood (his last son died at the churchyard gate of Falkendorf in 1340), its credit, and its Church standing, and the guilds filled the vacuum (the present Grand Ministry dates its charter to the house's extinction). Ferro is dead twenty-one years at present day; his book governs Ironvale, and his students teach on both sides.

**Lord Primarch Celestin IX** (r. 1317–1354 AR) — elected at thirty-eight; convened the **Council of Veridance** (1319–1324), summoned Ferro to deliver his refutation and kept him five years as the Council's witness, so that the Council answered his book line by line: it abolished assurance sales (too late), repudiated the Sufficient Seal and so ordered the Great Arrears (1320), codified the five sainthood tests (1324) and rebuilt seminary education. In 1324 it chartered **the Order of the Lantern** — teaching, missions, and quiet intelligence — from the outlaw night-singers of Aurelium's Saint-Leon quarter, who are older than the Reformation, and placed the Malverine prisons under its review. Their priest, **Father Anselin of Saint-Leon** (c. 1283–1315), carried as a child out of the quarter Quarne's riot burned in 1285 and later Ferro's student, had died untried in a Malverine prison-house nine years before. The lanterns were first carried because by day the singing was forbidden; the old Church kept the name as a claim that the Flame belongs in a vessel, carried where the Church wills. Celestin's Arrears-levy of 1326 provoked Rainald's Burning of the Seals and the princes' war.

**The Korrheim Accord** (1343 AR) — *Every roof under its lord's lamp, and every lamp where its dead lie.* Every parish yard went to the rite that had kept its dead sent, as the commissioners of the Night Count (1342) recorded it in the Schedule of Yards, and every recognised lord, whether duke, ministry, canton or chapter, keeps the lamp of his land's yards; no prince chose his confession. Sanctum canonists copied only the first half into their law-books, as *whose the realm, his the rite*. Built on the burial articles of the Falkendorf rising (1340), forced by the Grand Ministry's Iron Fast (1341–1342) and sworn with hands in the Weiss below the broken bridge at Aldersbruck, it leaves out the congregations that keep no yard, the Unsworn, first among them the river people who bury in the Preacher's Water; it binds no one's conscience and does not so much as mention the reunification mandate — a cold peace everyone signed and no one believes. Present-day Eclessia lives inside its silences and fights over its funerals.

## The Sestaran Merchant Age

**Bartolo Medori the Clearer** (c. 1215–1301 AR) — The Medori bought their seat in Fiorenzia's Roll of Faces in 1238, when the Grey Sweat had left nearly half its houses without a head. Bartolo built the bank into the peninsula's dominant credit house: he cleared the plague dead's estates, carried Sending payments, bought Alessior II's election (1274), unasked, because he had read the bishop's preaching on the seal, and took the Chancery registers as surety (1289). His ledger-maxim “lend to both, collect from the winner” is quoted as cynicism and practiced as policy.

**Lauro Medori of the Open Vault** (1289–1353 AR) — A mask-maker's son from Fiorenzia's river quarter, apprenticed to the counting-floors at twelve, who in 1311 married Bartolo's granddaughter and heir **Ginevra Medori** and took her name; she kept the inner books for half a century. The patronage apex: academies, galleries, the Fiorenzian flowering. In 1346, when the crowd came from the Pyre of Masks to the palace, he opened the vault against her ruling and the board's and gave the city its sheets of the Chancery registers. In 1349 the citizen households voted him the first hereditary Duke of Fiorenzia over the senate's head. The family has never agreed whether he saved the bank or gave away its best asset.

**Arrigo Medori** (duke from 1353 AR) — Lauro and Ginevra's son, the second hereditary Duke of Fiorenzia.

**Frate Severin of Fiorenzia** (c. 1312–1349 AR) — Flame-preacher. When the wax faces Fiorenzians keep of their dead began to speak, in the winter after the Sermoran "Bank of the Dead" pamphlet of 1345, he preached that the faces had become doors for the Hungers, which are shut by naming and singing the dead, never by fire. At the Feast of Souls in 1346 he begged the senate not to light the **Pyre of Masks**; it was lit, witnesses still argue about what they saw standing in that fire, and the crowd went to the Medori palace, where Lauro Medori gave him the city's sheets of the registers. Holding no office, he led the city's **Long Vigil** for four years, preached against the drowning of mask-makers and was not obeyed. In 1349 he preached against the citizens' gift of a hereditary chair to the Medori, and on the eve of the Feast of Souls he was found in the mask-makers' cellar, among the moulds of the burned faces, wearing a wax face nobody could lift from his own; he was buried in it outside the walls. In a world where the Flame is real, the question *was Severin kindled, proud or murdered?* has teeth: the Freeflame claim him, the Lantern studies him, Fiorenzia dreams about him.

**Taddeo Vetti** (c. 1278–1346 AR) — Sermoran-born Medori factor who kept the Bank's desks at Aurelium, Saronika and Qabir and wrote its factors a private guide to reading a sovereign before lending to one. His last desk failed in the Reform Wars defaults and the Bank sold him into Fiorenzia's Debtors' Corps (1336). In 1345 he sold a Sermoran printer the register extracts that became the "Bank of the Dead" pamphlet, and in the spring of 1346 he was found drowned in the dike he had been set to mend. The printer published his guide in 1347 as **Of Princes as Borrowers**: princes stripped of every sacred pretension and priced as borrowers. Banned by both churches, condemned by the Covenant, and found in the private shelves of every ruler on the board. Selections survive in [[Of Princes as Borrowers — Selected Maxims]].

**Ottavia Sele** (fl. 1328 AR) — Castaran salt-hauler pilot whose *Naris's Hand* crossed the Boiling Sea on a Breath day, bringing back the Viscaran chart-case that opened the route and Castara's rush of the 1330s.

**Duke Gaius Medori** — Present ruler of Fiorenzia and head of the Medori family, governing beside a senate whose old houses resent the purchased ancestry that made his title possible.

**Doge Terenzo Vescari** — Present compromise ruler of Sermora, balancing Admiralty houses, guilds, privateer captains, and the secrets accumulated by the Veiled Office.

**Duke Marcus Soran and General Aurelia Soran** — Present princely and military heads of Aversia. Their mercenary dynasty seized an office older Shield Houses still consider stolen.

**Pass-Keeper Salvi Corren** — Keeper of Vorthara's roads, gates, shared water stations, and external Concord seal. He is not ruler of the anarchic city.

**First Speaker Eliana Corvesi** — Head of Castara's Merchant Princes and advocate of continued Boiling Sea expansion despite labor unrest and contested foreign claims.

**Harbor Rector Sabina Valeris** — Present rector of Orsena, responsible for neutrality, cistern security, prisoner exchange, and the Harbor Book's exclusions.

**First Burgess Tomas Bellafor** — Presiding officer of Vardessa's guild benches and custodian of the neutral muster hall.

## The Steppe

**Qarayel, the Black Wind** (d. 1303 AR) — Herd-master of a minor Grass-Tarkhan clan, called by the name of his black stallion; united the banners in 1261 by stud-right under the Nine-Mane Standard; extracted escort fees, fair-ransoms and the grass-toll 1265–1300, and in 1289 gave Meshalem's return his word for a flat price per wagon; died undefeated; his old stallion, a grandson of the black colt, carried his body out of the camp unled, the grace-mares followed, and the herds came home on the eleventh day without him, in four bands to four pastures; grave unknown by policy. The assembly of 1304 named the four bands, each a grace-line, as the four Hordes. [[Qarayel and the Black Wind]]

**Gavrin the Farrier, the Grey Uncle** (c. 1215–1281 AR) — Velian bound craftsman taken from a plague-emptied Covenant well-fort; his blessing on a dying black colt (1249) renewed the horse-grace that made the Black Wind; refused freedom unless the camp's slaves were freed; murdered in 1281 (disputed). Saint Gavrin of the Halter in Thalmyria; the Wind-Saddler to Velian congregations.

## The Jirahar Thread

**The last River-King of Arqesh** (d. c. 676 BR) — Kassur III in the river records, remembered under incompatible names in Jirahar and Atherian ones. His empire had destroyed First Qiryath in 742 BR; his succession war and failed flood regime opened the return that produced Second Qiryath. He surrendered to Arezan in 688 and sealed the canal books to him.

**Rab Yequtiel of Zanthir, "the Witness at the Bed"** (c. 748–831 AR) — Jirahar physician who for fifty years witnessed the deathbeds of bound labourers in the bond-hospitals and salt pans of Zanthir's dry margin, where Covenant law required a witness before a name could be released. His *What the Register Keeps* argued from those beds that a register holds the name and lets the person go. Custodians quoted it to quiet the dying, Keepers of the Path denounced it as a licence for bondage, Velian copyists carried it into the belt's churches, and after the foundry's name-cages nobody in occupied Caleran could trust it.

**Meshalem the Twice-Turned** (c. 1260–c. 1316 AR) — A road-clerk of Migdal Melach who proclaimed himself the Mesakha in 1288 because a Black Wind ride had passed his wagons by; half the diaspora's tower towns sold their goods and took the return road under Qarayel's word, and Marzuq I's courts declared their charters forfeit behind them. At the Empty City in 1291 the Keepers asked him to lay his hand on the silent gate; twice he went down the stair before the whole return and twice came back without touching it. The return broke up with no homes to go to, and he swept the outer court as a mourner for a generation, until his death. The catastrophe — worse than any pogrom, because self-inflicted — explains the present-day Keepers' ferocious gatekeeping of prophecy and the resulting political doctrine: after Meshalem, every claimant is presumed false and none may approach the gate before examination, which is exactly the environment in which a true one would be missed. That irony remains central to Jirahar claimant stories.

**High Keeper Zerahiah** (present day) — First in dignity among independent Jirahar courts at Migdal Qesheth. He coordinates claimant scrutiny and gate cooperation without ruling the other towers. His greatest fear is another Meshalem; his greatest possible failure is refusing the true claimant for exactly that reason.

Several of the fourteen now carry their history openly in the era articles. The Saint of Ashen Wings is the Heldenberg slave courier whose revolt (c. 1116–1124) ended in the Oath of the Shafts and Korrheim's first cantons; he confessed the Cold Way experiment c. 1137 and died in exile in 1146 ([[High Noon and the Qarath Disaster]]). The Judge Beneath the Black Eaves ran warrants between Dvorna's two courts, and the trade in his forecasts hardened the Split ([[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]]). The Clerk of a Hundred Wells left frontier courts "the Clerk's ending", the date after which an emergency order must justify itself again, and Banu Mizan jurists claim him for their water law. The Preacher of the Second Gospel's counter-church sharpened the question of who may authenticate a sign and made bishops readier to let the emperor answer it.
