# Authorial Notes from Reader Articles — Imperial Age

Safeguards and rulings moved out of the public Atherian imperial-founding and Age of Glory articles during the November 2026 editorial cleanup; material already held in the Age of Glory, Imperial Founding and other control reviews was removed without duplication, and each item below keeps its original force.

- "Their later quarrels over Rome-like legitimacy cannot be projected backward into the first war-settlement leagues." Thalmyric–Eclessian legitimacy disputes are not to be read back into the war-settlement leagues. (The companion "no two ancient biological halves" ruling is already held in the World Historical Atlas ledger.)

## Causality Pass 03, 14 November 2026

The Star Pantheon's authorial truth was removed from the public Atherian religion files and replaced by an in-world quarrel among temple hymns, the Astral Keepers' Circle tables, Folk names for the same powers, and Jirahar and Redeemer teachers. The governing truth is restated in [[Age of Glory - Authorial History and Integration Review#Religion and supernatural identity]]: the Star Children are Menhir misread as a family; the Keepers' Circles are real observed groupings ([[The Second Circle]]; Locked Ruling on the Circles as Atherian ecological taxonomies), and the empty First Circle is Atherian theology ([[The Faewild Schism]]); Thanatos is the misclassified [[The Boatman]]. The moved sentences, each keeping its original force:

### [[Overview of the Astratheon Religion]]

- "Astratheon described several genuine powers through an inaccurate divine genealogy. Most of its Star Children corresponded to Menhir or powers encountered through their courts. Thanatos was an Atherian interpretation of [[The Boatman]], whose actual commission never permitted bribes, bargains over souls or membership in a celestial family. Political theology and received myth therefore reveal Atherian belief without settling the identities of the beings concerned."
- "These promises rested upon a false understanding of the Boatman. He carries human souls under a heavenly commission and cannot sell their judgement." Also: "Fraud arose when priests sold authority they did not have."

### [[Astratheon Religious Myths]]

- "Their kinships and divine titles belong to that tradition. Thanatos is the clearest error: the stories concern [[The Boatman]], whose true commission permits no bargaining over souls. Some events may preserve encounters with Menhir courts or Idol Masks, while others are civic fiction. Similar imagery provides no proof that every telling records the same manifestation."
- Zharok: "No known creature has been established as the literal Zharok."
- Hecatara at the Shadowed Crossing: "Its central bargain is theologically false: the Boatman never transferred custody of the dead."
- The Dance of Life and Death: "The actual Boatman lends no human soul to a god of inspiration. A surviving song may carry memory, love or residue without becoming the person it recalls."
- Thanatos and the Prince of Thieves: "…even though its picture of a bargaining Boatman was false."
- Verethis: "no evidence securely identifies the city or proves that it remains inhabited."

### [[Atherian Astrology System]]

- "The system records Atherian doctrine rather than a reliable anatomy of fate. Thanatos was the imperial name given to [[The Boatman]], who exercises no astrological dominion. The other signs gather cultural interpretations around real Menhir powers without proving that those powers assigned individual destinies."
- "These traits belong to Atherian astrology; the actual Boatman neither distributes them nor governs thieves."

### [[The Monomyth According to the Atherians]]

- "The resulting epic justified Atherian empire while preserving distorted memories of genuine liberation."
- "This is Astratheon theology rather than an account of creation. Humanity was created by the Lord. The Sun and Moon traditions concern Menhir powers, and the Giants arose from several kinds of direct and inherited descent. The poem nevertheless preserves the historical connection between Giant astronomy, dynastic power and the control of human lives."
- "Historical Giant society was a many-state world divided by marriage, trade, war and rival ancestry. Its rulers exploited human beings through different institutions. Their own civil wars and failed continuity systems helped bring the age to an end."
- "The received story transfers the Lord's commission to the Star Children. It also compresses the Unbinding's human revolt, Giant civil wars, plagues and heavenly severance into the acts of one celestial magician."
- "The historical Glass was already present and remains unexplained. The Lord sustained the remnant there through a long sojourn, during which people drawn from many Giant territories became the One Glass People. Other human societies survived outside the migration. The poem's surviving dissenters may preserve confused memories of both internal conflict and those Orphaned peoples."
- "This episode reworks the failed Glass sanctuary and the Prophet's death into a judgement by the stars… No Star priest inherited the Prophet's commission, and the cause and circumstances of his death remain disputed."
- "This conclusion turns a long historical formation into sacred election. The southern settlers separated gradually from the Near-Glass continuum. Their descendants conquered, allied and intermarried with existing societies before becoming Atherian. Proto-Jirahar and proto-Ahl-e-Ahd peoples developed through a later northern fission, while Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Nsizwa histories remained independent of the Glass migration."
- "It also rewrote the Lord as a celestial family…" and "Its theological claims remain those of Astratheon."

### [[The Common Sky and the Formation of Astratheon]]

- "The claim that the stars had commissioned humanity's conquests belonged to the religion. It did not establish a divine command for settlement or empire. Stories about celestial parentage also brought several distinct kinds of power into one supposed family."
- "a Star Child who supposedly guided the dead"; "promises which the priests could not fulfil."
- "The cult's explanation of the Boatman was false. He was not a child of the stars, and payments could not alter his routing of human souls. Elaborate funerals therefore reveal the beliefs, wealth and relationships of their participants without proving the afterlife promised by the officiants."

### [[The Atherian Empire Overview]]

- "Most of the named powers were Menhir understood through Atherian astronomy and local cult traditions. The figure called Thanatos arose from a different misunderstanding: priests incorporated signs of [[The Boatman]] into tales about theft and bargaining for passage after death."
- "Their stories of theft and payment were cult teachings, distinct from the Boatman's actual work."

## Causality Pass 10A, 14 November 2026

The founding articles were rewritten by [[Causality Pass - 10A Bending the Founding]]. They tell the supernatural turns through what witnesses saw and said. The authorial truth behind each is held in [[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review#Causality Pass 10A amendment (14 November 2026)]] and is not repeated in reader prose.

- [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]] and [[Seluc Varroth]]: soldiers say the Red Star "was finding its own contests" after the Writing-In, and the augurs call it disorder. Authorially the Writing-In broke Astaroth's substitution taboo and widened the failure clause of 370 as one cause among several; Venn's armed stand was a genuine contest and no breach.
- [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]], [[Samyra Nerethan]] and [[Darion Velsar]]: the towns "said the Provider had come back". Authorially the bitten wax of 352 is a genuine Feastling sign of a Gluttony opening around the Salt Field's stores, not the Bronze-Faced Provider, and it weakened at the unranked meal of summer 350.
- [[Seluc Varroth]]: Celebrants maintain that the Field "had never been closed, only emptied". Authorially the 370 contest ended with its last named party and the towns' judgment of the Nerethan house; Varroth's refusal and the Red Star priests' declaration are both human readings.
- [[Marelith]]: survivors say the bearer "had been freed of the city rather than killed". The bearer's fate stays authorially uncertain; the chain broke the founding covenant's terms, and nothing more is fixed.
- [[The Atherian Empire – Age of Glory]] and [[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]]: the painted board is told as the origin of the accession portrait. Its place in the Pride Mask's formation is held in [[Age of Glory - Authorial History and Integration Review#Religion and supernatural identity]].
- [[Aurelius Thalion]]: the line that he "learned to fear the Red Star" is his own fear; nothing in the grammar threatened a man who never accepted a field.
