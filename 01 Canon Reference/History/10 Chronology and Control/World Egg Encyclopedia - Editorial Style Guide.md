# World Egg Encyclopedia — Editorial Style Guide

This guide establishes the approved reference voice and editorial structure of the World Egg encyclopedia. It was first applied to the sixteen Tidewood articles and subsequently expanded for a library containing histories, biographies, peoples, places, institutions, supernatural beings, creatures, systems and received tales. It governs presentation and editorial review; controlling history and cosmology govern facts. [[Tidewood Historical Development - Authorial Review]] records the first application and [[World Egg Encyclopedia - Standards and Corpus Quality Audit]] records the first library-wide assessment.

## Approval and application

The author approved the Tidewood revision on 3 September 2026 as the standard for subsequent encyclopedia writing and revision, specifically endorsing its clarity, fuller explanations and removal of habitual corrective contrasts and other formulaic narration.

Apply this guide to all new reader-facing encyclopedia articles and to existing articles as they are revised. Use the Tidewood package as the worked example. Reference framing follows this standard; songs, dialogue, received tales and in-world documents retain the voices appropriate to their forms. Technical authorial controls continue to preserve the precise rulings behind the readable account.

The editorial standard is approved for general use. This approval does not record a completed rewrite of the remaining corpus or change any established lore.

## Research basis

### Published Britannica practices

Britannica Education describes specialist authorship, verification against reputable sources, continuing review and transparency about contributors and revisions. Its editorial organisation includes subject editors, fact-checkers, copy editors, supervisory editors and information architects. These are documented editorial practices, rather than a prescription for a sentence pattern. [Britannica Education, “How We Work”](https://britannicaeducation.com/about-us/), consulted 3 September 2026.

The publisher's 2025 Academic guide describes article contents, related reading, contributor information and revision history. These support navigation and accountability alongside the main account. The existing World Egg reader already provides contents and related-article navigation; this prose pass requires no software changes. [Britannica Academic Guided Tour](https://sek.euba.sk/www_write/files/eiz/Britannica_Academic_Guided_Tour.pdf), pp. 8–9.

### Observed article techniques

The accessible 1911 entries for Falmouth and Cornwall begin with physical orientation and develop their subjects through geography, economic activity, institutions and history. Falmouth's account explains the relationship between sheltered water, shipping and the port's development. Cornwall receives a broader topical treatment suited to a region. Their useful example is explanatory depth organised around the subject. [Falmouth](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Falmouth); [Cornwall](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Cornwall_%28England%29).

These are historical examples from the 1911 edition. Their vocabulary, factual claims and social judgements require historical context and are not adopted as present editorial authority. Modern main-site articles were inaccessible through the research tool during this review. No public internal Britannica stylebook was located. The rules below are our own Britannica-informed adaptations.

### Wikipedia practices useful at library scale

Wikipedia's three core content policies are neutrality, verifiability and the exclusion of unpublished original research. Its Manual of Style also treats the lead as a self-contained account of a subject's identity, context, importance and principal controversies. These rules belong to a collaborative reference work about the real world and cannot be transferred literally to an original setting. Their structural disciplines remain useful. [Wikipedia, “Core content policies”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Core_content_policies); [Wikipedia, “Manual of Style/Lead section”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Lead_section), consulted 5 September 2026.

Wikipedia's “summary style” places a concise account in a broad article and fuller treatment in linked child articles. Each child remains intelligible when reached directly. Its linking guidance similarly asks whether following a link would materially improve understanding and warns that excessive linking can obscure the useful connections. [Wikipedia, “Summary style”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Summary_style); [Wikipedia, “Manual of Style/Linking”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Manual_of_Style/Linking), consulted 5 September 2026.

The following adaptations govern the World Egg:

- **Neutrality becomes authority-weighted narration.** The encyclopedia states authorially fixed history directly. Partial memories, religious interpretations and disputed traditions are attributed to their holders and receive space according to their historical importance. Equal space is unnecessary when the evidence or controlling canon is unequal.
- **Verifiability becomes provenance.** A substantive statement must derive from controlling canon, an approved promotion or a recorded historical expansion. Reader prose need not carry research apparatus in every paragraph; the authorial review must preserve the decision and its sources.
- **No original research becomes no silent invention.** Worldbuilding necessarily creates new material. A voice edit may not create a fact, motive or relationship merely to improve a paragraph. Historical expansion identifies its inventions and passes chronology, geography, identity and collision review before becoming canon.
- **Notability becomes article-worthiness.** A subject deserves a separate page when it has a distinct history, function, relationship network or body of consequences that cannot be explained adequately in its parent article. A name, creature variant or minor office alone is insufficient. Material below this threshold belongs in a parent article, list or glossary.
- **Due weight becomes historical proportion.** Space follows the subject's importance, explanatory need and surviving knowledge. A famous battle may receive less space than the food system that sustained a civilisation. A disputed cult claim does not dominate a biography unless it changed that person's life or reputation.

Wikipedia's article-development guidance distinguishes stubs, developing articles and reviewed mature work. This encyclopedia uses the scoring rubric below for the same practical reason: canonical status establishes authority, while editorial maturity describes readability and completeness. [Wikipedia, “Article development”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Article_development), consulted 5 September 2026.

## Reader and voice

Write for an interested adult who may have arrived directly at the article. Use a third-person reference voice, explaining unfamiliar places, work and institutions. British English is the default; preserve proper names and deliberate speech in received tales.

Open by identifying the subject, its place or period, and its significance. Explain an unfamiliar office or practice when it first matters. A link should deepen an explanation already begun on the page. The reader should understand the opening without visiting several other articles.

Develop paragraphs through connected information. Describe how harbour shelter affects work, how that work supports neighbouring households, and how these dependencies shape a league. Vary sentence length with the material. Give actions precise agents and use concrete descriptions wherever the sources permit them.

Generosity means sufficient context. It requires no word quota, ornamental introduction, invented motive or new fact. Keep physical detail, humour and human particularity. Remove repeated conclusions that merely tell readers how to judge what was just described.

## Leads and stand-alone reading

Every reader-facing article begins with unheaded prose. The opening identifies the subject in ordinary terms, locates it in place or time where relevant, and explains its principal importance. A reader arriving through search should understand what kind of thing the subject is before encountering a specialised name, dispute or chronology.

A developed article's lead normally occupies one to three paragraphs and summarises the main body. Fifty to 180 words will suit most World Egg articles; the subject determines the final length. Brief local entries may use less when the first sentence supplies a complete definition. Long histories and major cultures may use more. The lead contains no important claim that the body fails to develop.

Received tales may place an authority notice before the historical setting, and source texts may open with an epigraph. These are deliberate forms, not substitutes for orientation. The reference introduction must still tell the reader where the tradition belongs, who carries it and why it matters.

Links supplement the lead. They never carry its missing definition. Avoid beginning with a list, unexplained quotation, technical classification or section heading.

## Sentence and paragraph review

- Replace habitual corrective contrasts with direct descriptions of what a subject does and how it works.
- Preserve substantive negatives: refused ransom, failed rescue, absent records and a person who never returned.
- Expand compressed labels where established facts explain their meaning. An account of provisioning, repairs and pilotage is more useful than unexplained “overlapping shore-rights”.
- Review rhetorical questions, repeated three-part lists, one-sentence moral endings and conspicuously balanced aphorisms. They may belong to a song, joke or speaker; avoid making them the narrator's recurring manner.
- State who sold captives, ordered executions, set false lights or destroyed an occupied building. Preserve affected people's work, relationships and subsequent lives.
- Attribute remembered sayings and disputed versions at the point of use. Do not invent quotations or historical witnesses to support a rewrite.

Searches flag passages for contextual reading. They never replace judgement with automatic substitutions or a ban on particular words.

## Summary architecture and article size

Broad articles explain the whole subject in proportion. Detailed articles deepen particular periods, people or institutions. When material receives a child page, the parent retains a concise account of what happened, why it matters and how it relates to the larger subject. A bare list of links is not a summary.

Child articles repeat enough context to stand alone. They should not open midway through a dispute whose actors and stakes appear only in the parent. Necessary repetition between parent and child is preferable to a reader losing the subject.

Very long pages should be divided when several sections have acquired independent histories or when their detail obscures the main account. Very short pages should be merged when expansion would merely repeat a parent. Length alone decides neither case. Wikipedia treats approximately 8,000 words as a point for considering division; the World Egg adopts this as a review signal, not a hard limit. [Wikipedia, “Article size”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Article_size), consulted 5 September 2026.

## Article forms

The encyclopedia uses a common voice and different structures. Headings follow the questions a reader brings to that class of subject.

### Peoples and cultures

Identify the people, principal homelands and historical formation. Distinguish ancestry, language, political membership and religion. Explain regional variation, household organisation, authority, livelihood, technology, neighbours, faith and the circumstances of 1360. Cultural self-descriptions and hostile stereotypes require attribution. An overview must not present one capital, elite or religious faction as the entire people.

### Regions, settlements and polities

Locate the place through rivers, coasts, roads, neighbouring country and terrain. Explain why settlement developed there, who lives there, how food and work sustain it, how authority operates and how its history produced the present landscape. A polity article also covers government, territorial reach, dependencies and disputed borders. A settlement article gives scale through functions and relationships instead of unsupported population precision.

### Historical periods and civilisations

Open with the period's boundaries, principal transformation and geographic scope. Develop political change alongside population, economy, technology, households, religion, art and relations with neighbouring peoples. Major eras need both a chronological spine and thematic explanation. A list of rulers or battles cannot carry the civilisation alone.

### Events, wars and migrations

State when and where the event occurred, who participated and what changed. Then explain antecedents, aims, preparation and resources, the course of events, losses and consequences. Later memory belongs after the event itself unless misunderstanding shaped the action while it occurred. Military accounts include supply, civilians, captives and political disagreement where these matter.

### Biographies

The lead gives the person's dates or approximate period, culture, principal offices or occupation, and historical significance. The body explains family and formation, relationships, work, pleasures, ambitions, decisive choices, responsibility, death or disappearance, and legacy. Reputation follows the life and its evidence. Saints, sinners, rulers and heroes remain people rather than catalogues of powers; supernatural afterlives receive a separate section and retain the required identity distinctions.

### Dynasties, houses, institutions and organisations

Explain origin, membership, purpose, resources, leadership and means of action. Follow changes across time and the relationships that gave the institution power. Offices require a description of selection, practical duties and limits. A faction page should contain an institutional history, not only goals, ranks and campaign hooks.

### Religions, cults and supernatural powers

Separate the being or power from human worship, iconography and doctrine. Begin with the identity knowable at the article's visibility level, then explain manifestations, relationships, places, worship, historical encounters and competing interpretations. Technical continuity and commission controls belong in the authorial review unless the distinction is itself necessary for reader understanding.

### Creatures and peoples of the other realms

Define origin, physical form, range, behaviour, reproduction or formation, intelligence, social relations and ecological consequences. Encounter behaviour may appear when it follows from the creature's life; tactical instructions and game statistics belong in a separate game-use or authorial article. A sapient people receives the social and historical depth afforded to human societies. A non-personal phenomenon should not be written as a nation or moral actor.

### Magic, technologies and systems

Explain what the practice is, who can use it, how it is learnt, what materials and labour it requires, what it reliably accomplishes and where it fails. Include its historical development and social consequences. Rules summaries may follow the account. They do not replace an explanation of how the practice appears in ordinary life.

### Objects, relics and works

Identify maker or origin where known, physical description, original use, later ownership and historical effect. Separate demonstrated behaviour from claims made on its behalf. A relic's response proves the scope of that response alone.

### Received tales and source texts

Provide a reference introduction, retain the full story or relevant text where the article preserves a telling, then explain performance, audience, customs, variants and historical setting. Dialogue, repetition and comic timing follow the work's own needs. The tradition's canonical existence remains distinct from the literal truth of every narrated event.

### Indexes and overview pages

Offer a readable map of the subject before the links. Group entries by a principle the reader can understand and state the relationship among them. Avoid link directories whose headings and bullet lists require prior knowledge of the setting.

## Reader prose and authorial controls

Keep uncertainty meaningful to readers in the article: conflicting genealogies, divergent songs, missing lives and uncertain identities. Use ordinary attribution rather than repeated authority labels.

Move design safeguards, continuity tables, collision rulings, campaign prompts and post-1360 restrictions into linked authorial controls. Their force remains unchanged. Preserve visibility metadata; rearranging a page does not authorise wider disclosure.

Established harms remain in readable history. Moving a design note must never remove the sole account of a crime, a victim or an irreversible loss.

Campaign prompts, encounter instructions, collision rulings, promotion gates and future design questions do not belong in reader-facing encyclopedia prose. Preserve them in an authorial review or game-use page linked through metadata or the relevant control index. Restricted visibility may protect a secret, but it does not turn a design note into an encyclopaedia article.

## Editorial workflow and provenance

Britannica describes distinct work by subject editors, fact-checkers, supervisory editors, copy editors and information architects, followed by continuing review and transparent identification of sources and revision. One author may perform several of these roles for the World Egg, but the passes remain separate. Combining them encourages elegant sentences to conceal factual invention or technically correct notes to remain unreadable.

Every substantial delivery therefore completes these passes in order:

1. **Subject and authority review:** establish the controlling canon, visibility and scope.
2. **Fact and continuity review:** check dates, geography, ancestry, relationships, soul and realm rules, and neighbouring articles.
3. **Coverage review:** check political, material, social, religious and ordinary-life dimensions appropriate to the subject.
4. **Copy-edit:** apply the house voice, direct syntax, British English and developed paragraphs.
5. **Information architecture:** test the lead, headings, parent summary, child independence, links, aliases and article-worthiness.
6. **Technical validation:** check frontmatter, stable anchors, navigation and rendering.
7. **Revision record:** name the reviewed files, controlling sources, substantive changes, unresolved questions and date of review in an authorial ledger.

The public article need not display a scholarly apparatus for the fictional world. The control record must make the origin of important claims recoverable. External historical or mythological research remains in research dossiers; the resulting World Egg fact cites its promotion decision rather than presenting a real source as evidence that the fictional event occurred.

Maps, diagrams and illustrations serve explanation. Captions identify what is depicted, its date or authority and any meaningful uncertainty. Decoration alone never compensates for a weak account.

## Editorial maturity score

Canonical authority and editorial maturity are separate. Score a reviewed article or coherent package from 1 to 5:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **1 — unusable** | Contradictory, contextless or primarily a private design fragment. A reader cannot reliably identify the subject. |
| **2 — outline** | Contains useful canon but depends on lists, jargon, campaign prompts or other pages for its basic meaning. Requires structural rewriting. |
| **3 — serviceable** | Accurate and useful, with an intelligible subject and some developed prose. Important dimensions, transitions, lead work or source separation remain incomplete. |
| **4 — strong** | Self-contained, well organised, proportionate and readable. Only local expansion, illustration or minor reconciliation remains. |
| **5 — exemplary** | Combines strong prose, complete appropriate coverage, exact authority, excellent navigation and a recorded multi-pass review. It can serve as a model for its article class. |

The score is diagnostic, not a judgement of the underlying idea. An article may remain canon at score 2 and require rewriting; a beautifully written research draft does not become canon through a score of 5.

## Editorial checks

Compare revisions with the working files captured at the start, including uncommitted work. Review facts, dates, names, relationships, geography, causality and uncertainty separately from style. Record genuine gaps instead of silently filling them.

Preserve filenames, aliases and stable block anchors. Retain heading destinations or update affected references when sections move. Check frontmatter, wikilinks and rendered articles; rerun the Atlas validator and whole-vault compatibility report.

A successful article lets a new reader identify the subject, understand how it worked and follow what changed without depending on the authorial ledger. It also remains consistent with its parent summary, gives proportionate attention to its major aspects and has enough substance to justify its separate page.
