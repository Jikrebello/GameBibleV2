# World Egg Encyclopedia — Editorial Style Guide

This guide establishes the approved reference voice for the World Egg encyclopedia, first applied to the sixteen Tidewood articles. It governs presentation and editorial review; controlling history and cosmology govern facts. [[Tidewood Historical Development - Authorial Review]] records the first application and its checks.

## Approval and application

The author approved the Tidewood revision on 3 September 2026 as the standard for subsequent encyclopedia writing and revision, specifically endorsing its clarity, fuller explanations and removal of habitual corrective contrasts and other formulaic narration.

Apply this guide to all new reader-facing encyclopedia articles and to existing articles as they are revised. Use the Tidewood package as the worked example. Reference framing follows this standard; songs, dialogue, received tales and in-world documents retain the voices appropriate to their forms. Technical authorial controls continue to preserve the precise rulings behind the readable account.

The editorial standard is approved for general use. This approval does not record a completed rewrite of the remaining corpus or change any established lore.

## Research basis

### Published Britannica practices

Britannica Education describes specialist authorship, verification against reputable sources, continuing review and transparency about contributors and revisions. Its editorial organisation includes subject editors, fact-checkers, copy editors, supervisory editors and information architects. These are documented editorial practices, rather than a prescription for a sentence pattern. [Britannica Education, “How We Work”](https://britannicaeducation.com/about-us/), consulted 3 September 2026.

The publisher's 2025 Academic guide describes article contents, related reading, contributor information and revision history. These support navigation and accountability alongside the main account. The existing World Egg reader already provides contents and related-article navigation; this prose pass requires no software changes. [Britannica Academic Guided Tour](https://sek.euba.sk/www_write/files/eiz/Britannica_Academic_Guided_Tour.pdf), pp. 8–9.

### Observed article techniques

The accessible 1911 entries for Falmouth and Cornwall begin with physical orientation and develop their subjects through geography, economic activity, institutions and history. Falmouth's account explains the relationship between sheltered water, shipping and the port's development. Cornwall receives a broader topical treatment suited to a region. Their useful example is explanatory depth organised around the subject. [Falmouth](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Falmouth); [Cornwall](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/1911_Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica/Cornwall_%28England%29).

These are historical examples from the 1911 edition. Their vocabulary, factual claims and social judgements require historical context and are not adopted as present editorial authority. Modern main-site articles were inaccessible through the research tool during this review. No public internal Britannica stylebook was located. The rules below are our own Britannica-informed adaptations.

## Reader and voice

Write for an interested adult who may have arrived directly at the article. Use a third-person reference voice, explaining unfamiliar places, work and institutions. British English is the default; preserve proper names and deliberate speech in received tales.

Open by identifying the subject, its place or period, and its significance. Explain an unfamiliar office or practice when it first matters. A link should deepen an explanation already begun on the page. The reader should understand the opening without visiting several other articles.

Develop paragraphs through connected information. Describe how harbour shelter affects work, how that work supports neighbouring households, and how these dependencies shape a league. Vary sentence length with the material. Give actions precise agents and use concrete descriptions wherever the sources permit them.

Generosity means sufficient context. It requires no word quota, ornamental introduction, invented motive or new fact. Keep physical detail, humour and human particularity. Remove repeated conclusions that merely tell readers how to judge what was just described.

## Sentence and paragraph review

- Replace habitual corrective contrasts with direct descriptions of what a subject does and how it works.
- Preserve substantive negatives: refused ransom, failed rescue, absent records and a person who never returned.
- Expand compressed labels where established facts explain their meaning. An account of provisioning, repairs and pilotage is more useful than unexplained “overlapping shore-rights”.
- Review rhetorical questions, repeated three-part lists, one-sentence moral endings and conspicuously balanced aphorisms. They may belong to a song, joke or speaker; avoid making them the narrator's recurring manner.
- State who sold captives, ordered executions, set false lights or destroyed an occupied building. Preserve affected people's work, relationships and subsequent lives.
- Attribute remembered sayings and disputed versions at the point of use. Do not invent quotations or historical witnesses to support a rewrite.

Searches flag passages for contextual reading. They never replace judgement with automatic substitutions or a ban on particular words.

## Article forms

**Regions and settlements:** identify location and character, then explain settlement, government, livelihood, religion and development in an order suited to the place. The regional overview must remain useful on its own. Settlement articles should explain physical relationships and distinctive work.

**History and events:** establish period, circumstances and participants before following actions and consequences. Supply missing transitions. Linked accounts may differ in detail but must agree on facts.

**Biographies:** identify period, occupation and significance; develop circumstances, relationships, decisions and later life. Explain reputations through the people and events that produced them. Preserve culpability and achievement without compulsory balancing verdicts.

**Kindreds and ordinary life:** explain membership, authority, labour and use through examples. Affection, recreation, learning and pleasure have their own place and need no apologetic introduction after violence.

**Beliefs and supernatural encounters:** attribute customary beliefs and disputed identifications. State established supernatural events at their existing authority level. The reference voice imposes neither automatic scepticism nor automatic authentication.

**Received tales:** provide a reference introduction, retain the full story where the article preserves a telling, then explain performance, customs and variants. Dialogue, repetition and comic timing follow the tale's own needs.

## Reader prose and authorial controls

Keep uncertainty meaningful to readers in the article: conflicting genealogies, divergent songs, missing lives and uncertain identities. Use ordinary attribution rather than repeated authority labels.

Move design safeguards, continuity tables, collision rulings, campaign prompts and post-1360 restrictions into linked authorial controls. Their force remains unchanged. Preserve visibility metadata; rearranging a page does not authorise wider disclosure.

Established harms remain in readable history. Moving a design note must never remove the sole account of a crime, a victim or an irreversible loss.

## Editorial checks

Compare revisions with the working files captured at the start, including uncommitted work. Review facts, dates, names, relationships, geography, causality and uncertainty separately from style. Record genuine gaps instead of silently filling them.

Preserve filenames, aliases and stable block anchors. Retain heading destinations or update affected references when sections move. Check frontmatter, wikilinks and rendered articles; rerun the Atlas validator and whole-vault compatibility report.

A successful article lets a new reader identify the subject, understand how it worked and follow what changed without depending on the authorial ledger.
