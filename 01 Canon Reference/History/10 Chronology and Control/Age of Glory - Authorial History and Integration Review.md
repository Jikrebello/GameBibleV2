# Age of Glory — Authorial History and Integration Review

This review records the canonical development of the Atherian Age of Glory from Thalion's settlement around 350 BR to the Redeemer boundary at 0 AR. It follows the authorised imperial-founding pass and completes the historical scope originally assigned to Package 2C. Package 3 retains the Redeemer, Underground, Conversion, later empire and Long Frontier.

Fifteen reader and reference articles establish the political sequence, provincial society, army, economy, learning and religion. Existing Garden articles remain unchanged. Revised Block III material is incorporated through separate historical destinations rather than being compressed into one imperial legend.

## Authority and sources

The controlling chronology fixes the Unification around 350 BR and the Redeemer's death and resurrection at 0 AR. The preceding [[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review]] controls the founding generation. The established culture, geographic and cosmological articles govern neighbouring peoples, routes and supernatural identities.

The historical structure draws upon broad patterns of the Roman principate and later imperial centuries: dynastic succession, adoption, civil-war claimants, provincial municipal government, professional armies, slavery, imperial grain systems, roads, public religion and the movement of citizenship beyond the founding city. These are structural grammars rather than a new one-to-one chronology. The fictional houses, events and relationships are World Egg inventions.

Thalion dies naturally in 318, at the table on Sarqen's landing, with Meren already recognised by the army and the governors. *(Amended by [[Causality Pass - 10A Bending the Founding]]: Meren was formerly the son of Evara and Velsar, adopted.)* Meren is Thalion's own younger son by Merath Nerethan, elder sister of Samyra, born at Esharat in 358; he becomes heir by Caelor's death in 324 without adoption, and through him the House of Thalion carries Nerethan descent. Valeran I is the house's only adoption. From 350 BR to 0 AR, imperial command passes among men. Adoption can carry a claim through a daughter's household or bring in a proven commander. Cassara, Tessa, Mariel and Lethara exercise influence through stores, correspondence, patronage and estates. The Four Eagles fight a short civil war over one imperial command.

All birth, reign and relationship intervals fit ordinary human lifespans. Later emperors claim continuity with Thalion without possessing his body, soul or a supernatural hereditary substance.

## Regional placement

The core government remains at Caleran in the Aurin heartland. Raveth is an eastern river province capable of supplying markets and garrisons; before c. 160 BR its towns were tributaries paying canal duty to the Vasharan road command at Avesh, and they joined by negotiation after the Arcelon road pulled their trade west. Arcelon is an eastern corridor joining a market basin, mountain passages and an inner-sea port, built to answer Vashar's Dawn Road. Lacaris is an eastern provincial garrison town. These locations use physical relationships without supplying premature coordinates or modern borders.

Qiryath passes from Vasharan to Atherian supremacy under the late Serrats (author-only c. 82–80 BR) through the War of the Two Dawns and the Trial before the Stars; Vashar holds it as a lease through the Dawn Portion, which ends in 65 AR. At 0 AR Vashar keeps its highland core, Avesh and most of the Ubar. Qahur is three crowns inside the Atherian sphere and is under no direct Atherian rule before 0 AR: Qemaret's lower river is bound by grain and harbour contracts with an Atherian station; Tenaret and Sakhret are crowned clients; Vasharan tribute lapsed during the War of the Two Dawns. Sarqen remains the setting's only royal country placed under a governor after its queen's fall.

Olveth is the already established Vardeni upland settlement. Its shelter-crown deepens that location without creating another people or Menhir. Sarqen remains the enlarged Second Harbour and gateway provincial centre. Its governor swears the spared towns' Table Oath at the landing every spring before the towns supply him, a condition Thalion accepted in 350; the meal without hierarchy of worth is the province's standing counter-rite to the imperial image. Samyra lives at Esharat as the towns' guest until 333, and Amesar in sanctuary on Ilamar until c. 320.

The empire's southern edge meets varied Folk societies through forts, treaties, recruitment and war. The western northern dry belt retains camel-and-well cultures preceding the First Catch. Sestaran harbours occupy varied political relationships. Jirahar communities retain distinct ancestry, law and religion in tower towns, diaspora quarters and provincial settlements. None becomes a mere Atherian regional caste.

## Material causality

The age's stability depends on continuing military pay, municipal collection, road and harbour maintenance, cultivated estates and transport. A ruler's order reaches a village through governors, councils, suppliers and armed enforcement. New articles explain the work rather than treating administration as an abstract virtue.

Public works provide water, movement and employment. They also consume stone, timber, money and labour. The quarry killings under Valeran and Arcelon's dead workers retain the price within the historical account. Military protection creates markets and exposes neighbouring communities to requisition and land seizure.

The Raveth sequence remains plain, with a native turn at its end: the first assessment is fixed in a good year; the Arcelon road levy takes the men who cleaned the flood channels under the old Vasharan canal duty; two harvests fail, a fair third is shipped out under contract and blight follows; Neriah imports grain and issues relief against countersigned debts; repayment failures transfer land; Neriah advises one estate to repair the channels and Elian II approves it; Lethara's officers replace the countersignature with an oath before the emperor's image, and Neriah resigns. Relief winds down by 113. Public text dates the hunger "from 119" and gives it no seven-year shape. Survival and dispossession follow from the same policy; the oath decides how meekly the dispossession is received.

The vacancy of 151 is held still by four things: Samor's divided river commands, the veil (no claimant can be acclaimed by the Face before the others consent), family memory of 247, and the fever summer that killed Orent II and filled the river camps with sick men while sparing Elian's eastern army.

## Religion and supernatural identity

> These rulings govern identity and truth. Where a ruling below limits what a supernatural power may cause, rather than what it is, the Consequence Rule in [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]] supersedes it.

The Star Pantheon remains a human imperial interpretation of established powers. Astaroth is a Menhir sovereignty; temple theology does not make him a moral judge of conquest. Thanatos remains a misclassification of the Boatman. Other regional identifications do not merge supernatural beings.

The Crown of Olveth is an `infrastructure-office` attached to one shelter purpose. It is neither alive nor a saintly relic. Its lethal response at attempted trophy use is bounded and reproducible under the established conditions. It proves no general theology.

The Emperor after the Emperor is a dependent Mask of Pride formed through standardised ruler imagery, praise and desire for unanswerable sovereignty. Its earliest well-supported independent response occurs during the empty succession of 247 BR. It can reproduce the imperial persona and intensify recognition; it cannot know unknown facts, command distant forces or determine rightful succession. Human emperors supply imagery and feeding architecture without creating the original Idol Throne.

No emperor becomes a god, Saint, Patron or immortal office through cult. Every ruler and mortal actor has a complete human soul. The Mask, portraits, public office, bodies, residues and legends remain separate. No Regnant succession or autonomous Archmask is promoted.

**The painted board (from c. 366 BR).** The accession portrait began as one of the painted boards Thalion's companies carried and swore to while he refused to take the field in person; it hung in the Caleran audience hall from 350, and every emperor from Meren was confirmed before it, each reign painting a new face over the old pose. Oaths and praise addressed to an absent ruler's standardised image are the earliest feeding architecture of the dependent Pride Mask. Nothing responded before 247. See [[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review#Causality Pass 10A amendment (14 November 2026)]].

**The speaking portrait (247 BR).** The Face's answer is the Mask's first autonomous response: it acts where its image is seen and addressed and intensifies obedience to the represented office. It knows nothing of stores or armies; the grain answer was the expected imperial sentence, and Mott chose the searches. Covering one image interrupts its pull in that room without starving it. Olarion, Thalion, Mott and each claimant remain distinct persons with complete souls; the Mask served whoever stood beside it. No second copy answered in 247. The Celebrants of the Veil keep the Face to 0 AR; the Red Star's priests keep the provincial copies and army standards, and the appetite moves outward through them. Whether the cloak fell before Mott died is disputed in-world and authorially open.

**The loan before the Face (Raveth).** The Mask intensified compliance where relief debts were sworn before its image. It created no debt and chose no policy. Neriah advised the estate, Elian II authorised it, and Lethara's officers chose the oath.

**The low door (61 BR).** A human rite. Officers believed it weakened the image's pull; the Celebrants called it superstition. Whether it does stays unresolved authorially.

**The wardens' test (from 326 BR).** Astaroth reveals the weakness of whatever is genuinely submitted to force. His bargain grammar forbids demanding victory without resistance and substituting a helpless victim for the named contestant. The 326 failure follows from the dedication's breach of the first taboo: a structure asked to stand without trial is forced to prove itself, which is Menhir law, not punishment. The Star's share is the genuine submission that the recognised price requires. Standing under keeps the named contestant (the official who opens the work) in the field; builders' tales of works that fell on officials who sent a slave to stand under them describe the second taboo and its failure clause (conflict spreading until nearby structures prove themselves). Astaroth stays morally blind; human choices decide who pays. Under [[Causality Pass - Direction]] decision 2 his structural answers to builders and siege engineers are standing practice from Darneth (380 BR) onwards, and the Torque Register's 350 AR line means that he answered Astrion's public test of legitimacy once. No warden's register comments on the Via Somnia's footing, and none may be used to explain the Sleepwalkers' Summer.

**The sign at Qiryath under the late Serrats.** The sign proved that one offering was accepted and chose no Keeper. Its absence at the Trial before the Stars proves nothing either: lawful offerings are not made to answer a governor's question, and grace is not given on demand. It went on answering ordinary offerings under the confirmed Keeper and under priests of the defeated houses who stayed. Its form and earlier history belong to [[Causality Pass - 01 Deep Past and the Ancient East]]. Its silence after 0 AR and the augurs' Three Silent Days ([[Causality Pass - 04 Who May Authenticate a Sign, 0 to 411 AR]]) stand side by side; neither may explain the other.

**The Witness of Dawn.** The Vasharan Pride Mask appears in one line of Daryam propaganda against the claimant who held Kavresh ("his mirrors showed him nations kneeling") and decides nothing, so the two empires' Masks are not a matched pair.

**The Sleepwalkers' Summer (c. 200 BR).** A registered Weird. Reader articles may tell it and report in-world claims (the augurs' withdrawn summons, the Church's prefiguration, Vorthara's standing grant); nothing may explain it. See [[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]].

**The Star Pantheon in reader prose.** The authorial truth stays here and in the cosmology files: the Star Children are Menhir misread as a family; "Star Child" is an imperial family-title; the Astral Keepers' Circles are real observed ecological groupings shaped by the Faewild Schism, and the empty First Circle is Atherian theology; Thanatos is the Atherian misclassification of [[The Boatman]]. Reader articles present this only as an in-world quarrel among temple hymns, the Keepers' Circle tables, Folk names for the same powers (the Pitiless Day, the Moon Court, the Hunt That Hunts Back, the laugh heard just before the path changes) and later Jirahar and Redeemer teachers. Moved sentences are listed in [[Authorial Notes from Reader Articles - Imperial Age]].

## Source hardness

Slavery, family separation, executions, confiscation, hostage-taking, massacre, forced labour, mine and quarry deaths, rural dependence and military displacement remain direct facts. Relief and useful government do not dissolve responsibility for these acts. The account also preserves affection, education, games, humour, travel, work and real civic pride.

The Low Door history fixes responsibility among upwardly mobile protagonists. Thessa reveals the refuge, all six accept the official lie, and Helon is executed for releasing hostage children. Their poverty and service origins neither excuse later decisions nor become a reason to deny their achievement.

Neriah recommends continuing the estate system, Elian II authorises it, and the imperial household benefits. Lethara and other estate managers take on the resulting lands, and her officers choose the oath before the image that Neriah refuses to administer. Each actor's responsibility follows their own choices. Later accusations against the Jirahar turn a specific imperial policy into an ethnic charge.

The age has no saints by rule. Its wonders are the speaking portrait and its copies, the wardens' answers from the Red Star, the Crown of Olveth, the sign at Qiryath and the Sleepwalkers' Summer. Its sinners in reader prose include Olarion and his informers, Avaren Mott, Cavar Oren, Vara Deneth's burning of the Arcelon settlement, Lethara's oath-taking officers, Velen I and the Low Door Companions; its righteous dissenters include the Olveth custodian beaten to death, the river-quarter household that would not kneel, Helon Var and Neriah, who designed the dispossession and refused the oath. Each keeps the ugly part of the record.

## The bridge to 0 AR

*Author-only narrative of 151 BR–0 AR, from Causality Pass 03.5 as decided. Exact years are public for 151, 119, 61, 42, 33, 24 and 0; the others here are working dates. The reader version is [[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]].*

**The vacancy, 151.** A summer fever came up the Aurin from the harbours, killed Orent II with no son and no named heir, and filled the river camps with sick men. For some weeks the empire had no quick way to make an emperor. Samor's division of the river army meant every approach to Caleran answered to a different district command, each fed from different country; a commander who marched first marched without the others' grain. The Face hung veiled, and the Celebrants would lift the cloth only after the senate and the armies agreed; they refused the crowds who brought their sick to the doors. The poor districts still told of the portrait carried ahead of the search parties, and senators whose grandfathers had sat with Mott did not want to be seen near it. So the commanders talked. The eastern cities, richer than ever and untouched by the fever, named their price: citizenship for the households that ran their councils, courts and roads. Elian Serrat commanded the healthy eastern army and came from such a household. The Face was unveiled last, before a ruler already agreed, and every later accession kept that order.

**The grant and its oath, 151–126.** Citizenship went to officers, teachers and municipal families able to meet public obligations, the people who would sit on councils, collect Raveth's new assessments, keep the Arcelon stations and pay for festivals. Each swore before the emperor's image in a governor's court or town hall, binding the grant's holders to the office rather than to a provincial commander. Jirahar scribes and interpreters swore with the copy veiled on Samor's precedent; the house of Qoreth became citizens so, and its chancery service is the "family's imperial service" behind Sha'il bar Qoreth's citizenship. Townsmen who could not meet the obligations were shut out. The oath multiplied; towns commissioned better portraits; the Mask was fed in a hundred halls.

**Raveth, from 119.** The first assessment (c. 160) was fixed in a good year. The road levy took the men who had cleaned the flood channels under the old Vasharan canal duty, and the channels silted. Two harvests failed, a fair third was shipped out under contract, and blight followed. Elian II gave the relief to Neriah bar Sorek, who bought grain, requisitioned boats, opened military stores and had every release countersigned by local witnesses. Relief went out as debt. Boats reached the river towns first; hill villages died waiting for the pack trains. When the debts could not be paid, Neriah advised one estate large enough to repair the channels, and Elian approved it. Lethara's officers ran it and replaced the countersignature with an oath before the emperor's image at the store; Neriah would not administer it and resigned. Countersigned households argued, delayed and kept part of their land; sworn households walked their animals to the estate office unasked. Relief wound down by 113.

**Cassen's prosperity, 101–76.** Cassen I, Lethara's son and Elian II's adopted heir, inherited the estate. Its revenue built baths and theatres in the central cities; its dependants resisted with unworked labour days, burned seed stores and absconding families. Raveth's grain reached the garrisons through estate contracts, so towns like Lacaris paid in prices what Raveth had paid in land. The Lacaris riot of c. 96 belongs to this economy.

**The War of the Two Dawns, c. 85–c. 74.** Late in Cassen's reign the Vasharan crown passed in dispute. Two Tiravan claimants held dawn audiences; the keepers of Daryam carried the royal flame to the one backed by the Ruzhan horse houses and withheld it from the one who held Kavresh, whose mirrors, his enemies said, showed him nations kneeling. The Avesh road command chose the loser. The victor purged it, pulled garrisons east and, needing silver, sold confirmations of offices across the western sanctuaries. Vasharan tribute from the Qahuri crowns lapsed.

**Qiryath, c. 82–80.** The sanctuary's Keeper fell on the cistern stair and died. The council named an interim Keeper from the tower houses, whose pilgrims now came mostly from Atherian provinces and were largely citizens, and whose gifts paid much of the upkeep. A river-quarter house long in Vasharan road service bought the confirmation. Before the purchaser arrived the interim Keeper made the pilgrimage offering, and the sign was given and recorded. A month later the purchaser took the altar with a Vasharan escort. The tower houses wrote to the Atherian governor at Arcelon in letters a Qoreth scribe, Ezrahel's grandfather, drafted in the chancery's forms. The governor came to the next pilgrimage with an augur of Astaroth, who proposed the Atherian remedy: test the claims and let the sign decide. The purchaser refused to make the Lord answer a foreigner's question. The interim Keeper offered, and no sign was given. The governor decided on the sanctuary's record of the earlier sign and the purchaser's refusal. The Qoreth scribe drafted the charter:
- sacrifice, communal courts and burial jurisdiction protected, including in the province's tower communities;
- no image to enter the sanctuary;
- the council chooses the Keeper and the governor confirms him;
- a daily offering on behalf of the emperor;
- the governor holds capital jurisdiction over offences against imperial rule and sits at the pilgrimage, with an augur for public rites, to hear cases the elders refer;
- the sanctuary's dues stand surety for the tribute and the payment to Vashar, and arrears may be taken from its treasury;
- one copy in each archive, sanctuary and chancery; disputes go to Caleran.

His house kept the sanctuary copy for three generations and trained its sons in both archives. The purchaser's house and the river-quarter families who would not serve under the charter went east into Vasharan country and told the Trial before the Stars in the towers that took them in. The sign went on answering the appointed offerings under the confirmed Keeper and under priests of the defeated houses who stayed; each side cited it.

**Velen and the Face, 76–61.** Early in Velen's reign (c. 74) the Tiravan victor, still fighting in Ruzhan, accepted the charter by treaty for the Dawn Portion, a yearly share of pilgrim dues paid to Avesh: "lease" in Vasharan records, "cession" in Atherian. Velen ruled through a narrow circle, removed popular commanders, had the Face unveiled at his river spectacles and exempted their banks from test as festival works. The banks failed and drowned workers and spectators. In 61 his guards, sworn before the image, killed him, expecting a reward from one of his relatives, and the relatives divided the commands.

**Doran, 61–42.** Doran moved first because the road stores (Miro Fen) and the capital's armour workshops (Brana Keth) declared for him, and Sena Varris held a crossing against a relative's force. He punished the assassins, re-veiled the Face, abolished the festival exemption and had his officers enter by the low door instead of swearing before the image. He paid his supporters with southern farms, reopening claims older surveys had seemed to settle. His daughter Mira married Othran, of an eastern house that knew the charters; as Othran I (42–24) he governed from the provinces and kept away from the audience hall.

**33 BR.** Isfir was born in a Jirahar tower town of the eastern provinces whose communal court sat under the charter's protection. The governor's hall in the nearest city held an imperial portrait; much of the farmland around the town was crown estate; imperial roads ran past the tower. Jirahar citizens swore with the image veiled. The sign was given each year at Qiryath, and Vashar collected the Dawn Portion and called it rent.

**Othran II and 0 AR.** Othran II succeeded in 24 to a state that worked: estates fed the cities, eastern citizens staffed the courts, and the provincial portraits were hungrier than the Face had been since 247. At Qiryath, Ezrahel bar Qoreth, Lord-Scribe and keeper of the sanctuary copy, knew the charter better than any governor, and his fear for "the towers' protections" was fear for his family's instrument. When a teacher from the towers drew crowds at the pilgrimage, every procedure he needed existed: a governor sitting for the pilgrimage with an augur (Severan) beside him; a clause for referring cases touching imperial rule; a right of complaint to Caleran; a burial jurisdiction under which an elder could ask for a body. The charter's later life (the surety clause as the 61 AR "pretext of arrears", the stopped offering for the emperor, the refugees of 65 going to communities founded by the charter's losers, the Dawn Portion ending in 65 and feeding the grievances behind the war of c. 94–103 AR) belongs to [[Causality Pass - 04 Who May Authenticate a Sign, 0 to 411 AR]] and the Redeemer-era reviews.
