## Geographic and identity controls

**Seravon** is the ancient populous island in the zone later associated with Sermora; it lies east of the western Thalmyric/Saronikan region, west of its neighbouring archipelago and west or north-west of most Eclessian territory. **Ilamar** is a separate central sanctuary island among the Elmarai, shoal and Seravon-facing routes; its precise position is open. **Caelovar** is the drowned Nythalum-founded colonial site within the central island world. **Marelith** was a later mobile harbour whose ordinary central circuit did not mean annual visits to every island. Its final trek went east of that circuit toward Ardel's central anchorages. **Black Reef** is its separate grounded 355 BR wreck on or near the eastward route. Exact coordinates, coastlines, the smaller islands and the names of most local polities remain open. The Elmarai Isles are one heartland, not all Elmaric islands or the entire future Saronikan kingdom.

The Elmaric family arose principally from local Orphaned populations, then incorporated Folk, halted Glass-descended newcomers and captives under varied conditions. It was not one ancient nation. Atherian-speaking Arkenan–Saronikan ports and Elmaric island communities overlapped through trade, mixed households and later rule; neither history erases the other. Seravon's later republican identity does not retroactively make its ancient dynasties a republic.

## Restricted and unresolved history

**Caelovar.** Authorially, the senior colonial dynasty tried to absorb rival living-control lineages and ordered a forced deep migration for its palace and chosen dependants while the foundations were already wounded. Contrary controls, contracting structures and unstable ground drew much of the city down. Human labourers and native-descended inhabitants resisted a ruling decision that would abandon them; their revolt did not cause the disaster. Transformation chambers changed existing human bodies, never their souls. Entry was rationed; some workers and rebels seized access, many candidates died and others remained unrescued. Public accounts may report colonial rule, drowning, rebellion and unequal survival, but the full control-graft chain is not common knowledge. The site-name's antiquity is unproved.

**Marelith.** A male Morlach ritual specialist followed a genuine but ambiguous tide-borne vision; survivor knowledge, Deep Envoy negotiation and the wounded reefback's own needs shaped the bargain. He entered the sea as its first living return. His fate is **permanently unknown**, even in this control. Later sacred rulers converted an exceptional bargain into recurrent demands, including children. Hunger after a poor fishing and raiding season led residents and crews to throw the dynasty into the sea. The subsequent council shifted tribute onto captives, enslaved foreigners and condemned people, and captains later dominated the polity. These are human decisions, not proof that Thalassor approved a hierarchy of lives. Deep Envoys are diverse and cannot collectively be made child-takers.

**The 355 BR attack.** Marelith independently accepted Ardel Veyr's hire. Melvo used a withdrawal and apparent command-ship prize to bring hidden attackers and sea-fire close; his returning fleet defeated Ardel's nearby main force in the same operation. This is the first operational Atherian use of the weapon in the selected history. Its formula, production, tactical quantities and later stores remain restricted. The public version can report the stratagem as differing accounts and the burning as historical fact. Marelith's urban mass grounded as Black Reef; most residents died or entered Atherian captivity. Smaller surface and deep diasporas survived. The reefback's fate is unresolved, and no later sighting is authorially confirmed by this promotion.

**Vorthara.** The Black Spire's builders and original function remain unknown. Its observed emanation disproportionately endangered Giants and people with strongly active Giant inheritance; it was not a general magical amplifier. The Qahuri-rooted Pale Hand was already the leading death-working faction by 350 BR and publicly rejected human soul-binding. A concealed circle or protected clients nevertheless practised it. Ordinary animated labouring corpses were generally soulless; the captive soul of a White Measure project and a vampire's retained human soul are different conditions. The identity of secret practitioners, victims and precise methods remains restricted. Qahuri arrivals were several waves without a fixed Vortharan migration year; Qemaret's fall and later closures are possible pressures, not proof of particular arrivals. Some Folk, other Orphaned specialists and perhaps individual ancient vampires joined the milieu without turning all of them into one Qahuri school.

The early town had a death-working quarter and distinct adjoining cult courts, as well as an ascent market, occupied Spire spaces and exterior terraces. These were not fixed named wards. The refuge–slave-market conflict crossed them; recurring seizures, rescues and reprisals are canon, but no singular pre-350 BR citywide uprising or settlement is established. At the imperial boundary Atheria accepted a limited nonintervention arrangement with a temporary gathering while surveilling surrounding routes. Exclusive named-service schedules, commission protections and tribunal procedure are **later expansion-era developments**, not 350 BR terms. No Spire Truce, permanent Shell anchors, Astral Conclave, Redeemer interdict or Concord seal existed in this early account.

## Editorial and chronology audit

The public split follows the approved encyclopedia style: direct leads, independently readable local entries, proportional parent summary and practical navigation. It preserves captive coercion, tribute, massacre and slave-market conflict in public prose without exposing protected mechanisms. The 355 BR linkage was checked against Ardel, Melvo and the chronology; Arkenan–Saronikan, Sermoran and Vortharan articles were checked for political backdating. Precise maps, local shrine names, smaller island polities, Black Reef anchorage and subsequent 350 BR–0 AR developments remain future work, not inferred canon.

The first promotion exposed an editorial failure: the initial public articles were too compressed to meet the established culture-package standard, and two public Vorthara pages linked readers to the secret present-day Pale Hand file. The subsequent revision added a developed regional atlas, expanded the people, place and city articles through material and social history, supplied outer-area and early-faction entrances, and redirected public Pale Hand links to its public historical article. These are expansions of the selected decisions, not approvals of new exact dates, polities or chart positions. The remaining absence of named minor islands and individual rulers is an intentional evidence and author-choice boundary rather than material silently invented for narrative fullness.
