# Imperial Founding — Authorial History and Integration Review

This review records the authorised imperial-founding canon pass of 4 September 2026. The user approved the recommended **Alternative A**, with Thalion as the Augustus-like founder, and **Route M**, an incoming maritime dynasty joined to a spared-town family. Fourteen new canonical articles integrate the founding generation and its antecedents. The wider Age of Glory remains the next part of Package 2C.

The historical additions are authorial facts, with partial knowledge and differing memories identified in the reader articles. They are not promotions of every possibility in the comparative research. The Caesar-like Thalion alternative and locally originating royal-house alternative remain unselected research branches.

## Scope and source precedence

Controlling sources are the established chronology, the two fissions, Caleran foundations, the Common Sky settlement, the integrated palace and Gateway histories, and the geography of the Aurin, Folk frontier, northern wells and Qabir–Zanthir gateway. The preceding working files, including uncommitted Package 2B and research additions, were captured before this pass.

The research inheritance is:

Ancient testimony and modern research remain cited in those dossiers. The fictional names, exact events and dates below are newly established World Egg history. They are not claimed as discoveries about Rome.

### Actor identities and supernatural limits, additions

This amendment follows the Consequence Rule in [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]]. All named humans remain complete human souls; no saint, damned office or returning ancestor is added.

1. **The Red Field.** Astaroth's bargain grammar governs it: a commander who dedicates arms and names his opponents is accepted into a genuine contest whatever its justice, and may not flee the field while a named opponent contests it; substituting a helpless victim for the named contestant is taboo. Caleran's office grew from the breach of that taboo in 609 (the Lustration of the Field), after which the augurs held that only a living commander standing in person could accept the Field. Harod's acceptance (466) ended with his death; Caleran believed the Field lay vacant for two generations afterwards, an unanswered challenge that bound no one *(11A2: vacant, not fouled)*. Casvilar's acceptance in 376 was real and bound him. The killing of 370 broke the contest's bounds, and Astaroth's failure clause ran: conflict spread until every faction near it had been forced to prove itself. It is **one cause of the succession wars among several**; the unpaid armies, the rival inheritances and the proscriptions are human causes and remain sufficient on their own. The failure clause ends when Thalion stands at the muster ground in 350 with no named challenger living. Astaroth remains morally blind: the acceptance proves no justice, commission or legitimacy, and the augurs' declaration of closure is a human reading of an ended contest.
2. **Varroth's ruling of 367** is a human interpretation by the First Celebrant. It happens to agree with the authorial position (acceptance is personal and dies with the Warden), which is why it held.
3. **The Noon Sighting (488).** A noon-bound place-mind of Father Sun answered an upland boundary rite performed by Vardeni soldiers at Sarven. For one hour every shadow in the lower Aurin pointed along the old star-sighted lines. The veterans paid the recognised price, a season's labour bareheaded beneath open sky. Some petitioners also asked the land to bear for them without limit, which is the Sun's taboo; the failure clause ran, and that autumn the public-land harvest ripened in days and rotted. The answer proves where boundaries had run, not who deserved the land. The Noon Roll is a surveyors' record of an observed event, not a relic.
4. **The Table Oath** is a human oath. Its meal closed a dominion overlay of the Bronze-Faced Provider (see [[Atherian Palace and Maritime Histories - Integration Review]]); swearing it confers no supernatural protection on a ruler. The towns' power over Vardek in 373 was political.
5. **Darneth.** Unchanged in nature: a structural answer, not a commission. Casvilar's later use of it to justify the Field is his own claim.

### The engine

Varroth's ruling of 367 is read as everyone in Atheria read it: the Field of 370 stands open while any of the **seven** challengers entered in Tolm's register lives, and no commander dares disband an army while it does. The war is therefore a hunt for seven men through the refuges of the inner sea, and every refuge answers by its own law. The Table Oath's suppliant clause (a stranger who reaches the table cannot be sold from it) shelters the last of them at Sarqen, and the same oath ends the Nerethan crown, spares the queen and binds the conqueror. Thalion's character is built on Astaroth's counterplay, refusing a field before accepting it: he never accepts a field, never commands in a battle, and his absence is filled by painted boards that become the imperial portrait.

### Actor identities and supernatural limits, additions

All named humans have complete human souls. No saint, damned office, returning ancestor, Mask person or new Weird is added.

1. **The Writing-In (367–364).** Entering helpless people as challengers and killing them on the muster ground substitutes helpless victims for the named contestants, which is Astaroth's taboo. The failure clause of 370, already running, widens: two foraging columns drawn to one bridge, neutral cities choosing sides, and the burning of Varroth's magazines in 364 are its human-visible effects. It remains one cause among several; unpaid armies, debt and appetite for confiscated land suffice on their own. Venn armed himself and stood to be fought, so his death was a genuine contest and no breach; Astaroth's grammar is morally blind, and the distinction says nothing of justice.
2. **Thalion's refusal.** He never dedicated arms or named an enemy before the Red Star, which is the recognised counterplay of refusing a false field before accepting it. His absence binds no one and costs him nothing under the grammar. His fear of the Red Star is his own.
3. **The Salt Field (353–350).** Velsar's acceptance was genuine and bound him. A named opponent is not bound to appear; Thalion contested through his armies, so Velsar could not leave. The augurs' saying that a board cannot contest is a correct human reading: the boards were never parties. The contest ended when Velsar died fighting Melvo, a named opponent present in person. Thalion's wish to keep Velsar there until he paid the recognised price of surrendering his claim was legitimate under the grammar and cruel in fact.
4. **The Fourth Collection (352–351).** The bitten wax is a genuine Feastling sign. Feeding a bound army in a blockaded country by worth, with officers' tables full and levies hungry, made a besieged-granary opening for Gluttony around the Salt Field's stores, drawing on the towns' sealed jars. No Mask fully formed. It was not the Bronze-Faced Provider, which ended in 520; the towns' belief that the Provider had returned is a misreading. The opening weakened as Gluttony forms weaken when nourishment reaches those denied it: at the summer meal of 350 every seat at the landing was filled, the conqueror, the deposed queen and the abandoned rower were served alike, and the next count found the wax whole. The Table Oath remains a human oath; the meal works through Gluttony's weakness, as in 520, not through any power in the words.
5. **Marelith's chain (355).** The founding covenant forbade a permanent anchorage. Held fast by Pyrassa's chain, the bearer broke the covenant's terms and fought free, tearing the city apart while the sea-fire burned it. The bearer's fate remains uncertain. The chain held the city; it shut no harbour. The ruling in [[Package E - Island and Vorthara Canon Integration Review]] stands: no wound opened and the Shoreless King did not stir.
6. **Sarqen's sea bargain** keeps its nature (a local manifestation within Thalassor's sea ecology; surrendered cargo; a flooded disused galley basin; no permanent binding). It moves to 351 and serves to take the royal galleys past Melvo's line to Hazreth. The officers' choice of treasury over wounded is human.
7. **The closing of 350.** The 370 contest ended when its last named party died and the failure clause had run its course; the spared towns' judgment of the Nerethan house in the spring of 350 was the last faction proving itself. Seneth's death at the table carries no supernatural consequence of its own. Thalion's standing, Varroth's refusal and the Red Star priests' declaration are human readings of an ended contest; none of them binds Astaroth.
8. **The painted board.** Standardised imagery of an absent ruler, oaths sworn before it and praise offered to it are early feeding architecture for the dependent Pride Mask that answers in 247. Nothing in it responds before 247. The board that hangs in the audience hall from 350 is the accession portrait of [[The Year of Four Eagles]]. Thalion's soul is untouched by it.

### Actor identities and supernatural limits, additions

1. **Vacant and fouled.** Harod's acceptance ended with his death; a vacant Field binds no one, and Caleran's belief that it "stood open" meant only that another commander might take it up. The breach of 370 ran Astaroth's failure clause. The valley war of the paid-off companies is its visible effect and is also ordinary veterans' violence, sufficient on its own. That no army may disband is a human reading with real danger behind it.
2. **Venn's stand.** An armed stand is a genuine contest and no breach. His survival was the squad's choice. Astaroth neither spared nor chose him, and the soldiers' saying that the Star let him go is a misreading that happens to respect the grammar. This replaces the 10A wording "his death was a genuine contest".
3. **The register** is a human record. Its loss changes nothing in Astaroth's grammar. It was trodden into the brine during the fight and lies under the salt pans; it has no power and answers nothing.
4. **Varroth on the salt.** His proclamation was a human reading of a genuine acceptance, as his augurs' had been in 10A. He was killed by a man in a fight, and nothing followed from it but grievance.
5. **Sarqen's sea bargain** keeps its nature: a local manifestation within Thalassor's sea ecology, surrendered cargo, a flooded disused basin, a current for chosen hulls and no permanent binding. The hulls now come in. The officers' choice of grain over wounded is human.
6. **The counted child.** The towns' yearly inspection of Meren is the Table Oath at work as a human oath. It proves nothing about him.
7. **Pyrassa, 355.** The wardens admitted hulls in distress, as the Chain Peace requires. No harbour was closed, no wound opened and the Shoreless King did not stir. [[Package E - Island and Vorthara Canon Integration Review]] stands.
8. **The Dawn Road** is an ordinary relay system. The fugitive's body was his own; whether Orvaz arranged the fall is open.
9. **The closing of 350.** Unchanged in substance: the 370 contest ended with its last named party. The college's refusal, Venn's sword and the Red Star priests' declaration are human readings and bind nothing. Venn meant the sword as an answer that Thalion might stand on the Field himself; reader prose leaves it unexplained.

The blend holds at about half: the fouled Field, the government it forces, the Writing-In, Marelith's covenant, the Salt Field, the Fourth Collection, the sea bargain and Velsar's binding are driven or bent by the supernatural; Darsil, Venn's stand, the hostages, the Dawn Road, Pyrassa's refuge, Ilar's failed motion, Seneth's killing and the meal at the landing are human.

### Alignment with the late-republic bend (11A1)

[[Causality Pass - 11A1 Second Bend - Gateway Wars and Late Republic]] was written the same day and changes what stands behind 370. The founding articles follow it: Casvilar had carried a Field since Kellovar (383) against Drenoc, who was never produced, and it died with him; the fouled contest of 370 is the seven's. The seventh name was entered as Drenoc's son, and he is the fugitive who dies at Darneth about 362. Seneth commanded the river line at Kellovar and fought for the senate at Velos; Draven is a ruined ship-owner; Tolm is quartermaster of the muster ground, and his complicity is disputed. Veyr came to Sarqen in 373 for grain, not as a beaten fugitive. Casvilar's veterans held their Noon Roll plots undischarged, and the companies Velsar pays off after Darsil are the first soldiers discharged since Kellovar.
