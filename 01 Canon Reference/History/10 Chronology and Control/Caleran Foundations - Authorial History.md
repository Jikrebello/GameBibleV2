# Caleran Foundations — Authorial History

This addendum establishes the private historical and metaphysical facts approved through the Caleran Foundations review and integrated in Package 2B on 4 September 2026. Public articles distinguish these facts from what surviving traditions disclose. The complete household telling is preserved separately in [[The Wolf's Two Sons]].

The earlier research dossiers remain unchanged as source history. Their proposal labels record their status when written. This canonical addendum and [[Atherian Formation and Republic - Delivery Review]] identify what has now been integrated.

## The actual fosterage and fratricide

### Ravessa's household

Around the twelfth century BR, **Ravessa** led the Faewild household of **Istren**, whose members hunted and maintained refuges along a wooded approach above the lower Aurin. She appeared as a large grey she-wolf and could speak to humans. Her household belonged to the predatory world associated with Orionis. It had its own attachments, rivalries and dependants.

Ravessa's principal opponent was **Orsul of Nereth**, the head of a neighbouring household. Nereth had taken a sheltered hunting basin and an approach through which Istren's dependants could move between their Faewild homes and a particular woodland in the human country. The loss crowded Istren into poorer ground. Ravessa wanted an allied human ruling house that could keep that woodland accessible, shelter her messengers and supply trained fighters and metal weapons during a limited attempt to recover the basin.

She watched the quarrels around the estuary because human settlement was already altering access to the woods. A hostile fort could obstruct the route she needed. A friendly household might guard it. Raising children with a plausible claim among the estuary's people offered her a longer and more dependable influence than hiring whichever captain happened to command the landing.

### Rescue and upbringing

**Avren** and **Caleth** were the young sons of **Meral**, an Enathi boat owner, and **Damaris**, a soldier from a maritime-derived household. Their parents had helped a coalition of river families resist **Odrava**, a captain who sought exclusive command of an estuary landing. When the coalition failed, Odrava killed Damaris, imprisoned Meral and ordered the children taken to a disused landing and drowned. Avren was old enough to speak; Caleth still needed help to eat.

The two men sent to kill them left them tied in a grounded boat, expecting the incoming water to finish the task. Ravessa found the children while moving through the woodland approach. She tore through the rope, carried Caleth and led Avren into the trees. Her choice was deliberate: she knew who their parents were and what their survival might mean to the families defeated by Odrava.

Ravessa brought them to a refuge near the passage into Istren's country. A human dependent, **Meral's former neighbour Alvessa**, helped feed and clothe them. Alvessa had sought shelter there after losing her home during the fighting. She could teach the children river speech, cook their food and carry out the daily work for which a wolf's body was poorly suited. Ravessa supplied protection and food, taught them to move through the wood and took an attentive interest in their growth. The boys learned the habits of her household and called her their second mother.

Their childhood contained ordinary pleasures within an unusual home. Avren delighted in following tracks and guessing where a hunted animal would turn. Caleth made small boats from bark and persuaded Alvessa to float them in every available stream. Ravessa tolerated their games until they interfered with a hunt. Both children learned that her affection could coexist with impatience, possessiveness and expectations they had scarcely begun to understand.

Meral escaped captivity several years later with the help of people who had continued to trade through the landing. She found her sons through Alvessa. Rebuilding her relationship with them took time: they remembered her unevenly, and Ravessa resented any proposal that might remove them from her influence. Meral resumed boat work among relatives and gradually brought the boys into contact with their human kin. Their adolescence passed between these households.

### The struggle at the river mouth

As young men, the brothers joined opposition to Odrava's successors. Avren gathered fighters from dispossessed households and from men whose seasonal employment had ended. Caleth worked with boat owners and grain suppliers, including relatives who had accommodated themselves to the existing rulers. Ravessa supplied guides along one wooded approach and expected their success to bring the promised human assistance.

The brothers' supporters seized a fortified landing after its provisioners refused further deliveries. Fighting killed defenders and several inhabitants caught in burning store buildings. Avren ordered the execution of a surrendered officer whom he held responsible for his father's death. Caleth helped households return to the riverfront and arranged repairs to the landing. Their coalition then joined neighbouring settlements under a common defence and a ruling household based at the estuary.

Later tradition calls this the foundation of Caleran. The brothers brought a new political centre into being among existing settlements. They built on inhabited ground, employed local craftspeople and depended on grain raised by communities already established along the river. The relationship between this foundation and every earlier buried structure remains uncertain.

The first years exposed differences between the brothers. Avren regarded command of the fighting men as the basis of kingship and promised Ravessa a force to recover her lost basin. Caleth wanted the coalition's river and hill households to share decisions about war and expenditure. He had married **Sareth**, a Serathi potter whose family supplied the rebuilt settlement. Through her relatives he had become responsible for people who feared being drawn into a conflict inside the Faewild.

Caleth's objections were sharpened by a failed reconnaissance. Men sent along Ravessa's route returned carrying a dead companion whose body had been mauled by Nereth's hunters. Caleth refused to provide boats or provisions for the larger expedition Avren was preparing. He offered Ravessa continued shelter and supplies while insisting that the soldiers remain at home. She considered this a betrayal of the purpose for which she had protected them.

### Fratricide and its aftermath

The brothers met at an unfinished timber hall to settle command of the proposed expedition. Caleth arrived with household representatives who intended to resist the levy. Avren brought armed followers. During the argument, Caleth ordered the stored provisions released back to the households that had supplied them. Avren struck him with a spear and killed him. He had chosen to preserve his command by killing the brother whose supporters were preparing to withdraw it.

Avren's men dispersed the opposing households and killed several who resisted. Others escaped by boat. Sareth recovered her husband's body with help from his mother's relatives and returned to Thesra with their children. She continued her craft there and refused the king's later invitation to join his household. Meral remained in the river country, maintaining contact with both branches of the family while refusing to attend Avren's accession feast.

The death damaged Ravessa's project. The boat families withdrew provisions, Avren's followers were occupied with securing his rule, and the expedition she had expected became impossible at its intended scale. She obtained a smaller body of hired fighters, recovered one refuge and failed to dislodge Orsul from the contested basin. Several of those who went with her never returned. Her connection with the royal household continued uneasily, giving neither party the dependable alliance it had sought.

Avren ruled long enough to repair the landing's defences and establish a household that outlived him. His dependence on armed supporters enlarged their landholdings, while the families loyal to Caleth maintained a separate memory of the city's beginnings. Later rulers inherited the practical gains of the foundation and the quarrel within its ancestry. They could claim descent from the founding house, marry into its opponents or honour both brothers; each choice carried consequences among the living.

## Giant ancestry and continuity

**House Iskaren is remotely descended from a Titan-Firstborn whose nonhuman parent was a bounded generative manifestation associated with Astaroth**. **House Velketh remotely descends from a separate Titan-Firstborn of a Typhonius manifestation**. These are two distinct exceptional ancestral conceptions. Their human mothers, circumstances, dates and the later fates of the Titan ancestors remain unwritten. Neither sovereignty divides, incarnates in full or authorises hereditary rule.

This establishes actual remote ancestry for the ruling houses without accepting their stories of stars descending into bodies. No ruler in the main narrative is an immediate child of a Menhir. The histories do not imply that repetition of a rite can produce another Firstborn.

No machine escape, Egg White evacuation or ancestor reconstruction is added to Arsuvan's collapse. The wider canon contains these possibilities, but this kingdom does not need to repeat each of them. Unspecified cadet survivors remain unassigned rather than silently converted into Patrons.

## Faewild persons and the wolf's scheme

**Ravessa and Orsul** are individually sapient `court-law` persons in Orionis-associated Faewild ecology. Their households comprise persons and dependants whose membership is not a new reusable species. Ordinary wolves, human attendants, household roles and nonpersonal features of their environment remain distinct categories. Orionis is the existing Menhir sovereignty; Ravessa is neither his complete manifestation nor a dependent Idol Mask.

Ravessa's route connects one woodland approach in the lower Aurin country with the vicinity of Istren's refuge. It can be crossed under local conditions known to her household. This pass establishes the ancient crossings needed for the rescue and failed expedition, not a permanent present-day route. Neither the Egg White nor the soul-road is involved. The children age normally and remain human throughout their upbringing.

Her practical scheme has limited aims:

1. Preserve children whose human kin could support a future estuary dynasty.
2. Retain access to the woodland through their settlement's goodwill.
3. Obtain shelter, metal equipment and a limited body of fighters for the struggle against Nereth.
4. Recover the lost hunting basin and approach, strengthening Istren against one rival household.

Ravessa can rescue and guide people through the route, supply food and affect the local struggle. She cannot compel love, guarantee inheritance, create a city, order the Menhir sovereignty or predetermine Caleran's republican and imperial future. Avren's promise is politically important because he chooses to honour it and attempts to impose its cost on others. It is not a soul-binding contract or a magical explanation of his murder.

Her affection is real, alongside her ambition and possessiveness. Alvessa and Meral provide essential human care, teaching and relationships. The fosterage therefore has a daily history instead of a miraculous image standing in for the work of raising children.

The scheme partly fails. Caleth's death deprives it of provisions and allies. A smaller force recovers one refuge at the cost of lives; the basin remains under Orsul. This is one dynastic conflict within a local court ecology. Its later outcome, Ravessa's ultimate fate and any authentic presence in 1360 remain open.

### Actual powers and errors of identification

- **Astaroth** is the Menhir of collision, resistance and structural force. Human war theology does not make him a commissioned military patron or identify him with Qianglong's War Horseman.
- **Lyraxis** concerns adaptive variation, change and creative play. Music and trickery are cultural expressions, not an exhaustive biological or moral description.
- **Orionis** concerns pursuit and predation. Ravessa's personal attachments do not convert the sovereignty into a universal guardian of children.
- **Hecatara** concerns routes and adjacency. Civic claims about moral choices, fate or dead souls exceed that domain.
- **Typhonius** concerns recombination and bodily transformation. Breeding and healing cannot manufacture the Image or return a dead human soul.
- **Arachnara** concerns hidden, distributed networks and related creaturely pressures. Her cult supplies neither omniscient archives nor a second lord of the human afterlife.
- **Thanatos**, in this tradition, falsely interprets **the Boatman** as a Star Child. The Boatman remains the existing psychopomp. Payments, inherited rank, civic membership and temple promises cannot bribe his routing or divert souls into an Astratheon afterlife.

The six Menhir do not become seven celestial siblings through collective belief. Human identifications, regalia and stories do not merge supernatural persons. Sun, Moon, land powers and other cults retain independent existence even when public theology maligns, marginalises or excludes them.

### Idol involvement at Sarven

A limited portion of the flattering royal visions at Sarven is activity of **one unnamed dependent Mask of Pride**, exploiting ceremonies of comparison, humiliation and sovereign superiority. Other visions may be dreams, staged wonders or misread observations. The Mask depends on the existing Pride Throne; the ceremony supplies imagery and feeding opportunities, not the Idol's creation.

This Mask is separate from Astaroth, the Giant rulers, their ancestors and their regalia. It receives no autonomous Archmask status, Regnant succession or continuous secret rule over Caleran. Its post-collapse fate is left unassigned. The name **Crowned Reflection** was considered during drafting and excluded because it already belongs to a protected succession hypothesis. No alias or connection to that hypothesis is introduced.

The Common Sky may preserve images once exploited by a Mask without carrying the original Mask into every later ceremony. A public religious synthesis is not itself a supernatural merger. No member of the ruling couple is unknowingly made into the Mask's incarnation by this review.

## Public knowledge and surviving evidence

The public founder account establishes the human family, estuary coalition, murder and survivors. It attributes Ravessa's identity and purpose to the foundation traditions. This addendum fixes the actual wolf-shaped court person, her limited Istren–Nereth conflict and the work of human carers without making that information common knowledge in 1360.

Sarven's physical remains belong to the former kingdom and later reuse. They do not authenticate every royal genealogy. The paired bowls are a living customary form; no surviving example is fixed as Meral's personal vessel. A wolf carving supplies an image, not proof that its maker encountered Ravessa.

The received tale's dialogue, omitted years and variant motives remain literary tradition. Avren's deliberate killing is authorial fact. Neither possession nor a supernatural compulsion excuses it. Sareth's departure, continuing craft and refusal of the king's invitation remain part of the historical aftermath.

## Protected and uncompleted facts

The earliest builders of the Deep Hill City remain unidentified. No return of a Titan ancestor, Idol succession, final human judgement, authentic modern apparition or outcome after 1360 is established. The Glass, Bird-Marked Sites, Anshara's final sovereign and the Year of Standing Stars retain their existing boundaries.

Exact ancient sites and full genealogies remain open where the account functions without them. The twelve-city Compact is real; its complete nominal roll is not supplied by this addendum. No Atherian settlement or campaign gains a divine commission through the public cult, and neither Ilaron nor Tarsenna becomes a Saint.
