# The Divergence Pass — Torque Register

The anti-derivative instrument. Diagnosis: material generated by the world's own physics never reads as borrowed; material imported from history reads borrowed wherever Earth's causal engine came with it. The cure is **torque** — for every imported beat, one consequence only this cosmology could produce, canonised here and made the famous part of the event.

## The Three Standing Rules

**1. The Forward Rule.** Real-history analogs may govern the *past* (familiarity is an entry ramp). From 1360 AR forward, **no event may be predictable from its analog** — the present and future run on world-native engines only (see [[The Age of Held Proofs]]). If a reader who knows the textbook can guess the next beat, the beat is wrong.

**2. The Torque Rule.** Every analog beat carries at least one canonised consequence below. When writing about any of these events, *lead with the torque* — the analog is background, the torque is the story.

**3. The Consequence Rule.** Supernatural controls govern *what is true*: who a being is, what a soul is, what a sign proves and what no ritual can confer. They do not govern *what is allowed to happen*. A Mask, Menhir, sign, bargain, relic, kindling or contract may decide who wins, who rules, who starves and what a people comes to believe, provided its nature obeys the cosmology and its price is paid. Every age carries at least one such consequence in this register.

Where an integration review or package limits a supernatural power's *consequences* rather than its *nature*, this rule supersedes it. The superseded rulings are:

- [[Package B - Qiryath and Eastern Empires Canon Integration Review]]: Astaroth's campaign correspondences and Father Sun's sign "provide information or passage only."
- [[Atherian Palace and Maritime Histories - Integration Review]]: the final gateway war as "a conventional regional war with local Mask activity", the bar on the salting acting against the gateway cult's Mask, and the ruling that the final gateway siege has no causal connection to Orthea.
- [[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review]]: the rejection of a Pride apparition as a duplicate device.

Ontological controls in those files remain in force: complete human souls, no pre-Redeemer saints, Masks distinct from persons, signs that prove only what they prove.

## The Register

Entries run in date order from the end of the Age of Chains to the eve of 1360. The register first covered only 292 AR onward; on 14 November 2026 the Causality Pass added the ages before 292 and filled the gaps after 395, and the bending passes of the same day (10A to 10F) replaced or added the entries for the sequences that still followed their analogues beat for beat. A second bend (records 11A1 to 11F) then broke the skeletons and date offsets the first had left: entries were rewritten where the order of fates changed, added for the new turning points and removed for events that no longer happen. Where an analog label names a beat that now has no counterpart, the entry says so. [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]] is the record of the decision behind each entry, and [[Causality Pass - Direction]] the rules they were written under. Entries with no analog, or none direct, bend the world's own chain rather than a borrowed beat; they stand here because later torques inherit them.

### Before the empire (to 350 BR)

**The Cost of Continuity (c. 2400–2260 BR — the fall of the god-kings).** TORQUE: Giant blood thinned, and the ancestral works answered thinner blood only when fed from outside: crews, then captives, then the granaries of living districts, at last children for the vaults. That hunger is the cry the Lord answered and the line along which the plagues struck; revolt began where the takers came. The famous catastrophes (a Naga lord's ritual drinking the Serpent's Spine, the Blood Sun bursting) are survivor legend for unmended dikes and an untended vent-stair. Vathra's sky roads failed the same way: Hecatara's displaced distance, carried by Titan blood while it was strong and by marched-out captives after it thinned, came back all at once and stretched the valleys under the crown-cities. Later peoples fear anything kept past its time because that is how the Giants ate people. [[Age of Chains]]

**The Round of the Hundred Wells (c. 2255 BR — the war-chief who becomes king).** TORQUE: a Menhir bargain binds the named petitioner's line *as it was reckoned that day*, so a crisis leader becomes a dynasty because only that blood can keep paying. Meya of Narash, who had sold captives in Vathra's wars, sealed the Hundred Wells against every shortcut, and every crown-mother since has walked the round at accession and called each well by name. That is why Narashi authority still runs through mothers, and why one crown-mother's fever in the Bronze-Road drought let raiders into the inner cisterns for a season. Ark law founded no office on a bargain, so Ahdari leaders stayed removable judges and their wells needed walls. [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]]

**The Provision Ends and the Four Deaths (c. 2180–2165 BR — the manna ceasing; Moses on Nebo).** TORQUE: the miracle stopped on schedule, because its commission was the refuge and not the people, and it stopped on a host that had forgotten how to farm. Its form was water: springs in channels of the glass and gardens that bore beside them in every season, so its end was a channel going dry. The Vacant Commission and the Five Staffs were fights over the seed jars the drought-struck rear camps were eating; the Southern Seizures took ploughmen with their fields, and enslaved Orphaned farmers taught their captors the farming year, which is where Atherian slavery and adoption custom begin. The southern camps' saying, *the Lord who stopped feeding us; the stars that still answer*, is the root of Atherian star-worship: a people whose gardens had never failed in a century could not tell a finished commission from abandonment, and the Menhir answer whoever performs the rite. The Prophet died in the season the bread stopped, and every people tells his death as an account of who was fed: the Jirahar Slow Road, the Ahdari Unpaid Well, the Atherian Ascent, the Folk satire of the Guest Who Died at the Door. All four keep one line, *no one is owned*; the spine keeps the rest unanswered. [[Age of Exodus]] · [[The Glass Sojourn]]

**The Copy-King of High Harbour (c. 1980–1960 BR — the Trojan War).** TORQUE: the coalition besieged an office, and the office ate its own harbour. A speaking image of the founder was the named party to the tide mistress's pact, and the captives of the collection raids were her price. When the blockade stopped the raids, the image named travellers from the harbour's own streets, down to the children of the craftspeople who kept the channel gates. A woman the commander had confined carried into the town what he intended, which was to keep the image and the channel. The craftspeople opened the water gate and broke the image in the fire. The pact defaulted on whatever left the harbour by sea as property, people held as goods included, and that is the Returnings: ships that carried nothing came home; captains learned to put their captives ashore; the commander drowned his and was lost with his fleet; and at one captain's bay the High Harbour women, having watched their captor rolled into the channel to no effect, cut a new channel and bargained in their own names. Southern sea custom since: no sea pact bound to an image or office "that cannot drown". [[The Siege and the Returnings]]

**The Reclaiming (c. 1450–1325 BR — the Late Bronze Age collapse).** TORQUE: the Bronze Roads ran partly on recast Ishkatari tomb metal that still bore its owners' title marks. When ruin traders opened deeper estate-vaults, the Gilded Wardens, Processional Coils and Estate Dead woke and walked the old ceremonial roads, which the trade had reused as trunk routes, recovering "misplaced property": caravans were collected and palace stores visited. Failed floods, palace wars and unpaid soldiers did the rest, and trunk roads failed before short ones. Iron became the road metal of the north and east because nobody dead owned it. [[Gilded Wardens]]

**The Water in the Wrong Season (c. 1290–743 BR — Gideon; Jephthah's daughter; Elijah's drought).** TORQUE: a named-petitioner bargain bound Tovan's line as the shelf villages reckoned it that day, through mothers, and the blood outlasted every judgement made about it. The water he named drowned House Sereq with the pledged children in its pens. The road judges bound his house to the rite as a blood-debt and barred it from office; at his death the streams answered his sister's children, and his sons drowned on the threshing floor trying to seize the rite. When Gedran fell (c. 1128) its gate captain carried a girl of the line, Naveth, down the river road, and an Arqeshite house kept her daughters' line by marriage for three centuries. Seram's levy starved the shelf branch off the shelf (c. 829) and the streams ran astray, and it was his Arqeshite wife Tilqa who went down the cistern and called them home (c. 824); the prophet Haviel's killing of her diviners came after the water and was judged as murder. In the siege of 743 a woman of the same line named the streams away from Qiryath. "Feed the cistern house; do not follow it" is the Jirahar line between a bargain and an office. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/70 History and Conflicts/Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria.md|Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria]]

**The Factor of Qemaret (c. 1084–1076 BR — the expulsion of the Hyksos).** TORQUE: the foreign crown fell to its dead creditor. Two low floods left the Yasharet pledge to Khaseret's House of the Returning Cargo unpaid; when Iddar sealed the store courts, the House's factor, Aramen of Sakhret, fed the unpaid garrisons from its warehouses, and Qemaret's gate guards opened the river gate to the men who had fed them. The soldiers treated the store courts as collateral, the House's cellars admitted only clients with clear accounts, and the last king was walled into its vault as returned cargo. The Yasharet households stayed, and the factor married the walled-up king's sister: Qemaret's king-list records no change of house, and Sakhret's begins a new one for the same kings. The southern restoration is court poetry. The empire was begun by the Yasharet on the dead's credit and widened by the Factor's heirs to keep paying it, and its bankruptcy in c. 858 completed the foreclosure. [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]]

**The Guardian at the Stair (c. 953–746 BR — Saul to David; Ahab and Naboth).** TORQUE: Qiryath's sign made kings by never refusing. A guardian who carried the appointed offering was cheered beside cool stones at every festival, so emergency commands became reigns, and House Oram held both the basket and one of the three witnessing hands. A wood-carrier who caught a fevered guardian's basket was cheered into the office while the old guardian still lived. When the two parties fought for the basket on the stair (c. 940) it reached the altar in the hands of a dying lamp-filler, and the stones were cool for him as for anyone. His widow Rava asked the custodians aloud whom the stones had answered; an Oram gave the answer, the offering, and the bearer was chosen by lot. Seram later used the same answer to put an Arqeshite image on the stair, and his soldiers killed two people at a custodian's door for a witness-staff they failed to take. The offering burned clean and the day was written with a blank. Ilbarun II's demand of 746 relied on the clean fire, and Qiryath's refusal on the blank. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/70 History and Conflicts/Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria.md|Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria]]

**The Dead Landlords of Qahur (c. 903–858 BR — the Ramesside temple estates).** TORQUE: the Aramen crown, put on the throne by a dead creditor, was bankrupted by its dead. Mortuary estates and Second Houses, some of whose owners were awake, held the Basin's best fields, metal and crews, paid no levy, lent grain and copper for the crown's wars and took fields in return. When Kassur I's provinces pushed Arqesh to the eastern caravan stages, the crown could pay its southern garrisons only in land already tilled for tombs. The garrisons sold themselves to temple and military houses, the desert mines were abandoned, and Qahur entered the Arqeshite century owned by its dead. [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]]

**The Court at Noon (c. 840–810 BR — Akhenaten, Amarna and the restoration).** TORQUE: the king set older dead on the dead landlords, and the bill came due in his son. The metal in the Second Houses' walls was recast Giant estate bronze that still carried title marks. Hesren III dug the receiver's seal of a buried estate out of the vaults under Meheret and sent its gilded procession, the Single Radiance, to take the marked metal back. The cleared fields went to the soldiers who had been paid in graves, and custodians who hid marked plate were carried off with their children. A receiver's seal gives the estate a claim on its bearer, and Hesren had hung it on his six-year-old son. Wasted by it, Deshren took the White Measure rite at seventeen and died bound to the heaped metal and the allotment tallies, with the seal sealed in beside him. The Radiance stopped. The king paid rent to his dead son for eight years, and after his death the stewards claimed the crown for a prince who had never worn it. Boat households, whom the road-bound guards could not reach, starved the rent and crowned Zaheret on a grain barge. The Arqeshite prince who walked into the palace walked out again with a tally making his house the dead prince's collector east of the river. [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]] · [[The White Measure and the Second Houses]] · [[Gilded Wardens]]

**The Withholding and the Cool Stones (743–654 BR — the Babylonian exile and return).** TORQUE: Qiryath's sign was physical and witnessed (an accepted offering burned without smoke and left the stones cool under the hands of three old houses), and it stopped in the siege's first winter, when the council declared the city's foreign residents unhoused and let them starve inside the walls. Nobody, the spine included, knows which act it answered. The exiles judged on witnesses alone and kept the Book of the Unanswered Years. Vashar paid for the first courses of the new sanctuary as a wager on the sign's return, and the city finished it; the offering made in its king's name smoked, and the court proclaimed the Witness of Dawn as the sign transferred to the crown. Six years later the stones went cool under an offering carried by the settler-spouses the severe party wanted expelled. The Jirahar have outlived one withholding, and they remember that it ended.

**The Voice at Delkos (695–c. 655 BR — Siwa, the bowing quarrel and Alexander's body, none of which now happens).** TORQUE: the corpse that governed was assembled over eleven years, and the Mask in it had already humbled the king once. In 695 Eryndor fed his army on grain lent by Khaseret's House of the Returning Cargo, a Qemaret Patron four centuries dead, against her masters' right to prepare his body. In 688 Kassur III's portable images, in which the Crowned Countenance was anchored, began to answer in the voice of the king most bowed to, and their bearers carried them to Eryndor. Kassur surrendered to Arezan and sealed the Gate House's water books to him, which is why the Ubar's canal offices served Vashar. Within a year the images ordered an Ortheian officer to bow, he was executed for refusing, and their bearers strangled a companion who mocked them; the Mask then spoke in the dead man's voice and called the king by his camp name before every petitioner, and Eryndor marched east to be rid of it. In 684 he died aboard the allied flagship at Nemeru, waiting for a wind home. The masters performed the final rite in a ship's hold with no house to close him into, bound his name into an anchor his soul had already left, and the Countenance filled it. The grandchildren of the Belyra deportees, who crewed the ship, cut its cables and carried him to Delkos. There the dead king declared himself alive and heirless and sealed grants. His half-brother, acclaimed in Ortheia, was killed as a usurper against a living king; his daughter Mehrava, seven years old, lived at Kavresh as a claim the corpse had disowned and Vashar kept, and the later Tiravans descend from her. The wars ran thirty-four years because a Pride Mask fed on rival petitioners and promised the same provinces twice. It ended when the masters testified against their own failed rite and Serevan's grandson walled the tomb; "a Delkos voice" is the eastern sea's phrase for a convincing counterfeit. [[Wars of the Inheritance]]

**The Wood that Closed (687–686 BR — the army's refusal at the Hyphasis).** TORQUE: no army refused Eryndor; a wood did. He had left Arqesh in 687 to get out of hearing of his own regalia. Beyond the Vashari highlands a Root-Mind grove offered passage for the valley terraces it wanted back. Eryndor refused the price, felled the speaking trees for a military road and marched on, leaving the cut ground behind him, and the Root-Mind's law ran: roots crossed the cut, worked timber sprouted, and the road behind the army edited itself into a different valley each morning. He cut his way out, leaving prisoners, the villages that had fed him and the farther garrisons on the far side; those garrisons, beyond anyone's reach, became the farther-eastern Successor. The wound he took in the cutting sent him to the seep that heals only those who stay, and, because he would not stay, to the White Measure. [[Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest]] · [[The Five Limits of Eryndor]]

**The Unreckoned Days (c. 652 BR — the Delian and Latin leagues).** TORQUE: Caleran ruled its allies through the sky before it ruled them by the sword. As keeper of the Compact's calendar it could leave a member's days out of the year, and the Common Sky held that offerings made on unreckoned days went unseen, so the city's own sailors would not put to sea. Verion's tyrant fell to the calendar in three seasons. [[The Celestial Compact and the Caleran Republic]]

**Tashqar's Bread (c. 612–601 BR — Hannibal in Italy).** TORQUE: the invader's gift was the weapon that failed him, and it ate him last. Tashqar sailed from Oresh without his city's commission, fought no battle for six years and fed wavering Atherian towns with grain from the Provider's table. Every town that ate it found its own sealed stores short at the next count, the seals unbroken and the wax bitten, and the armies sent after him starved in the country he had fed. "Do not eat the Gateway's bread" became the allies' proverb, and the alliance held out of fear of the gift more than loyalty to Caleran. When the towns' jars were empty the same bitten wax appeared in his own camp, and in 601 a crowd at Erenost, which he had fed for eleven years, killed him at the doors of its store. [[The Gateway Wars]]

**The Lustration of the Field (609 BR — the Forum Boarium burials).** TORQUE: Caleran faced an enemy who would not fight, and killed its Gateway hostages and captives on its festival ground to give Astaroth a contestant. The children had been delivered two years earlier as Qarzeth's proof that it had disowned Tashqar's war. The Red Star's law forbids substituting a helpless victim for the named contestant, and the next year the war spread exactly as that law's failure runs: Qarzeth's Council Houses voted the war their own. The augurs concluded that the victims had been wrong, not the killing, and created the Warden of the Red Field, a living commander who must stand in person and may not quit the field while his named enemy holds it. The first Warden, Celor Marath, offered battle below Thesra in 606. Every case Caleran later made against Qarzeth's altars was made from that festival ground, and the Gateway towns read the children's names back to it. [[The Gateway Wars]] · [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth.md|Astaroth]]

**The Unranked Table (c. 528–520 BR — the destruction of Carthage).** TORQUE: Qarzeth's notorious recoveries were real plenty, drawn by the Bronze-Faced Provider out of its dependent towns, and the Three Collections were the bill. The sanctuary's granaries opened only to names entered in the collectors' books. When Qarzeth's assembly voted to surrender the books to Caleran, the doors stayed shut, the city starved for a month and the cult's party took it back: the city surrendered and its god did not. The capital then fell as a Gluttony form falls. The towns stopped carrying, Ilya's list gave the dead back their names, the bronze face broke, and the survivors sealed the end at Sarqen with a meal at which no one was served by worth. Its rules became the Table Oath every Nerethan ruler swore at accession. The salt on the ruin came from that table, and the towns' own carriers laid it where the plenty had grown before Caleran's contractors could keep the stores, so that nothing could be served from that ground again. [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth]]

**The King's Bread at Ilyessa (506–491 BR — Marathon, Salamis and Xerxes' retreat).** TORQUE: the sea, not a fleet, took Vashar's war back, and it warned the king first. In 506 a fleet sent to seize Ilyessa's pilot families lay at anchor through a calm summer and settled at its moorings; the court called it sorcery, and Kazren concluded that whoever held the pilots held the water. The royal fleet passed the Narrows in 495 on terms the Ilyessan pilots kept with a tide court of Thalassor's grammar: keep moving, never ask the water to hold you still, never call the sea yours. In 493 Kazren took the title Keeper of the Roads by Land and by Water and anchored his granary fleet in the Ilyessa lee for the siege of Velanthe. The holds took the tide: water-lines inside dry planking, salt under sealed hatches, grain rotting in sound jars, hulls settling without a leak. His subject crews rowed for Velanthe's refuge fire, whose wardens, raised on Kelarion's Roll, admitted any hull in distress, and the League took the transports with the deserters at its oars. The army before Velanthe starved and the king died in its camp; his son bought his road home from Ardash Aresh with the western command, which is the root of the revolt of 462 and of Orvaz I's perjury. The League that won by giving refuge to its enemy's crews was raising its chains against its own members within a generation. [[The Eastern Sea Wars]] · [[The Refusal at Kelarion]]

**The Noon Sighting (488 BR — the Gracchi).** TORQUE: Damar's veterans asked the Sun, not the senate, to show the public land's boundaries, and for one noon hour every shadow in the lower Aurin pointed along the old lines. Every steward had seen his own wall's shadow, so the veterans took the land and nobody would put them off it, whatever month the augurs then added to the year. When the petitioners also asked the land to bear for them without limit, the harvest ripened in days and rotted, and in the hungry spring the leaseholding houses bought the plots back for bread. Nobody needed to kill Damar; he worked his own plot bareheaded for thirty years. The augurs' Licensed Sighting of 486 turned the disaster into a monopoly over signs, and the surveyors' Noon Roll waited a century for Casvilar's veterans. [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] · [[Father Sun]]

**The Closed Chains (466–461 BR — a naval league's revolts).** TORQUE: Caleran turned the Oar League's refuge custom, taken from Kelarion's Roll, into a privilege for cities that paid, and ordered harbours to close their chains to rebel hulls. In the gale of 466 the drowned harbour's old wound partly opened at Pyrassa: the closed harbour admitted no one, not even its own grain ships, until its wardens cut their chain. After that night no warden on the inner sea would raise a chain for Caleran, and the rebellion could be neither crushed nor won. The Chain Peace gave every harbour's chain to its own wardens and made refuge law for any vessel in distress, and Ilamar's custodians have kept a copy of the Roll ever since. [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] · [[The Shoreless King]]

**The Red Field (466–350 BR — the emergency command).** TORQUE: the Warden's office was a bargain with Astaroth that its holder could not lay down while a named opponent still contested the field. Harod's opponents lived at Avesh, so he lived in a tent on the muster ground for twenty-five years, killed two of the senators who tried to relieve him and died there. Casvilar accepted the Field at Kellovar in 383 against a Folk chief who then walked into a river-court's ford and was never produced. For thirteen years he could not be recalled, could not discharge his army and could not close the Field, and his augurs counted first his banker and then the senate as contestants. In 370 seven challengers entered their names, one of them as his named enemy's son, and struck before the contest opened, and that ground was fouled. Harod's Field had lain vacant after his death and bound no one, and Casvilar's own died with him; this one sent the first companies paid off after the victory at Darsil home with the war on their backs. By Varroth's ruling it stayed fouled while any of the seven lived, so for twenty years no commander dared disband an army and the succession wars became a hunt for seven men through the refuges of the inner sea. Thalion never accepted a field. When the last of the seven was dead he stood on the empty muster ground. The Common Sky's college, whose First Celebrant had died on Velsar's Salt Field with the register, would not declare it closed; the Red Star's priests did, and that is why Astaroth's priests take precedence in imperial ceremonies of victory and command. [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] · [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]]

**The Ended Acclamation (444 BR — no analog; the Acts 12 frame is withdrawn).** TORQUE: Orvaz I of Vashar let the harvest crowd at Kavresh hail him as the one who made the grain rise, and took children for his dawn-house. At the instant praise became worship, nobody on the terrace could say it. They could speak, curse and pray; the one sentence would not come, then or ever again, and on the third morning the dawn-house children could not say it either and were sent home. Nobody was struck. A Pride Mask starved of spoken praise went out of the terrace mirrors for a generation, a king announced by stammering heralds reigned five more years, and Tiraz III's governors stopped fearing the crown. [[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]]

**The Ford at Kellovar (383 BR — Alesia).** TORQUE: the besieged did not die between the lines. The people Drenoc turned out of the starving hill went down to the ford in the wood below it, which belonged to a river-court whose toll was paid in household tokens, and crossed in files behind their token-bearers. Drenoc went into the water to bargain for those who had no token. They crossed and came out at fords days apart, and he was not seen on either bank. Casvilar took the hill and could not close the Field he had accepted against Drenoc. He gave the ford to his ally Morveth, who paid at it with Atherian silver, and at Darneth he cut off the hands of the ford guides who surrendered. The same ford drowned Lucar's army in 377 AR. [[The Southern Campaigns of Teren Casvilar]]

**The Writing-In (367–364 BR — the proscriptions).** TORQUE: soldiers who would not murder citizens would kill a challenger on the open Field, so the Four at the Stores wrote their enemies into the register of challengers and had them killed on the muster ground. Substituting helpless victims for the named contestants is Astaroth's taboo, and the war spread instead of closing until Varroth's own garrisons burned his magazines. Besral Venn, written in by his colleagues in 366, walked down to the muster ground with a borrowed practice sword. The first soldier went down, the squad would not make it nine against one on the sand where seven had killed one, and after him every written-in man asked for a blade. [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]]

**The Guests of Pyrassa (355–353 BR — Sextus Pompey).** TORQUE: the sea-heir lived. After Marelith burned, held by Pyrassa's own harbour chain, Ardel Veyr and the survivors rowed for Pyrassa's refuge fire, and the city that remembered the closed harbour of 466 admitted them under Kelarion's Roll. Melvo would not take a guest off his own city's quay, his first quarrel with Thalion. The terms came after the defeat and held, and the survivors' sheds became the Burnt Quarter. [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]] · [[The Refusal at Kelarion]]

**The Salt Field and the Filled Seat (353–350 BR — Actium and Alexandria).** TORQUE: there was no decisive sea battle, no flight with the treasury and no double suicide. Velsar accepted a Red Field on Sarqen's salt pans and named Thalion, who never came; bound to the salt, Velsar's army had to be fed where it stood, and the queen's levies by worth, the Fourth Collection, brought Gluttony's bitten wax back into the spared towns' jars. A sea bargain brought grain in past the blockade at the price of a third of it poured into the sea and the wounded left on a flooded quay. When Hadram killed Casvilar's last murderer at the towns' table, the Table Oath that had crowned his mother put her house out and seated an abandoned rower in her place. Melvo killed Velsar on the field, and the First Celebrant died there beside him. Samyra saved herself and her children by sitting at the towns' table as a stranger, where the oath forbade anyone to take her, with Thalion's own son, whom the towns had counted at that table every spring for eight years, on the bench beside her. Thalion had to eat there and swear the oath to have the country. Every governor of Sarqen swore it after him, and the wax was whole at the next count. [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]] · [[Samyra Nerethan]]

### The Atherian Empire to the Redeemer (350 BR–0 AR)

**The Star's Share (from 326 BR — the Fidenae collapse).** TORQUE: Thalion's heir Caelor died under a treasury hall the Red Star's priests had blessed and nobody had loaded; under Astaroth's law a work asked to stand without trial is forced to prove itself. From then on rune-trained wardens loaded every public work before the Red Star until a crack showed its weakest point, broke the portion built to be broken (the Star's share) and made the official who opened the work stand under the arch while the first wagons crossed. The extra stone drove the quarry strike of 274. Arcelon's embankment, tested in the dry and fallen in the rains, gave the empire "tested in the dry" and the rule that a test answers only the question asked. From Samor to Kelsen every emperor was made by standing under an arch of his own opening. Velen I exempted his festival banks because the Emperor does not stand under, and drowned with his audience. Astaroth stays morally blind; the custom decides who pays. [[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]] · [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth.md|Astaroth]]

**The Year the Portrait Spoke (247–245 BR — the Year of the Four Emperors).** TORQUE: Olarion allowed no heir to be painted on the board the armies swore to, and in the empty succession the accession portrait at Caleran answered in the Emperor's voice, the first autonomous response of the dependent Pride Mask later called the Emperor after the Emperor. The Face had begun as one of the painted boards Thalion's companies carried and swore to while he refused to take the field in person. Avaren Mott governed in its name for two winters. No commander starved the capital: clerks who read the grain tallies in its sight read them full, no grain was bought, and its assurance that no imperial granary was empty sent soldiers behind the carried image into the poor districts, where the father of a household that would not kneel was killed in its doorway. A kinsman who came to be painted as heir was told that the Emperor lived and needed none. Samor Veloran brought grain in over a span at the water gate, stood under it, threw his cloak over the Face, would not be addressed by it and would not be painted in its place. Since then the Face has stayed veiled in the Common Sky's custody, unveiled last at an accession, while the Mask feeds through provincial copies; emperors were made under an arch until Velen I, and from 61 BR Doran's officers entered by the low door instead of swearing before the image. Mott was the first man executed for questioning it; whether the cloak had slipped, and whether it answered him, is disputed. [[The Painted Reign]]

**The Seat Filled out of Season (151 BR — Severus and the citizenship edict).** TORQUE: no provincial general won a civil war. A fever held the river commands still, and the spared towns of Sarqen, who had filled the emperor's empty seat at their unranked table with ferrymen's widows for a century, laid their boards out of season and put their governor in it. The eastern cities stood behind the seat for citizenship, and the commands' price for accepting an emperor made at a table was that every new citizen swear before the emperor's image, with the copy veiled for Jirahar scribes. [[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]]

**The Loan before the Face (Raveth, from 119 BR — Joseph in Egypt).** TORQUE: the famine minister did not buy the land for the crown. Neriah bar Sorek wrote relief as debts owed by whole villages on countersigned tallies, to be dug off in the flood channels; the emperor's sister's stewards made a crown estate of them, and her officers had each debtor swear alone before the emperor's image. Where debts were sworn before the image, the dependent Pride Mask intensified compliance, and those households walked their animals to the estate office unasked, while villages holding the Keeper's tallies argued, went to law and kept part of their land. Raveth remembers its dispossession through the image as well as through the Jirahar administrator, who resigned rather than administer the oath and left the villages their copies. [[The Keeper of Borrowed Grain]]

**The Trial before the Stars and the Qiryath Charter (under the late Halreths — Pompey and the Hasmoneans).** TORQUE: during Vashar's War of the Two Dawns, Qiryath's sign answered an interim Keeper while a rival held a Vasharan purchase of the office. The Atherian governor's augur asked the sign to decide between them; the purchaser refused, the interim Keeper offered, and the fire smoked. The governor ruled on the sanctuary's written record, and the charter a Qoreth scribe drafted (an imageless sanctuary, a daily offering for the emperor, the governor's capital jurisdiction at the pilgrimage, the dues as surety, copies in two archives) governed Qiryath until 67 AR. Its clauses returned in 0 AR and in 61 AR, when the emperor's offering lay unburnt for nine mornings and the governor sat in judgement on it at the pilgrimage. Vashar took the Dawn Portion of the pilgrim dues and called it rent. [[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]]

**The Guard on the Terrace (61 BR — the Praetorian murders).** TORQUE: no guard killed an emperor. Velen I's untested festival banks went into the Aurin with him on them while the Face stood unveiled on the terrace above, and his guard, sworn before the image, stood in its ranks facing the portrait and did not go down to the water. Doran punished no one. He ended the oath before the image and had his officers come in by the low door. ### The Redeemer and the conversion of the empire (0–411 AR)

**The Three Silent Days (0 AR — the cessation of oracles).** TORQUE: from the hour of the Binding to the third morning, no augural register in the empire records an answer. The colleges sealed the days, Tyros carried them into the open, and the Great Purge was fought to recover the copies. The registers prove a silence and nothing more. The bishops have held the originals since 383 and never published them. [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]]

**The Great Purge (c. 36–45 AR — the first-century persecutions).** TORQUE: a records war. The High Augurate recovered the books carried off by the colleges that followed Tyros, burned Jareth for "forging registers" before the Pillar into which it had leaded the Nails, and made publishing an unregistered wonder a crime. The Underground learned to write its miracles as weather and good sense, which is why the faith's earliest records understate their own signs.

**The Nine Mornings and the Empty Litters (61–67 AR — the Jewish War).** TORQUE: the war began from a sign nobody had entered. Since the Silence every offering at Qiryath had smoked, and in the spring of 61 the emperor's, supplied with its wood by the governor, lay raw on the altar for nine mornings before the pilgrims. The governor's augur called it fraud under the Purge's edict, and soldiers sent up the stair to burn a bull in pitch killed the altar's custodians and two hundred others. The Keeper went on offering for the emperor every morning for six years, and Jirahar jurists hold that Qiryath never broke its charter. No army came for three years. When the city fell in 67, dried out by the Red Star's survey of its cistern galleries, the legate's augur lit the emperor's bull on the altar and the house burned from it. The sanctuary's vessels had already gone through the city's Way-Gate to a bench the Keepers have never named, and the gate has not opened since. The triumph carried empty litters, and the treasury charged every Jirahar household for their contents: the Litter Debt, whose rolls Vestian's searchers used three centuries later. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]]

**The Dawn Tower (62–64 AR — Masada, moved before the city's fall).** TORQUE: the legions went to the Dawn Road first, to shut Qiryath's door to Vashar, and found a deep tower, so they dug down instead of ramping up, dropping the outer gallery roofs where a siege frame loaded before the Red Star had broken. Its Way-Gate's partner bench, in the tower where the houses that refused the Qiryath charter had settled, would take the households and no one who had borne arms. For nine days the keeper Yaltah bat Ozer sent the households through while the fighters held the stairs; she broke the scroll-keys as the soldiers came, and the legions killed whoever was left. Jirahar gate benches still cite Shahar when they weigh another tower's danger against its people, and three years later none would open for Qiryath's. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]]

**The Guests at the Table (Sarqen, 187–189 AR — the servile wars; the end of the Antonines).** TORQUE: the dynasty ended at a meal. Runaways of the Sarqen rising sat down at the spared towns' unranked table, from which no stranger can be sold, on the morning the governor came to swear the Table Oath. He could not swear without making guests of his family's property, the towns supplied no unsworn governor, and for two years no grain fleet sailed; troops who fed their officers first brought Gluttony's bitten wax back into the sealed jars. In 189 the emperor Marek Vessan came himself, ate between a carter and the tally-woman Melqa of Belqara, and swore. The oath made every guest unsaleable, his mother's house disowned him on the landing, and he lived out his life as the towns' guest while the provincial commands issued orders in his name. [[Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian - Emperors Wars and Revolts|Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian]]

**The Kept Lamps (c. 242–248 AR — Decius).** TORQUE: the edict answered a real Mask. Grave-lamps that leaned towards mourners and burned without oil fed the mutinies of the unpaid eastern garrisons, so Tervan made every household relight its lamps for the dead from the Stars' fire and burn them green on the colleges' copper-salted oil. Night watches walked the graves for yellow flames and found the burial fraternities first. The congregations relit from flames that had never burned green, their reconciliation quarrel turned on whom to give fire, and the daughter-flame custom began with Amaryah's lamp hidden in a bread oven at Veridun Minor. The green was scraped from the temple bronzes, and the edict ended in 248 when the High Augur of Caleran would not open the year before a pitted image. The colleges sold the opening of campaign seasons for the next forty years, and a voided season made Cassian's army in 288. [[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization]]

**The Ironvale Sworn Account (291 AR — the Forty of Sebaste, bent).** TORQUE: the first sign sworn before crown officers outside the augurs' power. A guard, and a script-keeper freed so that his oath would stand, swore that the flame on the Forty was no Shell-fire. Cassian's camp read the account in the Aurin towns in 292; the crown's own prison roll still contradicts it. [[The Forty Flames]]

**Cassian's Vision (292 AR — Constantine).** TORQUE: the sign stopped a battle and can still be checked. Cassian's vanguard was crossing the frozen Aurin by night to fall on the court's sleeping camp when the Flame stood on the river and the ice went out under the Stone Bridge of Velos. Both armies saw it, no battle was fought, and the court's soldiers crossed the bridge to him. The reach **has never frozen since**, in eleven centuries of hard winters — a standing micro-theophany that passes every relic-court test and belongs to the *crown*, not the Church: history's first fight over whether a miracle can be owned (it continues). The augurs entered the sign in the crown's name; from c. 296 a Warden of the Reach kept its winter book, the price of empire-wide restitution (the Velos Concord), which Halmenth signed last, "as a record of ice". Cassian was baptised in the unfrozen reach in 316 by the bridge chapel's crown chaplain, his own eldest son Torian, whom he had ordained by force after the sealed answer of c. 309; and in 377 Vestian gave the book and chapel to the bishops and kept the water. That deathbed baptism is a named scandal — **the Cassian Wager**: one unrepented reign answered with one perfect absolution, cited by every deathbed-delayer since, condemned by every confessor, and unresolved because in this world the Weighing is real and *nobody knows if it worked.*

**Astrion's Restoration (348–351 AR).** Astrion demanded that the Stars answer as publicly as the river at Velos. **Astaroth answered Astrion's public test of legitimacy once**, revealing the weakness of the loaded support of an imperial standard at Caleran in 350. His structural answers to builders and siege engineers were standing practice (the Darneth precedent of 380 BR; the wardens' test from 326 BR); this was the only time he answered a test of a reign. Engineers cleared the ground before it failed. Astrion treated the answer as public support for his reign. The response established a fact about the loaded support, within Astaroth's power over resistance and structural weakness. The augurs' signed record reached the Sanctum and was sealed in its deepest registry. His survey at Qiryath stopped when the Jirahar crews refused to work under the Red Star's wardens, whose rite had dropped those galleries in the siege; no power acted there. Astaroth gave no terms for Astrion's proposed second test, of victory, and afterwards answered neither the last college's petition in 383 nor the eastern command at its crossing in 384. In 351 he stood beneath an untried span at the Emperor's Cut while the wounded crossed; it failed by the builders' standing law of the untried work, not as a second answer to his reign, and it crippled him without killing him. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]] · [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth.md|Astaroth]]

**The Two Standards (351–384 AR — Julian's death, Jovian, the brother emperors and the Frigidus, bent).** TORQUE: Astrion's single answer made him an emperor nobody could finish. Crushed at the Emperor's Cut and reported dead, he lost Caleran to Lucar Aster and kept the east, where for twenty years he was carried in a litter under the Answered Standard as emperor beyond the depot line. Every spring he asked the Red Star for a second answer, and every spring his augurs wrote down that none had come. The command that had watered his column kept its augurs and the standard for twelve years after his death. In 384 it asked the Red Star for terms at the decisive crossing and got none, and its own Redeemer units negotiated. Vestian won because the Stars would not answer his enemy.

**The Kellovar Ford (c. 368–377 AR — Adrianople).** TORQUE: since Cassian's market treaties the southern army had crossed the river-courts' fords behind Folk guides carrying household tokens, and Lucar's chaplains took the tokens as idols. The fords kept an older toll: a column with no token could cross if it delivered a traveller, who came out on the far bank and could not return. When the Grobi came down the frozen rivers the garrisons paid that toll in the children of their own allied fort districts, and stewards sold some of the children and wrote them down as delivered. The households escaped across the fords on hidden tokens, towards their children; and when Lucar followed the last of them into the ford below Kellovar, where Drenoc had gone into the water in 383 BR, with no token and no one delivered, the court took his army and carried him dry on a stolen silver token. He spent three days as a guest at the fire of the house whose token it was, and his own rearguard killed him when he rode back out of the water. The court exacted and judged nothing. Vestian, recognised while Lucar was still across the river, read it as judgement on a divided empire. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]]

**The Dissolution at Caleran (383 AR).** TORQUE: the last High Augur, Ilios Marrant, asked the Red Star to show his college's worth, setting no load and naming no price, and nothing answered before the crowd. By statute the authentication of signs passed from augurs to bishops, with the Deep Register and the three silent days.

**The Second Silence and the Raveth Hearing House (385–c. 389 AR — no analog; Callinicum).** TORQUE: the Profession's reception lamps did not kindle. Willing baptisms still kindled; state professions burned yellow. The court blamed hidden rites and the Jirahar, banned household worship and then took the lamp out of the oath, the first time an institution hid a falling yield instead of repenting of it. The house the Raveth crowd burned held sworn accounts, from baptised families of Jirahar descent, that the lamps had not kindled; clergy pressed Vestian to drop the rebuilding because rebuilding would restore the record, and the survivors carried the argument to Qabir, where it was condemned at Halmenth in 411. Aurelium recorded the Silence and sealed the record. The Church has never ruled whether it was a judgement, a lesson or the Flame keeping its terms. [[Vestian Persecutions and the Long Diaspora]]

**The Empty Charter (c. 396 AR).** TORQUE: Vestian struck out "in perpetuity" and wrote *before the Mesakha*. The Church reads never; the Jirahar read a promise in their persecutor's hand. The charter does not say who recognises the Mesakha, which makes it the only law in the world that a sign can repeal. [[Qiryath Reqa]]

**The Lamp Not Relit (Halmenth, 411 AR — Ephesus).** TORQUE: the regency's sale of tax freedom to relic estates needed holy objects to hold the Fire, and Qedrel would certify no relic. At Halmenth he read Qabir's count of the unkindled lamps and was charged under the augurs' own old law; Qabir's deacons would not relight their lamp, a daughter of Amaryah's taken from the bread oven in 248, from the Ember's daughter brought by warship. Halmenth's ferrymen rowed him to a Sea Fleet ship. The regency struck the gateway sees off its roll instead of pursuing him, and because no recognised cleric would certify their relics, the condemned cities kept paying the land tax the rest of the empire was escaping and were left alone for it. The Velian Church grew inside the empire's edge, not in exile. [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]] · [[The Velian Church]]

### The Long Frontier and the Fall (398–905 AR)

**The Guttering (398–905 AR — Gibbon's decadence).** TORQUE: the palace's hymns stopped kindling while the frontier hermits' went on, because the Flame answers persons and purposes, not offices. The court hid the failures as "postponed", hired Sestaran Shell-fire to fill the ranks, and then faked kindlings: the Copper Lamp of 681 began a Covenant file of counterfeit fire that ended with Corvenas III's accession, and counterfeit fire is a breach. The Flame did not save the capital with the most relics; it saved the refuge with the fewest. Thalmyria's theophany and Eclessia's protocol are the Guttering remembered from opposite ends. [[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]]

**The Two Awards (514–523 AR — the war of 602–628 and its client subsidies).** TORQUE: the Toll War broke the dry country by doubling its titles. Each crown voided the other's well-awards and granted the same wells again, neither withdrew a grant at the peace, and every well of any size had two lawful holders. Mujalib grew up in a well-house that held half a well, and the jurists who later voided Atherian charters for their witnesses had learned the method at home. [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

**The Bread of the Bound and the end of Vashar (c. 549–560 AR — the Justinianic catastrophes and the fall of Sasanian Persia).** TORQUE: the Covenant's founding proof was food. Through the Thin Floods the 4,444 dug the oasis while children starved below, and grain went to those who sealed their names. Every name on the roll was owed bread in a failed year, and the bound carriers would lift only what was given: the two terms that sent the breach-courts north in 568. Vashar was taken by its own promise: its kings ruled by keeping the roads, the famine emptied the relay stations, and Covenant jurists declared the last King at Dawn forsworn. The Ruzhan horse-lords who sealed for bread became the Covenant's officers, and one of their houses produced Qahir. [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

**The Three Seals (563–597 AR — the Rashidun).** TORQUE: no line of caliphs succeeded the Prophet, because the Prophet went on living. Mujalib gave his authority away under three Seals (Water to Zuhayr and the guard of the wells, Names to the college, the Roads to Qatada) and lived thirty-three years more without setting one holder over the others. "The Hand-Sealed" are those three. The Fourth Seal is the instrument that would have named a holder over them; two appeared at his death in 596, and Zuhayr became the first caliph on the college's. The three Seals still divide the Covenant in 1360: the caliph of the wells at Al-Qahur, the Unnamed Custodian's Vault and the Sultan-Caliph's roads. [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]]

**The First Unsealing (568–612 — the Arab conquests).** TORQUE: the conquest was substantially a *legal event*, and it was made to feed a roll. The Covenant owed bread to every sealed name, and its bound carriers would lift only grain that had been given, so the armies marched with **portable breach-courts**, and half the northern provinces were never stormed — they were **served**: municipal charters examined, defects found, sovereignty ruled void, and terms offered that entered the townspeople on the roll, took a share of each harvest as the Given and set a bound keeper at the town well. "The Unsealing was served, not fought" is the Covenant's proudest sentence and the conquered's bitterest, and it is why the Velian States' descendants still keep their pre-conquest charters in fireproof chests — waiting, some say, for a counter-ruling. The towns that went first were those whose charters had been sworn under the Stars before augurs: when the court asked for the witness, it was a dissolved college and a silent star. The conquest stopped where hermit-bishops had re-sealed charters and the Fire had come on the wax. The one pitched battle, the Dry Ford (575), was an Atherian victory, and the winning army broke up at wells that drew only for the roll. The advance also stopped because the 4,444 are a finite number, and by about 610 every one was placed.

**The Keeper of Bir Qassim (603 AR — Karbala).** TORQUE: the Prophet's grandson lived, and his daughter died beside a full well. The station wells of the North Well Road were kept by bound offices of the 4,444, which drew for travellers under the Covenant's protection. When a flood pushed Hilal's household off the road named in Qatada's safe conduct, the keeper stopped drawing for the fifty-eight named and went on drawing for their unnamed drovers, and a court ruled the protection void. On the sixth night Hilal read the instrument at the well-mouth clause by clause, and after each clause the bucket came up dry. At dawn the escort refused to disarm the household and carried thirty-seven of them to the strait, and Qatada, ordered to arrest his own men, rode after them in silence. Sixteen died of thirst, Rumaitha among them; the caliph's column killed the five still living at the well and fourteen drovers who had given them water. The Reading of the Wells repeats the reading, *And yet the well was dry*, and reads thirty-five names, Rumaitha's first and the drovers' last. Hilal went veiled until his death in 631, as the Wasiyin have since, and Sealed-Blood villages still draw by hand and let no bound office keep their wells. [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]]

**Tamsar and the Suspension (611–612 AR — no analog).** TORQUE: the Long Frontier Peace was made by two frightened armies. At Tamsar a breach-court's ruling failed against a town re-sealed in the Fire, a Caleran reliquary burned yellow before both armies, and a Sealed-Blood jurist told the Covenant soldiers their court sat under a seal nobody dared open. Neither side gave battle. The instrument of 612 held every disputed charter "neither void nor in force": the first deterrence by unopened proof, and the root of the Age of Held Proofs. The Velian charters in their fireproof chests wait on it still.

**The Kindled Marches (612–905 AR — the Byzantine themes).** TORQUE: each frontier march was held under one charter sworn in the Fire and renewed at every accession by the emperor in person, kneeling before a frontier monk on whom the Fire came. An emperor who would not ride to his marches could not call them, which is how Corvenas I won the throne in 713 and why Corvenas III's written orders could not move a march in 905. Thalmyria's military provinces descend from them.

**Oswin's Examination (711 AR — the rise of the papacy).** TORQUE: Aurelium's primacy began with a negative result. Oswin examined the Velos Standard, sent east to rally the provinces for a claimant, recorded it silent and held the eastern oaths in trust; the winner confirmed his right to examine because it suited him. The east could afford honest tests because, since its tax fleet sank in 653, its bishops kept the revenue either way. Protocol was born where the truth was cheap.

**What the Register Keeps (c. 780–831 AR — Maimonides' *Guide*).** TORQUE: the age's great Jirahar book is a physician's record of bound deathbeds. Banu Mizan law required a witness at every bound deathbed before the name could be released, and Rab Yequtiel sat at those beds in Zanthir's bond-hospitals and salt pans for fifty years; he argued from them that a register keeps the name and lets the person go. It was true when written and became untrustworthy in one case after 885, when the foundry's cages took the dying, which is why it could not quiet occupied Caleran's funerals. [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

**The Sacrificial Wind and Oronda (c. 866–876 AR — no analog).** TORQUE: the Reception began with a rescue the emperor did not command. A host led, by soldiers' account, by a drowned ferrywoman held the road behind a convoy on the river marches and withdrew when the army's commander, carrying Corvenas II's orders, demanded conquest. The emperor carried the Velos Standard into the Velian belt to do what she had refused, and before Oronda it did not kindle in front of both armies. Eight years later Qahir obtained the ruling that the crusade had broken the peace of 612: the empire handed its enemy the loophole.

**Qahir's Prisoners (878–885 AR — the Ottoman rise).** TORQUE: the dynasty began with a pay chest of names. Nobody could pay the northern horse that had saved the Velian belt, so its commander took the Reception's nine thousand unransomed prisoners instead, entered them under his own name at Iqashar and paid his riders from their labour on the Chain Furnace yard and the Basin canals. By 881 his was the only paid army in the Covenant, and the caliph gave him the Seal of the Roads, unheld since Qatada, with the new title of Sultan. The ruling of 884 that voided the Suspension also made the prisoners forfeit, which is why the first name-cages held them. [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

**The Clause of the Given (c. 549; 885–886 AR — Ottoman gunpowder).** TORQUE: the Covenant fought three centuries without devils because the 4,444 were sealed to "take nothing that is not given". Qahir's revolution was a ruling before it was a forge: the Clause binds parties, not powers, and a captured Depth beast, a condemned man's forfeited name and a nameless construct are party to nothing. The foundries followed the jurists, and the dissent of the people of the Clause has been outvoted, never answered.

**The Serving of Caleran (905–908 AR — 1453).** TORQUE: the capital was taken half by law, in the name of a sultan who never saw it. Idris was thirty-one and sonless and every oath in the army named him alone, so his jurists kept him at Iqashar while Qahir's engine-master Barzin and the breach-court train read Caleran's own charter, last re-sealed with warlock fire, and offered each district its own terms. The great engines came to the river dyke that kept the Aurin out of the lower city, and the Depth-Core frames waded the flood to lean on the Star's share, the length its builders had made to break in the wardens' test; Thalmyric tradition says the Red Star showed it, the Covenant says a dead college's archive did. Corvenas III could have saved the city only by passing tests in public: kindling a summons at the marches, conceding the lost north, submitting his relics to Aurelium. He chose the rites that could fail in private, and on the last day took the public one on the dyke with the Velos Standard; it failed before both armies, and the holding quarters took terms that afternoon. At dusk the engines broke the dyke and the river drowned the poor quarters no court had served. The breach-court would accept from him only his person and his relics, and by the Instrument of the Ash Hall he became their captive keeper until his death in 908. The refuge could raise no standard of its own while he lived. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Invasion and the Fall.md|The Invasion and the Fall]]

**The Fall of Caleran (905) and the Impounded Ember.** Idris al-Qari, by courier from Iqashar, ruled the Eternal Ember neither holy nor his own and impounded it as **a deposit held for the final audit**, his keepers' own names pledged as surety that it would be neither divided, removed nor put out. A deposit is kept where it stands and open to view, so the basilica around it was never sealed and its liturgy went on under guard for fifty-two years. Addressed to the audit and not to Idris, it was the one instrument of his that survived the Widowed Seals. In 957 the third generation of keepers held it against their own amir's guard, and eleven died at the lamp. The Reconquest found the daughter flame of Amaryah's lamp burning, with a Covenant ledger recording fuel and custody. **Veridun paid the invoice in full**, discharging the deposit and releasing the keepers' names. The account preserves the work of the occupiers who kept the holy lamp burning and the obligation the restored government accepted. [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]]

### Reconquest, two crowns and High Noon (905–1232 AR)

**The First Catch (905–920 — the plains horse-revolution).** TORQUE: the scattered imperial studs were **hymn-blessed remount lines** — war-sanctified stock — and Grey Sister's line carries the blessing still, diluting by generation. Tarkhan horse-genealogy is not sentiment; it is the actuarial tracking of a **fading grace**, which is why the sacred lines are priced above provinces and why a Grey Sister foal given in treaty is the steppe's version of a relic translation. The blessing is a steadiness before bound things: horses of the early lines do not shy from Depth beasts, bound spirits or speaking engines. That is why the Long Reach of 951 broke a Covenant frontier built on hound-wells and panic, and why the Horse War (c. 975–1010) peaked while the lines were young.

**The Night of the Ovens (906 AR — the Pokrov).** TORQUE: the refuge was saved by bread it had been ordered to burn. The hearth-keepers of Veridun Minor refused the council's order to burn the store-cave flour and baked it all night; at dawn the ovens' smoke lay over the valley for the five days the bread lasted, the column's engines called terms into it and its court found no gate to read at, refugees dreamed of a woman banking an oven, and the levies dreamed of home and walked there. Veridun's rule that the Mother comes in icons and dreams and never in fields held: nobody saw her. The feast is kept with oven doors open at dawn and soldiers standing vigil instead of watch. Veridun's own households call it the Night of the Ovens. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun.md|The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun]]

**The Breath of Askre (917–918 AR — Covadonga).** TORQUE: Thalmyria's first field victory was a granary turned into a grave, and nothing fell on anybody. Mount Askre breathes a heavy dead air into Caelmount's store caves, which is why they keep grain, and the hereditary cave-wardens shut the breathing-doors the night before Amir Qazem's column arrived. The column's engineers had tested the ash road under the Red Star, as Barzin's had tested Caleran's dyke, and crossed without losing a wagon; nobody asked the Star about the air. Some six hundred men and every name-custodian died the first night, and the rest wasted away through the winter beside grain they could not reach while their levies, whose rolls lay with the dead, deserted to the amnesty. The valley would never store bread in those caves again. Caedros promised to feed it, took Virelos (921) to keep the promise and was crowned there (922). The Church remembers a hermit's lamp; the valley says *they came for the bread and slept beside it.* [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/70 History and Conflicts/The Battle of Caelmount.md|The Battle of Caelmount]]

**The Widowed Seals and the Southern Threshold (929–957 AR — the Ottoman Interregnum).** TORQUE: the Qahirid army was bound to the sovereign *by name*, with the heir written in as successor-holder. In 929 the heir, taught by a jurist of the people of the Clause, refused at fourteen to take the instruments up, and Idris died before the re-swearing muster, widowing every instrument at once: levies walked home, southern garrisons woke unsworn, the silt hounds of the northern wells slipped their feeding names, and the Depth-Core engine later called the Elder walked through Iqashar's dye-yards until a feeder gave its name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir". That formula became **the Sublime Threshold**, a holder that cannot die, witnessed at Al-Qahur. It is why the court bears that name, why Al-Qahur's acknowledgment still matters, why the Basin's military households hold leases whose terms have run, and why the Cull of Brothers began as lodging, not killing: the first lodged name was the refusing heir's own. The occupied south, meanwhile, was sworn to a man rather than to the Covenant, and when its amir was taken alive at Caleran in 957, every district beyond Caleran was left sworn to a man in Thalmyric keeping. [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]] · [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Reclamation of Caleran.md|The Reclamation of Caleran]]

**The Keepers at the Lamp (957 AR — Toledo).** TORQUE: Caleran was not surrendered by its ruler. Its amir tried to carry off the Ember as the price of a welcome home; the deposit's Covenant keepers, whose names were pledged that it would never be moved, held it against him, eleven died, and the dockers burned his ships. The keepers then made the terms that named the garrison families protected and left their amir outside them as the thief of a deposit; he was taken in the harbour fort nineteen days later. Iqashar counts them among its honest dead, and Caleran prays for them by name. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Reclamation of Caleran.md|The Reclamation of Caleran]] · [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]]

**The Captive's Releases (957–c. 971 AR — the taifas).** TORQUE: the south was taken apart by its own amir's hand. Every garrison beyond Caleran was sworn to Durayd by name, and Caedros II kept him in a walled house at Veridun and had him sign their releases. An amir who took a release with terms opened his gates without breaking faith; an amir who refused had to swear his garrison to himself, in defiance of both empires. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Reclamation of Caleran.md|The Reclamation of Caleran]]

**The Canal Heads (957–1052 AR — the taifas and parias).** TORQUE: the amirates that refused release fell to water, not tribute. The occupation's estates drank from canals whose heads lay in hills the mountain leagues had held since the 910s; Caedros II's engineers took the heads valley by valley and set water-courts there with the Banu Mizan hour-cup, and farmers who registered got their share while the amirs kept walls above dry fields. No tribute passed between amir and emperor in either direction. The bronze tokens the Southern Houses carried north in 1052 were the occupation's canal shares, which is why the Southern Deeds are a quarrel over water as well as land. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Founding Flame.md|The Founding Flame]]

**The Banner at Kaltfurt and the Unworn Crown (974; 1006–1041 AR — Charlemagne's coronation and Verdun, bent twice).** TORQUE: the march banner kindled for a miller's widow, Hedwen, shielding a refugee column. The Sanctum's court examined it honestly, recorded it answering and credited it to Adalmar; the Primarch who heard Hedwen would not crown over a living witness, and his successor struck her testimony through and crowned Adalmar in the mud of the ford in 1017, after Veridun had refused to unhood its own proof. The rule that made him emperor, that an emperor is the man who proves lawful, then barred every heir he had: no Adalmarid banner ever answered, the Crown of Embers was never worn again, and when his grandsons drew lots at Drossfeld (1041) for the right to be examined, the lot came up blank. Eclessia's borders are that afternoon's truce line, and the smudged tablet of the middle march is its oldest lawsuit. [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]]

**The Breaking and the Hooded Lamp (982–1052 AR — iconoclasm, bent twice).** TORQUE: no emperor decreed it and no empress ended it. The restored church's lamps guttered for the Guttering's reason, and when a bought chaplain's lamp stayed dark while a town burned, its survivors began asking every lamp in the empire to burn for them and breaking the ones that would not. The Fire does not come for the asking, so the test broke true lamps with false. Tavoran III hooded the Great Lamp of Veridun to keep it from the question; the Breakers' own hero, Makarios, spent his life teaching that there is no test; the crown gathered the surviving lamps into houses to protect them and its keepers sold the oil until the empire's household fires died (1049). Families walked to the Ember for a light, and the Rekindling (1052) relit every lamp from it, so that Thalmyria's whole fire "burns on a receipt", all but the Great Lamp, which was found burning when the hood came off after sixty-six years. Thalmyric theophany is the memory of the Breakers' question; Eclessian protocol looks to Veridun like the same question in vestments. [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]]

**The Sayings for the Foot of a Lamp (c. 986–1012 AR — John of Damascus, bent twice).** TORQUE: the answer to the Breakers was merchandise made under their noses. A Velian-born potter-monk at Keramion stamped forty sayings on the feet of copper-a-piece clay lamps; the Breakers smashed the bowls and left the feet, which households mortared into their walls. *Nobody breaks a door because the guest is late.*

**The Standing of the Water (994 AR — the Saxon wars).** TORQUE: the winter after the Drowning, Wolfsmere rose on a windless night and took the Markish shore-fort; the nine survivors laid it down. Eclessia's southern frontier has stopped at the lake country ever since, and the Peace of the Bent Oak (999), won in the bishops' own court by the baptised chief whose safe-conduct the king had broken, struck the grants beyond the lake from the register and wrote in the lake-law: no bell, no boat, no iron. The four thousand were the clans' living land-register, drowned at a recital of boundaries that contradicted the king's grants.

**The Pledged Custodian (1003–1011 AR).** TORQUE: a lending house holding the Vault chief's true name made every bound levy's oath doubtful, and the Covenant army sat in its camp through Thalmyria's Breaking for want of certainty about whose soldiers they were. The Custodian freed the Vault by surrendering his own name into its deepest custody, and the ink of it faded from every page. Every Custodian since has done the same, which is why the Unnamed Custodian is the one officer no court can blackmail.

**The Four Nails of Makarios (1015 AR — 1054, bent).** TORQUE: the Split was a refusal to lift a hood. Aurelium offered to record Veridun's emperor as lawful if the Great Lamp burned under examination. The emperor was afraid to know and the Patriarch would let nobody ask; Makarios nailed the hood down while the legate stood on the steps. The first refusal of a test as an act of sovereignty, and the root of deterrence by unopened proof. [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]]

**The Bells and the Unlifted Anathemas (1015 AR — 1054).** TORQUE: both churches came to believe that whichever lifts its anathema first confesses that the grieving bells were its own. The Bells stay unexplained ([[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]]); the belief is in-world, and it is why neither anathema has been lifted.

**The Two Houses of the Vault (1059 AR).** TORQUE: custody was split between the living (Iqashar) and the dead and sacred (Al-Qahur), with the Living Register's chief's own name held in the Deep Vault. The capital and the sacred city cannot rule without each other, and, unknown to anyone in-world, the architecture can never be audited from inside.

**The Choosing at Strazgorod (1059–1061 AR — the Rus, bent).** TORQUE: there was no embassy and no test of faiths. A Grass-Tarkhan raid put out a heathen prince's house-fire, and Vesari hearth-law made a house the client of whoever relit its fire. Radomir would be no neighbour's man, so his fire-keeper Zdena carried the cold lamp to Veridun to ask for a light from the Ember that had relit an empire. Makarios the Adamant refused to light a heathen hearth, and the cold lamp took the Flame in her hands, lit by no one, before witnesses of both churches, among them a Sanctum examiner, who recorded it answering. Makarios kept it as the Fire's chosen dwelling, which under hearth-law made Radomir Veridun's client, and he took his fire from the Sanctum's altar instead, becoming the client of a church two months' ride away. There was no marriage: the kingless lords of the eastern march chose him king in a moot of sabres (1064). That lamp, **the Virelian Lamp, still burns in Veridun's cathedral**, tended for three centuries by the fire-keeper's descendants as an honoured hostage. At every Virelian succession election since the first, in 1103, the Patriarchate formally offers to *return the lamp*, and a fire handed back from another's hall is a lent fire: the standing seduction behind every "eastern candidate," and the reason the Sanctum's agents attend Virelian elections with unclerical attention.

**The Cold Way (1123 AR — no direct analog).** TORQUE: Korrheim's first cantons were won when escaped mine slaves sent condemned overseers through a broken mountain passage and used what they learned to break the counts' army. Their liberator hid what the passage did to the prisoners and confessed it fourteen years later. The cantons keep their feast and, the next morning, the victims' names.

**The District Lamps and the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139 AR — Basil II's frontier).** TORQUE: a lawsuit fought with engines. The Court of Breaches ruled Caedros III's frontier districts a breach of the surrender deeds held by the Southern Houses, and the mercy towers proclaimed property-for-surrender terms to settlers who held their land on condition of defence and for whom surrender meant forfeiture. The settlers' lamps kindled because they were defending their homes, the emperor's own at Kolonna among them; the persuasion engines could not model a people with nothing to sell, escalated where they should have stopped, and one tower was lost. The Covenant then ransomed its captured custodians, because their registers held the names of its living soldiers.

**The Unanswered Funeral (1139–1162 AR — Basil II's succession, bent twice).** TORQUE: the Wall of the World left his succession to the Fire to bar his own son, the butcher of Vresna, without naming him. At the bier no claimant's lamp kindled; the son's flared with bought script-fire, and the court, wanting an emperor before morning, silenced the warlock who smelled it and acclaimed him. A district widow's lamp kindled, and the district armies made her warrant theirs and beat him in three years. Nobody was blinded: on the widow's word he kept a chapel over the men he had killed. Theophany's own temptation, to make the Fire decide politics, put a counterfeit on the throne first and the districts second.

**The Hunt of 1163 (no direct analog).** TORQUE: the Great Southern Purge cut the boundary groves; a court's Hunt rode the marcher camps for three nights and took an Aveline chapter and Folk households alike. The Purge ended, and the Elsin tradition won Folk policy for a generation.

**The Floor beyond the Count (c. 1171–1173 AR — no direct analog).** TORQUE: a mortality beneath a raptor of white fire followed the census couriers of a prince seeking a crown, and stopped at a bereaved household's threshing floor. Elenara stayed a duchy, and census law still requires a stated purpose.

**The Ledger of Debts (1190–1243 AR — Suleiman's kanun).** TORQUE: the classical age's great book is a confession. Suhrab abandoned the forty-year attempt to rank the provincial codes and entered the law's killings of its own people as debts, each closed with a clause every code must yield to. Its first book is Bir Qassim, read at the dry well in 1194 to mourners who answered each clause *And yet the well was dry*; the crowds named him the Debtor. Its book of the Southern Deeds produced the Unburdened Deeds (1212) and so Qarath's Well. [[Historical Spine of the Covenant]]

**Qarath's Well and the Poured Cup (1214–1225 AR — Manzikert and the Komnenian rise, bent twice).** TORQUE: the battle was lost to terms cut into a well-curb in three scripts: a guest's peace until the next sunset, and a debt, "the price of the water", which the house names. Nikandros, a Kedravan commander schooled in a refuge monastery, read them aloud and was overruled; the army drank at dusk, the emperor last, and sickened at dawn when it took up arms against its host. No sovereign met a sovereign. At sunset the well's bound warden named the price in wet letters on the curb, *a well for every cup*, and the Sultan-Caliph, who had wanted a bounded cession, could not lower it. Each drinker owed the well of his holding and stayed sick until he gave it; the Throne's cup was held to be owed the aqueduct head at Kolonna, and the emperor would not give it. The court made it worse: the chancellor pressed the sick emperor's seal to a decree taking the drinkers' farms, and when the emperor had himself carried out of his own palace, told Iqashar the debt had left with him. The Covenant answered that the Throne had drunk and had not left the palace. The next line began when the emperor, still sick eleven years after the well and ten of them in a monastery cell, poured out a cup of water untasted at Virelos and gave his claim to the man who had read the curb (1225). Every emperor since pours out the cup at his coronation, and none drinks as a Covenant guest or signs a Covenant instrument. [[High Noon and the Qarath Disaster]]

### Plague, the Black Wind and the held proofs (1225–1360 AR)

**The Refusal of the Lamp (1227 AR — Byzantium's appeals to the West, reversed).** TORQUE: the restorer asked for nothing. The Sanctum came to him with the Crown of Embers, Eclessian lances and three stolen valleys, and its price was a **test**: the Great Lamp of Veridun tried by the Sanctum's relic court. Nikandros I refused a crown: "a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned." The second refusal of that test, after Makarios's nails, and the one that made refusal state policy.

**The Grey Sweat (1232–1247 — the Black Death).** TORQUE: on Earth plague is biology; here mass death is a **soul-logistics catastrophe**. The Sweat is remembered as *the years the ferry queued*: the Vestibule's intake overwhelmed, burial rites collapsing faster than bodies could be blessed, and the **Undertow's harvest of the century** — their oldest standing routes date from it, bought in bulk from the unburied. Church quarantine doctrine was therefore written about **rite-integrity, not contagion** (a sealed village with a living priest was "clean"; a healthy village with a dead one was not), and the Ash-Walkers were not merely hysterics — where they walked, the records show, the thin places closed. Nobody has ever wanted to study why. In Covenant lands the Sweat is *the Emptying of the Benches*: deaths outran the witnesses needed to release bound names and hold garrison oaths, frontier forts opened their gates (the Open Gates, 1238–1246), and the Vault's living and dead registers have never balanced since. In Virelia the quarantine rule became the **bond of the clean village**: magnates sealed priestless villages and moved the survivors onto demesne under bond for a priest and burial, the root of Virelia's bound estates; in the west tenants used the same rule to walk to a living priest and won the clean-road charters. The fever killed priests faster than parishioners, and Ash-Walkers carried the ash of the unblessed pyres to churchyards that still had one. In Thalmyria laity sang their own dead out, and fewer villages were haunted.

**The Ferryman Primarch (1240–1242 AR — the Western Schism).** TORQUE: the schism is the plague's speed inside the conclave. The elected Primarch died unsung on his election night; his twin reigned in his ring for twenty months under the quarantine rule's threat and issued the Ferry Rule for lay funerals; the dead brother's unsent figure exposed him. The two colleges split over whether a false Primarch's acts send the dead, and the doubt fed the Chancery of Sendings. No rival cities, no antipopes, no council to depose them. [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|The Grey Sweat]]

**The Rider of the Benches (1241–1243 AR — Suleiman and Mustafa).** TORQUE: the Guard's power over the succession came from a father's grief. Prince Mihrzad held the Qarath-frontier forts in the Emptying by riding fort to fort as a living witness, carried the Sweat into three of them and died where only his Guard escort survived to see it; with no competent witness his name could not be released. Suhrab's last book named the Guard bench a competent witness so that it could be, and every accession since has been made before that bench. [[Historical Spine of the Covenant]]

**The Black Colt (1249 AR — the rise of a steppe conqueror).** TORQUE: Qarayel's empire was a renewed grace. Grey Sister's lines were thinning when Gavrin, a Velian slave farrier, sang the old remount hymn over a dying black colt; the colt and its get stood before bound things. Qarayel built his coalition by stud-right, extracted from frontiers whose bound wardens could not frighten his horses, and lost the advantage as the grace thinned (1300). The herds came home from his burial in four bands, and the Partition of the Herds (1304) could only name them as the four Hordes. [[Qarayel and the Black Wind]]

**The Pageless Prince (1249–1252 AR — the Ottoman law of fratricide).** TORQUE: a lodged prince whose register page burned in the Sweat walked free under "no page, no lodging". After him the Cull used the knife: "the Vault can lose a page; the grave cannot." Since Suhrab's last book (1243) the Guard's officer bench has witnessed every accession, which is what "Guard-brokered" means.

**Dead Paper and the Sufficient Seal (1252–1290 AR — indulgences; the Borgia papacy).** TORQUE: intercession works, so the Sending Houses worked where rites were sung, and the trade spread on evidence. The bought Primarch who broke it was an ascetic, and his sin was sincerity and alms. Medori credit bought Alessior II's election without his asking; his Bull of the Sufficient Seal (1283) made the seal itself the Sending, and he gave letters away by the hundred thousand, so that parishes stopped singing for their poor. An unsung sealed letter is a lien on a named soul's passage that Patrons, the Undertow and brokers can press. He died in debt for his election and was seen at his signing desk: no dome, no nepotism, no poison. The Council of Veridance repudiated the Seal and so owed the Great Arrears, the old Church singing its own debts from registers the Medori have held since 1289. [[Freeflame Reformation]]

**The Lantern (1285–1324 AR — the Jesuits).** TORQUE: the reforming order is older than the Reformation. It began as lay choirs who sang the poor out for nothing under Gerlind's rule of a priest present, was burned out of Saint-Leon in 1285 and sang at night for forty years. Its priest died untried in an Inquisitors' cell nine years before the Council chartered it, which is why the Inquisitors answer to it now. No soldier, no wound, no founder-general.

**The Twice-Turned (1288–1291 AR — Sabbatai Zevi).** TORQUE: the claimant rode a sign from the Black Wind and the Wind's own word; the Covenant ruined his followers by voiding their charters; at the Empty City he was asked to touch the held gate and twice turned back. No arrest by a sultan, no apostasy, no exile; the gate stays untested. [[Mesakha Traditions]]

**The Blackwater Funerals and Aldersbruck (1290–1299 AR — the posted theses; Worms).** TORQUE: the reformer was dead before the reform. A Sending House chaplain kept his count in his psalter's margins and drowned with his miners; a tally-keeper read the margins at their funeral, and the movement was a women's funeral strike with no doctrine for more than twenty years. The synod tried the book because it could not try the man; the book did not burn, the test was voided, and the country was charged a second fee for its funerals. No theses, no door, no trial of the man, no martyrdom by authority. [[Freeflame Reformation]]

**The Ashenmen and the Preacher's Water (1299–1343 AR — the Peasants' War).** TORQUE: the Blackwater's drowned went into the Weiss below the broken bridge at Aldersbruck, and that reach is **the Preacher's Water**: oaths sworn on it hold strangely well, the relic courts' one water-test came back *inconclusive* (the file is sealed; inconclusive frightened them more than positive would have), and the Freeflame, whose theology forbids relics, are stuck with a **river** — which cannot be enshrined, sold, or stolen. The Ashenmen swore in it in 1306, after the second fee was sold to brokers, and no band broke its oath in twenty-three years. They fought out of the pits of the upper Weiss and starved the iron country; the Caldrian drowning of the Brenholt drifts (1314) killed some three hundred people without ending the war. Eleven of them swore on the same water to the Mother's appearance at Sarnwell (1311), so the old Church's proudest proof rests on its enemies' oaths. The Risings ended when Rainald burned the seals (1329) and fulfilled the oath's letter. The Korrheim Accord (1343) was sworn with hands in the same water, which is why a peace nobody believes in has held; the movement's first congregations, who bury in it, were left outside.

**The Council before the Princes (c. 1309–1329 AR — Calvin; Trent).** TORQUE: the systematiser was the Sanctum's commissioned prosecutor, whose fair statement of the funerals became their order and whose refutation never came. The old Church's council sat early and briefly (1319–1324), before any prince had reformed, because its own churchyards walked and the heretics' lay quiet; it answered the book with its author in the room, repudiated the Seal and ordered the Arrears. The levy that paid for them is what turned an old-Church duke, shown his own father unsung, into the first reformed prince. The Counter-Reformation caused the princely Reformation. [[Freeflame Reformation]]

**The Factor's Book (c. 1318–1347 AR — *The Prince*).** TORQUE: the banned book is a lender's manual for reading sovereigns, written for the Medori counting-floors by a factor the Bank later sold into its own Debtors' Corps. He sold the register extracts that started the Fiorenzian crisis, was drowned for it, and a Sermoran press printed his desk-book under a title and a dedication he never gave it. No exile, no plea for office, no advice to a prince. [[Of Princes as Borrowers — Selected Maxims]]

**The Night Count (1340–1343 AR — Augsburg).** TORQUE: no prince chose his rite. A canton rose sooner than dig up its dead, a war-priest's burial articles gave the rule, a princeless guild ministry banked Ironvale's furnaces until the princes talked, and commissioners sat up in disputed churchyards to see whether the dead walked. Every yard went to the rite that had kept its dead sent and every lord took his land's lamp: *every roof under its lord's lamp, and every lamp where its dead lie*. A yard unsent for a year and a day is forfeit, so the quarrel now runs through funerals. The date stands because the present board and playable settings need it. [[The Korrheim Accord]]

**The Faces and the Open Vault (1345–1349 AR — Savonarola; the Medici restoration).** TORQUE: Fiorenzia's dead are wax faces on its walls, and when a pamphlet named the unsung among them the faces began to speak. The senate burned them at the Feast of Souls against the friar's warning, and something stood in the fire. The bank was never expelled: a mask-maker's son who had married into it opened the vault and gave the city its sheets, the friar led a four-year Vigil from them, and the citizens voted the bankers a hereditary chair over the senate's head. The friar preached against the gift and was found dead in a wax face nobody could lift. No carnival, no flight, no return, no ordeal, no execution of the friar. [[Historical Spine of the Sestaran Concord]]

**The Great Split, the Reformation, the crusade cycles.** Already majority-native (Kindling Question, Caedros's conditional hymns, routing assurances, Ansgar's Count, the unbought funerals, the voided test at Aldersbruck, the second fee, the Weiss oaths, the war from the mines, the Great Arrears and their levy, the Council's witness, the Night Count) — the torque holding: keep leading with the doctrine-mechanics, not the 1054/1517 furniture. The Church's *numbering* of crusades stays: bureaucratic numbering is in-world habit, not Earth residue.

**Resolved in later culture passes:** copied governing bodies and signature social vocabulary were replaced with setting-native institutions. Broad translation titles such as Doge remain deliberate conventions where controlled by the relevant culture hub.
