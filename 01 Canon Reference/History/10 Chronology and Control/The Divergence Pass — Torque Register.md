# The Divergence Pass — Torque Register

The anti-derivative instrument. Diagnosis: material generated by the world's own physics never reads as borrowed; material imported from history reads borrowed wherever Earth's causal engine came with it. The cure is **torque** — for every imported beat, one consequence only this cosmology could produce, canonised here and made the famous part of the event.

## The Three Standing Rules

**1. The Forward Rule.** Real-history analogs may govern the *past* (familiarity is an entry ramp). From 1360 AR forward, **no event may be predictable from its analog** — the present and future run on world-native engines only (see [[The Age of Held Proofs]]). If a reader who knows the textbook can guess the next beat, the beat is wrong.

**2. The Torque Rule.** Every analog beat carries at least one canonised consequence below. When writing about any of these events, *lead with the torque* — the analog is background, the torque is the story.

**3. The Consequence Rule.** Supernatural controls govern *what is true*: who a being is, what a soul is, what a sign proves and what no ritual can confer. They do not govern *what is allowed to happen*. A Mask, Menhir, sign, bargain, relic, kindling or contract may decide who wins, who rules, who starves and what a people comes to believe, provided its nature obeys the cosmology and its price is paid. Every age carries at least one such consequence in this register.

Where an integration review or package limits a supernatural power's *consequences* rather than its *nature*, this rule supersedes it. The superseded rulings are:

- [[Package B - Qiryath and Eastern Empires Canon Integration Review]]: Astaroth's campaign correspondences and Father Sun's sign "provide information or passage only."
- [[Atherian Palace and Maritime Histories - Integration Review]]: the final gateway war as "a conventional regional war with local Mask activity", the bar on the salting acting against the gateway cult's Mask, and the ruling that the final gateway siege has no causal connection to Orthea.
- [[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review]]: the rejection of a Pride apparition as a duplicate device.

Ontological controls in those files remain in force: complete human souls, no pre-Redeemer saints, Masks distinct from persons, signs that prove only what they prove.

## The Register

Entries run in date order from the end of the Age of Chains to the eve of 1360. The register first covered only 292 AR onward; on 14 November 2026 the Causality Pass added the ages before 292 and filled the gaps after 395, and the bending passes of the same day (10A to 10F) replaced or added the entries for the sequences that still followed their analogues beat for beat. [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]] is the record of the decision behind each entry, and [[Causality Pass - Direction]] the rules they were written under. Entries with no analog, or none direct, bend the world's own chain rather than a borrowed beat; they stand here because later torques inherit them.

### Before the empire (to 350 BR)

**The Cost of Continuity (c. 2400–2260 BR — the fall of the god-kings).** TORQUE: Giant blood thinned, and the ancestral works answered thinner blood only when fed from outside: crews, then captives, then the granaries of living districts, at last children for the vaults. That hunger is the cry the Lord answered and the line along which the plagues struck; revolt began where the takers came. The famous catastrophes (a Naga lord's ritual drinking the Serpent's Spine, the Blood Sun bursting) are survivor legend for unmended dikes and an untended vent-stair. Vathra's sky roads failed the same way: Hecatara's displaced distance, carried by Titan blood while it was strong and by marched-out captives after it thinned, came back all at once and stretched the valleys under the crown-cities. Later peoples fear anything kept past its time because that is how the Giants ate people. [[Age of Chains]]

**The Round of the Hundred Wells (c. 2255 BR — the war-chief who becomes king).** TORQUE: a Menhir bargain binds the named petitioner's line *as it was reckoned that day*, so a crisis leader becomes a dynasty because only that blood can keep paying. Meya of Narash, who had sold captives in Vathra's wars, sealed the Hundred Wells against every shortcut, and every crown-mother since has walked the round at accession and called each well by name. That is why Narashi authority still runs through mothers, and why one crown-mother's fever in the Bronze-Road drought let raiders into the inner cisterns for a season. Ark law founded no office on a bargain, so Ahdari leaders stayed removable judges and their wells needed walls. [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]]

**The Provision Ends and the Four Deaths (c. 2180–2165 BR — the manna ceasing; Moses on Nebo).** TORQUE: the miracle stopped on schedule, because its commission was the refuge and not the people, and it stopped on a host that had forgotten how to farm. The Vacant Commission and the Five Staffs were fights over the seed jars the drought-struck rear camps were eating; the Southern Seizures took ploughmen with their fields, and enslaved Orphaned farmers taught their captors the farming year, which is where Atherian slavery and adoption custom begin. The southern camps' saying, *the Lord who stopped feeding us; the stars that still answer*, is the root of Atherian star-worship: a people fed daily for a century could not tell a finished commission from abandonment, and the Menhir answer whoever performs the rite. The Prophet died in the season the bread stopped, and every people tells his death as an account of who was fed: the Jirahar Slow Road, the Ahdari Unpaid Well, the Atherian Ascent, the Folk satire of the Guest Who Died at the Door. All four keep one line, *no one is owned*; the spine keeps the rest unanswered. [[Age of Exodus]] · [[The Glass Sojourn]]

**The Copy-King of High Harbour (c. 1980–1960 BR — the Trojan War).** TORQUE: the coalition besieged an office, not a king. A speaking image of the harbour's founder was the named party to the tide mistress's pact, and the captives of the collection raids were her price. The commander wanted the image whole, to inherit the harbour and its tide; the shore households wanted it broken; the harbour's own craftspeople broke it in the fire. The pact defaulted on every ship carrying the harbour's plunder, and that is the Returnings: one returning lord drowned the High Harbour women of his household to pay a pact that no longer existed. Southern sea custom since: no sea pact bound to an image or office "that cannot drown". [[The Siege and the Returnings]]

**The Reclaiming (c. 1450–1325 BR — the Late Bronze Age collapse).** TORQUE: the Bronze Roads ran partly on recast Ishkatari tomb metal that still bore its owners' title marks. When ruin traders opened deeper estate-vaults, the Gilded Wardens, Processional Coils and Estate Dead woke and walked the old ceremonial roads, which the trade had reused as trunk routes, recovering "misplaced property": caravans were collected and palace stores visited. Failed floods, palace wars and unpaid soldiers did the rest, and trunk roads failed before short ones. Iron became the road metal of the north and east because nobody dead owned it. [[Gilded Wardens]]

**The Water in the Wrong Season (c. 1290 BR — Gideon and Abimelech).** TORQUE: a named-petitioner bargain bound Tovan's line as the shelf villages reckoned it that day, through mothers. The water he named drowned House Sereq with the pledge-children in its pens; the road judges gave his household no office, because the Prophet's people founded none on a bargain; and at his death the streams answered his sister's children. His sons held her son on the threshing floor to make him call the water for them, the rite was missed, and the stream drowned them there. "Feed the cistern house; do not follow it" is the Jirahar line between a bargain and an office. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/70 History and Conflicts/Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria.md|Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria]]

**The Factor of Qemaret (c. 1084–1076 BR — the expulsion of the Hyksos).** TORQUE: the foreign crown fell to its dead creditor. Two low floods left the Yasharet pledge to Khaseret's House of the Returning Cargo unpaid; when Iddar sealed the store courts, the House's factor, Aramen of Sakhret, fed the unpaid garrisons from its warehouses, and Qemaret's gate guards opened the river gate to the men who had fed them. The soldiers treated the store courts as collateral, the House's cellars admitted only clients with clear accounts, and the last king was walled into its vault as returned cargo. The Yasharet households stayed. The southern restoration is court poetry; the empire's conquests paid the dead, and its bankruptcy in c. 858 completed its founding. [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]]

**The Guardian at the Stair (c. 959–746 BR — Saul to David; Bathsheba and Nathan; Ahab, Naboth and Elijah).** TORQUE: Qiryath's sign made kings by never refusing. A guardian who carried the appointed offering was cheered beside cool stones at every festival, so emergency commands became reigns, and a wood-carrier who caught a fevered guardian's basket became guardian. When Yedran left a ford garrison to die rather than miss the stair, the widow Rava asked the custodians aloud whom the stones answered; the answer, the offering, ended the claim, and the bearer was chosen by lot. Qoram later used the same answer to put an Arqeshite image on the stair, killing the custodian who would not witness beside it. The stones were cool, as they always were, and Neshkar II's demand of 746 relied on that morning. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/70 History and Conflicts/Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria.md|Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria]]

**The Dead Landlords of Qahur (c. 903–858 BR — the Ramesside temple estates).** TORQUE: the Aramen crown, put on the throne by a dead creditor, was bankrupted by its dead. Mortuary estates and Second Houses, some of whose owners were awake, held the Basin's best fields, metal and crews, paid no levy, lent grain and copper for the crown's wars and took fields in return. When Kassur I's provinces pushed Arqesh to the eastern caravan stages, the crown could pay its southern garrisons only in land already tilled for tombs. The garrisons sold themselves to temple and military houses, the desert mines were abandoned, and Qahur entered the Arqeshite century owned by its dead. [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]]

**The Court at Noon (c. 842–814 BR — Akhenaten, Amarna and the restoration).** TORQUE: the sun cult was a war on the dead landlords, and the dead won it from inside the palace. Ithren III's Radiance confiscated the estates of the dead for the soldiers who had been paid in graves and forbade burial at Aseret. His sick son took the White Measure rite from the Copper Orchard and died bound to the confiscated treasury, so the deeds of nearly every field the Radiance had taken were sealed inside one dead king's house. The soldiers who rose against his rent-collectors crowned Zaheret at Tenaret and kept the temple fields; the temples bought, married and sued their way back over the following decades, often on White Measure credit. Aseret stayed inhabited as the dead king's rent town, and the Arqeshite prince who walked in to have stewards' deeds confirmed was lost there. [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]] · [[The White Measure and the Second Houses]]

**The Withholding and the Cool Stones (743–654 BR — the Babylonian exile and return).** TORQUE: Qiryath's sign was physical and witnessed (an accepted offering burned without smoke and left the stones cool under the hands of three old houses), and it stopped in the siege's first winter, when the council drove foreigners out to die between the lines. Nobody, the spine included, knows which act it answered. The exiles judged on witnesses alone and kept the Book of the Unanswered Years. Vashar rebuilt the altar as a wager on the sign's return; the offering made in its king's name smoked, and the court proclaimed the Witness of Dawn as the sign transferred to the crown. Six years later the stones went cool under an offering carried by the settler-spouses the severe party wanted expelled. The Jirahar have outlived one withholding, and they remember that it ended.

**The Wood that Closed (687–686 BR — the army's refusal at the Hyphasis).** TORQUE: no army refused Eryndor; a wood did. Beyond the Vashari highlands a Root-Mind grove offered passage for the valley terraces it wanted back. Eryndor refused the price, felled the speaking trees for a military road and marched on, leaving the cut ground behind him, and the Root-Mind's law ran: roots crossed the cut, worked timber sprouted, and the road behind the army edited itself into a different valley each morning. He cut his way out, leaving prisoners, the villages that had fed him and the farther garrisons on the far side; those garrisons, beyond anyone's reach, became the farther-eastern Successor. The wound he took in the cutting sent him to the seep that heals only those who stay, and, because he would not stay, to the White Measure. [[Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest]] · [[The Five Limits of Eryndor]]

**The Voice at Delkos (695–c. 655 BR — Siwa, the end of Darius and Ptolemy with Alexander's body).** TORQUE: the corpse that governed was assembled over eleven years. In 695 Eryndor fed his army on grain lent by Khaseret's House of the Returning Cargo, a Qemaret Patron four centuries dead, against her masters' right to prepare his body. In 688 Kassur III's portable images, in which the Crowned Countenance was anchored, began to answer in the voice of the king most bowed to; their bearers carried them to Eryndor, and the Mask learned his voice while he lived. Kassur, a king with no face, surrendered to Arezan and sealed the Gate House's water books to him, which is why the Ubar's canal offices served Vashar. In 684 Eryndor died aboard Serevan's flagship in the roads of Nemeru, waiting for a wind to take him home; the masters performed the final rite in a ship's hold with no house to close him into, bound his name into an anchor his soul had already left, and the Countenance filled it. At Delkos the dead king declared himself alive and heirless and sealed grants. The half-brother Demeran held was murdered because a reigning corpse made him worthless; Parena's daughter Mehrava lived at Kavresh as a claim the corpse had disowned and Vashar kept, and the later Tiravans descend from her. The wars ran thirty-four years because a Pride Mask fed on rival petitioners and promised the same provinces twice. It ended when the masters testified against their own failed rite and Serevan's grandson walled the tomb; "a Delkos voice" is the eastern sea's phrase for a convincing counterfeit. [[Wars of the Inheritance]]

**The Unreckoned Days (c. 652 BR — the Delian and Latin leagues).** TORQUE: Caleran ruled its allies through the sky before it ruled them by the sword. As keeper of the Compact's calendar it could leave a member's days out of the year, and the Common Sky held that offerings made on unreckoned days went unseen, so the city's own sailors would not put to sea. Verion's tyrant fell to the calendar in three seasons. [[The Celestial Compact and the Caleran Republic]]

**Zamaret's Bread (c. 612–601 BR — Hannibal in Italy).** TORQUE: the invader's gift was the weapon that failed him. Zamaret fed wavering Atherian towns with grain from the Provider's table, and every town that ate it found its own sealed stores short at the next count, the seals unbroken and the wax bitten. "Do not eat the Gateway's bread" became the allies' proverb, and the alliance held out of fear of the gift more than loyalty to Caleran. [[The Gateway Wars]]

**The Lustration of the Field (609 BR — the Forum Boarium burials; Fabius inverted).** TORQUE: Caleran killed its Gateway hostages and captives on its festival ground to give Astaroth a contestant. The Red Star's law forbids substituting a helpless victim for the named contestant, and the next year the war spread exactly as that law's failure runs. The augurs concluded that the victims had been wrong, not the killing, and created the Warden of the Red Field, a living commander who must stand in person and may not quit the field while his named enemy holds it; so the first Warden, Celor Marath, offered battle below Thesra in 606 where a delaying general would have waited. Every case Caleran later made against Qarzeth's altars was made from that festival ground, and the Gateway towns read the children's names back to it. [[The Gateway Wars]] · [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth.md|Astaroth]]

**The Unranked Table (c. 526–520 BR — the destruction of Carthage).** TORQUE: Qarzeth's notorious recoveries were real plenty, drawn by the Bronze-Faced Provider out of its dependent towns, and the Three Collections were the bill. The capital fell as a Gluttony form falls: the towns stopped carrying, Ilya's list gave the dead back their names, the bronze face broke, and the survivors sealed the end at Sarqen with a meal at which no one was served by worth. Its rules became the Table Oath every Nerethan ruler swore at accession. The salt on the ruin came from that table and was laid where the plenty had grown, so that nothing could be served from that ground again. [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth]]

**The King's Bread at Ilyessa (495–491 BR — Salamis and Xerxes' retreat).** TORQUE: the sea, not a fleet, took Vashar's war back. The royal fleet passed the Narrows of Ilyessa in 495 on terms the Ilyessan pilots kept with a tide court of Thalassor's grammar: keep moving, never ask the water to hold you still, never call the sea yours. In 493 Xarun took the title Keeper of the Roads by Land and by Water and anchored his granary fleet in the Ilyessa lee for the siege of Velathene. The holds took the tide: water-lines inside dry planking, salt under sealed hatches, grain rotting in sound jars, hulls settling without a leak. His subject crews rowed for Velathene's refuge fire, whose wardens, raised on Kelarion's Roll, admitted any hull in distress, and the League took the transports with the deserters at its oars. The army before Velathene starved, and Ardash Aresh's price for the Avesh grain made the western road command a family possession, the root of the revolt of 462. The League that won by giving refuge to its enemy's crews was raising its chains against its own members within a generation. [[The Eastern Sea Wars]] · [[The Refusal at Kelarion]]

**The Noon Sighting (488 BR — the Gracchi).** TORQUE: Damar's veterans asked the Sun, not the senate, to show the public land's boundaries, and for one noon hour every shadow in the lower Aurin pointed along the old lines. When the petitioners also asked the land to bear for them without limit, the harvest ripened in days and rotted, and Damar died in the bread crowd that followed. The augurs' Licensed Sighting of 486 turned the disaster into a monopoly over signs, and the surveyors' Noon Roll waited a century for Casvilar's veterans. [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] · [[Father Sun]]

**The Closed Chains (466–461 BR — the Social War).** TORQUE: Caleran turned the Oar League's refuge custom, taken from Kelarion's Roll, into a privilege for cities that paid, and ordered harbours to close their chains to rebel hulls. In the gale of 466 the drowned harbour's old wound partly opened at Pyrassa: the closed harbour admitted no one, not even its own grain ships, until its wardens cut their chain. The Chain Peace made refuge law for any vessel in distress, and Ilamar's custodians have kept a copy of the Roll ever since. [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] · [[The Shoreless King]]

**The Red Field (466–350 BR — Sulla and Caesar).** TORQUE: the Warden's office was a bargain with Astaroth that its holder could not lay down while a named opponent still contested the field. Harod's opponents lived at Avesh, so he governed from a tent for twenty-five years and died in it. Casvilar accepted the Field in 376 and could not surrender his command. Seven challengers entered their names and struck before the contest opened, and by Varroth's ruling the Field stood open while any of the seven lived, so for twenty years no commander dared disband an army and the succession wars became a hunt for seven men through the refuges of the inner sea. Thalion never accepted a field. When the last of the seven was dead he stood on the empty muster ground; Varroth would not declare it closed, the Red Star's priests did, and that is why Astaroth's priests take precedence in imperial ceremonies of victory and command. [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] · [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]]

**The Ended Acclamation (431 BR — Herod Agrippa, Acts 12).** TORQUE: Orvaz I of Vashar let the harvest crowd at Kavresh hail him as the one who made the grain rise, and took children for his dawn-house; he was struck down at the instant praise became worship. What went out was the light in the terrace mirrors: the Witness of Dawn left Vashar's royal cult for a generation, and Tiraz III's governors stopped fearing the king. [[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]]

**The Writing-In (367–364 BR — the proscriptions).** TORQUE: soldiers who would not murder citizens would kill a challenger on the open Field, so the Four at the Stores wrote their enemies into the register of challengers and had them killed on the muster ground. Substituting helpless victims for the named contestants is Astaroth's taboo, and the war spread instead of closing until Varroth's own garrisons burned his magazines. Belisar Venn, written in by his colleagues, walked down to the muster ground with a borrowed practice sword and was killed fighting. [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]]

**The Salt Field and the Filled Seat (353–350 BR — Actium and Alexandria).** TORQUE: there was no decisive sea battle and no double suicide. Velsar accepted a Red Field on Sarqen's salt pans and named Thalion, who never came; bound to the salt, Velsar's army had to be fed where it stood, and the queen's levies by worth, the Fourth Collection, brought Gluttony's bitten wax back into the spared towns' jars. When Amesar killed Casvilar's last murderer at the towns' table, the Table Oath that had crowned his mother put her house out and seated an abandoned rower in her place. Melvo killed Velsar on the field. Samyra saved herself and her children by sitting at the towns' table as a stranger, where the oath forbade anyone to take her, and Thalion had to eat at that table and swear its oath to have the country. Every governor of Sarqen swore it after him, and the wax was whole at the next count. [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]] · [[Samyra Nerethan]]

### The Atherian Empire to the Redeemer (350 BR–0 AR)

**The Star's Share (from 326 BR — the Fidenae collapse).** TORQUE: Thalion's heir Caelor died under a treasury hall the Red Star's priests had blessed and nobody had loaded; under Astaroth's law a work asked to stand without trial is forced to prove itself. From then on rune-trained wardens loaded every public work before the Red Star until a crack showed its weakest point, broke the portion built to be broken (the Star's share) and made the official who opened the work stand under the arch while the first wagons crossed. The extra stone drove the quarry strike of 274. Arcelon's embankment, tested in the dry and fallen in the rains, gave the empire "tested in the dry" and the rule that a test answers only the question asked. Velen I exempted his festival banks because the Emperor does not stand under, and drowned his audience. Astaroth stays morally blind; the custom decides who pays. [[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]] · [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth.md|Astaroth]]

**The Year the Portrait Spoke (247 BR — the Year of the Four Emperors).** TORQUE: in the empty succession the accession portrait at Caleran answered in the Emperor's voice, the first autonomous response of the dependent Pride Mask later called the Emperor after the Emperor. The Face had begun as one of the painted boards Thalion's companies carried and swore to while he refused to take the field in person. Avaren Mott governed through it, and its assurance that no imperial granary was empty sent soldiers behind the carried image into the poor districts, where the father of a household that would not kneel was killed in its doorway. Samor Veloran had the Face covered with a soldier's cloak and would not be addressed by it. Since then the Face has stayed veiled in the Common Sky's custody, unveiled last at an accession, while the Mask feeds through provincial copies; from 61 BR Doran's officers entered by the low door instead of swearing before the image. Whether the cloak fell before Mott died is disputed. [[The Year of Four Eagles]]

**The Loan before the Face (Raveth, from 119 BR — Joseph in Egypt).** TORQUE: where relief debts were sworn before the emperor's image, the dependent Pride Mask intensified compliance, and those households walked their animals to the estate office unasked while their countersigned neighbours argued and kept part of their land. Raveth remembers its dispossession through the image as well as through the Jirahar administrator, who resigned rather than administer the oath. [[The Keeper of Borrowed Grain]]

**The Trial before the Stars and the Qiryath Charter (under the late Serrats — Pompey and the Hasmoneans).** TORQUE: during Vashar's War of the Two Dawns, Qiryath's sign answered an interim Keeper while a rival held a Vasharan purchase of the office. The Atherian governor's augur asked the sign to decide between them; the purchaser refused, the interim Keeper offered, and the fire smoked. The governor ruled on the sanctuary's written record, and the charter a Qoreth scribe drafted (an imageless sanctuary, a daily offering for the emperor, the governor's capital jurisdiction at the pilgrimage, the dues as surety, copies in two archives) governed Qiryath until 65 AR. Its clauses returned in 0 AR and 61 AR. Vashar took the Dawn Portion of the pilgrim dues and called it rent. [[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]]

### The Redeemer and the conversion of the empire (0–411 AR)

**The Three Silent Days (0 AR — the cessation of oracles).** TORQUE: from the hour of the Binding to the third morning, no augural register in the empire records an answer. The colleges sealed the days, Tyros carried them into the open, and the Great Purge was fought to recover the copies. The registers prove a silence and nothing more. The bishops have held the originals since 383 and never published them. [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]]

**The Great Purge (c. 36–45 AR — the first-century persecutions).** TORQUE: a records war. The High Augurate recovered the books carried off by the colleges that followed Tyros, burned Jareth for "forging registers" before the Pillar into which it had leaded the Nails, and made publishing an unregistered wonder a crime. The Underground learned to write its miracles as weather and good sense, which is why the faith's earliest records understate their own signs.

**The Dawn Tower (65–68 AR — Masada).** TORQUE: the last stronghold was a deep tower, so the legions dug down instead of ramping up, dropping the outer gallery roofs where a siege frame loaded before the Red Star had broken. Its Way-Gate's partner bench, in the tower where the houses that refused the Qiryath charter had settled, would take the households and no one who had borne arms. For nine days the keeper Yaltah bat Ozer sent the households through while the fighters held the stairs; she broke the scroll-keys as the soldiers came, and the legions killed whoever was left. Jirahar gate benches still cite Shahar when they weigh another tower's danger against its people. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]]

**The Kept Lamps (c. 242–248 AR — Decius).** TORQUE: the edict answered a real Mask. Grave-lamps that leaned towards mourners and burned without oil fed the mutinies of the unpaid eastern garrisons, so Tervan made every household relight its lamps for the dead from the Stars' fire and burn them green on the colleges' copper-salted oil. Night watches walked the graves for yellow flames and found the burial fraternities first. The congregations relit from flames that had never burned green, their reconciliation quarrel turned on whom to give fire, and the daughter-flame custom began with Amaryah's lamp hidden in a bread oven at Veridun Minor. [[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization]]

**The Ironvale Sworn Account (291 AR — the Forty of Sebaste, bent).** TORQUE: the first sign sworn before crown officers outside the augurs' power. A guard, and a script-keeper freed so that his oath would stand, swore that the flame on the Forty was no Shell-fire. Cassian's camp read the account in the Aurin towns in 292; the crown's own prison roll still contradicts it. [[The Forty Flames]]

**Cassian's Vision (292 AR — Constantine).** TORQUE: the site is verifiable. The Aurin at the Stone Bridge of Velos **has never frozen since**, in eleven centuries of hard winters — a standing micro-theophany that passes every relic-court test and belongs to the *crown*, not the Church: history's first fight over whether a miracle can be owned (it continues). The augurs entered the sign in the crown's name; from c. 296 a Warden of the Reach kept its winter book, the price of empire-wide restitution (the Velos Concord). Cassian was baptised in the unfrozen reach in 316, and in 377 Vestian gave the book and chapel to the bishops and kept the water. That deathbed baptism is a named scandal — **the Cassian Wager**: one unrepented reign answered with one perfect absolution, cited by every deathbed-delayer since, condemned by every confessor, and unresolved because in this world the Weighing is real and *nobody knows if it worked.*

**Astrion's Restoration (348–351 AR).** Astrion demanded that the Stars answer as publicly as the river at Velos. **Astaroth answered Astrion's public test of legitimacy once**, revealing the weakness of the loaded support of an imperial standard at Caleran in 350. His structural answers to builders and siege engineers were standing practice (the Darneth precedent of 380 BR; the wardens' test from 326 BR); this was the only time he answered a test of a reign. Engineers cleared the ground before it failed. Astrion treated the answer as public support for his reign. The response established a fact about the loaded support, within Astaroth's power over resistance and structural weakness. The augurs' signed record reached the Sanctum and was sealed in its deepest registry. The collapse at Qiryath was a separate event with unresolved causes. Astaroth gave no terms for Astrion's proposed second test, of victory, and afterwards answered neither the last college's petition in 383 nor the eastern command at its crossing in 384. He died in 351 standing beneath an untried span at the Emperor's Cut while the wounded crossed; it failed by the builders' standing law of the untried work, not as a second answer to his reign. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]] · [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth.md|Astaroth]]

**The Two Standards (351–384 AR — Valens, Procopius and the Frigidus, bent).** TORQUE: Astrion's single answer outlived him as an army. For thirty-three years the command that had watered his column kept augurs and the Answered Standard, though no second answer came, and its augurs said so honestly. In 384 it asked the Red Star for terms at the decisive crossing and got none, and its own Redeemer units negotiated. Vestian won because the Stars would not answer his enemy.

**The Kellovar Ford (c. 368–377 AR — Adrianople).** TORQUE: since Cassian's market treaties the southern army had crossed the river-courts' fords behind Folk guides carrying household tokens, and Lucar's chaplains took the tokens as idols. The forts could not reach the far banks when the Grobi came; the fort-district households, their children pledged and sold for relief grain, escaped across the fords on hidden tokens; and when Lucar followed the last of them into the ford below Kellovar, the court took his unpaid army and carried him dry on a stolen silver token. He spent three days as a guest at the fire of the house whose token it was, and his own rearguard killed him when he rode back out of the water. The court exacted and judged nothing. Vestian, recognised while Lucar was still across the river, read it as judgement on a divided empire. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]]

**The Dissolution at Caleran (383 AR).** TORQUE: the last High Augur, Ilios Marrant, asked the Red Star to show his college's worth, setting no load and naming no price, and nothing answered before the crowd. By statute the authentication of signs passed from augurs to bishops, with the Deep Register and the three silent days.

**The Second Silence and the Raveth Hearing House (385–c. 389 AR — no analog; Callinicum).** TORQUE: the Profession's reception lamps did not kindle. Willing baptisms still kindled; state professions burned yellow. The court blamed hidden rites and the Jirahar, banned household worship and then took the lamp out of the oath, the first time an institution hid a falling yield instead of repenting of it. The house the Raveth crowd burned held sworn accounts, from baptised families of Jirahar descent, that the lamps had not kindled; clergy pressed Vestian to drop the rebuilding because rebuilding would restore the record, and the survivors carried the argument to Qabir, where it was condemned at Halmenth in 411. Aurelium recorded the Silence and sealed the record. The Church has never ruled whether it was a judgement, a lesson or the Flame keeping its terms. [[Vestian Persecutions and the Long Diaspora]]

**The Empty Charter (c. 396 AR).** TORQUE: Vestian struck out "in perpetuity" and wrote *before the Mesakha*. The Church reads never; the Jirahar read a promise in their persecutor's hand. The charter does not say who recognises the Mesakha, which makes it the only law in the world that a sign can repeal. [[Qiryath Reqa]]

**The Lamp Not Relit (Halmenth, 411 AR — Ephesus).** TORQUE: the regency's sale of tax freedom to relic estates needed holy objects to hold the Fire, and Nasriel would certify no relic. At Halmenth he read Qabir's count of the unkindled lamps and was charged under the augurs' own old law; Qabir's deacons would not relight their lamp, a daughter of Amaryah's taken from the bread oven in 248, from the Ember's daughter brought by warship. Halmenth's ferrymen rowed him to a Sea Fleet ship. The regency struck the gateway sees off its roll instead of pursuing him, and because no recognised cleric would certify their relics, the condemned cities kept paying the land tax the rest of the empire was escaping and were left alone for it. The Velian Church grew inside the empire's edge, not in exile. [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]] · [[The Velian Church]]

### The Long Frontier and the Fall (398–905 AR)

**The Guttering (398–905 AR — Gibbon's decadence).** TORQUE: the palace's hymns stopped kindling while the frontier hermits' went on, because the Flame answers persons and purposes, not offices. The court hid the failures as "postponed", hired Sestaran Shell-fire to fill the ranks, and then faked kindlings: the Copper Lamp of 681 began a Covenant file of counterfeit fire that ended with Corvenas III's accession, and counterfeit fire is a breach. The Flame did not save the capital with the most relics; it saved the refuge with the fewest. Thalmyria's theophany and Eclessia's protocol are the Guttering remembered from opposite ends. [[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]]

**The Bread of the Bound and the end of Vashar (c. 549–560 AR — the Justinianic catastrophes and the fall of Sasanian Persia).** TORQUE: the Covenant's founding proof was food. Through the Thin Floods the 4,444 dug the oasis while children starved below, and grain went to those who sealed their names. Vashar was taken by its own promise: its kings ruled by keeping the roads, the famine emptied the relay stations, and Covenant jurists declared the last King at Dawn forsworn. The Ruzhan horse-lords who sealed for bread became the Covenant's officers, and one of their houses produced Qahir. [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

**The First Unsealing (568–612 — the Arab conquests).** TORQUE: the conquest was substantially a *legal event*. The armies marched with **portable breach-courts**, and half the northern provinces were never stormed — they were **served**: municipal charters examined, defects found, sovereignty ruled void, terms offered. "The Unsealing was served, not fought" is the Covenant's proudest sentence and the conquered's bitterest, and it is why the Velian States' descendants still keep their pre-conquest charters in fireproof chests — waiting, some say, for a counter-ruling. The towns that went first were those whose charters had been sworn under the Stars before augurs: when the court asked for the witness, it was a dissolved college and a silent star. The conquest stopped where hermit-bishops had re-sealed charters and the Fire had come on the wax.

**The Keeper of Bir Qassim (603 AR — Karbala).** TORQUE: Mujalib's grandson died beside a full well. The station wells of the North Well Road were kept by bound offices of the 4,444, which drew for travellers under the Covenant's protection. When a flood pushed Husan's household off the road named in its safe conduct, the keeper stopped drawing for the fifty-eight named and went on drawing for their unnamed drovers. After three days and seven dead of thirst, Husan read the instrument at the well-mouth clause by clause, and after each clause the bucket came up dry; at dawn the escort killed thirty-four. The Mourning of the Wells repeats that reading, *And yet the well was dry*, and Sealed-Blood villages still draw by hand and let no bound office keep their wells. [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]]

**Tamsar and the Suspension (611–612 AR — no analog).** TORQUE: the Long Frontier Peace was made by two frightened armies. At Tamsar a breach-court's ruling failed against a town re-sealed in the Fire, a Caleran reliquary burned yellow before both armies, and a Sealed-Blood jurist told the Covenant soldiers their court sat under a seal nobody dared open. Neither side gave battle. The instrument of 612 held every disputed charter "neither void nor in force": the first deterrence by unopened proof, and the root of the Age of Held Proofs. The Velian charters in their fireproof chests wait on it still.

**The Kindled Marches (612–905 AR — the Byzantine themes).** TORQUE: each frontier march was held under one charter sworn in the Fire and renewed at every accession by the emperor in person, kneeling before a frontier monk on whom the Fire came. An emperor who would not ride to his marches could not call them, which is how Corvenas I won the throne in 713 and why Corvenas III's written orders could not move a march in 905. Thalmyria's military provinces descend from them.

**Simeon's Examination (711 AR — the rise of the papacy).** TORQUE: Aurelium's primacy began with a negative result. Simeon examined the Velos Standard, sent east to rally the provinces for a claimant, recorded it silent and held the eastern oaths in trust; the winner confirmed his right to examine because it suited him. The east could afford honest tests because, since its tax fleet sank in 653, its bishops kept the revenue either way. Protocol was born where the truth was cheap.

**What the Register Keeps (c. 780–831 AR — Maimonides' *Guide*).** TORQUE: the age's great Jirahar book is a physician's record of bound deathbeds. Banu Mizan law required a witness at every bound deathbed before the name could be released, and Rab Yequtiel sat at those beds in Zanthir's bond-hospitals and salt pans for fifty years; he argued from them that a register keeps the name and lets the person go. It was true when written and became untrustworthy in one case after 885, when the foundry's cages took the dying, which is why it could not quiet occupied Caleran's funerals. [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

**The Sacrificial Wind and Oronda (c. 866–876 AR — no analog).** TORQUE: the Second Crusade began with a rescue the emperor did not command. A host led, by soldiers' account, by a drowned ferrywoman held the road behind a convoy on the river marches and withdrew when the army's commander, carrying Corvenas II's orders, demanded conquest. The emperor carried the Velos Standard into the Velian belt to do what she had refused, and before Oronda it did not kindle in front of both armies. Eight years later Qahir obtained the ruling that the crusade had broken the peace of 612: the empire handed its enemy the loophole.

**The Clause of the Given (c. 549; 885–886 AR — Ottoman gunpowder).** TORQUE: the Covenant fought three centuries without devils because the 4,444 were sealed to "take nothing that is not given". Qahir's revolution was a ruling before it was a forge: the Clause binds parties, not powers, and a captured Depth beast, a condemned man's forfeited name and a nameless construct are party to nothing. The foundries followed the jurists, and the dissent of the people of the Clause has been outvoted, never answered.

**The Serving of Caleran (905–908 AR — 1453).** TORQUE: the capital was taken half by law, in the name of a sultan who never saw it. Idris was sonless and every oath in the army named him alone, so his jurists kept him at Iqashar while Qahir's engine-master Barzin and the breach-court train read Caleran's own charter, last re-sealed with warlock fire, and offered each district its own terms. The great engines came to one span of the land wall, the Star's share its builders had made to break in the wardens' test; Thalmyric tradition says the Red Star showed it, the Covenant says a dead college's archive did. Corvenas III could have saved the city only by passing tests in public: kindling a summons at the marches, conceding the lost north, submitting his relics to Aurelium. He chose the rites that could fail in private, and on the last day took the public one on the wall with the Velos Standard; it failed before both armies, and the holding quarters took terms that afternoon. The breach-court would accept from him only his person and his relics, and by the Instrument of the Ash Hall he became their captive keeper until his death in 908. The refuge could raise no standard of its own while he lived. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Invasion and the Fall.md|The Invasion and the Fall]]

**The Fall of Caleran (905) and the Impounded Ember.** Idris al-Fattah, by courier from Iqashar, ruled the Eternal Ember neither holy nor his own and impounded it as **a deposit held for the final audit**, his keepers' own names pledged as surety that it would be neither divided, removed nor put out. A deposit is kept where it stands and open to view, so the basilica around it was never sealed and its liturgy went on under guard for fifty-two years. Addressed to the audit and not to Idris, it was the one instrument of his that survived the Widowed Seals. In 957 the third generation of keepers held it against their own amir's guard, and eleven died at the lamp. The Reconquest found the daughter flame of Amaryah's lamp burning, with a Covenant ledger recording fuel and custody. **Veridun paid the invoice in full**, discharging the deposit and releasing the keepers' names. The account preserves the work of the occupiers who kept the holy lamp burning and the obligation the restored government accepted. [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]]

### Reconquest, two crowns and High Noon (905–1232 AR)

**The First Catch (905–920 — the plains horse-revolution).** TORQUE: the scattered imperial studs were **hymn-blessed remount lines** — war-sanctified stock — and Grey Sister's line carries the blessing still, diluting by generation. Tarkhan horse-genealogy is not sentiment; it is the actuarial tracking of a **fading grace**, which is why the sacred lines are priced above provinces and why a Grey Sister foal given in treaty is the steppe's version of a relic translation. The blessing is a steadiness before bound things: horses of the early lines do not shy from Depth beasts, bound spirits or speaking engines. That is why the Long Reach of 951 broke a Covenant frontier built on hound-wells and panic, and why the Horse War (c. 975–1010) peaked while the lines were young.

**The Mother's Veil (906 AR — the Pokrov).** TORQUE: the refuge was saved by bread it had been ordered to burn. The hearth-keepers of Veridun Minor refused the council's order to burn the store-cave flour and baked it all night; at dawn the ovens' smoke lay over the valley for three days, the column's engines proclaimed terms into it, refugees dreamed of a woman banking an oven, and the levies dreamed of home and walked there. Veridun's rule that the Mother comes in icons and dreams and never in fields held: nobody saw her. The feast is kept with oven doors open at dawn and soldiers standing vigil instead of watch. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun.md|The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun]]

**The Broken Road (917–918 AR — Covadonga).** TORQUE: Thalmyria's first field victory was bought from the Red Star by road-menders. The Star that showed the Covenant the Star's share of Caleran's wall showed Caelmount's menders where their road would fail, at the price of their terraces. The span broke under the engine and custodians' wagons in mid-column, and the trapped van wasted away through the winter while its levies, their rolls in the ravine, deserted to the amnesty. Caedros promised to feed the valley, took Virelos (921) to keep the promise and was crowned there (922). The Church remembers a hermit's lamp; Eclessian polemic remembers the Star. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/70 History and Conflicts/The Battle of Caelmount.md|The Battle of Caelmount]]

**The Widowed Seals and the Southern Threshold (929–957 AR — the Ottoman Interregnum).** TORQUE: the Qahirid army was bound to the sovereign *by name*, with the heir written in as successor-holder. The heir drowned in a palace cistern and Idris died before the re-swearing muster, widowing every instrument at once: levies walked home, southern garrisons woke unsworn, the silt hounds of the northern wells slipped their feeding names, and the Depth-Core engine later called the Elder walked through Iqashar's dye-yards until a feeder gave its name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir". That formula became **the Sublime Threshold**, a holder that cannot die, witnessed at Al-Qahur. It is why the court bears that name, why Al-Qahur's acknowledgment still matters, why the Basin's military households hold leases whose terms have run, and why the Cull of Brothers began as lodging, not killing. The occupied south, meanwhile, was sworn to a man rather than to the Covenant, and when its amir fled Caleran in 957, every district beyond Caleran was left sworn to an absent man and fell apart into separately sworn amirates. [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]] · [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Reclamation of Caleran.md|The Reclamation of Caleran]]

**The Keepers at the Lamp (957 AR — Toledo).** TORQUE: Caleran was not surrendered by its ruler. Its amir tried to carry off the Ember as the price of a welcome home; the deposit's Covenant keepers, whose names were pledged that it would never be moved, held it against him, eleven died, and he fled by sea. The keepers then made the terms that named the abandoned garrison families protected. Iqashar counts them among its honest dead, and Caleran prays for them by name. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Reclamation of Caleran.md|The Reclamation of Caleran]] · [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]]

**The Canal Heads (957–1052 AR — the taifas and parias).** TORQUE: the amirates fell to water, not tribute. The occupation's estates drank from canals whose heads lay in hills the mountain leagues had held since the 910s; Caedros II's engineers took the heads valley by valley and set water-courts there with the Banu Mizan hour-cup, and farmers who registered got their share while the amirs kept walls above dry fields. No tribute passed between amir and emperor in either direction. The bronze tokens the Southern Houses carried north in 1052 were the occupation's canal shares, which is why the Southern Deeds are a quarrel over water as well as land. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Founding Flame.md|The Founding Flame]]

**The Banner at Kaltfurt and the Examination of the King (974; 1001–1049 AR — Charlemagne's coronation and Verdun).** TORQUE: the march banner kindled for a miller's widow, Hedwen, shielding a refugee column; the Sanctum's court examined it honestly, recorded it answering and credited it to Adalmar, then crowned him as the emperor who had "proved lawful" under Simeon's trust of 711. Her testimony is in the register, struck through: Eclessia's imperial claim rests on a true sign that was not the emperor's. Once examinations were the price of crowns, Adalmar's grandsons could each buy one, so in 1049 they drew lots at Drossfeld instead; the smudged eastern line of the third lot is the middle march's oldest lawsuit. [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]]

**The Ashfall Omen and the Breakings (983–1052 AR — iconoclasm, bent).** TORQUE: the restored church's lamps guttered for the Guttering's reason, and Leovar III, whose own war-lamp had gone dark, read the Great Lamp shrinking to a bead as the Fire refusing vessels. No empress restored the lamps. A drought sank the wells, families lifted the lamps their mothers had hidden and some came up burning; the Breaker died of a drought well's flux, his son hesitated, and the households carried a thousand lamps into Veridun and unhooded the Great Lamp with their own hands (1013). The son who had let them in then locked every lamp away to protect it after his Master of Lamps was burned forcing one at Meliton (1029), and the crown's keepers sold the oil until the empire's household fires died in the lamp-houses (1049). There was nothing left to restore: families walked to the Ember for a light, and Theodosia's Rekindling (1052) relit every lamp from the fire the Covenant had kept, so that Thalmyria's whole fire "burns on a receipt". [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]]

**The Sayings for the Foot of a Lamp (987–1013 AR — John of Damascus).** TORQUE: the answer to the Breaking was merchandise. An exiled potter-monk stamped forty sayings on the feet of cheap clay lamps in Velian kilns; Covenant customs officers sealed the crates and collected the duty; the Breakers smashed the bowls and left the feet, which households mortared into their walls. The argument that ended the Breaking travelled at a Covenant tariff.

**The Standing of the Water (994 AR — the Saxon wars).** TORQUE: the winter after the Drowning, Wolfsmere rose on a windless night and took the Markish shore-fort; the nine survivors laid it down. Eclessia's southern frontier has stopped at the lake country ever since, and the Peace of the Bent Oak (c. 1005) bought the lake's sleep with Weylan's baptism and the lake-law: no bell, no boat, no iron.

**The Pledged Custodian (1003–1011 AR).** TORQUE: a lending house holding the Vault chief's true name made every bound levy's oath doubtful, and the Covenant army sat in its camp through Thalmyria's First Breaking for want of certainty about whose soldiers they were. The Custodian freed the Vault by surrendering his own name into its deepest custody, and the ink of it faded from every page. Every Custodian since has done the same, which is why the Unnamed Custodian is the one officer no court can blackmail.

**The Bells and the Unlifted Anathemas (1015 AR — 1054).** TORQUE: both churches came to believe that whichever lifts its anathema first confesses that the grieving bells were its own. The Bells stay unexplained ([[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]]); the belief is in-world, and it is why neither anathema has been lifted.

**The Two Houses of the Vault (1059 AR).** TORQUE: custody was split between the living (Iqashar) and the dead and sacred (Al-Qahur), with the Living Register's chief's own name held in the Deep Vault. The capital and the sacred city cannot rule without each other, and, unknown to anyone in-world, the architecture can never be audited from inside.

**The Choosing at Strazgorod (1059–1061 AR — the Rus, bent).** TORQUE: there was no embassy and no test of faiths. A Grass-Tarkhan raid put out a heathen prince's house-fire, and Vesari hearth-law made a house the client of whoever relit its fire. Radomir would be no neighbour's man, so his fire-keeper Zdena carried the cold lamp to Veridun to ask for a light from the Ember that had relit an empire. Makarios the Adamant refused to light a heathen hearth, and the cold lamp took the Flame in her hands, lit by no one, before witnesses of both churches, among them a Sanctum examiner, who recorded it answering. Makarios kept it as the Fire's chosen dwelling, which under hearth-law made Radomir Veridun's client, and he went to the Sanctum, whose register named the lamp his. That lamp, **the Virelian Lamp, still burns in Veridun's cathedral**, tended for three centuries by the fire-keeper's descendants as an honoured hostage. At every Virelian succession election since the first, in 1103, the Patriarchate formally offers to *return the lamp*, and a fire handed back from another's hall is a lent fire: the standing seduction behind every "eastern candidate," and the reason the Sanctum's agents attend Virelian elections with unclerical attention.

**The Cold Way (1123 AR — no direct analog).** TORQUE: Korrheim's first cantons were won when escaped mine slaves sent condemned overseers through a broken mountain passage and used what they learned to break the counts' army. Their liberator hid what the passage did to the prisoners and confessed it fourteen years later. The cantons keep their feast and, the next morning, the victims' names.

**The District Lamps and the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139 AR — Basil II's frontier).** TORQUE: a lawsuit fought with engines. The Court of Breaches ruled Caedros III's frontier districts a breach of the surrender deeds held by the Southern Houses, and the mercy towers proclaimed property-for-surrender terms to settlers who held their land on condition of defence and for whom surrender meant forfeiture. The settlers' lamps kindled because they were defending their homes, the emperor's own at Kolonna among them; the persuasion engines could not model a people with nothing to sell, escalated where they should have stopped, and one tower was lost. The Covenant then ransomed its captured custodians, because their registers held the names of its living soldiers.

**The Hunt of 1163 (no direct analog).** TORQUE: the Great Southern Purge cut the boundary groves; a court's Hunt rode the marcher camps for three nights and took an Aveline chapter and Folk households alike. The Purge ended, and the Elsin tradition won Folk policy for a generation.

**The Unanswered Funeral (1139–1167 AR — Basil II's succession, bent).** TORQUE: the Wall of the World was no childless ascetic. He was a married, convivial godfather of the districts who spent his windfall and borrowed more, and he left his succession to the Fire to bar his own son, the butcher of Vresna, without naming him (the Kolonna Declaration, 1139). At the bier no claimant's lamp kindled; the son's flared with bought script-fire and his rivals blinded him; a district widow's lamp kindled, and the district armies made her warrant theirs. Theophany's own temptation, to make the Fire decide politics, put the districts on the throne and left them his debts.

**The Floor beyond the Count (c. 1171–1173 AR — no direct analog).** TORQUE: a mortality beneath a raptor of white fire followed the census couriers of a prince seeking a crown, and stopped at a bereaved household's threshing floor. Elenara stayed a duchy, and census law still requires a stated purpose.

**The Ledger of Debts (1190–1243 AR — Suleiman's kanun).** TORQUE: the classical age's great book is a confession. Suhrab abandoned the forty-year attempt to rank the provincial codes and entered the law's killings of its own people as debts, each closed with a clause every code must yield to. Its first book is Bir Qassim, read at the dry well in 1194 to mourners who answered each clause *And yet the well was dry*; the crowds named him the Debtor. Its book of the Southern Deeds produced the Unburdened Deeds (1212) and so Qarath's Well. [[Historical Spine of the Covenant]]

**Qarath's Well and the Poured Cup (1214–1225 AR — Manzikert and the Komnenian rise, bent).** TORQUE: the battle was lost to terms cut into a well-curb in three scripts: a guest's peace until the next sunset, and a debt, "the price of the water", owed by any guest who broke it. Nikandros, a Kedravan commander schooled in a refuge monastery, read them aloud and was overruled; the army drank at dusk, the emperor last, and sickened at dawn when it took up arms against its host; the Kedravan contingents, who kept the refuge rule against sworn water, withdrew in order; Grey Sister's lines, which do not shy from bound things, rode through. The emperor was not captured. Suhrab fed him three days as a guest and sent him home with a bill for the water: the Southern Deeds districts. The court made it worse. It called the drinkers' guesthood a surrender, took their farms under the districts' own tenure law (the Forfeiture of the Drinkers), and deposed the emperor to make the bill his private debt; the Covenant answered that he had drunk as the Throne, which does not die and is not deposed. The next line began when the deposed drinker poured out a cup of water untasted at Virelos and gave his claim to the man who had read the curb (1225). Every emperor since pours out the cup at his coronation, and none drinks as a Covenant guest or signs a Covenant instrument. [[High Noon and the Qarath Disaster]]

### Plague, the Black Wind and the held proofs (1225–1360 AR)

**The Refusal of the Lamp (1227 AR — Byzantium's appeals to the West).** TORQUE: the price of Eclessian soldiers was a **test**: the Great Lamp of Veridun tried by the Sanctum's relic court. Nikandros I refused: "a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned." The first refusal of a test as an act of sovereignty, and the root of deterrence by unopened proof.

**The Grey Sweat (1232–1247 — the Black Death).** TORQUE: on Earth plague is biology; here mass death is a **soul-logistics catastrophe**. The Sweat is remembered as *the years the ferry queued*: the Vestibule's intake overwhelmed, burial rites collapsing faster than bodies could be blessed, and the **Undertow's harvest of the century** — their oldest standing routes date from it, bought in bulk from the unburied. Church quarantine doctrine was therefore written about **rite-integrity, not contagion** (a sealed village with a living priest was "clean"; a healthy village with a dead one was not), and the Ash-Walkers were not merely hysterics — where they walked, the records show, the thin places closed. Nobody has ever wanted to study why. In Covenant lands the Sweat is *the Emptying of the Benches*: deaths outran the witnesses needed to release bound names and hold garrison oaths, frontier forts opened their gates (the Open Gates, 1238–1246), and the Vault's living and dead registers have never balanced since. In Virelia the quarantine rule became the **bond of the clean village**: magnates sealed priestless villages and moved the survivors onto demesne under bond for a priest and burial, the root of the second serfdom. In Thalmyria laity sang their own dead out, and fewer villages were haunted.

**The Ferryman Primarch (1240–1242 AR — the Western Schism).** TORQUE: the schism is the plague's speed inside the conclave. The elected Primarch died unsung on his election night; his twin reigned in his ring for twenty months under the quarantine rule's threat and issued the Ferry Rule for lay funerals; the dead brother's unsent figure exposed him. The two colleges split over whether a false Primarch's acts send the dead, and the doubt fed the Chancery of Sendings. No rival cities, no antipopes, no council to depose them. [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|The Grey Sweat]]

**The Rider of the Benches (1241–1243 AR — Suleiman and Mustafa).** TORQUE: the Guard's power over the succession came from a father's grief. Prince Mihrzad held the Qarath-frontier forts in the Emptying by riding fort to fort as a living witness, carried the Sweat into three of them and died where only his Guard escort survived to see it; with no competent witness his name could not be released. Suhrab's last book named the Guard bench a competent witness so that it could be, and every accession since has been made before that bench. [[Historical Spine of the Covenant]]

**The Black Colt (1249 AR — the rise of a steppe conqueror).** TORQUE: Qarayel's empire was a renewed grace. Grey Sister's lines were thinning when Gavrin, a Velian slave farrier, sang the old remount hymn over a dying black colt; the colt and its get stood before bound things. Qarayel built his coalition by stud-right, extracted from frontiers whose bound wardens could not frighten his horses, and lost the advantage as the grace thinned (1300). The Partition of the Herds (1304) divided the grace-lines and made the four Hordes. [[Qarayel and the Black Wind]]

**The Pageless Prince (1249–1252 AR — the Ottoman law of fratricide).** TORQUE: a lodged prince whose register page burned in the Sweat walked free under "no page, no lodging". After him the Cull used the knife: "the Vault can lose a page; the grave cannot." Since Suhrab's last book (1243) the Guard's officer bench has witnessed every accession, which is what "Guard-brokered" means.

**Dead Paper and the Sufficient Seal (1252–1297 AR — indulgences; the Borgia papacy).** TORQUE: intercession works, so the Sending Houses worked where rites were sung, and the trade spread on evidence. The bought Primarch who broke it was an ascetic, and his sin was sincerity. Medori credit bought Alessior II's election without his asking; his Bull of the Sufficient Seal (1283) made the seal itself the Sending, the trade lost every limit, and under him and Silvio Quarne letters outran singers. An unsung sealed letter is a lien on a named soul's passage that Patrons, the Undertow and brokers can press. He died in debt for his election and for a dome painted with the names of the letter-bought dead, and was seen walking beneath it: no nepotism, no poison, no son carving a state. Ansgar's Grievances charge fraud and danger; the Council of Veridance repudiated the Seal and so owed the Great Arrears, the old Church singing its own debts from registers the Medori have held since 1296. [[Freeflame Reformation]]

**The Twice-Turned (1288–1291 AR — Sabbatai Zevi).** TORQUE: the claimant rode a sign from the Black Wind and the Wind's own word; the Covenant ruined his followers by voiding their charters; at the Empty City he was asked to touch the held gate and twice turned back. No arrest by a sultan, no apostasy, no exile; the gate stays untested. [[Mesakha Traditions]]

**The Blackwater Funerals and Aldersbruck (1297–1305 AR — the posted theses; Constance).** TORQUE: the Grievances were sung over drowned miners, not posted, and spread as a song into a funeral strike. The synod put the book, not the man, to the fire; the book did not burn, the test was voided, and the river took the prisoner from a bridge-tower that neither legate nor duke would take responsibility for. No safe-conduct, no recantation scene, no ashes. [[Freeflame Reformation]]

**The Ashenmen and the Preacher's Water (1305–1343 AR — the Peasants' War; the Hussite wagon-forts).** TORQUE: the Weiss took Ansgar from the bridge-tower at Aldersbruck in 1305, and the reach below the broken bridge is now **the Preacher's Water**: oaths sworn on it hold strangely well, the relic courts' one water-test came back *inconclusive* (the file is sealed; inconclusive frightened them more than positive would have), and the Freeflame, whose theology forbids relics, are stuck with a **river** — which cannot be enshrined, sold, or stolen, and which their enemies note is therefore the most Freeflame relic conceivable. The Ashenmen swore in the Weiss in 1306, and no band broke its oath in twenty-three years. They fought out of the pits and drifts of the upper Weiss and starved the iron country; no field army could dig them out, and the Caldrian drowning of the Brenholt drifts (1314) killed some three hundred people without ending the war. There was no great battle, no wagon-fort and no massacre that closed it: the Risings ended only when Rainald, the duke whose iron they held, burned the seals (1329) and fulfilled the oath's letter. The Korrheim Accord (1343) was sworn with hands in the same water, which is why a peace nobody believes in has held. The radical Unsworn refused to swear on a relic, and nobody would put the reunification mandate into an oath on that river.

**The Refutation that Founded a Church (c. 1312–1352 AR — Calvin; Frederick the Wise; Trent).** TORQUE: the systematiser was the Sanctum's commissioned prosecutor, whose fair statement of the heresy became its order and whose refutation never came; the prince who might have sheltered him was the jailer's son, and Ferro refused him. The old Church's council answered the book with its author in the room as witness and closed in the year he died.

**The Lantern (1338 AR — the Jesuits).** TORQUE: the counter-reform order was an outlaw brotherhood of night-singers descended from the Saint-Leon quarter the Sanctum's treasurer burned, led by a survivor of that fire who had studied under the reformers' author. No soldier, no wound, no sickbed conversion.

**The Mirror in the Grey Coat (c. 1340 AR — *The Prince*).** TORQUE: the adviser was a bondsman, not an exile; he wrote in Fiorenzia's Debtors' Corps and dedicated the book to his creditor as an offer of payment, before the city's troubles began. No dismissal by a returning dynasty, no plea for office. [[The Mirror for Sovereigns — Selected Maxims]]

**The Iron Fast (1341–1343 AR — Augsburg).** TORQUE: the territorial peace came when a princeless guild ministry banked Ironvale's furnaces and an inconclusive water-test frightened the old-Church delegates into the river; it was sworn in the water that took Ansgar. The formula and terms stand because the present board needs them. [[The Korrheim Accord]]

**The Pyre the Bank Lit (1345–1349 AR — Savonarola; the Medici restoration).** TORQUE: the Medori paid for the Pyre of Masks to answer the friar, who begged them not to light it; something stood in the fire; the family fled with the city's dead; the friar never ruled and led a four-year Vigil; he died in a mask nobody could lift; and the city bought its dead back by making the bankers hereditary dukes. No expulsion vote, no ordeal by fire, no hanging. [[Historical Spine of the Sestaran Concord]]

**The Great Split, the Reformation, the crusade cycles.** Already majority-native (Kindling Question, Caedros's conditional hymns, routing assurances, the Hundred Grievances, the unbought funerals, Ansgar's Count, the voided test at Aldersbruck, the Weiss oaths, the war from the mines, the Great Arrears and the Council's witness) — the torque holding: keep leading with the doctrine-mechanics, not the 1054/1517 furniture. The Church's *numbering* of crusades stays: bureaucratic numbering is in-world habit, not Earth residue.

**Resolved in later culture passes:** copied governing bodies and signature social vocabulary were replaced with setting-native institutions. Broad translation titles such as Doge remain deliberate conventions where controlled by the relevant culture hub.
