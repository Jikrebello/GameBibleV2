## Authorial truth behind the siege cycle

The rescue, coalition dispute, siege, sack and three returning histories described in the public article are established events. The leading commander orders the sanctuary woman's rape and confinement. The challenging captain also holds enslaved people and withdraws partly over his own following and share of stores. The shipmistress's husband dies; she and the escaped woman continue organising rescues. Neither woman is reduced to an occasion for a warrior's anger.

The fighting and local manifestations occur. The pilot enables the fighter to break the bronze and end Astaroth's appearance at the breakwater. The songs' blood from divine armour is not promoted as a wound to the sovereignty. The subsequent murdered prisoners have no part in the companion's death. The bereaved fighter chooses revenge despite the deception used to encourage it.

The father's retrieval secures one body and a pause for burial. The sack follows feigned withdrawal, a tide change and inside assistance given in expectation of terms. Commanders break those terms; the High Giant dies bodily; the palace image is broken during escape. There is no wooden horse, Titan severance or triumph over an Idol Throne.

The refuge-violating officer and the captain pursued by the tide are one human. He is distinct from the coalition commander, the challenging captain, the bereaved fighter and the changing hostess's guest. The shipmistress is another independent participant. These identities are fixed without giving them complete names, genealogies or a future saintly or infernal status.

The pursued captain's captive escapes after the first wreck and joins rescuers. His later hanging of enslaved women includes victims who acted under coercion; restoration of his house begins a continuing feud. The changing hostess's crew retains changed bodies, unequal releases and a companion who stays. The shipmistress settles among an already inhabited shore community and contributes to its first shared vessel. Burning the old ships versus consensually dismantling them remains an unresolved received variant.

## The gateway cult and final war

> [!note] Amended November 2026
> The limits below on the cult's Mask and on the salting are superseded where they restrict consequence rather than identity; see the Consequence Rule in [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]].

The ancestral provider is **one dependent Gluttony Mask**. Its bronze face, serving chambers and institutional supply of victims support a local manifestation. It encourages another offering whenever protection fails. Children are killed and bodily offerings consumed. Their complete human souls are neither eaten into the Mask nor reproduced in the image. The rite's ordinary fires create no infernal fire realm; Hell's existing elemental character is unchanged.

The capital's priests, supplying officials and protective civic faction are culpable actors. Some elites offer their own children; others purchase children or use captives. Internal opponents, hiding families, escaped temple workers and defecting towns materially contribute to intervention. Ending child sacrifice is an established **major cause** of the final Atherian war, alongside strategic, commercial and territorial motives. It is not merely a retrospective pretext.

The war policy singles out the capital. Lesser towns accepting the settlement retain their communities. An initial surrender is followed by expanded demands for abandonment, leading to renewed resistance among inhabitants with different positions on the cult. The final siege actually releases children and destroys the sacrificial institution. Atherian murder, rape, enslavement and command responsibility also remain fixed.

This damage is local and material. It does not permanently sterilise the whole country, explain a desert, alter an elemental realm or become a reliable anti-Idol weapon. The loss of sacrifice, supply and sanctuary ends this established local means of manifestation; Gluttony's Throne survives.~~

**Replaced 14 November 2026 ([[Causality Pass - 02 Gateway Wars and the Late Republic]], item 02.3).** The Provider's plenty was real: like the Guest with Shark Teeth at Tidewood, the dependent Mask concentrated the Gateway country's food into the capital through its feeding arrangement, so Qarzeth's recoveries after 646 and 601 were genuine while the dependent towns' sealed stores emptied. The Three Collections were the bill. By the last collection the capital held a bounded dominion overlay; there was no Archmask promotion and no Regnant. The capital fell in the order of the Gut-Maw defeat grammar: supply and invitation routes cut (the Rope Camp stoppage, the towns' defection and the blockade); named remains recovered (Ilya's list, copied from the collectors' own lists); the organ-object broken (the bronze face, whose breaker stays disputed); and the overlay closed by a meal without hierarchy of worth at Sarqen's landing, which is borrowed from the Banquet Tyrant grammar and applies here because the overlay, not an Archmask court, was closed. The salt was Gateway salt from Hazreth and Sarqen, laid at the towns' request on the districts where the plenty had been concentrated. It worked as the last act of a defeat already complete; on its own it does nothing, as the salted threshold of the Provider house at Vorthara (c. 505–498) shows. The damage remains local: no desert, no elemental change, no general anti-Idol weapon. Gluttony's Throne survives.

**Amended 14 November 2026 ([[Causality Pass - 11A1 Second Bend - Gateway Wars and Late Republic]]).** The first pass still followed its analogue's order of fates, and the following rulings replace the corresponding statements in this section and in the chronology table above.

- **Zamaret's Bread, c. 612–601.** Zamaret Ordan sails from Oresh on his own account. The Council Houses disown him before Caleran's senate and deliver the hostage cohort due in 611 (renewals fell in 646, 639, 632, 625, 618 and 611). He fights no pitched battle before 606; the Atherian armies of 611 and 609 are lost to hunger, desertion and the bitten stores. The victims of the Lustration of 609 are captured Gateway soldiers and the 611 cohort, by then about eight to sixteen. The Lustration brings Qarzeth into the war (608), which is the failure clause running. Celor Marath names Zamaret in 607 and holds the Thesra road in 606. From about 603 the Provider's draw falls on Zamaret's own stores, because every table laid in the Provider's name belongs to its feeding arrangement and the allied towns' stores are exhausted. A crowd at Erenost kills him at the joint store in 601; the burial under the threshold is Erenost's story and has no effect. The peace of 601 restricts the fleet and renews no hostages. Marath lays down the office.
- **The Rope Camp, c. 536–531.** The camp is not broken. Collectors who follow Damen's escort in are killed by the camp, and two are tortured for the names on their lists. The council swears to the carriers' terms in 531. With no names entered from the four towns, the Provider's feeding arrangement goes unfed and the sanctuary doors stay shut at the next festival; the council breaks its oath, and Meleq Ordan's crews and Harzun's riders take the collectors back into the towns. The council has Damen killed. Sapha of Hadrath's marriage to Meleq is a surety for Hadrath's obedience. The reprisals, not a battle at the camp, drive the towns to Caleran.
- **The surrender, c. 528–526.** The clause "an initial surrender is followed by expanded demands for abandonment" is replaced. Mera Castren's inspection policy carries the senate. Qarzeth gives up the children then held, its arms and warships, and on the third demand its assembly votes to hand over the collectors' books. The books were the Mask's invitation route: a name entered was a guest promised. With the books gone the doors do not open, the city goes hungry, the cult's party kills the two Harbour Judges who sealed the surrender, and new books are begun in the capital's own streets. No power refused the surrender; "its god had not" is the Gateway country's reading. Castren and the eleven chests are taken out by sanctuary store-workers. Caleran sends no fourth demand. The demand that the citizens break the bronze face on the shore is withdrawn from canon.
- **Valeor lives** to the sack, ransoms forty-one children from Marelith's captains out of his own estate and dies about 514.
- **The salting.** The sentence "laid at the towns' request" is replaced: carriers from Hazreth and Sarqen laid the salt themselves, by night, after Atherian grain contractors sealed the sanctuary's store compounds. The contractors intended to keep the stores; whether the senate did is not ruled. The salt still worked only as the last act of a defeat already complete, and the Atherian commander reported it as his own order.
- Meleq Ordan's fate remains lost from the rolls. The bronze face's breaker remains disputed even authorially.

The surviving towns' wells, farms, workshops, ports and households sustain rebuilding. Some assist the intervention and remember liberation; others suffer heavily while escaping planned erasure. The population is never collectively equated with the cult. Descent does not transmit culpability or give the queen a supernatural identity.

## The royal country and outstanding choices

The shared basis of the two dynasty routes is promoted: the country survives through lesser towns; Second Harbour is one of them, enlarged into a royal centre; recovery and hereditary authority develop over c. 520–400 BR; the later queen inherits this country. Her claim rests on the surviving communities and developed kingdom, not on an instantly rebuilt capital or an unchanged republic.

**Route L remains open:** a locally rooted spared-town family gains authority through supply, repairs, marriages and Atherian recognition. **Route M remains open:** an incoming maritime command marries into a powerful spared-town family and develops a hereditary crown. Route M's recommendation is not an authorial choice. Neither founder, marriage, accession incident nor genealogy is fixed through the common account.

Both Thalion architectures remain research. The existing canonical seizure and unification around 350 BR is unchanged. No predecessor's assassination, testamentary heir, territorial triumvirate, queen's romance or Actium-equivalent battle enters canon here. Selection of the imperial architecture must precede those biographies.

## Sources, transformations and unselected material

The research basis is the approved [[90 Archive & Drafts/10 Research/Mythic and Sacred History/Atheria before the Imperial Founding - Palace Remnants, Maritime Rivals and the Queen's Inheritance.md|palace and maritime comparison]], including its later epic and sacrificial-war revision. Its two historical source dossiers remain research:

- [[90 Archive & Drafts/10 Research/Mythic and Sacred History/Palaces, Sea Migrations and the Long Fall - Mycenaean Research and World Egg Comparison.md|Palaces, Sea Migrations and the Long Fall]] distinguishes archaeological palace history from later heroic literature. The *Iliad* supplies coalition quarrel, coerced captivity, divine intervention and burial; the sack and some return traditions draw on later epic summaries; selected *Odyssey* episodes provide predation, coercive hospitality and a violent homecoming. Neither Homer nor one Sea Peoples invasion is treated as a complete historical explanation of collapse.
- [[90 Archive & Drafts/10 Research/Mythic and Sacred History/Carthage and the Making of Rome's Overseas Republic - Peoples, Punic Wars and Destruction.md|Carthage and Rome's overseas republic]] documents the successive wars and source disagreements. Evidence for some child sacrifice does not establish its suppression as Cato's principal historical motive. Appian describes other punitive town destructions. Carthage's salting lacks ancient attestation; later reception and the distinct Judges 9 salting narrative are separately identified. Capital-only destruction, salting and sacrifice as a major war cause are deliberate World Egg selections.

The transplantation changes geography, ancestry, political development, supernatural actors, siege method, surviving communities and royal aftermath. Source severity is retained through bodily coercion, rape, child sacrifice, torture, cannibal predation, execution, enslavement and irreversible deaths. No additional atrocity is borrowed merely because another cited source contains one.

Three shorter palace encounters remain unpromoted research: the abandoned landing, returning hostage and two brothers' winter. They receive no event in the dated chronology. Their next assessment belongs to the individual heroic-cast development after this integration, where they must supply a distinct local life rather than obligatory preliminaries to High Harbour. The optional gateway reformer's civilian career, additional Menhir navigation bargain and other supernatural possibilities likewise remain unselected; the approved principal histories function without them.
