# Authorial Notes from Reader Articles — Deep Past

Safeguards, rulings and exact figures moved out of public deep-past history articles during the November 2026 editorial cleanup; each item keeps its original force.

## [[Creation and the First Rebellions]]

- Creation, humanity's fall and the rebellion of created powers are not competing accounts of the same event.
- The defeated Adversary did not become the secret maker of the world.
- The Circles are not a tidy succession of divine families, and not every strange mountain is a battlefield of the Faewild Schism.
- Neither Atherian star genealogy nor Folk threshold tradition is a complete authorial cosmology. No complete first-family epic waits behind every later tale, and no mortal eyewitness biographies are to be added to the Schism.
- The Glass and the Bird-Marked Sites are not assigned to this sequence as a convenient missing piece.
- A heavenly judgement follows a commission, not the independent appetite of a luminous creature.

## [[Age of Chains]]

- The Prophet's movement entered an existing crisis of maintenance and inheritance; it did not secretly create every fault within it. The Great Unbinding was not a clean replacement of all Giants by one human state.
- The Broken Measure tricks spread from estate to estate without any secret continental organisation.

## [[Age of Scattering]]

- No conquest of the Southern Seizures was a commanded holy war, and no single policy describes every proto-Atherian league.
- The proto-Jirahar and proto-Ahl-e-Ahd tendencies were neither hereditary temperaments nor the decisions of two founding brothers.
- The symbolic Seven of *The Seven Who Stopped Seeking Home* founded no single people.
- No community inherits collective guilt merely by descending from the survivors of Nine Hearths, though its laws and wealth may still inherit the consequences.
- Exact figure replaced: Wrath rewrote **2,014** captives at Kharvos (reader text now "some two thousand, by the Nhalduran count").

## [[Age of Hearths and First Kings]]

- Neither the proto-Jirahar nor the western well-country tradition arose at one council or from a single founder.

## [[Ancient and Early Historic Ages]]

- Design rationale removed from the lead: the note "exists to make civilization feel earned: humanity does not move directly from Giant slavery to continental empire."
- No single ancient Sestaran people exits the Glass.
- [[Southern Cold Belt and Grobi Frontier]] is the governing note for the deep southern cold belt, the Ättar/Folk frontier and Grobi migration logic; southern cultures' political calendars, military routes, food systems and myths should reflect cold pressure.
- Exact figure replaced: Grobi "created when Wrath rewrote **2,014** captives at Kharvos" (now "descended from some two thousand captives, by the Nhalduran count, rewritten at Kharvos"); "nonmoral" dropped (Package D carries the moral-conscience ruling).

## [[Early Folk Clan Worlds]]

- Enaidfae court traditions preserve real knowledge alongside dangerous theological errors.
- The Ättar firstborn pact is a later southern institution, not a gift received during the Exodus.

## [[Proto-Jirahar Tower Towns]]

- The earliest tower builders were not a fully formed Jirahar nation carried intact out of the Glass.
- The Jirahar claim to the Prophet's original teaching does not mean every Jirahar person is holy, every later institution is pure, or that the Jirahar alone inherit the People of the Camp.

## [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]]

- A commissioned heavenly act may have bound one Titan or ended one recurrence system during the wider judgement in which Vathra fell; the Unyoking itself was human work.

## [[The Siege and the Returnings]]

- The exhibition of bronze said to come from the god's armour demonstrates the importance of the tale without identifying every object as part of the breakwater.

## [[Southern Cold Belt and Grobi Frontier]]

- Wrath performed the First Rewriting directly (Package D); the reader text now names the act without stating its agent.
- Exact figures replaced: First Rewriting **approximately 2284 BR** (now "around 2280 BR"); Nine-Drum Host **c. 1608–1596 BR** (now "around 1600 BR").

## [[Historical Atlas of the Southern Cold World]]

- Wrath's purpose at Kharvos was larger than the Nhalduran civil war: as Giant armies collapsed across the world, Furor intended to fill the open country with self-reproducing soldiers which could grow by consuming whatever they conquered. The Red Reckoner's regimen was Wrath's entry into Nashara's project.
- Real Menhir persons, Idol Masks, Egg White Patrons, residues and ordinary natural events could receive similar human names among the southern peoples without sharing an identity.
- The Deep Voice cannot restore a human soul, and no Menhir owns the whole art.
- Mother Moon's bargain did not create the Ättar, and war-voice did not supply its power.
- Wrath feeds Grobi nature without issuing every command through a species-wide mind.
- The first Grobi's surviving fragments of human language and trained response did not make any new being the human person whose body had been taken.
- Exact figures replaced: First Rewriting **c. 2284 BR** (now "around 2280 BR" in the body and "c. 2280" in the chronology table); War of the Empty Halls **c. 1608–1596 BR** in the chronology table (now "c. 1600 BR"; the section heading still carries the exact range); Gharuk **"between seven and nine feet tall and stronger than at least five human men"** (now "whom tradition makes nine feet tall and stronger than five men"). The 2,014 Kharvos captives and their four register categories (611 / 487 / 532 / 384) remain in the reader text, now attributed to Ilyen Marr's copied captive register.

## [[The Folk before Atheria]]

- Fäskogarfolk is useful only as a reconstruction: no ancient person owed loyalty to a Fäskogarfolk state, and no living clan possesses a pure claim to the whole continuum.
- Later Enaidfae identities drew selectively from the Belathi learned houses without becoming a direct continuation of one ancient order; they were never a timeless priesthood unchanged from the Great Unbinding.
- Rauven identity never meant collaboration or resistance by itself.
- The Suroth cult never defined all Vezhari shore religion, and its signs do not yet identify whether the answering power was Menhir, Mask or another local continuity.
- A ruler who received a real response from a local power did not thereby acquire moral title to a people or land.
- Production note removed: "This Package D account establishes southern participants, cargo and political interests without naming every ancient Sestaran precursor or fixing the full maritime map before Package E."
- Exact figures replaced: Ivara destroyed the Brael Ford fort **c. 1972 BR** (now "around 1970 BR"); War of Seven Gords **c. 1184–1161 BR** (now "c. 1180–1160 BR"). The section headings for the War of the Three Fosters (c. 1342–1328 BR) and the War of the Three Crossings (c. 1096–1089 BR) were left unchanged.

## [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Ättar/70 History and Conflicts/The Ättar before the Great Keel-Taking.md|The Ättar before the Great Keel-Taking]]

- Ättar society did not begin with sea-kings, four supernatural kindreds or a single migration from a lost homeland; earlier voyages did not make every earlier farmer a viking.
- Imperial weakness after 400 AR changed the scale and opportunity of Ättar voyages; it did not reveal a timeless essence waiting since the Unbinding.
- Mother Moon's bargain did not make four species or states, guarantee good judgement or replace stores, weapons, ships and allies. It did not restore the stolen years or make an ancestor's assent the child's consent. The first children claimed were not payments rendered just by the victory which followed.
- Exact figure replaced: the war ended "by **c. 1596 BR**" (date dropped from the body). The section headings "c. 1608 BR", "c. 1607–1603 BR" and "c. 1602–1596 BR" were left unchanged.

## [[War of the Empty Halls]]

- Urzha never joined the human cause or became the coalitions' friend.
- Exact figures replaced: war fought **"from approximately 1608 to 1596 BR"** (now "over several years around 1600 BR"); "three abundant summers **before 1608 BR**" (now "before the war"); Gharuk **"stood between seven and nine feet tall and possessed at least the strength of five human men"** (now "Tradition makes him nine feet tall and stronger than five men"); war ended "by **c. 1596 BR**" (date dropped).

## [[The Tarkhan before the Horse Hordes]]

- Dependent Idol Masks exploited certain ancient Tarkhan traditions: Wrath fed through feud standards demanding blood beyond the original offence; Greed fed where ransom and water became occasions for owning people; Pride attached itself to dynasties claiming that their ancestors had ruled the upper sky. The reader text now describes the practices without naming the powers.

## [[The Ahdari before the Covenant]]

- Among Ahdari stones and standards, dependent Idol Masks gathered around a smaller number: Wrath fed through vengeance standards demanding blood beyond the original offence; Greed through stones at which water, ransom and debt were pledged as property; Pride through genealogical objects said to prove an untouchable line. The reader text now describes the objects without naming the powers.
- Neighbouring Ahdari camps used similar rites while addressing different powers.
- The physical effects of high-place rites remained limited to the encounter itself and supplied no judgement upon the worshippers' claims.
- Patron bargains remained separate from human water custom and well government.
- Gateway civic history remained independent of the well country.
