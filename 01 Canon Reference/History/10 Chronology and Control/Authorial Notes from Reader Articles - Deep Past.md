# Authorial Notes from Reader Articles — Deep Past

Safeguards, rulings and exact figures moved out of public deep-past history articles during the November 2026 editorial cleanup; each item keeps its original force.

## [[Creation and the First Rebellions]]

- Creation, humanity's fall and the rebellion of created powers are not competing accounts of the same event.
- The defeated Adversary did not become the secret maker of the world.
- The Circles are not a tidy succession of divine families, and not every strange mountain is a battlefield of the Faewild Schism.
- Neither Atherian star genealogy nor Folk threshold tradition is a complete authorial cosmology. No complete first-family epic waits behind every later tale, and no mortal eyewitness biographies are to be added to the Schism.
- The Glass and the Bird-Marked Sites are not assigned to this sequence as a convenient missing piece.
- A heavenly judgement follows a commission, not the independent appetite of a luminous creature.

Causality Pass, 14 November 2026. The new paragraph on battlefield standards is the public graft of GP-I-01, the War of Broken Standards. The standards are mindless Broken Standards; Commission Husks and Unhoused Witnesses also haunt such fronts. None is a surviving angel or an angel's soul, and damage to a mindless Standard is not suffering.

## [[Age of Chains]]

- The Prophet's movement entered an existing crisis of maintenance and inheritance; it did not secretly create every fault within it. The Great Unbinding was not a clean replacement of all Giants by one human state.
- The Broken Measure tricks spread from estate to estate without any secret continental organisation.

- **Root of the collapse.** Dilution reduced access to ancestral structures, so each continuity work had to be fed from outside; this is the declared root of the collapse ([[The Great Unbinding]], amended). The survivor legends are false as stated: no ritual dried the Serpent's Spine (the unmaintained river broke into a lower bed in a high flood), and the Blood Sun did not burst through a ritual (it starved, and the untended volcanic works beneath Xolotlan vented after the vent-keepers fled). "The retreat into the vaults" was the Qianglong rulers' entry into recurrence; none returned in the body.

Four claims upon one victory do not establish four equal commanders or a universal Feast War. Mask history: [[Idol Kingdoms and the Hunger Wars#^atlas-id-r01]].
- **Sevenfold Feast War (GP-I-02).** A later umbrella for successive proxy conflicts in which dependent Masks and occasional Archmasks of different Idol Thrones contested Giant rites. No local war contained all seven Hungers; no Regnant succession is promoted.
- **Broken Standards (GP-I-01).** First-War remnants mined by Giant courts; lowering, obscuring or disconnecting the command mark ends its order locally.
- **The roster house (IP-01, c. 2320–2260 BR).** An infernal custody work held intercepted human persons and separately extracted skills so devil offices could compel dead specialists' work through living replacements. The intercepted remained human souls. When the stair flooded, officials chained prisoners below; the rebels sealed it from the Material side. Builder and present Hell-side owner remain open. The Consequence Rule now permits this episode to decide who owned dead labour in that court. [[Infernal Kings and the Wars of the Depth#^atlas-ip-01]].
- **Flight and the abandoned.** The Falling Wards (PR-P01, [[G-18 - The Falling Wards|G-18]]) and the Three False Processions ([[UH-01 - The Caravan That Cast Off Its Wards - Authorial Truth|UH-01]], [[G-62 - The Three False Processions|G-62]]) are separate courts with separate rulers. The Hunt of the Escaped Courts (GP-I-03) and the palace caravan whose tenants delayed the slaughter-yard (PT-R06) are told in [[Patron Cities and the Long Rent Histories]].
- **The court that lost the surface (MN-R02, c. 2320–2260 BR).** A Hecatara–Sylvorn court in later Brauvalis–Green March country. Its demands on Orphaned survivors were its exact bargain, not proof that later occupants were thieves. [[Menhir Courts through the Ages#^atlas-mn-r02]].

## [[Age of Exodus]]

Causality Pass, 14 November 2026 (items 01.1 and 01.4).
- **End of provision.** The provision belonged to the refuge's commission and ended with it. It thinned for about a decade after the command south (c. 2180–2170 BR); the crossing was staged c. 2170–2165 BR and provision ceased company by company at the threshold; the Prophet died near the threshold c. 2165 BR while rear companies were still inside. Author-only scale: about forty thousand entered c. 2265 BR and 100,000–120,000 reached the threshold. Reader text says nobody counted. Full sequence in [[The Glass Sojourn]].
- **The four deaths.** The Slow Road (Jirahar), the Unpaid Well (Ahdari), the Herald's Ascent (Atherian) and the Guest Who Died at the Door (Folk and Aurin satire) are in-world traditions only. The authorial truth of the death stays among the Sojourn's fixed unknowns; no new sealed weird is created and no captain is named in the murder tradition. "No one is owned" is the one line common to all four.
- **The rear-camp Last Cart.** The reader article tells the rear cart of the Seed Garden's G-03 entry (cloudless shade, the last cup, the dead child's shoe) as the rear camps' variant beside the public apostasy telling of [[G-03 - The Last Cart]]. The camps' claim that its hour of shade was "the last day the sky fed anyone" is in-world, not a ruling on the provision's final day.
- **The Glass.** Its origin factions are those of the Weird register; nothing explains it.

Causality Pass 11B2, 14 November 2026 (second bend; light touch inside the intended Exodus frame).
- **Visible form of the provision.** The food provision is fixed as water and bearing: springs rising in channels of the glass at the appointed stages and anchor camps, and beds beside them that bear within weeks and in every season (barley for the camp bread, greens, beans). The reader text gives this as southern experience, not as cosmology.

- **The wrong seasons (GP-II-01, War of Unfinished Seasons).** Abandoned Giant recurrence machinery kept artificial seasons, plague gardens, famine works and death offices running. Menhir pressures (Veradia, Solrath, Morvalis, Nevaura, and others only where a real pressure intersects) completed, intensified, preserved or opposed each process according to jurisdiction, blind to the victims' guilt. Several regional conjunctions were later compressed into one war; no Menhir council met and no named Menhir is required at every site. Natural catastrophe is not divine moral judgement.
- **The city of the second hearth (PT-R02R; [[Patron Cities and the Long Rent Histories#^atlas-pt-r02r]]).** Craft, labour and carried matter built the refuge; the founder's memory contributed mass, not matter. Founder, city, memory-copies, anchor shards and souls remain distinct, and no copy is a returned human soul. The founder's identity, culture and continuity stay open. It is not Chalkhaven or the Unfinished Shore and has no connection to the Year of Standing Stars.
- **Year of Standing Stars.** Mentioned with the factions of [[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]] only; unresolved.

## [[Age of Hearths and First Kings]]

- Neither the proto-Jirahar nor the western well-country tradition arose at one council or from a single founder.
- Causality Pass, 14 November 2026. **Named-petitioner rule (01.3).** A Menhir bargain binds its named petitioner. Where the petitioner speaks for a line, the Boundary attaches the gift to that line *as the petitioner's community reckoned descent on the day of the petition*, and the gift lasts only while the line pays. A pact dynasty therefore inherits a frozen succession law and a crisis whenever an heir cannot pay. Pacts are one route to kingship among several; protection-into-rule (G-05) and conquest remain. Ark-derived law: no public office may rest on a bargain; private household bargains continued. This is why Tovan refused the protectorate, Yedran's house broke and Qiryath's guardians served for stated emergencies. The reader text presents the rule only as what people "found". **Iron (01.9):** smelted early and locally where salvage metal was scarce (Folk, Ättar; ore from the fens around Ossen and Odrani ridges); road metal of the north and east only after the Reclaiming (c. 1450–1325 BR). **Inventory promoted:** MN-R10R/G-67 (Withheld Season, c. 1850–1725 BR; the rider who refused substitution is a sapient court-law rider, not a saint or human soul; wording aligned with [[The Folk before Atheria]]); MN-R01 (Mounds of Several Kings, Folk uplands adjoining Brauvalis, c. 1500–1300 BR; the human custom of disqualifying maimed claimants, and rivals maiming rather than killing, is the promoted consequence; no single mound king ruled every Folk barrow); GP-II-03 (Crown-Market; a later scholarly umbrella for local recensions, not one continuous market; the bidders are Pride, Envy, Greed, Wrath and Lust Masks, royal office-personas, dead-ruler residue and occasional Patrons, all unnamed in reader prose; no crown creates kingship and no Mask becomes the ruler); OBA-N03 in brief (full account in [[The Ahdari before the Covenant]]).
- Causality Pass 11B2, 14 November 2026 (second bend). ## [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]]

- A commissioned heavenly act may have bound one Titan or ended one recurrence system during the wider judgement in which Vathra fell; the Unyoking itself was human work.
- Causality Pass, 14 November 2026. **Why the sky roads failed (01.2).** The routes were opened under Hecatara correspondences that recognised the First Road-Mother's `titan-continuity`. Hecatara displaces the distance of every shortened route onto the country below (canon's lengthening roads). Diluted heirs could no longer meet the access condition with blood, so they paid in prices Hecatara recognises: anchors, crews, tribute and captives delivered along the routes. In the Closing-Court years the failure term "displaced distance returns all at once" came due; the stretching valleys and the three-day walk between towers are that return. Ozhur IV's requisitions were bodies paid into a failing route. Reader prose gives the land and in-world readings only (weavers: the sky taking back what was borrowed; storm-house priests: lower-court impiety; Tarkhan singers: the Road-Mother collecting her roads).
- **Meya's bargain (01.3), c. 2255 BR.** Made at the dead anchor field above the Hundred Wells, through a Way-Weaver that formed there, under Hecatara's law. *Gift:* a sealed threshold; anyone approaching by a road shorter than the walked road arrives back where they set out. *Price:* the obligation to return by the long road; her line travels only on walked roads, and each holder at accession walks the whole circuit and names each well at its threshold (the Long Circuit). *Boundary:* the Hundred Wells and Meya's line, reckoned as her caravan house reckoned it on the day, through mothers. *Failure:* the threshold opens for the unwanted traveller. The named-petitioner rule is recorded under [[Age of Hearths and First Kings]]. The unnamed Bronze-Road crown-mother whose fever kept her from the walk (told in the Vathra atlas) is the failure term in action; raiders reached the inner cisterns that season. Meya's line survives to 1360 as a disputed road custom tolerated in Covenant law. The seal proves only its physical result (Package C); it confers no moral title.
- **Four lost cities (01.9).** Sairat, Molqara and Yazuran were lost in the war; Dazhur vanished within three years after it. Urzavad fell and Kel-Orun was grounded (Package C's six crown-cities). The in-world "three or four" stays: Tarkhan songs keep three by refusing to mourn Dazhur. The heading "The three uncertain cities" became "The lost cities"; no inbound anchor links existed. "Historical limits" became "Memory"; the negations moved here: later sightings of Sairat, Molqara, Dazhur or Yazuran prove no surviving dynasty.

## [[The Siege and the Returnings]]

- The exhibition of bronze said to come from the god's armour demonstrates the importance of the tale without identifying every object as part of the breakwater.

Causality Pass, 14 November 2026 (item 01.8). The speaking image is the founder's infrastructure-office speaking through a palace image: an office pattern with no soul and no Giant continuity. It was the named party to the tide mistress's inherited pact, which canon allows to attach to an office. When the craftspeople broke it, the pact defaulted, and its failure clause fell on everyone carrying the harbour's goods out by sea; the Returnings are that default, and ships carrying nothing from High Harbour were untouched. The homecoming drowning paid nothing, because no party remained to receive payment; the lord's seers misread the default. Cuts made: the captive-ransom quarrel and the blinding of the court being with the captain's boast. Kept with native causes: the confined woman, the companions eaten by the shore court and the drowned women of the homecoming. The southern custom against binding a sea pact to an image or office "that cannot drown" seeds later refuge law and is available to Causality Pass 02 at Kelarion.

Causality Pass 11B2, 14 November 2026 (second bend). The cycle no longer follows its sources' order of quarrel, duel, burial, sack, voyages and settlement; it keeps the two native hinges (the craftspeople break the image; ships that carried nothing from High Harbour came home) and is otherwise rebuilt. This note supersedes the 01.8 note above wherever the two differ, and supersedes the cycle paragraphs of [[Atherian Palace and Maritime Histories - Integration Review]] until that file is patched.
- **The pact through the siege.** The tide mistress performed throughout: flat water inside the breakwater, storms on the besiegers' anchorage in each of four winters. With the collection raids stopped, the founder's office-pattern named travellers from the harbour's own people (coalition prisoners, then debtors, servants and craft families by lot). That was the pact's exact price, executed by an office with no soul; the living house and its officers bear the guilt.
- **Why the image was broken.** The sanctuary woman confined by the commander escaped with the shipmistress's help and carried into the lower town what she had heard: he meant to keep the image and the channel. The craftspeople opened the water gate on sworn terms and broke the image themselves. The commanders broke the oath. There was no feigned withdrawal.
- **Powers.** Astaroth's mailed figure stays a bounded manifestation at an inherited thin place, supported by the old pier bronze; it ended when besiegers dug the bronze out and sold it. Nobody wounded it, and the songs' blood is withdrawn. The shore guide (Hecatara-associated court person) sold one short approach to the two women for a long inland return; the father's crossing is withdrawn. The wind envoy is withdrawn from the cycle. The likeness that urged the massacre is one dependent Wrath Mask wearing the faces of children taken through the channel; no dead soul was present and nobody was compelled. The inland ally's men chose to murder families who had opened the gate. The bereaved fighter and his companion are withdrawn.
- **The default.** After the image broke, the failure clause fell on whatever left the harbour by sea as property, persons held as goods included. A captive set free ashore ceased to fall under the pact's category of goods. This is the exactness of court law and carries no moral verdict; the shore tellers' moral is theirs.
- **The hulls.** The challenging captain sailed home before the second winter with slaves from the dependent villages and nothing from the harbour, and arrived. The commander ordered the sanctuary woman's rape and confinement, drowned bound women and children of the harbour at sea as "travellers", paid nothing by it and was lost with every hull; this is where the drowned women of the old homecoming now stand. The refuge-violating officer and the captain of the bay remain one man: he assaulted and carried off a gate-keeper's daughter (no sanctuary, no divine protection), lost her after the first wreck, paid the shore court's toll in bronze after it ate several companions, and was bound and drowned by the boat-owners of his own bay, which also paid nothing. The High Harbour women he had held opened a new channel and made a living-party pact with the same sea court: harbour goods through the channel, and a keeper who stands in the water at each season's turn and may be taken. The changing hostess's alterations do hide a body from the pact's pursuit; she sold them for service and also changed several who had refused. The shipmistress gathered captives put ashore by frightened captains and settled with them on an inhabited shore; the hulls were dismantled, and the ship-burning variant is withdrawn. The sanctuary woman reached that shore by the inland road.
- **No Saint or heavenly combatant** joins the cycle, and none of its court persons or Masks forms a council directing events, as before.
