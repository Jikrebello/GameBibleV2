# Authorial Notes from Reader Articles — Ancient East

Rulings, safeguards and exact figures moved out of the public ancient-east history articles during the November 2026 editorial cleanup; their force as canon is unchanged.

## [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/70 History and Conflicts/Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria.md|Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria]]

- Haviel's contest: "The flame and rain were genuine signs given for the exposure of the court cult. The fire left no active relic and conferred no continuing power upon the site."
- The gate at the siege: "This second sign answered their rescue. Seram had chosen to leave the families outside; Tilqa and Seram arranged Tobel's death; Haviel ordered the killings beside the dry channel. None of those acts acquired the authority of the signs." (Reader text now attributes the second sign to the Lower Stair recitation.)
- Received traditions: "Their continued performance is established; details concerning divine signs, private motives and later embellishment remain matters of interpretation."
- Exact figure replaced: Haviel returned when an Arqeshite-backed force threatened Qiryath in **835 BR** (reader text: "around 835 BR").

Causality Pass, 14 November 2026 (01.5, 01.7, inventory OBA-N01, cuts). **The sign of acceptance.** An accepted appointed offering burns without smoke and leaves the altar stones cool; elders of three custodial houses witness by touch and their names are written beside the day. The sign answers the sanctuary's appointed offerings, not persons, councils or kings; reading it as a verdict on city policy is human inference. Haviel's white flame (c. 842–835) was a separate sign outside the city. **The Withholding.** The first withheld offering fell in the siege's first winter, 743 BR, after the council expelled foreign residents to die between the lines; the sign stayed withheld through the fall (742), the exile, and the royal consecration offering (c. 660), and returned at the appointed offering carried by Yaelith's mixed households in 654 BR. That acceptance was authentic; the departing houses' charge of partisan witnesses is in-world dispute only. **The cause of the Withholding is unresolved even authorially.** Whether it answered the expulsion, the envoy's killing or the coming destruction is not decided here and must not be decided by any later file. The reader text gives each answer to an in-world party (Yavreth's party, the burial fraternities, Elior's followers). The *Book of the Unanswered Years* replaces the proposal's "Register of Unanswered Years". Usage: *the Withholding* for 743–654 BR; *the Silence* for the sign's ending at 0 AR (avoids Causality Pass 04's "Second Silence"). **The Reclaiming (c. 1450–1325 BR).** Title-marked Ishkatari salvage metal recalled dormant recovery works ([[Gilded Wardens]], Processional Coils, [[Estate Dead]]) when deeper estate-vaults were opened; they recovered marked metal along the old ceremonial roads, which the Bronze Roads reused as trunk routes. Confined to routes following Ishkatar's ceremonial roads. The Jirahar smiths' provenance custom is reader-level texture. **The Choir at the Eastern Road** is the reader face of OBA-N01 ([[Heavenly Campaigns and the Human Dead#^atlas-oba-n01]]): a genuine true-angel choir closed a road that slave dealers had made into an abduction passage for returning families, c. 700–650 BR; an ordinary caravan carried the last freed captives round it; later reopened stretches killed travellers. **Cuts.** Hador: the Nazirite dedication, the riddle and wager, Delira of the South Gate, the shaving, blinding and pillared hall are removed. Hador's killings on the road, Nareh's and her father's burning, the private war's dead, and the mill workers and prisoners killed with him remain; the orchard villages now hand him to Gedran for the reopening of its road, and "the Braids at Gedran" now names the braided halters they bound him with. Gedran's ruling household no longer dies with him, so Othan fights an undamaged Gedran. Yedran: the Bathsheba–Uriah frame is removed. Yedran withholds relief grain from Uriad's starving post to feast his eldest son's marriage alliance with a river house; Rava is Uriad's widow and her lament's golden bowl now fits. The sister's rape and the half-brother's killing are kept, re-caused through the grain rooms Yedran's sons held in the hungry years; the Amnon–Tamar–Absalom sequence (feigned sickness, delayed revenge at a feast) is removed. The sister remains unnamed.

## [[01 Canon Reference/History/30 Exodus and First Civilizations/Arqesh - River Cities, Kings and Deportation.md|Arqesh - River Cities, Kings and Deportation]]

- The Crowned Countenance (identity ruling also held in Package B): "During the later empire, these ceremonies attracted the dependent Pride Mask known as the Crowned Countenance. Compulsory praise and the public humiliation of subject rulers fed it. The Mask supplied flattering dreams, intensified the presence of royal ceremonies and occasionally made an image answer in the reigning king's voice." Reader text now gives the dreams and presence as courtiers' attribution.
- "Elsewhere in the riverlands, similar royal or river images belonged to local Menhir relationships, ordinary natural phenomena, frauds or other Idol Masks. Their shared artistic forms reflected Arqeshite culture rather than a single power behind every cult."
- Exact figure replaced: Arqeshite troops occupied road stations, captured eastern canal towns and transferred fields to military supporters **between 811 and 802 BR** (reader text: "over the following decade").

Causality Pass, 14 November 2026. (a) Narethi's marriage request is cut as a copied beat (the Zannanza affair) and with it the "two queens in 814" collision; Ashkur's 814 murder stays, now while escorting Radiance officials who fled Zaheret's restoration back to their confiscated temple fields. His killers remain a protected matter (Package B). (b) Ilbarun II's motive (01.5): the cool-stone judgements drew provincial disputes to Qiryath; the image demand was a test he could not lose. Iltara's alternative was to buy the merchants. The Withholding's cause stays unresolved here as everywhere. (c) The coastal deportations (01.7): authorial dating c. 740–730 BR; Belyra is the deportation harbour, so its 697–695 massacre carries a contested motive of reprisal. (d) Kaveh's apron cut: Ishara of the Soot Court now leads the workers who open Khalbar; Vasharan coronation hymns thank them beside the Daryam flame. The massacre that followed stays. (e) Kassur's portable images: brought to Eryndor with the body in 688, kept as regalia, carried with the corpse to Delkos in 673 and walled into the tomb c. 655. The Countenance remained anchored in them; see Eryndor below.

Causality Pass 11B1, 14 November 2026 (second bend). **Irduq.** The Sargon frame (basket, gardener, rival birth tales, a royal ancestor claimed by a later dynasty) is cut. Irduq is a foundling found on Teluq's regulator beam around 1370 BR on the night the head gate opened; he kept the gate and never ruled. What opened the gate is left to the three in-world accounts and is not ruled here. The event is legend, not a register Weird, and nothing later depends on its cause. The beam and the sleeping-child seal reached Arqesh's Gate House before House Ubar; whether by flood or by theft is open. The seal's authority over canal officials is custom and training, not a binding: Kassur III's sealing of the water books to Arezan in 688 worked because the officials chose to honour it. **Ilbarun II** dies in 731 (was 728) of ordinary marsh fever. **The fall (690).** Kassur III cut the rations of the dredging gangs and deportee quarters, and they rose and opened the gates. Their leaders are unnamed. Belyra deportees in the Nemeru yards ferried Eryndor's soldiers; the city of Nemeru stayed shut. Both armies sacked the city and set the gangs back to work under guard; the Decree of Roads and Houses came seventeen years later, in 673. No river portent attends the fall. Item (e) above is superseded: the images went over to Eryndor in 688 while Kassur lived, and from 680 they spoke in a dead companion's voice (see Eryndor below).

## [[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]]

- Daryam's fire: "The fire was ordinary created matter, kept alive through skill and fuel."
- The Right Order: the Lie was treated as human corruption "rather than a second power of creation".
- Ashara of the Three Questions: "No reliable tradition gives her supernatural powers."
- The Threefold Fire and the Witness of Dawn (Mask identity also held in Package B): "The three fires were ordinary lamps and braziers with separate histories. During Tavrad's reign, an Idol Mask began to imitate the radiance of the royal ceremony."
- "Highland springs and passes sometimes answered through established Menhir relationships or the actions of individual court persons."
- The White-Haired Fosterling's bird: "Its identity as a court person, a Patron visitor or a poetic addition remains unknown." (Withdrawn 14 November 2026: the Fosterling cycle is cut; see below.)
- Principal places: "These are relational placements. Coordinates and modern borders remain open."

Causality Pass, 14 November 2026 (01.5, inventory OBA-N13, cuts). **Arezan's wager.** Arezan's decree and first grants began the rebuilding of Qiryath and Tavrad completed it, as a bet that the sign would return under Vasharan protection. The royal consecration offering (c. 660) was unanswered; the acceptance of 654 answered the mixed households' offering, and Vasharan inscriptions crediting the crown are false. **The Witness of Dawn** is the dependent Pride Mask already ruled in Package B; it began imitating the royal radiance in the years after the unanswered consecration offering, and Daryam's claim that it was the withheld sign transferred to the king is a counterfeit. It decides nothing in Causality Pass 03's span (350 BR–0 AR). **The ended acclamation (431 BR)** is the reader face of OBA-N13 ([[Heavenly Campaigns and the Human Dead#^atlas-oba-n13]]): at the harvest audience at Kavresh a Witness-Judgement angel struck Orvaz I at the moment public praise of him as the giver of the harvest became accepted divine honour; his perjury of c. 462, the executed leaders and his demand for road-command children for the dawn-house belong to the judgement. The interruption cut the Witness of Dawn's access to the royal cult for about a generation without destroying the Mask or touching Pride's Throne; no Idol succession occurs, and the event is not a reusable test of legitimate sovereignty. Reader text keeps the physicians' sickness against the Jirahar interpreters' messenger. Tiraz III's accession after fighting among the royal kin is left unspecified pending Causality Pass 03's reign-list replacement. **Cuts.** The White-Haired Fosterling (Zal and the Simurgh) is cut; Razana Tiravan remains a physician and negotiator who refused the throne, with no exposure and no bird. The Champion and the Unrecognised Son (Rostam and Sohrab) is cut. Mazhara is no longer punished by the sale of her workers: Tavrad co-opts her as keeper of the royal flame, the orchards and their bonded workers pass to the flame's estate, and her sayings are carved in edited form; her students' uncarved text keeps her refusal to call the Witness of Dawn a sign. Rathava (c. 462) recants in the palace garden to save his labourers from sale; his school splits. **Kazren.** The error flagged by Causality Pass 02 ("inherited when Qarzeth's destruction had released Caleran ships") is already corrected in this article (Kazren accedes in 536 as Caleran prepares its final war; Qarzeth falls 520; Tasar revolts 514); only the archived Package B draft still carries the old sentence.

Causality Pass 11B1, 14 November 2026 (second bend). **Regnal framework** (replaces the list assumed above): Arezan I 704–671; Tavrad I 671–638; Tiraz I 638–629; Kavran I 629–594; Mithara I 594–552; Tiraz II 552–528; Kazren I 528–491; Orvaz I 491–439; Tiraz III 439–409; Orvaz II 409–after 350. The Achaemenid order (accession revolts, organiser, sea-war king, league era) is broken on purpose. **The relay road** is Mithara Tiravan's wartime work (c. 706–690), kept by hearth domains as their levy; the road commands grew from Arezan's gate-city captains. **Tavrad I** let the building grant lapse, as the Qiryath atlas now has it, and sent the consecration offering; Nemeru's rising of c. 659–657 answered the smoke. The Terrace of Mirrors was built to show the Witness of Dawn. The trilingual inscription is cut: the royal sentence over each relay house is *the king keeps the road*, and Mazhara's saying ends "when it is carved over every door". **Tiraz I** drowned in 629 when a canal district cut its embankment over his tax camp, an ordinary act of desperate villagers. **Kavran I** is the son of Vahrad Tiravan and Mehrava and so Eryndor's grandson. Daryam's keepers carried the flame to him past Tiraz's infant sons, which is the precedent for their choice in the War of the Two Dawns. **Kazren I** died of ordinary camp flux in the winter of 492–491. **Orvaz I** swore Avesh to the Aresh house at eighteen and took his revenge around 462. **The Ended Acclamation** (OBA-N13) moves to 444 BR and is reshaped to remove the Acts 12 frame. A Witness-Judgement angel stopped the acclamation at the moment accepted praise became worship, by closing the mouths of those present on that one formula. Nobody was struck or killed. The people present, the dawn-house children among them, could never again voice the praise; the effect did not pass to anyone absent. The children were sent home. Starved of the spoken praise, the Witness of Dawn lost its access to the royal cult for about a generation; the Mask was not destroyed, no Idol succession occurs, and the event is not a reusable test of sovereignty. Orvaz died of age in 439. The radiance returned to the mirrors around 410. Daryam's ten-year refusal of the flame to the child Orvaz II (409–399) forced the regency to swear that the mirrors would not be called a sign; whether later kings kept that oath belongs to the War of the Two Dawns. The cousin's revolt of 403 is withdrawn.

## [[Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest]]

- Collision safeguard (also held in Package B): "Eryndor belongs to an earlier political world than Teren Casvilar and Aurelius Thalion. He was a hereditary king who compelled Atherian cities to serve a royal campaign. Casvilar arose from the institutions of a mature republic, while Thalion converted victory in a civil war into lasting imperial government."
- The Qahuri sign (ruling also held in Package B): "A limited phenomenon associated with Father Sun occurred during the journey… It gave him neither divine parentage nor authority over Qahur." Reader text now attributes the Father Sun association to witnesses.
- "The marriage joined the alliance without removing the different ambitions of the two kings."
- Crowned Countenance at Eryndor's court (also held in Package B): "the royal Pride Mask known as the Crowned Countenance… The dead Ubar kings, Eryndor and the Mask remained separate persons and powers."

Causality Pass, 14 November 2026 (items 01.6, 01.7 and the cuts). **The Voice at Delkos — authorial truth.** Eryndor consented to the White Measure rite at Arqesh in 674 BR; Neresh of Qemaret supplied the masters and Serevan paid. The regimen had barely begun. The masters attempted the final rite at his death in 673 and it failed: his complete human soul went the ordinary human road. The rite closed his true name into an anchor his soul had already left. That empty anchor was filled by the estate-office built around his plans and his attendants' memories, and by the Crowned Countenance, a dependent Pride Mask still anchored in Kassur III's portable images, which Eryndor had kept as regalia. Within months the corpse spoke in his voice. The Voice is a dependent Mask using an office and residue: no Archmask, no accession, no transfer of sovereignty, and no part of Eryndor. Its knowledge of companions' secrets came from the attendants' memories folded into the office. Its double grants follow from the Mask feeding on rival petitioners' praise. The Qemaret masters' testimony of c. 657 was true, whatever Neresh's reasons for procuring it. Eryndor's cause of death stays open (Package B); the mineral regimen is one rumour among several. **Motive.** Pay set the timing of the war; the deportations from Belyra made it possible to declare. **Cuts.** Cleitus and Philotas are cut as Plutarch beats; the two killings stay, now caused by the quarrel over bowing to Kassur's images. The officer and companion remain unnamed (protected in Package B). The Alexander Romance incidents are reduced to one linking sentence; the bell's drowned attendants, the Sky-Lion dead, the abandoned prisoners and the missing traveller remain.

Causality Pass 10B1 and 11B1, 14 November 2026. The bullet above on the Qahuri sign is withdrawn: there was no oracle, no sign and no Qahuri title. **Authorial truths now in force.** Valeron was killed by soldiers of his standing infantry at Pelatha. Who began the shouting, whether anyone paid them and whether Demeran Kavos stood at the table are protected. The Pelatha companies never crossed the sea. About a third of Marenthe's treasury was shipped to the Common Sky's temple at Caleran before the gates opened in 698 and never returned; reader prose keeps the charge unanswered. Eryndor never saw Belyra. Nothing supernatural happened there, and the earlier protected matter of the bell is withdrawn. Khaseret is a genuine Patron. The loan of 695 bought her masters the right to prepare Eryndor's body and a chamber of her House corresponding to him, and put his garrisons at her store courts; the chamber was never filled. **Mehrava** is Eryndor's daughter by Parena, born in 691; the conception at the gorge is withdrawn. **The images.** The Crowned Countenance followed the bowing to Eryndor in 688. Its order to the officer, in the seventh year (681), was Mask behaviour: it feeds on compelled abasement, and after seven years of the east's bowing it tried the nearest proud man. After the bearers strangled the companion, the strongest shame in the hall was the king's, and the Mask fed on it by taking the dead man's voice. It used the hall's memory of him; no part of the companion, a complete human soul gone the ordinary road, was present. Whether the king nodded is protected. At Delkos the Mask took Eryndor's voice again because the failed rite's anchor and the estate-office were built on him. The closed wood is ordinary Root-Mind law (taboo: severing root connections and leaving the cut ground unused). The seep is restorative Egg White water under local conditions, and its keeper was human. The Sky-Lion pursuit is ordinary pride behaviour after egg theft. Eryndor's cause of death stays protected. The Belyra crews' flight with the body was their own decision; Serevan neither ordered nor prevented it.

## [[Ortheia and the Upland Kings]]

- "No settlement made Ortheia the birthplace or natural possession of the later Empire."
- "Its precise boundaries and royal seat remain open pending the revision of the regional map." (Also in the article's `needs` field and Package B's protected matters.)

Causality Pass, 14 November 2026 (01.9, 01.7). The first struck coin of the known world is Ortheian silver, c. 730 BR, minted to pay Valeron II's permanent infantry; the Successor courts spread coinage after 673. Earlier "foreign coins" elsewhere are stamped ingots and ring-silver. The four disagreeing accounts of Eryndor's motive (Ortheian songs, Caleran writers, Marenthan lists, Vashari stories) are the required in-world disagreement; authorially, pay set the timing.

Causality Pass 11B1, 14 November 2026. Demeran Kavos is the elected speaker of the Pelatha companies, not Valeron's general or Eryndor's appointed regent. The unnamed elder half-brother held the royal seal from 702, was acclaimed by the upland assembly in 673 and was killed on Demeran's order in 672. Marenthe was sacked by the home companies in 699–698.

Causality Pass 11B1, 14 November 2026. Amends the note above. The half-brother was killed because a "living" king made him a usurper; Demeran used the Voice once and denounced it when it granted the upland mines elsewhere. Mehrava was eighteen. The farther-eastern Successor is the garrisons cut off beyond the closed wood in 679, known from dispatches asking for pay, some of which the Voice answered. Causality Pass 02 plans a Chain War passage inside "Orvaz I and the war of clients"; this pass changed only the Rathava sentences in that section.

Causality Pass 10B1 and 11B1, 14 November 2026. **Ilyessa** is resolved, and the two bullets above that call it unresolved are withdrawn. A tide court of Thalassor's grammar in the Narrows, served by Ilyessan pilot families, permits passage. Its taboos are calling the sea owned and demanding stillness of water that has carried you. Its failure runs as pressure and return: weight and tide-marks in sealed holds, cargo taken back as salt and rot. Zadran's fleet in 502 broke the second taboo by lying at anchor through a season to starve the pilots out; Kazren's in 493 broke both. The court answers the act and not the pilots, who command nothing. The royal inquiry's finding of sorcery is false, and Kazren's inference from it is his own error. Ebris was human; what she knew of the court is protected. The design note "supernatural powers influence particular beliefs and moments without directing the campaigns" is superseded for Ilyessa by the Consequence Rule. **Other changes.** The Atherian squadron of 513 and the rebels' burning of lower Avesh are withdrawn. The Senate refused Liora because the Compact's war rolls were failing in the loose years. Lower Avesh burned in the winter of 492–491 at the hands of Ardash Aresh's troops. Tasar fell in 511. The siege fever and the beach of 503 are withdrawn. Velanthe's admission of Tasar's fugitives (511) and of the king's deserting crews (493) both fall under Kelarion's Roll; the second, given to an enemy in a genuine crisis, is the one that strengthened the seal. Kazren died of ordinary flux, and Orvaz I's accession is 491.
