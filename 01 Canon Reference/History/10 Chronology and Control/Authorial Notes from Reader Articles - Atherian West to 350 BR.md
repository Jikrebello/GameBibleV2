# Authorial Notes from Reader Articles — Atherian West to 350 BR

Safeguards, rulings and exact figures moved out of public Atherian, island and Vorthara history articles during the November 2026 editorial cleanup; safeguards already held by Package E, the Atherian Palace and Maritime review, Caleran Foundations or the Atherian Formation delivery review were deleted without duplication here.

## [[Age of City-States]]

- The stolen sounding-weight's later usefulness establishes neither ownership nor heavenly favour for the raiders.
- [[G-74 - The Raid That Returned One Tool|The Raid That Returned One Tool]] is separate from [[The Refusal at Kelarion]], whose storm, denied refuge and sealed Dragon belong to their own event.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* What rose at Kelarion is [[The Shoreless King]]; reader prose gives only the coast's name, "the King beneath the Breakwater", and the tellers' reading of it as punishment.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The Champion of Four Doors is authorially one autonomous Envy Archmask holding three separate, revocable leases from Wrath, Greed and Pride (ID-R04R / ID-P02R). The magistrates knowingly accepted the prices. There is no human champion, no composite Idol and no Regnant accession, and the city founds nothing in Caleran or Atheria. Reader prose says only that the cult league "knew what it was". The earlier line "a royal image could absorb the biography of a dead champion" was replaced because Idol canon denies the Archmask a stolen human biography.

## [[Age of Stars]]

- The G-64 summary is now attributed to prisoners' songs; the established account remains in [[UH-03 - The Prisoners Who Became a Border - Authorial Truth]].
- Exile did not turn all Jirahar into one wandering profession.
- No legend proves that the stars commissioned an empire, and a functioning relic does not authorise every person who carries it.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* Reader link to the author-only [[Unknown Histories - Authorial Accounts]] removed from the G-64 paragraph; the account remains there and in UH-03.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The city of the turned gate is [[OBA-N07 - Wheels at the Withdrawing Glory]], dated c. 608 inside Tashqar's war. The Lord commissioned a high-order choir to withdraw the refuge covenant's protection; the siege, executions and exile stay human acts, and Tashqar's allies were not made righteous. The city's name stays unknown.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* "The highland league of nine valleys" is Orthea's Nine-Valley League; the vanished allied soldiers are the witnesses killed in [[The Erasure of Orthea]].
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The noon hour of 488 was answered by a noon-bound place-mind of [[Father Sun]]; the rot was the failure of the "bear without limit" request. The 466 apparition at Pyrassa was a partial recurrence of [[The Shoreless King]].

## [[Arkenan and Saronikan Cities before the Empire]]

- Exact date bands replaced in reader prose: Kassilon's Erenost grain seizure, c. 1550–1500 BR (now "the sixteenth century BR"); Doreva's Pyrassan grain voyages, c. 1220–1180 BR (now "around 1200 BR"); Mesthra active at Irenai, c. 960–910 BR (now "the tenth century BR").
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The unreckoned-days sanction worked through the Common Sky's teaching and the crews' fear; no power withheld anything from Verion. The 466 apparition at Pyrassa was a partial recurrence of the Shoreless King.

## [[Palace Survivors and the Southern Heroic Age]]

- Exact date band replaced: Kassilon's seizure of Erenost's grain, c. 1550–1500 BR (now "the sixteenth century BR"). It followed the principal Giant collapse by many generations.

## [[The First Atherian City-States]]

- Early civic rites incorporated practices actually associated with Menhir and with Idol remnants, often misunderstanding the powers involved. Reader prose now says only "Giant courts and older cults".

## [[Road, Tower, River and Highland Life]]

- Springs, shore powers and Masks remained separate actors even when worshippers used similar language for water, fire or ancestors.

## [[Caelovar]]

- Transformed survivors did not become one uniform species and do not explain all later Deep Envoys; neither the transformed descendants nor other deep peoples explain all the others.
- Design note: continued habitation makes the site a place with descendants and neighbours, not merely a tomb awaiting adventurers.

## [[Marelith]]

- The reefback bearer was not a created island or an incarnation of Thalassor.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The 355 burning opened no wound and stirred no dragon; "a second Kelarion" is an island name only.

## [[Black Reef]]

- The name describes the blackened wreck only; Black Reef was not a formal city or a new island polity.

## [[Historical Atlas of the Elmaric Sea before 350 BR]]

- Caelovar's claimed parity with Nythalum expressed ambition, not shared antiquity or control of Nythalum's lunar programme.
- The Giant imperial horizons were large relationships of power, not a complete list of every coast and island institution.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* What rose under Pyrassa's breakwater in 466 was a partial recurrence of the Shoreless King, ended when the wardens cut the chain; reader prose uses only the coast's name, the King beneath the Breakwater.

## [[Seravon before 350 BR]]

- The ancient zone leaned towards dynastic houses. The later Sermoran republic of captains and merchant institutions resulted from subsequent conquest and civic struggle, not from an original constitution.
- The harbour founded twice is the history of one place, not a charter for the whole great island or an account of all Elmaric origins.

## [[Shoal and Lagoon Islands before 350 BR]]

- Shoal pilotage was neither a magical immunity to storms nor a single hereditary guild shared by every shoal.

## [[Vorthara before 350 BR]]

- Duplicates across the Vorthara trio were consolidated: plants, thresholds and cult-court competition now sit in [[Vortharan Working Precincts before 350 BR]]; the Hand's recruits, soul rule and possible ancient clients sit in [[The Pale Hand before 350 BR]].
- The Qahuri-rooted craft did not make every member Qahuri or every Qahuri funerary worker a necromancer.
- The existence of a cult house did not prove its patron existed as described or granted power. A house claiming an angelic visitor remained a human institution and gained no right to summon or hire a loyal angel.
- Neither the Hand's leadership in death-work nor one patron's power made any cult the ruler of Vorthara.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The Provider house formed no new Mask and gave the Provider no second anchor; its table drew at most Feastling fauna. The salt did nothing because no defeat was carried out there; the Hand's refusal of bodies cut its supply.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The Kept Doors are a mutual defence of a few households, not a citywide settlement (Package E).

## [[Vortharan Working Precincts before 350 BR]]

- Occupied inner rooms do not imply that anyone had explored or mastered the whole Spire; the difference between open and deeper thresholds was local power and use, not a measured descent into its unknown original plan.

## [[The Pale Hand before 350 BR]]

- Reader prose now attributes the soullessness of ordinary porters to the Hand's own claim; the controlling ruling remains in [[Package E - Island and Vorthara Canon Integration Review]].
- The Hand did not govern the Black Spire. Its present-day ward, materials and hidden practices cannot be assumed to have existed unchanged before 350 BR.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The refusal of c. 498 treats the Hand's public rule against soul-binding as already old; its concealed circle is unaffected.

## [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth]]

- Reader prose no longer names the Bronze-Faced Provider as a dependent Gluttony Mask or states that its Throne survived; both remain in [[Atherian Palace and Maritime Histories - Integration Review]].
- Removed ruling for review: "Good harvests following sacrifice strengthened the cult, even where weather and ordinary labour explained the plenty." The narrator's naturalistic explanation conflicts with the November 2026 Consequence Rule amendment and the causality review's proposal that Qarzeth's recovery was real Mask plenty.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The removed hedge is resolved: the plenty was real. The defeat order (carts stopped, dead named, face broken, survivors' meal) is the Gut-Maw grammar closed by a meal without hierarchy of worth, which ends the capital's bounded dominion overlay ("tables grew longer as the guests grew fewer"). No Archmask, no Regnant; Gluttony's Throne survives.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* Further reading's link to the restricted [[Gluttony Masks and Feast-Beasts]] was replaced with [[The Hunger Wars]].

## [[The Gateway Wars]]

- Sapha of Esharat, the midwife whose daughter Neral was sacrificed, is unrelated to Princess Sapha of Hadrath, Teshun Ordan's wife.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The jars' bite marks are Feastling signs of the Provider's draw; the Provider is the dependent Gluttony Mask fixed in [[Atherian Palace and Maritime Histories - Integration Review]].
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The Lustration of the Field broke [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth.md|Astaroth]]'s taboo on substituting a helpless victim for the named contestant; the defections of 608 are its failure clause running. The Warden's bar on quitting the field is Astaroth's taboo on fleeing an accepted field.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The irreconcilable records of the sack, and Teshun Ordan's absence from Caleran's rolls, are the work of the Unwritten Worm during the loose years; reader prose gives only the historians' quarrel.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* The bronze face's breaker stays disputed even authorially; the face broke as the organ-object once the names were recovered.
- *(Second bend 11A1, 14 November 2026)* The victims of 609 were the hostage cohort delivered in 611; reader prose no longer says they had been held since the first peace. Cohorts were delivered at the peace of 660 and renewed in 653, 646, 639, 632, 625, 618 and 611. The Council Houses sent the 611 cohort to prove they had disowned Tashqar.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* Tashqar truly sailed without the council's commission. Whether he wanted the Council Houses forced into the war is Oreshi tradition and is not ruled. The bitten wax in his own camp was the same Feastling sign: every table laid in the Provider's name belonged to the Mask's feeding arrangement, and once the allied towns' stores were exhausted the draw fell on the nearest stores. His killing at Erenost was a human act; the burial under the threshold did nothing.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* Celor Marath's contest ended in Astaroth's grammar when his named opponent ceased to contest the ground. Who killed Tashqar is immaterial to the Star; the augurs' year of argument was human.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* The doors that stayed shut in 531 and again c. 527 were the Mask's feeding arrangement going unfed. The collectors' books were its invitation route, a name entered being a guest promised. No power "refused" Qarzeth's surrender; "its assembly had surrendered, and its god had not" is the Gateway country's reading. New books begun by the cult reopened the route.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* Damen was killed by his own council. Sapha of Hadrath was married to Teshun Ordan as a surety and remains unrelated to Sapha of Esharat. Teshun's fate at the sack is still lost.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* The grain contractors meant to keep the sanctuary stores; whether Caleran's senate did is not ruled. The salt laid by the towns' carriers remains the last act of a defeat already complete. Valeor's forty-one ransomed children are an Esharati register figure and may be taken as true.

## [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]]

- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* Astaroth genuinely accepted Harod's field in 466 and Casvilar's in 376, since he accepts any real contest whatever its justice. Harod died of fever; the poison story is false. The challengers of 370 broke the contest's bounds, and Astaroth's failure clause ran as one cause of the succession wars among several, until Thalion stood on the Field unchallenged in 350.
- *(Causality Pass 02, 14 November 2026)* "The scattered valleys of the lost archive league" are Orthea's former valleys, assessed directly by Caleran after the league dissolved.
- *(Second bend 11A1, 14 November 2026)* The note above is amended in one respect: Astaroth accepted Casvilar's field at the siege lines of Kellovar in 383, against Drenoc. There was no frontier altar and no acceptance in 376. In Astaroth's grammar that contest ended on the night Drenoc ceased to contest the ground. The thirteen-year "open Field" was the augurs' reading, which suited Casvilar and then trapped him. His acceptance of the claimant's challenge in 370 was a new and genuine contest; the seven broke its bounds, and the failure clause ran as already ruled.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* The ford below Kellovar is the river-court of Hecatara's grammar ruled in [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]] §9, and token crossings in 383 BR worked as they do there. Drenoc asked passage for the tokenless as a named petitioner. The price exacted was the delivery of a traveller, himself, and surrendered destinations for those carried, who came out at other fords of the court's reach. Drenoc came out at a far southern ford, could return only by the long road, which Atherian posts held, and died among the Grobi-watch halls about 375 under another name. None of the three men shown to the augurs was Drenoc. The claimant of 370 was a Kellovar fosterling born beyond the ford and was not Drenoc's son. Reader prose keeps every part of this disputed, and the court judged no one.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* The mutilation at Darneth was Casvilar's choice and had no effect on the river-courts, which do not ask how a token is carried. The wrist-tying story is river-people's lore and is not ruled.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* Counting unwilling men as contestants is augurs' law, not Astaroth's. In 452–449 the Star accepted only the two contests Harod actually fought. The seven senators who fled, and Lethren Ostar in 379, accepted nothing and broke no taboo; no failure clause ran, because no helpless victim was killed in a contestant's place. The Writing-In of 367 is the first time since the Lustration of 609 that this happened.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* Harod never brought troops into Caleran; the seizure of 448 and its confiscations are withdrawn. Eshren Damar was not among the petitioners who asked the land to "bear without end". His thirty bareheaded years paid nothing further to the Sun; they were his own penance. The Long Month of 488 and the rebel cities' count of 466–461 were calendar decisions with no supernatural effect.
- *(Second bend 11A1)* Pel Ostar survived Rethmar and died in a water keeper's household there; his descendants are not ruled. Lethren Ostar died at Vorthara about 374. Whether a Kept Door took him in is Vortharan tradition and is not ruled.
