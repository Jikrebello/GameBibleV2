# Real-History Concordance

How the Known World maps onto real history — the people, their motivations, and how each borrowing gets recontextualized so it is load-bearing rather than a knockoff. Companion to [[Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)]].

## The Compass Key

The world is a **mirrored Mediterranean**: north is the desert (the Ahl-e-Ahd's North-Africa/Levant analog), the temperate middle belt holds the old imperial heartland, and south descends into cold forest, taiga, and permafrost (the Folk, the Ättar, and beyond them the Grobi — the pagan Baltic/steppe frontier, frozen). **Thalmyria faces west, Eclessia faces east.** This deliberately flips the real map: the "Byzantine" power sits where Western Rome sat and the "Catholic" world where the East was. The flip is a feature — it prevents one-to-one geographic tracing while keeping every historical dynamic intact.

**Ättar design register:** Viking-age and North Sea hall society, heroic hall poetry, jarls, seasonal raiding, weregild, thralls, and gift economies provide the social scaffold. Changeling traditions and a deliberately transformed skinwalker motif provide the supernatural layer. These comparison labels are creator-facing only; public canon uses halls, taking the keel, Moon-Seers, skinwalker chiefs, and Paint-Keepers.

## Era-by-Era Mapping

### Bronze Age & Exodus → Age of Chains, Exodus, Scattering

The Five Imperial Horizons and their many client crowns are the Bronze Age palace world seen from below: monumental, bureaucratic, divine-king systems where ordinary people are labor inputs. Their fall compresses the Bronze Age Collapse and the biblical Exodus into the [[The Great Unbinding|Great Unbinding]] — prophetic plagues are one part of the systems collapse alongside revolt, Giant civil war, failed escape, divine judgement, and material breakdown. The collapse has a native engine the analogs lack: thinning Giant blood made the old works demand crews, captives, grain and at last children, and the plagues struck along the line where bodies were taken. [[The Glass Sojourn]] extends the Mosaic wilderness grammar into approximately a century of refuge and ethnogenesis: a mixed host becomes one people, and the Great Prophet dies at the outward threshold as its last direct witness to the Giant world. Joshua 5's manna, which ends once the people can eat the land's produce, is bent twice. The provision is water and bearing, springs in channels of the glass and gardens beside them that never go out of season; nothing lies on the ground at dawn. It ends on its commission's schedule, as a channel going dry, before the host can farm, and the cost is a conquest of farmers. Moses on Nebo is replaced by four incompatible public deaths. The later three-generation sanctuary fails in a quarrel over the seed the rear camps were eating, while responsibility for that failure remains disputed. The Late Bronze Age collapse proper returns much later as the northern Bronze-Road disruption (c. 1450–1325 BR): it keeps its human causes, but its famous part becomes the Reclaiming, when Ishkatar's dead came for their title-marked metal. Lydian coinage is replaced by Ortheian army silver c. 730 BR. The Scattering then uses two fissions rather than one tribal partition: southern war-settlement leagues separate first; the continuing Near-Glass population divides into proto-Jirahar and proto-Ahl-e-Ahd only generations later. Independent Orphaned reassembly and later Braided ethnogenesis proceed alongside both.

**Recontextualization rule:** the Giants must never feel like "fantasy Egypt." Five dominant horizons contain dozens of overlapping states, dynasties, subject cities, and institutions. Their leavings are the setting's Dwemer-ruin equivalent: ancient Glass they did not create, blood-sun craters, sealed Titan bodies, folded courts, and Moon-stranded machine persons. Later cultures inherit different wounds; no one empire or Exodus company causes them all.

### Mixed Maritime Recovery → Proto-Atherian Heroic Age

The proto-Atherian south takes from Late Bronze Age mobility the mixture of raiders, migrants, mercenaries, settlers, captive markets and fortified ports—not one ethnic “Sea People” or one universal collapse. Glass measures and preserved crafts help reconnect several post-Giant recovery horizons, but local pilots, smiths, farmers, laborers and routes make those networks possible. Different regions later break under war, extraction and route failure at different times.

This is also the first of four changing Atherian identities: war-settlement membership, heroic-maritime allegiance, city-civilizational identity and republican-imperial citizenship. The stages inherit from one another without constituting an unchanged bloodline.

The *Iliad*'s war over Helen becomes a war over a speaking image bound to a tide pact. Causality Pass 11B2 broke the cycle's order as well. The blight after a seized woman, the wounded god, the river in the wreckage, the father's ransom of a body, the feigned withdrawal, the avenger's homecoming and the burned ships are gone. The besieged harbour pays its tide from its own streets; a woman the commander confined carries his intentions into the town; the craftspeople open the gate and break the image; and the voyages home are told by cargo, with the commander lost, a returning captain drowned by his own bay and the women he held bargaining with the sea in their own names. The victims of the older telling are kept under these causes. The siege lasts four winters, not ten years. [[Causality Pass - 11B2 Second Bend - Qiryath, the Siege and Qahur]]

### Greece → Age of City-States and Age of Stars

The Atherian city-states are the poleis: civic rivalry, leagues, tyrants, colonization, philosophy as civic technology. The Age of Stars is the classical-Hellenistic synthesis — astronomy, archives, law — that Rome inherited and militarized. The Star Pantheon forms here.

The Delian and Latin leagues survive as Caleran's custody of the Celestial Compact's calendar, rolls and ship money: a member whose days were left out of the year lost its own sailors (the Unreckoned Days, c. 652 BR). Kelarion's refused harbour and the Champion of Four Doors are native events with no analog to bend.

### Macedon and the Successors → Ortheia, Eryndor and the eastern kingdoms

Valeron II and Eryndor III supply the Macedonian political grammar omitted from the earlier city-state-to-Rome sequence. Valeron creates a professional royal instrument, pays it in the world's first coin and is killed by it at a pay table; Eryndor inherits it, leaves it at home under its own elected speaker, crosses the eastern sea with newer men and joins Arezan Tiravan against Arqesh. The war preserves independent Vashari, Arqeshite, Qahuri and Jirahar agency rather than turning the east into an empty field for one conqueror.

Eryndor's early death produces Vasharan, Ortheian, Delkosi, Qahuri and farther-eastern Successor states. Delkos, not Qahur, carries the strongest Ptolemaic court grammar. Ptolemy's seizure of Alexander's body becomes a corpse that governs: a voice in Eryndor's tomb disowns his heirs, which is why his half-brother, by then an acclaimed king, dies. Cleitus and Philotas are gone: the two deaths in Eryndor's hall are done by Arqeshite court officers and image-bearers at the images' own prompting. House Nerethan descends from a later Delkosi officer family and joins that inheritance to Esharati and junior Qahuri kin. Eryndor's later romances draw from the Alexander tradition across Greek, Syriac, Persian, Arabic, Hebrew and European literature. History no longer narrates them: [[The Five Limits of Eryndor]] keeps the tales, and the campaign articles keep only the people the incidents cost.

Causality Pass 10B1 bent the arc beat by beat, and its mechanisms stand where the second bend left them. Philip's assassination becomes Valeron killed by his own infantry at the Pelatha pay table when Marenthe withholds its silver; Siwa becomes a loan of grain from a dead Qemaret financier against the right to embalm the borrower; Darius murdered by Bessus and buried by his conqueror becomes Kassur's images changing voice and Kassur surrendering to Arezan and sealing the canal books to him; the Hyphasis becomes a Root-Mind wood closing the road behind an army whose king would not pay; death in Babylon becomes death aboard ship at Nemeru, waiting for a wind home; Roxane's murdered son becomes Mehrava, a living daughter and a contested Vasharan claim from whom later Tiravans descend. The *Alexander Romance* list (diving bell, griffin flight, Trees of Sun and Moon, the cook and the fish, the Gates against Gog and Magog) is replaced by native incidents in [[The Five Limits of Eryndor]]. No dated beat falls on an analog year, and the sequence keeps none of its source's spacing. [[Causality Pass - 10B1 Bending Eryndor and the Eastern Sea Wars]]

Causality Pass 11B1 then broke the skeleton that the first bend had left. Thebes and Antipater: the army that killed Valeron stays in Ortheia under its speaker Demeran, and sacks Marenthe on its own account in 699–698, three years after the crossing. Tyre and Siwa: Eryndor never takes Belyra, which falls to his fleet in 692 after Qahur; no crown gives him a title and no singer a divine parent, and the Aradus spring is withdrawn. Proskynesis: the king orders no bow and waives none; the images order it, and afterwards mock him in a strangled companion's voice until he leaves Arqesh. Arrhidaeus and Alexander IV: the half-brother is an acclaimed king killed as a usurper, and Mehrava is seven and living. Ptolemy: freed deportees sail the body, and Serevan arrives after it. Bactria: the cut-off garrisons are known from letters asking for pay. Offsets from the nearest Macedonian years now drift between 360 and 370 with the fixed anchors (706, 702, 690, 684) and are never constant. [[Causality Pass - 11B1 Second Bend - Eryndor, Vashar and Arqesh]]

**The TES move (Lorkhan/Shor dynamic):** the Star Pantheon is, behind the curtain, **Menhir worship misread**. The "High Augur of Astaroth" already implies it: Astaroth, Father Sun, Mother Moon and the rest are Second Circle Menhir whom the Atherians theologized into a civic pantheon — the same beings the Folk know by other names and opposite moral readings. Atherians worshipped Solzareth/Father Sun as ordering providence; the Ättar know the same being as the pitiless arithmetic of daylight. Same entity, two theologies, both partially right — that is your elf/human Lorkhan split, done with Menhir instead of a dead god. This is authorial canon.

Vashar's Achaemenid frame keeps Cyrus's restoration decree but loses the Shahnameh borrowings: Kaveh's apron, Zal and the Simurgh, and Rostam and Sohrab are cut. The Herod Agrippa frame is withdrawn: at the Kavresh acclamation of 444 nobody is struck, the praise cannot be spoken, and the royal cult's mirrors stay dark. The "prophet spared, workers sold" template is used once, for Haviel. The Persian Wars keep one beat of the Ionian springboard, a governor's meddling in a harbour city, and branch at the second. The mainland sends no fleet; nobody burns the provincial capital; Tasar falls in three years to its poorest quarter, and the war widens because Velanthe's wardens will not surrender fugitive hulls. Marathon has no counterpart: the fleet of 506 settles at anchor off Ilyessa, and the king's wrong lesson from it (hire the pilots) makes 493 his own fault. Salamis becomes the King's Bread at Ilyessa. Xerxes' retreat becomes a king dead of the flux, an army racing home by nations and an heir who buys his road with the Avesh command. The Delian League keeps its shape and its irony. The Achaemenid king-list is broken up: the relay road is a wartime work of the founder's sister, the second king is the one whose offering smoked, the third is drowned by a canal district, the Far-Road line comes in by the flame-keepers' choice, and a child-king closes the list. No Vasharan date falls on 499, 494, 490, 486, 480, 479, 478, 465, 431 or 404, and no interval repeats its source. [[Causality Pass - 11B1 Second Bend - Eryndor, Vashar and Arqesh]]

### Rome → The Atherian Empire

Unification ends the city-state age (c. 350 BR); the Age of Glory holds the Pax Romana's place and, after Causality Pass 11A2, none of its succession. **Aurelius Thalion** is the fixed founder: land concentration, military patronage, provincial wealth, and rival commanders broke the republic; Thalion outlasted the civil wars without commanding in them, took Caleran, governed openly from the store-house above the muster ground without pretending to restore the republic, and left supreme command to his own son. The roads, laws, and archives every successor now fights over were built by the republic and centralized by the imperial settlement.

The imperial-founding pass selected Thalion as Caesar's heir and founder. Teren Casvilar carries the Caesar-like conquest and assassination; Marovan Veyr and Lethren Ostar keep only the pressures of Pompey and Crassus: an older commander with eastern clients, and a creditor to whom both rivals owe money. After Causality Pass 10A, Darion Velsar, Samyra Nerethan, Seluc Varroth and Istran Melvo keep only the pressures of Antony, Cleopatra, Lepidus and Agrippa, and Thalion no longer reads as Augustus. After 11A2 their fates no longer follow those figures either: the priest-partner dies on the rival's field, and the admiral twice defies the founder. [[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review]] records the transformations and precise canonical sequence.

Causality Pass 02 bent the late republic first. The Gracchi become a land question the augurs will not sight and the Sun answers at Sarven; the Social War is fused with the revolt of the naval league's allies and fought over harbour chains; Sulla dies in an office he cannot lay down; the Delian treasury survives as Caleran's custody of the Compact's calendar, rolls and ship money. Pharsalus becomes an indecisive Velos and Pompey's murder a refused table and death by fever in sanctuary; Julia lives; Cato survives in exile and writes; Crassus's crosses become the sale of rebels north; the Ides lure becomes challengers entered in an arms register; Antony's Parthian campaign is aimed into the Avesh approaches.

The second bend (11A1) changed the order of fates. The Gracchan reformer is not killed: the veterans take the land on the strength of the noon shadows, lose it to a rotted harvest and sell it back for bread, and Damar farms bareheaded for thirty years. Sulla's march and proscriptions are cut; Harod never enters the city and kills two senators who came to relieve him. The Social War's citizenship grant is cut: the harbours strike after Pyrassa's night and the Chain Peace gives each its own chain. Crassus neither crushes Spartacus nor dies at Carrhae: Ostar forecloses on a slave rising in 410, sends his son to take possession of wells he bought on paper in 392, loses him alive to the well keepers, and is ruined in 379 when his own debtor's augurs enter him on the Red Field. Alesia's expelled do not die between the lines: Kellovar's people cross a river-court's ford on their household tokens, Drenoc pays the court with himself, and Casvilar, who had accepted the Red Field against him, cannot close it for thirteen years. That open Field replaces the Rubicon. Uxellodunum's spring is not cut; the severed hands remain and are aimed at ford guides. Pompey never meets Caesar in battle: the ports vote Veyr no fleet and Velos (377) is fought without him. The three assassins become the witness of the ford, a ruined ship-owner and a clerk who kept the book, and the lure is a young man entered as Drenoc's son. Dates no longer run at +326: the rising is 410, Rethmar 392, the Field 383, Velos 377 and Casvilar dies at sixty-one. The assassination stays at 370. [[Causality Pass - 11A1 Second Bend - Gateway Wars and Late Republic]]

The final era review bent two endings that had stayed on their analogues, and the second bends took both further. Vercingetorix's surrender, years in a cell and death after the triumph have no counterpart: Drenoc enters the ford below Kellovar to bargain for his people and is not seen again, no Atherian triumph displays him, and the river halls never forgive Morveth for paying at Kellovar's ford with Atherian silver. Sextus Pompey's capture and execution are cut (11A2): Ardel Veyr reaches the refuge fire of Pyrassa, whose own chain burned his ally, and lives out his life there under terms made after his defeat.

Causality Pass 10A bent the founding from 370 BR. Varroth's ruling becomes the engine: the fouled Field cannot be closed while any of the seven challengers lives, no army can disband, and the war is a hunt for seven men through the refuges of the inner sea. The Second Triumvirate becomes the Four at the Stores, a grain-store government that includes the senate's speaker; the proscriptions become the Writing-In, which breaks Astaroth's taboo and spreads the war, and Cicero's counterpart walks to the muster ground armed. Philippi is a great battle with no assassin in it, and Perusia a town eaten by a victorious army that cannot disband. Octavia's marriage and the Donations are cut. Naulochus keeps Marelith's burning but holds the city with Pyrassa's harbour chain to reach an assassin aboard, and Lepidus loses his magazines to his own garrisons. Actium becomes a blockade, a famine levy and the return of Gluttony's bitten wax. There are no suicides: Velsar is bound to his own Red Field and killed on it; Samyra is unmade by the Table Oath and saved by it, living seventeen years at Esharat; Hadram kills Casvilar's last murderer and lives in sanctuary on Ilamar; the younger children grow up with their mother. Augustus's restored republic, adoption and apotheosis are replaced by open rule from the store-house, a son by the queen's sister and a death at the towns' table. The painted war board of a commander who would not take the field becomes the portrait that answers in 247. No founding date coincides with its analogue's year or keeps its spacing. [[Causality Pass - 10A Bending the Founding]]

Causality Pass 11A2 bent the founding's skeleton, which 10A had left in order. Philippi now precedes the Triumvirate: Darsil is won in 369, settles nothing, and teaches the armies that a fouled Field cannot be closed by winning. Cicero's counterpart lives; the squad sent to kill him walks away, and he sells bread for sixteen years to the heir who agreed to his death. Lepidus's counterpart takes the register of the seven to the rival's field and dies there. Antony's Parthian war becomes a summer spent chasing one man up a road that outruns an army, with no battle and no losses. Sextus Pompey lives as a guest in the harbour whose chain burned his ally, and his treaty follows his defeat. Arsinoe is a hostage, not a victim, and dies in her husband's blockade; the hidden infant is replaced by a child the towns count every spring. The speech against the queen comes early and fails when the towns read Caleran the names of 609. Actium's flight is reversed into a grain convoy brought in at the price of a third of its cargo. No two beats keep their source's spacing. [[Causality Pass - 11A2 Second Bend - Founding and Age of Glory]]

The queen's dynasty follows the selected incoming-house route: Nerethan of Delkos joins Hadrima of Esharat's spared-town family. Sarqen is a surviving lesser port enlarged over generations, distinct from the destroyed sacrificial capital, and Samyra's legitimacy rests on the spared towns' Table Oath rather than on Cleopatra's grammar alone. The same oath unmakes her house in 350 and saves her life. Northern war uses defended water, camels and foot-mobile resistance; southern conquest encounters separate Folk coalitions. Neither landscape is a relocated Roman province.

The Age of Glory no longer follows the principate's successions (Causality Pass 03 and 11A2, November 2026). An emperor is made by the setting's own rites: painted on the board the armies swear to, seated at Sarqen's unranked table, stood under an arch. Tiberius's adoption becomes a daughter the standard-bearers will not carry and a husband painted in her place while she holds the table. The Year of the Four Emperors becomes two winters in which a portrait governs, its clerks read full granaries in its sight and nobody withholds the grain; the general who covers it refuses to be painted, and Galba's counterpart is executed for questioning a cloth. Vespasian's dynasty becomes a century of unpainted emperors agreed by divided commands, with the old house's hostage reigning between a warden-general and his grandson. The Fidenae collapse becomes an untested hall that kills the founder's heir and a Menhir test that every public work must pass, the Star's share. Severus and Caracalla's grant become a fever year in which the towns of Sarqen seat their governor and the eastern cities' price is citizenship, sworn before the emperor's image with a veiled form for Jirahar scribes. Joseph is reversed: the famine administrator opposes the crown estate and arms the villages with tallies. Elagabalus's murder becomes Velen I drowned under his own untested banks while his guard faces the portrait, and Severus's punishment of the guard becomes Doran's low door. No dated Age of Glory event falls on an analog year, and Olarion's death and Samor's accession no longer keep the spacing of AD 68–69. ### Iron-Age Europe and the Imperial Frontier → The Folk


The Folk draw structural inspiration from the clan, tribal, hillfort, federate, and successor societies encountered beyond and within the Roman frontier: Gaulish confederacies, Gothic war-leadership, Germanic halls, and the Brittonic, Welsh, Cornish, Scottish, and Irish persistence of local law and identity. This produces a political spectrum rather than one generic tribe. Some communities use stone, bronze, and scarce bloomery iron; others field mature iron-working confederacies, fortified towns, treaty systems, and armies capable of negotiating with empires.

The Enaidfae are old-rite Folk confederacies, not a fantasy ancestry or a direct ethnic analogue. Their local courts borrow the structural unease of traditions in which bright and terrible beings share etiquette without becoming morally safe. The powers are appeased because they are dangerous, not loved as benevolent parents. The Chapel-Folk carry the process by which converted frontier peoples remain culturally themselves rather than becoming foreign settlers overnight.

The annual Grobi winter war supplies a setting-native institution with no direct historical duplicate: frontier duty, honor migration along the Frost-Road, refuge architecture, hidden granaries, and seasonal war-kings. It keeps the Folk militarily consequential without unifying them into a nation.

**Recontextualization rule:** public canon uses Folk clans, courts, confederacies, and regional names. It does not use Gaulish, Gothic, Celtic, Germanic, Pictish, or other real-world labels as in-world taxonomy.

### Northern Nomad Empires and Plains Societies → The Tarkhan

The Tarkhan borrow political and military structures from Hunnic, Mongol, Turkic, and Oghuz histories: mobile courts, banner coalitions, kurultai legitimacy, remount wealth, extraction without occupation, contract cavalry, and the danger of hired frontier soldiers becoming rulers. The western dry belt adds ancient camel pastoralism, well law, caravan raiding, and slave markets. The northeastern grassland adds dispersed horse camps, migratory herd hunting, falcon reconnaissance, and the political importance of routes that settled states cannot hold continuously.

Indigenous North American plains histories, particularly Sioux and Apache traditions, inform selected Grass-Tarkhan relationships among mobility, horse adoption, hunting, kin networks, scouting, and resistance to surveyed frontiers. They are not treated as interchangeable with Eurasian nomad histories and do not supply direct names, sacred objects, dress, or flattened “tribal” imagery.

The Four Hordes transform succession-empire precedents into setting-native regional coalitions. White is the raid and reclamation frontier; Iron the employed-warrior-to-institutional-capture trajectory; Moon the ritual and constitutional center; Ash the road-peace and mercenary frontier. The Nine Wells provide an objective dispossession around which sincere present communities still have unequal claims and responsibilities.

The steppe's horse power is the fallen empire's own blessed remount stock (the First Catch), and its history follows that grace's clock: the Long Reach of 951 and the Horse War against the Covenant's hound-guarded wells while the lines were young; the Thinning; then Qarayel's rise and the Partition, bent through the horse-grace: a renewed blessing (1249), extraction timed by its thinning, and herds that came home in four bands, which the assembly of 1304 could only name. Qarayel's burial is no longer the Genghis legend: no burial riders, no killed witnesses, no trampled ground. His stallion carried him out of the camp unled and the herds came home without him; it remains a weird ([[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]]).

**Recontextualization rule:** public canon uses Well-Tarkhan, Grass-Tarkhan, Hordes, banner-lords, wind-speakers, sky-riding, word-price, and named routes. Hun, Mongol, Oghuz, Sioux, Apache, and related comparison labels remain creator-facing only.

### Rome after Qiryath → The Provincial Empire

The second century borrows from the Antonine age only its pressure, an empire whose emperors need both the grain ports and the frontier armies. Pass 11C removed the adoptive succession. Dorvan Dascen adopts no one; his daughter Ysane rules from the store-house with the eastern general Daven Talar and strikes his garrisons from the ledger in his lifetime; Garen Vardun is killed in the south; and the line ends in 189 when Marek Vessan eats at the spared towns' table among the runaways of the Sarqen rising and lives on as their guest. No emperor of the line is murdered by guards and no four-cornered civil war follows: the commands issue orders in a living emperor's name. Eutropius's third-century regional regimes supply only the pressure of unpaid frontier commands. There are no breakaway emperors: from 248 the High Augurate sells the opening of campaign seasons, and a voided season makes Cassian's army in 288. Dio's frontier negotiations and Herodian's account of troop transfers between Persia and the Rhine guide the distinct Vasharan and southern Folk wars. ### Christianity → The Redeemer's Faith

Already the vault's strongest mapping (ministry, execution, resurrection, persecution, catacomb church). The conversion-era mapping is embodied by:

- **Cassian of the Reach (sign 292 AR; reign to 316)** — Constantine, bent: a civil-war usurper whose night attack over the ice at Velos is stopped by the White Flame, who wins without a battle when the court's army crosses the bridge to him, and who legalises the faith without outlawing the stars. There is no motto and no battle of the bridge. His motivation remains ambiguous *in-world* while the authorial spine knows the vision was real — and knows the man remained ruthless anyway. His deathbed baptism, by the son he had forced into orders, creates the Cassian Wager. That gap between real grace and unchanged politics is the whole Conversion-era theme.
- **Astrion the Carried (at Caleran 348–352 AR; d. c. 372)** — Julian, inverted: a scholar-emperor raised in the new faith who restores the stars and stages a public test of legitimacy which the Red Star answers once. He does not die young. Crushed under an untried bridge he insists on standing beneath by the Red Star's builders' rite, he is reported dead, loses the capital to that report and lives twenty years more as a crippled rival emperor in the east, asking for a second answer that never comes. Sestaran humanists quietly adore him; the Church uses him as a cautionary tale.
- **Vestian the Pious (377–398 AR; Dissolution 383; compulsion from 385)** — Theodosius, bent: dissolves the augur colleges, wins one civil war against the Star-recognising eastern command (the Frigidus moved before compulsion and decided by a refused answer), and makes the faith compulsory through the Profession, whose lamps fail to kindle (the Second Silence; no analog). Thessalonica's decree follows a war instead of preceding it, and Callinicum becomes the Raveth hearing house. Inherited wound #9. The Vestian persecutions of the Jirahar occur inside this same reign.

**Triangle (Conversion era):** GOOD — sincere bishop-martyrs who remember the catacombs; BAD — court converts weaponizing heresy law for property and revenge; UGLY — the old augur aristocracy, selling their archives and legitimacy to the new order piece by piece.

Causality Pass 04 turns the era into a quarrel over who may authenticate a sign. Plutarch's cessation of oracles becomes the Three Silent Days (0 AR). Nero and Domitian become the Great Purge as a records war (c. 36–45). Decius's edict answers the Kept Lamps (245). Milan becomes the Velos Concord (c. 296), a trade of property for a miracle. Crispus and Fausta become the son and the High Augur (c. 309). Helena's Cross becomes the Nails' route through the governor's case chest to the Pillar. Ephesus becomes an effect of the Second Silence and Raveth. No beat from 245 to 398 keeps its analog's AD year; 292 stays. Causality Pass 10C bends the beats that stayed close: Decius's certificates become green grave-lamps on the colleges' salted oil and a reconciliation quarrel over who may be given fire; Julian's spear becomes the untried span at the Emperor's Cut; Adrianople keeps no admission, no pitched battle and no emperor dead in the field; Ephesus keeps no rival council, no deposed capital patriarch and no exile, and its condemned cities survive inside the empire as the last districts still paying the land tax. After two bends (10E, 11E) their skeletons are gone as well as their mechanisms: no emperor decrees the Breaking and no empress or council ends it; the defender of the vessels works inside the empire; the counterfeiting son reigns and is beaten and nobody is blinded; no sovereign hosts the beaten emperor, nobody deposes him, and he gives up his claim of his own will after ten years in a monastery cell; and the restorer does not appeal to the West, which comes to him with a crown and is refused. Caedros III reigns thirty-eight years against Basil II's forty-nine, Qarath falls fifty-five years after his death against Manzikert's forty-six, and no date sits on an analog year. [[Causality Pass - 11E Second Bend - Two Crowns and High Noon]]

### The 1054 Schism → The Great Split (1015 AR)

The filioque supplies the historical pressure, but the setting-native doctrinal weapon is the **Kindling Question**, not the older Procession formula. Veridun teaches that the Flame is uncreated and consents to dwell where sanctity prepares it; Eclessia teaches that the Flame as given to mortals is created grace entrusted to the apostolic body and governed through proof-protocol. Layered beneath it are primacy (Eclessian Lord Primarch versus Patriarch of Veridun), relic custody after the Reconquest, competing claims to Atherian inheritance, and the contested veneration of Caedros I.

Caedros is the empirical test case and the Talos-like dynamic within the no-apotheosis rule: Thalmyria treats him as a saint on the evidence of the Pillar and battle hymns; Eclessia rejects imperial self-authentication. **Authorial ruling:** the hymns kindle according to the righteousness and purpose of the singer, not Caedros's authorship and not institutional recognition. The result neither turns Caedros into a god nor lets either hierarchy own the Flame; it explains why both churches obtain genuine, inconsistent results. Older “Procession of the Flame” language may survive as a secondary scholastic analogy, but it must not be presented as the primary engine of the 1015 Split.

The rupture is a refusal to lift a hood. Aurelium offers Veridun the examination first; the Patriarch nails the lamp shut and the legate never enters the cathedral. Charlemagne's coronation follows the schism instead of preceding it by two centuries: Adalmar is crowned in 1017, standing in a drained ford, on a true kindling credited to the wrong person, and is the only emperor the east ever has, because the Sanctum's own rule of proof bars every heir. Verdun is a lot that comes up blank and a truce line that hardens into borders. The Saxon Wars are a register war the king loses to a lake, in which the massacre is the murder of an oral land-register and the baptised chief wins the peace in court. Vladimir's embassy is a dead hearth-lamp, and there is no dynastic union: Virelia is made by a moot of sabres. [[Causality Pass - 11E Second Bend - Two Crowns and High Noon]]

### Renaissance Italy → The Sestaran Concord

The governing dynamic is Renaissance Italy crossed with pirate republics, free imperial cities, and modern entrepôt microstates: intense local patriotism, narrow citizenship, private war, common fear of conquest, and specialization strong enough to make larger empires dependent.

Direct mappings: Fiorenzia/Medori draws from Florence/Medici; Sermora from Venice, Genoa and Caribbean privateer polities; Aversia from Milan under military dynasties; Vorthara from Kaer Maga's anarchic refuge translated through World Egg magic; Castara from Atlantic boom ports and colonial companies; Orsena from Singapore, Ragusa and neutral free ports; Vardessa from Frankfurt, Nuremberg, Swiss free cities and San Marino. The Seven-Seal Muster carries Swiss and defensive-league logic without creating a federal state.

**Taddeo Vetti** no longer carries the Machiavelli function: his *Mirror* is a factor's manual for lending to sovereigns, he leaked the registers that began the crisis and was drowned for it, and the dedication is a printer's. **Frate Severin** keeps the friar's place and loses the arc: the senate lights the fire, he is the bank's ally through a four-year Vigil and its critic at the end, and he dies in a wax face. Alessior II is an ascetic whose sin is the Sufficient Seal and its alms. The public notes must use native names and institutions rather than these analog labels.

The Roll of Faces lost nearly half its heads to the Sweat; the Medori fortune comes from the estates and Sendings of the plague dead and the registers taken in 1296. After the 1345 pamphlet the senate burns the city's wax faces (1346), a Medori son-in-law opens the vault, and the citizens vote the family a hereditary chair (1349). There is no expulsion, flight, restoration, ordeal or execution of the friar; the generations are not grandfather, grandson and fleeing son. [[Causality Pass - 11F Second Bend - The Road to 1360]]

**Triangle (Sestara):** GOOD — civic humanists who believe archives and art redeem the fractured inheritance; BAD — the Pale Hand / Fleshweaver end of Vorthara, where knowledge has no brakes; UGLY — the Medori, financing every side of every war including both churches' reunification schemes.

### The Reformation → Eclessia's Present Crisis (1297 AR – now)

The trigger is **routing assurances** — indulgences recontextualized into a world where the Vestibule is real, which sharpens the theology enormously: the reformer's charge is not merely corruption but *fraud against souls*, and the old Church's defense (that sincere intercession genuinely helps) is also not empty, because prayer and relics demonstrably work. Both sides have a real case; that is what makes it a war and not a morality play.

The mapped names are fixed and the sequence is no longer the analogue's (Causality Pass 11F). **Ansgar of Brenholt** is a Sending House chaplain who dies before the movement begins; its first act is a widows' funeral read from his margins by the tally-keeper **Irmel**. **Doctor Lucian Ferro** is the Sanctum's commissioned prosecutor and gives the funerals their doctrine sixteen years late. **Celestin IX** and the **Council of Veridance** (1319–1324) come before any prince reforms, and the levy for the Great Arrears is what turns **Duke Rainald of Ironvale**. The **Order of the Lantern** is older than the Reformation and its priest dies before its charter. [[The Korrheim Accord]] is drawn by the Night Count of churchyards. Engines that carry the chain: Ansgar's Count; the unbought funerals; the voided book-test and the second fee; the Weiss oaths and the war from the mines; Sarnwell sworn by Ashenmen; the Great Arrears; the Falkendorf yard; the Iron Fast; the Unsworn as river-buriers; Vessing's three witnesses, each against their own church. The Western Schism becomes the Twin Cardinals (1240–1242): the elected Primarch dies unsung on his election night, his twin reigns in his ring, and the quarrel is over whether a false Primarch's Ferry Rule sent the dead; there are no rival cities, no antipopes and no deposing council. Augsburg's *cuius regio, eius religio* survives only as the Sanctum canonists' half-quotation of the first line; the oath's second line, *and every lamp where its dead lie*, gives the land's dead the choice and the prince none.

**Regional inspiration register:** Aurelia carries the Papal States and Latinate church-principality function; Veridancia combines France, Burgundy, universities, and Catholic reform; Caldria combines Iberian crusading frontiers with Habsburg military administration; Ironvale carries Reformation Germany and the early industrial mining belt; Korrheim combines Swiss cantons with the confessional machinery of the Holy Roman Empire; Brauvalis draws on Bavaria, Bohemia, Austria, monasteries, and guild towns; Virelia carries the Polish-Lithuanian elective and magnate system over a Slavic eastern substrate; and the Sylvan Marches carry the Baltic and Livonian woodland-frontier function. These are dynamic registers, not one-to-one identities.

The Grey Sweat carries the Black Death's place and little of its furniture: a fifth dead, not a third; priests dying fastest; ash-bearers, not flagellants; and one rite-integrity rule producing both the western clean-road charters and Virelia's bond of the clean village, in place of a labour market (see [[The Grey Sweat - Authorial Truth]]).

**Triangle (Eclessia, present):** GOOD — reformers and old-Church saints who each sincerely serve the Redeemer; BAD — princes on both confessions using doctrine as a land-grab; UGLY — the reunification party, for whom the Thalmyric schism *and* the Reformation are both merely obstacles to a restored imperial church, by force if needed.

The Church's reunification mandate is now recursive comedy and tragedy at once: it must reunify with schismatic Thalmyria, reabsorb its own reformed duchies, and police heresy on the frontier — simultaneously, with less money than the Medori and less fire than Veridun. Grenzburg's Bishop/Inquisitor/Reliquarian triangle plugs into this directly. The duchy is officially Sanctum-loyal; its villages combine old-Church observance, Folk practice, and a growing Freeflame presence among migrants.

### The Present → the 1550s–1580s Cold War

The board resembles the 1550s–1580s on the surface; under the Forward Rule each trajectory has a native disruptor listed in [[The Age of Held Proofs - Authorial Control]]. The board is Habsburg–Ottoman–Valois plus confessional stalemate: Thalmyria and the Ahl-e-Ahd in armed frontier peace punctuated by proxy raids (the Hungary/Mediterranean frontier); Eclessia internally tripartite and diplomatically schizophrenic; Sestara financing and informing on everyone (Venice's Ottoman trade despite holy leagues); the New World race through Castara beginning to reroute the world's wealth away from the old frontier entirely — a slow pressure on every power that stays landlocked in old grudges, and excellent long-game material. Under the Forward Rule its outcome is not the Atlantic precedent: whatever the Boiling Sea routes do to the old frontier must turn on a world-native engine such as the Held Breath, patron arbitrage or a Held Proof.

## Standing Recontextualization Rules

1. **Steal the dynamic, not the costume.** Every borrowing must be re-derived from the World Egg's own metaphysics (indulgences → routing assurances; Greek fire → controlled Shell-fire with disputed sacred counterclaims; devshirme → contract-bound levies). If the analog works only because the reader knows the real history, it isn't done yet.
2. **The flip protects you.** Mirrored compass, inverted 1453, Orthodox-west/Catholic-east: keep systematically breaking one-to-one traceability while preserving motivations.
3. **Every era gets a triangle.** Good/Bad/Ugly per the Grenzburg model — and the Ugly party should always be the one whose cooperation is necessary and whose price compounds.
4. **Both sides get a real case.** Reformation, schism, Unsealing, reconquest: each faction's position must be the one its best people would sincerely hold. Villainy lives in method and appetite, not in having the wrong theology.
5. **Filtered analogs everywhere** (per the naming decision): one step sideways on all names; no von Bismarcks, no verbatim liturgical Arabic or Zulu after the rename pass.
6. **Quarry, not script.** Research supplies pressures, institutions and texture, not sequence. A borrowed incident must do work beyond its source having it; incidents that survive only because the analog contains them are cut. Cite sources for texture, never one per beat. If a reader who knows the analog can predict the next beat, bend it with a world-native cause (the Consequence Rule in [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]]) or cut it.
7. **Clocks never align.** AR and BR dates for analog events must not coincide with their real AD or BC years, and an analog sequence must not keep its source's spacing between events. A constant offset is the same fault: if every date in a sequence sits the same number of years from its analogue (as the late republic once ran at +326 and the Reformation at −212, both since bent), a reader can still set the clock. Check intervals, not only years.
8. **Skeletons, not only mechanisms.** A sequence is still borrowed if the same people meet the same fates in the same order, however native the causes. Bend the order of fates: let someone live who died, win who lost, or never arrive at all.
