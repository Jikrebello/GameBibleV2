# Signs and Proof — Authorial Truth

The author-only rulings behind the history of 0–411 AR, approved in [[Causality Pass - 04 Who May Authenticate a Sign, 0 to 411 AR|Causality Pass 04]] on 14 November 2026 under the [[Causality Pass - Direction|Direction]]. The same day [[Causality Pass - 10C Bending Kellovar, Halmenth and the Last Fortress|Pass 10C]] bent five sequences that still followed their analogues: §5 (Tervan's green lamps), §8 (Astrion at the Emperor's Cut), §9 (Kellovar), §12 (Halmenth) and §15 (the Dawn Tower, new). [[Causality Pass - 11C Second Bend - Tower Revolt to Vestian|Pass 11C]] then broke the skeletons that survived: it revised §5, §7, §8, §9 and §15 and added §16 (the Nine Mornings and the empty litters), §17 (the store-house and the table at Sarqen) and §18 (the sold seasons and the condemned ground). Reader articles show these truths only through what in-world witnesses saw and claimed. ## 1. The Commission Rule and the Crowded Field

This is the answer to the question the causality review put to the whole span: why did a faith whose signs really work take 292 years to win?

1. **Grace acts by commission, not on demand.** The White Flame answers the righteousness and purpose of a person and the commission under which it acts. It cannot be summoned to a hearing or produced to save a defendant; [[The Fireless Trial]] is the type case. A manifestation proves its commission and nothing beyond it. Because the Flame demands sanctity and coherence (Commandment 7), it does not ratify a lie, including a lie spoken under seal, in a register or as an act of state.
2. **The field is crowded.** A light on the dead, a fire that will not take or a lamp that burns without oil has at least four possible authors:
   - the Flame;
   - Shell-fire from kiln wards, signal scripts or forge furnaces;
   - a Menhir's bounded omen, which genuinely answers within its law and price;
   - an Idol's Mask, whose counterfeit kindling feeds on the devotion it draws (Virella's "two veils"; Bruckmoor proves it later).

   A wonder shows that something acted. It does not show what acted. [[Mesakha Traditions]] already requires signs that "cannot be staged by warlock script, contract, Shade, relic fraud, or Idol pressure".
3. **Law names signs.** From the Common Sky onwards the augur colleges kept the civic calendar and the register of answers (origin and legal form: [[Causality Pass - 03 Age of Glory and the Bridge to 0 AR|Pass 03]]). In imperial law a sign existed only once a chartered college had entered it with its terms, its witnesses and the name of the answering Star. A registered answer could open a campaign, void an election, or consecrate or condemn ground. An unregistered wonder was fraud or an unlicensed cult feeding something.
4. **The magistrate's profile was earned.** A cult that met at executed men's graves, kept lamps for them, reported lights on the dead and refused the colleges' examination matched, point by point, the profile the empire had learned from real Mask cults: the ruler-cult appetite, Qarzeth's Gluttony Mask, the grief cults of garrisons, and above all the Kept Lamps of 242–244. Many persecuting magistrates were sincere sacred hygienists who had seen a Mask feed. Their sincerity excuses none of the tortures, burnings and confiscations they ordered.
5. **Why witness carried the faith.** Proof could not compel belief. The believer cannot produce a sign on demand; the sceptic can always assign it to a rival author; the law gave the naming to an interested body; and belief is assent, which neither force nor calculation supplies. The faith spread by people, and its signs travelled as rumour among the already persuaded. A self-authenticating sign (the Aurin at Velos, which anyone can watch fail to freeze) broke the colleges' monopoly when three centuries of testimony had not.
6. **The Stars keep the same rule.** The Menhir answer inside their own grammar. Astaroth answers a field, a load and a price. His structural answers to builders and siege engineers are a standing practice from Darneth onward. He answered **Astrion's public test of legitimacy** once, in 350, because it put a real load at real hazard. He gave nothing to "victory without resistance": no terms for Astrion's proposed second test, none in any of the twenty springs Astrion's augurs asked again at Arcelon, nothing to Ilios Marrant's last petition of 383 (no load, no price, only a request to be shown worthy), and nothing to the eastern command's petition at the crossing in 384. The Church learned this rule as a defendant and forgot it as a ruler.

**Corollary for readers.** Every reader article in this span may let witnesses disagree about what a wonder was. None may settle it in narration.

## 2. The Three Silent Days (0 AR)

No augural register in the empire records an answer between the hour of the Binding and the third morning. Severan's register is the best known only because he read at Qiryath; every college's book has the same blank. Augury resumed on the fourth day unchanged.

- **Truth.** The fled powers held still while the Judge was among the dead. Menhir fusion was a flight from judgement, and every Menhir sovereignty, the Stars included, went still for those three days. This file makes no ruling on whether Idol Masks also fell silent; no register could have recorded it.
- **In-world.** The registers prove only that no answer came. The colleges sealed the days. Tyros carried knowledge of them into the open, the Great Purge was fought partly to recover the copies, and the bishops have held the originals since 383 and have never published them.
- **Interface.** Qiryath's Silence of Acceptance (permanent) and the Three Silent Days (three days) fall together at 0 AR. Neither explains the other, and no reader article may present either as the cause of the other. The Silence of Acceptance stays as the Jirahar and Church dispute it.
- **Severan** never read again. His college recorded that he "laid down the staff". Grief, fear or faith: unresolved.

This is a torque, not a weird. It is explained here and may be cited in the Torque Register.

## 3. The Great Purge (c. 36–45 AR)

A records war. Tyros's Dialogues argued that the colleges had recorded something they refused to discuss; junior augurs and register-keepers in several provincial colleges followed him, carrying copies of the sealed days. A defected college could enter the Flame's signs in public law. The High Augurate at Caleran obtained a commission of inquiry (c. 36), staffed it with augurs, paid informers from confiscations and charged defendants with forging or stealing sacred records. Contributing causes: the Odrun succession troubles that made the colleges' ceremonies indispensable, and Menos's risings in the Ironvale forges that supplied the army.

- The commission's edict made it a crime to publish a wonder the colleges had not entered. For about 250 years a congregation that wrote down a miracle wrote its own indictment. The Underground therefore recorded signs as practical judgement or hymn ("the lamp took well"; the Ashway coal test). **Ruling on the Ashway coal test:** some extinguished coals were wind and rain, some were not, and the record was written so that no one could tell the difference. No later reader article may decide any single crossing.
- The Lantern Cradle was the observatory of one of the defected colleges. Its coloured lanterns continue the old register marks. The hymns' claim that some burned without wick or oil is left open.
- **Virella** (d. 47) was killed by a commissioner whose warrant had lapsed and who kept poisoning her anyway. Tyros remains the last Companion to die in the Purge.
- The emperor of the Purge stays unnamed in reader prose.

## 4. The Four Nails (provenance only)

No document on the route states a count, and none may be written to.

1. **0 AR.** Sentences for rival kingship were reported to the emperor with the instruments as proof. The governor's case chest took the soldiers' nails, the notice of the charge and the receipt for the body. The timber stayed at Qiryath, unrecorded.
2. **0–c. 41.** The chest lay in the judgement archive of the Celestial Forum.
3. **c. 41.** The Purge commission produced the instruments at Jareth's trial to prove that the cult's founder was a condemned rival king, then had them leaded into the Pillar of Concordance as a monument to a sentence carried out. Jareth burned before it. The notice of the charge stayed in the chest and is lost.
4. **41–219.** The three early inventories stand exactly as the Weird register has them.
5. **c. 300–306.** Cassian's grant of the forum precinct carried the Pillar and the Nails to the Church.

**The Third Nail stays ⊘ SEALED.** Nothing above explains the count. The Weird register is not amended; the author declined even the clause "fused into the Pillar by the Purge commission". The Lives of bearers who swallowed shavings from the nails are demonstrably late.

## 5. The Kept Lamps (c. 242–244 AR)

An **Envy Mask** spread through unpaid eastern garrisons in the arrears after the second Vashar war, promising that comrades who died unpaid were "kept" by lamps over their graves. The lamps leaned towards mourners and burned without oil. The Mask fed on the grievance, and the mutinies drew heat from it. Augurs broke its central image c. 243–244.

- Tervan's Edict of Relit Lamps (245) answered a real Mask. It required every household to put out the lamps it kept for its dead in the Star's basin at a market altar and relight them from the college fire, then fed them college oil salted with verdigris scraped from temple bronzes, so that they burned green. Night watches walked the graves for yellow flames. The green is copper and nothing else: no Star put virtue into it, the colleges knew it, and it proved only that a household had bought the oil. Tinkers' *pot-green* matched it exactly, and a Mask can counterfeit any colour. The Kept Lamps' own lamps burned without oil, which is the one thing the edict's oil jars could genuinely expose. The edict caught the Redeemer burial fraternities first and Jirahar graves second.
- The edict left no certificates. Its records were the college stores' oil books, which listed who bought and so who did not. The reconciliation quarrel after 248 was a quarrel over whom to give fire to (those who went to the altar, those who bought pot-green, those who walked with the watch); *refused fire* is its lasting phrase. No ruling is made on any household's sincerity.
- **The scraped bronzes (247–248).** The verdigris came off the temples' own images, and by 247 half of them were pitted. No Star was diminished by it; a Menhir is not its statue. The High Augur's refusal to open the year of 248 before a scraped image was college politics, and it worked, and the colleges learned from it that an opening could be withheld for a price (§18). Tervan died of a fever on the eastern road.
- After 248 most congregations relit from a flame that had never burned green. Amaryah's keepers had hidden her lamp in a covered firepot in a neighbour's bread oven; theirs became the flame everyone sought, and the daughter-flame custom spread from that winter.
- **The Temple That Would Not Burn** (Cyrian, c. 246): the commissioners' three burnings were sincere anti-Idol purgations. What preserved the stone is not ruled here and stays open to its three interpretive voices; the authorial position is only that the Kept Lamps Mask had no part in the Cyrian shrine.

## 6. Ironvale and the Forty Flames (290–291 AR)

The flame on the Forty was a genuine, brief manifestation of the White Flame (already fixed in the Chronology). The 291 deposition is honest: the guard and Brask, the Ashforges' enslaved script-keeper, freed by Cassian's officers so his oath would stand, swore the furnace scripts were cold. The prison roll's silence about the flame is the crown's own lie of omission. Both crown records survive.

The Council of Second Flame (c. 383–384) was the first bishops' sign hearing after the Dissolution. It confirmed the Forty on the deposition. The deposition reached Aurelium only after 383.

## 7. Velos and the Crown's River (292–377 AR)

The Velos sign is the Flame's and is permanent. Its ownership is human:

- **292, the night on the ice.** No battle was fought at Velos. Cassian's vanguard, under his son Torian, was crossing the frozen Aurin at night to take the court's camp asleep when the Flame stood on the river and the ice went out under the bridge. The commission of that manifestation was the reach and the men on the ice; it stopped a slaughter and it has kept the water open. It pronounced nothing on Cassian's cause, which won because the court's soldiers saw the same thing and crossed the bridge to him. The Flame spoke no words. Every motto later given to it is a panegyrist's.
- **292.** The augurs of Caleran entered the reach as a sign answered to the emperor. This is why Cassian kept the colleges.
- **c. 302–305, the Velos Concord.** Chapel at the bridge, a Warden of the Reach paid by the crown to keep the winter ice-line register, and restitution decreed empire-wide in exchange. The sees had refused for ten winters to commemorate the augurs' entry, and the Concord came with the grant of the Celestial Forum, which is why Caleran signed first. Three sees signed c. 302. Halmenth's stewards held out three winters with the hall locked. The hearing at Halmenth in 305 was about the Concord itself; no elder had surrendered anything. Halmenth signed "as a record of ice".
- **c. 304, Cassian's household.** Two years after the Concord put a crown chapel and a crown book at Velos, the heir, Torian, asked the High Augur of Astaroth for an answer on the succession; an entry naming him would have been the strongest title in the empire. The answer was given and sealed. Cassian executed the High Augur. He did not kill his son: he had him ordained by the bishop of Caleran and confined to the bridge chapel as its chaplain. There was no rumour about the empress and she has no part in it. **What the answer said is not ruled here.** It may stay unknown for ever. The sealed answer and the hearing at Halmenth a year later have nothing to do with each other.
- **307.** Cassian, fifteen years after the ice and with restitution half done, was carried to Velos and baptised in the unfrozen water by the crown-paid chaplain, who was Torian. Torian consented to baptise him; what passed between them is not ruled. Whether the baptism availed is the Cassian Wager, and it stays unresolved: the Weighing is real and nobody knows. The validity of an ordination imposed by an emperor is a second open question in-world. The authorial position is only that an order compelled is as incoherent as a profession compelled (§11), and that a baptism does not depend on the worthiness or the willingness of the hand; neither sentence settles the Wager. Torian died at Velos c. 341.
- **Astrion** had the augurs re-enter the reach as Hecatara's answer (of crossings). Hecatara gave no such answer; the entry was a political re-registration, and the bishops were right to call it a forgery of authorship. This made the 350 test necessary to him.
- **377.** Vestian gave the register and chapel to the bishops. River and bridge stayed crown property. "It continues."

## 8. The Two Standards and the East (350–384 AR)

- Astaroth's 350 answer was real and bounded: a structural truth about a loaded standard.
- **340–350, the Entering and the survey.** Astrion's revival of the Purge edict was lawful; legalisation had never repealed it. It ran from his accession, and the colleges struck a decade of the sees' wonders, which is why Caleran would not have him back in 352. The survey at Qiryath stopped because the Jirahar crews refused to work under the Red Star's wardens, whose rite had dropped those galleries in 69–72. No wall fell and no power intervened. The wardens' frame was never loaded there, so nothing was answered. The treasury did hope to reach the gate chamber (§16).
- **351, the Emperor's Cut.** The timber span over the dry wash had not been loaded before the Red Star. Under the 326 BR rule a work asked to stand without trial is forced to prove itself, and it proved itself under its first heavy load, the wagons of wounded. Astrion stood under it in the opener's place, which kept him clear of the substitution taboo and put him exactly where the failure fell. This is the builders' standing law, morally blind, and not a second answer to his reign: the Torque Register's "once" stands. He did not ask the Star for anything at the wash; the augurs who said he made his body a test of victory were reading their own refusal into it. **He survived**, with his hips and legs crushed, and never walked again. The Star neither spared him nor judged him: the beams fell as they fell. The field record's question about the register is authentic. He said nothing about a carpenter; that line is withdrawn from canon.
- **Savel Orren** was never emperor. He refused the acclamation beside the wreck and died in Astrion's household at Arcelon.
- **Tellan** (Adran's younger son, Astrion's brother) brought water trains bought from the Well-Tarkhan carriers who had kept their contracts, along the eastern depot road, under the bronze standard of the test. The column survived. Tellan commanded the east in the field for his brother from 351 and in his own right from c. 372. He never claimed the throne, because the Accord forbade Astrion an heir and because no second answer came and his augurs said so honestly.
- **352, Lucar.** Caleran proclaimed Lucar Aster, Sevrin's son-in-law, on a false report of Astrion's death, and kept him when the report was corrected. There was no pair of brother emperors: Corlan Aster was Lucar's marshal and never held the title. He died c. 354. With his marshal dead Lucar could not hold both the Aurin approaches and the south, and the stand-off of 355–357 followed.
- **c. 357, the Accord of the Two Standards.** Astrion kept the name of emperor east of the depot line for life and could name no heir to it. The eastern command kept its revenue share on Sevrin's 307 terms, its augurs and the Answered Standard. Astrion died at Arcelon c. 372 of the long consequences of his injuries. He asked the Red Star for terms every spring and received none, and he never let his augurs write otherwise.
- **384.** At the decisive crossing the eastern augurs asked the Red Star for terms and got none: there was no field the Star recognised, only a request for victory. Vestian won because the Stars would not answer his enemy, and then the Flame would not answer his law.
- Tellan, who had outlived his brother by some eighteen years, ended as a pensioned captive on the Aurin and died in his bed before Vestian. The Answered Standard's fate is disputed in-world (melted for a bell; buried under a depot road). **Authorial truth:** it was melted. The bell rings in the Aurin valley still and is not marked.

## 9. The Kellovar Ford (377 AR)

The ford below Kellovar belonged to a Menhir river-court of Hecatara's grammar (tolls, thresholds, the consequence of passage), like most fords of the southern rivers. Its toll was paid in the small iron, bone and hair tokens Folk households carried: the household paid the court at the spring offering, and the token marked its bearer as of a paid household. A bearer with a token in hand could lead others across on a rope or a belt. From Cassian's market treaties onwards the southern army crossed behind allied Folk guides for exactly this reason.

- **The older toll.** Hecatara's grammar recognises the delivery of a traveller and a surrendered destination as prices. A party without a token could cross if one of its number was delivered: that person came out on the far bank and could not recross, because the destination surrendered was their own way home. A token put into a delivered person's hand ended it, since the court counts a token-bearer as paid. Hair and bone tokens are the same toll commuted: a household gives a part of itself each spring so that no member has to be given whole. The courts did not ask for children. The officers chose whom to deliver.
- **c. 368–377.** Lucar's chaplains confiscated the guides' and traders' tokens as idols; his officers sold the silver ones. Lucar had held the southern command himself since Corlan's death c. 354. The confiscation came fourteen years later and was his own chaplains' doing, with no cause in that death. In the winters of 369–371 the forts could not reach the far banks except by bridge, and the officers crossed by delivering children taken from the allied households of their own districts. Nobody was admitted at the forts and no grain was traded for anyone. Stewards sold some of the children handed to them for delivery; that, the selection of children and the taking of hostages were human choices. The war of 372–377 was the households' slow escape across the fords on hidden tokens, towards their delivered children, and the forts' hunt for token-carriers.
- **377.** The households crossed at night on their tokens. The field army followed with no token and no one delivered, behind the chaplains' blessing, which commissioned nothing, and the court took the crossing. Lucar carried a silver token (the Otter house's, taken from a guide in 369) on his horse's breast-strap as a trophy. **He did not know it would carry him.** The court does not ask whose token it is or how it was got; it carried him as of the Otter house. The three days at Cathra's fire, the salt, his true name and his return are true in substance; the offer of kinship and servitude is Folk song and is not ruled. His rearguard killed him on the near bank because they believed he had paid for himself. Who struck first is not ruled.
- Vestian was recognised at Caleran while Lucar was alive across the river. He did not arrange the killing and was glad of it.

The court is morally blind. It exacted without judging, and it carried the thief dry. The drowning proves only the ford's law, not the justice of the Folk cause (which was just on other grounds) or Heaven's judgement on Lucar (which this file does not rule). Vestian read it as a judgement on a divided empire; that was his reading.

## 10. The Dissolution and the War of the Two Standards (383–384 AR)

**Ilios Marrant**, the last High Augur of Caleran, asked leave for one final petition to the Red Star on behalf of the college that had made it answer in 350. Vestian granted it before a crowd in the Celestial Forum, before the Pillar that holds the Nails. The augurs set no load and named no price. Nothing answered, because there was nothing in the request the Star could answer. A converted augur later recorded Marrant's own reading of this; it is correct.

By statute the authentication of signs passed to the bishops of recognised communion, with the Deep Register (including the Three Silent Days and, after 384, the 350 record). Caleran 383, the provinces by 384, the east at the war's end.

## 11. The Second Silence (385–c. 389 AR)

**The pattern is real.** From the Profession of 385, reception lamps at compelled state professions burned yellow by the thousand, while willing parish baptisms went on kindling at the old uneven rate. The Flame did not leave the Church. It did not answer the compulsion, because a profession made to keep an office or a house is incoherent: the mouth says what the person does not hold.

- A white flame at a state profession meant only that the person had freely sought baptism (most such cases had sought it before the decree). A yellow flame supplied no finding about any individual's faith.
- The court's explanation (hidden rites, "Jirahar blindness") was false. The ban on household rites (c. 387) and the faster tower orders were built on it.
- **c. 389.** The lamp was taken out of the oath. This is the first time an institution hid a falling yield instead of repenting of it; the Guttering ([[Causality Pass - 05 The Guttering, 400 to 905 AR|Pass 05]]) is the long cost.
- Aurelium's sealed register survives incomplete in the Sanctum in 1360. Which district books are missing is not ruled.
- "Second Silence" was coined by the gateway-port congregations of Jirahar descent. The churches avoid it.

**Ruling on the Church.** As a body it did not lie; it hid. Caleran's ruling that a properly witnessed profession had civil force without a sign is the first statement of protocol theology.

## 12. The Man-Bearer Party and Raveth (c. 385–411 AR)

**Recommended position, adopted.** The Council of Halmenth's teaching on the Redeemer stands: the Man-Bearer Christology is wrong. The vessel critique of the empire ("the lamp is made by hands; the Fire comes after") is true. The condemnation mixed doctrine with prejudice about descent. Neither side is cleared.

- The Raveth hearing house kept the depositions as a record only; Jirahar courts claimed no jurisdiction over baptised descendants in the maternal line.
- The crowd that burned it (c. 389) had its own quarrel over rents and debts. The clergy who rallied it wanted the depositions gone. Vestian dropped the rebuilding because rebuilding would restore the record.
- **Qedrel** (b. c. 362, Qabir): reader by c. 383; kept Qabir's count of the unkindled in 385–388; carried copies to Raveth and was away when it burned; his mother's register was seized in the 390s; ordained c. 394; bishop of Qabir c. 402 over Caleran's objection on grounds of descent; condemned 411; escaped Halmenth by Sea Fleet ship; died at Qabir c. 440.

**The Council of Halmenth (411).** Its shape:

- **The money.** The regency's relic exemptions (from 398) needed the claim that a holy object holds the Fire. Qedrel refused to certify relic estates in his province ("another lamp made by hands"). The regents and relic-estate bishops wanted the teaching condemned because it threatened the sale of exemptions. The Christological question was real and the council answered it rightly; the haste and the venue were bought.
- **The charge.** Qedrel read Qabir's count aloud and asked for Aurelium's sealed register to be opened. Caleran's readers charged him under the Purge commission's edict against publishing an unentered wonder. Legalisation in 292 stopped its use against the congregations' books but never repealed it, and it passed to the bishops with the Deep Register in 383. This is the first use of that edict by bishops. Which district books are missing from Aurelium's register is still not ruled.
- **The descent canon** (no ordination for a man whose mother's register is kept beside his baptism roll) is prejudice, as above.
- **The lamps.** Communion was sealed by lighting each assenting see's lamp from a daughter of the Eternal Ember carried from Caleran by warship. Qabir's flame truly descends from Amaryah's lamp by the bread-oven route of 248–249, earlier than Caleran's Ember. Lineage of flame is custody, not grace: neither lamp carries the Fire, and neither church's claim to the older daughter decides anything. The Velians know the second half of that and forget the first.
- **The escape.** Bishop Ostrel of Halmenth voted with the majority and told his ferry crews nothing; they did it on their own account. The Sea Fleet captains refused the regents' search of their docks.
- **The outcome.** The regency struck the gateway sees from the roll of recognised communion instead of besieging Qabir. Unrecognised clergy could not certify relics or witness charters, so the condemned cities' land stayed taxable while the rest of the empire's slid into relic estates; the regents protected them as revenue. This is why the Velian cities met the Covenant with intact charters and full granaries (Pass 05's mutual refusal to witness charters begins here), and why the belt's tribute was worth the Banu Mizan's taking. There was no exile beyond the frontier.

## 13. The Empty Charter's Clause (c. 396 AR)

Vestian struck out "in perpetuity" and wrote *before the Mesakha* in his own hand, using the Jirahar word. Motives: the Second Silence had persuaded him the Return was near; a conditional prohibition binds until its condition is met; and the word was a sneer and a creed at once. The charter never says who recognises the Mesakha. A recognised sign would lift the ban on rebuilding, and two authorities claim the right to recognise one: the Church (heir to the augurs' power since 383) and the Keepers (holders of the Mesakha tests). The warmth beneath the Silent Gate stair in 1360 is therefore a legal emergency as well as a theological one. **What the warmth is remains unresolved.**

## 15. The Dawn Tower (Migdal Shahar, 62–64 AR)

Migdal Shahar was a deep tower on the Dawn Road with a Way-Gate paired to an older tower in Vasharan country where the houses that refused the Qiryath charter had settled (c. 80 BR). Pass 11C moved its fall from 68 to 64: it was the first great tower the legions took and it fell eight years before Qiryath, so it is no longer the revolt's last stronghold. Placed here because this file holds the era's authorial truth; the section number keeps older links stable.

- **The siege went down.** The legions took the gatehouse in 62 and spent two years sinking towards the lower city. The engineers' loaded siege frame before a priest of the Red Star was Astaroth's standing practice for siege engineers (Darneth precedent): a field, a load, a price (the frame). The answer was the failure line of the outer gallery roofs, and the engineers' stone dropped them. Astaroth is morally blind; the engineers and the legate chose to drop roofs on refuge halls.
- **The bench.** The receiving bench's terms (households, no one who had borne arms) were a sincere judgement of danger: Atherian vengeance on Vashar's border and the risk that Vashar would sell the refugees back. Both risks were real; Vashar was in fact asked and refused. Neither the bench nor Eshkar is cleared. Roughly four households in five went through; the exact count is not ruled. Some fighters went as fathers.
- **The end.** Yaltah bat Ozer broke the scroll-keys before the soldiers reached the chamber. Shahar's gate is closed for want of keys, not silent in the way Qiryath's is; no reader article may treat it as a sign. The legions killed the wounded and the old in the lower halls. The legate's claim that the rebels killed their own is false, and stays a live claim in-world.
- No ramp, no suicide pact and no speeches. Shahar is the precedent Jirahar gate benches cite for refusing another tower's danger, and it is why no bench would open for Qiryath's people eight years later (§16).

## 16. The Nine Mornings and the Empty Litters (61–72 AR)

The Tower Revolt no longer opens with a treasury raid or a stopped offering, and it no longer ends at a last fortress. Dates: outbreak 61; Shahar 62–64; the province's smaller tower towns dug out one at a time, 64–69; Qiryath besieged from autumn 69 and burned in 72; the triumph in 76.

- **The first morning** was green tamarisk and three days of rain. By the charter the governor supplied the emperor's beast and its wood from imperial stores, and that week his wood contractor had delivered billets cut the same month. No power acted on the altar.
- **Mornings two to nine** were Gedor bar Ammi, a carrier of the emperor's wood, who soaked the billets overnight because the governor's men had flogged his brother to death in 60. His confession of 62 was true and unbought. Zevad bar Tirzan's men killed him for it.
- **The Silence of Acceptance did not change** at any point in 61–72. Every offering that burned, burned with smoke, and the stones were hot. Nothing in the war was an answer of any kind, and this file still makes no ruling on the Silence itself.
- **Dessan Vole's entries.** His entry of fraud was right about eight mornings for reasons he did not know. His entry that the tenth morning's bull was "answered" was false: pitch burns. The charge under the Purge edict was lawful in imperial law. Sending soldiers up the stair broke the charter, and the Jirahar jurists who say Qiryath never broke it are correct: the Keeper offered for the emperor every morning to the last.
- **The offering never lapsed.** Elqan bar Sered kept it up as barley when the beasts were gone.
- **The fire of 72.** Vole lit the emperor's bull on Qiryath's altar with the sanctuary's cedar. Maruth had already given a standing order to fire the cedar stores once the precinct was taken. Both are true, and the house would have burned without the bull. The Red Star answered nothing on that altar; no load was set and no price paid, and "the Star's share" in Maruth's dispatch is rhetoric.
- **The cisterns.** The dropping of the cistern galleries was Astaroth's standing practice for siege engineers, as at Shahar: frames broken before a priest of the Red Star, a failure line shown, stone piled on it. Morally blind; the engineers chose the cisterns.
- **The vessels** went through Qiryath's gate lawfully, with the consent of a receiving bench, over two nights before the breach. **Which bench received them is not ruled here** and should be decided only when a story needs the vessels. No reader article may name it. The Keeper asked for the children first and was refused by every bench; that is true, and the Road Companies' taunt is unjust to him.
- **Why Qiryath's gate has not opened since is not ruled.** It is not closed for want of keys, as Shahar's is, and the Keepers' "silent, not dead" stands. Reader articles may report the towers' talk that the gate is still on the road with the vessels; none may state a cause. The frame the engineers broke in the gate chamber showed them the stair's failure line and nothing about the gate, which is outside the Red Star's grammar. The warmth beneath the stair in 1360 stays unresolved (§13).
- **Elqan bar Sered** died under torture at Arcelon in the winter after the fall without naming a bench.
- **The Litter Debt** is a human instrument: the weight of the vessels from the Qoreth inventory, charged by the head on every Jirahar household under Atherian rule until surrender, with payments never credited. The treasury laid it after the triumph of 76. It replaces the earlier "tribute in place of the sanctuary contribution". Its rolls are the lists Vestian's officers used. No state collects it in 1360; when it lapsed is not ruled.
- **Karsen Maruth** is the eastern commander who lost the succession war to Dorvan Dascen in 78. Every tower town of the province was a small Shahar; he dug them out one at a time and held the Dawn Road while his envoys demanded the Shahar households of Vashar, which is why Qiryath stood unbesieged for eight years. He came home for his triumph only in 76, when the search for the vessels was given up.

## 17. The Store-house and the Table at Sarqen (78–204 AR)

Human history, with one sign.

- **The reigns.** Dorvan Dascen (78–91) adopted no one. His daughter Ysane held the treasury ledgers and the ports; Daven Talar held the eastern army; they married in 92 and ruled as colleagues, she to 126, he to 119. She defunded the Avesh garrisons in 104 while he lived. Rovan Caelis (119–147), Daven's nephew, was acclaimed by the eastern army and accepted by Ysane. Garen Vardun (147–162) took the throne on the Vessan house's grain credit and was killed in the south by men of one of three clans to whom he had promised the same pasture; which clan is not ruled. Marek Vessan (b. 154) reigned 162–189, under his mother Irsa's regency to 171. No emperor of this line was murdered by guards.
- **The table, 187–189.** The Table Oath remains a human oath (Imperial Founding review). The runaways whom Melqa of Belqara led onto the landing were protected by its suppliant clause, and the towns' refusal to supply an unsworn governor was political. The bitten wax of autumn 187 was a genuine Feastling sign of a Gluttony opening, of the same kind as in 352 BR: troops feeding by worth in a country denied its meal. It was not the Bronze-Faced Provider. It closed after the meal of 189, when Marek ate at the table and swore.
- **Marek** was not deposed. He chose the grain for Caleran over his mother's house, was abandoned on the landing by the Vessans and his escort, and lived at Esharat as the towns' guest until c. 204. His oath was sincere. The commands' use of his name for fifteen years was a convenience.

## 18. The Sold Seasons and the Condemned Ground (248–292 AR)

- **Sold seasons.** From the Common Sky onwards a registered answer could open a campaign, and the treasury could lawfully pay only an opened one. After 248 the High Augurate sold openings. The Stars had no part in the sale: an augur's refusal to open is not a Menhir's refusal to answer, and most openings of these years were entries with no petition behind them. There were no breakaway emperors. Commands whose seasons were voided fought unpaid and kept depot revenue.
- **287–288.** The voiding of the southern frontier army's season is the direct cause of Cassian's acclamation.
- **Condemned ground, 288–291.** The colleges' entries condemning Redeemer halls and burial yards as cursed were forgeries of authorship: no Star had answered about any of that ground, and the augurs knew it. The exhumations to common pits are the origin of the wrong graves and mixed dead of the next generation. There was no hunt for books of signs, and no elder at Halmenth surrendered one.
- **c. 338, Veridun Minor.** The wardens' loading of the ruined shrine was the standing practice, and the altar step broke where it was weakest, over the chamber. The Red Star showed a structural fact and said nothing about whose bones lay in it. The college's entry ("a Star-priestess") and the bishops' claim ("Calira") are both human readings, and this file makes no ruling on whose bones they are.
