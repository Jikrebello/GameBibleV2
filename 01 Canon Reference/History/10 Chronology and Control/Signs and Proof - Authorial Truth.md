# Signs and Proof — Authorial Truth

The author-only rulings behind the history of 0–411 AR, approved in [[Causality Pass - 04 Who May Authenticate a Sign, 0 to 411 AR|Causality Pass 04]] on 14 November 2026 under the [[Causality Pass - Direction|Direction]]. Reader articles show these truths only through what in-world witnesses saw and claimed. Cross-references: [[White Flame Doctrine]] · [[The Making of Saints]] · [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]] · [[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]] · [[Authorial Notes from Reader Articles - Redeemer and Conversion]].

## 1. The Commission Rule and the Crowded Field

This is the answer to the question the causality review put to the whole span: why did a faith whose signs really work take 292 years to win?

1. **Grace acts by commission, not on demand.** The White Flame answers the righteousness and purpose of a person and the commission under which it acts. It cannot be summoned to a hearing or produced to save a defendant; [[The Fireless Trial]] is the type case. A manifestation proves its commission and nothing beyond it. Because the Flame demands sanctity and coherence (Commandment 7), it does not ratify a lie, including a lie spoken under seal, in a register or as an act of state.
2. **The field is crowded.** A light on the dead, a fire that will not take or a lamp that burns without oil has at least four possible authors:
   - the Flame;
   - Shell-fire from kiln wards, signal scripts or forge furnaces;
   - a Menhir's bounded omen, which genuinely answers within its law and price;
   - an Idol's Mask, whose counterfeit kindling feeds on the devotion it draws (Virella's "two veils"; Bruckmoor proves it later).

   A wonder shows that something acted. It does not show what acted. [[Mesakha Traditions]] already requires signs that "cannot be staged by warlock script, contract, Shade, relic fraud, or Idol pressure".
3. **Law names signs.** From the Common Sky onwards the augur colleges kept the civic calendar and the register of answers (origin and legal form: [[Causality Pass - 03 Age of Glory and the Bridge to 0 AR|Pass 03]]). In imperial law a sign existed only once a chartered college had entered it with its terms, its witnesses and the name of the answering Star. A registered answer could open a campaign, void an election, or consecrate or condemn ground. An unregistered wonder was fraud or an unlicensed cult feeding something.
4. **The magistrate's profile was earned.** A cult that met at executed men's graves, kept lamps for them, reported lights on the dead and refused the colleges' examination matched, point by point, the profile the empire had learned from real Mask cults: the ruler-cult appetite, Qarzeth's Gluttony Mask, the grief cults of garrisons, and above all the Kept Lamps of 242–244. Many persecuting magistrates were sincere sacred hygienists who had seen a Mask feed. Their sincerity excuses none of the tortures, burnings and confiscations they ordered.
5. **Why witness carried the faith.** Proof could not compel belief. The believer cannot produce a sign on demand; the sceptic can always assign it to a rival author; the law gave the naming to an interested body; and belief is assent, which neither force nor calculation supplies. The faith spread by people, and its signs travelled as rumour among the already persuaded. A self-authenticating sign (the Aurin at Velos, which anyone can watch fail to freeze) broke the colleges' monopoly when three centuries of testimony had not.
6. **The Stars keep the same rule.** The Menhir answer inside their own grammar. Astaroth answers a field, a load and a price. His structural answers to builders and siege engineers are a standing practice from Darneth onward. He answered **Astrion's public test of legitimacy** once, in 350, because it put a real load at real hazard. He gave nothing to "victory without resistance": no terms for Astrion's proposed second test, nothing to Ilios Marrant's last petition of 383 (no load, no price, only a request to be shown worthy), and nothing to the eastern command's petition at the crossing in 384. The Church learned this rule as a defendant and forgot it as a ruler.

**Corollary for readers.** Every reader article in this span may let witnesses disagree about what a wonder was. None may settle it in narration.

## 2. The Three Silent Days (0 AR)

No augural register in the empire records an answer between the hour of the Binding and the third morning. Severan's register is the best known only because he read at Qiryath; every college's book has the same blank. Augury resumed on the fourth day unchanged.

- **Truth.** The fled powers held still while the Judge was among the dead. Menhir fusion was a flight from judgement, and every Menhir sovereignty, the Stars included, went still for those three days. This file makes no ruling on whether Idol Masks also fell silent; no register could have recorded it.
- **In-world.** The registers prove only that no answer came. The colleges sealed the days. Tyros carried knowledge of them into the open, the Great Purge was fought partly to recover the copies, and the bishops have held the originals since 383 and have never published them.
- **Interface.** Qiryath's Silence of Acceptance (permanent) and the Three Silent Days (three days) fall together at 0 AR. Neither explains the other, and no reader article may present either as the cause of the other. The Silence of Acceptance stays as the Jirahar and Church dispute it.
- **Severan** never read again. His college recorded that he "laid down the staff". Grief, fear or faith: unresolved.

This is a torque, not a weird. It is explained here and may be cited in the Torque Register.

## 3. The Great Purge (c. 36–45 AR)

A records war. Tyros's Dialogues argued that the colleges had recorded something they refused to discuss; junior augurs and register-keepers in several provincial colleges followed him, carrying copies of the sealed days. A defected college could enter the Flame's signs in public law. The High Augurate at Caleran obtained a commission of inquiry (c. 36), staffed it with augurs, paid informers from confiscations and charged defendants with forging or stealing sacred records. Contributing causes: the Othran succession troubles that made the colleges' ceremonies indispensable, and Menos's risings in the Ironvale forges that supplied the army.

- The commission's edict made it a crime to publish a wonder the colleges had not entered. For about 250 years a congregation that wrote down a miracle wrote its own indictment. The Underground therefore recorded signs as practical judgement or hymn ("the lamp took well"; the Ashway coal test). **Ruling on the Ashway coal test:** some extinguished coals were wind and rain, some were not, and the record was written so that no one could tell the difference. No later reader article may decide any single crossing.
- The Lantern Cradle was the observatory of one of the defected colleges. Its coloured lanterns continue the old register marks. The hymns' claim that some burned without wick or oil is left open.
- **Virella** (d. 47) was killed by a commissioner whose warrant had lapsed and who kept poisoning her anyway. Tyros remains the last Companion to die in the Purge.
- The emperor of the Purge stays unnamed in reader prose.

## 4. The Four Nails (provenance only)

No document on the route states a count, and none may be written to.

1. **0 AR.** Sentences for rival kingship were reported to the emperor with the instruments as proof. The governor's case chest took the soldiers' nails, the notice of the charge and the receipt for the body. The timber stayed at Qiryath, unrecorded.
2. **0–c. 41.** The chest lay in the judgement archive of the Celestial Forum.
3. **c. 41.** The Purge commission produced the instruments at Jareth's trial to prove that the cult's founder was a condemned rival king, then had them leaded into the Pillar of Concordance as a monument to a sentence carried out. Jareth burned before it. The notice of the charge stayed in the chest and is lost.
4. **41–219.** The three early inventories stand exactly as the Weird register has them.
5. **c. 300–310.** Cassian's grant of the forum precinct carried the Pillar and the Nails to the Church.

**The Third Nail stays ⊘ SEALED.** Nothing above explains the count. The Weird register is not amended; the author declined even the clause "fused into the Pillar by the Purge commission". The Lives of bearers who swallowed shavings from the nails are demonstrably late.

## 5. The Kept Lamps (c. 242–244 AR)

An **Envy Mask** spread through unpaid eastern garrisons in the arrears after the second Vashar war, promising that comrades who died unpaid were "kept" by lamps over their graves. The lamps leaned towards mourners and burned without oil. The Mask fed on the grievance, and the mutinies drew heat from it. Augurs broke its central image c. 243–244.

- Tervan's Edict of Public Sacrifice (245) answered a real Mask. Its licence for every household's graves and lamps caught the Redeemer burial fraternities.
- After 248 most congregations relit from a flame never licensed. Amaryah's keepers had hidden her lamp in a covered firepot in a neighbour's bread oven; theirs became the flame everyone sought, and the daughter-flame custom spread from that winter.
- **The Temple That Would Not Burn** (Cyrian, c. 246): the commissioners' three burnings were sincere anti-Idol purgations. What preserved the stone is not ruled here and stays open to its three interpretive voices; the authorial position is only that the Kept Lamps Mask had no part in the Cyrian shrine.

## 6. Ironvale and the Forty Flames (290–291 AR)

The flame on the Forty was a genuine, brief manifestation of the White Flame (already fixed in the Chronology). The 291 deposition is honest: the guard and Brask, the Ashforges' enslaved script-keeper, freed by Cassian's officers so his oath would stand, swore the furnace scripts were cold. The prison roll's silence about the flame is the crown's own lie of omission. Both crown records survive.

The Council of Second Flame (c. 383–384) was the first bishops' sign hearing after the Dissolution. It confirmed the Forty on the deposition. The deposition reached Aurelium only after 383.

## 7. Velos and the Crown's River (292–377 AR)

The Velos sign is the Flame's and is permanent. Its ownership is human:

- **292.** The augurs of Caleran entered the reach as a sign answered to the emperor. This is why Cassian kept the colleges.
- **c. 296, the Velos Concord.** Chapel at the bridge, a Warden of the Reach paid by the crown to keep the winter ice-line register, and restitution decreed empire-wide in exchange.
- **316.** Cassian was carried to Velos and baptised in the unfrozen water by the crown-paid chaplain. Whether the baptism availed is the Cassian Wager, and it stays unresolved: the Weighing is real and nobody knows.
- **c. 309, Cassian's household.** The heir asked the High Augur of Astaroth for an answer on the succession; an entry naming him would have been the strongest title in the empire. The answer was given and sealed. Cassian executed his son and the High Augur; the empress lived. **What the answer said is not ruled here.** It may stay unknown for ever.
- **Astrion** had the augurs re-enter the reach as Hecatara's answer (of crossings). Hecatara gave no such answer; the entry was a political re-registration, and the bishops were right to call it a forgery of authorship. This made the 350 test necessary to him.
- **377.** Vestian gave the register and chapel to the bishops. River and bridge stayed crown property. "It continues."

## 8. The Two Standards and the East (350–384 AR)

- Astaroth's 350 answer was real and bounded: a structural truth about a loaded standard.
- **Tellan** (Adran's younger son, Astrion's brother) brought water trains bought from the Well-Tarkhan carriers who had kept their contracts, along the eastern depot road, under the bronze standard of the test. The column survived. Tellan never claimed the throne because no second answer came and his augurs said so honestly.
- **c. 357, the Accord of the Two Standards.** The eastern command kept its revenue share on Sevrin's 316 terms, its augurs and the Answered Standard.
- **384.** At the decisive crossing the eastern augurs asked the Red Star for terms and got none: there was no field the Star recognised, only a request for victory. Vestian won because the Stars would not answer his enemy, and then the Flame would not answer his law.
- Tellan ended as a pensioned captive on the Aurin and died in his bed before Vestian. The Answered Standard's fate is disputed in-world (melted for a bell; buried under a depot road). **Authorial truth:** it was melted. The bell rings in the Aurin valley still and is not marked.

## 9. The Kellovar Ford (377 AR)

The ford below Kellovar belonged to a Menhir river-court of Hecatara's grammar (tolls, thresholds, the consequence of passage). Its toll was paid in the small iron, bone and hair tokens Folk households carried. Lucar's chaplains had confiscated those tokens as idols, and his officers sold the silver ones. His army crossed unpaid and the river took the crossing; the Folk who had kept their tokens crossed dry.

The court is morally blind. It exacted without judging. The defeat proves only the ford's law, not the justice of the Folk cause (which was just on other grounds) or Heaven's judgement on Lucar (which this file does not rule). Vestian read it as a judgement on a divided empire; that was his reading.

## 10. The Dissolution and the War of the Two Standards (383–384 AR)

**Ilios Marrant**, the last High Augur of Caleran, asked leave for one final petition to the Red Star on behalf of the college that had made it answer in 350. Vestian granted it before a crowd in the Celestial Forum, before the Pillar that holds the Nails. The augurs set no load and named no price. Nothing answered, because there was nothing in the request the Star could answer. A converted augur later recorded Marrant's own reading of this; it is correct.

By statute the authentication of signs passed to the bishops of recognised communion, with the Deep Register (including the Three Silent Days and, after 384, the 350 record). Caleran 383, the provinces by 384, the east at the war's end.

## 11. The Second Silence (385–c. 389 AR)

**The pattern is real.** From the Profession of 385, reception lamps at compelled state professions burned yellow by the thousand, while willing parish baptisms went on kindling at the old uneven rate. The Flame did not leave the Church. It did not answer the compulsion, because a profession made to keep an office or a house is incoherent: the mouth says what the person does not hold.

- A white flame at a state profession meant only that the person had freely sought baptism (most such cases had sought it before the decree). A yellow flame supplied no finding about any individual's faith.
- The court's explanation (hidden rites, "Jirahar blindness") was false. The ban on household rites (c. 387) and the faster tower orders were built on it.
- **c. 389.** The lamp was taken out of the oath. This is the first time an institution hid a falling yield instead of repenting of it; the Guttering ([[Causality Pass - 05 The Guttering, 400 to 905 AR|Pass 05]]) is the long cost.
- Aurelium's sealed register survives incomplete in the Sanctum in 1360. Which district books are missing is not ruled.
- "Second Silence" was coined by the gateway-port congregations of Jirahar descent. The churches avoid it.

**Ruling on the Church.** As a body it did not lie; it hid. Caleran's ruling that a properly witnessed profession had civil force without a sign is the first statement of protocol theology.

## 12. The Man-Bearer Party and Raveth (c. 385–411 AR)

**Recommended position, adopted.** The Council of Halmenth's teaching on the Redeemer stands: the Man-Bearer Christology is wrong. The vessel critique of the empire ("the lamp is made by hands; the Fire comes after") is true. The condemnation mixed doctrine with prejudice about descent. Neither side is cleared.

- The Raveth hearing house kept the depositions as a record only; Jirahar courts claimed no jurisdiction over baptised descendants in the maternal line.
- The crowd that burned it (c. 389) had its own quarrel over rents and debts. The clergy who rallied it wanted the depositions gone. Vestian dropped the rebuilding because rebuilding would restore the record.
- **Nasriel** (b. c. 362, Qabir): reader by c. 383; kept Qabir's count of the unkindled in 385–388; carried copies to Raveth and was away when it burned; his mother's register was seized in the 390s; ordained c. 394; bishop of Qabir c. 402 over Caleran's objection on grounds of descent; condemned 411.

## 13. The Empty Charter's Clause (c. 396 AR)

Vestian struck out "in perpetuity" and wrote *before the Mesakha* in his own hand, using the Jirahar word. Motives: the Second Silence had persuaded him the Return was near; a conditional prohibition binds until its condition is met; and the word was a sneer and a creed at once. The charter never says who recognises the Mesakha. A recognised sign would lift the ban on rebuilding, and two authorities claim the right to recognise one: the Church (heir to the augurs' power since 383) and the Keepers (holders of the Mesakha tests). The warmth beneath the Silent Gate stair in 1360 is therefore a legal emergency as well as a theological one. **What the warmth is remains unresolved.**
