# Historical Spine of the Known World

This note is the causal backbone of the setting. It explains how the present conflict-board emerged from inherited wounds rather than disconnected lore.

## 1. Age of Chains

[[Age of Chains]] is the age of Giant dominion. Humanity is enslaved by false-god empires that leave behind ruins, forbidden sciences, sacrifice systems, stolen engineering, and the memory of power impersonating divinity.

**Inherited wound:** later empires are haunted by the fear that monument, law, and sacred language may become Giant tyranny in human form.

## 2. Age of Exodus

[[Age of Exodus]] begins when the Great Prophet breaks Giant rule and leads freed humanity across the [[Great Glass Desert]]. Liberation is real, but the Prophet's death and the failed sanctuary leave humanity freed before it is fully formed.

**Inherited wound:** every later culture inherits deliverance, disobedience, contested memory, and the question of what was lost when unity shattered.

## 3. Age of Scattering

[[Age of Scattering]] turns one exodus into many peoples: proto-Atherians, Jirahar, proto-Ahl-e-Ahd, Fäskogarfolk and Ättar, the Vesari river-folk of the east, Sestaran harbor peoples, Tarkhan, Nsizwa, and others.

**Inherited wound:** no people keeps the whole Exodus intact, but every people can claim some fragment of it.

## 4. Age of Hearths and First Kings

[[Age of Hearths and First Kings]] is the age of villages, hill forts, river towns, first kings, ancestor houses, Menhir pacts, oasis law, and local priesthoods.

**Inherited wound:** kingship protects people from chaos but also reintroduces domination after liberation.

## 5. Age of City-States

[[Age of City-States]] creates the first urban worlds: Atherian civic rivals, Sestaran ports, Jirahar tower towns, Ahl-e-Ahd oasis polities, roads, archives, walls, war engines, and early elemental scripting.

**Inherited wound:** cities preserve memory, but they also create debt, class, legal violence, and appetite for empire.

## 6. Age of Stars and Atherian Synthesis

[[Age of Stars]] is the late pre-imperial Atherian synthesis. Star cults, civic law, archives, roads, astronomy, and military discipline turn rival cities into a civilization that believes it is destined to organize the world.

**Inherited wound:** Atheria turns older fragments into law and beauty, but also converts spiritual loss into civic grandeur.

## 7. Atherian Unity and the Star Pantheon

The old Atherian Empire creates roads, cities, law, archives, imperial identity, and continental memory. Its Star Pantheon gives the empire a cosmic civic order.

**Inherited wound:** everyone wants Atheria's legitimacy, roads, ruins, and archives.

## 8. The Redeemer Event

Isfir the Redeemer appears inside imperial history, not outside it. His ministry confronts both imperial theology and legalistic religious expectation. His death, resurrection, ascension, and gift of the Flame reorder the spiritual meaning of history.

**Inherited wound:** the question of who truly understands the Redeemer becomes a political and metaphysical fault line.

## 9. Conversion and Persecution

The Redeemer's faith overtakes the Star Pantheon. Martyrs become saints. The empire baptizes itself into a new order, but conversion also produces coercion, persecution, archive rewriting, and violence against rejected or inconvenient communities.

**Inherited wound:** sanctity and institutional power become intertwined from the beginning.

## 10. Jirahar Exile

Jirahar communities, whose tower-town roots go back to the early historic ages, are driven into deeper exile. Some tower towns are destroyed, and survival becomes inseparable from memory, law, finance, and prophecy.

**Inherited wound:** the Jirahar remember the Church as persecutor, while Church cultures remember the Jirahar through suspicion, prophecy anxiety, and inherited guilt.

## 11. Ahl-e-Ahd Rise

The Ahl-e-Ahd develop from desert oath and water-right peoples into a contract civilization with legal metaphysics, bound entities, golem labor, and military enforcement. Their rise turns law into a weapon of statecraft.

**Inherited wound:** contract order becomes both a real civilizational strength and a spiritual danger.

## 12. The Fall of Atheria

Ahl-e-Ahd invasion, imperial weakness, failed crusades, and internal fracture break old Atheria. Caleran falls. Refugees, rebels, collaborators, and border peoples inherit different versions of the catastrophe.

**Inherited wound:** no successor state can prove itself without arguing over the fall.

## 13. Thalmyric Founding

Mountain resistance, holy fire, ascetic militarization, and imperial memory become Thalmyria. The Thalmyrics define themselves as the flame-bearing heirs of Atheria.

**Inherited wound:** reconquest becomes spiritual identity.

## 14. Eclessian Fragmentation

Eclessia survives in the east as a church-bound but politically fragmented world: kingdoms, duchies, reform currents, relic politics, feudal rivalries, and competing claims to true doctrine.

**Inherited wound:** unity is desired, feared, and constantly deferred.

## 15. Present-Day Fractured Inheritance War

The current board is not merely geopolitical. It is a struggle over who inherits Atheria, who owns the Redeemer's public meaning, who controls relics and archives, who commands lawful violence, and whose account of history becomes reality.

**Current question:** can restoration happen without another empire of coercion?
