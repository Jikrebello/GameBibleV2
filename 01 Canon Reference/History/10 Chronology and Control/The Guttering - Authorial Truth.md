# The Guttering - Authorial Truth

This page holds the authorial truth behind the history of 398–905 AR, approved from [[Causality Pass - 05 The Guttering, 400 to 905 AR|Causality Pass 05]] on 14 November 2026 under [[Causality Pass - Direction]]. Reader articles tell these events through what people saw and claimed; this page says what was actually happening. Nothing here may be stated as fact in public prose.

## The Guttering

**Nothing changed in the Flame.** From Vestian's death in 398 the rites of the imperial church's office-holders answered less and less, while frontier hermits, hospital keepers, village priests and the lay brothers of the remount studs kindled much as before. Three things changed in the people who called on the Fire:

- **Selection.** After Vestian, sees, choir places and relic custodies went to the loyal and to buyers. The share of officiants the Flame answered fell at court and in the rich sees, and held on the frontier, where nobody bought a hermitage.
- **Purpose.** Court rites existed to confirm a succession, make a district submit or display possession. The Flame answers a person's righteousness and purpose, never an institution's need to be seen holding it.
- **Coherence.** Commandment 7 demands sanctity *and coherence*. A witness who knowingly recites a false clause, such as a bought immunity or a forged boundary, receives nothing.

The cost structure holds throughout. Sanctity cannot be bought, so the court's purchases yielded nothing; the real price was paid in persons, from the bearers the crusade of seals broke to the frontier monks the marches depended on. Shell-fire carried its own price in gold, training, instruments and elemental risk. The chancery's word for kindlings, *yields*, is the error in one syllable.

**The three stages.**

1. **Concealment (from c. 398).** Failed rites were entered in the palace calendar as *postponed*.
2. **Substitution (from 447).** The first Sestaran Shell-fire cohorts were hired in the succession war of 447–452. By the sixth century licensed warlock fire was the standard imperial incendiary, and hired Ättar crews and Folk auxiliaries filled ranks a church-backed muster no longer filled.
3. **Counterfeit (from 681).** Shell-fire was used at ceremonies to imitate a kindling. The first case to become public was **the Copper Lamp**: Tavoran II's accession lamp in 681 was made to flare white by a Sestaran warlock hidden in the cathedral gallery with a brass fire-cup. In 683, unpaid for eighteen months, he sold the cup and a written account to a Covenant factor at Sermora. Counterfeit fire is fraud and "may create a breach" ([[The Holy Fire]]); the cup began the Covenant's file of counterfeit fire, which reached its fullest form in the record of Corvenas III's accession in 884.

**The surviving register.** One palace register of yields survives for 794–812. In thirty-one entries a later hand has changed *postponed* to *answered*. Eclessian historians have used it against Thalmyric claims since the eleventh century. It is genuine, and so are the alterations.

**Names.** *The Guttering* is the coinage of a Thalmyric refuge chronicler of the 930s: *in the palace the lamps guttered, and in the passes they burned*. Eclessian historians call the same centuries **the Unexamined Centuries**, arguing that the yields never fell and only the number of wonders anyone checked did. They are partly right: Aurelium's examined clergy kindled more reliably, because the register selected and formed holy clergy. Grace itself was never governable.

**The Kindling Question.** Veridun learned from the Guttering that office cannot command the Fire and only witnessed presence counts (theophany). Aurelium learned that unexamined claims hide fraud (protocol). Both lessons are true, and each church remembers half of the five centuries. The ruling on why hymns kindle remains the one in [[Real-History Concordance]]: by the righteousness and purpose of the singer.

**Caleran and Veridun.** In 905 Caleran's rites were performed by a court whose purpose was to keep what it held, and Corvenas III's last public call on the wall had the same purpose; it failed for the Guttering's reason. The Ember went on burning through fifty-two years of occupation; it neither defended the city nor went out. Veridun in 906 was a refuge full of people intent on protecting the defenceless, beside Veridun Minor's hearth tradition, which had never been an endowed office. The Veil was a commission given for a city's need, and it came through the hearth-keepers' disobedience: they refused the council's order to burn the flour and fed the city instead, and the smoke of that baking is what lay over the valley. The smoke's stillness for the five days the bread lasted and the shared dreams were the commission's work. Who was sent stays unresolved; Thalmyric piety's Amaryah is one possibility among several. Whether the levies' going home was part of the answer, or the act of unpaid men in mountain snow who had dreamed of home, stays unresolved too. The Flame protects persons, not possessions; Corvenas signed for his relics and the women of Veridun Minor burned none of the bread.

*Bent 14 November 2026 by [[Causality Pass - 10D Bending the Long Frontier and the Covenant]]: the Veil is no longer a figure spreading fire in the sky before a storm, and the Fall's 1453 furniture (the young conqueror at the walls, two weak points, three possible deaths, the great church sealed) is replaced below. Bent again the same day by [[Causality Pass - 11D Second Bend - Covenant Rise and the Fall]]: the Covenant's rise no longer follows prophet, four caliphs, dismissed general, river battle, protected-subject terms and martyred grandson; the engines come to the river dyke and not to a land wall; Idris is thirty-one and the "Conqueror" gloss is retired; the drowned heir of 446 and the dead heir of 709 both live; Qahir's rise has its step.*

**After 905.** The office-church died with Caleran. The refuge leagues chose chaplains by service, and the march monasteries' living-witness rite became the model for the army chaplaincy. Most who fought the return fought to protect people, which is why the 950s produced the first documented battle manifestations. The reliability faded again once the restored empire re-endowed its church; the Ashfall Omen of the 980s is the same pattern in its second generation, and Tavoran III's reading of it is his own.

## The four houses

| House | Years | Power base | Why it rose | Why it fell |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vestianic** | 398–447 | court bishops and paymasters | Vestian's son inherited the bishops who had made his father | the regency's bishops had tonsured the only heir as a boy; the old emperor died in 447 and the abbot would not leave his cloister |
| **Saldane** | 452–587 | the southern fort-line army | the great sees acclaimed its commander in 447–452 and were paid in immunities | the Toll War debts, the famine requisitions and the loss of the north |
| **Tavoranid** | 587–709 | the remnant field army and the frontier bishops | they held the line while the Saldanes lost it | a palace fever killed Tavoran II in nine days and left his son alive but unable to ride the marches within the year and a day (the Fever Week, 709) |
| **Corvenid** | 713–905 | the marches, then the Caleran chancery | Corvenas I rode the marches in his sick brother-in-law's place, and the oaths stayed with the man who had knelt | the five pressures of 885–905 |

Each change of house followed a change in who paid the army. Immunity sales moved provincial wealth into tax-free relic estates; the registers kept every lost northern province, because striking one off would concede the Covenant's ruling in writing, so over three centuries the legal claim became the budget. Gold stayed heavy for warlocks and foreign captains; soldiers' silver was debased in the eighth century and again after 878.

Named rulers: Vestian's son (unnamed, r. 398–447); **Teomar Saldane** (r. 509–531); the Saldane emperor of the famine years, called *the Granary Thief* in the north (unnamed); **Tavoran I** (r. 587–624); **Tavoran II** (r. 681–709); **Corvenas I** (r. 713–746); **Corvenas II** (r. 861–884, abdicated); **Corvenas III** (r. 884–905). Tavoran II's son, the bedridden emperor and Caleran claimant of 709–713, stays unnamed, as does Vestian's tonsured grandson, who lived on as an abbot until about 467.

## The kindled marches

After 612 about a dozen frontier districts faced the Velian belt, the desert edge and the western strait. Each was held under one charter-oath, re-sealed in the Fire and renewed at every accession, in person, before a witness on whom the Flame actually came. Civil and military command sat in one sworn person because a second instrument would leave a seam a breach-court could serve. A march left unrenewed for a year and a day could be served like any dead-witness town, and a commander who left his march without a kindled summons breached his own oath. Land was held for service, tax-free; frontier monasteries kept the standing witnesses, the hospitals and the remount studs.

This is why Corvenas I won in 713, why written orders could not lawfully move a march in 905, and why Thalmyria's military provinces and soldier-tenure exist. The hymn-blessed studs of [[First Catch and Grey Sister]] were march studs kept by lay brothers whom the Guttering never reached.

**The Ash Guard (option kept).** The later Corvenids kept no palace guard of Ättar; that reading is retired by 11D as too close to its analogue. They hired baptised Ättar crews to hold the capital's harbour and the Kedravan coast, sworn to the emperor's own ships. Those crews are the plausible ancestors of the Thalmyric Ash Guard through the men who sailed for Kedrava in 905. Thalmyric tradition may claim an older household guard; nothing here supports it.

## The Unsealing

**The defect.** Many northern municipal charters had been witnessed under the Stars by augur colleges. When Vestian ended the Star rites and dissolved the colleges (383–384), the witness lapsed. *If Pass 04 accepts augurs as charter witnesses,* the lapse is a Menhir witness bound to the living law of the Star rites falling silent with that law. *If it does not,* the defect is the colleges' extinction as legal persons. Reader prose is written to stand on either reading. No Atherian court recognised the defect; Covenant law did, since an instrument whose witness is extinct can be voided. The dissolved colleges' families had sold their archives piece by piece, so the breach-courts knew which towns to visit first.

**What a service did.** The ruling bit through the town's own contracts. Garrison oaths, guild privileges and debts hung from the master charter, so when it fell the soldiers were released and the creditors panicked. The Covenant's terms were about grain, names and water: the town kept its walls, magistrates and altars; its householders were entered on the court's roll; it owed a measured share of each harvest, the Given; a bound keeper was set at its chief well to draw for the roll; and in a failed year the roll was to be fed from the Basin. Towns that refused were stormed, by men. The older list of terms (tribute, disarmament, restricted testimony, protected worship) is retired; the restrictions on arms and testimony in the present People of the Lamp status grew up later under the Banu Mizan compacts and the Qahirids.

**Why the courts went north.** The Seal of Names bound the Covenant to feed every name on the roll in a failed year, and the bound carriers of the 4,444 lift only what has been given. When the flood came thin in 567 the roll was larger than the Basin's bread. The breach-courts existed to make northern grain a lawful gift. Mujalib neither blessed nor forbade them; both traditions put words in his mouth, and the line Rumaitha's household kept is close to something he said.

**The Dry Ford, 575.** The northern commander believed a court unable to protect its escort could not lawfully sit. The law said no such thing. He won the fight, hanged the jurists and undid nothing they had sealed. His army then marched home through served towns in the dry season, and the bound keepers at their wells drew for the roll and for nobody else. The keepers acted within their instruments; they took nothing. The army sacked three of its own towns for their cisterns and broke up by autumn. Qatada lost the only pitched battle of the conquest. The people of the Clause later cited the Dry Ford as the first time a bound office's keeping had done an army's work.

**The crusade of seals, from 587.** Where the Flame came on a seal, the charter was witnessed in the Fire and could not be re-typed ([[White Flame Doctrine]]: the Flame resists predatory contract re-typing). Where a court cleric officiated and nothing came, the seal was wax. Where Shell-fire was substituted, the seal was fraud. Honest bishops would not recite clauses they knew to be false, so they sealed truncated charters that stripped out bought immunities and forged boundaries; many magnates preferred Covenant terms, which confirmed their estates for the Given and fed their tenants from the roll in a bad year. A few court-witnessed re-seals held, where the court cleric happened to be holy.

**Why it stopped.**

1. Charters re-sealed in the Fire could not be served, and the line of 612 lies roughly where they held.
2. After 597 every service sat under a disputed seal.
3. Qatada halted the northern army in 598 because he would serve no notice under a seal that might be overturned, and Zuhayr could not dismiss a marshal sealed by the Prophet's own hand. The halt ended the field war.
4. The Clause of the Given barred the 4,444 from conquest, and their number was finite: by about 610 every bound office was set at a well, on a road or in the oasis, and a town taken without a keeper had to be garrisoned and fed.
5. Garrisons and famine country had to be fed, and the Covenant was dividing among caliphs, Qazmarid and the Banu Mizan.
6. Each side held a proof it dared not test, and the Suspension wrote that fear into law.

**Tamsar, 611.** The Covenant's ruling failed because Tamsar's charter had been re-sealed in the Fire in 607 by a hermit-bishop. Caleran's reliquary failed because it was carried to make a district submit (the Guttering). The plea of the disputed seal was a real legal weakness. All three failures were genuine; nobody staged anything. It is the first recorded deterrence by unopened proof, and the root of [[The Age of Held Proofs - Authorial Control|the Held Proofs]].

**The Velian chests.** The Velian cities were served under Zuhayr's seal and took Banu Mizan protection c. 610 without surrendering their charters. If the Fourth Seal were proved forged, every service made in Zuhayr's name would fall and the Velian charters would revive. Qahirid interest in never opening the Seal is territorial as well as dynastic; Qazmarid has a standing interest in the chests. See [[The Fourth Seal - Authorial Truth]].

## Vashar and the Bread of the Bound

The Ubar Toll War (514–523) left every well of any size in the dry country with two lawful holders. For a millennium the well-houses had held water under awards witnessed by one crown or the other; in the war each crown voided the other's awards and granted the same wells again, and neither withdrew its grants at the peace. (The earlier version, in which both crowns cut client stipends, is retired by 11D.) The Covenant's jurists learned at home how to void an instrument for its witness. The Thin Floods (546–552) and the yoke-sickness (548–551) broke the settled economy. The Covenant's founding proof was food. Camels were largely spared by the murrain, so the desert clans kept their mobility while the settled world lost its traction.

The Crown of Roads and Dawns ruled by the king's oath to keep the roads. Famine emptied the relay stations and left the Dawn Road unkept; Covenant jurists declared the crown forsworn on its own founding idea, Mazhara of Daryam's saying turned on it by foreign lawyers. The method that later took the Atherian north was first tried here. Among those who sealed for bread were the horse-lords of Ruzhan; Qahir ibn Ruzbeh descends from one of them, and *Ruzbeh* is a family name kept for three centuries. The Qahirids are therefore a converted Vasharan house without Ahdari sacred lineage, which is why they legitimised themselves by personal instrument (Pass 06).

The Nine Wells had no sealed instrument to serve: the Tarkhan held water by labour, burial and use. The Covenant took them by force in the drought year of 558. "First Submission" is a service invented afterwards for a conquest that had none. The pattern holds for the Covenant's whole history: legal stalemate with peoples who hold charters, permanent war with peoples who do not.

**The Vault of Names as ration roll** is one jurist's claim in-world. Authorially the famine roll and the Vault are separate records; the jurist was wrong about the institution and right about the habit of mind.

## The Clause of the Given and the Ruling on Parties

The recited Covenant of the 4,444 binds the offices to dig, lift, carry, keep and guard, and says the bound "shall take nothing that is not given". *The Clause of the Given* is a translation convention; its native form belongs to the locked Covenant register and is not fixed here. Witnessed-Seal jurists read it for three centuries as barring the 4,444 from conquest, so from 568 to 905 the Covenant fought with human armies, contract logistics, pure-pattern siege engines and breach-courts.

- **Consent.** Mujalib's tribes sealed a covenant of water in a famine; a clause of war would not have bound them.
- **The Creditor.** A revelation proved by provision was the deeper hook, and a devil-host at Caleran's gates would have been visible; the Creditor works at three removes. Whether it profits from the name-cages is left open.
- **Commandment 7.** The founding covenant shows names and consent; Qahir's ruling is the loophole; the Slave-Soul line is coercion. No sentence makes it lawful: the captive remains a complete human soul, imprisoned.

In 885–886 Qahir obtained from the college at Al-Qahur the **Ruling on Parties**: the Clause binds parties, not powers. A captured Depth beast was never sealed into anything; a condemned man's name is forfeit by sentence (the jurists' claim); a pure construct has no name. A minority, the *people of the Clause*, argued that a covenant of provision cannot be honoured by conquering with what it did not name. They lost; they have been outvoted, never answered.

**Why not before 885.** Capturing Depth fauna needed route-registries of mapped Hell, compiled slowly through the Long Frontier by Banu Mizan scholars and breach-assessors together. The ruling needed a patron strong enough to force it. Until 884 the Suspension left a new weapon nothing it could lawfully be used against. Being party to nothing, the engines cannot be served either, so Atherian chanceries had no legal answer to them, only Vortharan counter-scripts.

## The Velos Standard and Oswin

The **Velos Standard** is the battle standard the court held to have been carried at the Stone Bridge in 292. It is genuine. It is distinct from the Answered Standard of Astrion's 350 test.

- **711.** The Caleran claimant in the War of the Two Oaths sent it east to rally the provinces. **Oswin of Aurelium** (bishop c. 694–731) examined it publicly and recorded it silent. It was silent because of the purpose it was sent for: a summons to civil war. Oswin's verdict was right in outcome and wrong in reasoning; the protocol tradition's founding verdict condemned a true relic for the wrong cause. Neither church could survive learning this.
- **876.** Corvenas II carried it before Oronda to receive the Velian cities under a pretext. It was silent again, for the same kind of reason.
- **884–905.** Corvenas III put it in the Ash Hall. On the last day of the siege he carried it onto the river dyke and it was silent a third time, for the same kind of reason: a call to keep what he held. It is the "object listed in Corvenas III's final inventory", the schedule of the Instrument of the Ash Hall, whose later fate is a Caleran campaign hook.

The east tested honestly after 653 because its bishops kept the revenue whichever way a test fell. Protocol was born where the truth was cheap.

## The Captain of the Sacrificial Wind

In the 860s floods and raiders drove the river marches' garrisons back from the lower fords. Two rescues of those retreats are told together in [[Saints and Sinners of the Chronicles]] and [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]; they are separate events.

- **The drowned banner.** A Material cult of [[AF-R02 - Apostate of the Drowned Standard|the Apostate of the Drowned Standard]] held flooded redoubts that annexed the river roads they protected. It held scraps of the withdrawal order, never the original standard. The levy after the safe crossing is AF-R02's "particular history".
- **The Captain (c. 866).** A redeemed human saint acting under one bounded commission, the corrected form of the Human Captain of the Host (ST-R04), with HC-N09 as the true angel of the host: witnesses saw bright riders and a red-gold hawk-geometry around a white-fire core, its wings like wind-torn standards, and heard the command in a wind before each fight. In life she was a ferrywoman of the river marches, born in a Velian village of the belt, who drowned c. 858 bringing children over in a flood. Her commission was to hold the road behind one convoy; victory, disciplined retreat and refusal were all within it. When the convoy was safe, the army's commander demanded she lead them on into enemy country; the orders he carried from Corvenas II named the Velian belt, and crossing would have broken the peace of 612 eight years early. The host withdrew because the charge had ended. An officer who rode on after the raiders without support died with his followers; he stayed on the imperial side of the line.

Caleran's clergy never recorded it: she was a woman, Velian-born, and she had refused the emperor. Aurelium entered it in the register of answered fire. The Covenant's border clerks recorded it in one line. This is why Thalmyric doctrine dates the first *documented* battle manifestations to the 950s: Caleran's church declined to document this one, and angels commanded by a saint are a different thing from bearers kindling the Fire in battle.

Its consequence is the Reception. Corvenas II believed Heaven had fought beside his army, believed it would do so again for a cause he thought better, and carried the Velos Standard into the belt to do what the saint had refused.

## Oronda and the broken peace

At Oronda in 876 the Velos Standard was raised before both armies and nothing came. On the walls the Velian clergy lit one lamp, as in their churches. Whether that lamp kindled is **deliberately unresolved**. The crusade collapsed in 878; the treasury could not ransom the prisoners.

In 884 Qahir obtained the **Ruling of the Broken Suspension** from the Witnessed-Seal college: by attacking on the pretext of a lapse, Atheria had breached the instrument of 612, so the Suspension was void. The empire handed its enemy the loophole. In 885 Qahir's officers took the Velian client courts from the Banu Mizan regency; a Banu Mizan marriage line kept Zanthir at Qabir as tributary emirs, which is why Zanthir is "the last pearl of the Banu Mizan" although the dynasty's rule in the belt ended c. 885. The Banu Mizan compacts outlived the dynasty (Pass 06).

## The Fall

**Five pressures.**

1. **Money.** The registers carried a north lost 290 years earlier; an honest muster would show empty depots, and striking the north off would concede it.
2. **The Guttering.** Corvenas III's accession re-seal in 884 was finished with Shell-fire when the court witness failed. Covenant intelligence had the file, and Caleran's own charter was thereby servable.
3. **The east** would not move without an examined summons.
4. **The law.** The Suspension was void and every march was bound to its ground; leaving without a kindled summons would breach the march oath and expose the march to service.
5. **Qahir's machine**, an industry rather than five monsters.

**Corvenas III's trap.** He was sincerely devout, and every remedy needed a public test: to call the marches he had to ride to them and kindle a summons, and failure there would void every march charter at once; to reform the treasury he had to concede the north in writing; to win the east he had to submit his relics to Aurelium. So he did what the capital could hide. The Ash Hall was the Guttering's purest error, a collector's attempt to gather enough holy objects that the Fire *must* come. **He did try the summons alone**, the night before the Jubilee rites of 903, and nothing came: he asked the Fire to prove his reign. The palace rumour is true and stays a rumour in public.

**The machine.** Capture companies on the routes into mapped Hell; name-cage foundries fed by a docket of breach-court death sentences; Shell extraction from Covenant-held anchors; coerced Ahdanite levies trained as engineers and foundry crews; Vault-sealed supply contracts; and the Surrender line, which in 905 meant only lesser engines and siege tables travelling with the breach-court train. They read each district's title and offered each district its own terms. The five great golems at the Fall were three Depth-Core and two Slave-Soul; no great Surrender frame existed until c. 944 (Pass 06).

**The crusade prisoners.** The first name-cages held prisoners of the Reception whom Caleran never ransomed, condemned under the 884 ruling. One of the two great Slave-Soul engines at the Fall held such a prisoner. Whether he is the soul released at Crown Gate in 907 is open. Thalmyric "martyr irons" is therefore literal.

**Qahir's rise.** Qahir (b. c. 848) commanded the northern horse in the Velian belt in 874–878. Neither the Banu Mizan regency nor the caliph at Al-Qahur could pay it, and he took the crusade's unransomed prisoners, about nine thousand, in place of wages. He entered them under his own name in a register at Iqashar, the first of his personal instruments and the beginning of the Vault's working halls there, worked them on the Chain Furnace yard and the Basin canals and paid his riders from the hire. In 881 the caliph gave him the Seal of the Roads, in the college's keeping since Qatada's death in 604, with the new title of Sultan. The Ruling of the Broken Suspension (884) also made his prisoners forfeit, which is why the first name-cages held them.

**Idris's absence.** Idris (b. c. 871) acceded in 902 at thirty-one, with daughters and no son; Qahir's instruments named Qahir and then Idris and nobody after him. His jurists forbade him the field because his death would have widowed the whole army, the exact catastrophe of 931 averted in advance. He governed the campaign by courier from Iqashar and never saw Caleran. The city was taken by Qahir's engine-master **Barzin** (a Ruzhan horse-lord's son, in his sixties) and the chief jurist of the breach-court train. The epithet *al-Qari* is a jurist's title, "the Reader", given for a city opened by reading. The earlier epithet *al-Fattah*, with its glosses "the Opener" and "the Conqueror", is retired; Thalmyric chroniclers call him the Letter-Caliph. He was the first of his house styled Sultan-Caliph. Ruzbeh's birth in 915 was followed by a two-year muster that wrote him in as successor-holder.

**Astaroth.** Barzin's engineers asked the Red Star where the Aurin dyke would fail under the load of the great engines, in the standing practice of builders and siege engineers ([[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review|the Darneth precedent]]). Astaroth answered within his grammar: a field, a load, a price. He showed **one** length, the Star's share built into the dyke in the Age of Glory to break in the wardens' test and rebuilt after it; the rebuilt length was the dyke's weakest point, and it had never been tested again because the colleges that recorded it were dissolved in 383. The red light the watch saw on the water along the dyke's foot the night before the assault is the answer's visible edge. The price was a lesser engine frame broken under the test load. The Covenant also held a dead college's archive, bought at Sermora, that located the length; both claims are true, and the Star's answer confirmed the archive. The answer was structural knowledge and nothing more; it pronounced no verdict on Caleran. The Star that answered Astrion's test at Caleran in 350 answered Caleran's enemies in 904, and answered Qazem's engineers on the Caelmount road in 917 ([[The Covenant after Caleran - Authorial Truth]]). The engines came to the dyke and not to the land wall (11D): Depth-Core beasts go to water, and the three Depth-Core frames could stand in the flooded river where no army could.

**The last day.** When the Depth-Core engines went into the river below the Star's share, he carried the Velos Standard onto the dyke and called on the Fire before both armies. It was the public test at last, made for the same purpose as every private one, to keep the city he held, and it failed for the same reason. The failure was decisive in a way no engine was: the jurists entered it, and the quarters still holding took terms that afternoon because the emperor's own Fire had refused him in front of them. The engines broke the dyke at dusk. The Aurin, still high with the late flood, came into the lower city, and the tanners' and boatmen's quarters, which held no charter and had been offered no terms, drowned. Quarters that had not taken terms by dark were stormed, with killing, rape and enslavement through the night.

**The Instrument of the Ash Hall.** The breach-court held his charter void since the counterfeit re-seal of 884, so in Covenant law he had no city to surrender, only his person and his household. He signed what it would take: himself and the Hall's relics, listed one by one, the Velos Standard among them. He named no person but himself; he did not think to, and the court would not have accepted a list of people from a man who held no charter over them. He believed to the end that the relics must be kept together until the Fire came to them. He lived as their keeper under guard and died of a winter fever early in 908. The Covenant inventory of 908 that records his body among the relics with the lamps unlit is accurate. **There is no mystery about his death**; the old three-death structure is retired.

**Consequence.** While he lived, the refuge councils governed in the name of a captive emperor whose own instrument had surrendered him, and no commander could raise a standard without rebelling against a living sovereign. His death released them, which is why the Mountain Rebellion begins in 908, and why Caedros's coronation argues continuity without legality.

**The basilica.** Idris's courier-borne ruling made the Ember a deposit kept where it stood and open to view, so the basilica of the Celestial Forum was never sealed or converted; the liturgy continued under guard for fifty-two years.

**Sarion the Pale.** A monk of a march monastery stoned during the Jubilee for saying *"The inked ones are coming. They walk with chains and offers of peace. The crown will burn in silence."* He had been a copying clerk in the chancery in 884 and had seen the accession re-seal finished with warlock fire. Whether anything beyond that knowledge informed his words is left open. His saying went onto a memorial wall after 957.

**The Borrowed Temple (IP-06, c. 905–907).** Per [[AF-R18 - Exile Behind the Borrowed Temple]]: an autonomous Pride Archmask; a provincial commander's chosen bargain; executed prisoners; constructed war-broods with no souls of their own; refugees denied water for refusing the acclamation; one genuine bounded commission of an unnamed redeemed saint, with loyal angels, preserving the prisoners' names at the last threshold while living people broke the acclaim engine and closed the route. The Exile survives elsewhere.

**The children of the old house (HC-N13, c. 907).** The Heir with Five Remembered Wings, whom devotees call the Saint, received one commission: testify that neither blood nor mutilation transfers a human soul, guard the children while the compact was destroyed and confront the office's current bearer. "No wound inherits the child."

## Interfaces

- **Pass 04.** Needs Halmenth 411 with Qedrel's communion concentrated in the northern dry-margin cities (Qabir among them), a doctrine denying that any institution can summon the Fire, and mutual refusal of Orthodox and Velian clergy to witness each other's charters. Vestian's dates follow the Direction (377, 385, 398).
- **Pass 06.** Idris (b. c. 871, r. 902–931) inherits the Ruling on Parties and its dissenters; he is sonless until 915 and never goes to Caleran (Pass 10D). His heir Ruzbeh refuses the instruments in 929 and is lodged in 932 (Pass 11D). The first great Surrender frame comes after 905. The Ember belonged to no instrument Idris's jurists could serve. Charters re-sealed in the Fire could not be voided in 905, so the occupation rewrote land through coerced consent. The Qahirids are a Ruzhan house. The Covenant formed its view that Atherian holy fire was spent during the Guttering, so refugee kindling in the 950s was a strategic surprise.

## What reader prose must not state

- That nothing changed in the Flame, or any of the three causes above as fact.
- That Oswin's relic or the Velos Standard was genuine.
- That the Captain's host was HC-N09, or anything about her judgement.
- That Corvenas III attempted the summons alone.
- That the Creditor shaped the Clause or profits from the cages.
- That Astaroth answered the Covenant engineers; reader prose reports the claim.
- That the prisoner in a particular engine is a named crusade captive.
- Any answer to the Thirty Sisters or the Veil's sender, or that the smoke and the dreams were the commission's work rather than what witnesses reported.
- Why Corvenas III's last call failed. His surrender, captivity and death in 908 are public fact and may be stated.
