# The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register

The sibling of [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]], stocking Commandment 3's third category. A **torque** explains an event through the world's physics; a **weird** refuses to explain at all — the register of things that happened, are attested, and have no answer, *including in this spine*. This is what real history supplies that invented history usually lacks: the Dancing Plague, the sweating sickness that simply stopped, the ghost lights over the trenches — events that fit no one's system, which is precisely why they feel true.

## The Rules of the Weird

1. **UNRESOLVED means unresolved.** No future document may explain a weird. Factions interpret; scholars argue; the spine stays silent. Exactly **three entries** below carry a sealed answer (marked ⊘), held against the one campaign that might someday need to pay one off. All others are open *even to us*.
2. **Weirds are texture, not keys.** Plots may approach, orbit, and be haunted by them; a plot that *resolves* one is a setting-defining event requiring a deliberate authorial decision, never a convenience.
3. **One per age, roughly.** Scarcity is the flavor. A history stuffed with anomalies reads as a magic system wearing a trench coat.
4. **Every weird gets contested in-world.** Each entry lists what the factions say, because the arguing is half the point.

## The Register

**The Glass** (before everything). The Great Glass Desert predates the Prophet's plagues, predates — by the Giants' own surviving records — the Giants' interest in going near it. Something turned three hundred leagues of land to glass before recorded history, and every civilization since has simply routed around the fact. *Factions:* the Church says judgment-before-judgment; the Covenant says the first breach of the first contract; the wind-speakers say the sky once touched the ground and regretted it. **UNRESOLVED.**

**The Year of Standing Stars** (Age of Scattering). Every culture's oldest calendar shares the same one-year discrepancy, patched differently by each: a year in the deep Scattering when, all the oldest records agree, **the stars did not move.** The Atherian astronomers built an entire intercalary theology around it; the Jirahar Sifra d'Masai skips it with the phrase *"and of that year, the road only"*; Folk king-lists count it as a winter with no name. *Factions:* everyone agrees it happened; no two agree what "it" was. **UNRESOLVED** — and the deep end of the register: the one entry that smells of time.

**The Sleepwalkers' Summer** (c. 200 BR). For one season, the people of three Atherian provinces walked in their sleep — nightly, harmlessly, and all, the wardens' records insist, *in the same direction*. The empire, being the empire, surveyed the trampled line and built a road on it (the **Via Somnia**, still in use, still oddly good footing). The walking stopped everywhere on the same morning. The road's terminus is an unremarkable hillside that has been excavated four times by three civilizations and found, four times, to contain dirt. *Factions:* the augurs claimed a summons withdrawn; the Church claims prefiguration of the pilgrim roads; Vorthara has a standing grant nobody accepts. **UNRESOLVED.**

**The Third Nail** (Redeemer era). Of the Four Nails fused into the Pillar of Concordance, the earliest inventories — three independent ones — list *three*. Every inventory after 219 AR lists four. No account of an arrival, a restoration, or a correction exists, and the relic courts of both churches, who agree on nothing, jointly decline to discuss it. The replicas worn across the world are always four. *Factions:* officially, a scribal error repeated thrice; unofficially, the single most avoided question in relic scholarship. **⊘ SEALED** *(a true answer exists in the spine's deepest drawer, unwritten here by design — decide it only if a story must).* 

**The Averted Legion** (Underground Centuries, c. 150 AR). A legion sent to burn a catacomb church found it empty — and their own names painted on the walls in fresh paint, each name beside a date. The legion disbanded within the month; the imperial record says mass desertion and stops there. The legend says the dates were the days each man later died, in order. The legend is unverifiable because deserters are hard to trace, which is, believers note, exactly what you'd expect. *Factions:* Church — mercy as warning; Sestaran historians — brilliant church counter-intelligence; the Hollow Flame, interestingly, forbids its cells from discussing it. **UNRESOLVED.**

**The Thirty Sisters** (the Fall, 905–906 AR). During the refugee winter, thirty ships arrived at Sestaran ports over eleven days — cargo intact, meals half-eaten, rudders lashed, sailing *in formation*, crewed by no one. No wreckage, no bodies, no claimants for most of the cargo. The hulls were sound; some were repainted and reused, and Sermoran sailors have tracked the "Sisters" through resales for four centuries — the Admiralty's underwriters will not, to this day, insure two of them in the same convoy. *Factions:* the Undertow-aware whisper wetward interception gone wrong (or right); Naris-devotees say the saint brought the ships home because ships are innocent; the underwriters say nothing and charge accordingly. **UNRESOLVED.**

**The Two Winters and the Apology** (1101–1103 AR). Two winters with no summer between — ash-grey skies, and no eruption in any record, Thalmyric volcanoes conspicuously quiet. Famine, flagellants, three failed heresies. Then the third year brought the finest harvest in a century, remembered as **the Apology** — a name whose theology no church endorses and every farmer uses. Border parishes' calendars still disagree by a season downstream of the chaos. *Factions:* Menhir-scholars mutter about a court in mourning; the Church counts it a chastisement-and-mercy; the Folk say the sky owed a debt and paid it. **UNRESOLVED.**

**The Walking of the Small** (c. 1180 AR). On one autumn morning, children across three duchies left their homes walking west toward Caleran — hundreds, calm, carrying bread. All were turned back at the watershed by (the accounts genuinely differ) a wall of birds, a preaching stranger, or nothing they could name. All came home — except forty. And the forty are the weird: no baptismal records, no household rolls, no graves — the documents say they never existed, while three duchies' worth of parents remained certain, to their deaths, that someone was missing. The Church ruled the whole event a fever-dream of grief after the local famine. The ruling has never once been read aloud without someone in the room objecting. **⊘ SEALED.**

**The Quiet Ninth** (1247 AR). The Grey Sweat did not fade. It **stopped** — everywhere, in the same week, no final wave, no tapering; the last recorded case in every surviving register falls within nine days of the Feast of Souls. And in that same week, the Ash-Walker processions disbanded — mid-march, everywhere at once, without instruction; asked why, the penitents gave versions of the same answer: *it was finished being needed.* The Church claims the penance was accepted. The Church has also never repeated the claim in formal doctrine, which scholars find more interesting than the claim. *Factions:* every power in the world would like credit; none has dared take it. **UNRESOLVED.**

**The Bells of the Split** (1015 AR). At the hour of the mutual anathemas — attested in letters from eleven towns along the watershed border, written before the news could have traveled — church bells rang unrung. Each side's chronicles record it as the *other* side's bells, grieving. The letters agree only that there were no ringers, and that the sound was wrong: bells struck, said one Brauvalis prior, *"from the inside."* *Factions:* omen of judgment (each side, against the other); a Sestaran acoustician's earthquake theory (beloved of people who weren't there). **UNRESOLVED.**

**The Held Breath** (recurring, the Boiling Sea). Once in a generation, give or take, the hurricane belt falls flat calm for a single day — hemisphere-wide, glassy, windless. Ships that dare it make impossible crossings. A handful, across the centuries, have returned insisting they sailed for a week on a sea one day wide; their logs agree with them and with nothing else. Castara's pilots keep a standing kit for "a Breath day" and will not discuss the return-lag with passengers. *Factions:* Thalassor's sleep, the sea holding its tongue for a funeral, patron arbitrage — pick your port. **UNRESOLVED.**

**The Bird-Marked Sites** (the Emerald Expanse). The old Viscaran survey charts mark certain Expanse sites with a bird glyph. No legend-key survives. Every bird-marked site yet visited has been empty — *sweptly* empty, veterans say: not abandoned but tidied, as if awaiting inspection. The University's artifact wing has quietly stopped assigning bird-marked digs, officially for budget reasons. **⊘ SEALED** *(ties permitted to the Age of Chains and Nythalum's returns — decide only when the New World arc needs its spine).* 

**Qarayel's Grave** (1303 AR). Listed here for completeness: the steppe's holiest secret is a weird by *policy* — a thousand horses, no returning riders, and a succession waiting on a discovery the Moon Horde forbids. The spine holds no location. Neither does anyone else. **UNRESOLVED**, and the Tarkhan intend to keep it that way.

## The Register's Own Rule

When a story needs mystery, *reach here before inventing new weirdness* — accumulation around the same dozen anomalies is how a world grows a mythology instead of a mess. And when scholarship, prophecy, or player ingenuity presses on an entry: the world may always argue, and the answer may always be *"the records disagree."* That sentence, used honestly, is the most ASOIAF tool in the kit.
