# Authorial Notes from Reader Articles — Long Frontier to Present

Safeguards and rulings moved out of public history articles for 400–1360 AR during the November 2026 editorial cleanup; each item keeps its original force.

## [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]

- The seizure of the Nine Wells in 558 cannot be rewritten as voluntary Tarkhan submission.
- The Velian border people were not merely a theological label pasted onto unchanged Thalmyrics.
- Frontier coexistence did not mean mixed towns agreed that all religions were true. Material accomplishment and moral judgement remain separate; neither golden-age nostalgia nor a story of uninterrupted oppression is sufficient for the frontier.
- The frontier town stories (the Patron port, the furnace refuge, the translation company, the Physician and Interpreter) belong to different communities and do not prove a single invisible mechanism underneath every frontier institution. The furnace refuge's revolt did not found a lasting metropolis.

## [[The Fall and the Long Reconquest]]

- None of the Veil (906), the Battle of the Shattered Engines (907), the Sestaran compact (908–909) or the Mountain Rebellion (908–921) was secretly caused by a single fellowship.
- No successful escape makes the Thirty-First Crew's abandonment of refugees an accident. The reader article had called the tale "separate from the unexplained Thirty Sisters"; [[The Historical Seed Garden]] instead leaves the crew's relation to the Sisters unresolved.
- The Ember's survival and Veridun's payment did not authenticate the conqueror's theology or guarantee every subsequent claim to Caleran.
- The Returners' Second Sack concerned one municipal atrocity, not the entire Reconquest, and is separate from the household story of the Bread Jar and from the otherworld Treasury of the Unreturned.
- The later histories of the General of the Glass Court and the Warden of the Last Redoubt belong to separate afterlives, not a hidden explanation of Caedros's war.

## [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]]

- Each church's accusation against the other (theophany as presumption; protocol as a cage for God) is partially correct, which is what makes the dispute permanent. The ruling on why the hymns kindle is held in [[Real-History Concordance]].
- Neither church's case can be dismissed as a private insult or a conspiracy which a single adventuring company might have prevented. The partition of Adalmarid lands did not leave an empty east waiting for a new unifier, and the faithful on either side could not inherit innocence simply by inheriting a rite.

## [[High Noon and the Qarath Disaster]]

- The Ransomed Standard was not another sacred fellowship. Later disagreement about command does not erase what its members actually did.

## Rewrites of 1225–1360 (Causality Pass 09, November 2026)

The reader articles for 1225–1360 were rewritten from [[Causality Pass - 09 The Road to 1360]]. The safeguards and truths below were kept out of their prose.

### [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|The Grey Sweat]]

- The fever's mortality was biological; rites changed who was sent, not who died. Thalmyria's smaller number of hauntings is a claim its own records make, and the authorial record agrees only where its lay singers were righteous.
- The walking dead were residue and unsent souls at thin places within the soul-road controls. The reader article's "Several duchies' records note" stays an attribution.
- The Ossuary Crown is the coherence-piece of a mature Ossuary Mind, a full-agency Astral person, not the dead and not a collective soul (GP-IV-02). Its voices are accurate copies. Its 1360 custody is split among a Sanctum reliquary, an abbey claim and a Patron's suit.
- Gerlind's sanctity was genuine; the closings at her singing were truthful naming and sanctity together, owned by no rite. The Saint-Leon apparition of 1285 was staged by Quarne's office.
- The Quiet Ninth is told and not explained. See [[The Grey Sweat - Authorial Truth]].

### [[Freeflame Reformation]] and [[The Korrheim Accord]]

- An unsung assurance letter is a lien that Patrons, the Undertow and infernal brokers can press against a named soul's passage; no letter ever touched judgement. Reader prose says only what Freeflame preachers and Lantern investigators claimed about dead paper.
- The woman's voice that joined Ansgar's night singing at Brenholt stays a report.
- Oaths on the Preacher's Water do hold; why they hold is deliberately unresolved, even in the authorial record, as is Ansgar's sainthood (the Psalter is a held proof). The 1342 water-test file stays sealed. No reader article states why the oaths hold.

### [[Qarayel and the Black Wind]] and [[Historical Spine of the Tarkhan]]

- Gavrin's blessing was a genuine kindling answering the singer's righteousness and purpose; the grace persists in the black colt's line and thins by generation. He was killed by the Thalmyric envoy under a sealed order from Evandros's logothete. Reader prose keeps the three in-world accounts and does not rule.
- The grace-lines explain the Partition of the Herds, not Qarayel's grave, which remains a weird.

### [[Historical Spine of the Sestaran Concord]] and [[Sestara and the Wider World]]

- The Medori's Covenant ancestry and the Creditor connection stay unstated in public prose ([[Medori Family - Authorial Truth]]). The registers taken in 1289 are public; their use as instruments of the family's inherited contract craft is not.
- The Held Breath crossing of 1328 is told as an occurrence; its return-lag is reported, not explained.

### [[Challenges and Future Tensions]] and [[Present-Day Courts and Crowns (1360 AR)]]

- The emperor's flame-silences are undecided under the Forward Rule: kindling, grief or illness.
- Orinel's watered oil and the songs' bird of fire are both left standing.

## Bends of the road to 1360 (Causality Pass 10F and 11F, November 2026)

The Twin Cardinals, Alessior II, the Reformation, the Fiorenzian sequence, Meshalem and Qarayel's burial were bent away from their analogues in [[Causality Pass - 10F Bending the Road to 1360]]. A second pass, [[Causality Pass - 11F Second Bend - The Road to 1360]], then broke the skeleton and the clock of the Reformation and Fiorenzian sequences and re-caused the Sweat's settlements, Sarnwell, Vessing, the Accord and the Partition of the Herds. The reader articles carry the 11F sequences; these truths and safeguards stay out of their prose. Where 10F and 11F disagree, 11F governs.

- **The Twin Cardinals.** Aimery of Corvel died unsung on his election night; the figure at the conclave garden wall in 1242 was his unsent soul, and it went when named and sung. Aubert's motives were both fear of a sealed conclave and wanting the chair. Ferry Rule funerals worked where the singers named the dead truthfully, as Gerlind's did; neither Aubert's authority nor Gelasin's confirmation changed any soul's passage. Reader prose keeps the two colleges' quarrel and does not rule on Aubert.
- **The Sweat's settlements.** The clean-road charters of the west and Virelia's bond of the clean village came from one rule, the Church's quarantine by rite, applied by whoever held the patronage of the parishes. Ash-Walkers carried ash and names; neither the ash nor their fasting carried any power.
- **Alessior II.** Sincere, ascetic and wrong: the Bull of the Sufficient Seal was false in every particular. He did not know Bartolo had bought his election until after it. The letters he gave away were liens like the ones Quarne sold. His own Sending was sung; what the night clerks saw at his signing desk in 1289 was residue at a thin place made in a room where hundreds of thousands of unsung promises had been sealed, and the blank letters found there were written by living clerks who had long signed in his hand. Reader prose reports the sightings and the Sanctum's denial.
- **Ansgar and Aldersbruck.** Ansgar drowned in the Blackwater in 1290, a Sending House chaplain in good standing, and never preached the notes in his margins. The hill legend that he came out by the adit is false, and the nameless friar at mine-head funerals is several men over seventy years. He was never tried. The legate Tiburce Vaudrey voided the book-test of 1299 to avoid either admitting that a dead chaplain's psalter had passed it or carrying the scandal to the Sanctum, and the second fee was his own device for making the unbought funerals pay for the Houses they had starved. Why oaths on the Preacher's Water hold remains unresolved, even here, as does Ansgar's sainthood; the Psalter is a held proof.
- **Irmel.** A tally-keeper who read a dead man's book aloud and never claimed more than that. No control rules on whether giving the dead to the Weiss sends them. The Unsworn's dead are not known to walk, and with no yard to watch nobody has been able to count.
- **The war from the mines.** The Ashenmen's hangings of collectors and Sending House chaplains and the Caldrian drowning of the Brenholt drifts (1314) are both atrocities and stay on the page. No reader article may make the drowned drifts a necessary act of war or the hangings a just sentence.
- **Sarnwell.** The eleven Ashenmen swore truthfully to what they saw. The Amaryah controls treat the apparition as genuine; this pass changed its witnesses and left that ruling where it was.
- **Ferro.** He believed the parts of his book that rested on Ansgar's Count, Gerlind and his own record of Aldersbruck, and never believed congregations could govern themselves without becoming cruel; he could not write the refutation because he could not answer the Count. He never converted. Reader prose keeps the three in-world readings (Freeflame, Lantern, Veridun) and does not choose.
- **Anselin of Saint-Leon.** A survivor of the 1285 riot whose mother died in it; the night-singers' priest. He died of a fever in an unheated Malverine cell in the winter of 1315, untried and by neglect; nobody ordered his death. No control decides whether the house he died in is the Closed House of the 1357 hearing. The Lantern's later investigative culture, including its dismissed investigators' abuses, belongs to the order, not to him.
- **The Arrears-levy and Rainald.** The levy was an honest attempt to pay a real debt, and Rainald's refusal was grief, pride and a need for iron together. Neither side of the princes' war inherited the right of it.
- **The Night Count.** A quiet churchyard shows that its dead were truthfully named and sung, by whichever rite; it shows nothing about doctrine. A walking yard can have other causes, among them thin places, staged hauntings and Idol feeding. The Schedule of Yards is a human instrument built on a real signal that its makers misread, and some of its entries were won on a staged or mistaken night. No control lists which.
- **The faces, the Pyre of Masks and Severin.** A grief-Mask of the Weeping Courts' kind began feeding through Fiorenzia's wax faces when the pamphlet named the city's unsung dead. The liens on those names gave it purchase and the families' grief fed it. It spoke each household's own suspicion back to it, which is why what the faces said about debts, bastards and wills was so often right; the dead did not testify through wax. The Pyre burned its doors and did not destroy it, and it was what witnesses saw standing in the fire. It then worked through paint. The master hanged in 1348 had been painting for it; whether he knew is left open for [[The Severin Panels]]. Severin was genuinely kindled in the first years of the Long Vigil, when singing the names discharged the liens and closed thin places. In 1349 he found the Mask's last face among the moulds and put it on deliberately, meaning to carry it out of the city in his own body, and it killed him. Whether that act was sanctity or pride, and whether the Mask left Fiorenzia with him, stays open. The Medori did not kill him. The face is buried with him outside the walls; no control decides what opening the grave would do.
- **Lauro and Ginevra Medori.** Lauro opened the vault from conscience and calculation together. Ginevra made sure only fair copies of names left it; the sealed letters stayed inside. The Long Vigil discharged every Fiorenzian lien the family held.
- **Vetti.** The *Mirror* is a factor's desk-book for lenders, and the printed dedication to Lauro is the Sermoran printer's. Vetti sold the register extracts to buy out his paper and because he hated the coat; the Medori's internal-discipline office had him drowned in 1346. The Vetti name survives among Sestaran factors (Cassian Vetti of the Caleran Credit Houses among them); no control fixes any living Vetti's descent from him.
- **Vessing.** The sexton, the carter and the mother each report honestly what they perceived. No control says why each heard another church's teaching, or whether the Weeping Courts' claim is confession, lie or infiltration.
- **The lodged prince.** Marzuq III's youngest brother is alive in the walled garden at Al-Qahur and is party to no plot at 1360. Lodging is a contract restraint and not soulbinding. No control decides whether the Deep Vault will lose his page.
- **Meshalem.** He was not the Mesakha. He believed he was until the gate stair. He turned back because he had begun to doubt and would not lay a doubting hand on the gate; nothing about the gate was tested or decided, and no control rules on what touching it would have done. The Sweepers' belief that his refusal proved him is an in-world reading.
- **Qarayel's burial and the Partition** are a weird. Nothing here, in the Tarkhan articles or in any control explains where the stallion went, why the mares followed, why the herds came home or why they came in four bands to four pastures. Faction readings, the Ash claim that wind-speakers cut the herds among them, stay faction readings.

## [[The Age of Held Proofs]]

- No side of the Reformation inherited moral legitimacy simply because an older institution had sinned.
- The Tarkhan partitions after 1303 are not a verdict upon the worth of the preceding society. Castara's expeditions do not establish contact with every remote culture already living beyond the known world's maps.
- Open local histories in 1360: the Company with No Charter has no completed destiny; the Nameless Castellan's dispute has no chosen authentic ruler. The rebuilt Garden's stories are things people tell, not proof of one predetermined ending: some customs preserve a genuine event, some legends combine several people, some feared relics are false, and useful tools may retain a duty nobody can fully explain.
- The 2026 rewrite removed the public link to the author-only [[Present-Day Political Board]] from the article's "Historical articles" list.
- Vessing's three testimonies each match one party's doctrine; the Weeping Courts' claim may be confession, lie or infiltration of something genuine. The Sanctum has not ruled, and no control rules.
- No living actor knows every event recorded in the histories. Investigators require independent kinds of evidence; a Saint, archive or copied ancestor cannot narrate the whole past on demand. The sources do not choose the next emperor, resolve every apparition or decide which present claim will prevail (see the Forward Rule in [[The Age of Held Proofs - Authorial Control]]).
