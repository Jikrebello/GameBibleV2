# Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)

This is the authoritative AR chronology. [[World Historical Atlas MOC]] expands its periods into connected histories, peoples, local traditions and otherworld sequences. [[Named Figures of the Known World]] provides the biographical register, while [[Real-History Concordance]] holds creator-facing historical analogues. The Great Split occurred 345 years before the present day.

## The Dating System

The common calendar dates from the execution and Resurrection of Isfir the Redeemer at Second Qiryath. **AR** means “After the Redeemer”. Earlier years are **BR**. The churches maintain the calendar. The Jirahar count from the Great Prophet's death, the Ahl-e-Ahd from Mujalib's revelation (541 AR), and Atherian old-style reckoning from the Unification around 350 BR. Folk traditions reckon by kings and winters.

**Present day: 1360 AR.**

*(Two registers govern this timeline's texture: [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]] — world-native consequences of analog events — and [[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]] — the deliberately unresolved. Entries below marked ✶ are weirds: attested, argued, and unexplained even in the spine.)*

## Mythic and Legendary Time (traditional dates)

- **The Deep Past (undatable):** Creation, the Great Rebellion, the fall of humanity, and the yolk exiles. The future Menhir and Idols deserted the defeated Adversary and culpably chose fusion. After the Menhir became morally blind Faewild sovereignties, the [[The Faewild Schism]] broke historical Trium and produced the recurring blocs later called the Circles. All of this predates the Giant imperial ages; in-world sources compress it into the Mono-Myth.
- **c. 3400–2300 BR — Age of Chains.** The Five Imperial Horizons and a many-state Giant political world enslave humanity.
- **c. 2400–2300 BR — The Closing Courts.** Thinning Giant blood makes every continuity work demand more labour, captives, grain and at last children. Ishkatar's dike crews are marched to tomb-works and the Serpent's Spine goes unmended; Xolotl's calendar thickens; Qianglong's Famine Caste empties the granaries into the recurrence vaults and the Unending Muster. [[Age of Chains]]
- **c. 2385–2335 BR — War of Stolen Victories.** Two Sarrakai river crowns fight over a prisoner-dug canal; the boat families who hold the sluices are killed after surrendering, and freed captives remain bound to the fields as tenants. [[G-69 - The Four Faces of One Victory]]
- **c. 2300–2260 BR — The Great Unbinding.** Prophetic signs, human revolts, Giant civil wars, bounded heavenly severance, and imperial collapse converge; the Great Prophet leads the principal liberated remnant into the Great Glass Desert. The plagues strike the works through which bodies were taken, and revolt begins where the takers came.
- **c. 2300–2260 BR — [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]].** Ozhur IV and Varakesh fight the War of Altitude while tether crews, camel houses, soldiers and captives cut the flying kingdom's ground system. Urzavad falls, Kel-Orun is grounded and Dazhur casts off a dependent ward; Sairat, Molqara and Yazuran pass beyond reliable Material history, and Dazhur vanishes within three years (the four lost cities; northern counts give three or four). As the sky roads fail, the valleys beneath them stretch, and Ozhur IV's requisitions march captives out along the routes.
- **c. 2300–1900 BR — The Years of Loose Water in the Sacred Basin.** Ishkatar's river control fails: unmended dikes break in a flood year and the river takes a lower bed. Orphaned human communities at Tenaret, Qemaret, Sakhret and smaller settlements preserve cultivation, burial and local water knowledge while canals break and successor households fight over the surviving works.
- **c. 2265–2165 BR — [[The Glass Sojourn]].** The Lord shelters and sustains the mixed Ark host while the Giant world collapses outside. Approximately a century of common law, worship, labor, language, marriage and burial forms one people. Commanded south around c. 2180 BR, the host finds the provision thinning over a decade; it ends company by company at the outward threshold during a crossing staged over several seasons. The Prophet dies near the threshold around c. 2165 BR, with rear companies still inside, as the last living direct witness to the Giant world.
- **c. 2255 BR — The sealing of the Hundred Wells.** Meya of Narash bargains at the dead anchor field above the wells; travellers who take the short roads in arrive back where they set out, and every crown-mother since walks the round of the wells at accession. [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]]
- **c. 2165–1900 BR — Age of Scattering and Orphaned Reassembly.** Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Nsizwa continue independent reassemblies outside the Ark; the two Ark fissions unfold alongside them; later contacts begin the Braided histories of other peoples. ✶ Somewhere in these centuries falls the **Year of Standing Stars**, which every calendar patches differently.
- **c. 2165–2130 BR — The Vacant Commission.** The Prophet leaves no universal successor. Bounded camp offices continue while the attempted common southern sanctuary fails through disputes over the seed reserve, which the drought-struck rear camps are eating and the southern companies need to plant, and over movement, land, punishment and command.
- **c. 2130–2050 BR — The First Fission.** Southern war-settlement leagues separate gradually from a continuing Near-Glass camp continuum. Families, offices and trade remain entangled across the division.
- **c. 2050–1900 BR — The Southern Seizures.** Proto-Atherian forts, river settlements and ports expand through massacre, slavery, hostages, displacement, treaty, adoption, intermarriage and local resistance. Several connected recovery networks form and break at different times; there is no single Atherian migration or universal collapse. Settlers who do not know the farming year take farming households with the fields; enslaved Orphaned farmers teach their captors to sow. Folk memory calls it the time "the fed came for the farmers".
- **c. 1980–1930 BR — [[The Siege and the Returnings]].** A mixed maritime coalition besieges High Harbour, approximately 1980–1960 BR, over the speaking image of its founder through which the harbour's tide pact was kept; the harbour's own craftspeople break the image in the fire. The sea's pursuit of everyone carrying the harbour's goods, changed households and separate return journeys continue into the following generation.
- **c. 1950–1750 BR — The Northern Fission.** Overlapping the late Scattering and early Hearths, water, distance, marriage, raid, trade and reciprocal Tarkhan contact divide the Near-Glass continuum into early Jirahar and Ahdari formations.

## Early Historic Ages

- **c. 1900–1400 BR — Age of Hearths and First Kings.**
- **c. 1900–1650 BR — Deepening of the [[Nine Wells of Tergai]].** Tergai houses, former tether workers and neighbouring camps link older sources through cisterns, ramps and repair yards. Tarkhan tenure and Ahdari use develop together without making the wells one people's exclusive creation.
- **c. 1880–1412 BR — Senareth crowns and the first Twin-Reed age.** Neshara Senareth begins the First Long Crown; the basin divides after c. 1580, and Khevar II reunites it in 1412 through war, marriage and the execution of rival claimants.
- **c. 1850–1725 BR — The Withheld Season.** Watershed halls between Tidewood and the Gale-Crown keep seed, stock and their dead sealed past the thaw; a High Hunt rides the seasonal roads; release cuts become southern custom. [[G-67 - The Marks of Necessary Release]]
- **c. 1700–850 BR — Formation of [[The Gateway Republic]].** Successive Ahdari migrations join established shore communities in the country of the later Qabir–Zanthir gateway; an unequal maritime civic association develops over subsequent generations.
- **c. 1700–1550 BR — The Beaten Animal's Road.** A seer hired to curse an Ahdari well-coalition is stopped on the road by what his beast saw first. [[The Ahdari before the Covenant]]
- **c. 1600 BR — [[War of the Empty Halls]].** A great Grobi offensive nearly destroys the settled Ättar ranges. Four ruling coalitions accept Mother Moon's firstborn bargain, creating the enduring Lion, Ox, Bear, and Wolf pact-kindreds.
- **c. 1540–1439 BR — Foundation of the [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]].** Meresha of Isethar develops the discipline and opens the Court of Unspent Lamps at her death. Her complete human soul remains bound to her body and private Egg White estate.
- **c. 1450–1325 BR — Northern Bronze-Road disruption and the Reclaiming.** Failed floods, drought, seizure and unpaid soldiers break long exchange while Ishkatari recovery works wake on the old processional roads and collect title-marked salvage metal; trunk roads fail first. Boran Tall-Hand's salvage-copper convoy ends at Daram Ford; a fevered Narashi crown-mother leaves the wells open for a season; iron becomes the northern road metal. Tarkhan and Ahdari communities rely increasingly on regional markets, wells and client service.
- **c. 1400–800 BR — Age of City-States.**
- **c. 1280 BR — Silent Month at Saqara.** Harun Savel violates a disarmed market and massacres merchants, pilgrims, slaves and children. Traffic moves to Mazar, whose market grows from the refugees and diverted caravans.
- **c. 1248–1076 BR — Yasharet rule in Qahur.** Talzara Yasharet establishes a mixed Delta dynasty at Qemaret. The period enlarges lower-river trade and White Measure houses; Khaseret opens the House of the Returning Cargo in 1126.
- **c. 1160 BR — [[The Refusal at Kelarion]].** Harbor rulers deny refuge during a killing storm; [[The Shoreless King]] manifests and is sealed beneath the drowned breakwaters.
- **c. 1100 BR — Killing beneath Ashal Stone.** Kor Ashal's ruler sacrifices a debtor's son after failed seasons, executes the child's mother when she breaks the altar and loses the traffic upon which the oasis depends.
- **c. 1076–858 BR — Aramen restoration and Qahuri empire.** Aramen of Sakhret destroys the Yasharet crown and reunites the basin through violent southern restoration. Tomb estates, campaigns and specialised craft towns reach their greatest scale. Tamekha Rasen's dead estate helps provoke the Tenaret tomb-workers' stoppage in 903. Second Houses lend grain and copper for the crown's campaigns and take fields in return. By c. 858 the crown can pay its southern garrisons only in land the dead already own; the garrisons go over to temple and military houses, the desert mines are abandoned, and the empire dissolves into three crowns and many estates.
- **c. 1010–940 BR — The Champion of Four Doors.** A besieged inner-gulf city enthrones a protector with four doors and four prices, wins its war and then collapses as the prices collide. [[Age of City-States]] · [[The Hunger Wars]]
- **c. 842–814 BR — The Single Radiance.** Ithren III suppresses competing cults and mortuary houses in favour of an exclusive royal sun cult. Queen Zaheret restores the older temples after his death; the political and bodily cost of the purges survives the restoration.
- **c. 836–798 BR — Merod II and the eastern Qahuri intervention.** Arqeshite expansion follows the grain and caravan roads. The murder of Prince Ashkur in 814, while escorting Radiance officials who had fled Zaheret's restoration back to their confiscated temple fields, gives Merod a dynastic grievance for the occupation of Qahuri approaches already under way.
- **c. 800–350 BR — Age of Stars.** Atherian synthesis.
- **743 BR — The Withholding begins.** In the siege's first winter the council of Qiryath drives foreign residents out to die between the lines; at the next appointed offering the smoke rises and the stones are hot. Its cause is unresolved.
- **742 BR — First Destruction of Qiryath.** Neshkar II's Arqeshite army sacks the Jirahar sanctuary-city and deports its leading households.
- **c. 740–730 BR — The Terys deportations.** Arqeshite coastal governors seize Compact ships, hold their crews to ransom and march the Atherian merchant quarter of Terys inland; Valeron II makes the grievance the ground of the eastern mandate.
- **c. 735–706 BR — Valeron II's Ortheian hegemony.** The upland king reorganises military service and compels several cities of the Celestial Compact to support a common eastern campaign.
- **c. 730 BR — First struck coin.** Ortheian silver is minted to pay Valeron II's standing infantry; the Successor courts spread coinage after 684.
- **706–684 BR — [[Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest|Eryndor III of Ortheia]].** Eryndor destroys Olyssar, crosses the eastern sea and joins Arezan Tiravan against Arqesh. His campaigns include the siege of Terys, divided reception in Qahur and an army refusal on the farther roads. He dies at Arqesh in 684 during a White Measure rite he had consented to, performed by masters from Neresh of Qemaret and paid for by Serevan.
- **690 BR — Fall of Arqesh.** Arezan's Vashari coalition, Eryndor's army, rebel governors, Nemeru crews and forced workers converge upon the Ubar capital.
- **688 BR — Death of Kassur III.** The Arqeshite king is killed in flight; Eryndor receives the body and the portable royal images and keeps the images as regalia.
- **684–c. 650 BR — [[Wars of the Inheritance]].** Eryndor's death divides the conquests among Arezan, Demeran Kavos, Serevan of Delkos, Neresh of Qemaret and farther eastern commanders. Within months a voice in Eryndor's tomb at Delkos claims to be the living, heirless king and promises the same provinces twice; the heirs, made worthless, are murdered. Around 657 the Qemaret masters testify that the deathbed rite failed, and around 655 Serevan's grandson walls the corpse and images into the tomb.
- **684 BR — Decree of Roads and Houses.** Arezan permits selected deported communities and sacred objects to return under Vasharan supremacy.
- **c. 680–672 BR — The Olyssar seat.** Caleran buys ships from Eryndor's unpaid commanders, lands its own unpaid sailors on Olyssar's burned fields and seats them in the Celestial Compact as Olyssar. From about 672 Caleran holds two of the twelve seats.
- **c. 660 BR — Consecration of the Second Qiryath.** The rebuilt sanctuary opens while the inheritance wars continue. The consecration offering, made in the Vasharan king's name, smokes.
- **c. 660–646 BR — The Tammur Tables, first of [[The Gateway Wars]].** After three dry Aurin springs, Compact cities buy Gateway grain at Tammur, where Qarzeth sells only to buyers who eat at the Provider's festival. Caleran blockades the market; Aster Vey takes Tammur from Harbour Judge Qelmar Suthar in 649. The peace of 646 gives Caleran Tammur and Gateway Council House children as hostages, renewed every seven years. Qarzeth rebuilds its fleet in two seasons.
- **c. 654 BR — The cool stones.** The sign returns at Second Qiryath under an offering carried by Yaelith bat Coren's mixed households, after the severe faction's demand to expel them is refused. The Withholding ends.
- **c. 652–649 BR — Verion's unreckoned years.** Caleran strikes Verion's festival and contract days from the common calendar; Verion's crews will not sail, its ship-owning houses expel the ruling family, and the city is "restored to the sky" with magistrates confirmed at Caleran.
- **c. 612–601 BR — Zamaret's Bread.** Zamaret Ordan of Oresh feeds wavering Atherian towns with Gateway grain, and their own sealed stores come up short. Atherian armies are lost in 611 and 609. In **609** Dema Varin presides over the Lustration of the Field, the killing of Gateway captives and hostage children for Astaroth on Caleran's festival ground. In **c. 608** an allied inner-gulf city purges its registered guests and falls (the city of the turned gate). In **607** Celor Marath becomes the first Warden of the Red Field and in **606** holds the approaches below Thesra. Zamaret withdraws in 601; Tammur stays Atherian.
- **c. 600–540 BR — The Provider's plenty and the bill.** Qarzeth's granaries fill while its dependent towns' sealed stores empty; Ilamar's custodians record the short jars c. 551 and refuse to certify the sanctuary's grain. The [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth|Years of Three Collections]], 548–540, follow.
- **c. 536–531 BR — The Rope Camp stoppage.** Carriers of Belqara, Esharat, Hazreth and Sarqen stop carrying grain and children to the capital. Meleq Ordan breaks the camp with King Harzun of Hadrath and marries his daughter Sapha; the reprisals drive the towns to Caleran.
- **c. 526–520 BR — Destruction of Qarzeth.** The siege runs 525–520. Atherian forces end the sacrificial institution and rescue children, while committing murder, rape and enslavement during the sack. Ilya's list names the dead, the bronze face is broken, and the survivors' meal at Sarqen's landing becomes the spared towns' **Table Oath**. The central city is razed and salted with Gateway salt from Hazreth and Sarqen; Esharat, Belqara, Hazreth, Sarqen and other communities accepting the settlement retain their people.
- **c. 520–513 BR — [[The Erasure of Orthea]] and the loose years.** A valley district stripped by Caleran's siege levies kills its collectors; the league erases it and its witnesses, who include allied units marching home from Qarzeth. [[The Unwritten Worm]] manifests; during the loose years the war rolls of the sack lose their attributions. The archive valley is collapsed on the Worm c. 513 and the Compact's rolls are rebuilt at Caleran.
- **520–518 BR — Marelith's captains buy and resell rescued children.** Many go on to Esharat's ransom funds; Ilya curses the captains by name.
- **c. 520–400 BR — [[The Spared Towns and the Queen's Country|Royal recovery among the spared towns]].** Surviving cultivation and ports support rebuilding. Nerethan of Delkos arrives in 499, marries Hadrima of Esharat in 497, and establishes the household from which Arenes obtains hereditary recognition in 457 after eating at the unranked table and swearing the Table Oath. Sarqen, the enlarged Second Harbour, becomes the royal seat.
- **514–491 BR — [[The Eastern Sea Wars]].** Governor Mazdan Aresh revives an old Delkos grant to manipulate Tasar's succession for ships and hostages; Tasar's revolt draws Atherian fleets into war with Xarun I of Vashar. Vasharan armies retake the coast, but defeat at the Narrows of Ilyessa ends their attempt to impose lasting rule upon the Atherian leagues.
- **c. 505–498 BR — The Provider house at Vorthara.** Qarzethi exiles open a house of the Provider; it starves when the Pale Hand refuses it bodies.
- **497–469 BR — The League of the Eastern Oar.** Its refuge custom comes from Kelarion's Roll. After 491 Caleran's assessors fix the members' ship money; c. 480 Pyrassan grain hulls are refused at Velathene's chain in a gale.
- **431 BR — The Ended Acclamation.** Orvaz I of Vashar is struck down at the Kavresh harvest audience as the crowd hails him as maker of the grain; the terrace mirrors of the royal cult stay dark for a generation.

## The last republican generations

- **489–486 BR — [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic|The Noon Sighting]].** Eshren Damar's bill for the Ilyessa veterans is blocked by the augurs' Long Month (489). At Sarven in 488 the noon shadows show the old public-land lines, recorded in the Noon Roll; the public harvest rots that autumn. Damar is killed in a bread crowd in 487; the Licensed Sighting of 486 gives the augurs sole right to seek or record a sign over public things.
- **469–461 BR — The war of the closed chains.** Velathene and the shipless allies refuse ship money. In the gale of 466 Pyrassa closes its chain on Caleran's order and the King beneath the Breakwater partly rises until its wardens cut the chain; Vespen Harod accepts the Red Field. The Chain Peace of 461 brings unequal citizenship, pulls down Velathene's walls, makes refuge law for any vessel in distress and lays a copy of the Roll at Ilamar.
- **466–c. 441 BR — Harod's Wardenship.** Harod governs from a tent on the muster ground, brings troops into Caleran in 448 and confiscates opponents' property, and dies in office of fever around 441.
- **c. 414–397 BR — Marovan Veyr's eastern commands.** Port settlements and provincial clients establish the older commander's following.
- **399 BR — The Spire Pursuit.** Lethren Ostar sells the captured rebels of a supply-corridor revolt north to the well trade; fugitives hunted at Vorthara are sent to the Olveth workings, and refuge households swear the Kept Doors.
- **c. 396–391 BR — The first partnership.** Casvilar, Veyr and Ostar cooperate through existing offices and personal relationships, without a collective public commission. Casvilar's daughter Calvena marries Veyr in 386.
- **c. 390–379 BR — [[The Southern Campaigns of Teren Casvilar]].** Conquest, alliance, enslavement and organised resistance reshape particular northern Folk districts. Kellovar falls in 383; surrendered fighters are mutilated at Darneth in 380. Darneth's siege frame breaks under the Red Star's test, and Casvilar claims it as heavenly confirmation.
- **381 BR — [[Lethren Ostar#The disaster at Rethmar|The northern expedition fails]].** Tavisha removes Rethmar's water stores from the line of advance and Kharuz divides Ostar's columns. Pel dies before his father. Northern forces release sick captives and water workers, ransom officers and exchange, admit or enslave others.
- **379–377 BR — The Noon Roll lands.** Casvilar's veterans claim public land marked on the Noon Roll; the leaseholding houses, Veyr's allies, demand that he lay down his command. In 377 Calvena publicly refuses her father's offer of a divorce from Veyr.
- **376–371 BR — The first civil war.** Casvilar accepts the Red Field and occupies Caleran; Velos (374) is indecisive and Veyr loses the sea when the Oar ports refuse him ship money. In 373 Veyr refuses Vardek's table at Sarqen and takes sanctuary at Ilamar; the spared towns rise for Samyra under the Table Oath and Casvilar backs them. Samyra's restoration is secured by 372; Veyr dies of fever at Ilamar in 371.
- **370 BR — Casvilar's assassination.** Challengers entered in Deceran Tolm's arms register kill him on the muster ground below the river stores before the contest opens. His designated heir Thalion, senior commander Velsar and surviving opponents contest his military and political inheritance.
- **367 BR — The public three-command government.** Thalion, Velsar and Varroth receive exceptional governing powers; proscriptions and confiscations finance their coalition. Varroth, as First Celebrant, rules the Red Field open and unclaimed.
- **363–359 BR — Victory and renewed division.** Republican armies fall at Darsil in 363; veteran settlement provokes a rebellion suppressed in 361. Evara's marriage to Velsar in 359 supports temporary cooperation.
- **358 BR — Velsar's north-eastern campaign** fails in the Avesh approaches against Vasharan relay riders and early snow; Merath is executed.
- **355–354 BR — Maritime victory and eastern royal claims.** In one linked 355 BR operation, Melvo destroys Ardel Veyr's independent ally [[Marelith]] and defeats Ardel's nearby main force among the central Thalassian anchorages. Ardel escapes east, where the Avesh command gives him up to Velsar's officers, and is executed in 354; Varroth loses military power. Velsar recognises Samyra's wider dynastic claims, increasing conflict with Thalion. Marelith, "the harbour without a chain", had sheltered proscribed families.
- **353–350 BR — [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession|The final succession war]].** The royal fleet is defeated outside Sarqen in 352. Thalion annexes the kingdom in 350; Velsar and Samyra die, Amesar is executed and the younger royal children enter Evara's custody. Corven Ilar's accusation against Samyra is answered with the Table Oath and the names of the hostages of 609.

## The Atherian Imperial Age

- **c. 350 BR–0 — [[Historical Atlas of the Atherian Age of Glory|Age of Glory]].**
- **c. 350 BR — Unification of Atheria.** [[Aurelius Thalion]] secures Caleran and concentrates supreme command, standing military pay, provincial appointments and taxation in the imperial settlement. Thalion stands unchallenged on the muster ground; the augurs declare the Red Field closed into his command, and Astaroth's priesthood takes first place in ceremonies of victory and command.
- **350–318 BR — [[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]].** Demobilisation, provincial appointments, regular assessments and adoption of Meren turn the founding victory into a continuing government. In 326 a treasury hall blessed by the Red Star's priests but never loaded falls on the heir Caelor (d. 324); from then on rune-trained wardens load every public work before the Red Star and break its Star's share, and the official who opens it stands under the arch.
- **318–247 BR — [[The House of Thalion]].** Meren I and his adopted successor Valeran I consolidate the empire. Quarrymen who strike rather than cut "stone for the Star to break" through the harvest are killed in 274. Olarion's informers swear their denunciations before the accession portrait. His purges and sudden death end the house.
- **247–246 BR — [[The Year of Four Eagles]].** Four claimants fight through the capital, river army, eastern fleet and western military districts. The accession portrait at Caleran answers in the Emperor's voice; Avaren Mott governs through it, and its assurance that no imperial granary is empty sends soldiers behind it into the poor districts. Samor Veloran covers it with a soldier's cloak and prevails. The Face stays veiled in the Common Sky's custody thereafter, unveiled last at each accession.
- **246–151 BR — [[The House of Veloran]].** Provincial rulers develop municipal government, roads and the eastern settlement. The Crown of Olveth responds in 219. ✶ The Sleepwalkers' Summer and the Via Somnia follow c. 200. The Arcelon campaign and road, built to answer Vashar's Dawn Road, follow c. 190–182; Raveth's river towns leave Vasharan canal duty and join by negotiation c. 160. A fever summer kills Orent II without an heir in 151.
- **151–61 BR — Serrat dynasty.** Elian I's negotiated accession pays the eastern cities with citizenship sworn before the emperor's image; Jirahar scribes swear with the copy veiled. The Raveth hunger from 119 preserves towns while transferring farms and labour to the imperial estate, and households whose debts were sworn before the image surrender their land unresisting. Under the late Serrats, Vashar's War of the Two Dawns lets Atheria take Qiryath under a charter after the Trial before the Stars, and Vashar takes the Dawn Portion of pilgrim dues. Velen I unveils the Face at his spectacles; his exempted festival banks drown workers and spectators.
- **c. 96–54 BR — [[The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door - Imperial History|The Low Door Companions]].** Six Lacaris servants rise from a riot into office through rescue, hostage-taking and a betrayed refuge. In 61 two of them supply Doran's march, and his officers adopt their low door.
- **61–24 BR — Doran and Othran I.** Doran re-veils the Face and has his officers enter their commands by a low door instead of swearing before the image; court marriage then establishes the last pre-Redeemer house. Isfir is born in 33 BR.
- **24 BR–0 — Othran II to the Redeemer boundary.** The empire remains politically intact; its response to the Redeemer continues in the following age.

## The Age of the Redeemer and the Conversion (33 BR–398 AR)

- **33 BR — Birth of Isfir** in a Jirahar tower town of the eastern provinces, under the Qiryath charter's protection of the towers.
- **3 BR–0 — The Ministry.** The Companions: thirteen called, one struck (see [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]]).
- **0 AR — The Binding and Resurrection at Second Qiryath.** Ezrahel's faction, keepers of the sanctuary copy of the Qiryath charter, refers the case to the Atherian governor, who orders execution outside the city. Isfir is buried before the Resting Day. Solana and other women find the tomb empty on the third morning. The appearances, Ascension and gift of the Holy Flame follow. See [[The Rise of the Redeemer]].
- **0 AR — The Silence of Acceptance.** Jirahar sacrifice continues at Qiryath, but its authenticated sign of divine acceptance never returns after the Resurrection.
- **0 AR — The Three Silent Days.** From the hour of the Binding to the third morning, no augur college in the empire records an answer from the Stars. Augury resumes on the fourth day, and the colleges seal the days. Severan lays down his staff and never reads again.
- **1–291 AR — The Underground Centuries.** The [[The Ember Underground|Ember Underground]], ✶ the Averted Legion (c. 150), the Fireless Trial and the Temple That Would Not Burn (c. 245–247). Under the Purge's edict, congregations record their signs as weather and good sense.
- **c. 15 AR — The conversion of Saint Aquilan the Turned** (born Sha'il bar Qoreth) on the road to Virelos.
- **c. 29–47 AR — The Companions' martyrdoms.** The Great Purge (c. 36–45) is a commission of the High Augurate to recover the augural books carried off by provincial colleges that followed Tyros. It burns Jareth before the Pillar of Concordance in 41, after leading into the Pillar the nails from the governor's case chest, and makes it a crime to publish a wonder the colleges have not entered. Virella dies in 47 at the hands of a commissioner whose warrant has lapsed.
- **c. 55 AR — The Dormition of Amaryah the Fire-Bearer** at Veridun Minor (see [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]).
- **61–68 AR — [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction|The Great Tower Revolt]].** The governor's officers take money from the sanctuary treasury on the pretext of arrears, and militants who teach that the sign will return when foreign rule ends raise the towers. Atheria destroys the sanctuary at Second Qiryath in 65 AR, ending Vashar's Dawn Portion with it, abolishes the city's charter government and installs a permanent garrison. The final rebel fortress falls in 68 AR. Many displaced Jirahar seek refuge outside direct Atherian rule.
- **78–171 AR — [[Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian - Emperors Wars and Revolts|Four reigns of municipal recovery]].** Tavian Dascen wins the succession war in 78. Daven Talar fights Vashar c. 94–103, a war fed by Vasharan claims to the lost Dawn Portion. Rovan Caelis secures shorter supply lines and southern market treaties. Neral Vardun campaigns against some southern Folk coalitions c. 150–162 and brings his son Marek into command.
- **171–c. 190 AR — Marek Vessan, civil war and the Sarqen rising.** Marek's guards kill him in 185. Rival provincial commands compete for control. Enslaved workers seize grain stores and interrupt shipping at Sarqen c. 186–189.
- **c. 214–225 AR — Second major war with Vashar in the Redeemer age.** The armies contest eastern road stations and tolls. Atheria transfers troops south to meet attacks there. A negotiated evacuation and exchange of captives end the campaign.
- **By the third century AR — Four principal regional sees.** Caleran coordinates relief and access to the capital, Aurelium trains readers and copies testimony, Halmenth serves the river and outer coast, and Sermora links the Elmaric ports. [[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization]]
- **c. 242–244 AR — The Kept Lamps.** A grave-lamp cult in the unpaid eastern garrisons feeds mutinies; augurs break its central image c. 243.
- **245–248 AR — The Edict of Public Sacrifice.** Emperor Tervan Marcellan requires witnessed offerings and written certificates across the empire in 245, and a licence for every household's graves and lamps; the Redeemer burial fraternities fall under it. Enforcement is strongest through 247. His successor suspends the general requirement in 248. Congregations relight from unlicensed flames, above all Amaryah's, hidden in a neighbour's bread oven at Veridun Minor, and the daughter-flame custom spreads.
- **248–288 AR — Provincial commands and court restoration.** Regional armies retain revenue for their own pay. From c. 281 the court reclaims taxes and appointments by negotiation, campaigns and reassignment of commands.
- **288–291 AR — The closing prosecutions.** Cassian's frontier army acclaims him in 288. The court holding Caleran has its augurs void his omens, seizes meeting places and the congregations' books of signs as counter-augury, and arrests recognised church officers. Provincial enforcement varies.
- **Winter 290 AR — [[The Forty Flames]].** Troops retake Ironvale's Ashforges after three days of rising. Forty prisoners refuse to renounce the Redeemer and die after three nights confined in an unlit furnace. A flame appears on the dead when it is opened. The common burial tally records thirty-nine, as one family buries its dead privately. **291:** before Cassian's officers, the guard who opened the furnace and Brask, the Ashforges' script-keeper, freed so his oath will stand, swear that the furnace was cold; the camp reads their account in the Aurin towns in 292.
- **292 AR — The Vision at the Stone Bridge of Velos and Legalisation.** **Cassian** sees the White Flame, wins the civil war and permits open worship. The Aurin at the bridge has never frozen since. The Caleran augurs enter the reach as a sign answered to the emperor, which is why Cassian keeps the colleges. He reigns to 316.
- **c. 296 AR — The Velos Concord.** A bridge chapel and a crown Warden of the Reach, accepted by the four sees in exchange for empire-wide restitution of confiscated communal property.
- **297–316 AR — [[Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR|Cassian's settlement]].** The four sees hear the Halmenth dispute in 297. Cassian grants the former Celestial Forum, with the Pillar of Concordance and the Nails, to Caleran's congregation (c. 300–310), and a daughter flame from Veridun Minor becomes the Eternal Ember. c. 309 he executes his eldest son and the High Augur of Astaroth over a sealed succession answer. In 316 he is baptised, dying, in the unfrozen reach at Velos: the **Cassian Wager**.
- **316–348 AR — [[The Reign of Sevrin]].** Cassian's younger son takes power after agreeing terms with the eastern command under his cousin Adran. Sevrin confirms restitution, refuses to transfer the wardenship at Velos, keeps the Star estates' supply contracts and protects southern frontier markets. In the 320s the Star college and the four sees dispute bones found at Veridun Minor and claimed for Calira. After his intended heir dies c. 343, Adran's son Astrion rises through the northern command and succeeds him in 348.
- **348–351 AR — [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion's restoration]].** Astrion restores temple stipends, puts a religious condition on public teaching, has the augurs re-enter the Velos reach as an answer of Hecatara and authorises a survey at Qiryath. In 350 the Red Star answers his public test of legitimacy once. In 351 he dies during the northern retreat; the field record gives him no last words.
- **351–352 AR — Savel Orren.** Astrion's brother **Tellan** saves the column with water trains under the bronze standard of the test, the Answered Standard. Savel, acclaimed in the field, restores church stipends and abdicates at the depot line in 352.
- **352–c. 366 AR — The Aster brothers.** Corlan holds Caleran and the west; Lucar the south and north; the east stays with Tellan. c. 357 the **Accord of the Two Standards** confirms the eastern command, its augurs and its standard. Corlan dies c. 366.
- **366–377 AR — Lucar alone.** The hard winters of 369–371 and Grobi raiding drive Folk households to the fort line; Lucar admits them in 371–372; his officers sell dependants and his chaplains seize the households' ford-tokens. War from 372. In 377 Lucar's army crosses the ford below Kellovar unpaid and breaks in the water; Lucar dies. Vestian secures the depots, the surviving army and recognition from Caleran.
- **377–398 AR — Emperor Vestian the Pious.** 377: the Velos chapel and its book pass to the bishops. By 379: the Folk settlement. 383: the **Dissolution** of the augur colleges at Caleran (the provinces by 384); the power to authenticate signs passes to the bishops. c. 383–384: the Council of Second Flame confirms the Forty. 383–384: the **War of the Two Standards**. 385: **the Profession**; the faith made compulsory. 385–c. 389: the **Second Silence**. c. 387: household rites banned. c. 389: the lamp taken out of the state oath. Vestian dies in 398.
- **377–398 AR — The Vestian Persecutions.** c. 389 the Raveth hearing house burned. c. 388–396 most remaining eastern towers razed or confiscated. c. 396 Qiryath cleared under its Empty Charter, with *before the Mesakha* in Vestian's hand. Existing diaspora communities absorb a second great displacement. [[Vestian Persecutions and the Long Diaspora]]

## The Vestianic Regency and the Decadence (398–540 AR)

- **398–447 AR — The Vestianic regency.** Vestian's son reigns through the court bishops and paymasters who made his father. Relic estates buy freedom from tax and audit, and provincial wealth moves into them. The palace's rites begin to fail while frontier rites go on kindling: the **Guttering**, as Thalmyric refuge chroniclers later name it. Eclessian historians call 398–905 the Unexamined Centuries. (See [[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]].)
- **c. 404 AR — The Voice Beside the Binding.** A reliquary voice certified by a bishop's readers names enemies of the state until, the prison chronicles claim, the clerk it imitated breaks it. [[HC-N10 - Voice Beside the Binding]]
- **411 AR — The Council of Halmenth.** "Fire-Bearer" affirmed; Bishop Nasriel of Qabir's "Man-Bearer" party, rooted in the gateway congregations that named the Second Silence and lost their sworn accounts at Raveth, condemned — his communion flees north and survives as the [[The Velian Church|Velian Church]].
- **446 AR — The drowned heir.** The emperor's only son and heir drowns when a harbour barge overturns at the Aurin mouth; the emperor dies in 447.
- **447–452 AR — The succession war.** The southern fort-line army defeats the Aurin fleet and the port councils. Its commander, first of the **Saldane** house, is acclaimed by the great sees and pays them in further immunities. Caleran hires its first Sestaran Shell-fire cohorts.
- **452–514 AR — The Saldane years of the Decadence,** which Aurelium's historians reckon 398–514. Ättar sea-raids on Kedrava; Folk pressure on the southern forts; Sestaran cities revive pre-imperial charters, Vardessa's the famous case, and sell ships and warlock contracts to the court in place of tax.
- **514–523 AR — The Ubar Toll War.** **Teomar Saldane** (r. 509–531) fights Vashar for the Ubar tolls and takes the western stations of the Avesh corridor, which he cannot afford to hold. Both crowns stop the stipends of the Ahdari well-houses, and the well-country falls into feud.

## Revelation and the First Unsealing (541–612 AR)

- **541 AR — The Revelation of Mujalib.** Tribal unification 545–567; Al-Qahur founded.
- **546–552 AR — The Thin Floods.** The Basin flood fails five summers in seven. The **yoke-sickness** (548–551) kills oxen and mules from the Basin to the Atherian north; camels are spared. The Saldane court keeps Caleran's bread dole by requisitioning northern stores.
- **c. 549 AR — The Bread of the Bound.** The 4,444 devils dig the oasis and channels at Al-Qahur through the famine; grain goes to those who seal their names into the Covenant.
- **555–560 AR — The end of Vashar.** With the Dawn Road's relay stations emptied by famine, Covenant jurists declare the last King at Dawn forsworn. Road commanders and the horse-lords of Ruzhan seal with the Covenant for bread; resisting highland valleys are reduced; the last king is pensioned at the oasis.
- **558 AR — [[Nine Wells of Tergai|Seizure of the Nine Wells]].** In the worst year of the dry-country drought that outlasted the Thin Floods, Covenant forces violently seize ancestral Well-Tarkhan water grounds. Later tradition recasts the conquest as voluntary submission.
- **568–612 AR — WAVE ONE: The First Unsealing.** **Qatada Sayf-al-Ahd** conquers the northern provinces. Breach-courts void town charters sworn before the dissolved augur colleges, and half the north is "served, not fought"; towns that refuse are stormed.
- **572–601 AR — The Four Rightly-Sealed Caliphs.** The disputed fourth seal.
- **575 AR — The Dry Ford.** The northern field army leaves its walls to attack a breach-court's escort and is destroyed by Qatada.
- **587 AR — Leovar I.** The remnant field army and the frontier bishops depose the Saldane house. **Leovar I** (r. 587–624) begins the crusade of seals: hermit-bishops re-seal surviving charters before living witnesses, and no court can void a charter on which the Fire came.
- **c. 594–598 AR — First Crusade (defensive).** The western field line holds at ruinous cost while bishops re-seal the towns behind it; the rotation of bearers begins with those it broke.
- **597 AR — The Fourth Seal disputed.** Atherian towns begin pleading that every breach-court sits under a disputed seal. **598 AR — Uthar dismisses Qatada;** the field advance stops.
- **601 AR — Emirate of Zanthir established.** **603 AR — Bir Qassim.** **c. 610 AR — the Velian States** installed under the **Banu Mizan**; the Velian cities keep their pre-conquest charters in fireproof chests.
- **611 AR — The standoff at Tamsar.** A breach-court's ruling fails against a march town re-sealed in the Fire, a Caleran reliquary fails to kindle before both armies, and neither side gives battle.
- **612 AR — The Long Frontier Peace begins.** The Banu Mizan broker the instrument Covenant law calls the Suspension: no service, storm or re-seal along the line, and every disputed charter held "neither void nor in force".

## The Long Frontier and Renewed War (612–905 AR)

The Long Frontier proper is 612–884 AR; the later entries describe the renewed war which ends in the Fall.

- **612–872 AR — Coexistence and friction.** The kindled marches hold the imperial side under oaths each emperor renews in person before frontier witnesses. The Banu Mizan golden age: translation houses, water-courts, libraries. **Rab Yequtiel of Zanthir** (c. 748–831) writes the *Guide for the Wandering*.
- **c. 640s AR — Relic tests.** The treasury ties relic estates' immunities to their relics answering a test; the tests are rigged at Caleran.
- **c. 641–804 AR — The frontier lives.** The Clerk of a Hundred Wells, the Mason of the Ninth Bastion, the Chain-Bearer of Sarrat, the Bitter Reaper, the Reader Behind the Sealed Door and the Smith of the Refuge Bell.
- **653 AR — The eastern tax fleet founders** in a Thalassian squall. The eastern bishops keep the revenue "for one year" and never surrender it; Aurelium's register of answered fire follows.
- **681–683 AR — The Copper Lamp.** Leovar II's accession lamp is made to flare with Shell-fire; the unpaid warlock sells his brass fire-cup to a Covenant factor, beginning the Covenant's file of counterfeit fire.
- **709 AR — The Fever Week.** Leovar II and his heir die nine days apart. **709–713 AR — The War of the Two Oaths.** Corvenas rides the marches and renews their oaths in person; the Caleran claimant cannot leave the capital.
- **711 AR — Simeon's examination.** Bishop Simeon of Aurelium records the Velos Standard silent and holds the eastern oaths in trust. **713 AR — Corvenas I** (r. 713–746) confirms Aurelium's right of examination: Simeon's primacy.
- **c. 866 AR — The Captain of the Sacrificial Wind.** On the river marches a host led, by soldiers' account, by a drowned ferrywoman holds the road behind a retreating convoy, then withdraws when the army's commander, carrying Corvenas II's orders, demands the conquest of the Velian belt.
- **872 AR — The Banu Mizan emir dies,** leaving a child; Caleran's jurists call the regent's renewal of the Velian compacts void. **872–873 AR — The heartland harvest fails.**
- **874–878 AR — Second Crusade.** Corvenas II (r. 861–884) marches to "receive" the Velian cities, which shut their gates. **876 AR — Oronda:** the Velos Standard fails to kindle before both armies. The crusade collapses in 878; its prisoners go unransomed.
- **884 AR — The broken peace.** Qahir ibn Ruzbeh obtains a ruling that the crusade voided the Suspension. The march commanders force Corvenas II to abdicate; **Corvenas III** (r. 884–905) succeeds.
- **885 AR — Banu Mizan rule in the Velian belt ends.** Qahir's officers take the client courts; a Banu Mizan line keeps Zanthir as tributary emirs.
- **885–886 AR — The Ruling on Parties.** The college at Al-Qahur rules that the Clause of the Given binds parties, not powers.
- **c. 885–902 AR — The Qahirid Resurgence.** **Qahir ibn Ruzbeh** rebuilds the war machine: Ahdanite mass levies, capture companies, name-cage foundries, lesser Surrender engines to travel with the breach-court train. Qahir dies in 902.
- **902–905 AR — Idris's campaign.** Through the northwestern approaches and seaward supply line; the breach-court train, with its lesser Surrender engines, serves Caleran district by district before the great engines reach the walls.
- **903–904 AR — The Jubilee.** Corvenas III holds a year of rites inside Caleran; **Sarion the Pale** is stoned for preaching that "the inked ones are coming".

## The Fall and the Thalmyric Rise (905–962 AR)

- **905 AR — WAVE TWO: The Fall of Caleran.** The breach-court train reads Caleran's own charter, last re-sealed with warlock fire, on its walls quarter by quarter; a late Aurin flood holds the southern levies three weeks short of the city and the march commanders stand bound to their ground. **Sultan-Caliph Idris al-Fattah** takes the capital at twenty-six and seals the martyrs' precinct in the Celestial Forum as evidence for the final audit. Corvenas III's death is told three ways. The empire dies; refugees flee south to the volcanic mountains. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Invasion and the Fall.md|The Invasion and the Fall]]
- **905–957 AR — The Impounded Ember.** Soldiers fail to put the Eternal Ember out; Idris rules it neither holy nor his own and impounds it as a deposit held for the final audit, its keepers pledging their own names as surety that it will be neither divided, removed nor put out. Addressed to the audit and not to Idris, it is the one instrument of his that survives 931. The Reconquest finds it burning, ledgered to the day — the most famous invoice in history, which Veridun pays in full, discharging the deposit and the keepers' names. (See [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]].)
- **c. 905–907 AR — The Borrowed Temple.** A provincial commander's bargain with the Exile is broken by prisoners and refugees at a star sanctuary; the province's claim to succeed Atheria dies there.
- **c. 905–920 AR — [[First Catch and Grey Sister|The First Catch]].** The fallen empire's border studs scatter into the northeastern grass. Idris's clerks had listed the studs among the estates to be seized; their lay brothers and handlers drive them north instead, and the occupation stays an army of camels and foot. Grass-Tarkhan hunters and herders catch them and become, within three generations, the world's finest light cavalry. Grey Sister's line begins; the older western camel culture continues.
- **906 AR — The Veil over Veridun.** During the Qahirid advance, watchers see a woman's figure spreading a veil of pale fire above the city; the siege breaks in a night storm. Thalmyria's supreme feast of the Mother.
- **906–908 AR — Third Crusade.** The eastward push stopped at Caldria, whose fortress culture dates from the defence.
- **907 AR — [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)|Battle of the Shattered Engines]].** Caldria holds Crown Gate Pass while Vortharan warlocks destroy one Depth-Core and one Slave-Soul Covenant golem and cripple a second Depth-Core, ending the northern advance and weakening occupation forces later exposed at Caelmount.
- **907 AR — "No wound inherits the child."** Witnesses say the Heir with Five Remembered Wings (c. 781–826), whom devotees call the Saint, stands in an escape house's doorway and guards an old client house's children against a claimant from Hell while the house destroys its compact.
- **908–909 AR — The Sestaran defensive compact.** Fiorenzia, Sermora, Aversia, Vardessa, and Orsena seal mutual defence in 908; Vorthara adds its external seal in 909 while refusing domestic oversight.
- **908–921 AR — The Mountain Rebellion.** Caedros of house Aetherion; [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/70 History and Conflicts/The Battle of Caelmount.md|Caelmount/the Valley Ambush]] (917); Virelos secured (921).
- **c. 910–913 AR — The signal house.** An occupation governor's warlocks trap a Herald of heaven in a warded mountain signal house (devotional tradition). The failed rescue gives both armies the rule that a surrender names its protected people before property, and the horn players their three broken notes.
- **922 AR — The Coronation at Virelos.** **Caedros I the Unbowed**, first Thalmyric Emperor.
- **922–949 AR — [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The War of the Burning Banners.md|The War of the Burning Banners]].** Refuge leagues, monastery armies, and return claimants are brought under one imperial command.
- **929 AR — The heir in the cistern.** Ruzbeh ibn Idris, fourteen, drowns in a palace cistern at Iqashar. Idris sets the re-swearing muster for the spring of 932.
- **931–938 AR — The Unsworn Years (the Widowed Seals).** Idris dies in the autumn of 931 and every instrument naming him and his heir is widowed. Levies go home; southern garrisons find nobody to obey; the silt hounds of the northern wells slip their feeding names ("the winter the wells barked"); the Depth-Core engine later called **the Elder** kills nineteen in Iqashar's dye-yards until the feeder **Gulnar** gives its feeding name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir". **Sultan-Caliph Mahdar ibn Idris** (r. 931–979), eight at accession, rules through the Regency of the Bench to 940, which makes the throne the **Sublime Threshold**, witnessed at Al-Qahur, and lodges a rival cousin's name in the Vault — the first form of the Cull of Brothers. **Durayd ibn Sahl** swears the occupied south to himself as Amir of the Southern Threshold (931–957). [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]]
- **933 AR — Qazmarid's proclamation.** The lord of Qazmarid takes the title Caliph-Wasi, arguing that the Qahirid claim and the Fourth Seal itself were widowed. Qazmari stations toll Covenant convoys in the western strait.
- **938 AR — The Basin leases.** The Basin captains re-swear to the Threshold for estates on fixed-term leases and become Al-Qahur's military households.
- **c. 944 AR — The first great mercy tower,** a Surrender Engine, is finished at Iqashar by overdrawing the Chain Furnace.
- **948 AR — Durayd offers to sell the Ember** back for its account; Caedros I's court refuses to acknowledge Covenant custody.
- **950–962 AR — [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Reclamation of Caleran.md|The Caleran campaign]].** The refugee armies come down on the occupied capital while Iqashar's field army is held on its northern wells; their war-lamps begin to kindle reliably. **Caedros II the Road-Maker** secures the coast road and the immediate river districts by 962.
- **951 AR — The Long Reach.** Grass-Tarkhan riders on hymn-blessed Atherian horses, which do not shy from bound things, break the hound-guarded northern wells and water at Tergai; Iqashar's field army is held on the grass to 957. The **Horse War** peaks c. 975–1010; in the 990s the first mercy tower proclaims terms to empty pasture as **the Talking Hill**.
- **955–956 AR — The last Slave-Soul engine** is sent south by sea; Thalmyric war-lamps open its name-cage before Caleran's outer works.
- **957 AR — Caleran retaken.** A custodian's refusal of a release entry at a bound dockworker's funeral raises the occupied districts, and Amir Durayd surrenders the city in his own name, the protected named first; **Caedros I dies at the Pillar of Concordance** at the moment of victory; witnesses swear the Flame took the body. Canonised in Thalmyria within a decade; canonisation rejected by Eclessia's relic courts.
- **962–1052 AR — [[The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)|The Long Reconquest continues]].** Durayd's surrender leaves the other occupied districts as a dozen separately sworn amirates, taken one at a time. Province-by-province return, occupation settlements, military colonies, and competing land registers reshape the heartland. The Velian States and Zanthir remain unrecovered.

## Two Crowns (962–1104 AR)

- **962–979 AR — Caedros II re-endows the Church.** Estates return to the monasteries and court appointees fill the sees; the war-lamps that kindled reliably in the 950s kindle less often.
- **974 AR — The banner at Kaltfurt.** A march banner taken up by the miller's widow **Hedwen** kindles while she shields a refugee column from a Grass-Tarkhan raid; the commander on the bank, **Adalmar**, is acclaimed king of the eastern marches the next year.
- **975–1019 AR — Adalmar the Great** joins the eastern bishops' principalities by war, marriage and a single register.
- **979–984 AR — Ilarion Aetherion.** **983–984 AR — the Ashfall Omen:** Mount Askre erupts above Caelmount; a lung-fever follows; the Great Lamp of Veridun shrinks to a bead for forty days; Ilarion dies; the army acclaims **Leovar III**.
- **979–1029 AR — Mahdar's line at Iqashar.** Mahdar's son reigns to c. 1018 through the Horse War, followed by a short reign; the second mercy tower is finished c. 1000.
- **982–1008 AR — The Folk Wars.** The eastern registers grant abandoned imperial frontier land held by Folk seer-oath. **993 AR — the Drowning at Wolfsmere** (four thousand captive oath-keepers and seers drowned "to drown the oath with its keepers"; nine survive under the water and become the first Lakekeepers; a standing leftward thin space — "the Lake That Remembers"). **994 AR — the Standing of the Water:** the lake rises on a windless night and takes the Markish shore-fort; the southern advance never crosses the lake country again. **c. 1005 AR — Weylan Oath-Bent** accepts baptism; the **Peace of the Bent Oak** writes the lake-law (no bell, no boat, no iron) into the march charters. The wars are declared ended in 1008.
- **984–1011 AR — Leovar III the Lampbreaker; the First Breaking** (from 987). War-lamps, relic-braziers and icon-lamps broken, flame-monks exiled, the Great Lamp hooded in bronze; the Reconquest slows; burial lapses in reconquered valleys. The Lamp Controversy opens: may the uncreated Fire be housed in made vessels? **Ioan of Velia** writes the *Three Orations on the Housed Fire* in the Velian belt, under the old Banu Mizan compacts and Covenant protection.
- **1001–1002 AR — The Examination of the King; the Coronation of Adalmar.** The Sanctum's relic court records the Kaltfurt banner answering and credits the sign to the king; Hedwen's testimony is entered and struck through. **Lord Primarch Silvane II** crowns Adalmar **Emperor of the Atherians** in the Sanctum Divinitatis in 1002, declaring discharged the eastern oaths held "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful" since 711. The Two Emperors problem begins. (Eclessian apologists later backdate Anthe's regency to excuse it: "a lamp-breaker's widow and her infant.")
- **1003–1011 AR — The Pledged Custodian.** The Custodian of the Vault of Names pledges his own true name to an Iqashari lending house to ransom a son taken by Tarkhan riders; from 1006 the Covenant army cannot march, and cannot exploit the First Breaking. In 1011 he surrenders his name into the Vault's deepest custody, the ink of it fades from every page, the Vault burns the lending house, and every Custodian since is **Unnamed**.
- **1011–1019 AR — Empress-Regent Anthe** for the child **Leovar IV**. **1013 AR — Council of the Thousand Lamps** at Veridun: hidden lamps carried in from wells, ovens and walls, some reported still burning (the well-lights); the Great Lamp unhooded; Ioan's formula received; the vessels restored.
- **1015 AR — THE GREAT SPLIT.** **Bonifac III** demands acknowledgement of Simeon's primacy — Aurelium's right to examine the restored lamps, the Great Lamp first — and the Sanctum's trusteeship of the imperial title; **Cardinal Hubald** nails the anathema to the Great Lamp's stand with four nails; **Patriarch Makarios the Adamant** answers in kind. ✶ The Bells of the Split. See [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]].
- **1015–c. 1080 AR — The border after the anathemas.** The ferry riot on the Lissos (1022) and the Saint of the Two Ferries; the Judge Beneath the Black Eaves at Dvorna; the prohibition of conviction for "the future fact".
- **1019–1046 AR — Ludovar the Mild,** recorded silent at every examination; his sons buy rival examinations and counterfeit kindled banners. **c. 1040 AR — the siege of Grauwacht** and the Saint at the Enemy Gate.
- **1029–1052 AR — The Second Breaking (the Impounding).** **1029 —** Leovar IV's Master of Lamps forces the wayhouse lamp of Pelin's Bridge against the name-bound garrison of Meliton and is burned to death before both armies; every lamp but the Great Lamp and the Ember is sealed in crown lamp-houses. **1049 —** Leovar IV dies of a fall on the cathedral ice; **Empress Theodosia the Kindler** (r. 1049–1071).
- **1029–1068 AR — Sultan-Caliph Tamim the Auditor.** **1033 — the Audit of the South:** the amirates are declared claimed but not defended. **1041 — the Muster at the Wrong Well:** the last relief army disperses for want of water after a copyist's error. **1044 — Zanthir's seasons:** the emir starves the last amirates of grain and agrees with Veridun to pay tribute to each empire in alternate seasons.
- **1049 AR — Partition of Drossfeld.** Adalmar's grandsons draw lots: **Aldwin** (→ Aurelia and the imperial title), **Lotharic** (→ the eastern kingdom later joined to Virelia), **Gervin** (→ the middle march: Caldria, which had held the line since 906, and the lands that became Brauvalis, Veridancia, Korrheim, Ironvale and the Sylvan Marches). The wax of Gervin's lot is smudged along its eastern line.
- **1052 AR — The Rekindling.** The last southern amirate surrenders; occupation-born families who reject return law go north as the Covenant's **Southern Houses**, carrying Covenant deeds to their land. Theodosia opens the lamp-houses and has every lamp in the empire relit from one flame carried on foot from the Ember at Caleran; the **Feast of the Rekindling**.
- **1059 AR — The two houses of the Vault.** The Living Register stays at Iqashar under the Unnamed Custodian; the names of the dead and the Custodians' surrendered names go south to the Deep Vault at Al-Qahur.
- **1060–1067 AR — First Southern Crusade.** Folk captives sent to the middle-march silver mines.
- **1061 AR — The Choosing at Strazgorod.** Prince **Radomir I** tests four faiths; in Veridun's Ember-Cathedral the envoys' own lamp takes the Flame unbidden before witnesses of both churches ("We went to see their fire, and it came to see us"); Makarios refuses to let it leave; Radomir chooses the Sanctum, marries Lotharic's heiress, and their son rules **Virelia**. The Kindling Question becomes public.
- **1067 AR — Mazrakan chartered** at the end of Iqashar's paved road; Tarkhan companies hired on word-price, so Iqashar buys the horse power it could not beat.
- **1068–1102 AR — The purge of the lintels** in Virelia: Vesari hearth-houses purged, ancestral faces burned, a kin-name forbidden.
- **1080s AR — The third mercy tower** is finished at Iqashar under one of the brief reigns between Tamim and Khurzad.
- **1101–1103 AR — ✶ The Two Winters and the Apology.** **1102 —** **Ermbold the Idle**, king of Aurelia and last Adalring emperor, deposed at the winter diet of Drossfeld; Sermora hangs the chain-tender who lowered its harbour chain to famine ships. **1103 —** Virelia's Council of Sabres holds its first election; Veridun first offers to return the Virelian Lamp; the Apology harvest. In Covenant lands the Basin's grain fails and the Well of Seven Pressures is turned to preserving grain, idling the foundry for most of a decade; the Court of Breaches' **grey-sky ruling** suspends impossible instruments instead of voiding them.
- **1104 AR — The Crown of Embers passes into trust** at the Sanctum Divinitatis, "until the realm is worthy of one head." The reunification mandate acquires its legal object.

## High Noon and Long Decline (1104–1290 AR)

- **1110–1170 AR — The Compact of the Knockers** in Aversia's high mines.
- **1112–1142 AR — Sultan-Caliph Khurzad.** Enforces the Southern Houses' deeds with mercy towers and loses the War of the Southern Deeds to Caedros III.
- **1116–1124 AR — The revolt of the Heldenberg shafts.** Escaped mine captives break the counts' winter army through the Cold Way; **1124 — the Oath of the Shafts** founds Korrheim's first cantons. Their liberator, a slave courier, confesses c. 1137 to what the Cold Way did to the overseers sent through it, and dies in exile in 1146 (the Saint of Ashen Wings).
- **1118–1167 AR — Caedros III, "the Wall of the World."** Military districts settled on frontier land emptied by the Two Winters, each holding owing a lance and a lamp. **1126 —** the Covenant's Court of Breaches rules the districts held under breached surrender deeds. **1131–1139 — The War of the Southern Deeds:** two mercy towers proclaim property-for-surrender terms to settlers who would forfeit their land by surrendering, and one is lost; the district lamps kindle in defence; **1134 — Kolonna**, where the emperor's own lamp kindles; dysentery kills Khurzad's heir in the siege camp (1135); a Covenant flank never moves on the Day of Two Readings (1137); the Covenant ransoms its captured custodians (1139), refilling Caedros's treasury. Dies childless.
- **1142–1190 AR — Khurzad's son.** **1151 —** a commission to bring the Covenant's provincial codes into one book.
- **1158–1163 AR — The Great Southern Purge;** **1163 —** riders without breath go through the marcher camps after the boundary groves are cut, and the Purge ends.
- **1166 AR — The siege of Sermora;** the hanged chain-tender is seen above the harbour wall. The last of Aldwin's line dies; the Primarchs rule Aurelia directly.
- **1167 AR — The Unanswered Funeral.** Caedros III leaves his succession to the Fire; seven claimants' lamps stay dark; a counterfeiter is blinded on the cathedral steps; the lamp of **Despina of Kolonna**, a district widow, kindles in the nave. **1167–1169 — the War of the Seven Lamps.**
- **1169–1198 AR — Andrion, "the Widow's Emperor,"** acclaimed by the district armies.
- **c. 1171–1173 AR — The Threshing Floor.** The prince of Elenara's census seizes dependants to win a royal crown; a mortality runs the census roads beneath a raptor of white fire and stops at a bereaved household's threshing floor at Kremna. Elenara remains a duchy; "the floor beyond the count."
- **c. 1180 AR — ✶ The Walking of the Small.**
- **1183 / 1204 AR — The definitions concerning the Mother.** The Sanctum dogmatises the **Unkindled Conception** and the **Rising of the Mother**; Veridun rejects the Sanctum's authority to define either doctrine.
- **c. 1185–1214 AR — The Ransomed Standard,** a company formed to recover bodies and pledged prisoners from ground neither army will enter; its fortunes break at Qarath.
- **1190–1243 AR — Sultan-Caliph Suhrab the Lawbinder,** a younger son whose jurist's renunciation of the throne the Court of Breaches voids as coerced (1190), after the heir is killed when a stand collapses at the Mazrakan spring fair (1189). The Covenant's apex; the *Ledger of Ledgers*.
- **c. 1201 AR — The Woman Who Returned the Keys** breaks a Velian amir's siege of a district town and surrenders its keys rather than turn the rescue into a hereditary crown.
- **c. 1203 AR — A weeping Lady first reported at Bruckmoor;** the first pilgrims are parents of the Walking.
- **1209–1214 AR — Markellos.** **1212 —** the Court of Breaches offers district families Covenant deeds without levy duty (the Unburdened Deeds).
- **1214 AR — The Battle of Qarath's Well.** Drought, fouled outer wells and a sworn well whose guest-peace is cut into its curb; the drinkers sicken at dawn when they take up arms; Tarkhan companies on blessed lines break the army; **Nikandros**'s Kedravan contingents, who did not drink, withdraw in order; **Markellos the Captive** stays and is taken; ransomed for the Southern Deeds districts; blinded by the regent **Sergian** to "widow" the ransom; the Court of Breaches holds that an instrument sworn to the Throne cannot widow; he dies of the wound-fever. Thalmyria's deepest self-inflicted wound.
- **1214–1225 AR — The post-Qarath civil wars.** **1217 —** Sergian killed in the Crown Hall; Caldrian captains seize three watershed valleys. **c. 1220 —** the Peace Not Sold in Vlastrava.
- **1225–1263 AR — Nikandros I the Restorer**, of a cadet Aetherion branch in Kedrava, crowned at Virelos. **1227 — the Refusal of the Lamp:** asked for Eclessian soldiers, the Sanctum demands that the Great Lamp of Veridun be tried by its relic court; Nikandros refuses ("a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned") and fights on alone. **1238–1246 — the Open Gates:** Covenant garrisons whose oaths have lost their witnesses in the plague march home, and Nikandros recovers about half the lost districts. Present-day Caedros IV claims this line.
- **c. 1232–1247 AR — [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|The Grey Sweat]].** The great mortality: a third of eastern Eclessia, a fifth of its western territories, in fifteen years of waves. Tenantry expands across much of Eclessia while Virelia's second serfdom hardens: its magnates seal priestless villages and bind their survivors (**the bond of the clean village**). Ash-Walker penitents seed later quietism and shrine hunger. Remembered as *the years the ferry queued* — a soul-logistics catastrophe as much as a plague (see [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]]). In Thalmyria (from c. 1234) laity sing their own dead out where priests die. In Covenant lands it is **the Emptying of the Benches** (c. 1236–1243): deaths outrun the witnesses needed to release bound names, and the Vault's registers never balance again. ✶ Ends with the **Quiet Ninth** (1247): the Sweat stops everywhere in one week, and the Ash-Walkers disband mid-march, everywhere, unasked.
- **c. 1235–1341 AR — The Medori century.** **Bartolo the Elder** builds the bank by clearing the estates of the plague dead; the Medori buy a seat in Fiorenzia's Golden Book in 1238, when the Sweat has emptied a third of its houses, and from 1252 carry the payments of the living to the Sending Houses; two generations later **Lauro the Magnificent** pays for the merchant-humanist flowering (c. 1301–1341).
- **1240–1242 AR — The Schism of the Twin Cardinals**, closed by **Lord Primarch Gelasin II**.
- **c. 1240 AR — Suhrab lodges his most gifted son** on the Guard's evidence that the prince swore wavering garrisons to himself. **1243 — Suhrab's last book** lets a sealed register stand where no witness survives and names the Guard's officer bench a competent witness for the Threshold; Suhrab dies; **Tahmur** accedes before the Guard bench.
- **1243 AR — The False Lady of Bruckmoor exposed** as an Idol-feeding operation after forty years of pilgrimage — a Weeping Courts architecture that fattened, in its last decade, on Grey Sweat grief; the site burned and salted. The relic courts' founding trauma.
- **c. 1248–1279 AR — Saint Gerlind of Lindenfurt** sings the plague dead out for bread; tried and acquitted 1266; canonised 1284, when her face is printed on assurance letters.
- **1249 AR — The black colt.** Gavrin the Farrier, a Velian slave in a Grass-Tarkhan camp, blesses a dying colt of Grey Sister's line just as the old horse-grace is thinning; its get stand before bound things.
- **1249–1252 AR — The Pageless Prince.** A lodged Qahirid prince whose register page was lost in the plague walks free under the law and raises the Basin's military households; killed 1252. The Cull turns from the lodge to the knife.
- **1251–1263 AR — The Ossuary Crown** speaks in the voices of the plague dead and testifies in title suits; **1263 — the Ruling of the Hollow Mouth**: "the dead do not testify through things."
- **1252 AR — The Chancery of Sendings.** Gelasin II reorganises assurance letters into Sending Houses that sing named rites for named dead; the Medori carry the payments.
- **1258–1261 AR — The March of the Redeemer's Sword.** Lords with plague-emptied estates march into the deeper cold forest for captives and are broken by winter and the Grobi.
- **1261 AR — The Kurultai of the Long Grass.** **Qarayel, the Black Wind**, herd-master of the black colt's line, is acclaimed under the **Nine-Mane Standard**, braided from the manes of the colt's nine proven sons.
- **1263–1290 AR — Evandros the Sower**, resettling the recovered districts and the plague-emptied interior.
- **1265–1300 AR — The Black Wind Years.** The first long ride burns the great Velian fair (1265); Iqashar pays escort fees because its bound wardens cannot frighten the colt's get; Sestaran caravans pay the grass-toll and trade turns to the sea. **1268 — Marzuq I. 1279–1281 — a Thalmyric embassy tries to buy Gavrin; he is murdered in 1281.** By 1300 the grace has thinned and the payments stop.

## Reformation and the Present (1290–1360 AR)

- **1279–1352 AR — Doctor Lucian Ferro** systematises the movement: *The Order of the Open Flame*; the Freeflame ecclesiology ("the Lord's Fire keeps no landlord"); sheltered by **Duke Rainald of Ironvale**.
- **1281–1296 AR — Lord Primarch Alessior VI** (House Malvora, bought with Bartolo Medori's credit). Industrial expansion of **routing assurances** to fund wars and the rebuilding of the Sanctum. **1282 — Silvio Quarne Cardinal-Treasurer:** printed letters, rates by age, blank letters; **1285 — Saint-Leon burned** after a staged apparition; from **c. 1288** unsung letters sold to brokers as **dead paper**. **1296 — Alessior dies; the Medori take the Chancery's master registers as surety.**
- **1288–1291 AR — Meshalem the Twice-Turned** proclaims himself the Mesakha and converts under Marzuq I.
- **1290–1329 AR — Stavren the Patient**; keeps the peace on Sestaran credit and founds the war college at Vatrin c. 1300.
- **c. 1295 AR — Caedros IV Aetherion born.** The Nikandran restoration line continues through the soldier-prince later called Ash-Blood.
- **1296–1297 AR — Ansgar's Count** at Brenholt: more than ten letters sold for every rite sung, and the unsung dead still walking.
- **1297 AR — The [[Freeflame Reformation]] begins.** Ansgar of Brenholt posts the *Hundred Grievances* against the assurance trade.
- **1299 AR — Silvio Quarne dies in Vorthara**; his ledgers vanish.
- **1301 AR — Marzuq II**; Bartolo Medori dies.
- **1303 AR — Death of [[Qarayel and the Black Wind|Qarayel]]**, undefeated, unsucceeded; ✶ the secret grave. **1304 — the Partition of the Herds:** Qarayel's horses divided by grace-line; the White, Iron, Moon and Ash Hordes take their characters from what they inherit. Iqashar hires Iron Horde companies the same year.
- **1305 AR — The Synod of Aldersbruck.** Ansgar refuses to recant and is burned; ashes scattered in the River Weiss; **the Unburnt Psalter** vaulted and denied. Lucian Ferro is the Synod's notary; the young Rainald of Ironvale watches.
- **1306–1329 AR — The Ashenmen Risings.** Brenholt's miners and farmers swear in the Weiss to fight until the last false seal is burned; the oaths hold. **1329 — the Burning of the Seals:** Duke Rainald burns Ironvale's Sending letters at Brenholt, the oath is fulfilled and the bands become his army; the princes' Reform Wars follow.
- **c. 1310 AR — Covenant night raids** on Thalmyric chapel-forts with Iron Horde horses and devil-mantled riders.
- **1311 AR — The Spring at Sarnwell.** In a valley the Ashenmen had stripped of images, an apparition of the Mother to a crippled washerwoman; the healing spring passes every relic-court test.
- **c. 1312–1316 AR — Ferro reaches Ironvale** with his record of Aldersbruck and writes *The Order of the Open Flame*.
- **1318 AR — The Night of Seven Flames at Orinel.** Iron-mounted night riders attack; seven signal braziers fail one by one; Caedros Aetherion leads the dawn counterattack and becomes a frontier hero.
- **1319–1322 AR — The Crimson Pilgrimage.** Caedros campaigns on Sermoran and Medori loans, breaks contract engines, and reconsecrates shrines through the Velian frontier.
- **1324–1325 AR — The Siege of Zehrim.** Surrender accepted; captured contract engines and ledgers burned; the altar restored without executing the garrison.
- **1326 AR — The Court of Breaches rules** that Zehrim's people remain bound in the Living Register.
- **1327–1329 AR — Stavren's succession.** The summer flux kills Stavren's sons (1327); Stavren adopts Caedros (1328) and dies (1329).
- **1328 AR — The crossing of the *Naris's Hand*.** The Castaran salt-hauler, under the pilot Ottavia Sele, crosses the Boiling Sea on a day the hurricane belt falls flat calm (✶ the Held Breath) and brings back the Viscaran chart-case; the Viscaran storm-keel is rebuilt by c. 1331.
- **c. 1329 AR — Caedros IV, adopted by Stavren, crowned at Virelos.**
- **1330–1354 AR — Lord Primarch Celestin IX;** the **Council of Veridance** (1337–1352); assurance sales abolished; the five tests codified; the **Order of the Lantern** founded by **Father Ivo the Lamed**.
- **c. 1330s AR — Castara opens the Boiling Sea routes;** the New World rush, on the strength of the 1328 charts and the rebuilt storm-keel. **1332 AR — Castara joins the Sestaran compact as the seventh seal.**
- **1333–1334 AR — The First Ash Road Peace.** Castaran surveyors stake wells on Ash pasture for an inland road and the Ash banners burn their camps (1333); in 1334 the city begins annual payment for declared escorts and passage across Ash-Horde country.
- **1337 AR — The Great Arrears.** The Council of Veridance orders the old Church's unsung rites sung; the Lantern sings them from registers bought from the Medori.
- **1340–1341 AR — Falkendorf and the end of Rainald.** The Freeflame canton of Falkendorf brings Korrheim to the edge of civil war and Rainald's last son dies there (1340); **1341** — Rainald dies without heirs; the Grand Ministry chartered; Lauro Medori dies.
- **1342 AR — The Sanctum's water-test** of the Preacher's Water is inconclusive and sealed.
- **1343 AR — [[The Korrheim Accord]].** *Every roof under its lord's lamp* (the Sanctum canonists' later rendering: *whose the realm, his the rite*), drafted at Heldenheim and sworn with hands in the Weiss. It excludes the radical Unsworn, is silent on the reunification mandate, and satisfies no one.
- **1345–1346 AR — The Bank of the Dead.** A Sermoran pamphlet names Fiorenzian dead whose Sendings were never sung (1345); Fiorenzia expels the Medori (1346).
- **1346–1349 AR — Frate Severin of Fiorenzia:** the Pyre of Masks; hanged and burned. **Nicanor Vetti's** *Mirror for Sovereigns* circulates banned (written c. 1340).
- **1349 AR-present — Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III**, Qahirid suzerain at Iqashar. His layered empire claims Al-Qahur, is rejected by the Sealed-Blood counter-caliphate of Qazmarid, and receives contested tribute from autonomous Zanthir. Witnessed by the Guard bench after the Cull, and cracked.
- **1351 AR — Patriarch Sophron the Unsleeping** enthroned, residing at Caleran.
- **1352 AR — The Lady of the Sloe-Field at Vessing.** Three shepherd children; a figure of banked fire; irreconcilable testimonies touching the Kindling Question. **The Sanctum has not ruled.**
- **1354 AR — Lord Primarch Alistair III** elected; he inherits the Arrears, the Unburnt Psalter, the Vessing file and the Crown of Embers in one set of vaults.
- **1357 AR — Empress Vasilia of Vlastrava dies.** The emperor's prolonged flame-silences begin after her fortieth-day. The Closed House hearing against the Malverine Inquisitors opens the same year.
- **1358 AR — Prince Nikandros dies** in a skirmish with Iron-mounted raiders in the Velian marches; the succession becomes a three-way struggle between Alexane, Sophios, and adoption.
- **1360 AR — PRESENT DAY.** **Caedros IV Aetherion** reigns in Veridun (regnal names recur; he is the fourth Caedros, not the fourth emperor). Eclessia is split three ways — Sanctum loyalists, Accord-protected reformed duchies, and the 345-year schism with Veridun — making the reunification mandate almost self-parodying. Armed frontier peace with the Covenant. Sestara finances everyone. Grenzburg's crisis year.

## Sanity Checks

Fall to coronation: 17 years (905–922). Caedros I, born c. 885, is 23 when the Mountain Rebellion begins (908), 37 at Virelos (922) and 72 when he dies at the Pillar of Concordance on Caleran's surrender (957). Idris, born c. 879, is 23 at Qahir's death (902), 26 when Caleran falls and 52 at his own death (931); Caleran stays under occupation, and the Ember under Covenant ledger, for 52 years (905–957), the last 26 of them under Durayd (931–957).

The Split arrives 58 years after Caleran's recovery, 13 years after Adalmar's coronation, and 37 years before the Long Reconquest's concluding Rekindling (1052), which ends a 23-year Impounding (1029–1052). Adalmar's imperial crown lasts one century before Ermbold's deposition (1002–1102); the Crown of Embers has been in the Sanctum's trust for 256 years (1104–1360). The Court of Breaches' ruling of 1126 is enforced at Qarath's Well 88 years later (1214).

Vestian reigns 21 years (377–398) and makes the faith compulsory in his eighth (385); the Conversion age runs 106 years (292–398). Mujalib's revelation comes 364 years before Wave Two (541–905), and Wave One to Wave Two spans 337 years (568–905). The Long Frontier Peace holds 262 years before Corvenas II's crusade (612–874) and 272 years before Qahir's ruling that the crusade broke it (612–884).

In the late republic, Harod governs from his tent for 25 years (466–c. 441); Casvilar holds the Red Field for 6 years before his murder (376–370), and the Field stands open 20 years until Thalion closes it (370–c. 350). The Withholding at Qiryath lasts about 89 years (743–c. 654).

On the steppe, the black colt is foaled 54 years before Qarayel dies (1249–1303), and the Black Wind's payments run 35 years (1265–1300). Bruckmoor draws pilgrims for 40 years (c. 1203–1243). At present, Ansgar has been dead 55 years (1305), Ferro 8 years (1352), and the confessional map has been frozen by the Accord for 17 years (1343). Caedros IV, born c. 1295, was about 23 at Orinel (1318) and about 34 at his coronation (c. 1329), and is about 65.

## Local histories beneath the dated spine

The Atlas adds local events at the scale of settlements, households and campaigns without moving the controlling dates above. [[River Leagues and Sea-Kings of the Early World]], [[Towns and Wars of the Long Frontier]] and [[High Noon Provinces and the Qarath Aftermath]] develop the principal thin periods. [[Distant Lands and Otherworld Histories]] uses relative sequences where no AR correspondence is established.

The seventy-five [[Myths and Legendary Histories MOC|localised traditions]] are not seventy-five new datable historical certainties. Their articles distinguish real inherited practices from disputed stories. Six [[Unknown Histories - Authorial Accounts|hidden events]] have separate authorial accounts. None explains a protected Weird or changes a post-1360 outcome.
