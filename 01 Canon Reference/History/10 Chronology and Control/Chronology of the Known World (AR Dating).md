# Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)

This is the authoritative AR chronology. [[World Historical Atlas MOC]] expands its periods into connected histories, peoples, local traditions and otherworld sequences. [[Named Figures of the Known World]] provides the biographical register, while [[Real-History Concordance]] holds creator-facing historical analogues. The Great Split occurred 345 years before the present day.

## The Dating System

The common calendar dates from the execution and Resurrection of Isfir the Redeemer at Second Qiryath. **AR** means “After the Redeemer”. Earlier years are **BR**. The churches maintain the calendar. The Jirahar count from the Great Prophet's death, the Ahl-e-Ahd from Mujalib's revelation (541 AR), and Atherian old-style reckoning from the Unification around 350 BR. Folk traditions reckon by kings and winters.

**Present day: 1360 AR.**

*(Two registers govern this timeline's texture: [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]] — world-native consequences of analog events — and [[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]] — the deliberately unresolved. Entries below marked ✶ are weirds: attested, argued, and unexplained even in the spine.)*

## Mythic and Legendary Time (traditional dates)

- **The Deep Past (undatable):** Creation, the Great Rebellion, the fall of humanity, and the yolk exiles. The future Menhir and Idols deserted the defeated Adversary and culpably chose fusion. After the Menhir became morally blind Faewild sovereignties, the [[The Faewild Schism]] broke historical Trium and produced the recurring blocs later called the Circles. All of this predates the Giant imperial ages; in-world sources compress it into the Mono-Myth.
- **c. 3400–2300 BR — Age of Chains.** The Five Imperial Horizons and a many-state Giant political world enslave humanity.
- **c. 2400–2300 BR — The Closing Courts.** Thinning Giant blood makes every continuity work demand more labour, captives, grain and at last children. Ishkatar's dike crews are marched to tomb-works and the Serpent's Spine goes unmended; Xolotl's calendar thickens; Qianglong's Famine Caste empties the granaries into the recurrence vaults and the Unending Muster. [[Age of Chains]]
- **c. 2385–2335 BR — War of Stolen Victories.** Two Sarrakai river crowns fight over a prisoner-dug canal; the boat families who hold the sluices are killed after surrendering, and freed captives remain bound to the fields as tenants. [[G-69 - The Four Faces of One Victory]]
- **c. 2300–2260 BR — The Great Unbinding.** Prophetic signs, human revolts, Giant civil wars, bounded heavenly severance, and imperial collapse converge; the Great Prophet leads the principal liberated remnant into the Great Glass Desert. The plagues strike the works through which bodies were taken, and revolt begins where the takers came.
- **c. 2300–2260 BR — [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]].** Ozhur IV and Varakesh fight the War of Altitude while tether crews, camel houses, soldiers and captives cut the flying kingdom's ground system. Urzavad falls, Kel-Orun is grounded and Dazhur casts off a dependent ward; Sairat, Molqara and Yazuran pass beyond reliable Material history, and Dazhur vanishes within three years (the four lost cities; northern counts give three or four). As the sky roads fail, the valleys beneath them stretch, and Ozhur IV's requisitions march captives out along the routes.
- **c. 2300–1900 BR — The Years of Loose Water in the Sacred Basin.** Ishkatar's river control fails: unmended dikes break in a flood year and the river takes a lower bed. Orphaned human communities at Tenaret, Qemaret, Sakhret and smaller settlements preserve cultivation, burial and local water knowledge while canals break and successor households fight over the surviving works.
- **c. 2265–2165 BR — [[The Glass Sojourn]].** The Lord shelters and sustains the mixed Ark host while the Giant world collapses outside. Approximately a century of common law, worship, labor, language, marriage and burial forms one people. Its provision is water: springs rise in channels of the glass at the appointed stages and anchor camps, and gardens bear beside them in every season. Commanded south around c. 2180 BR, the host finds the springs thinning over a decade; the last channels run dry company by company at the outward threshold during a crossing staged over several seasons. The Prophet dies near the threshold around c. 2165 BR, with rear companies still inside, as the last living direct witness to the Giant world.
- **c. 2255 BR — The sealing of the Hundred Wells.** Meya of Narash bargains at the dead anchor field above the wells; travellers who take the short roads in arrive back where they set out, and every crown-mother since walks the round of the wells at accession. [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]]
- **c. 2165–1900 BR — Age of Scattering and Orphaned Reassembly.** Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Nsizwa continue independent reassemblies outside the Ark; the two Ark fissions unfold alongside them; later contacts begin the Braided histories of other peoples. ✶ Somewhere in these centuries falls the **Year of Standing Stars**, which every calendar patches differently.
- **c. 2165–2130 BR — The Vacant Commission.** The Prophet leaves no universal successor. Bounded camp offices continue while the attempted common southern sanctuary fails through disputes over the seed reserve, which the drought-struck rear camps are eating and the southern companies need to plant, and over movement, land, punishment and command.
- **c. 2130–2050 BR — The First Fission.** Southern war-settlement leagues separate gradually from a continuing Near-Glass camp continuum. Families, offices and trade remain entangled across the division.
- **c. 2050–1900 BR — The Southern Seizures.** Proto-Atherian forts, river settlements and ports expand through massacre, slavery, hostages, displacement, treaty, adoption, intermarriage and local resistance. Several connected recovery networks form and break at different times; there is no single Atherian migration or universal collapse. Settlers who do not know the farming year take farming households with the fields; enslaved Orphaned farmers teach their captors to sow. Folk memory calls it the time "the fed came for the farmers".
- **c. 1980–1930 BR — [[The Siege and the Returnings]].** A mixed maritime coalition besieges High Harbour, approximately 1980–1960 BR, over the speaking image of its founder through which the harbour's tide pact was kept. When the blockade stops the collection raids the harbour pays the tide from its own streets, until its craftspeople open the water gate and break the image in the fire. The sea then takes its due from whatever left the harbour as property, people held as goods included, and the Returnings follow hull by hull into the next generation: ships that carried nothing come home, the commander's fleet is lost, and the High Harbour women of one captain's bay make a new bargain in their own names.
- **c. 1950–1750 BR — The Northern Fission.** Overlapping the late Scattering and early Hearths, water, distance, marriage, raid, trade and reciprocal Tarkhan contact divide the Near-Glass continuum into early Jirahar and Ahdari formations.

## Early Historic Ages

- **c. 1900–1400 BR — Age of Hearths and First Kings.**
- **c. 1900–1650 BR — Deepening of the [[Nine Wells of Tergai]].** Tergai houses, former tether workers and neighbouring camps link older sources through cisterns, ramps and repair yards. Tarkhan tenure and Ahdari use develop together without making the wells one people's exclusive creation.
- **c. 1880–1412 BR — Senareth crowns and the first Twin-Reed age.** Neshara Senareth begins the First Long Crown; the basin divides three ways after c. 1580, the middle river being governed from the Senareth tombs in the names of their statues, the Answering Kings; Khevar II, a cadet raised among the tomb priests and named by three statues, reunites it in 1412 through marriage, treaty and the execution of rival claimants.
- **c. 1850–1725 BR — The Withheld Season.** Watershed halls between Tidewood and the Gale-Crown keep seed, stock and their dead sealed past the thaw; a High Hunt rides the seasonal roads; release cuts become southern custom. [[G-67 - The Marks of Necessary Release]]
- **c. 1700–850 BR — Formation of [[The Gateway Republic]].** Successive Ahdari migrations join established shore communities in the country of the later Qabir–Zanthir gateway; an unequal maritime civic association develops over subsequent generations.
- **c. 1700–1550 BR — The Beaten Animal's Road.** A seer hired to curse an Ahdari well-coalition is stopped on the road by what his beast saw first. [[The Ahdari before the Covenant]]
- **c. 1600 BR — [[War of the Empty Halls]].** A great Grobi offensive nearly destroys the settled Ättar ranges. Four ruling coalitions accept Mother Moon's firstborn bargain, creating the enduring Lion, Ox, Bear, and Wolf pact-kindreds.
- **c. 1540–1439 BR — Foundation of the [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]].** Meresha of Isethar develops the discipline and opens the Court of Unspent Lamps at her death. Her complete human soul remains bound to her body and private Egg White estate.
- **c. 1450–1325 BR — Northern Bronze-Road disruption and the Reclaiming.** Failed floods, drought, seizure and unpaid soldiers break long exchange while Ishkatari recovery works wake on the old processional roads and collect title-marked salvage metal; trunk roads fail first. Boran Tall-Hand's salvage-copper convoy ends at Daram Ford; a fevered Narashi crown-mother leaves the wells open for a season; iron becomes the northern road metal. Tarkhan and Ahdari communities rely increasingly on regional markets, wells and client service.
- **c. 1400–800 BR — Age of City-States.**
- **c. 1280 BR — Silent Month at Saqara.** Harun Savel violates a disarmed market and massacres merchants, pilgrims, slaves and children. Traffic moves to Mazar, whose market grows from the refugees and diverted caravans.
- **c. 1248–1076 BR — Yasharet rule in Qahur.** Talzara Yasharet establishes a mixed lower-fan dynasty at Qemaret. Its pilots and caravan masters carry Qahuri garrisons to the desert mines, the eastern caravan stages and the first gateway ports on White Measure credit; Khaseret opens the House of the Returning Cargo in 1126, and her stewards take the crown's grain fleet, store courts and gate tolls in pledge.
- **c. 1160 BR — [[The Refusal at Kelarion]].** Harbor rulers deny refuge during a killing storm; [[The Shoreless King]] manifests and is sealed beneath the drowned breakwaters.
- **c. 1100 BR — Killing beneath Ashal Stone.** Kor Ashal's ruler sacrifices a debtor's son after failed seasons, executes the child's mother when she breaks the altar and loses the traffic upon which the oasis depends.
- **c. 1076–858 BR — The Factor's crown and the Qahuri empire.** After two low floods (c. 1084) Iddar Yasharet defaults on the crown's pledge; Aramen of Sakhret, factor of the House of the Returning Cargo, pays the unpaid garrisons from its warehouses, takes Qemaret around 1076 and marries Iddar's sister, and Iddar is walled alive into the House's harbour vault. Sakhret's king-list begins a new house and Aramen's poets call the foreclosure the release of the southern reed; Qemaret's list records the same kings as Yasharet heirs ruling under a factor. The crown's first revenues go to the dead, and it widens the Yasharet conquests to keep paying them. Tomb estates, campaigns and specialised craft towns reach their greatest scale. Tamekha Rasen's dead estate helps provoke the Tenaret tomb-workers' stoppage in 903. Second Houses lend grain and copper for the crown's campaigns and take fields in return. By c. 858 the crown can pay its southern garrisons only in land the dead already own; the garrisons go over to temple and military houses, the desert mines are abandoned, and the empire dissolves into three crowns and many estates.
- **c. 1010–940 BR — The Champion of Four Doors.** A besieged inner-gulf city enthrones a protector with four doors and four prices, wins its war and then collapses as the prices collide. [[Age of City-States]] · [[The Hunger Wars]]
- **c. 953 BR — Yedran at the stair.** House Oram, a custodial house, has held Qiryath's guardianship and grain rooms for three generations, and the lower town has not forgiven the four women Zeran Oram hanged around 962. When Zeran falls with fever on the sanctuary stair, the altar wood-carrier Yedran bar Aqev catches the offering; the crowd reads the cool stones as his confirmation, and the council names him guardian while Zeran still lives. Around 940, after Zeran's death, his sons and the lower town fight for the basket on the stair. It reaches the altar in the hands of a dying lamp-filler, Hanel bar Sem, and the stones are cool. Hanel's widow Rava bat Horel asks the custodians aloud whom the stones answered. The bearer is chosen by lot thereafter, Yedran's command lapses and he founds no house. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/70 History and Conflicts/Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria.md|Historical Atlas of Qiryath before Atheria]]
- **c. 840–829 BR — The Single Radiance.** Hesren III, king of the middle crown from c. 842, digs the receiver's seal of a buried Giant estate out of the vaults under Meheret and sets its gilded procession on the Basin's tombs, whose metal still carries the estate's marks. Cleared fields go to his garrisons; custodians who hide marked plate are carried off with their children. The seal hangs on his son Deshren from the age of six, and the estate wastes the boy. Around 829 Deshren takes the White Measure rite; the Radiance stops at Meheret with the stripped metal and the allotment tallies sealed inside, and his stewards collect rent in a dead prince's name, from his father first.
- **c. 836–798 BR — Merod II and the eastern Qahuri intervention.** Arqeshite expansion follows the grain and caravan roads. Around 810 Prince Ashkur escorts the dead prince of Meheret's fled stewards to the sealed palace, goes in with three of them and comes out alone on the fourth morning with a tally naming House Ubar steward of the dead prince's fields east of the river; Merod occupies the eastern canal towns as his collector, widening an intervention already under way.
- **c. 829–817 BR — The wandering streams and the image on the stair.** Qiryath's guardian Seram ben Teth marries Tilqa bat Keresh of an Arqeshite river house. His levy strips the villages that fed the cistern house, the rite of Tovan's line lapses, and for four autumns the shelf's streams run astray. Around 826 soldiers sent for a witness-staff kill the wife and brother of the custodian Tobel bar Yamin, who escapes; an Arqeshite royal image stands on the sanctuary stair, the offering burns without smoke and the day is written with a blank. Ilbarun II's demand of 746 relies on that morning. Around 824 Tilqa, whose mothers descend from a shelf girl sold east when Gedran fell, goes down the old cistern and the streams answer her. Haviel of the Lower Stair then has her diviners drowned in the lower town's cistern, and the road judges unhouse him; around 817 six women lift the eastern gate for the families shut outside.
- **829–814 BR — The Noon Rent and the river crown.** Hesren III dies around 821 as his dead son's tenant, and the stewards at Meheret claim the middle crown for the dead prince. In 815 the boat households refuse the rent cargoes and the allotment farmers kill the stewards and the king's markers; Zaheret of Tenaret is crowned in 814 on a grain barge at Tenaret's quay and leaves the cleared fields with the soldier-farmers. Temples recover their estates over the following decades, often on White Measure credit.
- **c. 800–350 BR — Age of Stars.** Atherian synthesis.
- **743 BR — The Withholding begins.** In the siege's first winter the council of Qiryath declares the city's foreign residents unhoused, so that no household may feed them, and they die within the walls; at the next appointed offering the smoke rises and the stones are hot. Its cause is unresolved.
- **742 BR — First Destruction of Qiryath.** Ilbarun II's Arqeshite army sacks the Jirahar sanctuary-city and deports its leading households.
- **c. 740–730 BR — The Belyra deportations.** Arqeshite coastal governors seize Compact ships, hold their crews to ransom and march the Atherian merchant quarter of Belyra inland; Valeron II makes the grievance the ground of the eastern mandate.
- **c. 735–706 BR — Valeron II's Ortheian hegemony.** The upland king reorganises military service and compels several cities of the Celestial Compact to support a common eastern campaign.
- **c. 730 BR — First struck coin.** Ortheian silver is minted to pay Valeron II's standing infantry; the Successor courts spread coinage after 684.
- **706–684 BR — [[Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest|Eryndor III of Ortheia]].** Valeron II is killed at the pay table at Pelatha by his own infantry when Marenthe's withheld silver leaves the count short (706). Eryndor leaves the companies that killed his father in Ortheia under their speaker Demeran Kavos, crosses the eastern sea with newer men (702) and confirms by letter the home companies' sack of Marenthe (699–698). His fleet blockades Belyra from 697 and takes it in 692; he never sees it fall. He feeds his army in Qahur on a loan from a dead Qemaret ship investor's house (695) and joins Arezan Tiravan against Arqesh. In 687 Kassur's images, which have taken a strangled companion's voice, drive him from his own audience hall. In 687–686 a Root-Mind wood whose price he refused closes the road behind his army in the farther east. He dies in 684 aboard Serevan's flagship in the roads of Nemeru, waiting to sail home, during a White Measure rite he had consented to, performed by masters whom Neresh of Qemaret had brought up from the Basin and paid for by Serevan.
- **690 BR — Fall of Arqesh.** Kassur III cuts the rations of the capital's dredging gangs and deportee quarters to feed his soldiers, and the gangs open the gates to Arezan's Vashari coalition and Eryndor's army. Deported Belyra families in the Nemeru yards ferry Eryndor's soldiers; both armies sack the city and set the gangs back to work under guard.
- **688 BR — The images change voice.** Kassur III's portable images begin to answer in Eryndor's voice, and their bearers carry them to him. Within a year the images order one of Eryndor's officers to bow, the officer is executed for refusing, their bearers strangle a companion who mocks them, and the images speak in the dead man's voice. Kassur surrenders to Arezan at Durqesh and seals the Gate House's water books over to him; he dies a guarded guest at Kavresh around 676.
- **684–c. 650 BR — [[Wars of the Inheritance]].** Eryndor's death divides the conquests among Arezan, Demeran Kavos, Serevan of Delkos, Neresh of Qemaret and the farther-eastern garrisons cut off beyond the closed wood. The Belyra deportees' descendants who crew his flagship sail the body to Delkos. Within months a voice in Eryndor's tomb at Delkos claims to be the living, heirless king and promises the same provinces twice; the king's half-brother, acclaimed in Ortheia, is killed by Demeran Kavos as a usurper against a living king (683), and the agents Demeran sends after Parena and her seven-year-old daughter Mehrava are hanged at Durqesh. Around 670 Mehrava is married to Vahrad Tiravan, Arezan's younger son, and from their son Kavran I (r. 629–594) the Tiravan kings claim the Far Road through her. Around 657 the Qemaret masters testify that the deathbed rite failed, and around 655 Serevan's grandson walls the corpse and images into the tomb.
- **684 BR — Decree of Roads and Houses.** Arezan permits selected deported communities and sacred objects to return under Vasharan supremacy.
- **c. 680–672 BR — The Marenthe seat.** Caleran buys ships from Eryndor's unpaid commanders, lands its own unpaid sailors on Marenthe's burned fields and seats them in the Celestial Compact as Marenthe. From about 672 Caleran holds two of the twelve seats.
- **c. 660 BR — Consecration of the Second Qiryath.** The sanctuary, begun on Vasharan grants and finished by the city after the grants lapsed, opens while the inheritance wars continue. The consecration offering, made in the Vasharan king's name, smokes.
- **c. 660–646 BR — The Tammur Tables, first of [[The Gateway Wars]].** After three dry Aurin springs, Compact cities buy Gateway grain at Tammur, where Qarzeth sells only to buyers who eat at the Provider's festival. Caleran blockades the market; Aster Vey takes Tammur from Quay Elder Qedran Suthar in 649. The peace of 646 gives Caleran Tammur and Gateway Council House children as hostages, renewed every seven years. Qarzeth rebuilds its fleet in two seasons.
- **c. 654 BR — The cool stones.** The sign returns at Second Qiryath under an offering carried by Yaelith bat Coren's mixed households, after the severe faction's demand to expel them is refused. The Withholding ends.
- **c. 652–649 BR — Verion's unreckoned years.** Caleran strikes Verion's festival and contract days from the common calendar; Verion's crews will not sail, its ship-owning houses expel the ruling family, and the city is "restored to the sky" with magistrates confirmed at Caleran.
- **c. 612–601 BR — Tashqar's Bread.** Tashqar Ordan of Oresh lands near Erenost on his own account and feeds wavering Atherian towns with Gateway grain, and their own sealed stores come up short. Qarzeth's Council Houses disown him and deliver the hostage cohort due in **611** to prove it. He fights no battle; Atherian armies are lost to hunger and desertion in 611 and 609. In **609** Dema Varin presides over the Lustration of the Field, the killing of Gateway captives and of those hostage children for Astaroth on Caleran's festival ground, and in **608** Qarzeth enters the war. In **c. 608** an allied inner-gulf city purges its registered guests and falls (the city of the turned gate). In **607** Celor Marath becomes the first Warden of the Red Field and in **606** holds the approaches below Thesra. In **601** a crowd at Erenost kills Tashqar at the doors of its joint store. The peace restricts the Gateway fleet and renews no hostages; Tammur stays Atherian.
- **c. 600–540 BR — The Provider's plenty and the bill.** Qarzeth's granaries fill while its dependent towns' sealed stores empty; Ilamar's custodians record the short jars c. 551 and refuse to certify the sanctuary's grain. The [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth|Years of Three Collections]], 548–540, follow.
- **c. 536–531 BR — The Rope Camp stoppage.** Carriers of Belqara, Esharat, Hazreth and Sarqen stop carrying grain and children to the capital. In 531 the council swears to their terms. At the next festival the sanctuary's doors stay shut for want of the towns' names, and the council breaks its oath: Teshun Ordan's crews and the riders of King Harzun of Hadrath, whose daughter Sapha is Teshun's wife, take the collectors back into the towns. The camp's leaders and the council's own envoy Damen are killed, and the reprisals drive the towns to Caleran.
- **c. 528–526 BR — The eleven chests.** Mera Castren's policy of inspection carries Caleran's senate over Orsen Valeor's demand for destruction. Qarzeth gives up its arms, the children then held and, by vote of its assembly, the collectors' books. The sanctuary's doors will not open without the books; the hungry city kills the Quay Elders who sealed the surrender and begins the lists again. Caleran votes for war.
- **c. 526–520 BR — Destruction of Qarzeth.** The siege runs 525–520. Atherian forces end the sacrificial institution and rescue children, while committing murder, rape and enslavement during the sack. Ilya's list names the dead, the bronze face is broken, and the survivors' meal at Sarqen's landing becomes the spared towns' **Table Oath**. The central city is razed, and carriers from Hazreth and Sarqen salt the sanctuary and its sealed store compounds with Gateway salt from their own pans; Esharat, Belqara, Hazreth, Sarqen and other communities accepting the settlement retain their people.
- **c. 520–513 BR — [[The Erasure of Orthea]] and the loose years.** A valley district stripped by Caleran's siege levies kills its collectors; the league erases it and its witnesses, who include allied units marching home from Qarzeth. [[The Unwritten Worm]] manifests; during the loose years the war rolls of the sack lose their attributions. The archive valley is collapsed on the Worm c. 513 and the Compact's rolls are rebuilt at Caleran.
- **520–518 BR — Marelith's captains buy and resell rescued children.** Many go on to Esharat's ransom funds; Ilya curses the captains by name.
- **c. 520–400 BR — [[The Spared Towns and the Queen's Country|Royal recovery among the spared towns]].** Surviving cultivation and ports support rebuilding. Nerethan of Delkos arrives in 499, marries Hadrima of Esharat in 497, and establishes the household from which Arenes obtains hereditary recognition in 457 after eating at the unranked table and swearing the Table Oath. Sarqen, the enlarged Second Harbour, becomes the royal seat.
- **514–491 BR — [[The Eastern Sea Wars]].** Governor Mazdan Aresh revives an old Delkos grant to manipulate Tasar's succession for ships and hostages; Tasar's revolt draws hired Atherian hulls into war with Kazren I of Vashar, though the Caleran Senate refuses aid. Tasar's poorest quarter opens a gate for bread in 511, its rebel quarters are sent to keep the Dawn Road relay stations, and Velanthe's wardens admit the fugitive hulls under Kelarion's Roll. In 506 a fleet sent to seize Ilyessa's pilot families settles at its moorings; Kazren blames sorcery and hires the pilots. In 493, after he claims the sea in his title and anchors his granary fleet in the Ilyessa lee, the grain spoils in sound hulls, his subject crews desert to Velanthe's refuge fire and the League of the Eastern Oar takes the transports at anchor. Kazren dies of the flux before Velanthe in the winter of 492–491. Ardash Aresh burns lower Avesh to hold its granaries and sells the eighteen-year-old Orvaz I his road home for the western command by family right, and the army's return in 491 ends Vashar's attempt to impose lasting rule upon the Atherian leagues.
- **c. 505–498 BR — The Provider house at Vorthara.** Qarzethi exiles open a house of the Provider; it starves when the Pale Hand refuses it bodies.
- **497–469 BR — The League of the Eastern Oar.** Its refuge custom comes from Kelarion's Roll. In 493 Velanthe's wardens admit Kazren's deserting subject crews under the Roll, beyond the League's own custom, and the League fleet that takes the royal transports is half rowed by them. After 491 Caleran's assessors fix the members' ship money; c. 480 Pyrassan grain hulls are refused at Velanthe's chain in a gale.

## The last republican generations

- **489–486 BR — [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic|The Noon Sighting]].** The augurs refuse Eshren Damar a sighting day at the renewal of the public leases (489). At Sarven in 488 the noon shadows show the old public-land lines, recorded in the Noon Roll. The augurs insert the Long Month behind the sighting to put it outside the year, and the Ilyessa veterans occupy the land all the same. The harvest rots that autumn. In 487 retainers of the leaseholding houses kill several veterans in a bread crowd, and the houses buy the plots back for bread and seed. The Licensed Sighting of 486 gives the augurs sole right to seek or record a sign over public things. Damar keeps his own plot and works it bareheaded until his death about 457.
- **469–461 BR — The war of the closed chains.** Velanthe and the shipless allies refuse ship money. In the gale of 466 Pyrassa closes its chain on Caleran's order and the King beneath the Breakwater partly rises until its wardens cut the chain; Vespen Harod accepts the Red Field against the rebel captains, who never come to fight him. From that winter no harbour warden will raise a chain for Caleran, and the rebel cities keep the year by Irenai's count. The Chain Peace of 461, sworn on Ilamar, gives every harbour's chain to its own wardens, pulls down Velanthe's chain towers, fixes the ship money and leaves further levies to each port's vote, makes refuge law for any vessel in distress and lays a copy of the Roll at Ilamar.
- **466–c. 441 BR — Harod's Wardenship.** Harod lives in a tent on the muster ground because the captains he named are at Avesh. When the senate tries to replace him (452–449), the augurs count the nine senators who sealed the decree as contestants; he kills two on the muster ground and the other seven are forfeited as fled. He dies in office of fever around 441.
- **444 BR — The Ended Acclamation.** At the Kavresh harvest audience the crowd is signalled to hail Orvaz I of Vashar as maker of the grain, and nobody on the terrace can speak the praise, then or afterwards. The children taken for his dawn-house are sent home, and the terrace mirrors of the royal cult stay dark for a generation. Orvaz dies in 439.
- **c. 414–397 BR — Marovan Veyr's eastern commands.** Port settlements and provincial clients establish the older commander's following.
- **410 BR — The Spire Pursuit.** Enslaved workers rise along the corridor supplying Veyr's eastern war. Companies hired on Lethren Ostar's credit break the rear of their column below the pass to Vorthara; Ostar takes the captives for debt and sells them north to the well trade. Fugitives bought at Vorthara by his agents are sent to the Olveth workings, and refuge households swear the Kept Doors.
- **c. 396–391 BR — The first partnership.** Casvilar, Veyr and Ostar cooperate through existing offices and personal relationships, without a collective public commission. In **392** Ostar's son Pel leads a hired army to take possession of Rethmar's reservoirs, which Ostar has bought on paper ([[Lethren Ostar#The disaster at Rethmar|the disaster at Rethmar]]). Tavisha empties the cisterns and Kharuz divides the columns. Pel is captured and stays among the wells; northern forces release sick captives and water workers, ransom officers and exchange, admit or enslave others. Casvilar's daughter Selvena marries Veyr in 389.
- **c. 390–379 BR — [[The Southern Campaigns of Teren Casvilar]].** Conquest, alliance, enslavement and organised resistance reshape particular northern Folk districts. At the siege of Kellovar in **383** Casvilar accepts the Red Field against Drenoc. The people Drenoc turns out cross the ford below the hill on their household tokens; Drenoc goes into the ford to bargain for those without tokens and is not seen again. The hill falls and the Field cannot be closed. Surrendered ford guides are mutilated at Darneth in 380. Darneth's siege frame breaks under the Red Star's test, and Casvilar claims it as heavenly confirmation.
- **379–377 BR — The Noon Roll lands and the banker.** Casvilar's veterans claim public land marked on the Noon Roll. In 379 Ostar sues Casvilar for his loans; the Warden's augurs enter him as a contestant, he flees to Vorthara, where he dies about 374, and his ledgers are burned. The leaseholding houses, Veyr's allies, demand that Casvilar produce Drenoc or lay down his command. Early in 377 Selvena publicly refuses her father's offer of a divorce from Veyr.
- **377–371 BR — The first civil war.** The senate declares the Field at Kellovar void; Casvilar's augurs count the signatories as contestants and he occupies Caleran. At Velos that autumn the senators' army fights him to a standstill without Veyr, whose fleet the Oar ports refuse to pay for. For four years Atheria has two governments, the eastern one fed on the gateway kingdom's grain. In 373 Veyr sails to Sarqen for that grain, refuses Vardek's table and takes sanctuary at Ilamar; the spared towns rise for Samyra under the Table Oath and Casvilar backs them. Samyra's restoration is secured by 372; Veyr dies of fever at Ilamar in 371.
- **370 BR — Casvilar's assassination.** Seven challengers are entered in the arms register kept by Dervan Tolm, led by Mavren Seneth and Corial Draven; the seventh is entered as the son of Drenoc of Kellovar, and Casvilar comes down to close his thirteen-year Field. They kill him on the muster ground below the river stores before the contest opens. Velsar claims the Field; Thalion, his designated heir, refuses it. Harod's Field had lain vacant since c. 441 and bound no one; Casvilar's is held to be fouled while any of the seven lives, and no commander dares disband an army.
- **369–366 BR — Darsil and the quartered winters.** Velsar destroys the army of the seven's provincial friends at Darsil in the summer of 369, and none of the seven is there. Two companies he pays off carry the war home to the upper Aurin and re-enlist, and no commander disbands a company afterwards. His army is quartered on Darsil; the town rises in the spring of 366 and his brother Tarsen hangs eleven organisers. Velsar's wife Miretha brings grain carts every autumn for ten years and dies there in 356.
- **367–364 BR — The Four at the Stores and the Writing-In.** Varroth, as First Celebrant, rules the Red Field fouled and unclaimed (367). Velsar, Thalion, Varroth and the speaker Besral Venn govern from the store-house above the muster ground. Their enemies are written into the register of challengers and killed on the muster ground. In the winter of 366 Venn is written in at Velsar's demand with Thalion's consent; he stands on the muster ground with a borrowed practice sword, the squad sent to kill him walks away, and he keeps the river stores for the rest of the war. The war spreads, and in 364 Varroth's own pass garrisons burn his magazines.
- **362–358 BR — The guest and the counted child.** Seneth sits at the spared towns' table at Sarqen from 362. Thalion's first wife Ilmara dies in 361; in 360 he marries Merath, Samyra's elder sister. Iren Sallas, one of the seven, walks out of Ilamar's sanctuary to his death (359). In 358 Merath bears Thalion's son Meren at Esharat, and Samyra's officers bring mother and child to the palace at Sarqen; Esharat lets the boy go on condition that the queen show him at the landing every spring.
- **355–353 BR — Marelith and the guests of Pyrassa.** In one linked 355 BR operation Melvo holds [[Marelith]] fast with Pyrassa's harbour chain, burns it with Corial Draven aboard and defeats Ardel Veyr's nearby main force among the central Thalassian anchorages. Ardel reaches Pyrassa with eleven hulls and the city's survivors and is admitted under the Chain Peace; Melvo refuses to take a guest from his own city's quay. Selvena makes terms there in 353, and Ardel lives out his life on the mole. Marelith, "the harbour without a chain", had sheltered families fleeing the Writing-In.
- **354 BR — The names at Caleran and the Dawn Road.** Corven Ilar asks the assembly to declare Samyra an enemy; spared-town envoys answer with the Table Oath and the names of the hostages of 609, Thalion stands while they are read, and the assembly does not vote. Velsar marches on Avesh to seize the fugitive of the seven sheltered there; Vasharan relay riders carry the man up the Dawn Road ahead of him all summer, and he returns without a battle and without his man.
- **353–350 BR — [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession|The Salt Field and the table at the landing]].** Velsar accepts a Red Field on Sarqen's salt pans naming Thalion and Melvo (autumn 353); Varroth comes east with the register to witness it and stays; Thalion does not come. Melvo closes Sarqen's anchorage (352); the queen's officers levy the towns for the bound army, the Fourth Collection, and at that autumn's count the towns' sealed jars are found a third empty, the wax bitten from beneath. That winter Vashar sends down the salted body of the Avesh fugitive, the sixth of the seven. In 351 a sea bargain brings Qahuri grain hulls in past the blockade at the price of a third of the cargo and a flooded basin, and the queen's officers leave the wounded on the basin's quay; Merath dies in the palace that winter. In spring 350 Hadram kills Seneth, the last of the seven, at the towns' table; the towns put the Nerethan house out, and Hadram takes sanctuary on Ilamar. Melvo kills Velsar on the Salt Field, and Tarsen and Varroth die there with him; the register is lost. Samyra sits at the towns' table as a stranger with her children and her nephew Meren; Thalion eats there three seats from her and swears the Table Oath, and Sarqen passes under a governor who swears it every spring.

## The Atherian Imperial Age

- **c. 350 BR–0 — [[Historical Atlas of the Atherian Age of Glory|Age of Glory]].**
- **c. 350 BR — Unification of Atheria.** [[Aurelius Thalion]] secures Caleran and concentrates supreme command, standing military pay, provincial appointments and taxation in the imperial settlement, governing from the store-house above the muster ground. With all seven challengers dead he stands on the empty muster ground. The Common Sky's college, its First Celebrant dead on the Salt Field, will not declare the Red Field closed, and Besral Venn answers the same request by sending his practice sword; the Red Star's priests declare the Field closed into Thalion's command, and Astaroth's priesthood takes first place in ceremonies of victory and command.
- **350–318 BR — [[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]].** The great discharge (350–346), provincial appointments and regular assessments turn the founding victory into a continuing government. In 326 a treasury hall blessed by the Red Star's priests but never loaded falls on the heir Caelor (d. 324); from then on rune-trained wardens load every public work before the Red Star and break its Star's share, and the official who opens it stands under the arch. Samyra dies at Esharat in 333. Thalion dies at the spring renewal on Sarqen's landing in 318, and his younger son Meren succeeds.
- **318–247 BR — [[The House of Thalion]].** Meren I, Thalion's son by Merath Nerethan, is the first heir painted in on the board the armies swear to. Two southern commands refuse to carry his daughter Cassara's face (292), so from 286 her husband Varelan I is the face on the board while the spared towns seat Cassara at Sarqen's table. Quarrymen who strike rather than cut "stone for the Star to break" through the harvest are killed in 274. Their son Olarion has his informers swear their denunciations before the accession portrait and allows no heir to be painted; he is found dead before it in 247.
- **247–245 BR — [[The Painted Reign]].** Three commanders raise standards in the western districts, the eastern fleet and the river army. The accession portrait at Caleran answers in the Emperor's voice, and Avaren Mott governs in its name for two winters. Clerks who read the grain tallies in its sight read them full, no grain is bought, and its assurance that no imperial granary is empty sends soldiers behind it into the poor districts. In 245 Samor Veloran brings grain in over a span thrown across the water gate, stands under it, covers the portrait with his cloak and refuses to be painted in its place. The Face stays veiled in the Common Sky's custody thereafter, unveiled last at each accession; Mott is the first man executed for questioning it.
- **245–151 BR — [[The House of Veloran]].** The unpainted emperors are agreed by the divided river commands and made under an arch. Samor I is followed in 229 by Aren Soreth, the last child of the old house and his own hostage, and then by his grandson Orent I (203) and Orent II (174). The Crown of Olveth responds in 219. ✶ The Sleepwalkers' Summer and the Via Somnia follow c. 200. The Arcelon campaign and road, built to answer Vashar's Dawn Road, follow c. 190–182; Raveth's river towns leave Vasharan canal duty and join by negotiation c. 160. A fever summer kills Orent II without an agreed successor in 151.
- **151–61 BR — Halreth dynasty.** In the fever year the spared towns lay their boards out of season and seat Sarqen's governor, Elian Halreth, in the emperor's empty place; the eastern cities back him for citizenship, sworn before the emperor's image, and Jirahar scribes swear with the copy veiled. In the Raveth hunger from 119 Neriah bar Sorek issues relief as village debts; the court turns them into a crown estate over his resignation, and households whose debts were sworn before the image surrender their land unresisting. Under the late Halreths, Vashar's War of the Two Dawns lets Atheria take Qiryath under a charter after the Trial before the Stars, and Vashar takes the Dawn Portion of pilgrim dues. Velen I teaches that the Emperor does not stand under and unveils the Face at his spectacles; in 61 his exempted festival banks drown workers, spectators and Velen himself while his guard stands facing the Face.
- **c. 96–54 BR — [[The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door - Imperial History|The Low Door Companions]].** Six Lacaris servants rise from a riot into office through rescue, hostage-taking and a betrayed refuge. In 61 two of them supply Doran's march, and his officers adopt their low door.
- **61–24 BR — Doran and Odrun I.** Doran re-veils the Face and has his officers enter their commands by a low door instead of swearing before the image; those officers choose his son-in-law Odrun to follow him. Isfir is born in 33 BR.
- **24 BR–0 — Odrun II to the Redeemer boundary.** The empire remains politically intact; its response to the Redeemer continues in the following age.

## The Age of the Redeemer and the Conversion (33 BR–398 AR)

- **33 BR — Birth of Isfir** in a Jirahar tower town of the eastern provinces, under the Qiryath charter's protection of the towers.
- **3 BR–0 — The Ministry.** The Companions: thirteen called, one struck (see [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]]).
- **0 AR — The Binding and Resurrection at Second Qiryath.** Ezrahel's faction, keepers of the sanctuary copy of the Qiryath charter, refers the case to the Atherian governor, who orders execution outside the city. Isfir is buried before the Resting Day. Solana and other women find the tomb empty on the third morning. The appearances, Ascension and gift of the Holy Flame follow. See [[The Rise of the Redeemer]].
- **0 AR — The Silence of Acceptance.** Jirahar sacrifice continues at Qiryath, but its authenticated sign of divine acceptance never returns after the Resurrection.
- **0 AR — The Three Silent Days.** From the hour of the Binding to the third morning, no augur college in the empire records an answer from the Stars. Augury resumes on the fourth day, and the colleges seal the days. Severan lays down his staff and never reads again.
- **1–291 AR — The Underground Centuries.** The [[The Ember Underground|Ember Underground]], ✶ the Averted Legion (c. 150), the Fireless Trial and the Temple That Would Not Burn (c. 245–247). Under the Purge's edict, congregations record their signs as weather and good sense.
- **c. 15 AR — The conversion of Saint Aquilan the Turned** (born Sha'il bar Qoreth) on the road to Virelos.
- **c. 29–47 AR — The Companions' martyrdoms.** The Great Purge (c. 36–45) is a commission of the High Augurate to recover the augural books carried off by provincial colleges that followed Tyros. It burns Jareth before the Pillar of Concordance in 41, after leading into the Pillar the nails from the governor's case chest, and makes it a crime to publish a wonder the colleges have not entered. Virella dies in 47 at the hands of a commissioner whose warrant has lapsed.
- **c. 55 AR — The Falling-Asleep of Amaryah the Fire-Bearer** at Veridun Minor (see [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]).
- **61–67 AR — [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction|The Great Tower Revolt]].** In the spring of 61 the emperor's daily offering lies unburnt on Qiryath's altar for nine mornings. The governor's augur enters it as fraud under the Purge's edict, soldiers sent up the sanctuary stair to burn a bull in pitch kill the altar's custodians and two hundred others, and militants who teach that the sign will return when foreign rule ends raise the towers. The legate Karsen Maruth goes round Qiryath to the Dawn Road. From 62 he digs down into the deep tower of Migdal Shahar; in the spring of 64 its Way-Gate passes the households to a tower across the Vasharan frontier whose bench refuses anyone who bore arms, and the keeper Yaltah bat Ozer breaks the scroll-keys before the legions reach the chamber. Qiryath, besieged from the autumn of 64 and dried out by the dropping of its cistern galleries, falls in 67 AR. Its Keeper has sent the sanctuary's vessels through the city's gate, which has not opened since; the legate's augur burns the emperor's offering on the altar and the sanctuary with it, and the triumph at Caleran carries empty litters. Atheria ends Vashar's Dawn Portion, abolishes the city's charter government, installs a permanent garrison and lays the price of the vessels, the Litter Debt, on every Jirahar household it rules. Many displaced Jirahar seek refuge outside direct Atherian rule.
- **78–162 AR — [[Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian - Emperors Wars and Revolts|The store-house and the roads]].** Dorvan Dascen wins the succession war in 78 and dies in 91 without a son or a named heir. His daughter Ysane Dascen, the Paymistress, who holds the ledgers and the ports, marries the eastern army's commander Daven Talar after a hungry winter and rules with him from the old store-house at Caleran. Daven fights Vashar c. 94–103, a war fed by Vasharan claims to the lost Dawn Portion; in 104 Ysane strikes the remaining Avesh garrisons from the pay ledger, and Daven governs the eastern roads from Arcelon until his death in 119. His nephew Rovan Caelis (119–147), Ysane's colleague until her death in 126, secures shorter supply lines and southern market treaties. Garen Vardun takes the throne in 147 on the grain credit of the Vessan house of Sarqen, campaigns against some southern Folk coalitions c. 150–162 and is killed in the south in 162.
- **162–c. 204 AR — Marek Vessan and the table at Sarqen.** Garen's son Marek, aged eight, reigns under his mother Irsa Vessan's regency until 171. Enslaved workers seize grain stores and interrupt shipping at Sarqen in 186. In 187 runaways led by Melqa of Belqara sit down at the spared towns' unranked table on the landing; the governor cannot swear the Table Oath among them, the towns supply no unsworn governor, no grain fleet sails for two years and the towns' sealed jars are found with the wax bitten. In 189 Marek comes in person, eats at the table and swears, which makes its guests unsaleable. His mother's house disowns him, and he lives at Esharat as the towns' guest until c. 204 while rival provincial commands issue orders in his name.
- **c. 214–225 AR — Second major war with Vashar in the Redeemer age.** The armies contest eastern road stations and tolls. Atheria transfers troops south to meet attacks there. A negotiated evacuation and exchange of captives end the campaign.
- **By the third century AR — Four principal regional sees.** Caleran coordinates relief and access to the capital, Aurelium trains readers and copies testimony, Halmenth serves the river and outer coast, and Sermora links the Elmaric ports. [[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization]]
- **c. 242–244 AR — The Kept Lamps.** A grave-lamp cult in the unpaid eastern garrisons feeds mutinies; augurs break its central image c. 243.
- **245–248 AR — The Edict of Relit Lamps.** Emperor Tervan Marcellan orders every household in the directly governed provinces to put out the lamps it keeps for its dead at a market altar, relight them from the Stars' college fire and keep them burning green on the colleges' copper-salted oil (245). Night watches walk the graves for yellow flames; the Redeemer burial fraternities are found first, Jirahar graves next, and tinkers sell *pot-green* to the rest. Enforcement is strongest through 247. The oil's green is verdigris scraped from the temple bronzes; Tervan dies in the spring of 248, and his successor suspends the general requirement that summer, after the High Augur of Caleran refuses to open the year before a scraped image. Congregations argue over whom to give fire, refuse it to those who walked with the watch, and relight from flames that never burned green, above all Amaryah's, hidden in a neighbour's bread oven at Veridun Minor; the daughter-flame custom spreads.
- **248–288 AR — The sold seasons.** The treasury may lawfully pay only a campaign the augurs have opened. The High Augurate sells openings to the commands that can pay; commands that cannot fight unopened and keep depot revenue for their own pay. From c. 281 the court and the High Augurate act together, voiding the seasons of commands that hold revenue back. In 287 the southern frontier army's season is voided.
- **288–291 AR — The closing prosecutions.** Cassian's frontier army acclaims him in 288. The court holding Caleran has its augurs void his omens and condemn the ground of the Redeemer congregations: meeting halls and burial yards are entered as cursed, forfeited and sold, the dead are carted to common pits, and recognised church officers are arrested. Provincial enforcement varies.
- **Winter 290 AR — [[The Forty Flames]].** Troops retake Ironvale's Ashforges after three days of rising. Forty prisoners refuse to renounce the Redeemer and die after three nights confined in an unlit furnace. A flame appears on the dead when it is opened. The common burial tally records thirty-nine, as one family buries its dead privately. **291:** before Cassian's officers, the guard who opened the furnace and Brask, the Ashforges' script-keeper, freed so his oath will stand, swear that the furnace was cold; the camp reads their account in the Aurin towns in 292.
- **292 AR — The night on the ice at Velos, and Legalisation.** In the first freeze of the winter the White Flame stands on the frozen Aurin under the Stone Bridge of Velos while **Cassian**'s vanguard is crossing the ice to surprise the court's camp, and the ice goes out beneath the arches. No battle is fought: the court's army crosses the bridge to him over three days. He takes Caleran and permits open worship. The Aurin at the bridge has never frozen since. The Caleran augurs enter the reach as a sign answered to the emperor, which is why Cassian keeps the colleges. He reigns to 316.
- **c. 296 AR — The Velos Concord.** A bridge chapel and a crown Warden of the Reach, accepted by three of the four sees in exchange for empire-wide restitution of the congregations' condemned property. Halmenth signs in 297.
- **297–316 AR — [[Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR|Cassian's settlement]].** In 297 the other three sees sit at Halmenth on that see's refusal of the Concord, and it signs "as a record of ice". Cassian grants the former Celestial Forum, with the Pillar of Concordance and the Nails, to Caleran's congregation (c. 300–310), and a daughter flame from Veridun Minor becomes the Eternal Ember. c. 309 his eldest son Torian asks the High Augur of Astaroth for an answer on the succession; Cassian executes the High Augur, has Torian ordained and shuts him into the bridge chapel at Velos as its chaplain. In 316 Torian baptises him, dying, in the unfrozen reach: the **Cassian Wager**.
- **316–348 AR — [[The Reign of Sevrin]].** Cassian's younger son takes power after agreeing terms with the eastern command under his cousin Adran, which put Adran's line next in the succession after Sevrin's sons. Sevrin confirms restitution, refuses to transfer the wardenship at Velos, where his brother Torian is chaplain until c. 341, keeps the Star estates' supply contracts and protects southern frontier markets. c. 338 the Red Star's wardens, loading a ruined shrine at Veridun Minor, uncover bones that the four sees claim for Calira and the Star college enters as a Star-priestess. Sevrin's only son dies c. 343, and Adran's son Astrion succeeds under the terms of 316 in 348.
- **348–351 AR — [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion at Caleran]].** Astrion restores temple stipends, revives the Purge's edict so that every wonder claimed since 292 must be entered by a college or struck (the Entering), has the augurs re-enter the Velos reach as an answer of Hecatara and authorises a survey at Qiryath, which stops when the Jirahar crews refuse to work under the Red Star's wardens. In 350 the Red Star answers his public test of legitimacy once. In 351, on the northern retreat, he stands beneath an untried timber span at a dry wash, the Emperor's Cut, while the wagons cross; it breaks under the wagons of wounded and crushes his hips and legs. He lives and never stands again. Savel Orren, commander of the guard, refuses to be acclaimed in his place.
- **351–352 AR — The false report.** Astrion's brother **Tellan** saves the column with water trains under the bronze standard of the test, the Answered Standard. A first courier tells Caleran that the emperor is dead; the sees, the port councils and the guard make **Lucar Aster**, Sevrin's son-in-law, emperor, and keep him when a second courier reports Astrion alive at Arcelon. Lucar repeals the Entering and restores church stipends.
- **352–c. 372 AR — Two standards.** Lucar reigns at Caleran, with his brother Corlan as marshal of the southern defences until Corlan's death c. 366. The east keeps Astrion, carried in a litter under the Answered Standard. c. 357 the **Accord of the Two Standards** leaves him the name of emperor east of the depot line for his lifetime, with no heir to it, and confirms the eastern command, its augurs and its standard. No second answer comes. Astrion dies at Arcelon c. 372; Tellan keeps the command and claims no throne.
- **366–377 AR — The tokens and the Kellovar ford.** From c. 368 Lucar's chaplains confiscate as idols the ford-tokens of the Folk guides who have led imperial columns over the river-courts' fords since Cassian's treaties, and of Folk traders at the fort markets. In the hard winters of 369–371 Grobi war-bands come down the frozen rivers. The guideless garrisons cross by the fords' older toll, a traveller delivered to the far bank who cannot return, and pay it in children taken from the allied households of the fort districts; stewards sell some of the children and enter them as delivered. From 372 the fort-district households escape across the fords on hidden tokens, towards their children, while the forts hang token-carriers. In spring 377 the last of them cross below Kellovar by night; Lucar's army follows at dawn with no token and no one delivered, and drowns. Lucar crosses dry on a confiscated silver token, is three days a guest at the Otter house's fire, and is killed by his own rearguard when he rides back. Vestian, holding the depots and paying the surviving units, is recognised at Caleran while Lucar is still across the river.
- **377–398 AR — Emperor Vestian the Pious.** 377: the Velos chapel and its book pass to the bishops. By 379: the Folk settlement. 383: the **Dissolution** of the augur colleges at Caleran (the provinces by 384); the power to authenticate signs passes to the bishops. c. 383–384: the Council of Second Flame confirms the Forty. 383–384: the **War of the Two Standards**. 385: **the Profession**; the faith made compulsory. 385–c. 389: the **Second Silence**. c. 387: household rites banned. c. 389: the lamp taken out of the state oath. Vestian dies in 398.
- **377–398 AR — The Vestian Persecutions.** c. 389 the Raveth hearing house burned. c. 388–396 most remaining eastern towers razed or confiscated. c. 396 Qiryath cleared under its Empty Charter, with *before the Mesakha* in Vestian's hand. Existing diaspora communities absorb a second great displacement. [[Vestian Persecutions and the Long Diaspora]]

## The Vestianic Regency and the Decadence (398–540 AR)

- **398–447 AR — The Vestianic regency.** Vestian's son reigns through the court bishops and paymasters who made his father. Relic estates buy freedom from tax and audit, and provincial wealth moves into them. The palace's rites begin to fail while frontier rites go on kindling: the **Guttering**, as Thalmyric refuge chroniclers later name it. Eclessian historians call 398–905 the Unexamined Centuries. (See [[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]].)
- **c. 404 AR — The Voice Beside the Binding.** A reliquary voice certified by a bishop's readers names enemies of the state until, the prison chronicles claim, the clerk it imitated breaks it. [[HC-N10 - Voice Beside the Binding]]
- **411 AR — The Council of Halmenth.** "Fire-Bearer" affirmed. Bishop Qedrel of Qabir, whose Man-Bearer party was rooted in the gateway congregations that named the Second Silence and lost their sworn accounts at Raveth, and who would certify no relic estate for the regency's tax exemptions, is condemned and charged under the augurs' old law against publishing an unentered sign. Qabir's deacons will not relight their lamp from the Ember's daughter; Halmenth's ferry crews get Qedrel to a Sea Fleet ship. The regency strikes the gateway sees off its roll and leaves them alone as taxpayers; the communion gathers there and survives as the [[The Velian Church|Velian Church]].
- **447 AR — The abbot who would not reign.** The old emperor dies. His only son, tonsured at eleven by the regency's bishops and made abbot of a relic estate on the lower Aurin, will not leave his cloister, and the court bishops will not release his vows.
- **447–452 AR — The succession war.** The southern fort-line army defeats the Aurin fleet and the port councils. Its commander, first of the **Saldane** house, is acclaimed by the great sees and pays them in further immunities. Caleran hires its first Sestaran Shell-fire cohorts.
- **452–514 AR — The Saldane years of the Decadence,** which Aurelium's historians reckon 398–514. Ättar sea-raids on Kedrava; Folk pressure on the southern forts; Sestaran cities revive pre-imperial charters, Vardessa's the famous case, and sell ships and warlock contracts to the court in place of tax.
- **514–523 AR — The Ubar Toll War.** **Teomar Saldane** (r. 509–531) fights Vashar for the Ubar tolls and takes the western stations of the Avesh corridor, which he cannot afford to hold. In the dry country each crown voids the other's well-awards and grants the same wells again; neither withdraws a grant at the peace, every well of any size has two lawful holders, and the well-country falls into feud.

## Revelation and the First Unsealing (541–612 AR)

- **541 AR — The Revelation of Mujalib** (b. c. 510), beside a well with two masters. **541–549 —** the kept wells: he seals the shares of some sixty disputed wells on clay tablets, and something keeps them.
- **546–552 AR — The Thin Floods.** The Basin flood fails five summers in seven. The **yoke-sickness** (548–551) kills oxen and mules from the Basin to the Atherian north; camels are spared. The Saldane court keeps Caleran's bread dole by requisitioning northern stores.
- **c. 549 AR — The Bread of the Bound.** The 4,444 devils dig the oasis and channels at Al-Qahur through the famine; grain goes to those who seal their names into the Covenant. Every name on the roll is owed bread in a failed year.
- **555–560 AR — The end of Vashar.** With the Dawn Road's relay stations emptied by famine, Covenant jurists declare the last King at Dawn forsworn. Road commanders and the horse-lords of Ruzhan seal with the Covenant for bread; resisting highland valleys are reduced; the last king is pensioned at the oasis.
- **558 AR — [[Nine Wells of Tergai|Seizure of the Nine Wells]].** In the worst year of the dry-country drought that outlasted the Thin Floods, the guard of the oasis wells, under its captain **Zuhayr ibn Qays**, violently seizes ancestral Well-Tarkhan water grounds. Later tradition recasts the conquest as voluntary submission.
- **563 AR — The Three Seals.** Mujalib, who takes no ruler's title, gives his authority away under three sealed instruments: the Seal of Water to Zuhayr and the guard of the wells, the Seal of Names to the college of scribes at Al-Qahur, and the Seal of the Roads to the marshal **Qatada Hafiz-al-Turuq**. Their holders are the Hand-Sealed. He lives until 596 and sets none of the three above the others.
- **568–612 AR — WAVE ONE: The First Unsealing.** The roll has outgrown the Basin's bread, and the bound carriers of the 4,444 lift only grain that has been given, so breach-courts go into the Atherian north under the escort of Qatada's horsemen. They void town charters sworn before the dissolved augur colleges. A served town keeps its walls and altars, enters its householders on the roll, owes a share of each harvest (the Given) and receives a bound keeper at its chief well. Half the north is "served, not fought"; towns that refuse are stormed.
- **575 AR — The Dry Ford.** The northern field army leaves its walls, beats Qatada's escort at a spring ford and hangs a breach-court's jurists. Marching home through served towns in the dry season, it finds the bound keepers drawing only for the roll, sacks three of its own towns for their cisterns and breaks up by autumn.
- **587 AR — Tavoran I.** The remnant field army and the frontier bishops depose the Saldane house. **Tavoran I** (r. 587–624) begins the crusade of seals: hermit-bishops re-seal surviving charters before living witnesses, and no court can void a charter on which the Fire came.
- **c. 594–598 AR — The War of the Seals (defensive).** The western field line holds at ruinous cost while bishops re-seal the towns behind it; the rotation of bearers begins with those it broke.
- **596 AR — Mujalib dies** at Al-Qahur, aged about eighty-six. **597 AR — The Fourth Seal disputed.** The college's instrument names Zuhayr ibn Qays, who becomes the first caliph; the Counter-Instrument of the Prophet's daughter Rumaitha names her son Hilal. Atherian towns begin pleading that every breach-court sits under a disputed seal. **598 AR — Qatada halts the northern army** until the seals can be tried; Zuhayr cannot dismiss a marshal sealed by the Prophet's hand, and the field advance stops.
- **601 AR — Emirate of Zanthir established.**
- **603 AR — Bir Qassim.** Hilal ibn Raiq, his mother Rumaitha and fifty-six others of their household travel to arbitration at Iqashar under Qatada's safe conduct. A flood pushes them off the named road, the bound keeper of the station well will not draw for them, and a court voids their protection. Sixteen die of thirst, Rumaitha among them. The escort refuses to disarm the rest and carries thirty-seven west to the strait, and Qatada rides after it; the caliph's column kills the five still alive at the well and fourteen drovers who had given them water. Hilal lives, veiled, at Qazmarid until 631. **604 —** Qatada dies on the road back, and the Seal of the Roads passes into the college's keeping. The Witnessed Seal/Sealed Blood divide.
- **c. 610 AR — The Velian States** installed under the **Banu Mizan**; the Velian cities keep their pre-conquest charters in fireproof chests. The Banu Mizan take the bound keepers off the belt's wells and measure its water by the hour-cup.
- **611 AR — The standoff at Tamsar.** A breach-court's ruling fails against a march town re-sealed in the Fire, a Caleran reliquary fails to kindle before both armies, and neither side gives battle.
- **612 AR — The Long Frontier Peace begins.** The Banu Mizan broker the instrument Covenant law calls the Suspension: no service, storm or re-seal along the line, and every disputed charter held "neither void nor in force".

## The Long Frontier and Renewed War (612–905 AR)

The Long Frontier proper is 612–884 AR; the later entries describe the renewed war which ends in the Fall.

- **612–872 AR — Coexistence and friction.** The kindled marches hold the imperial side under oaths each emperor renews in person before frontier witnesses. The Banu Mizan golden age: charter-houses, where the old empire's deeds are read and its learning copied, water-courts and libraries. **Rab Yequtiel of Zanthir** (c. 748–831), a Jirahar physician who witnesses bound deathbeds in Zanthir's bond-hospitals for fifty years, writes *What the Register Keeps*: a register holds the name and lets the person go.
- **c. 640s AR — Relic tests.** The treasury ties relic estates' immunities to their relics answering a test; the tests are rigged at Caleran.
- **c. 641–804 AR — The frontier lives.** The Clerk of a Hundred Wells, the Mason of the Ninth Bastion, the Chain-Bearer of Sarrat, the Bitter Reaper, the Reader Behind the Sealed Door and the Smith of the Refuge Bell.
- **653 AR — The eastern tax fleet founders** in a Thalassian squall. The eastern bishops keep the revenue "for one year" and never surrender it; Aurelium's register of answered fire follows.
- **681–683 AR — The Copper Lamp.** Tavoran II's accession lamp is made to flare with Shell-fire; the unpaid warlock sells his brass fire-cup to a Covenant factor, beginning the Covenant's file of counterfeit fire.
- **709 AR — The Fever Week.** A palace fever kills Tavoran II in nine days and leaves his son too weak to ride. **709–713 AR — The War of the Two Oaths.** Corvenas, the sick emperor's brother-in-law, rides the marches and renews their oaths in person, at first in his name; the emperor in Caleran cannot leave his bed, and the marches keep their oaths with the man who knelt.
- **711 AR — Oswin's examination.** Bishop Oswin of Aurelium records the Velos Standard silent and holds the eastern oaths in trust. **713 AR — Corvenas I** (r. 713–746) confirms Aurelium's right of examination: the Examiner's primacy.
- **c. 866 AR — The Captain of the Sacrificial Wind.** On the river marches a host led, by soldiers' account, by a drowned ferrywoman holds the road behind a retreating convoy, then withdraws when the army's commander, carrying Corvenas II's orders, demands the conquest of the Velian belt.
- **872 AR — The Banu Mizan emir dies,** leaving a child; Caleran's jurists call the regent's renewal of the Velian compacts void. **872–873 AR — The heartland harvest fails.**
- **874–878 AR — The Reception.** Corvenas II (r. 861–884) marches to "receive" the Velian cities, which shut their gates. **876 AR — Oronda:** the Velos Standard fails to kindle before both armies. The crusade collapses in 878; its prisoners go unransomed.
- **878–881 AR — Qahir's prisoners.** Nobody can pay the northern horse that saved the Velian belt. Its commander, **Qahir ibn Ruzbeh**, takes the crusade's nine thousand unransomed prisoners instead, enters them under his own name at Iqashar and pays his army from their labour. In 881 the caliph at Al-Qahur gives him the Seal of the Roads, unheld since Qatada, with the new title of Sultan.
- **884 AR — The broken peace.** Qahir ibn Ruzbeh obtains a ruling that the crusade voided the Suspension. The march commanders force Corvenas II to abdicate; **Corvenas III** (r. 884–905) succeeds.
- **885 AR — Banu Mizan rule in the Velian belt ends.** Qahir's officers take the client courts; a Banu Mizan line keeps Zanthir as tributary emirs.
- **885–886 AR — The Ruling on Parties.** The college at Al-Qahur rules that the Clause of the Given binds parties, not powers.
- **c. 885–902 AR — The Qahirid Resurgence.** **Qahir ibn Ruzbeh** rebuilds the war machine: Ahdanite mass levies, capture companies, name-cage foundries, lesser Surrender engines to travel with the breach-court train. Qahir dies in 902.
- **902–905 AR — Idris's campaign.** **Sultan-Caliph Idris al-Qari**, thirty-one, with daughters and no son, is kept at Iqashar by his jurists because every oath in the army names him and nobody after him. Qahir's engine-master **Barzin** and the breach-court train march through the northwestern approaches with a seaward supply line; the train, with its lesser Surrender engines, serves Caleran district by district before the great engines reach the walls.
- **903–904 AR — The Jubilee.** Corvenas III holds a year of rites inside Caleran; **Sarion the Pale** is stoned for preaching that "the inked ones are coming".

## The Fall and the Thalmyric Rise (905–962 AR)

- **905 AR — WAVE TWO: The Fall of Caleran.** The breach-court train reads Caleran's own charter, last re-sealed with warlock fire, on its walls quarter by quarter; a late Aurin flood holds the southern levies three weeks short of the city and the march commanders stand bound to their ground. The great engines come to the Aurin dyke that keeps the river out of the lower city, and the Depth-Core frames wade the flood to lean on the **Star's share**, the length its builders made to break in the wardens' test, which Thalmyric tradition says the Red Star showed Barzin's engineers. On the last day **Corvenas III** carries the Velos Standard onto the dyke and calls on the Fire before both armies; nothing comes, and the holding quarters take terms. At dusk the engines break the dyke, the river drowns the poor quarters no court had served, and every quarter without terms by dark is stormed. The breach-court accepts from him only his person and his relics (**the Instrument of the Ash Hall**); he lives as their captive keeper. The empire dies; refugees flee south to the volcanic mountains. [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Invasion and the Fall.md|The Invasion and the Fall]]
- **905–957 AR — The Impounded Ember.** Soldiers fail to put the Eternal Ember out; Idris's ruling, sent by courier from Iqashar, declares it neither holy nor his own and impounds it as a deposit held for the final audit, its keepers pledging their own names as surety that it will be neither divided, removed nor put out. A deposit is kept where it stands, so the basilica of the Celestial Forum is never sealed and its liturgy continues under guard. Addressed to the audit and not to Idris, it is the one instrument of his that survives 931. The Reconquest finds it burning, ledgered to the day — the most famous invoice in history, which Veridun pays in full, discharging the deposit and the keepers' names. (See [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]].)
- **c. 905–907 AR — The Borrowed Temple.** A provincial commander's bargain with the Exile is broken by prisoners and refugees at a star sanctuary; the province's claim to succeed Atheria dies there.
- **c. 905–920 AR — [[First Catch and Grey Sister|The First Catch]].** The fallen empire's border studs scatter into the northeastern grass. Idris's clerks had listed the studs among the estates to be seized; their lay brothers and handlers drive them north instead, and the occupation stays an army of camels and foot. Grass-Tarkhan hunters and herders catch them and become, within three generations, the world's finest light cavalry. Grey Sister's line begins; the older western camel culture continues.
- **906 AR — The Veil over Veridun.** A Qahirid column comes up the valley; the hearth-keepers of Veridun Minor refuse the refuge council's order to burn the store-cave flour and bake it all night. At dawn the ovens' smoke lies over city and valley, smelling of bread and cedar, for the five days the bread lasts. The column's engines call terms into it and its court finds no gate to read at; refugees dream of a woman banking an oven, the column's levies dream of home and walk there, and the column turns back with no charter read. Veridun calls it the Night of the Ovens. Thalmyria's supreme feast of the Mother.
- **906–908 AR — The Stand at Crown Gate.** The eastward push stopped at Caldria, whose fortress culture dates from the defence.
- **907 AR — [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)|Battle of the Shattered Engines]].** Caldria holds Crown Gate Pass while Vortharan warlocks destroy one Depth-Core and one Slave-Soul Covenant golem and cripple a second Depth-Core, ending the northern advance and weakening occupation forces later exposed at Caelmount.
- **907 AR — "No wound inherits the child."** Witnesses say the Heir with Five Remembered Wings (c. 781–826), whom devotees call the Saint, stands in an escape house's doorway and guards an old client house's children against a claimant from Hell while the house destroys its compact.
- **908–909 AR — The Sestaran defensive compact.** Fiorenzia, Sermora, Aversia, Vardessa, and Orsena seal mutual defence in 908; Vorthara adds its external seal in 909 while refusing domestic oversight.
- **908–921 AR — The Mountain Rebellion.** Corvenas III dies a captive among his relics in the winter of 908, freeing the refuge to raise its own standard; Caedros of house Aetherion forms the mountain league that summer. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/70 History and Conflicts/The Battle of Caelmount.md|Caelmount, the Breath of Askre]] (917–918): Amir Qazem's column reaches the valley's granary caves and lies down in them out of the snow. The cave-wardens have shut the vents that keep the mountain's bad air out; some six hundred men and every name-custodian die the first night, and the rest waste away through the winter while their levies desert to the amnesty. The valley can never store bread in those caves again. Caedros promises to feed it, and takes Virelos and its granaries in 921 to keep the promise.
- **c. 910–913 AR — The signal house.** An occupation governor's warlocks trap a Herald of heaven in a warded mountain signal house (devotional tradition). The failed rescue gives both armies the rule that a surrender names its protected people before property, and the horn players their three broken notes.
- **922 AR — The Coronation at Virelos.** **Caedros I the Unbowed**, first Thalmyric Emperor, crowned in the city he took to feed Caelmount.
- **922–949 AR — [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The War of the Burning Banners.md|The War of the Burning Banners]].** Refuge leagues, monastery armies, and return claimants are brought under one imperial command.
- **929 AR — The heir who would not hold.** Ruzbeh ibn Idris, fourteen and taught by a jurist of the people of the Clause, is brought to the Chain Furnace yard to take up his father's instruments and refuses them: he will hold nothing that was not given. Idris sets a re-swearing muster for the spring of 932, to write in his second son.
- **931–938 AR — The Unsworn Years (the Widowed Seals).** Idris dies in the autumn of 931 and every instrument naming him and the heir who refused is widowed. Levies go home; southern garrisons find nobody to obey; the silt hounds of the northern wells slip their feeding names ("the winter the wells barked"); the Depth-Core engine later called **the Elder** kills nineteen in Iqashar's dye-yards until the feeder **Gulnar** gives its feeding name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir". **Sultan-Caliph Mahdar ibn Idris** (r. 931–979), eight at accession, rules through the Regency of the Bench to 940, which makes the throne the **Sublime Threshold**, witnessed at Al-Qahur, and in 932 lodges Ruzbeh's name in the Vault, when Basin captains try to make him take the instruments up — the first form of the Cull of Brothers. **Durayd ibn Sahl** swears the occupied south to himself as Amir of the Southern Threshold (931–957). [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]]
- **933 AR — Qazmarid's proclamation.** The lord of Qazmarid takes the title Caliph-Wasi, arguing that the Qahirid claim and the Fourth Seal itself were widowed. Qazmari stations toll Covenant convoys in the western strait.
- **938 AR — The Basin leases.** The Basin captains re-swear to the Threshold for estates on fixed-term leases and become Al-Qahur's military households.
- **c. 944 AR — The first great mercy tower,** a Surrender Engine, is finished at Iqashar by overdrawing the Chain Furnace.
- **948 AR — Durayd offers to sell the Ember** back for its account; Caedros I's court refuses to acknowledge Covenant custody.
- **950–962 AR — [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Reclamation of Caleran.md|The Caleran campaign]].** The refugee armies come down on the occupied capital while Iqashar's field army is held on its northern wells; their war-lamps begin to kindle reliably. **Caedros II the Road-Maker** secures the coast road and the immediate river districts by 962.
- **951 AR — The Long Reach.** Grass-Tarkhan riders on hymn-blessed Atherian horses, which do not shy from bound things, break the hound-guarded northern wells and water at Tergai; Iqashar's field army is held on the grass to 957. The **Horse War** peaks c. 975–1010; in the 990s the first mercy tower proclaims terms to empty pasture as **the Talking Hill**.
- **955–956 AR — The last Slave-Soul engine** is sent south by sea; Thalmyric war-lamps open its name-cage before Caleran's outer works.
- **957 AR — Caleran retaken.** A custodian's refusal of a release entry at a bound dockworker's funeral raises the occupied districts. Amir Durayd sends his guard to carry the Ember to his ships; its Covenant keepers hold the lamp against him and eleven die, the dockers burn his ships at the mole, and he is taken alive in the harbour fort nineteen days later. The parishes open the river gate, and the surviving keepers open the forum precinct only on terms that name the garrison families he shut out and the surrendered quarters protected; **Caedros I dies at the Pillar of Concordance** at the moment of victory; witnesses swear the Flame took the body. Canonised in Thalmyria within a decade; canonisation rejected by Eclessia's relic courts.
- **962–1052 AR — [[The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)|The Long Reconquest continues]].** Every other occupied district is sworn to Durayd by name, and Caedros II keeps him in a walled house at Veridun until his death (c. 971) and has him sign their releases. Amirs who take a release and terms open their gates; those who refuse swear their garrisons to themselves. Caedros II's engineers take the canal heads in the hills and set water-courts there; registered fields get water, and most amirs are left with walls above dry fields. River citadels and the anchor works are besieged. Province-by-province return, occupation settlements, military colonies, and competing land registers reshape the heartland. The Velian States and Zanthir remain unrecovered.

## Two Crowns (962–1104 AR)

- **962–979 AR — Caedros II re-endows the Church.** Estates return to the monasteries and court appointees fill the sees; the war-lamps that kindled reliably in the 950s kindle less often.
- **974 AR — The banner at Kaltfurt.** A march banner taken up by the miller's widow **Hedwen** kindles while she shields a refugee column from a Grass-Tarkhan raid; the commander on the bank, **Adalmar**, is acclaimed king of the eastern marches the next year.
- **975–1019 AR — Adalmar the Great** joins the eastern bishops' principalities by war, marriage and a single register. Crowned Emperor of the Atherians in 1017.
- **979–984 AR — Ilarion Aetherion,** who sells the sees his father endowed. **982 AR — Anavra:** a bought chaplain's war-lamp stays dark on the gate while riders from the amirate of Meliton burn the town and carry off some three hundred people under name-bond; the survivors, led by the lamp-sergeant **Varkas**, break the lamp on their bishop's doorstep. **The Breaking** begins: the Breakers go from parish to parish asking every lamp to burn for them and smash the ones that stay dark. **984 —** the Vlastravan march army, sent to protect the sees, breaks its own war-lamps; Ilarion dies of a lung-fever; the army acclaims **Tavoran III**.
- **979–1029 AR — Mahdar's line at Iqashar.** Mahdar's son reigns to c. 1018 through the Horse War, followed by a short reign; the second mercy tower is finished c. 1000.
- **984–1011 AR — Tavoran III the Lampbreaker,** who breaks his own war-lamp before his army, lets the Breakers work and takes back the sees' estates. **986 —** to keep the Breakers from putting the Great Lamp of Veridun to their question he has it hooded in bronze; it is not seen again for sixty-six years. Flame-monks are driven out, household lamps smashed as "painted fire" or hidden in wells and ovens; the Reconquest slows; burial lapses in reconquered valleys (the march without mourners). The Lamp Controversy opens: may the uncreated Fire be put to the question, and is a dark lamp a lie? **Dovrin of Velia**, a Velian-born potter-monk at Keramion in the reconquered Aurin valley, makes cheap clay lamps stamped on the foot with the forty *Sayings for the Foot of a Lamp* ("nobody breaks a door because the guest is late"); the feet survive the Breakers. **1009 —** the hospice lamp at Sarmene kindles in the hands of the monk **Makarios** as he stands between a Breaker band and the sick; Tavoran makes him Patriarch, and he teaches the Breakers that there is no test. **1011 —** Tavoran III dies in an unlit room after Makarios refuses to lift the hood for him. **1012 —** the Synod receives Dovrin's sayings; the Breaking fades without a decree.
- **985–994 AR — The Folk Wars.** The eastern registers grant abandoned imperial frontier land held by Folk seer-oath; settlement drives, baptisms at spear-point, clan against clan. **987 —** the Green March war-leader **Weylan Oath-Bent** takes baptism and enters his clans' fields in the bishops' register. **993 AR — the Drowning at Wolfsmere:** Adalmar calls the clans' oath-keepers and seers to the lake under safe-conduct to recite the grove boundaries for his clerks; the recital contradicts his grants, and he has the four thousand bound and drowned "to drown the oath with its keepers"; nine survive under the water and become the first Lakekeepers; a standing leftward thin space — "the Lake That Remembers". **994 AR — the Standing of the Water:** the lake rises on a windless night and takes the Markish shore-fort; no lord takes up a grant beyond the lake again. **999 AR — the Peace of the Bent Oak:** Weylan sues the king in the bishops' court for the broken safe-conduct and wins; the grants south of the lake are struck from the register and the lake-law (no bell, no boat, no iron) is written into the march charters.
- **1003–1011 AR — The Pledged Custodian.** The Custodian of the Vault of Names pledges his own true name to an Iqashari lending house to ransom a son taken by Tarkhan riders; from 1006 the Covenant army cannot march, and cannot exploit the Breaking. In 1011 he surrenders his name into the Vault's deepest custody, the ink of it fades from every page, the Vault burns the lending house, and every Custodian since is **Unnamed**.
- **1006 AR — The Examination of the King.** At Adalmar's request the relic court of **Lord Primarch Valerin II** records the Kaltfurt banner answering and credits the sign to the king; Hedwen's testimony is entered, and Valerin will not crown a man over a living witness. Hedwen dies c. 1010 and Valerin in 1012; in 1013 **Octavin III**'s chancery strikes her testimony through.
- **1011–1055 AR — Tavoran IV,** Tavoran III's son, who carried a hidden war-lamp through the Breaking. From 1012 he opens crown lamp-houses where households may leave their lamps out of the Breakers' reach, under his Master of Lamps, **Kallion**.
- **1015 AR — THE GREAT SPLIT.** **Octavin III** offers Veridun the proof that Oswin's trust requires: lift the hood and let Aurelium examine the Great Lamp, and Tavoran IV will be recorded as the emperor who proves lawful; refuse, and the trust passes to Adalmar. **Patriarch Makarios the Adamant** nails the hood to the lamp's stand with four nails while **Cardinal Wigand** waits on the cathedral steps; Wigand reads the anathema to a shut door, and Makarios answers from the doorway. ✶ The Bells of the Split. See [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]].
- **1015–c. 1080 AR — The border after the anathemas.** The ferry riot on the Lissos (1022) and the Saint of the Two Ferries; the Judge Beneath the Black Eaves at Dvorna; the prohibition of conviction for "the future fact".
- **1017 AR — The Coronation of Adalmar.** Octavin III declares discharged the eastern oaths held "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful" since 711 and crowns Adalmar **Emperor of the Atherians** with the new **Crown of Embers** at the ford of Kaltfurt. The millers shut their sluices and he is crowned standing in mud. The Two Emperors problem begins. Adalmar dies in 1019, the only emperor the east ever crowns.
- **1019–1050 AR — Wolmar the Silent,** king of the east, recorded silent at every examination and never crowned; the Crown of Embers lies unworn in the royal chapel at Aurelium. From 1027 his sons and greater lords buy rival examinations and counterfeit kindled banners. **c. 1040 AR — the siege of Grauwacht** and the Saint at the Enemy Gate. **1041 AR — the Blank Lot of Drossfeld:** his sons **Aldwin**, **Hartwin** and **Gervin** agree to draw one name from Adalmar's helmet for the Sanctum's examination; Wolmar asks for a fourth, blank tablet "for the emperor who proves lawful", and the legate draws it. Each keeps what he holds that day until a banner answers: Aldwin the south with Aurelium and the keeping of the crown, Hartwin the eastern march, Gervin the middle march (Caldria, which had held the line since 906, and the lands that became Brauvalis, Veridancia, Korrheim, Ironvale and the Sylvan Marches). The wax tablet listing the middle march's holdings is smudged along its eastern line. **1047 —** Hartwin is killed by Grass-Tarkhan riders at the Orzva fords and leaves no heir.
- **1029–1049 AR — The Impounding.** **1029 —** Kallion forces the wayhouse lamp of Pelin's Bridge against the name-bound garrison of Meliton and is burned to death before both armies; Tavoran IV makes the lamp-houses compulsory and seals every lamp but the hooded Great Lamp and the Ember in them "until the Fire is safe"; their keepers sell the oil. **1046–1048 —** three dry summers; families deepening sunken wells lift hidden lamps, some reported still burning (the well-lights). **1048–1049 — the cold houses:** a village at Dryma breaks the seal on its lamp-house and finds it cold; in Veridun the guard captain **Arsen** breaks the seal on the capital's house before a thousand lifted lamps, and Tavoran IV finds his own lamp dead on the first shelf; crowds hang the keepers. Families walk to Caleran for a light from the Ember and are robbed and killed on the amirate roads; the emperor's daughter **Korinna** walks with them and is given the southern armies.
- **1029–1068 AR — Sultan-Caliph Tamim the Auditor.** **1033 — the Audit of the South:** the amirates are declared claimed but not defended. **1041 — the Muster at the Wrong Well:** the last relief army disperses for want of water after a copyist's error. **1044 — Zanthir's seasons:** Thalmyric squadrons close the coast to the Zanthiri grain barges that feed the last amirates; the emir at Qabir withdraws the barges and agrees to pay tribute to Veridun while the sea is open and to Iqashar while the caravans run.
- **1052 AR — The Rekindling.** The last southern amirate surrenders; occupation-born families who reject return law go north as the Covenant's **Southern Houses**, carrying Covenant deeds to their land and the bronze water-tokens of their fields. Every lamp in the empire is relit from one flame carried on foot from the Ember at Caleran along the newly joined roads; Tavoran IV, seventy-six, walks the first mile barefoot; Makarios draws his nails, and the Great Lamp is found burning under its hood; the **Feast of the Rekindling**, whose dark eve is the Night of the Cold Houses.
- **1055–1076 AR — Empress Korinna the Kindler.**
- **1059 AR — The two houses of the Vault.** The Living Register stays at Iqashar under the Unnamed Custodian; the names of the dead and the Custodians' surrendered names go south to the Deep Vault at Al-Qahur.
- **1059–1061 AR — The Choosing at Strazgorod.** **1059 —** Grass-Tarkhan riders burn Prince **Radomir I**'s hall at Strazgorod and its house-fire dies; Vesari hearth-law requires a house to relight from a greater fire or become the lender's client. **1061 —** his fire-keeper **Zdena** carries the cold lamp to Veridun to ask for a light from the Ember; Makarios refuses to light an unbaptised hearth; the lamp takes the Flame in her hands, lit by no one, before witnesses of both churches and a Sanctum examiner, who records it answering ("It chose my hand. I carried it"). Makarios keeps it. Unwilling to be Veridun's client, Radomir takes baptism from the Sanctum and lights his new hall from a fire carried from its altar. **1064 —** the kingless lords of Hartwin's eastern march and the Vesari princes choose him king in a moot of sabres: **Virelia**. Zdena's descendants tend the Virelian Lamp in Veridun. The Kindling Question becomes public.
- **1060–1067 AR — First Southern Crusade.** Folk captives sent to the middle-march silver mines.
- **1067 AR — Mazrakan chartered** at the end of Iqashar's paved road; Tarkhan companies hired on word-price, so Iqashar buys the horse power it could not beat.
- **1068–1102 AR — The purge of the lintels** in Virelia: Vesari hearth-houses purged, ancestral faces burned, a kin-name forbidden.
- **1080s AR — The third mercy tower** is finished at Iqashar under one of the brief reigns between Tamim and Khurzad.
- **1101–1103 AR — ✶ The Two Winters and the Apology.** **1102 —** at the winter diet of Drossfeld **Ermbold the Idle**, king of Aurelia and keeper of the unworn Crown of Embers, refuses to carry it to the Sanctum for examination ("a hat, not a relic") and sends it to the Primarch in a grain cart; he keeps his kingdom; Sermora hangs the chain-tender who lowered its harbour chain to famine ships. **1103 —** Virelia's Council of Sabres holds its first contested election; Veridun first offers to return the Virelian Lamp; the Apology harvest. In Covenant lands the Basin's grain fails and the Well of Seven Pressures is turned to preserving grain, idling the foundry for most of a decade; the Court of Breaches' **grey-sky ruling** suspends impossible instruments instead of voiding them.
- **1104 AR — The Crown of Embers passes into trust** at the Sanctum Divinitatis, "until the realm is worthy of one head." The reunification mandate acquires its legal object.

## High Noon and Long Decline (1104–1290 AR)

- **1110–1170 AR — The Compact of the Knockers** in Aversia's high mines.
- **1112–1142 AR — Sultan-Caliph Khurzad.** Enforces the Southern Houses' deeds with mercy towers and loses the War of the Southern Deeds to Caedros III.
- **1116–1124 AR — The revolt of the Heldenberg shafts.** Escaped mine captives break the counts' winter army through the Cold Way; **1124 — the Oath of the Shafts** founds Korrheim's first cantons. Their liberator, a slave courier, confesses c. 1137 to what the Cold Way did to the overseers sent through it, and dies in exile in 1146 (the Saint of Ashen Wings).
- **1121–1159 AR — Caedros III, "the Wall of the World,"** a convivial frontier soldier married to **Chrysanthe**, a Saronikan admiral's daughter, and godfather to more than four hundred district children. Military districts settled on frontier land emptied by the Two Winters, each holding owing a lance and a lamp. **1126 —** the Covenant's Court of Breaches rules the districts held under breached surrender deeds. **1131–1139 — The War of the Southern Deeds:** two mercy towers proclaim property-for-surrender terms to settlers who would forfeit their land by surrendering, and one is lost; the district lamps kindle in defence; **1134 — Kolonna**, where the emperor's own lamp kindles; dysentery kills Khurzad's heir in the siege camp (1135); a Covenant flank never moves on the Day of Two Readings (1137), and at the ford of **Vresna** the emperor's son **Orestan** has some four hundred of its surrendered levies cut down; the Covenant ransoms its captured custodians (1139). **1139 — the Kolonna Declaration:** the heir will be the claimant whose lamp the Fire kindles at the emperor's bier, barring Orestan without naming him. The ransom, then Sestaran loans, pay for High Noon; the treasury is in debt by the 1150s.
- **1142–1190 AR — Khurzad's son.** **1151 —** a commission to bring the Covenant's provincial codes into one book; it sits forty years without ranking them.
- **1158–1163 AR — The Great Southern Purge;** **1163 —** riders without breath go through the marcher camps after the boundary groves are cut, and the Purge ends.
- **1159 AR — The Unanswered Funeral.** Caedros III leaves his succession to the Fire; seven claimants' lamps stay dark; the lamp of his son Orestan flares with bought script-fire, and the court silences the warlock who smells it and acclaims him; the lamp of **Despina of Kolonna**, a district widow, kindles in the nave. **1159–1162 — the War of the Seven Lamps:** the district armies refuse "Hot-Coin", break his companies under Kolonna, and on Despina's word set him to keep a chapel over the dead of Vresna.
- **1162–1198 AR — Andrion, "the Widow's Emperor,"** acclaimed by the district armies. Inherits Caedros III's Sestaran debts and extends the district levy to pay them.
- **1166 AR — The siege of Sermora;** the hanged chain-tender is seen above the harbour wall. The last of Aldwin's line dies; the Primarchs rule Aurelia directly.
- **c. 1171–1173 AR — The Threshing Floor.** The prince of Elenara's census seizes dependants to win a royal crown; a mortality runs the census roads beneath a raptor of white fire and stops at a bereaved household's threshing floor at Kremna. Elenara remains a duchy; "the floor beyond the count."
- **c. 1180 AR — ✶ The Walking of the Small.**
- **1183 / 1204 AR — The definitions concerning the Mother.** The Sanctum dogmatises the **Unkindled Conception** and the **Rising of the Mother**; Veridun rejects the Sanctum's authority to define either doctrine.
- **c. 1185–1214 AR — The Ransomed Standard,** a company formed to recover bodies and pledged prisoners from ground neither army will enter; its fortunes break at Qarath.
- **1190–1243 AR — Sultan-Caliph Suhrab the Debtor,** a younger son and clerk of the codes commission whose jurist's renunciation of the throne the Court of Breaches voids as coerced (1190), after the heir is killed when a stand collapses at the Mazrakan spring fair (1189). The Covenant's apex. He abandons ranking the codes and writes the *Ledger of Ledgers*, a book of the occasions on which Covenant law killed the Covenant's own people, each entered as a debt and closed with the clause owed. **1194 —** he reads its first book, Bir Qassim, at the dry well; Sealed-Blood mourners answer each clause *And yet the well was dry*, and his diggers bring up dust from eighty fathoms.
- **c. 1201 AR — The Woman Who Returned the Keys** breaks a Velian amir's siege of a district town by reading his accounts aloud at his own pay-table, brings home the gate keys the town's council had sold him, and surrenders them rather than turn the rescue into a hereditary crown.
- **c. 1203 AR — A weeping Lady first reported at Bruckmoor;** the first pilgrims are parents of the Walking.
- **1209–1225 AR — Markellos the Drinker.** **1212 —** the Court of Breaches offers district families Covenant deeds without levy duty (the Unburdened Deeds).
- **1214 AR — The Battle of Qarath's Well.** Drought, fouled outer wells and a sworn well whose curb bears, in three scripts, a guest's peace until the next sunset and a debt, "the price of the water", which the house names, for any guest who breaks it. **Nikandros** of Kedrava reads the curb aloud to the council and is overruled; the army drinks, **Markellos** last of all; the Kedravans, who keep the refuge rule against sworn water, do not. The drinkers sicken at dawn when they take up arms; Tarkhan companies on blessed lines break the army; Nikandros withdraws in order with the wounded. At sunset on the second day the well's bound warden names the price in wet letters on the curb: **a well for every cup.** Suhrab, at Iqashar, cannot lower it. Each drinker owes the well of his own holding and keeps the water-sickness until he gives it; the Throne's cup is held to be owed the aqueduct head at Kolonna, and Markellos refuses. **Winter 1214 — the Forfeiture of the Drinkers:** the chancellor **Sergian** presses the sick emperor's seal to a decree giving the farms of every district holder who drank to his hired companies. **1215 —** Markellos leaves his own palace for the Kedravan monasteries; Sergian tells Iqashar the debt left with him; the Court of Breaches answers that the Throne drank, does not die and did not leave the palace; Covenant garrisons take the aqueduct head and the Southern Deeds districts. Thalmyria's deepest self-inflicted wound.
- **1215–1225 AR — The War of the Seal.** The emperor lives in a cell at Kedra while Sergian's companies, the drinkers, the Kedravan monasteries, the Saronikan admiralty and the duke of Elenara fight over his seal; the monasteries refuse forfeited farms ("we did not drink, and we will not eat"). **1217 —** drinkers' families kill Sergian in the Crown Hall; Caldrian captains seize three watershed valleys. **c. 1220 —** the Peace Not Sold in Vlastrava. **1225 — the Poured Cup:** at Virelos Markellos pours out a cup of water untasted and gives his claim to Nikandros for an oath to return the drinkers' farms; every later emperor pours out a cup at his coronation. Markellos dies a monk at Kedra (1231).
- **1225–1249 AR — Nikandros I the Restorer**, of a cadet Aetherion branch in Kedrava, crowned at Virelos by Markellos's gift; keeps half his oath by evicting Sergian's companies from the forfeited farms. **1227 — the Refusal of the Lamp:** unasked, the Sanctum offers him the Crown of Embers, Eclessian lances and the three Caldrian valleys if the Great Lamp of Veridun is tried by its relic court; Nikandros refuses ("a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned") and fights on alone. **1238–1246 — the Open Gates:** Covenant garrisons whose oaths have lost their witnesses in the plague march home, and Nikandros recovers about half the lost districts. He dies three years after the last garrisons leave, with the recovered districts still empty. Present-day Caedros IV claims this line.
- **c. 1232–1247 AR — [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|The Grey Sweat]].** The great mortality: about a fifth of eastern Eclessia and an eighth of its western territories in fifteen years of waves, and more than half the east's parish priests, because the fever followed those who went to the dying. A village without a priest is unclean: in the west tenants walk to wherever a priest lives and win the **clean-road charters**, while Virelia's magnates seal priestless villages and bind their survivors (**the bond of the clean village**). Ash-Walker penitents carry the ash of the unblessed pyres to churchyards that still have a priest, and seed later quietism and shrine hunger. Remembered as *the years the ferry queued* — a soul-logistics catastrophe as much as a plague (see [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]]). In Thalmyria (from c. 1234) laity sing their own dead out where priests die. In Covenant lands it is **the Emptying of the Benches** (c. 1236–1243): deaths outrun the witnesses needed to release bound names, and the Vault's registers never balance again. ✶ Ends with the **Quiet Ninth** (1247): the Sweat stops everywhere in one week, and the Ash-Walkers disband mid-march, everywhere, unasked.
- **c. 1235–1353 AR — The Medori century.** **Bartolo the Clearer** builds the bank by clearing the estates of the plague dead; the Medori buy a seat in Fiorenzia's Roll of Faces in 1238, when the Sweat has left nearly half its houses without a head, and from 1252 carry the payments of the living to the Sending Houses. His granddaughter **Ginevra** keeps the inner books from 1301; her husband **Lauro of the Open Vault**, a mask-maker's son who takes the name in 1311, pays for the merchant-humanist flowering and dies the first hereditary duke (1353).
- **1240–1242 AR — The Schism of the Twin Cardinals.** The plague-thinned conclave elects **Aimery of Corvel**, who dies unsung that night; his twin **Aubert** wears his ring for twenty months and issues the **Ferry Rule** (1241), letting any baptised person sing the funeral where no priest lives. When Aimery's unsent figure is seen at the conclave garden wall (1242), Aubert confesses, and two colleges split over whether his acts stand until both elect **Lord Primarch Gelasin II**, who confirms the Ferry Rule for the plague years only.
- **1241 AR — Prince Mihrzad,** Suhrab's most gifted son, rides the Qarath-frontier forts through the Emptying as a living witness, carries the Sweat into three of them and dies at a well-fort where only his Guard escort survives; his name cannot be released. **1243 — Suhrab's last book,** the book of the Benches, lets a sealed register stand where no witness survives and names the Guard's officer bench a competent witness for the Threshold; Mihrzad is the first released under it; Suhrab dies; **Tahmur** accedes before the Guard bench.
- **1243 AR — The False Lady of Bruckmoor exposed** as an Idol-feeding operation after forty years of pilgrimage — a Weeping Courts architecture that fattened, in its last decade, on Grey Sweat grief; the site burned and salted. The relic courts' founding trauma.
- **c. 1248–1279 AR — Saint Gerlind of Lindenfurt** sings the plague dead out for bread; tried and acquitted 1266; canonised 1284, when her face is printed on assurance letters.
- **1249 AR — The black colt.** Gavrin the Farrier, a Velian slave in a Grass-Tarkhan camp, blesses a dying colt of Grey Sister's line just as the old horse-grace is thinning; its get stand before bound things.
- **1249–1252 AR — The Pageless Prince.** A lodged Qahirid prince whose register page was lost in the plague walks free under the law and raises the Basin's military households; killed 1252. The Cull turns from the lodge to the knife.
- **1249–1296 AR — Evandros the Sower**, resettling the recovered districts and the plague-emptied interior, the work of two generations.
- **1251–1263 AR — The Ossuary Crown** speaks in the voices of the plague dead and testifies in title suits; **1263 — the Ruling of the Hollow Mouth**: "the dead do not testify through things."
- **1252 AR — The Chancery of Sendings.** Gelasin II reorganises assurance letters into Sending Houses that sing named rites for named dead, offering also to sing again rites sung under the Ferry Rule; the Medori carry the payments.
- **1258–1261 AR — The March of the Redeemer's Sword.** Lords with plague-emptied estates march into the deeper cold forest for captives and are broken by winter and the Grobi.
- **1261 AR — The Kurultai of the Long Grass.** **Qarayel, the Black Wind**, herd-master of the black colt's line, is acclaimed under the **Nine-Mane Standard**, braided from the manes of the colt's nine proven sons.
- **1265–1300 AR — The Black Wind Years.** The first long ride burns the great Velian fair (1265); Iqashar pays escort fees because its bound wardens cannot frighten the colt's get; Sestaran caravans pay the grass-toll and trade turns to the sea. **1268 — Marzuq I. 1279–1281 — a Thalmyric embassy tries to buy Gavrin; he is murdered in 1281.** By 1300 the grace has thinned and the payments stop.

## Reformation and the Present (1290–1360 AR)

- **1274–1289 AR — Lord Primarch Alessior II** (House Malvora), an ascetic plague orphan whose election Bartolo Medori's credit buys without his asking. **1282 — Silvio Quarne Cardinal-Treasurer:** printed letters, rates by age, blank letters; **1283 — the Bull of the Sufficient Seal:** a sealed letter places a soul in the Church's keeping before any rite is sung, and sales lose every limit; **1285 — Saint-Leon burned** after a staged apparition; **1287 —** some four hundred thousand letters sealed without charge for Aurelia's plague dead, and parishes stop singing for their poor; from **c. 1288** unsung letters sold to brokers as **dead paper**. **1289 — Alessior dies in debt for his election, and night clerks report him still at his signing desk; the Medori take the Chancery's master registers as surety.**
- **1279–1339 AR — Doctor Lucian Ferro**, notary at Aldersbruck (1299) and canonist at Veridance, writes the Freeflame's order, *The Order of the Open Flame* (c. 1313), as the first half of a refutation he never finishes ("the Fire keeps no landlord"); the Council of Veridance's unvoting witness (1319–1324); refuses Duke Rainald's invitation to Ironvale (1330).
- **c. 1284–1290 AR — Ansgar's Count.** Ansgar, chaplain of the Brenholt Sending House, tallies in the margins of his psalter more than ten letters sold for every rite he is given time to sing, sings the unsung names at night for nothing, and writes beside them the notes later called the *Brenholt Margins*.
- **1288–1291 AR — Meshalem the Twice-Turned**, a road-clerk of Migdal Melach whose wagons the Black Wind passed by, proclaims himself the Mesakha; half the tower towns take the return road under Qarayel's word, and Marzuq I's courts declare their charters forfeit. At the Empty City (1291) he twice goes down the gate stair before the Keepers and the return and twice comes back without touching the gate; the return breaks up. He sweeps the outer court as a mourner for a generation, until his death c. 1316.
- **1290 AR — The [[Freeflame Reformation]] begins.** A thaw-flood takes the bridge at Aldersbruck and breaks into the Blackwater pit at Brenholt. Forty-one miners drown, and Ansgar with them, gone down to sing for the trapped. At the funeral the tally-keeper **Irmel** reads his margins aloud in place of the rite, the widows answering *named, not bought*. The **unbought funerals** spread down the Weiss; by 1298 the Sending Houses of the upper Weiss have no buyers. No legate comes for nine years: until 1295 the Sanctum's legates are on the Caldrian frontier, where the relic court presses its claim in the War of the Crimson Banner, and the Chancery cannot read its own registers, which lie in the Medori vault.
- **c. 1295 AR — Caedros IV Aetherion born.** The Nikandran restoration line continues through the soldier-prince later called Ash-Blood.
- **1296–1329 AR — Stavren the Patient**, who comes late to the throne; keeps the peace on Sestaran credit and founds the war college at Vatrin c. 1300.
- **1299 AR — The Synod of Aldersbruck.** Unable to try a drowned man, the synod tries his book and puts Ansgar's psalter to the brazier; it does not burn. The legate voids the test, vaults **the Unburnt Psalter** in lead, has Irmel flogged and charges the Weiss country a **second fee** for every unbought funeral. Lucian Ferro, the synod's notary, records it all. The reach below the broken bridge, where the Blackwater's drowned went into the river, is **the Preacher's Water**.
- **1299 AR — Silvio Quarne dies in Vorthara**; his ledgers vanish.
- **1301 AR — Marzuq II**; Bartolo Medori dies.
- **1303 AR — Death of [[Qarayel and the Black Wind|Qarayel]]**, undefeated, unsucceeded; ✶ the secret grave: his stallion carries his body out of the camp unled, the grace-mares follow, and the herds come home on the eleventh day without him, in four bands to four pastures. **1304 — the Partition of the Herds:** the assembly names what the horses have done, and the White, Iron, Moon and Ash Hordes take their characters from the grace-line that came home to each. Iqashar hires Iron Horde companies the same year.
- **1306–1329 AR — The Ashenmen Risings.** After five winters of distraint for the second fee, and the sale of the unpaid fees to Vortharan brokers (1304), Brenholt's miners and farmers swear in the Weiss at the broken bridge to fight until the last false seal is burned; the oaths hold. They fight out of the pits and drifts of the upper Weiss, burning seals in the deep galleries and starving the iron country. **1314 — the drowning of the Brenholt drifts** by Caldrian engineers; about three hundred dead.
- **c. 1309–1314 AR — Ferro's commission.** The Sanctum commissions the canonist Lucian Ferro to set out the teaching of the unbought funerals, which nobody has written down, and refute it; he delivers *The Order of the Open Flame* (c. 1313), the reformed towns adopt it whole more than twenty years after their first funeral, and the refutation never comes. His licence to teach is suspended (1314); he is never tried.
- **c. 1310 AR — Covenant night raids** on Thalmyric chapel-forts with Iron Horde horses and devil-mantled riders.
- **1311 AR — The Spring at Sarnwell.** Eleven Ashenmen who had thrown a village's image of the Mother down its well see her set the pieces on the kerb, and the dry well runs over. They swear to it on the Weiss, a relic court takes their depositions (1313), and the healing spring passes every test.
- **1317–1354 AR — Lord Primarch Celestin IX.** The **Council of Veridance** (1319–1324) summons Ferro to finish his refutation and keeps him as its unvoting witness: assurance sales abolished and the Sufficient Seal repudiated; **the Great Arrears** ordered and the night-singers of Saint-Leon licensed to sing them (1320); the singers chartered as the **Order of the Lantern** and the Malverine prisons placed under its review (1324); the five tests codified (1324). The singers' priest, **Father Anselin of Saint-Leon**, had died untried in a Malverine prison-house in 1315.
- **1318 AR — The Night of Seven Flames at Orinel.** Iron-mounted night riders attack; seven signal braziers fail one by one; Caedros Aetherion leads the dawn counterattack and becomes a frontier hero.
- **1319–1322 AR — The Crimson Pilgrimage.** Caedros campaigns on Sermoran and Medori loans, breaks contract engines, and reconsecrates shrines through the Velian frontier.
- **1324–1325 AR — The Siege of Zehrim.** Surrender accepted; captured contract engines and ledgers burned; the altar restored without executing the garrison.
- **1326 AR — The Court of Breaches rules** that Zehrim's people remain bound in the Living Register.
- **1326–1329 AR — The Arrears-levy and the Burning of the Seals.** Celestin lays a levy on every diocese to pay for the registers and the singers (1326) and sends Lantern singers up the Weiss to sing the unbought dead again (1328). Duke Rainald of Ironvale, handed the levy and a roll showing his own father unsung, burns Ironvale's Sending letters at Brenholt (1329). The Ashenmen hold their oath fulfilled and come up out of the pits into his army, and the princes' war over who sings the Arrears and who pays for them begins.
- **1327–1329 AR — Stavren's succession.** The summer flux kills Stavren's sons (1327); Stavren adopts Caedros (1328) and dies (1329).
- **1328 AR — The crossing of the *Naris's Hand*.** The Castaran salt-hauler, under the pilot Ottavia Sele, crosses the Boiling Sea on a day the hurricane belt falls flat calm (✶ the Held Breath) and brings back the Viscaran chart-case; the Viscaran storm-keel is rebuilt by c. 1331.
- **c. 1329 AR — Caedros IV, adopted by Stavren, crowned at Virelos.**
- **c. 1330s AR — Castara opens the Boiling Sea routes;** the New World rush, on the strength of the 1328 charts and the rebuilt storm-keel. **1332 AR — Castara joins the Sestaran compact as the seventh seal.**
- **1333–1334 AR — The First Ash Road Peace.** Castaran surveyors stake wells on Ash pasture for an inland road and the Ash banners burn their camps (1333); in 1334 the city begins annual payment for declared escorts and passage across Ash-Horde country.
- **1339 AR — Lucian Ferro dies at Veridance** and is buried in the university's own yard.
- **1340–1342 AR — Falkendorf, the end of Rainald, the Iron Fast and the Night Count.** The Council of Heldenheim awards the reformed canton of Falkendorf's one churchyard to the old rite and orders its graves moved; Falkendorf rises, Rainald's last son dies at the yard gate, and the war-priest Father Gregor's burial articles end the rising: a yard stays with the rite that has kept its dead sent (1340). **1341** — Rainald dies without heirs; the Grand Ministry chartered. **Winter 1341–1342** — the Grand Ministry banks Ironvale's furnaces and sells iron to neither confession until the princes talk. **1342** — the Sanctum's water-test of the Weiss is declared inconclusive and sealed; the commissioners of the count sit up in disputed churchyards and draw the **Schedule of Yards**.
- **1343 AR — [[The Korrheim Accord]].** *Every roof under its lord's lamp, and every lamp where its dead lie*: every parish yard to the rite that has kept its dead sent, every lord under the lamp of his land's yards (the Sanctum canonists' later half-rendering: *whose the realm, his the rite*). Drafted at Heldenheim and sworn with hands in the Weiss below the broken bridge at Aldersbruck. It leaves out the Unsworn, the river congregations who keep no yard, is silent on the reunification mandate, and satisfies no one.
- **1345–1346 AR — The Bank of the Dead.** A Sermoran pamphlet, printed from extracts sold by the Medori factor **Taddeo Vetti** out of the Debtors' Corps, names Fiorenzian dead whose Sendings were never sung (1345); Vetti is found drowned in a dike the next spring. The wax faces Fiorenzians keep of their dead begin to speak. At the Feast of Souls of 1346 the senate burns them in the **Pyre of Masks**, which Frate Severin begs the city not to light; the crowd goes from the fire to the Medori palace, and **Lauro Medori** opens the vault and gives the city its sheets of the registers.
- **1346–1349 AR — Frate Severin's Long Vigil.** Holding no office, Severin leads Fiorenzia in singing every name on the sheets while its painters paint the burned faces back; crowds drown mask-makers as door-makers, and the appraisers hang a master painter for what his panels show (1348). When the last sheet is sung the citizen households vote the ducal chair hereditary in the Medori line over the senate's head (1349). Severin preaches against it and is found dead in the mask-makers' cellar on the eve of the Feast of Souls, wearing a wax face no one can lift. **Vetti's** guide for factors, printed at Sermora in 1347 as *Of Princes as Borrowers*, circulates banned.
- **1349 AR-present — Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III**, Qahirid suzerain at Iqashar. His layered empire claims Al-Qahur, is rejected by the Sealed-Blood counter-caliphate of Qazmarid, and receives contested tribute from autonomous Zanthir. Witnessed by the Guard bench after the Cull, and cracked.
- **1351 AR — Patriarch Sophron the Unsleeping** enthroned, residing at Caleran.
- **1352 AR — The Lady of the Sloe-Field at Vessing.** Three witnesses at the burial of a runaway's child outside the churchyard wall: a Sanctum sexton, a reformed carter and the Virelian mother. A figure of banked fire; irreconcilable testimonies touching the Kindling Question, each telling against the witness's own church. **The Sanctum has not ruled.**
- **1354 AR — Lord Primarch Alistair III** elected; he inherits the Arrears, the Unburnt Psalter, the Vessing file and the Crown of Embers in one set of vaults.
- **1357 AR — Empress Vasilia of Vlastrava dies.** The emperor's prolonged flame-silences begin after her fortieth-day. The Closed House hearing against the Malverine Inquisitors opens the same year.
- **1358 AR — Prince Nikandros dies** in a skirmish with Iron-mounted raiders in the Velian marches; the succession becomes a three-way struggle between Alexane, Sophios, and adoption.
- **1360 AR — PRESENT DAY.** **Caedros IV Aetherion** reigns in Veridun (regnal names recur; he is the fourth Caedros, not the fourth emperor). Eclessia is split three ways — Sanctum loyalists, Accord-protected reformed duchies, and the 345-year schism with Veridun — making the reunification mandate almost self-parodying. Armed frontier peace with the Covenant. Sestara finances everyone. Grenzburg's crisis year.

## Sanity Checks

Velsar is bound to the Salt Field for 3 years (353–350). Thalion rules 32 years from the closing (c. 350–318), and Samyra lives 17 years at Esharat after her house is put out (350–333). The Face governs Caleran for two winters (247–245), and emperors are made under an arch for 169 years (245–76). The Withholding at Qiryath lasts about 89 years (743–c. 654); Eryndor reigns 22 years (706–684), and the Wars of the Inheritance run 34 (684–c. 650). Mehrava, born 691, is seven at her father's death and about 21 at her marriage (c. 670). Kazren I reigns 37 years (528–491) and Orvaz I 52 (491–439), the Ended Acclamation falling five years before his death (444). In Qahur the seal hangs on Deshren for eleven years (c. 840–c. 829), and the Noon Rent runs 14 (829–815), the first eight with Hesren as his dead son's tenant (829–c. 821).

On the steppe, the black colt is foaled 54 years before Qarayel dies (1249–1303), and the Black Wind's payments run 35 years (1265–1300). Bruckmoor draws pilgrims for 40 years (c. 1203–1243). Aubert wears his brother's ring 20 months (1240–1242); Alessior II reigns 15 years (1274–1289) and Celestin IX 37 (1317–1354); the Ashenmen's oath holds 23 years (1306–1329); the Council of Veridance sits five (1319–1324); the Accord is sworn 53 years after the Blackwater funerals (1290–1343) and 19 years after the Council closed (1324); Severin's Long Vigil is reckoned four years by inclusive count (1346–1349). At present, Ansgar has been dead 70 years (1290) and his psalter vaulted 61 (1299), Ferro dead 21 years (1339), and the confessional map has been frozen by the Accord for 17 years (1343). Caedros IV, born c. 1295, was about 23 at Orinel (1318) and about 34 at his coronation (c. 1329), and is about 65. Nikandros I reigns 24 years (1225–1249), Evandros 47 (1249–1296) and Stavren 33 (1296–1329).

## Local histories beneath the dated spine

The Atlas adds local events at the scale of settlements, households and campaigns without moving the controlling dates above. [[River Leagues and Sea-Kings of the Early World]], [[Towns and Wars of the Long Frontier]] and [[High Noon Provinces and the Qarath Aftermath]] develop the principal thin periods. [[Distant Lands and Otherworld Histories]] uses relative sequences where no AR correspondence is established.

The seventy-five [[Myths and Legendary Histories MOC|localised traditions]] are not seventy-five new datable historical certainties. Their articles distinguish real inherited practices from disputed stories. Six [[Unknown Histories - Authorial Accounts|hidden events]] have separate authorial accounts. None explains a protected Weird or changes a post-1360 outcome.
