# The Country Beneath the Rains — Peoples and History

Below the Emerald Expanse lies a chain of inhabited watersheds, not one limitless cavern. Surface rain brings water, soil, wood, seeds, carcasses and contamination. Falling water draws air through upper mouths; heat lifts foul air toward distant chimneys. Fungi recycle imported energy. No luminous mushroom supplies a whole city with food for nothing.

## The descent into a country

The upper **Green Mouths** contain light wells, terraces and mixed surface–deep households. Below them, **Broken Galleries** reuse Giant freight cuts, mines and reservoirs. **Rain Country** holds the largest river towns. The **Furnace Roots** supply minerals and toxic heat. Individual Made Vaults preserve different legacies; particular False Depths cross into other realms and are not simply deeper caves.

Human settlement predates some Giant works. Later client estates enlarged extraction and moved captive labour through the Great Incline. During the Closing Courts, governors converted freight galleries into refuges for selected workers while barring dependants. The Incline closed amid fighting. Famine and reassembly followed; later Air-Kings claimed ownership of the shafts which kept people breathing.

## Peoples and sovereignties

The **Falling-Water Cities** are independent human communities at cataracts and light wells. Two principal civic traditions are provisionally called **Upper Fall** and **Broken Weir**: geographic labels, not finalized native names. Upper Fall regulates surface exchange through water houses; Broken Weir's assemblies limit hereditary gate ownership after a flood rebellion. Both have drowned rival farms and sealed refugee shafts.

The **Lower-Current Houses** are boat communities organized by kinship, adoption and apprenticeship. Some rescue fugitives; others sell them. Their route knowledge is not a Jirahar inheritance and gives them no Prophet's Roads.

The **Ash-Well Crown** is a human industrial monarchy, including some giant-blooded lines, not a surviving Giant sovereignty. It controls the safer descent into the foundries. Hereditary labour classes maintain furnaces, while royal ancestry claims conceal dependence on captured mechanics.

The **Shut Refuge** is a separate sealed population which survived by excluding others and preserving a narrow set of crafts. Its long enclosure damaged agriculture and political flexibility. Mouth households, meanwhile, trade with surface neighbours and are treated as unreliable by rulers above and below.

## Eleven local histories

A Lower-Current craft household retrieved its founder's tool from an upstream flood and found its handle replaced by a surface child's carving. The returning river had carried human contact, not an ancestral soul.
^atlas-cbr-01

At Broken Weir, a family carried a dying mother's breath in a prepared vessel to maintain one emergency door. Officials later claimed the vessel contained the woman. Her body and departed soul were not the retained air or its memory.
^atlas-cbr-02

Upper Fall once depended on one cultivated root trunk. Its ruler fed the military quay first during disease, starving the outer terraces. Growers grafted a second stock beyond the guards and broke the food monopoly at the cost of many already dead.
^atlas-cbr-03

Two boat Houses exchanged children as navigation apprentices. One child refused return after becoming attached to the foster family; adults turned the exchange into debt-hostage seizure. A later ferry custom lets the grown apprentice choose the boat while leaving compensation to the adults.
^atlas-cbr-04

A surface mouth and a deep ward each closed its side of a threatened passage. People trapped between suffocated. Their descendants mark two doors together because neither community can truthfully blame only the other.
^atlas-cbr-05

Ash-Well furnace households passed dangerous work to children. During a wage revolt, officials cut the strikers' chimney and killed families who had sheltered beside them. Escapers carried pierced clay masks which became signs of refusal.
^atlas-cbr-06
^atlas-cbr-07

A royal repair speaker answered petitioners with maintenance instructions. It was a constructed pattern, not the old king. One heir obeyed its direction to seal a flooded workshop despite knowing people remained inside, then blamed the dead ruler. The surviving repair manual preserves the living heir's responsibility.
^atlas-cbr-08

At the Falling-Water market, a flood bargain exchanged a boat household's seed stock for passage. The buyers later claimed ownership of the household itself. River allies broke the market landing to free them, ending a profitable fair and depriving neighbours of supplies.
^atlas-cbr-09

The Shut Refuge preserved grain infected by a seed-borne blight. Its keepers concealed the blackened seedlings and declining yields, then punished families who planted surface grain. A hungry district destroyed the infected store and accepted a season of reduced rations to establish another crop.
^atlas-cbr-10

One guide family sold outsiders a map of a **Single Kingdom Below**. Its incompatible routes concealed several real sovereignties and monopolized expedition fees. Missing parties and repeated errors expose the fraud; it is not evidence for a hidden Akakor-like people.
^atlas-cbr-11

## Powers and limits

A local Breath-Crown Mask exploits rulers who make air a reward for obedience. A Listener beyond one dry vault is an interested Patron, not sovereign of the entire underground. Neither caused every famine or made the deep peoples. Speaking maintenance systems remain nonpersons unless a separate bearer is established.

The approximate population remains a planning band of 90,000–160,000 across the country, not an exact census. Large dead Giant districts prove that older scale depended on imports which no longer arrive. Cities require visible food and ventilation before any further expansion.

## In 1360

Local surface exchange already exists. Castaran maps are unreliable and often inherit the guide family's monopoly. No completed diplomatic embassy joins the deep sovereignties to the known imperial world. The first formal embassy, its discovery, alliances and consequences remain under the Forward Rule.
