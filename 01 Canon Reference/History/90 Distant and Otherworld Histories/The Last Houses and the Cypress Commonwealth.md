# The Last Houses and the Cypress Commonwealth

This far-distant Orphaned society has no contact with the known world in 1360. Its history is available to the author and locally to its people, not to a Thalmyric library by an invented trade route. “Cypress Commonwealth” is a geographic description, not a finalized native endonym. It is neither Nsizwa nor a secret branch of the Glass People.

## Burial workers after the collapse

The first Houses grew from mortuary fraternities which controlled stores, prepared bodies and protected orphans when a Giant client state failed. Retainers attempted to seize the food; workers defended it and distributed dependants among surviving workshops. Skill, adoption and common obligation initially mattered as much as descent.

Over later generations, control of tomb endowments became political rank. Two living seats separated immediate government from inheritance and public memory. Poor districts maintained elite vaults while their own dead could be excluded from the official lineage. Expulsion from a House could remove home, livelihood and burial standing in one act.

## Cypress and decay

Embalmers discovered that prepared tissue endured in one root-vault only while an adjoining tract was allowed to decay. They fenced the grove and built an art of preservation around the paired conditions. A rich House later tried to keep every body unchanged; the neglected decay tract poisoned the preservation system, destroying remains its owners had called eternal.

The local effect belongs to an existing Menhir correspondence. The controlling Menhir or conjunction is deliberately unidentified until the distant region's full cosmology is established. No new Menhir is created. The bodily preparation, forbidden clearing and failed preservation are historical facts.
^atlas-lh-02

## The court beyond the cypress

An Egg White advisory court developed beside the mortuary polity. Different speakers included living Astral persons acting as Patrons, human delegates, patterned ancestral reconstructions, residue-born presences and offices with no inner life. A familiar voice did not identify the original dead person.

Several Houses bought counsel from copies sharing a copied spelling error. A junior embalmer exposed the common source and found that powerful families preferred the useful counsel to the truth about its origin. The court continued in reduced form after the exposure. It was never one species or a collective human afterlife.
^atlas-lh-03

## One city burns

The **Unclosed Line** rejected the Houses' right to select which lives could remain publicly remembered. Its opponents answered with a purge of one city and dependent wards. People were killed, branded and driven out; stolen mortuary techniques travelled with the refugees. The resulting white-ash customs belong to those displaced communities, not to a civilization-wide civil war.
^atlas-lh-04

## Keepers and mantle households

The **Keepers of the Last Door** are living investigators of speaking tombs. They began after a mistaken destruction killed a living captive whom officials had declared an ancestral impostor. Their four clay-bird marks warn that a body, voice, memory and person must be tested separately. No Keeper holds the Boatman's office.
^atlas-lh-05

**Mantle Houses** inherit armour and unfinished duties through living bearers. Smiths resize the suit, sometimes dramatically. Custodians later forced heirs to adopt a predecessor's name, marriage and old vendettas. A split household divided the armour so that no claimant could impersonate one unbroken hero. The later dance celebrates several people wearing altered pieces, not a soul passed through metal.
^atlas-lh-06

## The local present

The Houses remain a powerful mortuary oligarchy alongside mobile households, refugees and court clients. Their craft preserves some things unusually well while infrastructure and ordinary medicine remain uneven. None of the six traditions proves an ancestor's human soul has been recalled. Genuine sainthood, a held human soul, residue and a copy would each require separate identification.
