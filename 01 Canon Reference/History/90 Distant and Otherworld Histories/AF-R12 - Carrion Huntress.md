# The Carrion Huntress

These are historical epithets rather than a new universal infernal rank. The power described here is real; claims to cosmic necessity and moral title are not.

## The being behind the title

One autonomous Gluttony idol-pattern Archmask with a bounded martial route leased from Wrath.

## The dominion

Leads moving feast-hunts through Gluttony margins, Wrath roads and occasional Hell markets. Participation, not ancestry or species, makes a pack: initiates must hunt, kill and eat a named victim together.

## Project

Make shared atrocity the only bond members trust, so betrayal of the Hunt feels worse than murder of an outsider.

## Victims and methods

Engineered starvation, prisoner hunts, cannibal initiation, massacre and weak pack members consumed as proof that fellowship outranks pity.

## Separate histories

[[The Long Table]] is a wider Gluttony network of feast power; [[The Red Tally]] prices sacrificial violence through Wrath. The Huntress is one autonomous Archmask whose specific rite joins killing, pursuit and consumption.

## A particular history

### The feast on the snow road

A separate Grobi-facing Ättar upland settlement, c. 1160–1200 AR, lost its winter caravan. The Huntress's agents diverted food, then offered a hunt whose final quarry was a captive neighbour. Participants killed and ate the prisoner together. When the missing provisions were found, the leaders concealed them because hunger no longer excused what they had done.

A woman who had taken part refused the next hunt when her injured brother was named as quarry. She led him away and warned a rival household, admitting her own guilt. The pack burned the abandoned home. A later winter telling names the first victim before the escaping siblings; their courage never expunges the shared murder. Natural wolves and unrelated famine survivors are not members of this pack.

## In 1360

The Huntress and at least one pack remain active. Natural predators, hungry refugees and every act of battlefield cannibalism do not automatically belong to her.
