# Menhir Courts through the Ages

The Faewild is not one fairy kingdom. The Menhir remain enduring powers; the smaller persons, households and courts around them can migrate, feud, be overthrown or lose access to a place. A court's exact bargain is a fact about what it can do, not proof that it owns the people who suffer from it.

## The lost surface

**Later Brauvalis–Green March country, c. 2320–2260 BR.** Giant clients excavated a surface settlement and drove its Hecatara–Sylvorn court from visible dwellings. The court kept seasonal wells, cellars and thresholds. After the Giants' fall, it demanded fosterlings and tolls from Orphaned survivors who had not carried out the conquest. Families disappeared when they used a familiar cellar in the wrong season. Rescuers killed supposed collaborators on both sides. The surviving spring meals leave a child's place untouched: a memory of coerced fosterage, not a deed making later occupants thieves.
^atlas-mn-r02

## Several kings under several hills

**Folk uplands adjoining Brauvalis, c. 1500–1300 BR.** Several Sylvorn-facing mound courts competed for cattle ways and the artisans who maintained their entrances. Two mortal claimant houses buried a disputed elder under different royal signs. A court mutilated a rival office-holder, hoping to prevent him performing the return rite; human reprisals burned tenant houses. The settlement left one office vacant and divided the cattle route by season. There was never one mound king ruling every Folk barrow.
^atlas-mn-r01

## The withheld season

**Tidewood–Gale-Crown watershed, c. 1850–1725 BR.** A compact preserved winter stores, bodies and seed. Rulers refused the release on which that preservation depended. Nevaura–Morvalis pressure, carried through Hecatara-aligned routes, produced one temporary Hunt. It took corpses, living people, pregnant animals and needed food until the blocked seasonal way was opened. The Hunt was not an army of human dead or a permanent high monarchy. [[G-67 - The Marks of Necessary Release]] preserves the later domestic warning.
^atlas-mn-r10r

## The fortress which seemed to turn

**A pre-imperial coastal siege, c. 1050–900 BR.** A Hecatara–Thalassor stronghold could be reached only when tide, wind and the approach path coincided. Besiegers thought the walls revolved and repeatedly marched captives toward the entrance to discover its timing. Most were lost. A local pilot refused the next test and led several prisoners away; the army burned the pilot's home. The fortress's motions were changes of approach, not a travelling Patron city. A broken causeway and a tide-song retain the failed siege.
^atlas-mn-r04

## The stolen tool and the winter rowers

The [[G-74 - The Raid That Returned One Tool|sounding-weight raid]], c. 1120–980 BR, belongs to the Arkenan–Saronikan corridor. The [[G-75 - The Empty Oar of the Winter Tithe|Winter Tithe]], c. 230–310 AR, belongs to the western Kedravan coast. The first was a culpable human assault upon a populated court. The second involved a shore court paying an offshore court in rowers and route-watchers, substituting fishers, fosterlings and provincial prisoners. One rescuer's success left a vacancy until the underlying obligation was attacked. The two courts were not infernal, and the events were not one voyage.
^atlas-mn-r03r
^atlas-mn-r05

## Procession, treasure and contrary shores

**Western Folk country, c. 650–780 AR.** A night procession included court persons and living practitioners. A jealous relative denounced a returning participant, and officials tortured the accused into describing every neighbour as a member. The prosecution killed people who had never attended; the actual procession did not make the confessions true. A household keeps the accused's torn dancing shoe rather than the court's list.
^atlas-mn-r06r

**A hill above an early northern trade route, c. 1200–1000 BR.** Human claimant houses broke into a barrow treasury whose metal maintained a local court boundary. Removing it damaged surrounding households as well as enriching the raiders. The ensuing war killed custodians and tenants; a truce returned only the part needed to restore the boundary. The remaining treasure remains disputed, not unowned loot.
^atlas-mn-r07r

**A later Sestaran-facing coast, c. 950–1080 AR.** Fishing and mining households called two contrary local manifestations the Buccas. One bargain cleared a storm approach while the other required an undisturbed coastal seam. A marriage joined families dependent on opposite promises. When a mine expansion broke the seam, the fishers cut their relatives' hauling road and the mining house withheld winter fuel. Their later paired offerings remember a compromise made after deaths, not two moral halves of one god.
^atlas-mn-r09

## Under mountains and beyond mastery

The Aversian [[G-68 - The Three Knocks under the Mountain|Knocker compact]], c. 1110–1170 AR, joins sapient mine courtiers, nonpersonal Stone-Voice responses and ordinary miners. Officials ignored warnings, forced prisoners into lethal workings and executed alleged defaulters. It establishes no knocker species.
^atlas-mn-r08

In a far-southern chain beyond the outer sea, locally compared with c. 1080–1160 AR, a summit court fled when deeper altitude and exposure laws overwhelmed it. Its rulers seized human shelters and sacrificed guides during descent. The Orphaned highland people preserve cairns for the abandoned. They remain uncontacted by the core setting in 1360.
^atlas-mn-r11

A different remote ridge supports the **Court above the Unanswering Engine**. Its courtiers claimed to command an immense dormant Great Coherence. When the impersonal pattern shifted, the court could not persuade it to spare a tenant ledge and fled before the people living there. This was neither Trium's capital nor an outer god awakening. The court's lie concerned mastery; the deeper pattern had never promised anything.
^atlas-mn-r12r

## The surviving distinction

Court persons, human fosterlings, animals, place-minds, offices and natural responses must not be counted as one species. The defeated can become oppressors; a successful bargain can serve a murderer. The Lord's moral order is not replaced by the precision of a Menhir's terms.
