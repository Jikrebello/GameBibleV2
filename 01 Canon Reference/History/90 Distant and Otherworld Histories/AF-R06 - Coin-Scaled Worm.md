# The Coin-Scaled Worm

These are historical epithets rather than a new universal infernal rank. The power described here is real; claims to cosmic necessity and moral title are not.

## The being behind the title

One autonomous Greed idol-pattern Archmask leasing several infernal markets.

## The dominion

Moves between contract ports as a coin- and deed-scaled lamprey. It purchases prisoners from rulers, then lends those rulers the standing required to claim that no sale occurred.

## Project

Demonstrate that every relationship has a liquidation price, including dignity, kinship, burial and pardon.

## Victims and methods

Sale of children and dependants, famine pricing, marriage as collateral, betrayal for debt relief and servants punished to preserve the Worm's credit.

## Separate histories

[[The Creditor]] builds long-term anchor rent and concealed beneficial ownership; the Worm seeks conspicuous transactions, immediate humiliation and proof that the seller knew the price.

## A particular history

### The dowry weighed against bread

During a famine in a small imperial grain port, c. 460–500 AR, a merchant house borrowed against a daughter's promised marriage. The Worm's agents offered to discharge the debt if the family publicly priced the daughter, her dependants and their burial places as saleable goods. The father accepted, then tried to save his social position by calling the sale a generous alliance.

The daughter and several servants fled on a grain barge. Their escape cost the remaining household its stores; agents sold younger dependants when the promised price was not met. Later dockside wedding meals set aside a ration which no marriage payment may buy. Some rich families keep the custom while continuing to deal in the Worm's collateral. The Archmask's victory lay in an informed sale, not in creating scarcity.

## In 1360

Several infernal exchanges recognize its scales as transferable collateral. No Covenant revelation or 4,444 mantle belongs to it.
