# Distant Lands and Otherworld Histories

## Atlas narrative

The following account distinguishes established history from the received stories named within it.

## More than the known world's margin

The familiar empires occupy only part of inhabited history. Beyond them, people survived Giant collapse through different combinations of retreat, technology, captivity, alliance and loss. Distance is not a smooth circle of declining civilization. A ruined engineered landscape may contain an advanced enclave, stone-tool villages, dangerous old machinery and a powerful predatory state within the same watershed.

These regions have histories before explorers arrive. Their past is not a prelude to first contact, and their present need not be fixed by it. The Atlas distinguishes what the author knows from what any expedition in 1360 could learn.

## The human worlds

[[The Last Houses and the Cypress Commonwealth]] follows an uncontacted Orphaned people whose care of the dead became an oligarchic political order. Its Houses, court speakers and mortuary challengers are not one collective afterlife. Its purge struck one city and dependent wards, not an entire continent.

[[The Country Beneath the Rains - Peoples and History]] describes inhabited watersheds beneath the Emerald Expanse. Light, air, food and transport place real limits upon its cities. It is neither an endless natural dungeon nor a single kingdom. Surface contact has occurred locally; no formal embassy with the core setting has been completed.

[[Nythalum after the Ascension]] follows a different direction. Its ancestors attempted physical lunar escape. A genuine surviving Continuant is the same Giant through uninterrupted operation, not a restored human soul and not a backup which happened to speak convincingly.

## Powers with their own histories

[[Menhir Courts through the Ages]] records lost surfaces, seasonal hunts, sea raids and abandoned mountains. [[Idol Kingdoms and the Hunger Wars]] follows predatory cultural faces and rival projects without making humanity their creator. [[Patron Cities and the Long Rent Histories]] describes refugees, owners, routes and households whose interests do not coincide.

[[Infernal Kings and the Wars of the Depth]] concerns a cold, black, earthen and watery Hell. Fire and brimstone belong to Wrath's separate ecology. Infernal wars arise from incompatible ambitions, captive labour, offices and wounds; neither side is secretly necessary to keep creation functioning.

[[Heavenly Campaigns and the Human Dead]] records judgement, defence, retreat, failed mortal cooperation and bounded intercession. Loyal angels and redeemed Saints do not fall. Their manifestations can be defeated without changing their allegiance or destroying the person.

## Made shelter at the edge of matter

[[Constructed Shell Settlements]] describes refuges maintained against otherwise uninhabitable elemental conditions. The Shell has no native human-like civilization. Its pure patterns do not become people through complicated speech. Living inhabitants came from elsewhere; a body, soul, office and mechanism remain different things even when outsiders call all of them “elementals.”

## Reading parallel time

Some episodes can be related to an imperial date because a human traveller returned. Others have only an internal sequence: a court existed before a particular flight; a city was rebuilt after one anchor failed; a ruler held an office after its former bearer was expelled. The Atlas does not invent an AR year merely to make a table look complete.

The histories remain separate. A repeated image—a gate, a serpent, a feast or a stolen name—is not evidence that all regions share one secret ruler or that every disaster belongs to one cosmic war.
