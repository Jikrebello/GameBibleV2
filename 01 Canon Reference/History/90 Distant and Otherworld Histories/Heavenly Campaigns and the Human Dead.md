# Heavenly Campaigns and the Human Dead

Heaven is not another ambitious empire competing for a share of creation. The Lord commissions loyal created angels and redeemed human Saints. Their service can involve judgement, terrifying defence, pursuit, retreat, healing or refusal. Saints retain their earthly identities; angels are not saints born before humanity. Neither can be compelled by a city, order or collection of relics.

These events have different people, places and commissions. The Taken Trumpet's defeated manifestation did not fall. The Threshing Floor's bounded judgement did not cause the Grey Sweat. The Guardian's inability to prevent death does not mean abandonment of the person. The voluntary wall contained both willing and coerced deaths; later piety must not erase the difference.

## Lesser received traditions

The following are established regional tellings, not automatic authentication of another angelic person. Their ordinary settings and consequences are part of local history; their celestial identities remain claims.

**The Crow and the Accused — Folk–provincial border, c. 460–520 AR.** A jealous kinsman accused a woman of leading raiders to a winter store. A crow repeatedly carried the stolen store-cord to his own roof. Neighbours stopped the execution and found the concealed goods, but another accused person had already died. The tradition warns against both lying and expecting a bird to make every judgement for humans.
^atlas-hc-n04

**The Attendant in the Spear Field — frontier campaigning, c. 640–680 AR.** A surgeon refused a commander's order to leave enemy wounded beside a burning wagon. Tellers describe a small luminous attendant holding the smoke away while ordinary hands carried them. The commander imprisoned the surgeon; several patients died despite the rescue.
^atlas-hc-n06

**The Owl at the Uncrossed Line — Velian orchards, c. 820–860 AR.** Couriers found that a corrupted night signal drew travellers toward a raiding camp. An owl-shaped warning held one runner at the last safe branch. The runner broke the beacon instead of following it and lost the pay on which a sick household depended. This was a local signal route, not the soul-road.
^atlas-hc-n07

**The Captain of the Sacrificial Wind — a late-frontier retreat, c. 850–875 AR.** A mortal subordinate urged continued war after the stated rescue was complete. The received heavenly captain refused the pursuit and protected withdrawal instead. The subordinate attacked without support and died with his followers. The tradition is about accepting the end of a task, not an angel assuming command of a nation.
^atlas-hc-n09

**The Inheritor Who Inherited No Soul — Reconquest border, c. 980–1020 AR.** A new captain received a dead predecessor's arms and duties. An order demanded the old name and claimed the predecessor spoke through the bearer. The captain exposed a copied voice, refused an inherited massacre and lost the office. One human had not become another.
^atlas-hc-n12

**The Staff after the Long Silence — Aversian countryside, c. 1180–1220 AR.** A wandering teacher borrowed an old staff to shelter expelled workers. Custodians later sold access to it as returned universal revelation. The teacher broke the display fitting and returned to ordinary service; disappointed followers drove the household away. No second Prophet appeared.
^atlas-hc-n14

**The Eastern Road Choir — an old Jirahar-facing caravan route, c. 700–650 BR.** Traffickers made a permanent road into an abduction passage. A received winged choir closed that route while an ordinary caravan carried the last freed captives around it. Merchants lost their investment; reopened imitations later killed travellers. The route was not in the Glass.
^atlas-oba-n01

**The Beaten Animal's Road — early Ahdari wells, c. 1700–1550 BR.** A hired curse-seer beat a pack animal which would not pass a narrow cutting. Its resistance exposed an ambush and, in the received version, a terrible sword-bearing messenger. The rider had to release a threatened household rather than bless the employers' war.
^atlas-oba-n03

**Bread under the Last Tree — a southern provincial exile road, c. 470–510 AR.** A punished baker left bread for one exhausted traveller and was beaten for wasting a ration. The tale remembers an Attendant carrying the food, but not creating an inexhaustible store. A later monastery's attempt to claim the whole road from this mercy failed when the baker's descendants named the beating.
^atlas-oba-n05

**The Physician with a Borrowed Road-Name — a Jirahar household journey, c. 720–760 AR.** A stranger helped a sick traveller reach a marriage and recover a relative's body. The family mistook sound medical work for proof of the stranger's ancestry. Later tellers recognized an angel; the household's care, marriage and burial endured without proving that name.
^atlas-oba-n09

**Riders at the Seized Treasury — a provincial sanctuary, c. 420–455 AR.** Officers tried to take relief wealth for a ruler's war. The keeper was nearly beaten to death; the received riders drove the raiders away. Survivors later discovered that the keeper had also concealed a poor district's share. Rescue of the treasury did not acquit its custodian.
^atlas-oba-n10

**The Herald at the Unbarred Prison — eastern inland works, c. 120–160 AR.** A prison opened during a commanded release. Guards who let captives leave were punished by their own officers; some fled with the prisoners and some were executed. The received Herald saved a passage without granting universal innocence or making the guards' sacrifice disappear.
^atlas-oba-n12

**The Ended Acclamation — a Star-age ruler cult, c. 480–430 BR.** A ruler accepted worship as the source of a harvest and began demanding children for the cult. A frightening interruption ended the public acclamation; subjects later disputed whether sickness or a messenger struck the ruler. The Pride route had grown through chosen worship, not because the people created an Idol.
^atlas-oba-n13

**The Contested Body — a Sestaran-facing town, c. 1280–1310 AR.** Two institutions claimed a dead craftswoman whose family sought ordinary burial. A received guardian prevented one theft while neighbours carried the coffin. The sign did not prove sainthood or transfer ownership of the woman to the institution which witnessed it.
^atlas-oba-n14

## Breachward workers and frightening schools

The **Three-Lock Prison Road**, c. 145–205 AR, developed through medicine, ransom and custody passage at three separate inland sites. Its massacre and betrayal are not the Red-Passage Witness's history. The **Rule of Five Closures**, c. 680–760, belongs to one mixed frontier corridor: a mnemonic learned through failed rescues, not five universal metaphysical laws.
^atlas-sb-p01
^atlas-sb-p02

The **School of the Open Cinder**, c. 760–840, combined stolen techniques from several sources. Its members tortured captured wardens, used refugees in trials and sold escapes which closed behind the poor. Governments employed specialists they publicly condemned. It was not simply the evil branch of the Five Closures.
^atlas-sb-p03

The **Saint of the Two Ferries**, c. 996–1035, is distinct from the landlocked enemy-gate intercession. The **Saint above the Harbour Wall**, c. 1120–1180, had first been executed by that city for opening refuge to outsiders; later defenders asked the same person to save them. A commission could protect the endangered without vindicating the rulers who killed the rescuer.
^atlas-sb-p04
^atlas-sb-p05

The **Closed House** records abuse before 1360 and an unfinished present investigation. A purported founder's return, verdict, restitution and institutional survival remain undecided.
^atlas-sb-p06

Other local Lives preserve a **Hospital without Fees** whose founders treated a dying debtor while later managers charged for burial; a **Sword Stopped at the Block** whose interruption saved one condemned person but left a murdered witness unrevived; and a **Refused Translation** in which moving a body would have emptied a living refuge of its endowment. None lets an institution summon its founder.
^atlas-st-r06
^atlas-st-r07
^atlas-st-r09

## What is not being multiplied

The proposed Phoenix at the First Binding and its associated Coil-Wing choir remain protected. The existing Great Unbinding commission is not reused as a second operation merely to fill the heavenly population. No saintly order inherits the Boatman's soul-road office. Shared saints, common titles and borrowed practices do not create a single international army.
