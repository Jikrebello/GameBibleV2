# Infernal Kings and the Wars of the Depth

Hell is black, wet, earthen and oppressive. Its dominions are built through weight, pressure, custody, appetite and the loss of room to move. A devil office is not a species, and an eel-like sovereign is not necessarily a True Dragon. Damned human souls, primordial fallen spirits, native fauna, imposed offices, Idol Archmasks and Abyssal wounds retain their separate natures.

## Why they wage war

There is no single Blood War required to keep the world balanced. The Quota Kingdom wants every rival's roads to remit captives or power upward. The Mire city profits when sentences fail and freed people become its dependants. The Wound Arsenal wants to turn dangerous contradictions into repeatable weapons. A wound-sovereign may destroy the distance another ruler needs in order to possess territory. These purposes can collide without either army becoming good.

The **Claimed First Front** is a lie of necessity: its ruler says that every atrocity is required to protect creation. The Drowned Standard sacrifices protected populations to sustain that claim. Its real refusal belongs to the primordial rebellion; stories of a later loyal angel falling in a human war are false.

## Six human-facing histories

In the Late Chains, a lesser court continued issuing work under dead specialists' names. Mortuary workers resisted while captives were left below a sealed service stair. This **Lower Roster Refusal** is separate from living prisoners used as boundaries.
^atlas-ip-01

During the late City-States, a grain league accepted the Quota King's settlement of a rivalry among seven courts. A household outside the grain gate starved while the seventh receipt preserved a profitable exemption. Its surviving grain-house custom breaks the ceremonial seventh loaf before counting tribute. The event did not create the Creditor or the Covenant's devil mantles.
^atlas-ip-02

An Underground provincial prison-port fed the **Lady of the Mire Keys**. She enabled escapes which placed replacements in the opened cells. A betrayed escapee family exposed one exchange; guards murdered its witness and the Lady sheltered the survivors on terms which kept them useful. Affection within her court did not excuse prison rape, hostage-taking or judicial murder.
^atlas-ip-03

During Conversion, the **War of Two Exceptions** pitted quota and escape jurisdictions over one prisoner. A clerk smuggled the prisoner away rather than publish a rule which would transfer someone else into custody. Both courts retaliated against the clerks' households. The escape denied one remittance; it did not abolish infernal law.
^atlas-ip-04

A Long-Frontier route hospital became the **House of Divided Instruments**. Its first patient consented to a dangerous procedure which really helped. The Surgeon then withheld a nonexperimental cure, kept captives conscious for trials and killed an assistant trying to release the casualty record. Independent safety knowledge later broke one monopoly without healing the whole wound-front.
^atlas-ip-05

During the Fall, a sanctuary town admitted the **Exile Behind the Borrowed Temple** through a mortal commander's promise of shared rule. Bodies were stolen for dragon-shaped war-broods and prisoners killed to stabilize entry. Local defenders rescued captives and restored the sanctuary's actual purpose, breaking that return attempt. Neither the commander nor this temple is the source of the wider Fall.
^atlas-ip-06

## Captains, mutinies and drowned cities

The **Black Companies** sell military service between dominions. One captain changed employers during a siege and delivered a human town as payment; surviving mercenaries later refused the same sale of their own families. The refusal fractured the company rather than redeeming all its work.
^atlas-hl-r03

The **Hunger That Refuses Rule** belongs to a damned human raider who treats the destruction of all shelters as freedom. A household which welcomed the raider learned that refusing a crown does not prevent domination. This is not a native moral abstraction or a Gluttony Throne.
^atlas-hl-r04r

During the **Mutiny of Broken Mantles**, damned bearers killed commanders and seized their offices, then continued operating the same prison labour. The underlying people and the machinery of authority were distinct; rebellion had not freed the captives.
^atlas-hl-r08r

Two **damned kings**, formerly rival captains in one Atherian frontier war, later fought over a drowned city. Their former friendship made betrayal personal without making the city's inhabitants mere illustrations of it. A ferryman district withheld transport from both and was flooded in reprisal.
^atlas-hl-r10

**Undertow privateers** raid across individual routes, not every sea. One captain is a damned human; the vessel is a constructed carrier. When captured families destroyed its return fittings, surviving offices could not replace the captain's knowledge. The wreck remains a local hazard.
^atlas-hl-r11r
