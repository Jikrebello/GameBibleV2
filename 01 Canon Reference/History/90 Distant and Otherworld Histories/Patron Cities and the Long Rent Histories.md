# Patron Cities and the Long Rent Histories

Patron is a relationship to an anchor, not a species or a divine rank. Egg White cities can contain humans, Driftkin, Astral persons, genuine surviving Giants, servants, fauna and nonpersonal machinery. The person who owns the anchor need not be the person who governs the streets.

## Flight and the abandoned

During the Great Unbinding, one lesser Giant court carried inhabited districts into the Egg White after stripping poorer wards of their support. The [[G-18 - The Falling Wards|Falling Wards]] remembers physical severing. A separate caravan sent copied ancestors along false routes and misdirected a Hunt into refugees: [[G-62 - The Three False Processions]]. Their ruler, tenants, objects and current whereabouts are not shared.

Mobile palace kingdoms subsequently fought through rent, marriage and the order in which districts attached to the travelling anchor. Tenants once delayed a court by refusing to attach the slaughter-yard before the living quarters. Soldiers punished them, but the delay let another ward escape. The palace survived without recovering every subject it claimed.
^atlas-pt-r06

## The Second Hearth

**After the southern departure, c. 2120–1780 BR.** A refugee city began with carried matter, builders and a founder's remembered domestic routes. Affection helped stabilize its streets; craft and labour made it habitable. The founder neglected a living family while preserving an ideal childhood. Later citizens refused to let reconstructed memories determine who belonged.

The city eventually broke into surviving anchor fragments and displaced households. Its founders' heirs had denied residents absent from the beloved old memory and withdrawn their maintenance. [[G-19 - The Hearth Carried Twice]] preserves the survivors' adoption rite. This is a lost early polity, not Chalkhaven or the Unfinished Shore, and its copies were not returned human souls.
^atlas-pt-r02r

## The port and the absent court

**Long Frontier, c. 680–850 AR.** The Port of Borrowed Standing arose on a Zanthiri-facing route through contributed anchors. Human, Driftkin and Astral governors limited outside owners' seizure rights. Poor residents maintained the physical city while rich founders supplied less visible support. Rival Patrons kidnapped people just outside protection and made witnesses indispensable enough to enslave. One escapee family was rescued only after another household was delivered as replacement; later survivors broke the tongue-seal ceremony in public. The ruined port custom remains separate from Vorthara.
^atlas-pt-r01

In the **Absent Court of the Desired City**, an Astral owner departed for a more desirable refuge while leaving rent demands operating. Regents sold tenant labour to maintain a palace nobody visited. A kitchen district stopped preparing ceremonial meals and fed its children instead; the regents shut its water. The ensuing revolt ended meal tribute but not dependence on the absent owner's anchor. Its history follows the Second Hearth's remembered collapse, without sharing its founder or population.
^atlas-pt-r03

## Annexation and the black galleys

A **Realm-Drag** took part of a Material river district during a later frontier succession war. The Anchor-Lord died before completing the annexation. Proxies fought over whether to restore the farms or auction the half-moved neighbourhood. Families cut their own houses loose, losing stored grain and relatives in the attempt. Their land's current owners remain disputed; the event did not annex an entire province.
^atlas-pt-r05

The **Black-Galley League** grew through trade between hostile Patron realms after the first escaped courts. Captains trafficked residents as cargo and used enslaved navigators. A navigator withheld a true return name after his daughter was sold, stranding buyers and crew together. The league's later oath forbids that betrayal of crew families while continuing to permit atrocities against outsiders. No galley is part of the Thirty-First Crew.
^atlas-pt-r07

## Warning tales and three aligned fortresses

Patron-city storytellers also tell the **Dead City of Warnings**. Its warning is the folly of reading an inhabited city's catastrophe as an invitation to collect its wealth. This telling belongs to the [[Constructed Shell Settlements#^atlas-el-r02|dead brass city's]] received history; it does not establish a second ruined polity in the Egg White. Travellers often mistake a story's place of performance for the place where it happened.
^atlas-pt-r08

Three independently governed fortresses later entered a defensive alignment. Each relied on the others at the vulnerable interval. When one refused a war levy, the other two used the alignment to sacrifice it. The surviving refugees remember three governments and two betrayals, not one three-bodied sovereign. Their history follows the first Black-Galley trading circuits; no artificial Material year is assigned.
^atlas-pt-r09r

## Exiles and short mercy

The **Treasury of the Unreturned**, c. 905–970 AR, sheltered refugees and wealth from the Fall. Its living and Astral administrators made return expeditions a source of rent. Heirs eventually argued that exile was their home, while a restoration cult demanded continued sacrifice. Its people and treasury are separate from the municipal Second Sack and the single Bread-Jar house.
^atlas-pt-r10r

During the Grey Sweat, the **Palace of the Short Mercy** admitted plague refugees without honestly disclosing how return time differed. Three nights inside could consume years outside. Some returned to dead spouses and inherited children older than themselves. The host's rescue was real and culpably misleading. The unequal time did not cause the Sweat or the Quiet Ninth.
^atlas-pt-r11

## The regent and the unfinished fortress

A **Regent of Dormant Anchors** sold interpretations of several remote owners' outputs. The owners were separately real persons; their proxies and machines were not fragments of one mind. After the regent died, rival interpreters fought over a grain convoy while its cargo spoiled. This local succession ended one monopoly without revealing every owner's purpose.
^atlas-pt-r12r

The **Name-Fortress** has an unfinished 1360 dispute. Veterans have disappeared, claimants offer conflicting credentials and surviving copies speak old names. The archive is not the former castellan's soul. No current claimant, authentic manifestation, disclosure or final outcome is selected.
^atlas-pt-r04
