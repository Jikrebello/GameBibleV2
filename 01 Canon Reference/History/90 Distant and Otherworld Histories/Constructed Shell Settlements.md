# Constructed Shell Settlements

The Shell is not a country waiting to be colonized. Its apparent landscapes form around maintained reference frames. Air, food, water, direction, waste disposal and a way home must be supplied. Pure Shell patterns and constructed simulations are not persons. Human refugees, Driftkin, Astral people and living animals retain their own natures.

## Brass that still sheltered people

**A stranded legacy Fire platform, established before the late imperial frontier.** The Living Brass Refuge grew around a carried stone civic core, ceramic cooling walls and a leased Astral ballast. Its rulers were living human guild oligarchs; an Astral owner controlled the anchor, not every inhabitant. Refuge became hereditary maintenance debt. Officials sealed slaves, prisoners and insolvent households behind cooling doors to save wealthy districts. A revolt captured the water exchangers but could not dispense with them.

The old Material threshold failed before 1360. Later Astral trade kept portions inhabited; no repeatable fifteenth anchor is created. This large city is separate from UH-04's small failed furnace refuge. Its most valuable craft is reliable cooling, not a magic source of food.

## Brass that became a warning

A different Fire city died when its ruler cut water to rebellious wards and sealed the return route to prevent flight. Pressure and heat propagated inward until the palace also failed. A later expedition mistook the surviving automata and ornamental fountains for a habitable court. Its leader opened a drinking basin despite a warning and died; the survivors left their treasure to carry one injured companion home. The city is a ruin, not the future of the Living Brass Refuge. Egg White tellers call its received story the Dead City of Warnings; that name does not establish a second city.
^atlas-el-r02

## The pressure-brine court

**A post-imperial legacy Water frame beyond a drowned coast.** Shipwrecked human households, Driftkin traders and human divers altered for pressure work built sealed galleries around carried stone. Every human retained a complete human soul; the Driftkin retained their separate lives and were not manufactured by the frame. The council controlled air and access to the outer pressure channels. A ruler offered citizenship to stranded arrivals, then assigned their children to dangerous exterior work. A strike disabled one pressure intake and drowned both guards and workers before a settlement reduced the hereditary obligation.

The pressure workers were not a native mermaid or triton people. Their altered bodies made a return to ordinary surface labour difficult, and their families continued depending on the galleries after hereditary service ended. The court remains unconnected to a repeatable Material gate.
^atlas-el-r03r

## The city carried by a wounded animal

A Water-littoral city was fastened to one immense non-sapient Material leviathan. Living households depended upon its feeding migrations. Officials drugged and cut the creature to steer it, then detached poor districts when leaking frames threatened the rest. A coastal community tried to divert the carrier after an impossible wake destroyed fishing villages. City leaders answered that killing it would murder their people while concealing less destructive choices they had refused.

The ensuing diversion saved part of the coast and stranded a detached quarter. The animal's later route is unknown. It is not a world-turtle species, Menhir body, Great Coherence or True Dragon.
^atlas-el-r04r

## The fleet that surrendered home

An Air-littoral refugee fleet formed during regional wars after imperial Conversion. Human captains governed Material vessels; an Astral Anchor-Lord controlled stable arrival. Navigators first contributed remembered routes freely, then captains extracted them from prisoners and children. Donors forgot home signs without losing parts of their souls.

A mutiny broke formation and exposed two vessels to lethal pressure failure. Survivors won the right to retain copied return instructions, but still could not compel the Anchor-Lord to provide an arrival. Gardens, condensers, livestock and imported provisions sustained the remaining ships. There was no native cloud civilization.
^atlas-el-r05r

## Weight and the seam marches

A legacy Earth platform became the **Weight-Seed Kingdom** under a human mining dynasty during the Long Frontier. Its valuable pellets continued reinforcing after placement. Rulers sealed dissidents inside strengthening walls; a miner misdirected a supply batch to reopen a prison and collapsed the adjoining store. Later workers test each batch against a sacrificial block because neither age nor a royal stamp makes the material safe.
^atlas-el-r06

The **Seam Marches** were separately founded Water–Air platforms, not natural para-elemental nations. Their shared conductor carried useful cooling and dangerous surges. In a border war one town diverted a surge into its rival and discovered that the same line kept its own nursery breathable. A repair truce followed deaths on both sides. The line survives in sections; it is not an additional plane.
^atlas-el-r07r

## The hidden quarter at Blackwall

**Blackwall Compression Yard, Ironvale; founded in the 1320s–1340s, concealed in 1360.** Abandoned delvers and fugitive work households extended unused Earth-side maintenance chambers. Their refuge borrows life support from this one existing anchor. A separately owned Astral emergency tether belongs to a local Astral person; it does not create another Material installation.

The household council falsifies some casualties and diverts capacity from uninformed crews. Children have been born inside. Discovery could end concealment or bring conscription, expulsion or a deliberate safety purge. No disclosure, rescue, sabotage or survival outcome is fixed. “Fourteen-Anchor Free Port” remains a scholars' comparison, not the name of a city owning all fourteen.
^atlas-el-r08r
