# Idol Kingdoms and the Hunger Wars

The Idols were not made by human belief. Humanity supplies imagery, biography, institutions and repeated choices through which existing Hungers feed. Each Idol remains one guilty sovereignty: betrayed commission, Hunger, Throne, Regnant, Masks, autonomous Archmasks and historical Sediment are not interchangeable persons.

## A victory divided

**Sarrakai, Late Chains.** A canal war produced surrender killings, triumphal appropriation, slave sales and later revenge against replacement tenants. Wrath, Pride, Greed and Envy gained different routes through those acts. [[G-69 - The Four Faces of One Victory]] preserves a relief recut four times. Four claims upon one victory do not establish four equal commanders or a universal Feast War.
^atlas-id-r01

The Xolotlan **Red King without a Face** rose around sacrificial command during the Closing Courts. Captives were cut, burned and bled; deserters' families were killed. An autonomous Wrath Archmask seized several failing Red Tally networks and failed to take the Throne. Some later cults called the defeated claimant merciful because it hated different enemies. No Regnant succession is established.
^atlas-i-i02

## Faces fighting for one Hunger

**Atherian inner-gulf cities, late City-States into Stars.** Blood houses, theatrical victors, office families and beauty cults promoted incompatible Pride biographies through public processions. Their sponsors forced marriages, murdered witnesses and mutilated defeated images. Scholars later called the rivalry the **Parliament of Borrowed Faces**. It was not a chamber of independent gods.
^atlas-id-r02r

In a separate inner-gulf city, the **Champion of Four Doors** was an autonomous Envy Archmask which stole a human champion's biography and accepted three foreign gifts. Each gate supported a different route and demanded incompatible conduct. Victory left prisoners and former beneficiaries liable to the next payment. Closing one door injured its access but did not turn it into a composite Idol.
^atlas-id-r04r

## Protection as ownership

**A late-frontier river enclave.** A threatened population accepted an autonomous Archmask's protection. Its maintained anchors required living tenants, so it repelled an invading army. It then killed households who tried to leave and called their departure an attack upon everyone else's safety. The **Archmask Protectorate** preserved a settlement while enslaving its inhabitants to continued survival. It is not a redeemed Idol or proof that the Regnant ceased to exist.
^atlas-id-r03

## Feasts and invitations

Along one imperial supply road, Gluttony courtiers bought the people whom other Hungers had made disposable: abandoned sick, captives and corpses claimed as waste. A household fed its children from the feast and discovered a missing relative among its provisions. Refusing the next invitation cost its ration access; guides helped it flee while kitchens retaliated against neighbours. Cannibal consumption was bodily, not an ornamental metaphor.
^atlas-id-r05

During a separate provincial siege, Lust's promised belonging and Gluttony's promised nourishment competed over the same district. A family admitted a rescuing guest who transferred its dependants to the rival feast. Assault, abduction, starvation and consumption followed. The **Siege of Invitation and Consumption** concerned those two overlays, not every Hunger at once.
^atlas-id-r07r

## Martyrs, colonies and buried faces

The **Borrowed Martyr** was a tortured and executed human whose image fed separate Pride and Envy cults. Neither Mask contained the victim's soul. Families, pilgrims and rival cities suffered violence over a death neither counterfeit owned. [[G-70 - The Empty Grave and the Two False Shrines]] records the surviving public tradition.
^atlas-id-r06r

In a later imperial port, competing **cult colonies** occupied a grain office, a military patronage network and a marriage institution. They exploited residents already there rather than settling an empty land. A strike broke one colony's supply, and officials murdered strikers under the pretext of fighting another Mask. The usable granary survived after the predatory route failed; food distribution did not become inherently evil.
^atlas-id-r08

After an image purge, old foundation stones were reused in a Conversion-era district. Their buried instructions produced conflicting commands when a ruler rebuilt the festival route. Human factions killed and exiled one another to decide which face was authentic. This **Insurrection of the Buried Faces** does not prove that Sediment formed a conscious opposition or that an old Regnant returned.
^atlas-id-r09r

## What remains protected

The **Archmask That Ate Its Biography** remains a protected identification problem. Its erased bodies, kinship routes and foundations may be described as evidence of historical violence; the proposed autonomous person and its survival across those erasures are not promoted without their own proof. No true Idol succession is established here.
^atlas-id-r10r

Six archfiend epithets belong in this ecology: the Red-Lease General to Wrath, the Coin-Scaled Worm to Greed, the Carrion Huntress and Spore Empress to Gluttony, the False Prince to Envy and the Borrowed-Temple Exile to Pride. Their infernal alliances do not make Hell fiery or turn dragon-shaped bodies into True Dragons.
