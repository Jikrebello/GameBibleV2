# Folk Heroes and Legendary Adventurers

The people's canon — the figures sung in taverns rather than taught in seminaries. Rule of the register: folk heroes belong to the **reliability system**: some are certainly real, some certainly invented, most unverifiable, and the arguing is part of the inheritance. Each entry: the legend, what the records say, and the hooks it leaves lying around.

**Brannoc of the Hundred Fords** — the marches' outlaw-saint. During the Grey Sweat he robbed tithe-barns and assurance-sellers and *paid the ferry for the poor* — funding burial rites for families the fees would have damned to the north yard. THE LEGEND: a hundred escapes across a hundred fords; a bishop who hunted him and, at the end, buried him with his own hands. THE RECORDS: three duchies hold his grave; the tithe-ledgers of the Sweat years do show inexplicable anonymous settlements. HOOKS: "Brannoc's purse" caches still surface; every marches outlaw claims the mantle; the bishop's private letters exist and the Sanctum would prefer they didn't.

**Dame Verena the Drakesbane** — Caldria's dragon-slayer, feasted for four centuries — except it was an **emerald drake**, the valley's law lapsed with it, and the "troubles of Verena's Vale" began within the decade. The Church keeps the feast; the Folk keep the ledger. HOOKS: the vale's troubles persist; her sword (a proof-marked Aversian blade) is a contested relic; the drake's heart-gem was never accounted for.

**Sella Two-Oars** — the Saltborn heroine who rowed alone into the **Held Breath** to reach her becalmed husband's ship, and came back — a week older on a one-day sea, with a story she told exactly once. THE RECORDS: harbor rolls confirm the departure, the return, and the discrepancy in her log. HOOKS: her rowboat hangs in the Naris-chapel at Halmenth; her descendants are pilots; her once-told story was written down by a priest whose notes are lost, probably.

**Matteo the Latch** — Sestara's gentleman-thief: robbed the Medori vault's outer rings, returned everything with interest and a written critique of their security, was hired, improved the vault for a decade, and vanished — allegedly through a door of his own design that appears on no plan. HOOKS: every great heist since is "a Latch job"; his critique is studied at the counting-schools; the door, if it exists, is still in the vault.

**Mother Averil** — Grenzburg's own. When a starving Grobi splinter-band camped at her croft's fence in a killing winter, she fed them at the fence-line every dusk for a week — and the band moved on, taking nothing, leaving a spear planted tip-down at her gate. "AVERIL'S TOLL" now means feeding the desperate before they become the dangerous; the spear hangs in Grenzburg's gate-chapel. THE RECORDS: the croft existed; the spear is real and Grobi-made; everything else is the marches deciding what it needs to believe. HOOKS: splinter-bands still occasionally honor "the fence-law" — nobody knows how the custom travels among them, and the wise don't test it twice.

**Yeva of the Unwritten** — the first runaway: the serf-girl who tore her own page from the estate ledger and rode east into the grass. Every Virelian runaway "rides with Yeva"; the Unwritten's camps keep an empty saddle at fires. THE RECORDS: a Radomir-era estate book *does* survive with one page cleanly excised — which proves, as the szlachta point out, precisely nothing, which the serfs find hilarious. HOOKS: the excised page allegedly survives; whoever holds it holds either a relic of freedom or a lord's proof of title, depending on who's reading.

**Karas the Water-Carrier** — the Covenant's loophole-saint: the illiterate laborer who defended his village before a breach-court and won, using the court's own maxim against the seizure of his neighbors' names. Jurists formally deplore and universally teach "the Karas defense." THE LEGEND: he asked one question — *"Does the Covenant witness me too?"* — and the breach-judge wept. THE RECORDS: case exists, transcript sealed. HOOKS: the sealed transcript; the annual water-carriers' feast the authorities neither permit nor prevent; every village advocate since who begins "as Karas asked—".

**Tamin Grey-Cap** — the Markish trickster: the apprentice who obeys every order *exactly* (told to "put the ale where it can't be drunk," he bricked it into the chapel wall, where it was found, aged to glory, forty years on — the origin, three abbeys claim, of their best vintage). Hundreds of tales; no records; needs none. HOOKS: Grey-Cap tales are how the marches teach contract-literalism to children — the Covenant border garrisons trade them with genuine professional appreciation.

**The Lantern-Brothers, Aldo and Pree** — the delver trade's patron legends: mapped forty barrows in eleven years, died in the forty-first, and every delver since toasts *"the forty-first lamp"* before a descent. Their surviving maps are brilliant, contradictory, and still traded — the contradictions, veterans insist, are deliberate warnings in a private code no one has cracked. HOOKS: a "true Aldo map" is the trade's holy grail and its commonest forgery; the forty-first barrow's location is known, marked, and by unanimous trade custom, *left alone*.

**Ishbel Longstride** — the courier who outran the Black Wind's tribute-riders across two hundred miles to warn the fair at Velian Qafra — honored on *both* sides, because the Tarkhan hold that great running honors the grass, and Qarayel himself is said to have sent her a foal of Grey Sister's line. THE RECORDS: the fair survived; a Longstride line of horses exists and wins things. HOOKS: her route is now a race run every third year; the foal's descent is claimed by four stables; the Tarkhan judge the race and have never once been accused of favor.

## The Register's Use

Folk heroes are the setting's **portable morality** — each one teaches a rule of the world in story form (Averil teaches the fence-law; Karas teaches that even the Covenant's knife has a handle; Verena teaches the drake/dragon confusion; the Brothers teach the forty-first lamp). Use them as: tavern texture, quest patterns (every "Latch job" and "Brannoc cache" is a radiant seed), contested relics, and — per the reliability rule — occasional shocking survivals: one of the figures above is, in the spine's quiet opinion, *still alive*. The register does not say which.
