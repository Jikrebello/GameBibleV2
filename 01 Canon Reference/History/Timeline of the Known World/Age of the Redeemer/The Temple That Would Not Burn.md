> _“They lit the flame. But the flame refused them.”_  
> —Inscription carved into the last standing wall of the Temple at Cyrian

### **Date**: Estimated between ~447–450 AE

Early period of the Atherian persecutions, during the harshest crackdowns on Redeemer worship.

### **Location**:

**Cyrian**, a mid-sized provincial city in the western empire, known for its marble quarries and observatory-temple to **Lyraxis**, the Muse-Star.

---

### **Overview**

According to records preserved in both **Eclessian ecclesiastical histories** and **Thalmyric chronicles of early miracles**, the Temple at Cyrian was among the first Star Pantheon temples repurposed by Redeemer believers for clandestine worship.

It was partially collapsed after an earthquake—seen by many in the faith as a sign of divine displeasure with the stars—and taken over by local believers who transformed its inner sanctum into a **shrine to the Redeemer**, hanging chains over the shattered altar and kindling fire each night in the name of mercy.

When Imperial enforcers discovered the heresy, the entire complex was ordered burned to the ground as a public warning.

But **it would not burn.**

---

### **Recorded Attempts to Destroy the Temple**

1. **First Attempt**:  
    Pitch-soaked tapestries, dry wood, and sacred oils were poured through the nave and ignited.  
    The flames erupted, then **snuffed out instantly**, leaving the star-forged pillars **glowing cold**.
    
2. **Second Attempt** (Three days later):  
    A pyre of manuscripts and carved effigies was constructed inside. Imperial witnesses swore the fire **rose up**, but **would not consume the stone**—and instead **burned one of the soldiers**, though no embers touched him.
    
3. **Third Attempt** (Festival of Astaroth):  
    The local Prefect ordered a bonfire lit in each corner of the temple and recited curses against the Redeemer. That night, **a lightning storm struck**, killing two of the prefect’s guards.  
    When dawn broke, the temple still stood—charred, but intact.

### **Interpretations and Scholarly Debates**

#### ðŸ•¯ï¸ **Eclessian View (Ecclesia Aurelia, High Council Histories)**:

The Temple at Cyrian is considered one of the **Three Silent Miracles** of the Era of Blood and Ash. It represents **divine resistance**: the Holy Flame cannot be wielded by tyranny, and the sanctuary of the Redeemer cannot be destroyed by injustice.

It is often cited in **sermons about spiritual fortitude** and appears in religious art as a **ruined colonnade bathed in gold firelight**, often with a single unburned candle atop a cracked altar.

---

#### ðŸ”¥ **Thalmyric Interpretation (Order of the Phoenix Ascendant, Doctrine of Sacred Rebirth)**:

The Thalmyric Church interprets the event not as preservation—but as **transmutation**. The temple’s refusal to burn is seen as the **first proof that the Redeemer’s flame is purifying, not destructive**. It burned away unworthy intentions and false worship—but preserved that which had been consecrated anew.

For Thalmyric mystics, Cyrian is symbolic of **inner sanctification**—that the body may suffer, but the soul, if bound to the Flame, cannot be consumed.

---

#### ðŸ“œ **Imperial Secular Records (Codex Aurelianus, Volume IX)**:

One archivist dismisses the incident entirely, claiming the temple was built with **anomalously resistant quarry stone** and **high humidity** from nearby springs. The "failed burnings," according to this version, were due to **logistical incompetence** and poor weather.

This view is echoed in some modern Concord universities as an example of **“miraculous embellishment through oral transmission.”**

---

### **Legacy**

- The **site of the Temple of Cyrian** became a **pilgrimage destination** after the faith was legalized.
- A cathedral now stands beside the ruins, built from the original stone—its altar still crowned with **unchanging flame** in some accounts.
- Redeemer iconography often shows the temple as **a heart-shaped structure encircled by fire**—a symbol of **endurance through trial**.
