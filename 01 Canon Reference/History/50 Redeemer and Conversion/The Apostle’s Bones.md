# The Apostle’s Bones

> _“You cannot bury a saint without burying a flame. And flames find a way out.”_  
> —Common saying among early Flamebearers

“The Apostle’s Bones” are the **purported remains of Saint Calira of the Broken Blade**, the Companion who died defending Amaryah's house at Veridun Minor in 29 AR. They came to light about 338, when wardens of the Red Star loaded the altar platform of a ruined shrine of Astaroth at Veridun Minor and the step cracked open under their weights, with a broken sword hilt lying beside the bones. The four sees claimed them for Calira. The Star college, whose own test had opened the chamber and which still held the lawful power to say what such a find was, entered them as the bones of a Star-priestess executed for blasphemy. The quarrel was the first relic dispute of the legal Church, and the find has never stopped being argued over.

### **Date**:

The find, about 338 AR, under Emperor Sevrin. The bishops' hearing followed within the decade. After 383 the bishops' readers re-entered the find, and after the Great Split of 1015 both churches renewed their claims.

---

### **The Discovery**

The town meant to build the new church of the **Swordless Flame** at Veridun Minor on the site of a ruined Astaroth shrine near the house where Amaryah had lived. No public work could be built on until the wardens had loaded it before the Red Star and broken the Star's share. When they piled their weights on the old altar platform, the step gave way, and under it the wardens uncovered:

- A **sealed chamber** beneath the former altar of the Astaroth shrine
- A **skeleton encased in ash-layered stone**, with signs of defensive wounds and ritual binding
- A **broken sword hilt**, snapped at the crossguard, a **bronze Redeemer talisman**, and a cloth fragment embroidered with the phrase:
    
    > _“She fell with her face to the flame.”_
    

The local bishop proclaimed them the **bones of Calira**, killed in the raid of 29 AR while holding the doorway with a sword that had snapped at the crossguard. The blade of that sword, the **Broken Blade**, had come to Caleran with the daughter flame and hung in the church that became the Cathedral of the Eternal Ember.

---

### **The Dispute of 338**

####  **The Star college**

The augur college of the province still held the lawful power to enter any wonder or sacred find in its books, and the governor was bound to its entry. Its augurs said that the Red Star had shown its own wardens the chamber, examined it and entered the bones as those of a **Star-Priestess executed for blasphemy** and bound beneath her own altar, which explained the ritual binding and the ash. They said the cloth was a later Redeemer addition, and demanded the remains be **sealed or destroyed** to avoid “heresy by veneration.”

####  **The four sees**

The bishops answered that a Star-priestess would not have been buried with a Redeemer talisman and a soldier's sword. Caleran wanted the bones laid beside the Broken Blade in its new cathedral. Veridun Minor's keepers, who had tended Amaryah's lamp for three centuries, refused to let them leave the town. Aurelium sent readers to compare the find with every surviving account of the raid of 29.

####  **The hearing**

Sevrin let the four sees hear the case, as his father had let three of them sit on Halmenth's refusal of the Concord in 297, and the bishops met in a chapel within sight of the ruined altar. They found for Calira in matters of communion: the faithful might venerate the bones and keep her feast before them. The governor held to the augurs' entry in law: the remains could not be carried in public procession or moved from Veridun Minor. The bones stayed where they were found, in a sanctuary built over the old altar, and pilgrims came to them anyway.

In 383, after the augur colleges were dissolved and their books passed to the bishops, the bishops' readers struck the augurs' entry and entered the bones as Calira's. Old-cult families at Veridun Minor kept the augurs' version alive for generations.

---

### **The Hilt and the Blade**

The hilt in the grave snapped at the crossguard, and the Broken Blade in Caleran snapped at the crossguard. Pilgrims have asked for a thousand years whether they are two halves of one sword. The Cathedral's keepers have never allowed the two to be laid side by side. The late Eclessian [[Codex of The Era of Blood and Ash]] adds a third relic, a "fractured sword-pommel" at Ironvale's Sanctuary of Ash, which no one at Ironvale has ever been able to find.

---

### **Relic Lore and Phenomena**

Several phenomena surrounded the bones and were used to argue for (or against) their authenticity:

- **The bones were warm to the touch**, even after exposure to cold.
- A novice scribe tasked with transcribing the discovery fainted and later spoke verses in an unknown dialect—believed to be early Redeemer cant.
- The broken hilt reportedly caused **paper to smoulder** when wrapped in official imperial decrees.

Later lore claims that, during a procession of the bones, **a drought ended** in Veridun Minor after thirty-one days of failed prayer. The governor had forbidden processions; the townspeople say they carried the bones round the inside of the sanctuary walls, which the order did not cover.

---

### **After the Great Split**

The quarrel revived when the churches divided in 1015.

####  **Eclessian Claim**

- Calira was venerated in the eastern provinces largely through **oral tradition**.
- Her name appears in the **Book of the Bound**, where her teachings focus on **faith through communal protection**.
- Eclessian bishops argued that her remains belonged in the **Sanctum Divinitatis** in Aurelia, where the relic could serve the faithful.

####  **Thalmyric Claim**

- Thalmyric clergy maintained that Calira was **spiritually Thalmyric**, citing her soldier's background and a version of her Life that sets her last stand in a mountain pass. They made her one of the earliest models for the **Order of the Vigilant Flame**.
- They asserted the bones should be housed in **Vlastrava**, in a sanctum built to honour the Redeemer’s warriors.

After years of conflict the two churches agreed that the bones would remain at Veridun Minor in a **neutral sanctuary**, with Thalmyric and Eclessian orders holding **joint custodianship** and alternating rites by season. The arrangement became a model for later **relic-sharing treaties**, known as **Sanctified Custody Concords**.

---

### **Theological Significance**

- Later writers count the find as a **turning point in sainthood doctrine**: from _narrative martyrdom_ to _physical sanctification_.
- It strengthened the claim that **relics are active conduits of divine flame**.
- It also began the long debates over the **dangers of relic fraud, relics claimed for crowns, and relic monopoly**, and over who has the right to say whose bones are whose.

---

### **In Iconography**

- Calira is often shown holding **a broken blade and open flame**, symbolising her sacrifice and incomplete martyrdom.
- The **Sanctuary of the Swordless Flame** features her likeness in armour with **no weapon**, only chains.
- In Eclessian art, she lights the way for pilgrims.  
    In Thalmyric art, she **stands atop a battlefield**, defiant.

---

### **Modern Pilgrimage and Doctrine**

- The site of the bones remains a **neutral pilgrimage destination**, drawing both ascetics and martial orders.
- **Miracles of healing, prophetic dreams, and sword-bearers choosing nonviolence** are frequently attributed to visits.
- The **"Caliran Oath"** is a vow taken by guardians of relics:
    
    > _“We hold not power. We carry the proof.”_
