# The Reign of Sevrin

## The succession of 316 AR

Cassian gave his younger son Sevrin command of the southern reserve and its winter depots while his eldest son was still heir. Sevrin dealt with garrison towns and Folk communities that supplied scouts and livestock. He kept the command after Cassian executed the eldest son and the empress. The published orders empowered Sevrin to muster and pay the reserve. The court had no publicly witnessed designation making him sole heir. [[Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR|Cassian and the Four Sees]]

When Cassian died in 316, the capital guard acclaimed Sevrin after the treasury issued the next pay warrant under his seal. Eastern officers withheld recognition and backed **Adran**, Cassian's nephew and a commander with clients on the eastern roads. Adran accepted command under Sevrin once Caleran agreed to honour the army's debts, protect his household and hear petitions for property seized in the last court purge. Caleran kept control of imperial appointments and the main treasury. The eastern command kept a share of local revenue for the road garrisons. The agreement prevented a new march on Caleran.

## Government and frontier

Sevrin confirmed the 293 restitution instruction and earlier judgements. He refused episcopal requests to reopen the executions. Families could petition over confiscated goods. Star estates continued to supply grain, and soldiers who objected to offerings could still take Cassian's witnessed service oath.

The four sees continued to use the arrangements made under Cassian. Caleran carried petitions to court. Aurelium kept copies of decisions. Halmenth's burial and relief stewards enforced rights of access, and Sermoran hosts maintained contact with congregations beyond imperial ports. Sevrin confirmed grants without requiring the churches to commemorate his father as a saint. The executions remained a reason for some families to avoid imperial thanksgiving rites.

Between c. 324 and 338, winter raids damaged southern supply roads. Sevrin protected markets inside the fort line and renewed safe conduct for named Folk communities. Some accepted the terms and returned captives. Others lost access or moved trade beyond the forts. The new garrisons needed more carts and supplies, and the towns could petition the governors who also heard restitution cases. The eastern command continued to negotiate passage, tolls and exchanges with Vashar. [[The Imperial Army and the Frontiers]] · [[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]]

## Astrion's accession

Sevrin's intended heir died on campaign c. 343. Sevrin later entrusted the northern desert approaches to **Astrion**, Adran's son and Cassian's great-nephew. Born c. 317 AR, Astrion was raised by Flame tutors and took an interest in the Star learning preserved by the eastern administration. He kept his troops supplied and negotiated passage with some well communities.

When a transfer threatened soldiers' stipends and left their households exposed, officers pressed Astrion to claim the throne. Sevrin died of illness in 348 before the quarrel turned into open war. The capital recognised Astrion after he confirmed outstanding pay and provincial appointments. His restoration of the Stars followed his accession.
