# The Reign of Sevrin

## The succession of 307 AR

Cassian gave his younger son Sevrin command of the southern reserve and its winter depots while his eldest son Torian was still heir. Sevrin dealt with garrison towns and Folk communities that supplied scouts and livestock. About 304 Torian asked the High Augur of Astaroth for an answer on the succession. Cassian executed the augur over the sealed reply, had Torian ordained and shut him into the bridge chapel at Velos as its chaplain. Sevrin kept his command through the purge that followed. His orders let him muster and pay the reserve, but nobody had heard Cassian name him heir. [[Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR|Cassian and the Four Sees]]

When Cassian died in 307, baptised days earlier by the son he had unmade, the capital guard acclaimed Sevrin after the treasury issued the next pay warrant under his seal. Eastern officers withheld recognition and backed **Adran**, Cassian's nephew and a commander with clients on the eastern roads. Some of them talked of fetching Torian out of his chapel, and Torian sent word that he would not come. Adran accepted command under Sevrin once Caleran agreed to honour the army's debts, protect his household and hear petitions for property seized in the last court purge. The terms also wrote Adran's line into the succession after Sevrin's sons. Caleran kept control of imperial appointments and the main treasury. The eastern command kept a share of local revenue for the road garrisons. The agreement prevented a new march on Caleran.

## Government and frontier

Cassian had died with restitution half done, and Sevrin spent much of his long reign confirming it. He kept his father's judgements, and refused the bishops who wanted the sentence and the ordination of 304 reopened. Families could still sue for confiscated goods. Star estates went on supplying grain, and soldiers who objected to offerings could still take Cassian's witnessed service oath.

The four sees went on as they had under Cassian: Caleran carried petitions to court, Aurelium copied every ruling, Halmenth's stewards kept the graveyard gates open and Sermora's hosts kept in touch with congregations beyond the empire's ports. Sevrin confirmed grants without requiring the churches to commemorate his father as a saint. The High Augur's execution remained a reason for some old-cult families to avoid imperial thanksgiving rites, and the forced ordination a reason for some clergy.

## The river and the sealed book

Two things Sevrin would not give up. The first was the river at Velos. Every winter the bishops petitioned for the wardenship of the reach, the crown office that measured the open water and kept its book, and every winter Sevrin refused. The bridge chapel stayed a crown chapel and its chaplain stayed a crown servant. All through his reign that chaplain was his elder brother, who died at Velos about 341, and a chapel handed to the bishops would have put a disinherited heir under a bishop's protection. The bishops kept the restitution they had been paid for accepting the Warden. The second was the sealed answer that had unmade his brother. Sevrin inherited the crown registrar's sealed books with the throne and never opened the one about the succession. Courtiers who asked why were told that the emperor already knew who reigned.

The augurs still held the lawful power to say what a sign was. About 338 the wardens of the Red Star came to Veridun Minor to load a ruined shrine of Astaroth before the town built on it. The altar step cracked under their weights and opened on a chamber with a woman's bones in it and a broken sword hilt beside them. The four sees claimed them for the Companion Calira. The Star college, whose own test had found them, entered them as the remains of a Star-priestess executed for blasphemy. Sevrin let a hearing of the sees sit on the find, kept the governor to the college's entry in law, and left the bones in Veridun Minor. [[The Apostle’s Bones]]

Between c. 324 and 338, winter raids damaged southern supply roads. Sevrin protected markets inside the fort line and renewed safe conduct for named Folk communities. Some accepted the terms and returned captives. Others lost access or moved trade beyond the forts. The new garrisons wanted more carts and grain, and the towns that supplied them complained to the same governors who heard restitution cases. The eastern command continued to negotiate passage, tolls and exchanges with Vashar. [[The Imperial Army and the Frontiers]] · [[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]]

## Astrion's accession

Sevrin's only son died on campaign c. 335. His daughter had married **Lucar Aster**, who held Sevrin's old command of the southern reserve, but the terms of 307 stood, and they named Adran's line. Sevrin entrusted the northern desert approaches to **Astrion**, Adran's son and Cassian's great-nephew. Born c. 317 AR, Astrion was raised by Flame tutors and took an interest in the Star learning preserved by the eastern administration. He kept his troops supplied and negotiated passage with some well communities.

Sevrin died of illness in 340, and the throne passed by the terms of 307. The capital recognised Astrion after he confirmed outstanding pay and provincial appointments. The bishops who had petitioned Sevrin every winter for the river found that the new emperor had been taught the faith as a boy and had kept none of it. His restoration of the Stars followed his accession. [[Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR|Astrion and the Road to Vestian]]
