# Cassian and the Four Sees

## The reign in outline

Cassian won the civil war in 292 AR after seeing the White Flame at the Stone Bridge of Velos. He permitted open worship and petitions for confiscated property. He funded basilicas, kept the augur colleges and Star offices that served his government, executed his eldest son and empress on unpublished evidence, and accepted baptism as he was dying in 316. The daughter flame brought from Veridun Minor became the Eternal Ember during his reign. ## Who gets the confiscated property

In 293 Cassian directs governors to return property taken under anti-Redeemer orders to the local body that held it. A bishop, several elders or the stewards of a burial ground can present a claim on that body's behalf. The governor reads the former title against the seizure inventory, donor records, burial rolls and sworn testimony about who controlled the property before confiscation. Earlier seizures, including those under Tervan, qualify where a congregation and its claim can still be identified. Local proceedings and uneven surviving records make restitution take years.

| Claim | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **A meeting hall, relief store or burial ground held in common** | Return possession to the congregation through its recognised custodians, free of charge. An owner who acquired it in good faith at a treasury sale can apply to the treasury for repayment. The claim for repayment runs separately from delivery of the property. A purchaser who profited from a false denunciation can be refused repayment. |
| **A house where a patron hosted worship** | Return the house to the patron or heirs when the evidence establishes household title. A room or yard demonstrably given for permanent common use goes back to the assembly. When the congregation buried its dead in a household plot by permission, the governor can secure access to graves while leaving title with the household. |
| **A room held by two successor congregations, or a site with an uncertain chain of custody** | Put the site in temporary municipal custody pending a hearing. Existing burials and relief distributions can continue under named stewards. An episcopal hearing can advise on which officers speak for the old assembly. The governor rules on title and possession, subject to an appeal to the emperor. |
| **A public monument, functioning Star estate or Qiryath sanctuary land** | The former Celestial Forum precinct enters church use by a specific imperial grant. Star estates retain ordinary title unless a claimant can prove an actual seizure of congregation property. Qiryath's sanctuary lands and tribute fall under the settlement imposed after the revolt. A Redeemer assembly with Jirahar members can recover a confiscated meeting room while those households still owe the tribute. The garrison and exile remain. |

Cassian's grants fund specific buildings and services. Municipal councils resist blanket exemptions that would put the same obligations on fewer households.

Cassian needs the same Star institutions for municipal continuity and military ceremonies that bishops sometimes petition against. He accepts a service oath before witnesses from soldiers who object to an offering. At some forts the officer who administers the oath still joins the augurs at the traditional opening of the campaign season. Cassian also confirms the contracts under which temple estates deliver grain. Provincial households keep supplying the state through familiar offices, and commanders gain a lawful way to retain Redeemer soldiers.

## The hearing at Halmenth, 297 AR

During the closing prosecutions an elder in Halmenth surrendered a chest of letters after officers threatened his household. The surviving bishop restored him to fellowship. A group of confessors and the families of two arrested couriers disputed whether he could serve as steward again. They worshipped separately and claimed to represent the same pre-seizure assembly. Both groups petitioned for its hall, burial yard and relief chest. A ship supplier who had bought the hall from the treasury asked for the sale price and the cost of a new roof. Local mediation failed because the bishop's own judgement was part of the appeal.

Cassian issues travel warrants for bishops and witnesses, and sends an assessor to record the civil result. Halmenth hosts. Its own bishop gives evidence and recuses himself from judging his earlier decision. Clergy and lay stewards speak for the parties. The bishops who hear the appeal use testimony about the confiscation, election of elders and subsequent arrests. The sitting leaves each see with a different stake.

| See | What it accepts and what it insists on |
|---|---|
| **Caleran** | Takes the lead on putting cases before the treasury and supports enforceable judgements. Its bishop wants a named steward who can be held to an account. That access helps the other sees until Caleran starts using its priority at court to press for its preferred claimants. |
| **Aurelium** | Compares the seizure inventory and surviving copies of the letters. It supports mutual review of a disputed bishop and sends signed copies of the ruling to each see. Its readers press for specific findings about what was surrendered before a person can be publicly blamed for an arrest. |
| **Halmenth** | Insists that both groups keep burying their dead and drawing upon relief during the dispute. Harbour stewards secure burial access through the civil judgement. The see wants visiting bishops to hear appeals, especially when a local bishop is a party, while keeping routine discipline local. |
| **Sermora** | Brokers an orderly handover with the purchaser and offers another venue for the dissenting congregation. Its merchants want predictable rules for purchase and indemnity. Its bishop insists that letters of communion travel on their own authority to ports whose rulers owe Cassian no obedience. |

The bishops confirm the elder's admission to worship and bar him from handling the community's correspondence or funds. They find enough evidence to establish the surrender of the chest. The surviving record leaves the cause of the two arrests uncertain. The governor restores the hall and relief chest to the continuing assembly through newly elected stewards and gives both groups access to the graveyard. The buyer surrenders the hall immediately and receives partial reimbursement a year later. The confessors keep meeting in the rented room. A later petition asks Cassian to evict them. He refuses the eviction, but assigns the next local building grant to the recognised assembly.

The four sees agree to exchange decisions and invite outside bishops when a dispute puts the local overseer on trial. The emperor can convene a hearing, enforce a property judgement and choose whom his treasury funds. Bishops still judge communion, discipline and suitability for office. In practice a grant can make one bishop's judgement easier to live by than another's. The 297 settlement concerns restitution and office. The later Council of Halmenth in 411 concerns the nature of the Redeemer and leads to a lasting schism.

## The forum after the court leaves

The Caleran congregation takes on the pavement, drains and staff of a former judgement precinct. Families can bring the names of the condemned to a public vigil. The old Pillar stays in the precinct where imperial officers once read judgements. The keepers of Veridun Minor retain Amaryah's household lamp and bring its daughter flame and the vessel venerated as Solana's jar to Caleran. The court pays builders and puts the emperor's name on the building. The keepers provide the flame, train attendants and bring fuel from the congregation's income. At the dedication the court gives public thanks for peace and the congregation keeps a vigil for the people killed at the site. Some clergy pray for Cassian by name and decline requests to commemorate him beside the martyrs. Others read the victory at Velos as the start of a saint's life and later cite his baptism. Cassian is commemorated locally as a benefactor, and the question of his sanctity remains contested. [[The Making of Saints]]
