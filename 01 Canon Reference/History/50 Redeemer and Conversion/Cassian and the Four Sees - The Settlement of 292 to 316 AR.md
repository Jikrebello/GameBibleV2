# Cassian and the Four Sees

## The reign in outline

Cassian won the civil war in 292 AR after seeing the White Flame at the Stone Bridge of Velos. He permitted open worship and petitions for confiscated property. He funded basilicas, kept the augur colleges and Star offices that served his government, executed his eldest son and empress on unpublished evidence, and accepted baptism as he was dying in 316. The daughter flame brought from Veridun Minor became the Eternal Ember during his reign. ## Who gets the confiscated property

In 293 Cassian directed governors to return property taken under anti-Redeemer orders to the local body that had held it. A bishop, several elders or the stewards of a burial ground could present a claim on that body's behalf. The governor read the former title against the seizure inventory, donor records, burial rolls and sworn testimony about who had controlled the property before confiscation. Earlier seizures, including those under Tervan, qualified where a congregation and its claim could still be identified. Local proceedings and uneven surviving records made restitution take years.

| Claim | Outcome |
|---|---|
| **A meeting hall, relief store or burial ground held in common** | Return possession to the congregation through its recognised custodians, free of charge. An owner who had acquired it in good faith at a treasury sale could apply to the treasury for repayment. The claim for repayment ran separately from delivery of the property. A purchaser who had profited from a false denunciation could be refused repayment. |
| **A house where a patron hosted worship** | Return the house to the patron or heirs when the evidence established household title. A room or yard demonstrably given for permanent common use went back to the assembly. When the congregation had buried its dead in a household plot by permission, the governor could secure access to graves while leaving title with the household. |
| **A room held by two successor congregations, or a site with an uncertain chain of custody** | Put the site in temporary municipal custody pending a hearing. Existing burials and relief distributions could continue under named stewards. An episcopal hearing could advise on which officers spoke for the old assembly. The governor ruled on title and possession, subject to an appeal to the emperor. |
| **A public monument, functioning Star estate or Qiryath sanctuary land** | The former Celestial Forum precinct entered church use by a specific imperial grant. Star estates retained ordinary title unless a claimant could prove an actual seizure of congregation property. Qiryath's sanctuary lands and tribute fell under the settlement imposed after the revolt. A Redeemer assembly with Jirahar members could recover a confiscated meeting room while those households still owed the tribute. The garrison and exile remained. |

Cassian's grants funded specific buildings and services. Municipal councils resisted blanket exemptions that would put the same obligations on fewer households.

Cassian needed the same Star institutions for municipal continuity and military ceremonies that bishops sometimes petitioned against. He accepted a service oath before witnesses from soldiers who objected to an offering. At some forts the officer who administered the oath still joined the augurs at the traditional opening of the campaign season. Cassian also confirmed the contracts under which temple estates delivered grain. Provincial households kept supplying the state through familiar offices, and commanders gained a lawful way to retain Redeemer soldiers.

## The hearing at Halmenth, 297 AR

During the closing prosecutions an elder in Halmenth surrendered a chest of letters after officers threatened his household. The surviving bishop restored him to fellowship. A group of confessors and the families of two arrested couriers disputed whether he could serve as steward again. They worshipped separately and claimed to represent the same pre-seizure assembly. Both groups petitioned for its hall, burial yard and relief chest. A ship supplier who had bought the hall from the treasury asked for the sale price and the cost of a new roof. Local mediation failed because the bishop's own judgement was part of the appeal.

Cassian issued travel warrants for bishops and witnesses, and sent an assessor to record the civil result. Halmenth hosted. Its own bishop gave evidence and recused himself from judging his earlier decision. Clergy and lay stewards spoke for the parties. The bishops who heard the appeal used testimony about the confiscation, election of elders and subsequent arrests. The sitting left each see with a different stake.

| See | What it accepted and what it insisted on |
|---|---|
| **Caleran** | Took the lead on putting cases before the treasury and supported enforceable judgements. Its bishop wanted a named steward who could be held to an account. That access helped the other sees until Caleran started using its priority at court to press for its preferred claimants. |
| **Aurelium** | Compared the seizure inventory and surviving copies of the letters. It supported mutual review of a disputed bishop and sent signed copies of the ruling to each see. Its readers pressed for specific findings about what had been surrendered before a person could be publicly blamed for an arrest. |
| **Halmenth** | Insisted that both groups keep burying their dead and drawing upon relief during the dispute. Harbour stewards secured burial access through the civil judgement. The see wanted visiting bishops to hear appeals, especially when a local bishop was a party, while keeping routine discipline local. |
| **Sermora** | Brokered an orderly handover with the purchaser and offered another venue for the dissenting congregation. Its merchants wanted predictable rules for purchase and indemnity. Its bishop insisted that letters of communion travel on their own authority to ports whose rulers owed Cassian no obedience. |

The bishops confirmed the elder's admission to worship and barred him from handling the community's correspondence or funds. They found enough evidence to establish the surrender of the chest. The surviving record leaves the cause of the two arrests uncertain. The governor restored the hall and relief chest to the continuing assembly through newly elected stewards and gave both groups access to the graveyard. The buyer surrendered the hall immediately and received partial reimbursement a year later. The confessors kept meeting in the rented room. A later petition asked Cassian to evict them. He refused the eviction, but assigned the next local building grant to the recognised assembly.

The four sees agreed to exchange decisions and invite outside bishops when a dispute put the local overseer on trial. The emperor could convene a hearing, enforce a property judgement and choose whom his treasury funded. Bishops still judged communion, discipline and suitability for office. In practice a grant could make one bishop's judgement easier to live by than another's.

## The forum after the court leaves

The Caleran congregation took on the pavement, drains and staff of a former judgement precinct. Families could bring the names of the condemned to a public vigil. The old Pillar stayed in the precinct where imperial officers had once read judgements. The keepers of Veridun Minor retained Amaryah's household lamp and brought its daughter flame and the vessel venerated as Solana's jar to Caleran. The court paid builders and put the emperor's name on the building. The keepers provided the flame, trained attendants and brought fuel from the congregation's income. At the dedication the court gave public thanks for peace and the congregation kept a vigil for the people killed at the site. Some clergy prayed for Cassian by name and declined requests to commemorate him beside the martyrs. Others read the victory at Velos as the start of a saint's life and later cited his baptism. Cassian is commemorated locally as a benefactor, and the question of his sanctity remains contested. [[The Making of Saints]]
