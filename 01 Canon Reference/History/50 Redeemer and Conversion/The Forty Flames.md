# The Forty Flames

> _“One flame can light a city. Forty can burn down an empire.”_
> —Inscribed above the Gate of the Martyrs in Ironvale

## The Ashforges rising, winter 290 AR

During the closing prosecutions, inspectors found enslaved workers singing Redeemer hymns in Ironvale's Ashforges and made arrests. When the authorities ordered the prisoners transferred, workers in adjoining crews broke open the holding yard, took food and opened an outbound gate. People of several faiths joined. Some fought the guards while others helped children and injured workers escape along the fuel track. Troops retook the yards on the third day and killed people during the assault and reprisals. [[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization]]

## Three nights in the furnace

After retaking the yards, the authorities identified forty prisoners as members of the forbidden assembly. They offered release from sentence to anyone who renounced the Redeemer and named the assembly's hosts. The forty refused. Guards locked them inside a disused, unlit furnace and left the flue open to the winter air. Exposure, thirst and injuries killed them over three nights.

When a guard and forge workers opened the chamber, a brief, unconsuming flame appeared on the dead. The furnace stayed cold. A worker's account carried the sign into the local Life. The prison roll counted forty and recorded death by exposure. It omitted the conditions offered before confinement and the flame at the opening.

A porter let one family take its dead away in secret, fearing that the authorities would use the burial to identify surviving kin. The common burial tally records thirty-nine. The private grave and the name of the person buried there are unknown to later custodians. The missing entry and memories of actual fugitives fed the story that one of the Forty survived.

## Ashwake after legalization

The households remembered the dead in private until legalization. Under Cassian they established access to the common graves through the restitution process and later leased the disused furnace precinct from the forge estate. They arranged access around the working yards and tended the site, which became the Sanctuary of the Forty. Aurelium received copies of the prison and burial records and circulated the Life. The local observance grew into **Ashwake**, with names read and lamps lit at dawn. Other sees adopted the observance in their own time. ## Liturgical and theological legacy

### The Doctrine of the Living Burnt

This tradition takes the furnace sign as an image of illumination and refinement. Later preachers of the [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/50 Economy and Warfare/Thalmyric Military Orders.md|Order of the Returning Standard]] read the deaths as a passage from witness into service. The sign is genuine, and judgements about the conduct of the rising or the sanctity of individual relics require their own evidence. [[The Making of Saints]]

### Communal commemoration

The churches honour forty people under one name. Ironvale's commemorations can also name the dead from the rising when records survive.

> _“They died together, because they lived together. That is the greater miracle.”_
> —Archbishop Thallor of Aurelia, _Sermons at Ashwake_

---

### **Relics and Memorials**

- The **Ashforged Bones** are enshrined at the Sanctuary of the Forty. Claims of incorruption, articulation and individual identification remain disputed.
- The **Emberbrands** are iron medallions of a palm and flame. Custodians attribute the metal to the furnace doors.
- The **Ashwake Veil** is attributed to widows of the slain. Some worshippers report that the cloth warms during prayers for justice.

---

### **Artistic Representations**

- In **mosaics**, the Forty are shown seated in a circle, heads bowed, a small flame in each palm.
- In **Thalmyric statuary**, the central martyr holds a hammer wreathed in light—a reference to the Saintmaker.
- In **Eclessian stained glass**, the flames are **golden and clean**, with an angelic figure hovering above them, whispering final absolution.

---

### **Cultural Memory**

- On the **Day of Ashwake**, congregations in Ironvale and across Eclessia light forty candles in silence at dawn.
- In the Thalmyric **Chain March**, penitents walk in linked iron chains, one link for each martyr, in public repentance and solidarity with the poor.

---

### **Scholarly and Sectarian Debate**

- Some **Concord historians** claim the event was symbolic, with “forty” as a metaphor for completeness.
- The **Order of the Bound Flame** maintains that only **thirty-nine were killed**, and that the final martyr escaped and founded the **first Redeemer forge-convent**.
- A controversial Thalmyric epistle even claims one of the martyrs **spoke from the flame**, and that his voice was **heard centuries later in the Sanctuary crypt**—a claim still debated today.

---

### **Canonization and Codification**

Later sources credit the **Council of Second Flame** with confirming the existing observance and accepting communal testimony about the unnamed dead. Its date and institutional setting remain uncertain.
