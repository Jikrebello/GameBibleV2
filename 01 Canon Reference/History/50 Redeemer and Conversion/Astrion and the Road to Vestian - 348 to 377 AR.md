# Astrion and the Road to Vestian

Astrion succeeds Sevrin in 348 AR and restores the Stars to imperial favour. The Stars answer his public test once. He dies at thirty-four on a northern desert campaign in 351, struck by a spear whose thrower no witness saw. His successors return imperial favour to the Redeemer's faith. Vestian takes power in 377, and compulsory faith follows in 380. [[The Reign of Sevrin]] · [[The Conversion of the Empire]]

## Astrion, 348–351 AR

### 348–349 · Temples, stipends and schools

Astrion spent his youth with Flame tutors and readers who still knew how to interpret Star calendars. As emperor he wants the civic cult to recover the authority it once drew from schools, relief and public ceremony. He restores suspended stipends and gives temple stewards standing to claim rents diverted from demonstrably Star endowments. Governors review disputed plots under Cassian's rules of title and evidence. The 293 restitution order and the legal right to Redeemer worship remain in force. Property awarded to an assembly stays with it. A market town can reopen its temple, keep its church and owe taxes to both institutions' former patrons.

Astrion directs endowed temples to reserve grain for travellers and burial money for people without kin. The order works where a temple still has tenants, wells or stores. A poor shrine may lose its stipend altogether when its steward cannot make the returns the court demands. Astrion also ties publicly paid posts in rhetoric, calendar teaching and civic history to a teacher's willingness to honour the rites in the classical texts. Redeemer pupils can attend. Some teachers lose their salaries and move to household schools. Families that can pay follow them, while other pupils lose access to the training that opens a clerkship.

### 349–350 · Qiryath and the public answer

Jirahar petitioners ask for supervised access to the old sanctuary precinct, permission to tend graves and relief from tribute for work on the ruined cisterns. Astrion authorizes a survey and lets representatives from outside the empire attend under safe conduct. Some petitioners seek an eventual sanctuary, others want legal access to graves and water, and some will accept neither under the garrison's conditions. Astrion hopes to reopen a politically useful road, gain a source of credit for the northern command and embarrass bishops who speak as though Qiryath can never prosper again. A wall gives way during clearance and injures workers. The garrison closes the works, and Astrion leaves for war before resolving the petitions. The charter annulled in 70 stays annulled. No sacrifice resumes, and the Silent Gate remains closed. The injured workers and their families retain claims for wages and aid.

Astrion stages his public verification in **350** at a Caleran mustering ground. The augurs put a serviceable bronze standard and its stone socket under an agreed load. The lower support shows a hairline fracture before witnesses, and the fracture changes direction as engineers shift the load. They find the disclosed weakness and clear the ground before the support fails. The event is entered in two forms. The public proclamation says the Red Star has answered the emperor. The augurs' signed record specifies the test, what was risked and how far the response extended. The record eventually reaches the Sanctum, where it is sealed.

The augurs' account describes an answer within the Red Star's domain. Soldiers take it as a promise of victory, engineers as a warning and Redeemer clergy as evidence that a created power can answer. A second public test is called off when the augurs will give no terms for it.

### 350–351 · The northern expedition

A dispute over requisition at the northern wells ends with imperial couriers killed and stores burned. Several Ahdari well houses demand compensation for water, animals and people seized by imperial agents. Well-Tarkhan carriers withdraw from some contracts, while other crews continue carrying supplies under their own agreements. Astrion treats the attacks as a breach of safe conduct and takes a punitive column past the dependable depot line. His early seizure of a fortified store gives him a victory to report. When defenders remove animals and close secondary wells, the captured store cannot supply the column or the civilians following it.

On the retreat in **351**, Astrion rides to a disordered baggage crossing during an attack. A spear strikes him in the side. No witness can identify who threw it. The field record fixes the wound and death. Church tradition later gives him the words *“You have won, Carpenter”*, and Sestaran humanists take up the same words with a different meaning. The thrower, Astrion's last words and his final disposition remain disputed. [[The Ahdari before the Covenant]] · [[Well-Tarkhan]] · [[The Imperial Army and the Frontiers]]

## 351–377 · From the retreat to Vestian

| Ruler | How he gains power and what his government leaves |
|---|---|
| **Savel Orren, 351–352** | Officers acclaim the commander of the imperial guard while the army is still withdrawing. He buys passage and water for the survivors by relinquishing two exposed road forts, recognising the well houses' right to set caravan terms beyond them and returning captives. Families attached to the forts may leave with the garrisons. Households and traders who remain face new fees and patrons. Savel restores stipends to recognised Redeemer congregations, repeals Astrion's religious condition for public teachers and leaves ordinary Star property under local title. He dies suddenly on the journey back. |
| **Corlan Aster, 352–375, senior emperor** | The commanders and civil officers recognise Corlan, who controls the Aurin approaches and can guarantee pay after the failed expedition. He appoints his brother Lucar co-emperor with the eastern and northern commands. They share one imperial title and law, with separate field treasuries and appointments within their commands. In 355–357 a kinsman of Astrion challenges Lucar's authority in the east. He gains supporters by promising to restore lost posts and reimburse evacuated households. The bid collapses when Lucar secures the road garrisons' pay and offers most units safe return to their former commands. Corlan holds the capital and continues financing the southern defences. He dies in 375 while inspecting them. |
| **Lucar Aster, 352–377, co-emperor and then sole emperor** | Lucar finances the eastern roads and keeps frontier levies in separate accounts. In disputes between Redeemer bishops he begins giving disputed revenues to whichever claimant accepts his terms for supervising army chaplains and provisioning. Clergy appeal to other sees and governors under older rules. A temple that can supply the government may still keep its estate. In 376, after failed harvests and fighting farther south, Folk households seek admission across the fort line. Some come under older treaties, others with separate leaders and terms. Lucar allows settlement and recruits fighters. Officers divert grain, take hostages beyond the agreed terms and sell some dependants into slavery. Armed resistance spreads among the admitted households and draws in outside groups. Other Folk keep trading with the forts and help towns evacuate. |
| **Vestian, from 377** | Lucar marches south before reserves and civilian transport are in place. At a contested crossing on the supply routes around Kellovar, his field army breaks and he dies. **Vestian**, already a senior commander with a reserve on the inner roads, holds the depots and draws support from garrison officers and grain towns by keeping their stores open and paying surviving units. The capital recognises him in 377 after he promises an inquiry into the frontier officers and protection for the main roads. Some Folk leaders negotiate directly with his command. The status of individual households, captives and disputed grants takes longer to settle. |

## What Vestian inherits

Vestian inherits field accounts kept by command, officers with power to move provisions, and a precedent for deciding which congregations get buildings and stipends. Caleran can carry petitions to the court. Aurelium has duplicate records and teachers who lost posts under Astrion. Halmenth and Sermora have working networks for housing refugees, returning captives and supplying armies. These institutions give Vestian access to people and supplies while he rebuilds the field army.

He also inherits overlapping land claims. Families have deeds from temple restorations, church grants, frontier settlements and military requisitions. In the eastern provinces, creditors and neighbours can point to the old Jirahar tribute rolls and Astrion's survey petitions when trying to claim tower lands. Those records pass into Vestian's administration. His court's subsequent choice to turn prejudice and property claims into persecution belongs to his reign. His accession in 377 gives him the means to pursue a religious settlement. Compulsion in 380 and the later destruction and confiscation of towers follow in the established sequence.
