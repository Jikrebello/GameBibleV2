# Astrion and the Road to Vestian

Astrion succeeded Sevrin in 348 AR and restored the Stars to imperial favour. He tried to give them a sign as public as the unfreezing river at Velos, and in 350 the Red Star answered his test once. He died at thirty-four on a northern desert campaign in 351, struck by a spear whose thrower no witness saw. His brother Tellan saved the retreating army with water and kept the eastern command, its augurs and the standard of the test for a generation. The Aster brothers returned imperial favour to the Redeemer's faith, and the younger, Lucar, lost his army and his life at the Kellovar ford in 377. Vestian took power that year; compulsory faith followed in 385. [[The Reign of Sevrin]] · [[The Conversion of the Empire]]

## Astrion, 348–351 AR

### 348–349 · Temples, stipends and schools

Astrion spent his youth with Flame tutors and readers who still knew how to interpret Star calendars. As emperor he wanted the civic cult to recover the authority it had once drawn from schools, relief and public ceremony. He restored suspended stipends and gave temple stewards standing to claim rents diverted from demonstrably Star endowments. Governors reviewed disputed plots under Cassian's rules of title and evidence. Cassian's restitution decree and the legal right to Redeemer worship remained in force. Property awarded to an assembly stayed with it. A market town could reopen its temple, keep its church and owe taxes to both institutions' former patrons.

Astrion directed endowed temples to reserve grain for travellers and burial money for people without kin. The order worked where a temple still had tenants, wells or stores. A poor shrine might lose its stipend altogether when its steward could not make the returns the court demanded. Astrion also tied publicly paid posts in rhetoric, calendar teaching and civic history to a teacher's willingness to honour the rites in the classical texts. Redeemer pupils could attend. Some teachers lost their salaries and moved to household schools. Families that could pay followed them, while other pupils lost access to the training that opened a clerkship.

### 349–350 · Velos, Qiryath and the public answer

The open water at Velos was the Redeemer's strongest argument, and every winter crowds walked out on the ice above the bridge to look at it. Since Cassian's day the reach had stood in the augurs' books as a sign answered to the emperor, kept by a crown warden. Astrion had the augurs enter it again as an answer of Hecatara, the Star of crossings, since a bridge is a crossing. The bishops called the new entry a forgery of authorship. The bridge crowds kept calling the black water the Flame's, and the chaplain at the bridge chapel went on saying the prayers of the Velos Concord. The quarrel showed Astrion that a book entry would not move a crowd. He needed a sign people could watch.

Jirahar petitioners meanwhile asked for supervised access to the old sanctuary precinct at Qiryath, permission to tend graves and relief from tribute for work on the ruined cisterns. Astrion authorised a survey and let representatives from outside the empire attend under safe conduct. Some petitioners sought an eventual sanctuary, others wanted legal access to graves and water, and some would accept neither under the garrison's conditions. Astrion hoped to reopen a politically useful road, gain a source of credit for the northern command and embarrass bishops who spoke as though Qiryath could never prosper again. A wall gave way during clearance and injured workers. The garrison closed the works, and Astrion left for war before resolving the petitions. The charter annulled in 70 stayed annulled. No sacrifice resumed, and the Silent Gate remained closed. The injured workers and their families retained claims for wages and aid.

Astrion staged his public verification in **350** at a Caleran mustering ground. The augurs put a serviceable bronze standard and its stone socket under an agreed load before a crowd of soldiers, citizens and clergy. The lower support showed a hairline fracture, and the fracture changed direction as engineers shifted the load. They found the weakness it disclosed and cleared the ground before the support failed. The event was entered in two forms. The public proclamation said the Red Star had answered the emperor. The augurs' signed record specified the test, what was risked and how far the response extended. The record eventually reached the Sanctum, where it was sealed.

The augurs' account described an answer within the Red Star's domain. Soldiers took it as a promise of victory, engineers as a warning and Redeemer clergy as evidence that a created power could answer. Astrion asked for a second public test, of victory itself. The augurs would give no terms for it, and it was called off.

### 350–351 · The northern expedition

A dispute over requisition at the northern wells ended with imperial couriers killed and stores burned. Several Ahdari well houses demanded compensation for water, animals and people seized by imperial agents. Well-Tarkhan carriers withdrew from some contracts, while other crews continued carrying supplies under their own agreements. Astrion treated the attacks as a breach of safe conduct and took a punitive column past the dependable depot line. His early seizure of a fortified store gave him a victory to report. When defenders removed animals and closed secondary wells, the captured store could not supply the column or the civilians following it.

On the retreat in **351**, Astrion rode to a disordered baggage crossing during an attack. A spear struck him in the side. No witness could identify who threw it. The field record fixes the wound and death and gives him no last words, only a question: had the baggage with the register crossed? Preachers read the question as an apostate clinging to a Star answer; the eastern army read it as a commander's care for the men who had stood under the standard. Church tradition later gave him the words *“You have won, Carpenter”*, and Sestaran humanists took up the same words with a different meaning. The thrower, Astrion's last words and his final disposition remain disputed. [[The Ahdari before the Covenant]] · [[Well-Tarkhan]] · [[The Imperial Army and the Frontiers]]

## 351–377 · From the retreat to Vestian

### 351–352 · Water on the depot road

The column came back across the dry belt with its wounded on carts and its animals dying. Officers acclaimed **Savel Orren**, commander of the imperial guard, while the army was still withdrawing. What saved it was water. **Tellan**, Astrion's younger brother and Adran's other son, held the eastern depot road. He bought water from the Well-Tarkhan crews who had kept their contracts and brought it west in skins on camels, under the bronze standard of the 350 test. Soldiers who had watched the crack run through its socket now drank under it. The eastern army has called it the **Answered Standard** ever since, and called Tellan the Water-Bringer.

Savel returned captives to the well houses and recognised their right to set caravan terms beyond the depot line. Two exposed road forts were abandoned in the withdrawal. Families attached to the forts left with the garrisons; households and traders who stayed faced new fees and patrons. Savel restored stipends to recognised Redeemer congregations, repealed Astrion's religious condition for public teachers and left ordinary Star property under local title. When Caleran declared for Corlan Aster in 352, Savel gave the crown back at the depot line and lived out his life on an estate. Guard songs remember him as the emperor who gave it back.

### 352–366 · Two brothers and the eastern standard

The commanders and civil officers in the capital recognised **Corlan Aster**, who controlled the Aurin approaches and could guarantee pay after the failed expedition. Corlan held Caleran and the west. He named his brother **Lucar** co-emperor with the southern and northern commands. They shared one imperial title and law, with separate field treasuries and appointments.

The east stayed with Adran's house. Tellan had a paid command, a sign he could show, the Vasharan frontier at his back and soldiers whose fathers he had saved. He never claimed the throne. In all the years that followed the Red Star never answered the east a second time, and his augurs said so honestly. About 357, after two seasons of stand-off on the road garrisons, the brothers and Tellan settled in the **Accord of the Two Standards**. Corlan confirmed the eastern command's share of local revenue on the terms Sevrin had given Adran in 316, its augurs and its standard. Eastern units swore a Star oath beneath the Answered Standard; western units swore Cassian's service oath. Officers who had hoped the east would march on Caleran found their pay secured instead, and most units accepted the settlement.

Corlan financed the southern defences. He died about 366 while inspecting them.

### 366–377 · Hard winters and the Kellovar ford

Lucar ruled alone from 366. He financed the roads and kept frontier levies in separate accounts. He took the wardenship of the reach at Velos back into crown hands and sold the bridge chapel's revenue to bishops who accepted his terms for army chaplains. In disputes between Redeemer bishops he began giving disputed revenues to whichever claimant accepted his terms for supervising chaplains and provisioning. Clergy appealed to other sees and governors under older rules. A temple that could supply the government might still keep its estate.

The winters of 369–371 were the hardest anyone living could remember. Rivers froze to the bed, and Grobi war-bands came down them on the ice. Folk households sought admission across the southern fort line. Some came under older treaties, others with separate leaders and terms. Lucar admitted them in 371–372 and recruited fighters. His officers diverted grain, took hostages beyond the agreed terms and sold some dependants into slavery. His chaplains searched the camps for idols and took the small iron, bone and hair tokens that Folk households carried to pay the river-courts at fords; the officers sold the silver ones. Armed resistance spread from 372 among the admitted households and drew in outside groups. Other Folk kept trading with the forts and helped towns evacuate.

In **377** Lucar marched south before reserves and civilian transport were in place. His road ran to the ford below Kellovar, the wooded hill where Casvilar had starved the Folk four centuries before. Folk tradition holds that the ford's court took its toll in household tokens. Lucar's army crossed without paying. The water rose under the column, and the crossing broke. Folk fighters who still carried their tokens crossed dry, and the field army broke in the water. Lucar died there. Folk elders say the river exacted its toll and judged nothing. Vestian's preachers called the defeat a judgement on a divided empire. [[Folk Faith and Appeasement]]

**Vestian**, already a senior commander with a reserve on the inner roads, held the depots and drew support from garrison officers and grain towns by keeping their stores open and paying surviving units. The capital recognised him in 377 after he promised an inquiry into the frontier officers, protection for the main roads and the bridge chapel at Velos to the bishops. Some Folk leaders negotiated directly with his command and were settled under their own elders by 379. The status of individual households, captives and disputed grants took longer to settle.

## What Vestian inherits

Vestian inherited field accounts kept by command, officers with power to move provisions, and a precedent for deciding which congregations got buildings and stipends. Caleran could carry petitions to the court. Aurelium had duplicate records and teachers who had lost posts under Astrion. Halmenth and Sermora had working arrangements for housing refugees, returning captives and supplying armies. These gave Vestian access to people and supplies while he rebuilt the field army.

He also inherited two signs in two sets of books. The river at Velos still refused to freeze, and its book was a crown warden's. The Red Star's answer of 350 stood in an augurs' record, and an eastern army still marched under the standard it had answered. Vestian, who had read Kellovar as a judgement, wanted one authority to hold every such sign. That conviction led to the dissolution of the augur colleges in 381 and to the war with the eastern command.

He inherited overlapping land claims as well. Families had deeds from temple restorations, church grants, frontier settlements and military requisitions. In the eastern provinces, creditors and neighbours could point to the old Jirahar tribute rolls and Astrion's survey petitions when trying to claim tower lands. Those records passed into Vestian's administration. His accession in 377 gave him the means to pursue a religious settlement, and his court later chose to turn prejudice and property claims into persecution.
