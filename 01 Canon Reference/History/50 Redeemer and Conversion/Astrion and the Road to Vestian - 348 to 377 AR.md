# Astrion and the Road to Vestian

Astrion succeeded Sevrin in 348 AR and restored the Stars to imperial favour. The Stars answered his public test once. He died at thirty-four on a northern desert campaign in 351, struck by a spear whose thrower no witness saw. His successors returned imperial favour to the Redeemer's faith. Vestian took power in 377, and compulsory faith followed in 380. [[The Reign of Sevrin]] · [[The Conversion of the Empire]]

## Astrion, 348–351 AR

### 348–349 · Temples, stipends and schools

Astrion spent his youth with Flame tutors and readers who still knew how to interpret Star calendars. As emperor he wanted the civic cult to recover the authority it had once drawn from schools, relief and public ceremony. He restored suspended stipends and gave temple stewards standing to claim rents diverted from demonstrably Star endowments. Governors reviewed disputed plots under Cassian's rules of title and evidence. The 293 restitution order and the legal right to Redeemer worship remained in force. Property awarded to an assembly stayed with it. A market town could reopen its temple, keep its church and owe taxes to both institutions' former patrons.

Astrion directed endowed temples to reserve grain for travellers and burial money for people without kin. The order worked where a temple still had tenants, wells or stores. A poor shrine might lose its stipend altogether when its steward could not make the returns the court demanded. Astrion also tied publicly paid posts in rhetoric, calendar teaching and civic history to a teacher's willingness to honour the rites in the classical texts. Redeemer pupils could attend. Some teachers lost their salaries and moved to household schools. Families that could pay followed them, while other pupils lost access to the training that opened a clerkship.

### 349–350 · Qiryath and the public answer

Jirahar petitioners asked for supervised access to the old sanctuary precinct, permission to tend graves and relief from tribute for work on the ruined cisterns. Astrion authorized a survey and let representatives from outside the empire attend under safe conduct. Some petitioners sought an eventual sanctuary, others wanted legal access to graves and water, and some would accept neither under the garrison's conditions. Astrion hoped to reopen a politically useful road, gain a source of credit for the northern command and embarrass bishops who spoke as though Qiryath could never prosper again. A wall gave way during clearance and injured workers. The garrison closed the works, and Astrion left for war before resolving the petitions. The charter annulled in 70 stayed annulled. No sacrifice resumed, and the Silent Gate remained closed. The injured workers and their families retained claims for wages and aid.

Astrion staged his public verification in **350** at a Caleran mustering ground. The augurs put a serviceable bronze standard and its stone socket under an agreed load. The lower support showed a hairline fracture before witnesses, and the fracture changed direction as engineers shifted the load. They found the disclosed weakness and cleared the ground before the support failed. The event was entered in two forms. The public proclamation said the Red Star had answered the emperor. The augurs' signed record specified the test, what was risked and how far the response extended. The record eventually reached the Sanctum, where it was sealed.

The augurs' account described an answer within the Red Star's domain. Soldiers took it as a promise of victory, engineers as a warning and Redeemer clergy as evidence that a created power could answer. A second public test was called off when the augurs would give no terms for it.

### 350–351 · The northern expedition

A dispute over requisition at the northern wells ended with imperial couriers killed and stores burned. Several Ahdari well houses demanded compensation for water, animals and people seized by imperial agents. Well-Tarkhan carriers withdrew from some contracts, while other crews continued carrying supplies under their own agreements. Astrion treated the attacks as a breach of safe conduct and took a punitive column past the dependable depot line. His early seizure of a fortified store gave him a victory to report. When defenders removed animals and closed secondary wells, the captured store could not supply the column or the civilians following it.

On the retreat in **351**, Astrion rode to a disordered baggage crossing during an attack. A spear struck him in the side. No witness could identify who threw it. The field record fixes the wound and death. Church tradition later gave him the words *“You have won, Carpenter”*, and Sestaran humanists took up the same words with a different meaning. The thrower, Astrion's last words and his final disposition remain disputed. [[The Ahdari before the Covenant]] · [[Well-Tarkhan]] · [[The Imperial Army and the Frontiers]]

## 351–377 · From the retreat to Vestian

| Ruler | How he gained power and what his government left |
|---|---|
| **Savel Orren, 351–352** | Officers acclaimed the commander of the imperial guard while the army was still withdrawing. He bought passage and water for the survivors by relinquishing two exposed road forts, recognising the well houses' right to set caravan terms beyond them and returning captives. Families attached to the forts could leave with the garrisons. Households and traders who remained faced new fees and patrons. Savel restored stipends to recognised Redeemer congregations, repealed Astrion's religious condition for public teachers and left ordinary Star property under local title. He died suddenly on the journey back. |
| **Corlan Aster, 352–375, senior emperor** | The commanders and civil officers recognised Corlan, who controlled the Aurin approaches and could guarantee pay after the failed expedition. He appointed his brother Lucar co-emperor with the eastern and northern commands. They shared one imperial title and law, with separate field treasuries and appointments within their commands. In 355–357 a kinsman of Astrion challenged Lucar's authority in the east. He gained supporters by promising to restore lost posts and reimburse evacuated households. The bid collapsed when Lucar secured the road garrisons' pay and offered most units safe return to their former commands. Corlan held the capital and continued financing the southern defences. He died in 375 while inspecting them. |
| **Lucar Aster, 352–377, co-emperor and then sole emperor** | Lucar financed the eastern roads and kept frontier levies in separate accounts. In disputes between Redeemer bishops he began giving disputed revenues to whichever claimant accepted his terms for supervising army chaplains and provisioning. Clergy appealed to other sees and governors under older rules. A temple that could supply the government might still keep its estate. In 376, after failed harvests and fighting farther south, Folk households sought admission across the fort line. Some came under older treaties, others with separate leaders and terms. Lucar allowed settlement and recruited fighters. Officers diverted grain, took hostages beyond the agreed terms and sold some dependants into slavery. Armed resistance spread among the admitted households and drew in outside groups. Other Folk kept trading with the forts and helped towns evacuate. |
| **Vestian, from 377** | Lucar marched south before reserves and civilian transport were in place. At a contested crossing on the supply routes around Kellovar, his field army broke and he died. **Vestian**, already a senior commander with a reserve on the inner roads, held the depots and drew support from garrison officers and grain towns by keeping their stores open and paying surviving units. The capital recognised him in 377 after he promised an inquiry into the frontier officers and protection for the main roads. Some Folk leaders negotiated directly with his command. The status of individual households, captives and disputed grants took longer to settle. |

## What Vestian inherits

Vestian inherited field accounts kept by command, officers with power to move provisions, and a precedent for deciding which congregations got buildings and stipends. Caleran could carry petitions to the court. Aurelium had duplicate records and teachers who had lost posts under Astrion. Halmenth and Sermora had working networks for housing refugees, returning captives and supplying armies. These institutions gave Vestian access to people and supplies while he rebuilt the field army.

He also inherited overlapping land claims. Families had deeds from temple restorations, church grants, frontier settlements and military requisitions. In the eastern provinces, creditors and neighbours could point to the old Jirahar tribute rolls and Astrion's survey petitions when trying to claim tower lands. Those records passed into Vestian's administration. His accession in 377 gave him the means to pursue a religious settlement, and his court later chose to turn prejudice and property claims into persecution.
