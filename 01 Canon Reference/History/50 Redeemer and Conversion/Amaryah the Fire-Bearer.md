# Amaryah the Fire-Bearer

The Mother of the Redeemer — the most venerated woman in the world's history, and the great *theological* dispute of the faith (as the Kindling Question is its constitutional one). Every branch of the Redeemer's faith is partly defined by what it teaches about her.

## Her Life

Amaryah bath Yoreth, a Jirahar carpenter's wife of an eastern tower town: poor, devout, unlettered, of a lineage the genealogy-scrolls trace (with suspicious tidiness, scholars note) back to the Great Prophet's own house. The traditions agree on little else about her early life and everything about her character: she treasured, she endured, she did not explain.

At the Binding, she stood at the foot of the Pillar of Concordance for three days. Tradition holds the Redeemer's only words from the monolith spoken to individuals were spoken to her and to Saint Calira standing beside her: *"Daughter, behold your guardian. Guardian, behold my mother."*

After the Ascension, Calira took her south into the mountain country, away from the Purge. Her hearth at **Veridun Minor** became the first shrine of the faith — the lamp on her table lit from the ember Saint Solana carried out of Caleran in a sealed jar. That chain — **Solana carried it, Amaryah kept it, Calira died for it** — is the physical pedigree of the mother-flame now burning in the Cathedral of the Eternal Ember, and the root of the Patriarchate's **Ember Succession**.

## The Dormition

She died — or in the universal phrase, **fell asleep** — at Veridun Minor around 55 AR, an old woman, with the surviving Companions gathered (tradition says Elsin walked out of the Folk borderlands unsummoned and arrived the same hour). On the third day her tomb was found open and empty, filled with the scent of bread and cedar. What this means is, characteristically, disputed.

## The Title and the Council of Halmenth (411 AR)

The fight over her title is the faith's Ephesus. One party taught she was **Fire-Bearer** (*she bore the Flame itself*: the child in her womb was already the uncreated Fire's dwelling); the party of **Bishop Nasriel of Qabir** taught she was **Man-Bearer** (*she bore the vessel*: the man Isfir, whom the Fire crowned at his ministry's beginning — "the lamp was made in her; the Fire descended later"). The **Council of Halmenth (411 AR)** condemned the Man-Bearer teaching: to divide the vessel from the Fire, the council ruled, is to make the Redeemer a possessed man rather than the Flame made flesh.

**Nasriel's communion did not die.** It fled north into the desert cities beyond imperial reach, and when the Covenant conquered those lands it survived as a sealed-contract client faith — see [[The Velian Church]]. The heretics outlived the empire that condemned them.

## The Four Traditions

### Veridun: the Fire-Bearer of the Hearth

Maximal veneration, minimal definition. She is the Fire-Bearer, the Ever-Sealed, "more honorable than the phoenixes, beyond compare more glorious than the solar hosts." Her Dormition is kept as the empire's tenderest feast; her **hearth-fire** is the Patriarchate's living link to the apostolic age; the **Standing Hymn** (sung only on one's feet, all night) is the summit of Thalmyric liturgy. She appears in **icons and dreams — never in fields**: Veridun accepts no meadow apparitions, holding that the Mother does not perform. The empire's supreme Marian memory is the **Veil over Veridun (906 AR)**: during the Qahirid advance after Caleran's fall, watchers on the walls saw a woman's figure above the city spreading a veil of pale fire, and the siege broke in a night storm. The Feast of the Veil is second only to the Rekindling.

### Eclessia: the Blessed Mother, Defined

The same Fire-Bearer title, plus centuries of juridical definition. The Sanctum has dogmatized the **Unkindled Conception** (she was conceived "without ash" — untouched by the fall's soot, prepared as a pure vessel; defined 1183 AR after two centuries of scholastic war) and the **Rising of the Mother** (bodily assumption, defined 1204 AR). Eclessian piety counts prayers on **ember-bead chaplets**, crowns her images in May-analog processions, and — decisively — accepts **tested apparitions**:

- **The Spring at Sarnwell (1311 AR)** — she appeared to a crippled washerwoman; the spring that opened there passes every relic-court test; the healing registry is the Church's proudest modern archive. *(Lourdes.)*
- **The False Lady of Bruckmoor (1243 AR)** — a weeping apparition drew forty years of pilgrimage before the Inquisition of Saint Malveris proved it an **Idol-feeding operation**: an Envy-architecture harvesting the devotion of the desperate. The site was burned and salted. Bruckmoor is why the relic courts test everything, and what Veridun cites whenever Eclessia authenticates a new field-miracle.
- **The Lady of the Sloe-Field at Vessing (1352 AR)** — three shepherd children, a figure of banked fire, and a message no two of them report identically; one version touches the Kindling Question itself. **The Sanctum has not ruled.** Eight years of silence: confirmation would inflame Veridun and the Freeflame; condemnation would break the marches' piety; and everyone remembers Bruckmoor. The pending case sits in Alistair III's registry beside the Unburnt Psalter, his vault of things too dangerous to open.

### The Freeflame: First of the Kindled

Honored, not invoked. She is "the first of the kindled and the pattern of every kindling — but **a lamp is not the Fire**." Ferro's *Order of the Open Flame* devotes a warm, careful chapter to her obedience and forbids her intercession in the same paragraph. Ashenmen mobs smashed Marian images in the risings — a guilt the movement's second generation handles badly: reformed congregations quietly keep her feast under other names, and everyone pretends not to notice.

### The Velian Church: the Blessed Man-Bearer

The condemned remnant, surviving under Covenant contract in Zanthir and the old Velian cities. They honor her deeply — as the mother of the man the Fire crowned. Their standing provocations: married clergy, no images at all, and above all their liturgy — prayed to this day **in the Jirahar dialect the Redeemer himself spoke**, while both great churches worship in imperial Atherian. The despised heretics pray in his mother-tongue, and know it, and mention it.

## Story Use

The Vessing ruling as a live political bomb; Bruckmoor-style counterfeit apparitions as investigation quests; the Veil of Veridun contested by Eclessian polemicists ("mass delusion of a starving city"); Velian Church communities as tolerated-yet-despised minorities in Covenant and frontier settings; the Solana–Amaryah–Calira ember chain as relic-heist material of the highest order.
