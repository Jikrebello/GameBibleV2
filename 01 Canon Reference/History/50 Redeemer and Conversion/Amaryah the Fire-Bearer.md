# Amaryah the Fire-Bearer

The Mother of the Redeemer — the most venerated woman in the world's history, and the great *theological* dispute of the faith (as the Kindling Question is its constitutional one). Every branch of the Redeemer's faith is partly defined by what it teaches about her.

## Her Life

Amaryah bath Yoreth, a Jirahar carpenter's wife of an eastern tower town: poor, devout, unlettered, of a lineage the genealogy-scrolls trace (with suspicious tidiness, scholars note) back to the Great Prophet's own house. The traditions agree on little else about her early life and everything about her character: she treasured, she endured, she did not explain.

Amaryah stood with Saint Calira at the Binding outside Second Qiryath and remained until Isfir's death. Tradition remembers the words by which he entrusted them to one another. They mourned through the Resting Day after his burial.

After the Ascension and the gift of the Holy Flame at Qiryath, Calira accompanied Amaryah to **Veridun Minor**. Solana carried a coal from the congregation's hearth in a covered clay firepot. Amaryah used it to light her lamp, and her house became a shrine and refuge. Calira died defending it in 29 AR. Local households took turns tending the lamp. When Tervan Marcellan's edict of 249 demanded a licence for every lamp kept over a grave or in a shrine, the keepers would not license hers. They carried it in a covered firepot to a neighbour's house and hid it at the back of the bread oven, where it burned among the loaves until the edict was suspended in 253. Congregations that would not worship by a licensed flame came to that oven for fire, and the custom of lighting a church's lamp from an older lamp's flame spread from that winter. After legalization a daughter flame was carried to Caleran. This custody is the root of the Patriarchate's **Ember Succession**. [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]]

## The Dormition

She died — or in the universal phrase, **fell asleep** — at Veridun Minor around 55 AR, an old woman, with Solana, the last living Companion, and the households of the dead Companions gathered (tradition says Elsin, three years in his secret grave, was seen walking out of the Folk borderlands unsummoned and arrived the same hour). On the third day her tomb was found open and empty, filled with the scent of bread and cedar. What this means is, characteristically, disputed.

## The Title and the Council of Halmenth (411 AR)

One party taught she was **Fire-Bearer** (*she bore the Flame itself*: the child in her womb was already the uncreated Fire's dwelling); the party of **Bishop Nasriel of Qabir** taught she was **Man-Bearer** (*she bore the vessel*: the man Isfir, whom the Fire crowned at his ministry's beginning — "the lamp was made in her; the Fire descended later"). The **Council of Halmenth (411 AR)** condemned the Man-Bearer teaching: to divide the vessel from the Fire, the council ruled, is to make the Redeemer a possessed man rather than the Flame made flesh.

The Man-Bearer party was born in a public square. In the harbours of Sermora and the gateway ports, Qabir among them, many Redeemer congregations were of Jirahar descent; they kept their mothers' registers beside their baptism rolls and some prayed in the Jirahar dialect. When Vestian made the faith compulsory in 385 and thousands of reception lamps at the state ceremonies burned yellow, these congregations said that they had heard such a silence before, at Qiryath, and named it the **Second Silence**. Their preachers said of the state's lamp, *the lamp is made by hands; the Fire comes after*. Within a generation the saying had been turned on the Redeemer himself. Nasriel, born at Qabir about 362 to a baptised family whose mother's line came from a Sea Fleet household, kept Qabir's count of the unkindled professions as a young reader and carried the copies to a Jirahar hearing house at Raveth. He was on the road when a crowd burned it. His mother's register was seized in the 390s, and when Qabir elected him bishop about 402, Caleran objected on grounds of his descent. The Velian Church still holds that the council condemned his ancestry along with his doctrine. [[The Conversion of the Empire]] · [[Vestian Persecutions and the Long Diaspora]]

**Nasriel's communion did not die.** It fled north into the desert cities beyond imperial reach, and when the Covenant conquered those lands it survived as a sealed-contract client faith — see [[The Velian Church]]. The heretics outlived the empire that condemned them.

## The Four Traditions

### Veridun: the Fire-Bearer of the Hearth

Maximal veneration, minimal definition. She is the Fire-Bearer, the Ever-Sealed, "more honorable than the phoenixes, beyond compare more glorious than the solar hosts." Her Dormition is kept as the empire's tenderest feast; her **hearth-fire** is the Patriarchate's living link to the apostolic age; the **Standing Hymn** (sung only on one's feet, all night) is the summit of Thalmyric liturgy. She appears in **icons and dreams — never in fields**: Veridun accepts no meadow apparitions, holding that the Mother does not perform. The empire's supreme Marian memory is the **Veil over Veridun (906 AR)**: during the Qahirid advance after Caleran's fall, watchers on the walls saw a woman's figure above the city spreading a veil of pale fire, and the siege broke in a night storm. The Feast of the Veil is second only to the Rekindling.

### Eclessia: the Blessed Mother, Defined

The same Fire-Bearer title, plus centuries of juridical definition. The Sanctum has dogmatized the **Unkindled Conception** (she was conceived "without ash" — untouched by the fall's soot, prepared as a pure vessel; defined 1183 AR after two centuries of scholastic war) and the **Rising of the Mother** (bodily assumption, defined 1204 AR). Eclessian piety counts prayers on **ember-bead chaplets**, crowns her images in processions, and — decisively — accepts **tested apparitions**:

- **The Spring at Sarnwell (1311 AR)** — she appeared to a crippled washerwoman in a valley the Ashenmen had stripped of images; the spring that opened there passes every relic-court test; the healing registry is the Church's proudest modern archive.
- **The False Lady of Bruckmoor (c. 1203–1243 AR)** — a weeping apparition drew forty years of pilgrimage until 1243, when the Inquisition of Saint Malveris proved it an **Idol-feeding operation**: an Envy-architecture harvesting the devotion of the desperate. The site was burned and salted. Bruckmoor is why the relic courts test everything, and what Veridun cites whenever Eclessia authenticates a new field-miracle.
- **The Lady of the Sloe-Field at Vessing (1352 AR)** — three shepherd children, a figure of banked fire, and a message no two of them report identically; one version touches the Kindling Question itself. **The Sanctum has not ruled.** Eight years of silence: confirmation would inflame Veridun and the Freeflame; condemnation would break the marches' piety; and everyone remembers Bruckmoor. The pending case sits in Alistair III's registry beside the Unburnt Psalter, his vault of things too dangerous to open.

### The Freeflame: First of the Kindled

Honored, not invoked. She is "the first of the kindled and the pattern of every kindling — but **a lamp is not the Fire**." Ferro's *Order of the Open Flame* devotes a warm, careful chapter to her obedience and forbids her intercession in the same paragraph. Ashenmen mobs smashed Marian images in the risings — a guilt the movement's second generation handles badly: reformed congregations quietly keep her feast under other names, and everyone pretends not to notice.

### The Velian Church: the Blessed Man-Bearer

The condemned remnant, surviving under Covenant contract in Zanthir and the old Velian cities. They honor her deeply — as the mother of the man the Fire crowned. Their standing provocations: married clergy, no images at all, and above all their liturgy — prayed to this day **in the Jirahar dialect the Redeemer himself spoke**, while both great churches worship in imperial Atherian. The despised heretics pray in his mother-tongue, and know it, and mention it.
