# Amaryah the Fire-Bearer

The Mother of the Redeemer — the most venerated woman in the world's history, and the great *theological* dispute of the faith (as the Kindling Question is its constitutional one). Every branch of the Redeemer's faith is partly defined by what it teaches about her.

## Her Life

Amaryah bath Yoreth, a Jirahar carpenter's wife of an eastern tower town: poor, devout, unlettered, of a lineage the genealogy-scrolls trace (with suspicious tidiness, scholars note) back to the Great Prophet's own house. The traditions agree on little else about her early life and everything about her character: she treasured, she endured, she did not explain.

Amaryah stood with Saint Calira at the Binding outside Second Qiryath and remained until Isfir's death. Tradition remembers the words by which he entrusted them to one another. They mourned through the Resting Day after his burial.

After the Ascension and the gift of the Holy Flame at Qiryath, Calira accompanied Amaryah to **Veridun Minor**. Solana carried a coal from the congregation's hearth in a covered clay firepot. Amaryah used it to light her lamp, and her house became a shrine and refuge. Calira died defending it in 29 AR. Local households took turns tending the lamp. When Tervan Marcellan's edict of 245 ordered every lamp kept over a grave or in a shrine to be put out before the Stars and relit from the college fire, to burn green on the colleges' oil, the keepers would not carry hers to the market altar. They carried it in a covered firepot to a neighbour's house and hid it at the back of the bread oven, where it burned among the loaves until the edict was suspended in 248. Congregations that would not worship by a flame that had burned green came to that oven for fire, and the custom of lighting a church's lamp from an older lamp's flame spread from that winter. After legalisation a daughter flame was carried to Caleran. This custody is the root of the Patriarchate's **Ember Succession**. [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]]

## The Falling-Asleep

She died — or in the universal phrase, **fell asleep** — at Veridun Minor around 55 AR, an old woman, with Solana, the last living Companion, and the households of the dead Companions gathered (tradition says Elsin, three years in his secret grave, was seen walking out of the Folk borderlands unsummoned and arrived the same hour). On the third day her tomb was found open and empty, filled with the scent of bread and cedar. What this means is, characteristically, disputed.

## The Title and the Council of Halmenth (411 AR)

One party taught she was **Fire-Bearer** (*she bore the Flame itself*: the child in her womb was already the uncreated Fire's dwelling); the party of **Bishop Qedrel of Qabir** taught she was **Man-Bearer** (*she bore the vessel*: the man Isfir, whom the Fire crowned at his ministry's beginning — "the lamp was made in her; the Fire descended later"). The **Council of Halmenth (411 AR)** condemned the Man-Bearer teaching: to divide the vessel from the Fire, the council ruled, is to make the Redeemer a possessed man rather than the Flame made flesh.

The Man-Bearer party was born in a public square. In the harbours of Sermora and the gateway ports, Qabir among them, many Redeemer congregations were of Jirahar descent; they kept their mothers' registers beside their baptism rolls and some prayed in the Jirahar dialect. When Vestian made the faith compulsory in 385 and thousands of reception lamps at the state ceremonies burned yellow, these congregations said that they had heard such a silence before, at Qiryath, and named it the **Second Silence**. Their preachers said of the state's lamp, *the lamp is made by hands; the Fire comes after*. Within a generation the saying had been turned on the Redeemer himself. Qedrel, born at Qabir about 362 to a baptised family whose mother's line came from a Sea Fleet household, kept Qabir's count of the unkindled professions as a young reader and carried the copies to a Jirahar hearing house at Raveth. He was on the road when a crowd burned it. His mother's register was seized in the 390s, and when Qabir elected him bishop about 402, Caleran objected on grounds of his descent. The Velian Church still holds that the council condemned his ancestry along with his doctrine. [[The Conversion of the Empire]] · [[Vestian Persecutions and the Long Diaspora]]

The regency that summoned the bishops to Halmenth had a reason of its own. Since Vestian's death an estate that kept a relic could buy freedom from the land tax, and Qedrel would certify no relic estate in his province: a reliquary, his preachers said, was another lamp made by hands. If the gateway's teaching spread, no one would pay for an exemption the Church called worthless. A warship brought a daughter of the Eternal Ember from Caleran in a covered firepot, and the bishops sat beside it in the harbour church at the mouth of the Celion, where gulls came in at the doors. The Fire-Bearer party pointed to that flame: her lamp, lit from the Qiryath coal, had burned unbroken for nearly four centuries, and the Fire had lived in her house. Qedrel stood and read Qabir's count of the unkindled professions district by district, a white flame here and there among thousands of yellow ones, and asked the council to open Aurelium's sealed register beside it. The council refused. Caleran's readers then charged him under the augur colleges' own old law, which had passed to the bishops with the colleges' books in 383: he had published a sign, or the want of one, that no recognised authority had entered.

The council condemned the Man-Bearer teaching and forbade the ordination of any man whose mother's register was kept beside his baptism roll. At its close every bishop who assented lit his see's lamp from the Caleran flame to carry home. Qabir's deacons had brought their own fire from the gateway, a daughter of Amaryah's lamp as well, carried out of the bread oven at Veridun Minor in the winter of 248, two generations before Caleran's Ember was lit, and they would not relight it from a flame that had come under a warship's guard. Qabir's lamp left the church burning, and out of communion. The regents' officers came for Qedrel that night. Halmenth's ferry crews, whose bishop **Ostrel** had voted to condemn his teaching, rowed him out of the harbour to a Sea Fleet ship wintering in the inlet below the Sea Tower; his mother's line came from the fleets, and the captains would not let the officers search their docks. He reached Qabir by sea in the spring.

**Qedrel's communion did not die, and it did not go into exile.** The regency would not march on a gateway port for one bishop. It struck Qabir and the cities that kept communion with it off the roll of recognised sees, so that their clergy could neither certify a relic nor witness a charter. No one there could buy an exemption, and within a generation those cities were among the few districts of the empire still paying their land tax in full; the regents left them alone. The condemned gathered in them from the harbours and the eastern towns. Qedrel died at Qabir about 440, and his grave-lamp was lit from his city's own flame. When the Covenant conquered those lands the communion survived as a sealed-contract client faith — see [[The Velian Church]]. The heretics outlived the empire that condemned them, and they say their lamp is an older daughter of the Mother's than Caleran's Ember.

## The Four Traditions

### Veridun: the Fire-Bearer of the Hearth

Maximal veneration, minimal definition. She is the Fire-Bearer, the Ever-Sealed, "more honourable than the phoenixes, beyond compare more glorious than the solar hosts." Her Falling-Asleep is kept as the empire's tenderest feast; her **hearth-fire** is the Patriarchate's living link to the apostolic age; the **Standing Hymn** (sung only on one's feet, all night) is the summit of Thalmyric liturgy. She appears in **icons and dreams — never in fields**: Veridun accepts no meadow apparitions, holding that the Mother does not perform. The empire's dearest memory of the Mother is the **Veil over Veridun (906 AR)**: during the Qahirid advance after Caleran's fall, the hearth-keepers of Veridun Minor baked the refuge's flour through the night against the council's order to burn it, the smoke of their ovens lay over the city for the five days the bread lasted, smelling of bread and cedar, and refugees and besiegers alike dreamed of a hearth. Veridun counts it hers because nobody saw her: she came, as she always comes, in a dream and an oven. The Feast of the Veil is second only to the Rekindling.

### Eclessia: the Blessed Mother, Defined

The same Fire-Bearer title, plus centuries of juridical definition. The Sanctum has dogmatised the **Unkindled Conception** (she was conceived "without ash" — untouched by the fall's soot, prepared as a pure vessel; defined 1183 AR after two centuries of scholastic war) and the **Rising of the Mother** (bodily assumption, defined 1204 AR). Eclessian piety counts prayers on **ember-bead chaplets**, crowns her images in processions, and — decisively — accepts **tested apparitions**:

- **The Spring at Sarnwell (1311 AR)** — she appeared to eleven Ashenmen at a village well they had fouled with her broken image, set the pieces on the kerb, and the dry well ran over; the eleven swore to it on the Weiss, the spring passes every relic-court test, and the healing registry is the Church's proudest modern archive.
- **The False Lady of Bruckmoor (c. 1203–1243 AR)** — a weeping apparition drew forty years of pilgrimage until 1243, when the Inquisition of Saint Malveris proved it an **Idol-feeding operation**: an Envy-architecture harvesting the devotion of the desperate. The site was burned and salted. Bruckmoor is why the relic courts test everything, and what Veridun cites whenever Eclessia authenticates a new field-miracle.
- **The Lady of the Sloe-Field at Vessing (1352 AR)** — three witnesses at a runaway's child's grave, a sexton, a reformed carter and the mother; a figure of banked fire; and a message no two of them report identically, each version telling against the witness's own church; one version touches the Kindling Question itself. **The Sanctum has not ruled.** Eight years of silence: confirmation would inflame Veridun and the Freeflame; condemnation would break the marches' piety; and everyone remembers Bruckmoor. The pending case sits in Alistair III's registry beside the Unburnt Psalter, his vault of things too dangerous to open.

### The Freeflame: First of the Kindled

Honoured, not invoked. She is "the first of the kindled and the pattern of every kindling — but **a lamp is not the Fire**." Ferro's *Order of the Open Flame* devotes a warm, careful chapter to her obedience and forbids her intercession in the same paragraph. Ashenmen mobs smashed images of the Mother in the risings — a guilt the movement's second generation handles badly: reformed congregations quietly keep her feast under other names, and everyone pretends not to notice.

### The Velian Church: the Blessed Man-Bearer

The condemned remnant, surviving under Covenant contract in Zanthir and the old Velian cities. They honour her deeply — as the mother of the man the Fire crowned. Their standing provocations: married clergy, no images at all, and above all their liturgy — prayed to this day **in the Jirahar dialect the Redeemer himself spoke**, while both great churches worship in imperial Atherian. The despised heretics pray in his mother-tongue, and know it, and mention it.
