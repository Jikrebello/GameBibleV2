# The First Church

The first Church was the congregation that formed at Second Qiryath in 0 AR. Its first work was to tell what happened, baptise those who believed, eat together in remembrance of Isfir, and care for people who would otherwise go without food or burial. By the time the city fell in 65 AR, people were doing that work in houses and workshops across the empire. Qiryath was their point of origin. They could survive its destruction because other households had already learned the work.

[[The Rise of the Redeemer]] describes the Resurrection and gift of the Holy Flame. [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]] follows the witnesses and their missions. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]] follows the war that displaced the first congregation.

## 0 AR — A congregation in the city

After the Ascension, the remaining Companions, Amaryah and other followers stayed in Qiryath. At the next pilgrimage gathering, the Holy Flame came upon the people assembled there. Travellers heard the proclamation in the languages they used at home. The people who had seen the empty tomb and eaten with Isfir spoke publicly about the Resurrection. The first teaching read his death and rising through the Scrolls and called hearers to repentance, baptism and a different way of living with their neighbours. The households that welcomed them gave them somewhere to return after the festival crowds went home.

Some believers continued prayer and study in the sanctuary courts. They also gathered in houses to break bread. As the gatherings grew, the host of the first meeting room lent the space at different hours so cooks and labourers could attend after work. The Companions taught and testified. The households that kept the doors and food stores decided what they could provide that week. Solana's work at the gathering house gave her practical knowledge of whose family had room, whose oven was working, and which visitors had no kin in the city. Jareth began collecting testimony while witnesses were still close enough to ask what they had seen.

## The first dispute is over food

In the first years, the households offered grain, oil or money according to what they had. The people handing out food relied on quarter lists and local kin to identify recipients. Widows from travelling Jirahar households were missed because their names were entered under camps or towns that had already moved on. A woman who helped prepare the meals brought the missed names to the Companions. The people who brought food chose servers from both the city and travelling households. The servers called out the names in two languages, and a neighbour could answer for a widow too ill to come. Teaching and testimony stayed with the Companions; the bread stayed with the servers.

Smaller assemblies reproduced the practice with a room, shared provisions and people who knew their neighbours. They accepted gifts on terms that left their stewards free to feed those in need. When officials began seeking names, some servers stopped keeping a complete list in the house. A register that helped a server find a widow could also lead an officer to her door.

## Local pressure and the roads

The first open clash began when witnesses continued teaching in a public sanctuary court after officials ordered them to stop. The council could shut the court to them and punish those of its own people it could reach, and Atherian officers could arrest anyone for breaking the peace. Some elders pressed for arrests, while other teachers continued to argue with Isfir's followers or objected to turning an argument into a criminal case. The first known imperial response was provincial.

As houses came under scrutiny, members of travelling companies gave teachers places in caravans and recommended them to kin at the next landing. Some went because they had work or family elsewhere. Others left after a summons or the loss of their meeting place. Letters travelled with the same people who carried goods, news of deaths and requests for money. Dorn helped maintain the roads and shelters that the early mission used. Calira accompanied Amaryah and Solana to Veridun Minor, where the hearth that received Solana's coal also fed travellers. By about 15 AR there was an assembly in Virelos for Aquilan to pursue. The network was already growing when he joined it.

## About 15 AR — Aquilan enters through the door he meant to break

Sha'il bar Qoreth carried warrants to Virelos. After the encounter on the road, the people he had come to arrest sheltered him while he was blind. Someone who knew what his warrants could do took responsibility for bringing him into the house. The congregation baptised him and fed him. Families harmed by his earlier work could still refuse him access to names and safe houses. For a time, a believer who knew his former work accompanied him when he approached another congregation, vouching for what they had seen him do since Virelos.

Aquilan had enough standing in both Jirahar and imperial life to write to communities divided over his teaching. He argued from the Scrolls that the Path is fulfilled in the Redeemer. His travels and letters carried disputes between eastern towns, Atherian cities, Sestaran ports and Folk borderlands. [[The Aquilan Epistles — Selected Passages]]

## Who can share the table

At Halmenth, baptisms associated with Naris brought in working households who kept different kitchens. In Sermora, Jirahar seafarers and local households met under the same roof. An early dispute began when a Jirahar host would feed a newly baptised neighbour but hesitated to eat at that neighbour's table. The host could make the provisions at home meet the stricter household rules. A meal prepared by the neighbour raised questions about slaughter, food offered to other powers and the possibility of sharing one cup. Some people argued that the new households must first enter the Jirahar covenant. Others had already shared worship, care for the sick and work with them for years.

Before the main Purge, messengers from several assemblies brought those cases to a meeting in Qiryath while some of the Companions could still attend. The agreement admitted believers through baptism without requiring Jirahar communal conversion. It expected mixed assemblies to prepare a common table people could attend in conscience, to refuse food knowingly offered in worship of the Stars or other powers, and to continue relief across household and language lines. Jirahar believers continued their own household obligations. Host communities worked out how to honour them when they ate together. Some patrons kept seating their own households apart even after the agreement was read aloud. Aquilan's letters addressed disputes that continued after the ruling.

## 29–60 AR — The Purge and the passing of the witnesses

Calira died defending Amaryah's refuge in 29 AR. The larger wave of persecution in about 36–45 AR reached communities that already differed in size, wealth and local standing. It began inside the augur colleges. Tyros, once an astrologer-priest, had been telling anyone who would listen that every college's book of answers was blank for the three days after the Binding, and junior augurs in several provinces had come over to him with copies. The High Augurate at Caleran won a commission of inquiry about 36. Its augurs rode into the towns with paid informers behind them, searched households for copies of the colleges' books and charged the people they caught with forging or stealing sacred records, since belief alone was no crime they could prove. Its edict made it a crime to publish any wonder the colleges had not entered, and congregations learned to write their signs down as weather. At Caleran, Jareth died in the Celestial Forum in 41 AR. Tyros and Aquilan died there about 45 AR. At Halmenth, soldiers drowned Naris while he was baptising. Congregations lost teachers and couriers at different times. Some closed a public gathering and continued meeting by household. Some patrons bought safety by giving up servants. Others kept prisoners' children and lost their property for it.

The first Church had to choose how to hear the testimony of people who had recanted under threat, how to support their families and whether they could safely be entrusted with names again. The loss of witnesses gave authority to the people who could still teach what they had received and could produce letters other houses recognised. Elders oversaw particular assemblies, and stewards continued looking after meals, burials and aid. The homes which first hosted a meal could by then have obligations extending through several generations. Amaryah died around 55 AR, and Solana, by then the last of the Companions, kept her lamp. The household's practice of tending it continued in Veridun Minor as the people who remembered Qiryath personally grew fewer.

## 61–68 AR — The city falls and its people carry the Church

When revolt began in 61 AR, believers in Qiryath faced the same competing duties as their neighbours. Some left with families on routes already used by the assemblies. Some stayed to care for dependants or to keep burial work going. Relief from other towns reached people through kin and commercial contacts while the roads were still open, and by the same roads came word that Solana had died in her sleep at Veridun Minor in 63 AR, far from the fighting, among the pilgrims who still came to the lamp. The siege broke those contacts. The sanctuary fell in 65 AR and the last fortress in 68. Atheria replaced Qiryath's charter government with direct rule. Many displaced Jirahar found refuge beyond Atherian jurisdiction. Among the city's believers, some went with those households and others reached assemblies inside the empire.

The survivors carried teaching, burial names, letters and the memory of the places where Isfir had spoken and been buried. They could find congregations in cities which had already sent and received teachers and relief. In Caleran, the memory of the martyrs eventually gave the former forum a church. At Veridun Minor, local households kept Amaryah's lamp through the long period of uneven persecution. At Qiryath, the terrain and loss of the families who knew it eventually defeated claims to identify the exact tomb.
