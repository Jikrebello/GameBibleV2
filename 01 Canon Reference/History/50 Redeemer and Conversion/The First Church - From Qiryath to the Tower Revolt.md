# The First Church

The first Church is the congregation that forms at Second Qiryath in 0 AR. Its first work is to tell what happened, baptize those who believe, eat together in remembrance of Isfir, and care for people who would otherwise go without food or burial. By the time the city falls in 70 AR, people are doing that work in houses and workshops across the empire. Qiryath is their point of origin. They can survive its destruction because other households have already learned the work.

[[The Rise of the Redeemer]] describes the Resurrection and gift of the Holy Flame. [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]] follows the witnesses and their missions. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]] follows the war that displaced the first congregation.

## 0 AR — A congregation in the city

After the Ascension, the remaining Companions, Amaryah and other followers stay in Qiryath. At the next pilgrimage gathering, the Holy Flame comes upon the people assembled there. Travellers hear the proclamation in the languages they use at home. The people who saw the empty tomb and ate with Isfir speak publicly about the Resurrection. The first teaching reads his death and rising through the Scrolls and calls hearers to repentance, baptism and a different way of living with their neighbours. The households that welcome them give them somewhere to return after the festival crowds go home.

Some believers continue prayer and study in the sanctuary courts. They also gather in houses to break bread. As the gatherings grow, the host of the first meeting room lends the space at different hours so cooks and labourers can attend after work. The Companions teach and testify. The households that keep the doors and food stores decide what they can provide that week. Solana's work at the gathering house gives her practical knowledge of whose family has room, whose oven is working, and which visitors have no kin in the city. Jareth begins collecting testimony while witnesses are still close enough to ask what they saw.

## The first dispute is over food

In the first years, the households offer grain, oil or money according to what they have. The people handing out food rely on quarter lists and local kin to identify recipients. Widows from travelling Jirahar households are missed because their names are entered under camps or towns that have already moved on. A woman who helps prepare the meals brings the missed names to the Companions. The people who bring food choose servers from both the city and travelling households. The servers read out the distribution with an interpreter, and someone can answer for a household that cannot be present. Teaching and testimony remain the Companions' work. The people doing relief gain authority over the work for which they answer.

Smaller assemblies reproduce the practice with a room, shared provisions and people who know their neighbours. They accept gifts on terms that leave their stewards free to feed those in need. When officials begin seeking names, some servers stop keeping a complete list in the house. A register that helps a server find a widow can also lead an officer to her door.

## Local pressure and the roads

The first open clash begins when witnesses continue teaching in a public sanctuary court after officials order them to stop. The council can deny use of the court and discipline people answerable to it. Atherian officers can hear charges over breaches of public order and execute warrants within their jurisdiction. Some elders press for arrests, while other teachers continue to argue with Isfir's followers or object to turning an argument into a criminal case. The first known imperial response is provincial. The date and content of Othran II's personal response remain open.

As houses come under scrutiny, members of travelling companies give teachers places in caravans and recommend them to kin at the next landing. Some go because they have work or family elsewhere. Others leave after a summons or the loss of their meeting place. Letters travel with the same people who carry goods, news of deaths and requests for money. Dorn helps maintain the roads and shelters that the early mission uses. Calira accompanies Amaryah and Solana to Veridun Minor, where the hearth that receives Solana's coal also feeds travellers. By about 15 AR there is an assembly in Virelos for Aquilan to pursue. The network is already growing when he joins it.

## About 15 AR — Aquilan enters through the door he meant to break

Sha'il bar Qoreth carries warrants to Virelos. After the encounter on the road, the people he came to arrest shelter him while he is blind. Someone who knows what his warrants can do takes responsibility for bringing him into the house. The congregation baptizes him and feeds him. Families harmed by his earlier work can still refuse him access to names and safe houses. For a time, a believer who knows his former work accompanies him when he approaches another congregation. They vouch for what they have seen him do since Virelos.

Aquilan has enough standing in both Jirahar and imperial life to write to communities divided over his teaching. He argues from the Scrolls that the Path is fulfilled in the Redeemer. His travels and letters carry disputes between eastern towns, Atherian cities, Sestaran ports and Folk borderlands. His mission is distinct from the Companion roll. The empty seat stays empty. ## Who can share the table

At Halmenth, baptisms associated with Naris bring in working households who keep different kitchens. In Sermora, Jirahar seafarers and local households meet under the same roof. An early dispute begins when a Jirahar host will feed a newly baptized neighbour but hesitates to eat at that neighbour's table. The host can make the provisions at home meet the stricter household rules. A meal prepared by the neighbour raises questions about slaughter, food offered to other powers and the possibility of sharing one cup. Some people argue that the new households must first enter the Jirahar covenant. Others have already shared worship, care for the sick and work with them for years.

Before the main Purge, messengers from several assemblies bring those cases to a meeting in Qiryath while some of the Companions can still attend. The agreement admits believers through baptism without requiring Jirahar communal conversion. It expects mixed assemblies to prepare a common table people can attend in conscience, to refuse food knowingly offered in worship of the Stars or other powers, and to continue relief across household and language lines. Jirahar believers continue their own household obligations. Host communities work out how to honour them when they eat together. Some patrons keep seating their own households apart even after the agreement is read aloud. Aquilan's letters address disputes that continue after the ruling.

## 29–63 AR — The generations overlap

Calira dies defending Amaryah's refuge in 29 AR. The larger wave of persecution in about 36–45 AR reaches communities that already differ in size, wealth and local standing. At Caleran, Jareth dies in the Celestial Forum in 41 AR. Tyros and Aquilan die there about 45 AR. At Halmenth, soldiers drown Naris while he is baptizing. Congregations lose teachers and couriers at different times. Some close a public gathering and continue meeting by household. Some patrons buy safety by giving up servants. Others keep prisoners' children and lose their property for it.

The first church has to choose how to hear the testimony of people who recanted under threat, how to support their families and whether they can safely entrust them with names again. The loss of witnesses gives authority to the people who can still teach what they received and can produce letters other houses recognize. Elders oversee particular assemblies, and stewards continue looking after meals, burials and aid. The homes which first hosted a meal can now have obligations extending through several generations. Amaryah dies around 55 AR. Solana survives until 63 AR. Their household's practice of tending the lamp continues in Veridun Minor as the people who remembered Qiryath personally grow fewer.

## 66–73 AR — The city falls and its people carry the Church

When revolt begins in 66 AR, believers in Qiryath face the same competing duties as their neighbours. Some leave with families on routes already used by the assemblies. Some stay to care for dependants or to keep burial work going. Relief from other towns reaches people through kin and commercial contacts while the roads are still open. The siege breaks those contacts. The sanctuary falls in 70 AR and the last fortress in 73. Atheria replaces Qiryath's charter government with direct rule. Many displaced Jirahar find refuge beyond Atherian jurisdiction. Among the city's believers, some go with those households and others reach assemblies inside the empire.

The survivors carry teaching, burial names, letters and the memory of the places where Isfir spoke and was buried. They can find congregations in cities which already sent and received teachers and relief. No successor city receives every surviving witness or record. In Caleran, the memory of the martyrs eventually gives the former forum a church. At Veridun Minor, local households keep Amaryah's lamp through the long period of uneven persecution. At Qiryath, the terrain and loss of the families who knew it eventually defeat claims to identify the exact tomb.
