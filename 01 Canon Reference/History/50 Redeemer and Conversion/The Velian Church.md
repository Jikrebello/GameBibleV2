# The Velian Church

The Velian Church is the third ancient communion of the Redeemer's faith. Its Man-Bearer doctrine was condemned at the Council of Halmenth in 411 AR but never extinguished.

## Doctrine

Following Bishop Nasriel of Qabir, Velians teach that Amaryah bore the perfect human vessel and that the uncreated Fire descended upon and crowned the Redeemer at the opening of his ministry: *the lamp was made in her; the Fire came after.* Eclessian and Thalmyric theologians hold that this divides the Redeemer's person. Velians answer that it preserves the Fire's transcendence and proves no institution can possess it.

## History

Velians trace their communion to the baptised families of Jirahar descent in Qabir and the gateway ports who, under Vestian's compulsory Profession of 385, said aloud that the state's reception lamps had burned yellow. Nasriel, then a young reader at Qabir, kept his city's count of the unkindled lamps and carried copies to the hearing house at Raveth; he was away when a crowd burned it about 389. His congregations first said of the state's lamp, *the lamp is made by hands; the Fire comes after*, and within a generation turned the saying on the Redeemer himself. Qabir elected Nasriel its bishop about 402 over Caleran's objection to his mother's family. Velians say Halmenth condemned him in 411 for his descent as much as for his doctrine; Eclessian and Thalmyric historians answer that the doctrine was enough.

After Halmenth, Nasriel's communion survived in desert-margin cities, Qabir chief among them. When the Covenant came north in 598–612, Leovar I's hermit-bishops were re-sealing imperial towns' charters in the Fire, and the Velian cities had no part in it: bishops of Halmenth's communion would not witness charters for congregations Halmenth had condemned, and Velian clergy held that the Fire crowns a vessel and does not seal an empire's deeds. Covenant courts took the Velian cities under Caliph Uthar's disputed seal.

About 610 the cities negotiated sealed protection with the Banu Mizan: tribute, disarmament, and testimony restrictions in exchange for worship, property, clergy, and communal courts. They kept their old charters and locked them in fireproof chests. Velian families say the chests are waiting for a counter-ruling; Covenant jurists know that proof of a forged Fourth Seal would unsettle every judgement made in Uthar's name, the Velian ones among them. Under the Banu Mizan, Velian physicians, translators, and copyists preserved substantial Atherian learning, and Velian songs claim the Captain of the Sacrificial Wind, the ferrywoman whose host fought on the river marches in the 860s, as a daughter of the belt.

In 874 the Second Crusade marched to "receive" the Velian cities on the ground that their compacts had lapsed. They shut their gates and would not open the chests in a war. Before Oronda in 876 the crusaders raised the Velos Standard and nothing came, while the Velian clergy lit a single lamp on the walls; their songs say it took the Fire. In 885 Qahir ibn Ruzbeh's officers ended Banu Mizan rule in the belt.

The Long Reconquest harmed the church as well as its Covenant rulers. Reconquest authorities often treated Velians as collaborator-heretics and remembered 874 as betrayal; Velians remembered an army that called itself their liberation and came for their grain. Its present heartland is Zanthir and the Velian States, with smaller congregations in Iqashar, Al-Qahur, and frontier ports.

## Life and Worship

Velian clergy may marry. Churches are whitewashed and image-free, lit by a single lamp. The Catholicos of Qabir heads a hierarchy recognized by neither Veridun nor the Eclessian Sanctum. The liturgy preserves the Jirahar dialect associated with the Redeemer's earthly life.

## Present Day

Several hundred thousand adherents live principally under Emir Badis and the client-state compacts. Their status as [[The People of the Lamp|People of the Lamp]] gives enforceable protection without equality. Qazmarid is harsher; Thalmyric and Eclessian missions compete for congregations; political collapse in Zanthir could destroy the church's institutional heartland.
