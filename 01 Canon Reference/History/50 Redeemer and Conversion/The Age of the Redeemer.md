# The Age of the Redeemer

The Age of the Redeemer encompasses the life of Isfir, his public ministry from approximately 3 BR to 0 AR, his execution at Second Qiryath by Atherian authority and his bodily Resurrection on the third day. His followers held that these events transformed the human soul-road, and later churches, empires and persecutions developed around that claim. The age follows the first assemblies through the Great Tower Revolt to the fall of Second Qiryath.

## A life inside imperial history

Isfir was born in 33 BR to a Jirahar household in the tower-town and caravan world of the eastern provinces. He was raised in the Scrolls, Peshara argument, craft, pilgrimage and the memory of two exiles. His ministry, c. 3 BR–0 AR, entered an empire already full of roads, armies, rival sacred claims and people whose lives were constrained by debt, descent and public reputation. He proclaimed the Lord's kingdom, healed, cast out corrupting powers, condemned Star worship and challenged both imperial domination and legal authorities who used correct forms without mercy. He claimed the fulfilment of the law and the Jirahar covenant and exposed their misuse. His followers included Jirahar and people of other nations, while his Mesakha and divine claims divided Jirahar courts.

## The Binding and its consequences

At Second Qiryath, Lord-Scribe Ezrahel bar Qoreth and a majority of the participating senior elders brought Isfir to the Atherian governor on a charge of rival kingship. The governor held the power to pass a capital sentence. High Augur Severan urged public punishment, and imperial soldiers bound and nailed Isfir to a timber outside the city. An elder who challenged the testimony claimed his body. He was buried before the Resting Day. [[The Rise of the Redeemer]] gives the sequence of the trial and burial.

On the third morning, Solana and other women found the tomb empty. The risen Isfir appeared to his followers, showed them his wounds and ate with them. After the Ascension, the gathered community received the Holy Flame in Qiryath. Teaching, prayer, shared meals and care for people in need sustained the first congregation. Its people carried the proclamation through the households and roads they already knew.

The first assembly chose servers from settled and travelling households after its relief lists missed widows. As the faith spread to ports and provincial towns, a meeting at Qiryath recognised baptism as sufficient for admission without requiring entry into the Jirahar covenant. The ruling shaped mixed congregations whose members still differed over food and the obligations of a common table. [[The First Church - From Qiryath to the Tower Revolt|The First Church]]

The Silence of Acceptance at Qiryath followed the Resurrection. Sacrifice went on, and the smokeless fire and cool stones that had answered it since the return from exile never came back. Later persecutors turned this silence into a hereditary guilt laid on every Jirahar. The guilt belonged to the men who chose, on the council's bench and in the governor's court, and to none of their grandchildren.

The empire's own augurs recorded a shorter silence. From the hour of the Binding to the third morning, no augur college anywhere recorded an answer from the Stars. Severan, who had read for the governor at Qiryath, wrote that the stars did not answer, and never read again; his college recorded that he "laid down the staff". Augury resumed on the fourth day, and the colleges sealed the three blank days in their books. When the Companion Tyros, once an astrologer-priest himself, made those days a public question, junior augurs followed him with copies of their books, and the High Augurate answered with the **Great Purge** of c. 36–45. Its commission burned Jareth, beheaded Tyros and Aquilan, and made it a crime to publish a wonder the colleges had not entered. For the next two and a half centuries congregations wrote their miracles down as weather and good sense. [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]] · [[The Ember Underground]]

## Households around the remembered lives

The first chains of help were small enough to fail through one frightened decision. A host admitted strangers, a guide waived payment, a soldier warned a family or an informer named a room. The [[G-10 - The Road of the Empty Purse|Empty-Purse Road]] recalls mercy among people who were not all exemplary and did not all remain faithful. A smuggler could aid a persecuted household and steal from another.

Food, illness, childbirth and the care of the dead supplied the daily work behind later Lives. People were tortured and recanted, informed under threat or for reward, died refusing worship, or survived through compromise and later attempted restitution.

## Revolt and Underground

The Great Tower Revolt of 61–72 AR began when the emperor's daily offering lay unburnt on Qiryath's altar for nine mornings, the governor's augur called it fraud, and soldiers sent up the stair to burn a bull in pitch killed the altar's custodians and two hundred others. Militants promised that the lost sign would come back once foreign rule ended. The legions went first to Migdal Shahar on the Dawn Road, whose households escaped through its Way-Gate in 64, and then to the province's smaller tower towns. Second Qiryath fell in 72 AR, its cisterns broken and its sanctuary burned from its own altar, after its Keeper had sent the sanctuary's vessels away through the city's gate. Atheria ended Qiryath's charter government, put the city under direct provincial rule and charged every Jirahar household for the missing vessels. Many displaced Jirahar found refuge beyond imperial jurisdiction. Some of Qiryath's believers went with them, and others found homes among congregations already established within the empire.

During the Underground Centuries, 1–291 AR, local officials, emperors and mobs acted differently. Some congregations flourished long enough to acquire property before losing it. Others survived through service households and burial fraternities. Magistrates who had watched lamp cults and grave cults feed on soldiers' grief saw the same signs in congregations that kept lamps at executed men's graves, and Tervan Marcellan's edict of 245 made every household relight its grave-lamps green from the Stars' fire after a cult called the Kept Lamps had fed mutinies in the eastern garrisons. Their local histories survive in [[Saints and Sinners of the Chronicles]].

In the generations after Qiryath, municipal recovery, wars with Vashar, southern border treaties and the rising at Sarqen reshaped the empire around these congregations. [[Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian - Emperors Wars and Revolts|Atheria between Qiryath and Cassian]]

## What the age leaves

The era's Lives were copied and contested long afterwards. A Saint's earthly repentance could be celebrated by a church while people harmed by that life refused to join, and a copied passion might preserve one authentic act beside several invented tortures. The transition to [[The Conversion of the Empire]] changed who possessed the instruments of coercion.
