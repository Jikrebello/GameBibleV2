# The Reclamation of Caleran

The Caleran campaign of 950-962 AR recovered the old Atherian capital and opened the central lowlands. It was the sacred midpoint of the Long Reconquest. The city was taken by siege, by revolt within its occupied districts and, in the end, by the voice of one man: its Covenant amir, Durayd ibn Sahl, who held Caleran's oaths in his own name and surrendered them.

## The city the army came for

By 950 Caleran had been occupied for forty-five years. Its garrison families had been born there, its estates along the Aurin were worked by contract-bound survivors, and its port fed a regime that owed Iqashar little more than a name in the Friday prayers. When Sultan-Caliph Idris died in 931, every oath sworn to him had lapsed, and Durayd had sworn the southern cities and garrisons to himself as Amir of the Southern Threshold. Iqashar refused him engines unless he gave those oaths back to its throne. He would not, and so he held the old capital with garrison troops, walls and whatever he could buy.

The city's fear gathered round its funerals. Bound people's names sat in the occupation's name vaults, and the custodians seldom made the release entry Covenant law required at a death, because a released debt-name was a debt written off. Caleran's parish clergy taught that a held name might hold a soul. In 948 Durayd offered to sell the Eternal Ember back to Veridun for the price of its account; Caedros I's court refused to acknowledge that he had anything to sell.

## The Descent

Highland armies moved along repaired Atherian roads while coastal and local resistance disrupted occupation supply. Weaponized Holy Flame first appeared reliably in this campaign, carried through hymns, sanctified lamps, and bearers, with results no one could standardize.

The timing was the Thalmyric army's great fortune. In 951 Grass-Tarkhan riders broke through the Covenant's northern wells in the raid season Tarkhan memory calls the Long Reach, and Iqashar's field army spent the whole campaign on the grass, a season's march from Caleran. Thalmyric chroniclers later claimed Caedros had arranged it. Tarkhan genealogists deny that any envoy drank at their fires.

## The speaking engine, 956

Iqashar sent Durayd one thing. In 955 its last Slave-Soul engine came south by sea, crewed by men sworn to the Covenant throne and not to the amir. It reached Caleran's outer works in 956 and met the war-lamps. The lamps opened its name-cage. People of the occupied parishes, watching from the walls, said the frame cried out once in a Caleran voice, then knelt where it stood and did not rise. Thalmyric clergy said a prisoner had gone on to judgement, and Caleran families believed he was one of the Second Crusade's captives whom the old court had never ransomed, condemned to the foundry before the Fall; the Thalmyric name "martyr irons" comes from that belief. The Covenant crew said only that the cage had failed. The parishes that saw it remembered that the Flame had come for one of their own.

Why the Fire answered a refugee army when it had failed the old palace was argued at once. Thalmyric preachers said it had left the palace for the refuge with the hermits and hospital keepers who never stopped kindling; Covenant jurists, who had kept a file of the old court's counterfeit kindlings for two centuries, had believed it gone altogether.

The outer works themselves cost the besiegers dearly. Among the commanders who threw companies against them was the Warden of the Last Redoubt, who had once sealed a gate on fleeing families to save a royal banner, and whose men now died in assaults that seemed meant to prove he was no coward. His later forts, measured from their smallest doors, were his penance. [[G-35 - The Child's Cord at the Redoubt|The Child's Cord at the Redoubt]] tells how.

## Caleran, 957 AR

The city fell after a combined siege, negotiated surrenders, and revolt within occupied districts. The revolt began at a funeral. In the spring of 957 a custodian refused the release entry for a bound dockworker, and the mourners carried the coffin into the custodian's court. The quarter rose behind them. With no army coming from the north, Durayd opened negotiations.

The southern cities had sworn their oaths to Durayd, so he could release them, and he surrendered Caleran in his own name in the glazed courtyards of the occupation quarter. Horn players on the walls sounded the three broken notes, and his terms named the protected before anything else: the lives of the garrison families, the safety of the surrendered quarters, and the discharge of the Ember's account on payment. Iqashari chroniclers say he surrendered what he had stolen. The occupation-born families who later went north said Iqashar had abandoned him. One tradition puts him dead in the forum the same day as Caedros; another has him growing old in Qabir.

Thalmyric troops found the Eternal Ember still burning under Covenant guard, accompanied by fifty-two years of fuel and custody records.

Veridun paid the invoice in full. The payment acknowledged neither ownership nor gratitude; it acknowledged that the occupation had preserved what it could not extinguish. Covenant jurists add that it released the keepers' pledged names, and they cite it as proof that even the People of the Lamp honour a deposit.

Caedros I entered the martyrs' precinct in the former imperial forum and died at the Pillar of Concordance. Witnesses reported that the White Flame descended. No body remained. Thalmyria glorified him within a decade. Eclessian relic courts reject the evidence for his canonization.

## Completion, 957-962 AR

Caedros II secured the city, coast road, and immediate river districts. He rebuilt walls, protected surrendered quarters, and made Caleran the Patriarchal and pilgrimage center. The returners cut the words of Sarion the Pale, the march monk stoned in the Forum during the last emperor's Jubilee, onto a memorial wall: *"The inked ones are coming. They walk with chains and offers of peace. The crown will burn in silence."* The imperial court remained at Veridun because the return war was not over and the refuge institutions could not simply be uprooted.

Durayd's surrender released Caleran and nothing else. The amirs of the other occupied districts had sworn their garrisons through him; now each swore them again to himself, and by 962 the heartland beyond the coast was a dozen separate amirates. The next ninety years of the Reconquest were fought against them one at a time.
