# The War of the Burning Banners

The War of the Burning Banners was the series of campaigns and negotiated settlements through which the refuge armies of the Thalmyric highlands accepted Aetherion command between 922 and 949 AR. Their leaders controlled separate valleys, monasteries, roads and military households, and differed over whether reconquest or local independence should guide them. Caedros combined force, marriage, hostages and promises of restored land to create the army that later descended upon Caleran.

## The Banner Leagues

Every refuge valley had its own commander, its own saint, its own grievance and its own copy of Atherian law. Some banners wanted immediate return; others preferred defensible independence. Monasteries controlled grain and legitimacy; noble houses controlled cavalry; city leagues controlled workshops and roads.

## War and Settlement

Caedros combined negotiated precedence, marriage, hostages, limited campaigns, and the promise of return land. Defeated banners were more often incorporated than destroyed because the empire needed their soldiers and local knowledge.

The settlement established common musters, road taxes, campaign courts, and a return registry. Provincial autonomy survived beneath imperial command, creating the structure Thalmyria still uses.

## The Enemy across the Valleys

The banners fought one another under the eyes of a weakened enemy. In 931 Sultan-Caliph Idris died, two years after his heir had refused to take up his instruments, and the oaths that bound the occupation's army to the two of them lapsed. Durayd ibn Sahl, amir of occupied Caleran, swore the southern garrisons to himself and held the heartland without help from Iqashar. His towns and the banner valleys traded raids, grain and hostages for two decades. In 948 he offered to sell the Eternal Ember back to Veridun for the price of its custody account, and Caedros refused to acknowledge that the Covenant had anything to sell.

The hostage exchanges bred their own horrors. At one of them, chroniclers say, rescuers who had arranged the ambush they saved people from gave a returning woman a false family and killed one of her true companions as an impostor. She knew her old nurse by a work song and fled with her. Banner families afterwards tested returned captives with songs only their own households knew.

## The General of the Glass Court

Some banners reconquered highland towns before Caleran was in reach, and the first returns were not gentle. The commander later called the General of the Glass Court, a camp tailor who rose through pageantry and courage, hid damaged veterans from the parades, expelled mixed households and displayed surrendered rivals before executing them. When his oldest friend threatened to expose him, he killed the man with his own hands and laid the body among soldiers abandoned in an unsupported attack. Other commanders copied his towns, and return by expulsion became a banner habit before it became imperial practice. [[The Fall and the Long Reconquest]] tells his life, and [[G-34 - The Faces Beneath the Victors|The Faces Beneath the Victors]] tells how the hidden faces came back through the paint of his victory halls.

## Moral Memory

Imperial histories call the war unification. Local songs remember forced oaths, seized grain, and rival resistance leaders who were erased after accepting peace. Both are sung in the same valleys, sometimes by the same families.

## Outcome

By 949 AR the highland roads and supply depots could carry an army down to Caleran. The empire had become capable of reconquest and permanently dependent on military administration. The descent began in 950, and the next year the Long Reach of the Tarkhan horse pinned Iqashar's army in the far north, leaving the city no hope of relief.
