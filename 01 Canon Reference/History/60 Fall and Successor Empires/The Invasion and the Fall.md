# The Invasion and the Fall

The Fall was the destruction of unified Atherian government, completed when the armies of Idris al-Qari took Caleran in 905 AR. It was a campaign of lawyers and engines, and the sultan in whose name it was fought never came within nine hundred miles of the city. Qahir ibn Ruzbeh's foundries built five great golems of a kind the Covenant's own jurists had forbidden for three centuries, and the breach-court train split the capital district by district before the golems went down into the flooded Aurin and leaned on the dyke that kept the river out of the lower city. Inside the city the last emperor, Corvenas III, held rites that failed in private while the armies that might have saved him stood bound to their marches. When at last he tried a rite in public, it failed in front of both armies, and he surrendered the only things the Covenant's law said were his: himself and his relics.

## The Last Court

Emperor Corvenas III ruled from Caleran's Ash Hall, an imperial palace turned relic gallery after conversion. Later Thalmyric schoolbooks called him personally devout and strategically incapable. Historians who have read the Covenant's files describe a man in a trap: every remedy open to him, from riding to the marches to opening his relics to Aurelium's examination, needed a public test of his holiness, and he chose the rites that could fail behind closed doors. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/70 History and Conflicts/Prelude to Collapse.md|Prelude to Collapse]] tells that story. Provincial reports reached him through court factions that feared an honest muster would show the empty depots.

## Qahir's machine

Qahir ibn Ruzbeh rose on the prisoners of the Reception. He commanded the northern horse that saved the Velian belt in 874–878, and when neither the Banu Mizan regency nor the caliph's treasury could pay his riders he took the crusade's nine thousand unransomed prisoners instead. He entered them under his own name in a register at Iqashar, where his family kept the weigh-house on the caravan road, set them to cut cisterns and furnace pits, hired them to the Basin's canal councils and paid his army from the hire. By 881 the caliph at Al-Qahur had no soldiers who were not Qahir's, and gave him the old Seal of the Roads with the new title of Sultan. In 884 the college gave him the ruling that the crusade had broken the peace of 612, and in 885 his officers took the Velian client courts.

Between roughly 885 and 902 AR he rebuilt the Covenant war machine around mass levies, permanent Shell extraction, siege-golem foundries, contract logistics and disciplined frontier intelligence. It rested on a ruling he had won in 885–886: the old clause binding the 4,444 to "take nothing that is not given" bound parties, not powers, so beasts from Hell, the forfeited names of the condemned and nameless constructs could all go to war. His capture companies worked the routes into mapped Hell. His foundries hammered name-cages and fed them from the breach-courts' death sentences. Coerced Ahdanite levies became engineers and foundry crews, and supply contracts were sealed in the Vault of Names.

The engines most feared in 905 were the smallest. Lesser Surrender engines and their siege tables travelled with the breach-court train, read each district's title and offered each district its own terms. The first great Surrender Engine, a mercy tower, was finished at Iqashar only around 944. His successor Idris al-Qari inherited five great engines: three Depth-Core frames driven by captured Hell fauna and two Slave-Soul frames holding executed human prisoners. Thalmyric soldiers called the Slave-Soul frames *martyr irons* and believed the first cages held prisoners of the Reception whom Caleran had never ransomed, condemned under Qahir's ruling of 884. The Covenant's registers list them only as condemned.

## The Campaign, 902–905 AR

Qahir died in 902. His son Idris was thirty-one, with daughters and no son. Every instrument that bound the army named Qahir as its holder and Idris as the man who would hold it after him, and nobody after Idris; re-swearing it to a new successor meant musters, witnesses and fees across a season's march of country, and there was no successor to name. If Idris fell, every oath in the army fell with him, so his jurists would not let him go to war. He spent the campaign at Iqashar reading couriers' reports. The city was taken in his name by his father's men: Qahir's engine-master **Barzin**, a Ruzhan horse-lord's son who had built the foundries and was now in his sixties, and the chief jurist of the breach-court train. Covenant jurists call Idris *al-Qari*, the Reader, because the city was opened by reading. Thalmyric chroniclers, who never saw his face, call him the Letter-Caliph.

The breach-court train went first, through the northwestern approaches, with its supplies coming by sea. Before each district's gates its jurists read the district's charter and offered terms, and Caleran's own charter was served at its gates quarter by quarter. Covenant jurists held that the charter's last re-sealing, at Corvenas III's accession, had been finished with warlock fire. Guild quarters that held honest instruments of their own were offered separate terms, and some opened their gates.

Caleran's walls remained formidable, but its field armies had been dispersed, unpaid or committed to rival commands. The Ättar marine crews, months unpaid, sailed for Kedrava. A late flood on the Aurin held the southern levies three weeks short of the city. The bravest march commanders came on the emperor's written orders, and Covenant courts sat on their empty marches behind them.

Only when the city had split did the great engines come up, and they came to the river. Caleran's lower quarters lie behind the Aurin dyke, the embankment that carries the river wall. When the dyke was raised in the Age of Glory, its wardens built one length of it to break in their test under the Red Star, the Star's share, and rebuilt that length afterwards. The augur colleges that recorded where it lay were dissolved in 383, and their families sold the record with the rest of their archives. Thalmyric tradition says the Red Star, which had once shown Astrion the flaw in a loaded standard at Caleran, showed Barzin's engineers its own share of the dyke at the price of a lesser engine broken under the test load, and that on the night before the assault the watch saw a red light lying on the water along the dyke's foot. Covenant engineers say they found the length in a dead college's archive bought at Sermora. The river was still high with the late flood. The three Depth-Core frames, whose beasts go to water as a dog goes to a door, walked down the bank into the current and stood against the dyke from the river side, where no army could have stood.

## The last day

When the engines went into the river below the marked length, Corvenas III left the Ash Hall for the first time in the siege. He carried the Velos Standard onto the dyke above them and called on the Fire before both armies: the public test he had refused for twenty-one years. Nothing came. The Covenant's jurists entered the silence in their record, and through the afternoon the quarters that had held out took terms one after another. The engines broke the dyke at dusk, and the Aurin came into the lower city. The tanners' and boatmen's quarters held no charter worth voiding, and no court had troubled to serve them; they were under water to the lintels by midnight. Every quarter without terms by dark was stormed. Covenant levies came in through the gap on the falling water and killed, raped and took captives through the streets behind the river wall until morning.

The breach-court would not accept the city from its emperor. In Covenant law his charter had been void since 884, and he had nothing to surrender but his person and his household. Corvenas signed what it would take. The **Instrument of the Ash Hall** surrendered the emperor and the Hall's relics to Idris and listed the relics one by one, the Velos Standard among them; the only person it named was the emperor. He lived on in the Ash Hall under Covenant guard as the keeper of his own surrendered relics, and died there of a winter fever in 908. The Covenant inventory of that year records his body among the relics with the lamps around him unlit.

The refuge never forgave the instrument. The church at Veridun struck his name from its prayers for the dead, and the refuge councils governed in the name of a living emperor in Covenant custody until his death set them free to raise a new standard. Eclessian preachers held him up as the natural end of an unexamined court. Sestaran humanists pitied a man who had spent his life trying to make the Fire come to things.

## The Ember and the basilica

Soldiers who tried to put out the Eternal Ember found that it would not go out. Weeks later Idris's ruling came by courier from Iqashar: the lamp was neither holy nor his own, and was to be held as a deposit for the final audit by keepers whose own names stood pledged that it would be neither divided, removed nor put out. A deposit is kept where it stands and open to view, so the basilica of the Celestial Forum stayed open around it. For fifty-two years its clergy sang under a Covenant guard at the door, and it was the one church in occupied Caleran where the liturgy never stopped. Idris's officers also preserved the Pillar of Concordance. The keepers' custody became an occupation claim.

## Collapse and Flight

With the emperor in Covenant custody, the imperial command chain failed, and surviving regions improvised. Refugees moved toward Veridun, Virelos, rural estates, mountain monasteries, eastern ports, Eclessia and the grasslands. Grass-Tarkhan clans caught scattered imperial cavalry stock; their western Well-Tarkhan kin retained an older camel-and-well civilisation. The eastern provinces organised their own defence. During the refugee winter thirty ships came into Sestaran ports over eleven days, their cargoes intact and their meals half-eaten, sailing in formation with no one aboard. The Thirty Sisters have never been explained.

In the winter of 906 a Qahirid column came up the valley against the refuge at Veridun. The refuge council ordered the flour in the store caves burned rather than lose it to the column, and the hearth-keeping households of Veridun Minor refused: every oven in the crowded city baked through the night instead. At dawn the smoke of the ovens did not rise. It lay over the city and the valley, smelling of bread and cedar, for as long as the bread lasted, which the refuge reckoned at five days. The column's engines called their terms into it, and its court could find no gate, no witness and nobody to read a charter to. In the smoke the refugees dreamed of a woman banking an oven and the column's levies dreamed of their own mothers' hearths. On the third morning the levies began to walk home, and by the fifth the column had turned back with nothing served. Thalmyria keeps it as the Night of the Ovens. Its preachers say the Fire which would not come to Caleran's palace came to Veridun's ovens; Eclessian preachers answer that a starving city which has baked its last flour will dream anything. ## The Borrowed Temple

In one northern province an Atherian star sanctuary, closed and stripped centuries earlier, had become a prison and a cistern refuge. When imperial authority collapsed around 905, soldiers, prisoners, servants and refugees crowded into it. Its commander broke open the sealed chambers looking for weapons and found an offer instead. The refugees' accounts call the power behind it the Exile, a dispossessed queen in some tellings and a many-crowned dragon in others, who promised that the province would become the empire's true successor and offered dragon-shaped war-broods to make it so. In return it asked for the old altar, public acclamation and the bodies of the condemned.

The commander agreed. Prisoners were executed and their bodies built into the unfinished war-broods, and refugees who would not cry the acclamation were denied water. Then prisoners began vanishing from locked cells under false evacuation orders. Refugee and garrison accounts, which accuse each other of nearly everything else, agree on what followed. A figure they called the Saint of the Last Door stood at the lowest cell door with bright attendants, called the remaining captives by name and held the way while the living broke open the cells and carried out the wounded. Prisoners and servants flooded the altar chamber, smashed the apparatus of acclamation and dragged the unfinished war-broods into the cistern. Many were killed. The province's claim to succeed Atheria died there. Descendants of the rescuers keep a door duty: the old door is said to answer only when a threatened captive is named aloud and someone promises to carry that person out. [[The Hunger Wars]] and [[Saints and Sinners of the Chronicles]] tell the Exile and the saint.

## The children of the old house

In 907, as Caleran's fall emptied the frontier, a claimant from Hell came to an escape house to collect the surviving children of an old frontier client house, invoking a clause of blood and office in a compact the house had held for generations. Witnesses said the house's old heir, the Heir with Five Remembered Wings, dead for eighty years, stood in the doorway with five scars burning white like folded wings, testified that neither blood nor mutilation passes a human soul to anyone, and guarded the children while the house destroyed the compact. The claim failed and the hereditary office died with it. Custody courts still keep the sentence *No wound inherits the child.* [[G-46 - The Child Who Owed No Wound|The Child Who Owed No Wound]]

## The Northern Advance Breaks, 906-908 AR

After Caleran, a Qahirid army attempted to force the Caldrian road into Eclessia. The eastern provinces' charters had been re-sealed before witnesses Aurelium had tested, and no Covenant court could void them, so the army had to fight. Caldrian defenders held Crown Gate Pass while Vortharan warlocks destroyed one Depth-Core and one Slave-Soul engine and crippled a second Depth-Core at [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)]]. Vortharan contractors had studied Covenant script for Caleran's court for generations, and their counter-scripts drew on that study. The other two engines escaped with the retreat. The defeat ended the northern advance and dispersed forces the later mountain resistance could isolate.

## Occupation

Covenant government secured ports, aqueducts, roads, archives, and productive estates. Idris bound the army and the surrendered southern cities to himself by name, and wrote in a successor-holder only after his first son, Ruzbeh, was born in 915. The Atherian remount studs his clerks listed for seizure were driven north by their handlers, and the occupation remained an army of camels and foot. Repopulation combined garrison families, clients, contract-bound locals, imported labour and transplanted subjects. Bound survivors' names sat in local name vaults, whose custodians seldom made the release entry the law required at death. These communities made the occupation last, and made every later return a quarrel over somebody's house.
