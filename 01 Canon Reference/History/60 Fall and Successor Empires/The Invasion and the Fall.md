# The Invasion and the Fall

The Fall was the destruction of unified Atherian government, completed when Idris al-Fattah took Caleran in 905 AR. It was a campaign of lawyers and engines. Qahir ibn Ruzbeh's foundries built five great golems of a kind the Covenant's own jurists had forbidden for three centuries, and Idris's breach-court train split the capital district by district before the golems ever reached its walls. Inside the city the last emperor, Corvenas III, held rites that failed in private while the armies that might have saved him stood bound to their marches.

## The Last Court

Emperor Corvenas III ruled from Caleran's Ash Hall, an imperial palace turned relic gallery after conversion. Later Thalmyric schoolbooks called him personally devout and strategically incapable. Historians who have read the Covenant's files describe a man in a trap: every remedy open to him, from riding to the marches to opening his relics to Aurelium's examination, needed a public test of his holiness, and he chose the rites that could fail behind closed doors. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/70 History and Conflicts/Prelude to Collapse.md|Prelude to Collapse]] tells that story. Provincial reports were filtered through court factions that feared mobilisation would expose fiscal weakness.

## Qahir's machine

Qahir ibn Ruzbeh rebuilt the Covenant war machine between roughly 885 and 902 AR around mass levies, permanent Shell extraction, siege-golem foundries, contract logistics and disciplined frontier intelligence. It rested on a ruling he had won in 885–886: the old clause binding the 4,444 to "take nothing that is not given" bound parties, not powers, so beasts from Hell, the forfeited names of the condemned and nameless constructs could all go to war. His capture companies worked the routes into mapped Hell. His foundries hammered name-cages and fed them from a docket of breach-court death sentences. Coerced Ahdanite levies became engineers and foundry crews, and supply contracts were sealed in the Vault of Names.

The engines most feared in 905 were the smallest. Lesser Surrender engines and their siege tables travelled with the breach-court train, read each district's title and offered each district its own terms. The first great Surrender Engine, a mercy tower, was finished at Iqashar only around 944. His successor Idris al-Fattah inherited five great engines: three Depth-Core frames driven by captured Hell fauna and two Slave-Soul frames holding executed human prisoners. Thalmyric soldiers called the Slave-Soul frames *martyr irons* and believed the first cages held Second Crusade prisoners whom Caleran had never ransomed, condemned under Qahir's ruling of 884. The Covenant's registers list them only as condemned.

## The Campaign, 902–905 AR

Qahir died in 902. Idris, twenty-three years old, led the campaign through the northwestern approaches and the seaward supply line. The breach-court train went first. Before each district's gates its jurists read the district's charter and offered terms, and Caleran's own charter was served on its walls quarter by quarter. Covenant jurists held that the charter's last re-sealing, at Corvenas III's accession, had been finished with warlock fire. Guild quarters that held honest instruments of their own were offered separate terms, and some opened their gates.

Caleran's walls remained formidable, but its field armies had been dispersed, unpaid or committed to rival commands. The Ättar marine crews, months unpaid, sailed for Kedrava. A late flood on the Aurin held the southern levies three weeks short of the city. The bravest march commanders came on the emperor's written orders, and Covenant courts sat on their empty marches behind them.

Only when the city had split did the great engines come up, to two points on the land walls marked in the Covenant's siege tables. Thalmyric tradition says the Red Star, which had once shown Astrion the flaw in a loaded standard at Caleran, showed Qahir's engineers those two points at the price of a lesser engine broken under the test load. Covenant engineers say it was survey. The capital fell in 905 AR.

How Corvenas III died is told three ways. The soldiers' songs kill him on the land wall in the breach, holding a borrowed spear. A Covenant inventory of the Ash Hall records a body among the relics with the lamps around it unlit. A refugee tradition of the southern road says he walked out among the crowds unrecognised and died on the road among the people he had failed to call.

## Idris in Caleran

Soldiers who tried to put out the Eternal Ember found that it would not go out. Idris sealed the martyrs' precinct in the Celestial Forum as evidence and declared the Ember impounded property under guard. His officers preserved the Pillar of Concordance and assigned keepers to the lamp. Their custody became an occupation claim.

## Collapse and Flight

The emperor died, the imperial command chain failed, and surviving regions improvised. Refugees moved toward Veridun, Virelos, rural estates, mountain monasteries, eastern ports, Eclessia and the grasslands. Grass-Tarkhan clans caught scattered imperial cavalry stock; their western Well-Tarkhan kin retained an older camel-and-well civilisation. The eastern provinces organised their own defence. During the refugee winter thirty ships came into Sestaran ports over eleven days, their cargoes intact and their meals half-eaten, sailing in formation with no one aboard. The Thirty Sisters have never been explained.

In 906 watchers on the walls of the refuge at Veridun saw a woman's figure spreading pale fire above the city before a night storm broke the Qahirid siege. Thalmyric preachers have said ever since that the Fire which would not come to Caleran's palace came to Veridun's refugees. Eclessian preachers answer that it came to Veridun because Caleran had long since stopped examining what it claimed. [[The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun]]

## The Borrowed Temple

In one northern province an Atherian star sanctuary, closed and stripped centuries earlier, had become a prison and a cistern refuge. When imperial authority collapsed around 905, soldiers, prisoners, servants and refugees crowded into it. Its commander broke open the sealed chambers looking for weapons and found an offer instead. The refugees' accounts call the power behind it the Exile, a dispossessed queen in some tellings and a many-crowned dragon in others, who promised that the province would become the empire's true successor and offered dragon-shaped war-broods to make it so. In return it asked for the old altar, public acclamation and the bodies of the condemned.

The commander agreed. Prisoners were executed and their bodies built into the unfinished war-broods, and refugees who would not cry the acclamation were denied water. Then prisoners began vanishing from locked cells under false evacuation orders. Refugee and garrison accounts, which accuse each other of nearly everything else, agree on what followed. A figure they called the Saint of the Last Door stood at the lowest cell door with bright attendants, called the remaining captives by name and held the way while the living broke open the cells and carried out the wounded. Prisoners and servants flooded the altar chamber, smashed the apparatus of acclamation and dragged the unfinished war-broods into the cistern. Many were killed. The province's claim to succeed Atheria died there. Descendants of the rescuers keep a door duty: the old door is said to answer only when a threatened captive is named aloud and someone promises to carry that person out. [[The Hunger Wars]] and [[Saints and Sinners of the Chronicles]] tell the Exile and the saint.

## The children of the old house

In 907, as Caleran's fall emptied the frontier, a claimant from Hell came to an escape house to collect the surviving children of an old frontier client house, invoking a clause of blood and office in a compact the house had held for generations. Witnesses said the house's long-dead heir, the Saint of Five Remembered Wings, stood in the doorway with five scars burning white like folded wings, testified that neither blood nor mutilation passes a human soul to anyone, and guarded the children while the house destroyed the compact. The claim failed and the hereditary office died with it. Custody courts still keep the sentence *No wound inherits the child.* [[G-46 - The Child Who Owed No Wound|The Child Who Owed No Wound]]

## The Northern Advance Breaks, 906-908 AR

After Caleran, a Qahirid army attempted to force the Caldrian road into Eclessia. The eastern provinces' charters had been re-sealed before witnesses Aurelium had tested, and no Covenant court could void them, so the army had to fight. Caldrian defenders held Crown Gate Pass while Vortharan warlocks destroyed one Depth-Core and one Slave-Soul engine and crippled a second Depth-Core at [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)]]. Vortharan contractors had studied Covenant script for Caleran's court for generations, and their counter-scripts drew on that study. The other two engines escaped with the retreat. The defeat ended the northern advance and dispersed forces the later mountain resistance could isolate.

## Occupation

Covenant government secured ports, aqueducts, roads, archives, and productive estates. Idris bound the army and the surrendered southern cities to himself by name, with his heir written in as successor-holder. The Atherian remount studs his clerks listed for seizure were driven north by their handlers, and the occupation remained an army of camels and foot. Repopulation combined garrison families, clients, contract-bound locals, imported labour and transplanted subjects. Bound survivors' names sat in local name vaults, whose custodians seldom made the release entry the law required at death. These communities made occupation durable and later return legally explosive.
