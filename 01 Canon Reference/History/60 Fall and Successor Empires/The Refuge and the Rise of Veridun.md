# The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun

Veridun became a capital because it first became a refuge. Its volcanic highlands, defensible roads, monasteries, store caves, and surviving Ember-Succession clergy allowed it to receive people and institutions that Caleran could no longer protect.

## The Refuge City

After 905, Veridun absorbed imperial clerks, soldiers, relic custodians, guild families, provincial delegations, and rural refugees. Existing highland communities bore the cost. Caves became granaries, monasteries became hospitals, and private houses became chancelleries.

## The Veil, 906 AR

In the winter of 906 a Qahirid column came up the valley towards Veridun with a breach-court, lesser Surrender engines on their siege tables and several thousand levies. The refuge had no walls fit to stop it. Its council, made up of soldiers and court survivors, ordered the flour in the store caves burned at dawn so that the column would not eat it if the city were served or stormed.

The order went to the hearth-keeping households of Veridun Minor, the families who had kept Amaryah's lamp for nine centuries and had once hidden it in a bread oven. They would not burn bread-flour while children in the caves were hungry. Through the night they carried the flour down to every oven in the crowded city, and the refugees, the monks, the hospital keepers and the women of the town baked it all. Before dawn every household in Veridun had eaten its fill for the first time that winter.

At dawn the smoke of the ovens did not rise. It lay over the city and the valley, white and still, smelling of bread and cedar, the scent the old tradition gives Amaryah's empty tomb, and it stayed while the bread lasted, five days by the refuge's count. The soldiers on the walls could see nothing to watch, so they stood vigil instead. The column's engines called their terms into the smoke, district by district, and its jurists could find no gate to read at and nobody to hear them. In the smoke the refugees dreamed of a woman banking an oven for the night, and the column's levies, by their own later accounts, dreamed of their mothers' hearths in the Basin and the Velian belt. On the third morning the levies began to walk home in twos and threes, and their commander, with his levies leaving and his engines talking to fog, turned the column back with not one charter read.

Thalmyria remembers this as the **Night of the Ovens**. On its feast every household bakes through the night and leaves the oven door open at dawn. The event saved the refuge without settling who was sent: refuge preachers say Amaryah answered her own hearth; others say a saint of the refuge, or a sign granted for the city's need. The Covenant's campaign registers record five days of fog, levies who went home unpaid in the snow and a column that turned back. Eclessian polemicists call it the dream of a starving city that had baked its last flour. Why the Mother answered the refuge when the capital's rites had gone cold is still argued: refuge preachers said the Fire had always lived with the hermits, monks and hospital keepers who carried it to Veridun, and that it came to the women who would not burn the bread, while court survivors blamed the capital's sins.

## Caedros and the Mountain League

Caedros Aetherion was a minor-house commander. While Corvenas III lived as a prisoner among his relics in Caleran, the refuge councils governed in his name and no commander could raise a new standard without becoming a rebel against a living emperor. The news of his death reached Veridun in the spring of 908, and that summer Caedros joined refuge councils, surviving soldiers, monasteries, village militias, and dispossessed houses into a mountain league. His legitimacy came from service, from debts he paid, and later from coronation.

The resistance preserved population and roads before attempting reconquest. It evacuated exposed villages, denied passes, recovered archives, and offered amnesty to occupation troops who surrendered without carrying bound contracts into the mountains.

The amnesty was the refuge's sharpest weapon. The occupation's estates were worked by contract-bound survivors, and each family that slipped up the passes without its instrument took a share of the garrison's bread with it.

## The Signal House, c. 910–913 AR

Occupation governors answered with terror. One of them kept prisoners and announced surrenders from a mountain signal house above a captured city, and his warlocks warded its signal apertures. The devotional tradition says a Herald of heaven came there to proclaim a protected surrender, and the ward caught it: it crushed the Herald's pinions, split its trumpet and demanded that it announce that the prisoners had forfeited their names and property. The Herald refused. A rescue party from the refuge broke into the lower room and most of its members died there, but the survivors damaged the ward enough for the Herald to complete the true proclamation and withdraw. The governor executed more witnesses afterwards.

The refuge commanders suppressed the story for a generation, afraid that soldiers who learned heaven could lose a fight would stop fighting. Horn players kept it anyway, in the three broken notes sounded before spoils are divided; [[G-47 - Three Notes before the Spoils|Three Notes before the Spoils]] preserves their version. From the signal house came the rule, later kept by both armies, that a surrender names its protected people before it settles property.

## Virelos, 921 AR

At Caelmount in the winter of 917 the valley's cave-wardens turned their own granary into a grave for a Covenant column, and the store caves that had fed the refuge cities could never be used again. Caedros had promised the valley bread until it had granaries. Four winters of begging and buying grain did not keep that promise, and in 921 he took Virelos, whose granaries had fed an occupation garrison, to keep it. The capture gave the rebellion a defensible assembly and coronation city as well as its granaries. Veridun remained the administrative refuge; Virelos became the place where resistance accepted an emperor.
