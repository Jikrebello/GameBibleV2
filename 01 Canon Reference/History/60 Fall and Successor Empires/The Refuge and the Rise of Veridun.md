# The Refuge and the Rise of Veridun

Veridun became a capital because it first became a refuge. Its volcanic highlands, defensible roads, monasteries, store caves, and surviving Ember-Succession clergy allowed it to receive people and institutions that Caleran could no longer protect.

## The Refuge City

After 905, Veridun absorbed imperial clerks, soldiers, relic custodians, guild families, provincial delegations, and rural refugees. Existing highland communities bore the cost. Caves became granaries, monasteries became hospitals, and private houses became chancelleries.

## The Veil, 906 AR

When a Qahirid force approached Veridun, witnesses reported a woman's figure spreading pale fire above the city before a violent night storm broke the siege. Thalmyria remembers this as the Mother's Veil. The event saved the refuge without settling whether the apparition was Amaryah, another saint, or an image granted for the city's need. The Covenant's campaign registers record a storm, lost baggage and a column that turned back. Why the Mother answered the refuge when the capital's rites had gone cold is still argued: refuge preachers said the Fire had always lived with the hermits, monks and hospital keepers who carried it to Veridun, while court survivors blamed the capital's sins.

## Caedros and the Mountain League

Caedros Aetherion was a minor-house commander. He joined refuge councils, surviving soldiers, monasteries, village militias, and dispossessed houses into a mountain league. His legitimacy came from service, victory, and later coronation.

The resistance preserved population and roads before attempting reconquest. It evacuated exposed villages, denied passes, recovered archives, and offered amnesty to occupation troops who surrendered without carrying bound contracts into the mountains.

The amnesty was the refuge's sharpest weapon. The occupation's estates were worked by contract-bound survivors, and each family that slipped up the passes without its instrument took a share of the garrison's bread with it.

## The Signal House, c. 910–913 AR

Occupation governors answered with terror. One of them kept prisoners and announced surrenders from a mountain signal house above a captured city, and his warlocks warded its signal apertures. The devotional tradition says a Herald of heaven came there to proclaim a protected surrender, and the ward caught it: it crushed the Herald's pinions, split its trumpet and demanded that it announce that the prisoners had forfeited their names and property. The Herald refused. A rescue party from the refuge broke into the lower room and most of its members died there, but the survivors damaged the ward enough for the Herald to complete the true proclamation and withdraw. The governor executed more witnesses afterwards.

The refuge commanders suppressed the story for a generation, afraid that soldiers who learned heaven could lose a fight would stop fighting. Horn players kept it anyway, in the three broken notes sounded before spoils are divided; [[G-47 - Three Notes before the Spoils|Three Notes before the Spoils]] preserves their version. From the signal house came the rule, later kept by both armies, that a surrender names its protected people before it settles property.

## Virelos, 921 AR

The capture of Virelos gave the rebellion a defensible assembly and coronation city. Veridun remained the administrative refuge; Virelos became the place where resistance accepted an emperor.
