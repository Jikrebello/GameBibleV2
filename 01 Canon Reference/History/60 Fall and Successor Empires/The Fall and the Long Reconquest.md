# The Fall and the Long Reconquest

## Atlas narrative

The following account distinguishes established history from the received stories named within it.

## The fall was inhabited

Caleran fell in 905 AR to Idris al-Fattah. The imperial center did not take every household with it. Some fled, some collaborated, some resisted and some continued operating wells, hospitals, mills and grain stores under occupation. In later restoration rhetoric these different acts were often flattened into one accusation.

Ships became contested lifeboats. [[G-13 - The Thirty-First Crew|The Thirty-First Crew]] acquired a vessel amid killing and competition for berths; refugees and sailors were deliberately left behind. No successful escape makes their abandonment an accident. The tale is separate from the unexplained Thirty Sisters.

## Resistance and new government

The Veil over Veridun in 906, the Battle of the Shattered Engines in 907, the Sestaran compact of 908–909 and the Mountain Rebellion of 908–921 belong to overlapping resistance histories. None was secretly caused by a single fellowship. Caedros I's coronation in 922 turned refuge command into imperial government.

The War of the Burning Banners, 922–949, subordinated rival armies and return claimants. The company remembered at the Cinder Inn offers a smaller, morally uneven view of the same transition. Its members became a local ruler, commander, dissenter, holy figure, prosperous survivor and disputed absentee. An abuse of office, a subordinate's arranged death and the murder of surrendered rivals remained individual crimes even when later songs divided all six into heroes and traitors.

## Fifty-two years of occupied Caleran

The Eternal Ember survived occupation. Idris's guards treated it as property in dispute; Veridun later paid the recorded charge. That remarkable fact did not authenticate the conqueror's theology or guarantee every subsequent claim to the city.

The Caleran campaign ran from 950 to 962. Recovery in 957 coincided with Caedros I's death at the Pillar; reports that the Flame took his body became a great Thalmyric claim and an Eclessian controversy. The empire's return did not end with that moment. Caedros II secured the immediate river and coastal districts; the Long Reconquest continued province by province to 1052.

## Return to somebody else's home

Occupation had produced mixed families, resettled populations and surviving local institutions. Returners also possessed real memories of expulsion and loss. A house could have an exiled heir, an occupation-born occupant and a neighbouring family whose work kept its roof intact. Not every claim could be fulfilled.

The Returners' Second Sack concerned one municipal atrocity, not the entire Reconquest: a claimant army executed stewards and mixed families, expelled others into winter, destroyed a water-court register and issued false restoration titles. Some victims had collaborated; others had maintained refuge and public works. The victors erased the distinction. This event is separate from the household story of the Bread Jar and from the otherworld Treasury of the Unreturned.

## Legacies

Mountain forts, abandoned villages, rebuilt harbours and mixed cemeteries make this history visible. The General of the Glass Court transformed refugee shame into a cruel ideal of perfection. The Warden of the Last Redoubt learned too late that an admirable fort could consume the people it claimed to protect. Their later histories belong to separate afterlives, not a hidden explanation of Caedros's war.

The reconquered empire remained incomplete. Zanthir and the Velian States stayed outside imperial rule. Meanwhile eastern crowns made their own claims to Atherian inheritance. [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]] grew within that world while the Reconquest was still unfinished.
