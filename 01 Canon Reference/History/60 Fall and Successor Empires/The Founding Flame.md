# The Founding Flame

The Founding Flame is the ninety-year settlement after Caleran's recovery during which the Thalmyric Empire reclaimed the core heartland and built institutions capable of governing return.

## The Return Registers

Imperial clerks in each retaken town compared Atherian archives, occupation contracts, monastery charters, military grants and the testimony of anyone who could walk in to give it. No register could restore every displaced family. Cities received the largest share of returnees because they offered walls, courts, water, and work without requiring one ancestral claim to defeat another.

## Four Generations of Settlement

The return advanced through fortified roads, irrigation districts, negotiated towns, sieges, amnesties, and military colonies. Occupation-descended communities might retain land, accept imperial service, move under compensation, or face unlawful expulsion depending on local power.

The enemy on the far side of each wall was an amir, and no empire stood behind him. Every occupied district had been sworn to Durayd ibn Sahl by name, and after 957 Durayd sat in a walled house at Veridun, signing whatever releases Caedros II's clerks put before him. An amir who took a release with terms could open his gates lawfully; an amir who refused swore his garrison to himself. Iqashar, fighting Tarkhan riders on its northern wells, sent none of them an army.

Most of those that held out were won with water. The occupation's estates drank from canals whose heads lay in the hills, and the hills had belonged to the mountain leagues since the 910s. Caedros II's engineers took the canal heads one valley at a time and set water-courts at each, measuring every field's share by the hour-cup as the Banu Mizan had once done. A field registered at a Thalmyric water-court got its share; a field still entered in an amir's register got none. Farmers walked up the channels with their old bronze tokens to register, and the amir was left with his walls and a dry valley. The amirates that did not need the hills cost more. One hill citadel with deep cisterns held out for eleven years on camel-meat and rain, and the river citadels on the Aurin had to be besieged. Several amirs opened their gates to the first commander who brought a release in Durayd's hand and would sign a protection for the garrison families. The return registers grew out of the water-courts and these bargains, one district at a time.

## The Breaking and the Impounding

Villages whose bought chaplains' lamps had stayed dark turned on the vessels that had sustained the refuge, and the soldiers sent to stop them joined in: war-lamps smashed in camp, icon-lamps melted down, flame-monks driven out of the valleys where they had buried the dead of the return. The Breaking and the Impounding that followed became civil wars over what kind of civilisation had returned.

The Covenant could exploit neither. During the Breaking its army stood idle at Iqashar from 1006 to 1011 while a lending house held the pledged true name of the Custodian of the Vault of Names. During the Impounding, Tamim the Auditor's treasury declared in 1033 that the southern amirates would be claimed and no longer defended, and the last relief army sent south dispersed at the wrong well in 1041.

The Impounding ended when the crown lamp-houses were broken open in 1049 and found cold, their keepers having sold the oil. In 1052, at the Rekindling, every lamp in the empire was relit from one flame carried on foot from the Ember at Caleran, down the restored roads and into the recovered provinces. The last amirate surrendered to the armies of the emperor's daughter Theodosia that year. Occupation-born families who rejected return law and would not register at the water-courts went north with their deeds and the bronze water-tokens of their fields and became the Covenant's Southern Houses.

## The Incomplete Empire

The settlement did not recover the Velian States or Zanthir. It created a strong heartland, a permanent frontier, and the claim that every unrecovered province remained imperial land. Zanthir's port lived by the sea in summer and by the caravan road in winter. In 1044, when Thalmyric squadrons closed the coast to the grain barges that fed the last amirates, the emir at Qabir withdrew the barges and kept his autonomy by paying Veridun while the sea was open and Iqashar while the caravans ran.
