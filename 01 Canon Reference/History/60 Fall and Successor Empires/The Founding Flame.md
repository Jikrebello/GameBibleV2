# The Founding Flame

The Founding Flame is the ninety-year settlement after Caleran's recovery during which the Thalmyric Empire reclaimed the core heartland and built institutions capable of governing return.

## The Return Registers

Imperial clerks in each retaken town compared Atherian archives, occupation contracts, monastery charters, military grants and the testimony of anyone who could walk in to give it. No register could restore every displaced family. Cities received the largest share of returnees because they offered walls, courts, water, and work without requiring one ancestral claim to defeat another.

## Four Generations of Settlement

The return advanced through fortified roads, irrigation districts, negotiated towns, sieges, amnesties, and military colonies. Occupation-descended communities might retain land, accept imperial service, move under compensation, or face unlawful expulsion depending on local power.

The enemy on the far side of each wall was an amir, not an empire. When Durayd ibn Sahl fled Caleran for Qabir in 957 he left every other occupied district sworn to an absent man; each re-swore its garrison to its own amir, and Iqashar, fighting Tarkhan riders on its northern wells, sent none of them an army.

Most of them were won with water. The occupation's estates drank from canals whose heads lay in the hills, and the hills had belonged to the mountain leagues since the 910s. Caedros II's engineers took the canal heads one valley at a time and set water-courts at each, measuring every field's share by the hour-cup as the Banu Mizan had once done. A field registered at a Thalmyric water-court got its share; a field still entered in an amir's register got none. Farmers walked up the channels with their old bronze tokens to register, and the amir was left with his walls and a dry valley. The amirates that did not need the hills cost more. One hill citadel with deep cisterns held out for eleven years on camel-meat and rain; river citadels on the Aurin had to be besieged; another amir sold his town for safe passage north and a chest of his own deeds; several opened their gates to the first commander who would sign a protection for the garrison families. The return registers grew out of the water-courts and these bargains, one district at a time.

## The Breakings

Lamp-breaking emperors turned their soldiers on the vessels that had sustained the refuge: war-lamps smashed in camp, icon-lamps melted down, flame-monks driven out of the valleys where they had buried the dead of the return. The First and Second Breakings became civil wars over what kind of civilisation had returned.

The Covenant could exploit neither Breaking. During the first, its army stood idle at Iqashar from 1006 to 1011 while a lending house held the pledged true name of the Custodian of the Vault of Names. During the second, Tamim the Auditor's treasury declared in 1033 that the southern amirates would be claimed and no longer defended, and the last relief army sent south dispersed at the wrong well in 1041.

Empress Theodosia ended the Second Breaking in 1052. At her Rekindling every lamp in the empire was relit from one flame carried on foot from the Ember at Caleran, down the restored roads and into the recovered provinces. The last amirate surrendered to her armies that year. Occupation-born families who rejected return law and would not register at the water-courts went north with their deeds and the bronze water-tokens of their fields and became the Covenant's Southern Houses.

## The Incomplete Empire

The settlement did not recover the Velian States or Zanthir. It created a strong heartland, a permanent frontier, and the claim that every unrecovered province remained imperial land. Zanthir's emir had starved the last amirates of his grain and fleet in 1044 and bought his autonomy with tribute paid to Veridun and Iqashar in alternate seasons.
