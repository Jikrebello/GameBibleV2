# The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door — Imperial History

The Low Door Companions were six provincial servants who entered military and administrative service after saving Magistrate Temar Avol during the Lacaris riot around 96 BR. Their advancement continued for more than three decades. In 61 BR two of them held the road stores and armour workshops that let Doran reach Caleran first after Velen I drowned under his own festival banks, and Doran made their private custom of entering by a low door the way his officers took up their commands. Imperial biographies later celebrated their humble origins while suppressing the killings, hostages and family betrayal that accompanied their rise.

## Six servants

Lacaris was an eastern garrison town whose military stores stood beside workshops and a crowded service quarter. Its bread came from the crown estates of Raveth through contracts made for the garrisons, and when estate prices rose, the town paid. A dispute over arrears and food prices became a riot after soldiers tried to clear the market. Fire spread into the magistrate's residence.

Sena Varris, an ostler familiar with the surrounding roads, led five companions through a refuse passage beneath the dining platform. Oren Tal was a singer and household messenger; Brana Keth repaired armour; Thessa Qor interpreted for hostages; Miro Fen worked in the kitchens; Helon Var kept rooms in which important captives were housed. Together they dragged Avol through smoke and brought him to the garrison. They had to stoop through the passage on their hands and knees, and they never forgot it.

Avol rewarded them with paid places in his household. Their knowledge proved useful during the inquiry and the effort to restore supplies. They formed a friendship around work and the memory of having been overlooked together. When they met to eat, they entered the room through a low side door rather than the main one, stooping as they had in the refuse passage.

## Advancement and guilt

Avol recommended the six for provincial service. Sena scouted roads, Oren carried messages and composed marching songs, and Brana organised armour repair. Thessa negotiated with communities in their own languages. Miro learnt military provision, while Helon supervised hostages.

During a mutiny, they detained the children of officers to force a surrender. In a later raid, troops under their authority killed inhabitants after a village had yielded. The companions called the dead rebels in their official account.

Their most consequential decision came when Governor Pelar threatened to destroy both Lacaris's service quarter and an imperial granary after local resistance. Thessa revealed the country refuge where her cousins and other fugitives were hiding. The army killed the people there and spared the larger town. All six accepted the official account that called the refuge an armed camp.

## Separation

Their careers then divided. Sena became a frontier commander. Oren entered a provincial archive and wrote the first sustained history of the six, removing his own role from several decisions. Brana directed military workshops at Caleran. Miro managed road stores and retained the plain bowl of Thessa's youngest murdered cousin. At every meal afterwards he set places for all six and served from the child's bowl.

Helon released three hostage children whose execution had been ordered after their father's revolt. He was convicted of stealing imperial property and executed. Thessa continued as an interpreter but refused the governorship offered later. She never reconciled with the surviving members of her family.

## The march of 61

When Velen I drowned under his untested festival banks in 61 BR, his relatives divided the available commands and argued over the capital. Doran, governor of the central military district, needed food and armour for a fast march. Miro Fen held the road stores between Doran's district and Caleran, and Brana Keth held the capital's armour workshops. Both declared for Doran and supplied his column before any relative's. Sena Varris held a river crossing against a relative's force for three days. Doran reached Caleran first and was recognised as emperor.

Velen's guards had sworn before the emperor's image, and when the banks went they had stood in their ranks facing it while he drowned. Doran wanted officers who would turn their heads, and he adopted the companions' custom for them. A new officer crossed a low threshold into his command, stripped of insignia and carrying a plain bowl, in place of the oath before the image. Officers came to believe that the low door kept the image's pull off a soldier; the Celebrants of the Common Sky called the belief superstition. Command houses across the empire built low entrances for the purpose.

At the companions' last recorded meal, around 54 BR, only four attended. Miro set places for all six and used the child's bowl in the service.

## History and legend

The riot, Avol's rescue, the companions' service, Helon's execution and the declarations of 61 are supported by independent provincial material. Oren's account, workshop marks and surviving household traditions also establish the refuge betrayal.

[[G-09 - The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door|The received tale]] changes names and sometimes counts five or seven companions. It gives the events a compact moral shape. The officers who stooped through Doran's low doors were seldom told whose bowl they carried.
