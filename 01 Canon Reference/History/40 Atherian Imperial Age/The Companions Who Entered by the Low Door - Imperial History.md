# The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door — Imperial History

The Low Door Companions were six provincial servants who entered military and administrative service after saving Magistrate Temar Avol during the Lacaris riot around 96 BR. Their advancement continued for more than three decades. Imperial biographies later celebrated their humble origins while suppressing the killings, hostages and family betrayal that accompanied their rise.

## Six servants

Lacaris was an eastern garrison town whose military stores stood beside workshops and a crowded service quarter. A dispute over arrears and food prices became a riot after soldiers tried to clear the market. Fire spread into the magistrate's residence.

Sena Varris, an ostler familiar with the surrounding roads, led five companions through a refuse passage beneath the dining platform. Oren Tal was a singer and household messenger; Brana Keth repaired armour; Thessa Qor interpreted for hostages; Miro Fen worked in the kitchens; Helon Var kept rooms in which important captives were housed. Together they dragged Avol through smoke and brought him to the garrison.

Avol rewarded them with paid places in his household. Their knowledge proved useful during the inquiry and the effort to restore supplies. They formed a friendship around work and the memory of having been overlooked together.

## Advancement and guilt

Avol recommended the six for provincial service. Sena scouted roads, Oren carried messages and composed marching songs, and Brana organised armour repair. Thessa negotiated with communities in their own languages. Miro learnt military provision, while Helon supervised hostages.

During a mutiny, they detained the children of officers to force a surrender. In a later raid, troops under their authority killed inhabitants after a village had yielded. The companions called the dead rebels in their official account.

Their most consequential decision came when Governor Pelar threatened to destroy both Lacaris's service quarter and an imperial granary after local resistance. Thessa revealed the country refuge where her cousins and other fugitives were hiding. The army killed the people there and spared the larger town. All six accepted the official account that called the refuge an armed camp.

## Separation

Their careers then divided. Sena became a frontier commander. Oren entered a provincial archive and wrote the first sustained history of the six, removing his own role from several decisions. Brana directed military workshops. Miro managed stores and retained the plain bowl of Thessa's youngest murdered cousin.

Helon released three hostage children whose execution had been ordered after their father's revolt. He was convicted of stealing imperial property and executed. Thessa continued as an interpreter but refused the governorship offered later. She never reconciled with the surviving members of her family.

At the companions' last recorded meal, only four attended. Miro set places for all six and used the child's bowl in the service. Later command houses developed a low entrance through which a new officer crossed stripped of insignia while carrying such a bowl.

## History and legend

The riot, Avol's rescue, the companions' service and Helon's execution are supported by independent provincial material. Oren's account, workshop marks and surviving household traditions also establish the refuge betrayal. No single source preserves every participant's motive.

[[G-09 - The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door|The received tale]] changes names and sometimes counts five or seven companions. It gives the events a compact moral shape. The historical companions were skilled, wounded human beings who also became responsible for injuries inflicted on others.
