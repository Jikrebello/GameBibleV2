# The House of Veloran

The House of Veloran ruled Atheria from 246 to 151 BR. Its four emperors came from a provincial military family and governed during the broadest period of internal peace in the Age of Glory. Their roads, eastern campaigns and municipal settlements extended Atherian institutions while increasing the labour and revenue demanded from provincial communities.

## Samor I, 246–229 BR

Samor entered Caleran as victor of the Year of Four Eagles. He paid soldiers, confirmed officers and destroyed the remaining military organisation of his rivals. Daran Thalion's surrendered supporters were screened household by household: some entered Samor's service, while others were executed or dispossessed.

Marriage between Samor's daughter Tessa and Aren Cassar, a surviving descendant of the old imperial house, gave the new dynasty a connection to its predecessor. Tessa already took part in correspondence and supply. The marriage enlarged her relationships without making her authority dependent upon her husband.

Samor reorganised the central river army into commands supplied from different districts. No single commander would control every approach to Caleran. The arrangement made another rapid seizure more difficult and required greater coordination from the palace.

## Tessa I, 229–203 BR

Tessa succeeded her father after years of public work. Her reign is associated with bridges, cisterns, survey schools and agreements setting the obligations of provincial towns. She travelled through the Aurin and eastern cities and expected governors to explain local shortages in the presence of municipal representatives.

The works brought clean water and shortened journeys. They also required stone, timber and compulsory labour. Tessa punished theft by contractors while defending the assessments that supplied them. Provincial songs consequently remember both a ruler who listened and the people who died building what she approved.

In 219 BR, her general Cavar Oren seized the [[The Crown of Olveth|Crown of Olveth]] from a mountain shelter and tried to wear it during a victory procession. The object killed him and burned several attendants. Tessa returned it after army specialists confirmed the limited conditions under which it responded.

## Orent I, 203–174 BR

Orent was Tessa's nephew and adopted son. He had governed eastern districts and maintained friendships among harbour councils. His accession confirmed that adoption and service could carry dynastic authority alongside descent.

His eastern campaigns secured the Arcelon corridor and several mountain approaches. Commanders destroyed settlements that supported resistance and moved captives to road works. The capital's account celebrated a connected route and the end of raiding. [[The Two Roads of Arcelon]] preserves the provincial knowledge and deaths removed from that history.

Orent enjoyed hunting and maintained preserves near provincial residences. Villages lost access to woodland they had used for fuel and grazing. He also employed local trackers and sponsored studies of animals and plants whose copies circulated through imperial schools.

## Mariel I, 174–151 BR

Mariel, Orent's eldest daughter, inherited an experienced administration and fewer active wars. Her reign encouraged municipal building, travel and scholarship. Tax relief after two regional crop failures helped towns preserve seed and repair water systems.

She supported public disputations in which advocates from several philosophical schools addressed the same practical question. Their contests attracted visitors and comic imitation. Provincial teachers gained patronage, though writers who attacked the imperial household could still lose posts or face exile.

Mariel had no surviving child. She adopted no heir before a sudden fever killed her in 151. The principal commanders declined to fight immediately and accepted Elian Serrat after negotiations involving the senate and eastern cities.

## Inheritance

Later histories called the Veloran period the Peace of the Open Roads. The description fits merchants, students and officials able to use those routes during its calmer decades. Frontier households, conscripted labourers and people displaced by campaigns experienced the same roads differently.

The dynasty's provincial origin widened the range of families able to imagine themselves at the centre of imperial life. It also demonstrated that victory, marriage and adoption could establish a house with no descent from Thalion.
