# The House of Veloran

The House of Veloran ruled Atheria from 246 to 151 BR. Its four emperors came from a provincial military family and governed during the broadest period of internal peace in the Age of Glory. Their roads, eastern campaigns and municipal settlements extended Atherian institutions while increasing the labour and revenue demanded from provincial communities.

## Samor I, 246–229 BR

Samor entered Caleran as victor of the Year of Four Eagles. He paid soldiers, confirmed officers and destroyed the remaining military organisation of his rivals. Daran Thalion's surrendered supporters were screened household by household: some entered Samor's service, while others were executed or dispossessed.

Marriage between Samor's daughter Tessa and Aren Cassar, a surviving descendant of the old imperial house, gave the new dynasty a connection to its predecessor. Tessa already managed correspondence and supply for her father's household. Aren served in the river command and took part in the settlements with the towns which supported Samor. Samor adopted him after the marriage and secured the support of the river officers for his accession.

Samor reorganised the central river army into commands supplied from different districts. No single commander would control every approach to Caleran. The arrangement made another rapid seizure more difficult and required greater coordination from the palace.

His first law concerned the accession portrait in the audience hall, which had spoken in the Emperor's voice during the war and which he had covered with a soldier's cloak. The Face, as Caleran called it, was to stay veiled except at accessions and on the founding anniversary, and questioning it became a capital offence. Custody passed to the First Celebrant of the Common Sky, whose college had refused the rites Avaren Mott performed before it, and away from the priests of the Red Star. At every later accession the Face was unveiled last, after the armies and senate had agreed. The Celebrants of the Veil became a lasting rival to the Red Star's priests at court, while the copies of the imperial face in governors' courts and army standards stayed in the Red Star's keeping.

## Aren I, 229–203 BR

Aren succeeded Samor in 229. His government built bridges and cisterns, supported survey schools and negotiated agreements setting the obligations of provincial towns. Tessa brought reports of shortages from municipal households into court correspondence. Aren heard governors and town representatives together before confirming assessments.

The works brought clean water and shortened journeys. They also required stone, timber and compulsory labour. Aren punished theft by contractors while defending the assessments that supplied them. Provincial songs remember both the reopening of routes and the people who died building them.

In 219 BR, the general Cavar Oren seized the [[The Crown of Olveth|Crown of Olveth]] from a mountain shelter and tried to wear it during a victory procession. The object killed him and burned several attendants. Aren returned it after army specialists confirmed the limited conditions under which it responded.

## Orent I, 203–174 BR

Orent was Tessa's nephew. Aren adopted him after he had governed eastern districts and cultivated ties among harbour councils. His accession confirmed that adoption and service could carry dynastic authority alongside descent.

Early in his reign, around 200 BR, came the Sleepwalkers' Summer. For one season the people of three provinces rose from their beds at night and walked, harmlessly and all, the wardens' records insist, in the same direction. Families tied children to bedposts and woke on the road beside them. Orent's surveyors followed the trampled line, and the empire built a road on it, the Via Somnia, which is still in use and still has oddly good footing. The walking stopped everywhere on the same morning. The road ends at an unremarkable hillside that has since been dug four times by three civilisations and found, each time, to contain dirt. The augurs said that a summons had been withdrawn. Later the Church claimed the summer as a prefiguration of its pilgrim roads, and Vorthara keeps a standing prize for anyone who can explain it, which nobody has collected.

His eastern campaigns secured the Arcelon corridor and several mountain approaches. Vashar's Dawn Road let its relay riders and reserves cross the eastern country faster than any Atherian column, and Orent wanted a road of his own. Commanders destroyed settlements that supported resistance and moved captives to road works. The capital's account celebrated a connected route and the end of raiding. [[The Two Roads of Arcelon]] preserves the provincial knowledge and deaths removed from that history, including the embankment that passed its test in the dry season and slid onto the labourers in the rains.

Orent enjoyed hunting and maintained preserves near provincial residences. Villages lost access to woodland they had used for fuel and grazing. He also employed local trackers and sponsored studies of animals and plants whose copies circulated through imperial schools.

## Orent II, 174–151 BR

Orent II, the son of Orent I, inherited an experienced administration and fewer active wars. His reign encouraged municipal building, travel and scholarship. Tax relief after two regional crop failures helped towns preserve seed and repair water systems.

The Arcelon road changed the eastern map in his reign. Raveth's river towns had paid canal duty to the Vasharan road command at Avesh, but their trade now ran west along the new road, and late in Orent's reign their councils negotiated entry into the empire. Atheria took over the river dues, stationed troops at the landings and fixed the province's first assessment in a good year.

His elder sister Mariel supported public disputations in which advocates from several philosophical schools addressed the same practical question. Their contests attracted visitors and comic imitation. Provincial teachers gained patronage, though writers who attacked the imperial household could still lose posts or face exile.

Orent had no surviving child and died in the fever summer of 151 before naming an heir. The principal commanders declined to fight immediately. Samor's divided river commands meant none of them held every approach to Caleran, the fever had filled their camps with sick men, and the veiled Face could not be uncovered for anyone until the others agreed. The senate and eastern cities negotiated recognition of Elian Serrat, whose marriage into the extended Veloran family helped keep its municipal advisers in office. The Face was unveiled last, before a ruler already chosen.

## Inheritance

Later histories called the Veloran period the Peace of the Open Roads. Merchants, students and officials travelled those roads in its calmer decades; frontier households, conscript labourers and families moved off campaign ground had built them, and their songs remember the building. The dynasty's provincial origin let a river-army family's grandchildren sit in the audience hall, and many more provincial families began to imagine their own there.
