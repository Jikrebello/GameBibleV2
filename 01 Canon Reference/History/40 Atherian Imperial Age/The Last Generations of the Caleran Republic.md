# The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic

The last generations of the Caleran republic inherited an extensive network of allies, conquered districts and maritime obligations. Between the destruction of the gateway capital around 520 BR and the murder of Teren Casvilar in 370 BR, disputes over land, citizenship and military service increasingly involved the use of armed force within Atheria itself. Provincial commands supplied the money and followers with which prominent families contested power at Caleran.

The [[The Gateway Wars|Gateway Wars]] had enlarged these resources without distributing their benefits evenly. Some families owned productive land and shares in supply ventures; others returned from service to debt or damaged farms. The royal country across the sea was rebuilding around its spared towns. Both societies continued to change through several generations before the commanders of the imperial founding were born.

## Land, admission and public violence

Around 495 BR, the magistrate Eshren Damar proposed limiting the renewal of large leases on land held by the republic. Smaller cultivating households would receive a greater share, including families whose men had repeatedly served abroad. His supporters included veterans, established citizens with little property and allied farmers whose work sustained land they could not obtain on comparable terms.

The proposal threatened families that had joined public leases to neighbouring private estates. Opponents challenged Damar's powers and obstructed the surveying parties. In 491 BR, armed retainers broke into a gathering near the public grain stores and killed him with several supporters. The leases under dispute largely remained in place. His surviving associates continued to seek admission and land through patrons who could offer them protection.

Between about 468 and 461 BR, several allied towns withheld troops after renewed demands for service. Their leaders sought a recognised share in decisions about levies and access to civic rights. The republic offered admission to some households while sending forces against towns that continued to resist. Families with influential sponsors gained opportunities that their neighbours lacked. The settlement enlarged Atherian citizenship while preserving grievances about the means by which it had been obtained.

Military intervention at home became more direct under Vespen Harod. In 448 BR, he brought troops into Caleran during a contest over commands and public appointments. His supporters executed opponents and sold confiscated property. Purchasers included the family of the later financier [[Lethren Ostar]]. Harod eventually relinquished his exceptional command, but the restored magistracies operated among families whose wealth and losses had been changed by his victory.

## Three different careers

[[Marovan Veyr]] acquired distinction through campaigns among eastern ports and maritime clients, principally between 414 and 397 BR. He secured winter harbours, suppressed raiding coalitions and imposed settlements on defeated rulers. Some towns welcomed safer shipping; others supplied hostages and grain. His followers expected Caleran to recognise the appointments and rewards he had promised them.

Lethren Ostar maintained a different network. Estates, loans and investments in transport gave him access to households that needed credit or assistance with public affairs. He cultivated clients through attention and practical help. In 399 BR, he commanded forces against an uprising of enslaved workers and armed fugitives along a supply corridor. Captured rebels were crucified beside the road. The victory strengthened his position without giving him Veyr's standing as a commander.

[[Teren Casvilar]] belonged to a Caleran family with substantial civic ancestry and insufficient money for its ambitions. Ostar's support helped him obtain office and finance public undertakings. Casvilar proved an effective speaker and intermediary. He could secure hearings, resolve particular debts and introduce people whose interests had previously kept them apart. His generosity also created obligations he would later call upon.

## The first partnership, c. 396–391 BR

The three men cooperated because each possessed something the others needed. Veyr wanted recognition for his eastern arrangements and land for followers. Ostar sought access for his clients and a military distinction of his own. Casvilar could organise support among assemblies and office-holders, then use their cooperation to obtain a long southern command.

Their partnership held no permanent public office of its own. Each continued to act through commands, magistracies and personal relationships. This allowed allies to deny that they had created a separate government while coordinating decisions that opponents struggled to reverse.

Casvilar's daughter Calvena married Veyr in 386 BR, after the alliance was established. The marriage gave both households a regular means of communication and became a source of genuine affection. Calvena managed visitors, correspondence and household resources during her husband's absences. Her death in childbirth in 380 BR removed a relationship through which the men had continued to speak even when public business divided them.

## Conquest and the weakening alliance

Casvilar's [[The Southern Campaigns of Teren Casvilar|southern campaigns]], from about 390 to 379 BR, extended Atherian command among inhabited forest districts. He demanded supplies from allies, took hostages, defeated a resistance coalition and ordered punitive mutilation after a later surrender. Officers and soldiers benefited from his attention and rewards. Captured communities supplied enslaved people and wealth to the households celebrating him.

Ostar meanwhile sought his own distinction beyond the northern frontier. His expedition ended at [[Lethren Ostar#The disaster at Rethmar|Rethmar]] in 381 BR, where defended wells, organised attacks on supply and a failed withdrawal destroyed his army. His son died before him. Survivors returned through ransom or captivity, carrying accounts of a country their commanders had understood poorly.

Ostar's death removed a source of credit and negotiation. Veyr increasingly relied on senators and eastern clients who feared Casvilar's growing military following. They demanded that he relinquish command before seeking further office. Casvilar insisted that his veterans' grants and his own protection against prosecution must be secured first. Decisions about troops, appointments and land transformed rivalry into a contest neither side trusted the existing institutions to settle.

## The first civil war, c. 376–373 BR

In 376 BR, Casvilar advanced towards Caleran with troops still under his command. Some towns admitted him after promises concerning arrears and local property; others resisted and lost garrisons or leading households. Veyr withdrew towards the eastern coast, where his longer-established clients could provide ships and supplies.

Control of Caleran gave Casvilar access to its treasury and appointments, but the eastern armies remained capable of resisting him. Campaigns across the highland approaches depended on pack routes, food stores and the loyalty of cities between the two coasts. The fighting culminated near Velos in 374 BR. Casvilar's forces broke Veyr's field position and cut access to one of his supply routes.

The senator Arest Corven, who had joined Veyr while criticising the exceptional powers of both commanders, took his own life after the defeat. Other opponents accepted Casvilar's pardon. Their return enlarged his government while making continued participation dependent upon the man who had defeated them.

Veyr sailed to the gateway kingdom. Its ruler Olymes had recently died, leaving [[Samyra Nerethan]] and her younger brother Nethar in a contested succession. The royal minister Vardek ordered Veyr killed at a landing in 373 BR, expecting to secure Casvilar's favour. Veyr's wife escaped with members of their household. Casvilar intervened instead in the struggle for the royal government, supported Samyra and had Vardek executed. Nethar died during the subsequent fighting in 372 BR.

## Casvilar's government

Between 373 and 370 BR, Casvilar attempted to govern a republic whose most important decisions increasingly required his approval. He confirmed some civic grants, recalled selected exiles, reduced parts of the immediate debt burden and assigned land to veterans. Different households benefited from different measures, while subject communities supplied much of the property and revenue.

His relationship with Samyra produced an acknowledged son, Amesar. The child belonged to her royal house. Casvilar's chosen civic heir was his sister Lysara's son, [[Aurelius Thalion]], whose family and public standing placed him within Caleran's political world. The distinction later made the two inheritances a source of conflict.

Casvilar retained the senate and magistracies, placing supporters in commands and accepting increasingly personal honours. Former opponents could speak again in public while knowing that their safety depended upon his continued favour. Trusted officers also resented appointments given to newer clients and the prospect that exceptional government would become permanent.

## The assassination, 370 BR

The conspiracy united people who disagreed about the government they wanted afterwards. Deceran Tolm had served as Casvilar's quartermaster and possessed his confidence. Mavren Seneth had accepted pardon after supporting Veyr and believed civic independence could not recover while Casvilar remained supreme. Corial Draven, a commander with naval connections, resented the distribution of authority and supplied armed support.

Tolm persuaded Casvilar to attend a meeting in an assembly hall overlooking the river stores. The conspirators surrounded and stabbed him. The killing removed the person around whom appointments, debts, pardons and military promises had accumulated. It left those obligations in the hands of people who could not agree how to meet them.

[[Darion Velsar]], Casvilar's senior lieutenant, secured access to his correspondence and officers. The public funeral gave bereaved followers an occasion to assemble and accuse the killers. Thalion returned to claim the inheritance, while senators led by Belisar Venn hoped to use him against Velsar. The [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession|succession wars]] grew from these competing claims and the armies still waiting to be paid.
