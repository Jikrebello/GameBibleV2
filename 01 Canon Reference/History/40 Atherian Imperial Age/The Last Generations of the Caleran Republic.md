# The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic

The last generations of the Caleran republic inherited a long roll of allies, conquered districts and maritime debts. Between the destruction of the gateway capital around 520 BR and the murder of Teren Casvilar in 370 BR, quarrels over land, ships and service were fought with armed force inside Atheria itself. The republic's own rites sharpened them. Its augurs claimed sole right to read the sky over public land; its harbours closed their chains against its allies in a storm; and its emergency command, the Warden of the Red Field, could be accepted but never safely laid down.

The [[The Gateway Wars|Gateway Wars]] had enlarged Caleran's resources without sharing them evenly. Some families owned productive land and shares in supply ventures; others came home from the fleets to debt or damaged farms. Across the sea, the spared towns were rebuilding around Sarqen under an oath sworn over a meal. Both societies changed through several generations before the commanders of the imperial founding were born.

## The Noon Sighting, 489–487 BR

The rowers and soldiers who broke Xarun of Vashar's fleet at the Narrows of Ilyessa in 493 were mostly small citizens and allied crews. They came home in 491 to find the republic's public land leased in great blocks to a few families, the same families who supplied most of the augurs. That land had been entered "on the sky". Its boundaries were star-lines sighted from the old stones on Sarven's terrace, and they could be re-sighted only on days the augurs declared open.

In 489 the magistrate **Eshren Damar** proposed to cap the renewal of the great leases and settle the Ilyessa veterans on the surplus. The augurs answered with the calendar. They inserted **the Long Month**, an extra month that carried the renewal day past the end of Damar's term. The observers of Irenai, trained in methods older than Caleran's, disputed the intercalation in public, and Damar's veterans began to say that the sky belonged to whoever paid for the augurs.

At midsummer 488 Damar led his veterans to Sarven without a single augur. Vardeni hill soldiers among them performed an upland rite their grandmothers used to ask the Sun to show a boundary, kneeling bareheaded on the terrace at noon. Something answered. For one hour every shadow in the lower Aurin pointed along the old lines: fence posts, olive trees, standing men and the walls of the great estates all cast their shadows the same way, and the shadows showed where estate walls had been built across public land. Surveyors with Damar measured as fast as they could, and their record of that hour became known as **the Noon Roll**.

The veterans paid what the upland rite required: a season's work bareheaded under the open sky, without shade. Some of the petitioners had also asked that the recovered land "bear for us" without end. That autumn the public-land harvest ripened in days. Barley headed and bleached in a week, figs split on the branch, and the fields were rotting before the reapers could reach them. The hungry spring that followed gave Damar's enemies their crowd. In 487 retainers of the leaseholding houses killed him with several supporters in a bread crowd at the public grain stores.

The augurs called the noon hour an unlicensed sign and the rotten harvest its punishment. Damar's followers called the harvest the price of greed and the sign itself a gift. In 486 the senate passed **the Licensed Sighting**: no one but an augur might seek a sign over public things, or record one. The leases stayed where they were. The Noon Roll stayed with the veterans' families, copied and recopied and kept in roof beams, and a century later Casvilar's veterans would carry copies of it onto the same land.

## The war of the closed chains, 469–461 BR

The League of the Eastern Oar had been formed in 497 to fight Xarun. After his withdrawal, Caleran kept the fleet at sea against his successor Orvaz I and fixed what every member owed for it. Thesra and Olveth, which had no ships, paid in silver and sons. Cities that fell behind found the League's harbour chains raised against their hulls, even in bad weather. Around 475 Oar commanders seized the families of Elmarai pilots who crewed Pyrassa's grain fleet as surety for Pyrassa's payments, because pilots' children were cheaper hostages than Pyrassan citizens.

In 469 Velathene, the fleet's own harbour, refused its assessment. Thesra, Olveth and the scattered valleys of the lost archive league refused theirs, and Verion and the Elmarai pilots joined them. Pyrassa, whose pilots' families were in Caleran's hands, stayed loyal. Orvaz sent the rebels timber and money and gave their leaders houses at Avesh.

Caleran ordered every League harbour to close its chain against rebel hulls. In the autumn gale of 466 Pyrassa obeyed. The night that followed is remembered on every coast of the inner sea. Pyrassa's harbour would admit no ship at all, its own grain hulls included; they circled outside on what sailors swore was the same wave, while the signal fires on the moles contradicted one another. Pilots who had learned Kelarion's old Roll of Refused Hulls ran for the refuge fire on the drowned harbour's reef, and lived. Survivors said something rose under Pyrassa's breakwater in the dark, crowned with broken towers, and the coast called it by the name it had used for seven hundred years, the King beneath the Breakwater. Before dawn Pyrassa's own wardens cut the harbour chain with axes, the gate opened and the ships came in. The rebel crews refused at the chains that night were led by Elmarai pilots to [[Marelith]], the wandering city that never anchored, and Marelith became known as "the harbour without a chain".

That same winter a rebel fleet stood off the mouth of the Aurin. Caleran revived the Red Field. The senator **Vespen Harod** went out to the muster ground below the river stores, dedicated his arms and named the rebel captains as his enemies. Allied resistance was broken over the next five years. The **Chain Peace** of 461 admitted loyal households and some rebel cities to citizenship on unequal terms, pulled down Velathene's walls, and made refuge law for any vessel in distress, of any allegiance. A copy of the Roll of Refused Hulls was laid with the custodians of [[Ilamar before 350 BR|Ilamar]], who heard refuge disputes from then on. The captains Harod had named were by then at Avesh.

## The Warden in the tent, 466–c. 441 BR

A Warden of the Red Field could not quit the field while an enemy he had named still held it, and Harod's enemies were alive and comfortable in Orvaz's houses. Harod would not sleep under a roof, lest the Red Star read it as flight. For twenty-five years he governed from a tent on the muster ground, and senators who wanted a decision came out to him through the mud. The senate tried to replace him in 452–449. Senators who formally set themselves against him found that the rite counted them as contestants too.

In 448 Harod brought troops into Caleran during a contest over commands and public appointments. His supporters executed opponents and sold their property. Purchasers included the family of the later financier [[Lethren Ostar]]. Harod spent his last years demanding that Orvaz hand over the captains for a fair contest, and Orvaz, who preferred Caleran tied to its own Warden, refused. Around 441 Harod died in his tent of a winter fever, still Warden. The augurs said the Red Star had taken its Warden. His enemies spread a story of Vasharan poison. For two generations Caleran believed that the field he had accepted still stood open, and the magistracies that resumed after him worked among families whose wealth and losses his confiscations had rearranged.

## Three different careers

[[Marovan Veyr]] made his name among eastern ports and maritime clients, principally between 414 and 397 BR. He secured winter harbours, suppressed raiding coalitions and imposed settlements on defeated rulers. Some towns welcomed safer shipping; others supplied hostages and grain. His followers expected Caleran to recognise the appointments and rewards he had promised them.

Lethren Ostar kept a different following. Estates, loans and investments in transport gave him access to households that needed credit or help with public affairs. In 399 BR he commanded the forces sent against a revolt of enslaved workers and armed fugitives along a supply corridor. He sold the captured rebels north in lots to the well trade, and the sales gave him contacts and appetites in the northern well country. A band of fugitives escaped up the pass to Vorthara, where his agents bought them out of the captive traders' pens and sent them to the Olveth ore workings; several Vortharan refuge households held their doors against his agents and swore a mutual defence remembered as **the Kept Doors**. The victory strengthened Ostar's position without giving him Veyr's standing as a commander.

[[Teren Casvilar]] belonged to a Caleran family with substantial civic ancestry and too little money for its ambitions. Ostar's loans helped him obtain office and finance public works. Casvilar was an effective speaker and intermediary. He could secure a hearing, settle a particular debt and introduce people whose interests had kept them apart, and his generosity created obligations he would later call in.

## The first partnership, c. 396–391 BR

The three men cooperated because each possessed something the others needed. Veyr wanted recognition for his eastern arrangements and land for his followers. Ostar sought access for his clients and a military distinction of his own. Casvilar could organise support among assemblies and office-holders, then use it to obtain a long southern command.

Their partnership held no public office of its own. Each continued to act through commands, magistracies and personal relationships, which let their allies deny that they had created a separate government.

Casvilar's daughter Calvena married Veyr in 386 BR, after the alliance was established. The marriage gave both households a regular way to speak and became a source of genuine affection. Calvena managed visitors, correspondence and household resources during her husband's absences, and she could talk to her father and her husband about matters neither would raise in the senate.

## Conquest and the breaking of the partnership

Casvilar's [[The Southern Campaigns of Teren Casvilar|southern campaigns]], from about 390 to 379 BR, extended Atherian command among inhabited forest districts. He demanded supplies from allies, took hostages, defeated a resistance coalition at Kellovar and ordered punitive mutilation after a later surrender at Darneth. Before the last engineering effort at Darneth, his engineers loaded a valuable siege frame under the Red Star's test until it broke, and the break showed them where force would carry through the defenders' works. Casvilar called the broken frame heavenly confirmation of his campaign. Officers and soldiers prospered from his attention and rewards; captured communities supplied enslaved people and wealth to the households celebrating him.

Ostar sought his own distinction beyond the northern frontier. His expedition ended at [[Lethren Ostar#The disaster at Rethmar|Rethmar]] in 381 BR, where defended wells, attacks on separated columns and a failed withdrawal destroyed his army. His son died before him. Ostar's death removed the partnership's banker and the one man both commanders owed.

From 379 Casvilar's returning veterans demanded land, and they came with copies of the Noon Roll. The lines its surveyors had measured in 488 still showed which estate walls stood on public land, and the families who had held those leases for more than a century were Veyr's allies in the senate. They demanded that Casvilar lay down his command before seeking further office. Casvilar refused while his veterans' grants and his own safety from prosecution were unsecured. In 377 he offered Calvena a divorce from Veyr. She refused it in public, in front of her father's officers, and went east with her husband's household. Atherian writers later called her refusal the end of the partnership. It was the moment people could see it had ended.

## The first civil war, 376–371 BR

Ordered in 376 to lay down his command, Casvilar dedicated his arms at a frontier altar and named the senators who had demanded his surrender. His augurs proclaimed that he had accepted the Red Field. His enemies said he had stolen it. Either way he could not now lay down his command while the men he had named still opposed him, and he advanced on Caleran with troops still under arms. Some towns admitted him after promises about arrears and local property; others resisted and lost garrisons or leading households. Veyr withdrew towards the eastern coast, where his older clients could provide ships and supplies.

The armies met near **Velos**, the bridge-town on the highland road, in 374 BR. Neither broke. Casvilar held the field at nightfall; Veyr held the road behind him. The war was settled at sea instead. Veyr needed ship money from the Oar ports, and the ports remembered whose senate had closed the chains against them in 466. They refused him. Without a fleet he could not hold the eastern coast, and he sailed for the gateway kingdom, where Olymes had recently died and [[Samyra Nerethan]] and her young brother Nethar were contesting the succession.

At Sarqen in 373 the minister **Vardek**, ruling for Nethar, invited Veyr to land and eat, intending to kill him at the table and win Casvilar's favour. An Esharati elder warned Veyr that the table was laid with an empty seat, which the spared towns' oath forbade, and Veyr turned back to sea. He took sanctuary at Ilamar with Calvena and the senator **Arest Corven**, who had joined him while criticising the exceptional powers of both commanders. Casvilar demanded Veyr's surrender. The custodians refused, and Casvilar left the island alone, because the certificates that let his armies buy grain were issued by Ilamar's court. Corven began there the history of the republic that later writers used against Casvilar's heirs.

The spared towns, learning that Vardek had laid a table to kill a guest, rose for Samyra. Casvilar needed the grain of Esharat and backed the side that already held it. The towns judged Vardek under their oath, and Casvilar's officers executed him. Nethar died in the fighting in 372. Veyr died of fever at Ilamar in 371, with Calvena beside him. Other opponents accepted Casvilar's pardon, which enlarged his government while making their safety depend on the man who had defeated them.

## Casvilar's government

Between 373 and 370 BR, Casvilar governed a republic whose decisions increasingly required his approval. He confirmed civic grants, recalled selected exiles, reduced parts of the immediate debt burden and settled veterans on the land of the Noon Roll. His enemies called those grants theft from the old leaseholders; his veterans called them the noon hour come true. Subject communities supplied much of the property and revenue.

His relationship with Samyra produced an acknowledged son, Amesar, in 372. The child belonged to her royal house. Casvilar's chosen civic heir was his sister Lysara's son, [[Aurelius Thalion]], whose family and public standing placed him within Caleran's political world. The two inheritances later became a source of war.

Casvilar kept the senate and magistracies, placed supporters in commands and accepted increasingly personal honours. He also kept the Field. As Warden he had to receive in person any challenger who entered his name at the muster ground, and he could not lay down his command while an enemy he had named still stood. Former opponents could speak again in public, knowing that their safety depended on his favour. Trusted officers resented commands given to newer clients and the prospect that exceptional government would never end.

## The assassination, 370 BR

The conspiracy joined people who disagreed about the government they wanted afterwards. **Deceran Tolm** had served as Casvilar's quartermaster and kept the arms register of the Field. **Mavren Seneth** had accepted pardon after supporting Veyr and believed civic independence could not recover while Casvilar remained supreme. **Corial Draven**, a commander with naval connections, resented the distribution of commands and supplied armed men.

Seneth, Draven and five others entered their names in Tolm's register as challengers, a lawful act that obliged the Warden to receive them. Tolm arranged the day. They met Casvilar at the muster ground below the river stores and struck before the contest had been opened, surrounding and stabbing him with the weapons they had brought to dedicate. The killing removed the man around whom appointments, debts, pardons and military promises had gathered, and left those obligations to people who could not agree how to meet them. Augurs and soldiers alike said that a contest struck before it opened could not be closed while any of the seven lived, and that whatever had been loosed on the muster ground would run until every faction had proved itself.

[[Darion Velsar]], Casvilar's senior lieutenant, secured his correspondence and officers and claimed the Field. The public funeral gave bereaved followers an occasion to assemble and accuse the killers. Thalion returned to claim the inheritance and refused the Field in the forum, while senators led by Belisar Venn hoped to use him against Velsar. The [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession|succession wars]] grew from these competing claims and from the armies still waiting to be paid.
