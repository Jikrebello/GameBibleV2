# The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic

The last generations of the Caleran republic inherited a long roll of allies, conquered districts and maritime debts. Between the destruction of the gateway capital around 520 BR and the murder of Teren Casvilar in 370 BR, quarrels over land, ships and service were fought with armed force inside Atheria itself. The republic's own rites sharpened them. Its augurs claimed sole right to read the sky over public land; its harbours closed their chains against its allies in a storm; and its emergency command, the Warden of the Red Field, could be accepted but never safely laid down. Two men held that office in these years. Vespen Harod sat in a tent for twenty-five years waiting for enemies who would not come. Teren Casvilar carried the office for thirteen, because the enemy he had named walked into a river and was not seen on either bank.

The [[The Gateway Wars|Gateway Wars]] had enlarged Caleran's resources without sharing them evenly. Some families owned productive land and shares in supply ventures; others came home from the fleets to debt or damaged farms. Across the sea, the spared towns were rebuilding around Sarqen under an oath sworn over a meal. Both societies changed through several generations before the commanders of the imperial founding were born.

## The Noon Sighting, 489–486 BR

The rowers and soldiers who took Xarun of Vashar's granary fleet at its moorings off Ilyessa in 493 were mostly small citizens, allied crews and the king's own deserting oarsmen. They came home in 491 to find the republic's public land leased in great blocks to a few families, the same families who supplied most of the augurs. That land had been entered "on the sky". Its boundaries were star-lines sighted from the old stones on Sarven's terrace, and they could be re-sighted only on days the augurs declared open.

The leases fell due for renewal in 489. The magistrate **Eshren Damar**, who had commanded a squadron at Ilyessa, asked the augurs to open a sighting day so that the lines could be walked before the houses signed again. The augurs found cloud, a bird on the wrong hand and a festival in the way, and declared no day open that year. Damar's veterans began to say that the sky belonged to whoever paid for the augurs.

At midsummer 488 Damar led his veterans to Sarven without a single augur. Vardeni hill soldiers among them performed an upland rite their grandmothers used to ask the Sun to show a boundary, kneeling bareheaded on the terrace at noon. Something answered. For one hour every shadow in the lower Aurin pointed along the old lines: fence posts, olive trees, standing men and the walls of the great estates all cast their shadows the same way, and the shadows showed where estate walls had been built across public land. Surveyors with Damar measured as fast as they could, and their record of that hour became known as **the Noon Roll**.

The augurs answered with the calendar. They announced that the year had required an extra month, **the Long Month**, and that they were inserting it behind the midsummer just past, so that the day of the sighting had stood outside the year. The Common Sky taught that whatever was done on an unreckoned day went unseen. The observers of Irenai, trained in methods older than Caleran's, came to Caleran and disputed the count in public. It made little difference on the land. Every tenant and steward in the lower Aurin had watched the shadow of his own wall lie along the old line. When the veterans walked onto the surplus behind the Noon Roll that summer, the houses could find no one willing to put them off, and by harvest several thousand men were camped on fields they called their own.

The veterans paid what the upland rite required: a season's work bareheaded under the open sky, without shade. Some of the petitioners had also asked that the recovered land "bear for us" without end. That autumn the harvest on the noon land ripened in days. Barley headed and bleached in a week, figs split on the branch, and the fields were rotting before the reapers could reach them.

The settlers had staked everything on that harvest, and the spring of 487 found them hungry on ground they had no seed to sow. At the public grain stores in Caleran, retainers of the leaseholding houses went into a bread crowd with staves and knives and killed several of Damar's veterans. After that the houses had no need of killing. Their stewards came out to the noon land with carts of bread and seed-corn and bought it back plot by plot, for what a family could eat before the next harvest. By 486 most of it lay inside the old leases again, and many of the men who had sold it were working it as the houses' tenants.

The augurs called the noon hour an unlicensed sign and the rotten harvest its punishment. Damar's followers called the harvest the price of greed and the sign itself a gift. In 486 the senate passed **the Licensed Sighting**: no one but an augur might seek a sign over public things, or record one. Damar was neither tried nor killed. He kept his own plot below Sarven's terrace, refused every offer for it and worked it bareheaded in all weathers for the thirty years he had left, long after the season the rite required. Leaseholders pointed him out to their sons as the man who had starved the Aurin. The Vardeni hill villages said he was the only petitioner who went on paying. The Noon Roll stayed with the veterans' families, copied and recopied and kept in roof beams, and a century later Casvilar's veterans would carry copies of it onto the same land.

## The war of the closed chains, 469–461 BR

The League of the Eastern Oar had been formed in 497 to fight Xarun. After his withdrawal, Caleran kept the fleet at sea against his successor Orvaz I and fixed what every member owed for it. Thesra and Olveth, which had no ships, paid in silver and sons. Cities that fell behind found the League's harbour chains raised against their hulls, even in bad weather. Around 475 Oar commanders seized the families of Elmarai pilots who crewed Pyrassa's grain fleet as surety for Pyrassa's payments, because pilots' children were cheaper hostages than Pyrassan citizens.

In 469 Velathene, the fleet's own harbour, refused its assessment. Thesra, Olveth and the scattered valleys of the lost archive league refused theirs, and Verion and the Elmarai pilots joined them. Pyrassa, whose pilots' families were in Caleran's hands, stayed loyal. Orvaz sent the rebels timber and money and gave their leaders houses at Avesh.

Caleran ordered every League harbour to close its chain against rebel hulls. In the autumn gale of 466 Pyrassa obeyed. The night that followed is remembered on every coast of the inner sea. Pyrassa's harbour would admit no ship at all, its own grain hulls included; they circled outside on what sailors swore was the same wave, while the signal fires on the moles contradicted one another. Pilots who had learned Kelarion's old Roll of Refused Hulls ran for the refuge fire on the drowned harbour's reef, and lived. Survivors said something rose under Pyrassa's breakwater in the dark, crowned with broken towers, and the coast called it by the name it had used for seven hundred years, the King beneath the Breakwater. Before dawn Pyrassa's own wardens cut the harbour chain with axes, the gate opened and the ships came in. The rebel crews refused at the chains that night were led by Elmarai pilots to [[Marelith]], the wandering city that never anchored, and Marelith became known as "the harbour without a chain".

That same winter a rebel fleet stood off the mouth of the Aurin. Caleran revived the Red Field, which no commander had accepted since Celor Marath laid it down. The senator **Vespen Harod** went out to the muster ground below the river stores, dedicated his arms and named the rebel captains as his enemies. The captains declined the contest. Their fleet stood away east when the weather cleared, and by the following year the men Harod had named were living in the houses Orvaz had given them at Avesh.

The war was never fought to a finish. After the night at Pyrassa no harbour warden on the inner sea would raise a chain at Caleran's order. Through that winter, in one port after another, the wardens hauled their chains up onto the moles and left them lying in the weather where the crews could see them. Caleran's assessors could close no harbour, and the rebels, who lived by the grain trade as much as anyone, could take none. The rebel cities kept the year by the count Irenai's observers published and Caleran kept it by its augurs', so that for five years a contract sealed in one harbour was already a week old in the next. Orvaz paid just enough to keep the quarrel alive.

The **Chain Peace** of 461 was sworn on Ilamar, on ground neither side owned. Every harbour's chain passed to its own wardens, who swore never to raise it at another city's order or against any vessel in distress, of any allegiance. Velathene's chain towers were pulled down at the islanders' demand, so that the League's own harbour could never again close on a member. The fixed ship money still went to Caleran's treasury; anything beyond it needed each port's own vote. Thesra and Olveth paid in silver from then on and sent no more sons. The Elmarai pilots' families went home. One year was kept again, at Caleran, with Irenai's observers present at every intercalation. A copy of the Roll of Refused Hulls was laid with the custodians of [[Ilamar before 350 BR|Ilamar]], who heard refuge disputes from then on. The peace said nothing about the captains at Avesh. The senate could pardon rebels. It could not unname a Warden's enemies.

## The Warden in the tent, 466–c. 441 BR

A Warden of the Red Field could not quit the field while an enemy he had named still held it, and Harod's enemies were alive and comfortable in Orvaz's houses. He had dedicated his arms on the muster ground, and the muster ground was his field. He would not sleep under a roof, lest the Red Star read it as flight. For twenty-five years he lived in a tent on that ground. No levy could be raised and no fleet sent without the republic's Warden, so senators who wanted a decision came out to him through the mud. A town of sutlers, cookshops, armourers and moneylenders grew up around the tent, and children were born in it who had never seen the muster ground empty.

Harod asked more than once to be relieved. Between 452 and 449 the senate tried. Nine senators put their seals to a decree naming another man to the command, and the augurs ruled that men who set themselves formally against a Warden had entered his field as contestants. Harod was bound to receive them and they were bound to come. Two came down to the muster ground with swords. They were old colleagues of his, and he killed them both; one account has him weeping and another has him laughing. The other seven left the city. The camp held that they had fled the field. Soldiers stripped their houses, the goods were sold from carts on the muster ground, and their families were left to the charity of kin. No senator set his seal against a Warden again in Harod's lifetime.

He spent his last years demanding that Orvaz hand over the captains for a fair contest, and Orvaz, who preferred Caleran tied to its own Warden, refused. Around 441 Harod died in his tent of a winter fever, still Warden. The augurs said the Red Star had taken its Warden. His enemies spread a story of Vasharan poison. For two generations Caleran believed that the field he had accepted still stood open. The sutlers' town was cleared from the muster ground, and the families who had fed it for a generation went home rich. One of them was the house of [[Lethren Ostar]].

## Three different careers

[[Marovan Veyr]] made his name among eastern ports and maritime clients, principally between 414 and 397 BR. Since the Chain Peace no league could compel those ports, and a commander who wanted their ships had to win them one harbour at a time. Veyr secured winter harbours, suppressed raiding coalitions and imposed settlements on defeated rulers. Some towns welcomed safer shipping; others supplied hostages and grain. His followers expected Caleran to recognise the appointments and rewards he had promised them.

Lethren Ostar never held a command. His grandfather had sold bread and lamp oil in the town around Harod's tent, and the family came away from the muster ground holding the debts of half the senate. Estates, loans and investments in transport gave Ostar access to households that needed credit or help with public affairs. In 410 BR enslaved workers and fugitives from the road gangs rose along the corridor that supplied Veyr's eastern war and burned the stores they had been made to fill. The estate owners hired companies on Ostar's credit, and those companies broke the rear of the rebel column at the foot of the pass to Vorthara. Ostar took the captives in settlement of the owners' debts and sold them north in lots to the well trade, and the sales gave him contacts and appetites in the northern well country. The head of the column reached Vorthara, where his agents bought fugitives out of the captive traders' pens and sent them to the Olveth ore workings; several Vortharan refuge households held their doors against his agents and swore a mutual defence remembered as **the Kept Doors**.

[[Teren Casvilar]] belonged to a Caleran family with substantial civic ancestry and too little money for its ambitions. Ostar's loans helped him obtain office and finance public works. Casvilar was an effective speaker and intermediary. He could secure a hearing, settle a particular debt and introduce people whose interests had kept them apart, and his generosity created obligations he would later call in.

## The first partnership, c. 396–391 BR

The three men cooperated because each possessed something the others needed. Veyr wanted recognition for his eastern arrangements and land for his followers. Ostar wanted the senate's leave for a venture in the north. Casvilar could organise support among assemblies and office-holders, then use it to obtain a long southern command.

Their partnership held no public office of its own. Each continued to act through commands, magistracies and personal relationships, which let their allies deny that they had created a separate government.

Ostar's venture came first and failed first. Dealers in the well country had sold him shares in the reservoirs of Rethmar, a winter gathering whose water belonged to its keepers and to no dealer. In 392 he sent his son Pel north with hired companies, surveyors and bailiffs to take possession. The keepers emptied the cisterns ahead of them, and the columns died between dry wells ([[Lethren Ostar#The disaster at Rethmar|the disaster at Rethmar]]). Pel was taken alive and did not come home. Ostar had stayed in Caleran. He was poorer by an army, and he looked to Casvilar's southern command to repay him in captives.

Casvilar's daughter Calvena married Veyr in 389 BR, when she was sixteen and he was forty-six. The marriage gave both households a regular way to speak and became a source of genuine affection. Calvena managed visitors, correspondence and household resources during her husband's absences, and she could talk to her father and her husband about matters neither would raise in the senate.

## The southern command and the Field at Kellovar, 390–379 BR

Casvilar's [[The Southern Campaigns of Teren Casvilar|southern campaigns]], from about 390 to 379 BR, extended Atherian command among inhabited forest districts. He demanded supplies from allies, took hostages and paid Ostar in captives. In 383 he besieged the coalition of the Folk chief Drenoc on the wooded hill of Kellovar. His command was a year from its term, and a relief host was gathering in the forest behind him. He dedicated his arms at the siege lines, named Drenoc and accepted the Red Field, the first commander to do so since Harod. No senate could recall him while Drenoc held the hill, and no officer could advise retreat.

He expected to close the Field within the month. Hunger emptied the hill instead. The people Drenoc turned out as useless mouths went down to the ford in the wood below Kellovar, which the river people held to belong to a court of its own, and crossed on their household tokens. Drenoc followed them into the water to bargain for those who had no token. They crossed, and he was not seen on either bank. Casvilar took the hill, a few hundred starved fighters and no chief. His augurs could not say that his named enemy was dead, and they could not say that he had been beaten. They said the Field stood open.

At Darneth in 380 his engineers loaded a valuable siege frame under the Red Star's test until it broke, and the break showed them where the ridge's rampart would fail. Many of the fighters who surrendered there were ford guides, men whose household tokens had carried Kellovar's people over the rivers. Casvilar had their hands cut off. He called the broken frame heavenly confirmation of his campaign. Officers and soldiers prospered from his attention and rewards; captured communities supplied enslaved people and wealth to the households celebrating him.

## The banker and the open Field, 379–377 BR

From 379 Casvilar's veterans came home, and they came with copies of the Noon Roll. The lines its surveyors had measured in 488 still showed which estate walls stood on public land, and the families who had held those leases for more than a century were Veyr's allies in the senate. The veterans wanted the land and their discharge. Casvilar could promise the first. He could not give the second, or lay down his own command, while his Field stood open. For the rest of his life parties from both factions went beyond the southern rivers looking for Drenoc or his grave. The augurs were shown three men said to be Drenoc, and rejected all three.

The supply of captives ended with the war, and in 379 Ostar called in his loans. He summoned the Warden before the city's court for debt. Casvilar's augurs remembered the nine senators of Harod's day. A man who summoned a Warden from his field, they ruled, had set himself against it, and they entered the banker as a contestant. Ostar was sixty-seven and had never worn a sword. He left Caleran by night for Vorthara. The camp declared that he had fled the field, and his ledgers were carried out to the Warden's tent. Casvilar burned the ones that named himself and his officers, and with them the debts of several thousand small borrowers. The city cheered him for a week.

The leaseholding houses understood what they had seen. A Warden who could unmake the richest man in Caleran with a line in a register could unmake them, and his veterans were already walking their boundaries. They demanded that he produce his enemy or lay down his arms, and Veyr took their side. Early in 377 Casvilar offered Calvena a divorce from Veyr. She refused it in public, in front of her father's officers, and went east with her husband's household. Atherian writers later called her refusal the end of the partnership. The partnership had ended two years earlier in the Warden's tent, when the augurs wrote down the banker's name.

## The first civil war, 377–371 BR

In the spring of 377 the senate declared the Field at Kellovar void and named a successor to the southern command. Veyr's seal stood first on the decree. Casvilar's augurs entered every man who had sealed it as a contestant, and he marched on Caleran with his field, as he put it, carried on his soldiers' backs. Some towns admitted him after promises about arrears and local property; others resisted and lost garrisons or leading households. The senators he had named fled east along the highland road, and Veyr went by sea to raise the ports.

The armies met that autumn near **Velos**, the bridge-town on the highland road. The senators had raised theirs from Veyr's old clients, and Veyr was to bring the fleet round behind Casvilar with his own veterans aboard. He never came. Since the Chain Peace no one could order the Oar ports to pay for a fleet, and the ports remembered whose senate had closed the chains against them in 466. They voted him nothing. His galleys stayed in their sheds, and the army at Velos fought without him. Neither side broke. Casvilar held the bridge at nightfall and the senators held the road east. Veyr and his father-in-law never met in the field.

For four years Atheria had two governments and two granaries. Casvilar held Caleran, the Aurin and the outer coast, and fed his armies on certificates written at Ilamar. The senators and Veyr held the eastern roads and lived on the grain of the gateway kingdom, whose king Olymes owed Veyr favours. Olymes died in 375. His children [[Samyra Nerethan]] and Nethar contested the succession, the grain ships came late and then came short, and in 373 Veyr sailed for Sarqen to ask for them in person.

At Sarqen the minister **Vardek**, ruling for Nethar, had decided that Casvilar would win. He invited Veyr to land and eat, intending to kill him at the table and send the news to Caleran. An Esharati elder warned Veyr that the table was laid with an empty seat, which the spared towns' oath forbade, and Veyr turned back to sea. By then Casvilar's allies held the passage north. Veyr put in at Ilamar with Calvena and the senator **Arest Corven**, who had joined him while criticising the exceptional powers of both commanders, and took sanctuary there. Casvilar demanded his surrender as a named contestant. The custodians refused, and Casvilar left the island alone, because the certificates that let his armies buy grain were issued by Ilamar's court. Corven began there the history of the republic that later writers used against Casvilar's heirs.

The spared towns, learning that Vardek had laid a table to kill a guest, rose for Samyra. Casvilar needed the grain of Esharat as badly as Veyr had, and he backed the side that already held it. The towns judged Vardek under their oath, and Casvilar's officers executed him. Nethar died in the fighting in 372. With the gateway grain in Casvilar's hands the eastern army could not be fed, and its senators began to come in. Veyr died of fever at Ilamar in 371, with Calvena beside him. The men who accepted Casvilar's pardon found that their names stayed in the augurs' book with a line drawn through them, still legible.

## Casvilar's government

Between 373 and 370 BR, Casvilar governed a republic whose decisions increasingly required his approval. He confirmed civic grants, recalled selected exiles and settled veterans on the land of the Noon Roll. His enemies called those grants theft from the old leaseholders; his veterans called them the noon hour come true. The grants had a flaw the veterans felt every year. They held the land as soldiers of an open Field, without discharge, and could neither sell a plot nor leave it to a son until the Field was closed. Subject communities supplied much of the property and revenue.

His relationship with Samyra produced an acknowledged son, Amesar, in 372. The child belonged to her royal house. Casvilar's chosen civic heir was his sister Lysara's son, [[Aurelius Thalion]], whose family and public standing placed him within Caleran's political world. The two inheritances later became a source of war.

Casvilar kept the senate and magistracies, placed supporters in commands and accepted increasingly personal honours. He also kept the Field. He slept in a tent pitched in the court of his own house, as Harod had slept on the muster ground, and as Warden he had to receive in person any challenger who entered his name in the arms register. Former opponents could speak again in public, knowing that their safety depended on his favour. Trusted officers resented commands given to newer clients and the prospect that exceptional government would never end.

## The assassination, 370 BR

By 370 the Field at Kellovar had stood open for thirteen years, and nearly everyone in Caleran wanted it closed. The veterans wanted their discharge. The pardoned wanted their names out of the book. Casvilar, at sixty-one, wanted to close it with a victory in front of the whole city.

The seven men who killed him had little else in common. **Mavren Seneth** had commanded the river line below Kellovar on the night the hill emptied. He had fought for the senate at Velos and taken the Warden's pardon, and he told anyone who would listen that the Field could never close, because he had watched Drenoc walk into the ford. **Corial Draven**, a ship-owner, had held a third of Ostar's paper when the ledgers burned. Four others had lost land, kin or commands. The seventh was a young man of the river halls whom Seneth had found beyond the southern fords and Draven's ship had carried north. He was entered in the arms register as the son of Drenoc of Kellovar. The halls of Darneth and the clerks of Caleran have disagreed ever since about whether he was.

The register was kept by **Deceran Tolm**, quartermaster of the muster ground. He entered seven lawful names, as his office required, and fixed the day. Casvilar's augurs had rejected three Drenocs. They accepted a son because the Warden told them to: a son could stand in his father's quarrel, and a Warden who beat him would have beaten his named enemy's blood. On the appointed morning Casvilar came down to the muster ground below the river stores bareheaded, with his augurs, to receive the challengers' arms. The seven closed round him as he reached for the first of them and stabbed him with the weapons they had brought to dedicate. He was dead before the contest had been opened.

The killing removed the man around whom appointments, debts, pardons and military promises had gathered, and left those obligations to people who could not agree how to meet them. Seneth had meant to close a Field. Augurs and soldiers alike said that a contest struck before it opened could not be closed while any of the seven lived, and that whatever had been loosed on the muster ground would run until every faction had proved itself. Whether Tolm knew what the seven intended was never settled. His family said he had only kept the book.

[[Darion Velsar]], Casvilar's senior lieutenant, secured his correspondence and officers and claimed the Field. The public funeral gave bereaved followers an occasion to assemble and accuse the killers. Thalion returned to claim the inheritance and refused the Field in the forum, while senators led by Belisar Venn hoped to use him against Velsar. The [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession|succession wars]] grew from these competing claims and from the armies still waiting to be paid.
