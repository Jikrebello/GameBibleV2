# The Gateway Wars

The **Gateway Wars** were three wars between the Caleran republic, its allies and the [[The Gateway Republic|Gateway Republic]] of Qarzeth, fought between about 660 and 520 BR. The first began over bread sold at a festival table. The second turned on an invader whose gifts of grain emptied the storehouses of the towns that accepted them, and on a sacrifice Caleran made at its own festival ground. The third ended with the Gateway capital starved, broken, burned and salted, its bronze-faced god defeated and its lesser towns sworn to an oath that outlived both republics.

The wars made overseas commitments a permanent concern of Caleran's government. They enlarged provincial revenue and the followings of military patrons, imposed repeated service on coastal and river households, and filled Atherian estates and markets with enslaved people. They also left Caleran with an office it could not close and the Gateway country with two lists of children's names. Both mattered for more than two centuries.

## The sea and the alliances

Caleran stood at the mouth of the Aurin on the outer coast. To fight in the inner Thalassian it needed other people's harbours: Arkenan and Saronikan ports that supplied ships, pilots, rope and repair yards, and landward routes along which grain moved from the Aurin towns to the sea. Some ports were close allies. Others served for a price or because they had lost a previous war.

Qarzeth commanded a cultivated country where desert roads met inner-sea shipping. Its harbour was crowded with ships, but its strength lay in its granaries, which visitors described as the fullest on any coast. Gateway merchants said their god fed the city. Caleran's grain buyers came to believe them.

Smaller states had interests of their own. A town afraid of its neighbour could invite one republic into a quarrel its people had expected to stay local, and then find both republics' armies in its fields.

## The Tammur Tables, c. 660–646 BR

Three springs in succession, around 662–660 BR, the rains failed over the Aurin. The river ran low past Nerava, seed shrivelled in the furrows, and the Compact cities that bought grain abroad found the usual markets already stripped. The nearest surplus lay at **Tammur**, an inner-sea market town held by the Gateway Republic.

Qarzeth's factors at Tammur sold grain on one condition: the buyer first ate at the Provider's festival. Long tables were laid in the market square beneath a bronze face, with bread, oil, salt fish and wine for every merchant who sat down. Buyers who ate went home with full holds. Pyrassan grain houses, whose city had lived on imported grain since Doreva's day, ate and bought, and several brought small bronze faces home to hang above their store doors.

Caleran, as host of the [[The Celestial Compact and the Caleran Republic|Celestial Compact]], forbade the trade. Pyrassa bought anyway. Around 652 Caleran's ships closed the approaches to Tammur, and when Verion's ruler withheld his levy, Caleran struck his city's festival and contract days out of the common year until Verion's own crews refused to sail. The Harbour Judge **Qelmar Suthar** treated the blockade as an attack on the whole Gateway Republic. The Atherian commander **Aster Vey** promised his senate a short expedition and stayed four years.

Both governments raised further levies as ships were lost. Inland farms sent grain they could not spare to ports they would never see. Families expected home after one season waited through three. Aster Vey took Tammur in 649 after a Gateway relief convoy was caught on a lee shore and broken on the rocks within sight of the town.

The peace of 646 gave Caleran Tammur. The Gateway Council Houses also surrendered children as hostages, to be renewed every seven years, and the children were lodged with Atherian households near Caleran's festival ground. Within two seasons Qarzeth had a new fleet on the water. Caleran's shipwrights could not account for the timber or the bread that fed the carpenters. Gateway sailors said the Provider fed its own, and Caleran's senate began to fear a city that healed faster than it could be hurt.

## Zamaret's Bread, c. 612–601 BR

**Zamaret Ordan** belonged to the hereditary captains of **Oresh**, the Gateway's old overseas harbour on the inner sea. Around 612 BR he landed on the inner-sea side of Caleran's allied country with Gateway citizens, Oreshi crews, hired companies and a train of grain ships. He did not march on Caleran. He went from town to town offering relief from Caleran's levies and sacks of Gateway grain.

Several towns accepted. Their people ate well through a hard season. At the next count of the public stores, jars sealed full at harvest were found half empty, the seals unbroken and small bite marks in the wax. Caleran's priests said the Gateway's god was eating from the jars. Zamaret's officers blamed Atherian sabotage. The towns that had taken the grain went hungry while still owing the invader for his gift.

Caleran's armies tried to defend their allies and force a decisive battle. They were destroyed in 611 and again in 609. A loyal hill town could still choke the invader's supply after he had won a battle in the valley below it, and Zamaret spent much of the war besieging small places that would not open their gates.

### The Lustration of the Field, 609 BR

After the second army was lost, Caleran turned to the Red Star. On the festival ground that [[Ilaron and Tarsenna]] had built, where captives had been killed once before, the magistrate **Dema Varin** presided over the killing of captured Gateway soldiers and of the hostage children held since 646, boys and girls of about eight to sixteen. They were offered to Astaroth, the Red Star, "to give the Star a contestant". Atherian annals call the rite **the Lustration of the Field** and name no victims. The Gateway families kept the children's names, the first of their two lists.

The following year more allies changed sides and the war reached the islands and the inner gulf. The augurs drew a lesson from the failure, and it was not a moral one. The victims had been the wrong kind; the Red Star wanted a man who would stand against a named enemy. From 607 the field of battle could be accepted only by a living commander in person, the **Warden of the Red Field**, who might not quit the field while the enemy he had named still held it. No one in Caleran said the killing had been wrong.

### The city of the turned gate, c. 608 BR

In the inner gulf, an allied city kept a fortified refuge gate, a granary for strangers and a house of names where guests were registered. As Zamaret's allies gathered outside and the saying "do not eat the Gateway's bread" passed along the coast, the city's magistrates read the register of guests as a list of enemy agents. Guards dragged registered foreigners from their beds, separated mixed households, executed several families in the square and seized their grain. Others were driven out through the gate into the camps of the besiegers. Clerks altered the gate's covenant so that the dead appeared to have broken guest-law first.

Survivors said that the lamps of the refuge gate then went out one by one without smoke, and that the register in the gatehouse turned to plain parchment. The city fell before the season ended. Church tellers later called the darkness a heavenly withdrawal, Star priests called it a failed machine from the Giant age, and the city's name is not agreed. Its survivors carried roof tiles out of the houses that had taken part, and that procession is still repeated where a disgraced sanctuary is put to another use. [[G-38 - The Houses Unhoused before the Gate Turns|The Houses Unhoused before the Gate Turns]] preserves one telling.

### The stand below Thesra, 607–601 BR

In 607 **Celor Marath** became the first Warden of the Red Field. A Warden could not delay, and Marath did not. In 606 he offered battle on the approaches below **Thesra**, the Serathi hill town whose teachers had shaped Caleran's calendar, and held them. Atherian annals call the stand a victory. Thesran house-books count the dead, the burned olive terraces and the ancestor tombs used as breastworks, and call it a draw that Thesra paid for. Dema Varin, whose rite had made the office, lived to see it consume the armies he voted for.

The war was decided less on the field than in the storehouses. Towns that had taken Zamaret's grain refused the next shipment, and "do not eat the Gateway's bread" became the allies' proverb. The alliance held out of fear of the gift more than loyalty to Caleran. Zamaret withdrew in 601 with his army intact and his coalition gone. The settlement restricted the Gateway fleet and renewed the hostages with new children. Tammur stayed Atherian. Marath came home with a following that alarmed the senate's older houses.

## The fast recovery and the bill, c. 600–540 BR

Qarzeth recovered faster than anyone could explain. Its granaries filled while those of its dependent towns emptied. At Esharat and Belqara, jars sealed at harvest were found short in spring, again with the seals intact. Around 551 the custodians of [[Ilamar before 350 BR|Ilamar]], the sanctuary island whose officers certified grain measures for half the sea, recorded the Esharati and Belqaran findings and refused to certify the Provider's grain. Orsen Valeor and Mera Castren later cited the same record for opposite purposes.

The Provider's sanctuary demanded more with every year of plenty. By the 550s its doors opened only when a living child's name had been entered on the collectors' household lists. The [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth|Years of Three Collections]], 548–540 BR, were the bill coming due. Families hid children in orchards, cargo and relatives' houses. **Ilya of Qarzeth** copied the names of imprisoned children from the sanctuary stores and escaped in a grain cart. **Sapha of Esharat**, a midwife, organised carriers and farming households into a rescue route; collectors killed her husband and took her daughter **Neral**, who was sacrificed in Qarzeth. The border kingdom of **Hadrath**, under King Harzun, held with the capital.

## The Rope Camp stoppage, c. 536–531 BR

The towns answered with their carts. Carriers and boat crews from **Belqara**, **Esharat**, **Hazreth** and **Sarqen** camped on the rope-makers' ground outside Qarzeth and stopped carrying grain and children into the capital. The camp became known as **Rope Camp**. For the first time in living memory the capital's markets ran short.

**Damen**, a transport officer, carried the council's first terms into the camp. Collectors were caught following his escort in, and the camp's harder men seized him. Some camp leaders killed the collectors they caught and tortured others to make reconciliation impossible. **Yasra of Belqara**, speaking for districts already stripped by the collections, stayed with the camp without accepting its murders.

**Meleq Ordan**, of Oresh's captains, broke the camp with the help of **King Harzun of Hadrath**, and married Harzun's daughter **Sapha of Hadrath**, no kin to the midwife of Esharat. Government forces executed camp leaders and killed or enslaved the dependants they captured. The reprisals drove the towns to Caleran. Families also kept more intimate inheritances: unpaid carriage, relatives who never came home, and neighbours killed by people with whom they would have to trade again.

## The last war, c. 526–520 BR

Fugitive families carried Ilya's list to Atherian ports. In Caleran the elderly senator **Orsen Valeor** stood with survivors beside him and demanded that the child-taking city fall. He made the demand in a city that had killed Gateway children within his grandfather's lifetime, and the exiles answered him with the names of the hostages of 609. **Mera Castren** drew the opposite lesson from the same festival ground: Caleran had stopped, so Qarzeth could stop too, under inspectors and permanent hostages. Valeor answered that the rooms would reopen the day the inspectors sailed. His coalition gathered people who wanted rescued children, safer shipping, land, profit and glory.

Caleran first demanded the captives and the dismantling of the sacrificial establishment, and Qarzeth surrendered weapons and hostages. Then Caleran demanded the god. The people were to carry the Provider's bronze face out of the sanctuary and break it on the shore. The assembly refused. Even citizens who hated the collections had eaten from the Provider's granaries all their lives, and opponents of sacrifice defended their houses beside the cult's patrons. The lesser towns offered the Atherians food and passage in return for their survival.

The blockade lasted five campaigning seasons. With the towns' carts stopped, the famous granaries emptied for the first time anyone could remember; the priests killed more children, and the granaries stayed empty. Hunger took children, the old and enslaved labourers first. Belqara and Esharat fed the besiegers, and Sarqen took in refugees and repaired allied ships. Valeor died before the final assault. When the wall was breached in 520, Ilya led a detachment to the holding rooms, where soldiers found living children beside cremated remains and her list put names to the dead. Then the bronze face broke. Atherian annals credit a soldier with an axe; Esharat credits its own midwife Sapha and her carriers; Ilya's surviving fragment says only that "it broke when the last name was spoken".

Through the rest of the city Atherian soldiers murdered residents, raped captives and herded survivors for sale, and their commanders let them. Rescued children were torn from their families again when the prisoners were divided, and some were sold. Captains from the wandering harbour-city of [[Marelith]] bought them cheap and resold many to Esharat's ransom funds at a profit, and Ilya cursed those captains by name. A few days later, at Sarqen's landing, the people the war had spared shared one meal at which no one was served by rank and no seat was kept empty. Its rules became the spared towns' **Table Oath**: *no child for the table; no seat kept empty; no guest served by worth.* [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth]] follows the siege and the rescue in full, and [[The Spared Towns and the Queen's Country]] the meal and the towns' oath.

## Burning and salting

Caleran's senate had decided that Qarzeth would never again rule the Gateway country. Engineers burned and razed the capital, spoiled its cisterns, broke its channels and carted off its stone. At the spared towns' request they salted the sanctuary, the gardens and the store compounds with salt from the Gateway's own pans, where the plenty had grown. Atherian annals record a punishment. Gateway tradition calls it closing the table, and says that "the salt of the table and the salt of the ruin were one salt".

The salted ground died. The fields beyond it went on bearing, and the lesser towns kept their wells, workshops and landings. Parents who had welcomed the end of the sacrifices also cursed the soldiers who had sold their neighbours' children, and the spared towns kept both memories.

The records of the sack do not agree. Caleran's rolls name no officer for the sale of rescued children; Esharat's ransom registers name officers whom no Caleran roll places at the siege. Atherian archivists blamed Gateway forgers and Esharati elders blamed Caleran's clerks. Later historians point to the Compact's archive, which lay in a highland valley where the rolls of the war were being copied when the valley was lost, and whose name the copies themselves disagree about.

## Consequences for the two countries

The spared towns became the centre of [[The Spared Towns and the Queen's Country|the country that followed]]. Their farms, small ports and kinship sustained the people who remained and those who returned, and a later crown at Sarqen ruled them only after swearing their oath. The ruined capital stayed a salted field at the edge of their country.

Caleran gained revenue, access to the inner sea and wider opportunities for its patrons. Commanders rewarded followers, suppliers enlarged their businesses, and captives entered estates and town households. Boatmen called out season after season for the fleets learned to bargain, and at Nerava they refused to load. Caleran kept the Warden's office among its rites, waiting for a commander bold enough to accept it, and the Gateway towns kept the hostages' names. Both reappear in [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]].
