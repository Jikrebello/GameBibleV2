# Darion Velsar

Darion Velsar (408–350 BR) was a commander of Teren Casvilar's armies, one of the Four at the Stores who governed Caleran after Casvilar's murder, and the principal eastern rival of Aurelius Thalion. He won the largest battle of the succession wars and lost the war to a man who never came to fight him. In 353 he accepted a Red Field of his own on the salt pans below Sarqen, and he died on it three years later.

## Soldier and companion

Velsar's standing grew through service under Casvilar. Soldiers knew his physical confidence, readiness to share food and ability to speak to a tired company without ceremonial distance. He enjoyed music and performance at a gathering and could reward a subordinate with conspicuous generosity.

Those qualities helped hold troops together. They also encouraged dependence on his personal attention. A commander who pleased him might obtain support quickly, while anger or distraction could leave a difficult request unanswered. He was capable of sustained military work when an immediate campaign concentrated his interests.

His wife Miretha Ardel managed property, received officers' families and maintained connections during his absences. Her political activity drew on resources of her own. Their household became an important centre for people whose prospects depended on continued military employment.

## Inheritance and public power

After Casvilar's assassination in 370 BR, Velsar possessed seniority, papers and the loyalty of experienced officers. He claimed that Casvilar's place on the Red Field had passed to him as senior lieutenant. Thalion refused the Field outright. In 367 Seluc Varroth ruled that it could pass to no one and stood open while any of Casvilar's seven challengers lived. Velsar joined Thalion, Varroth and the speaker Belisar Venn as the Four at the Stores, governing from the store-house above the muster ground because armies that could not disband still had to be fed.

The soldiers would not murder citizens. Velsar proposed the Writing-In: the partners' enemies were entered in the register of challengers, so that killing them on the muster ground counted as war on the open Field. He asked for Venn's name, because Venn had opposed it and kept the river stores. The partners authorised that killing and many others. Confiscated estates helped pay the armies.

The seven themselves would not fight. Velsar broke the army their friends raised at Darsil in 363, the largest battle of the wars, and found none of the seven among the dead or the prisoners. He took the eastern commands and the royal clients afterwards.

## Darsil and Miretha

An army that could not disband could not be settled on land either, and Velsar quartered his victorious companies on Darsil. The town starved through the winter of 362–361 and rose in the spring. His brother Tarsen, commanding the garrison, took the remaining grain into the citadel and hanged eleven organisers in the market. Velsar's wife Miretha Ardel came east with carts of grain bought from her own estates, fed the town for a month and died there of the winter fever in 360. Velsar sent neither the order nor the grain, and no countermand.

In 358 he marched north-east into the approaches of Avesh, the Vasharan road-city that had sheltered Caleran's fugitives and rebel captains since Vespen Harod's day. Vasharan relay riders carried word of his columns ahead of them. The Avesh command moved its reserves along the Dawn Road faster than his baggage could follow, took his supply convoys at a bridge station, and left the rest to the weather; early snow closed the pass behind him, and he came back with half the animals he had taken out. Tarsen did not come back. On the retreat Velsar seized people and stores from a smaller allied ruler whom he blamed for the defeat. The allies who heard of it did not forget, and the next time he asked for help they counted their own granaries first.

## Samyra and the eastern settlement

Samyra's kingdom supplied grain, vessels and credit. Velsar offered military protection against her neighbours and against Thalion, who had married her elder sister Merath. Their relationship developed personal attachments alongside those purposes, and their children Talven and Lethia were born in 357 and 354.

At Samyra's request, Velsar's officers seized Merath at Esharat in 358, days after she had given birth to Thalion's son, and executed her. The women of Esharat hid the child and the town would not give him up. Thalion never forgave Velsar or the queen.

Velsar's officers also executed Ardel Veyr in 354, when the Avesh command gave him up. By then Velsar's own veterans were deserting to Thalion. Many of them had served under Casvilar, and their general was protecting the queen, who kept Mavren Seneth, Casvilar's murderer, at the spared towns' table.

## The Salt Field

In 353 Velsar answered the deserters with the rite they understood. On the salt pans below Sarqen he dedicated his arms before an altar of the Red Star and named Thalion and the admiral Istran Melvo as his enemies. His augurs proclaimed that the Star had accepted him. He expected Thalion to be forced to meet him, since no Atherian commander, he believed, could keep an army after refusing a named enemy in person.

Thalion never came. His companies fought Velsar's outposts behind painted boards of his face, and Velsar, bound to a field whose named enemy still contested it, could not leave the salt. His army had to be fed where it stood. The queen's levies on the towns to feed it, which the towns called the Fourth Collection, turned the country against her.

In the spring of 350 the towns put Samyra's house out, and within a month nobody would feed Velsar's army. It scattered. He stayed on the salt with a few officers who would not leave him. Thalion meant to leave him there until he renounced his claim before the Red Star's priests. Melvo, the other man Velsar had named, would not leave a soldier to that. He came ashore with forty crewmen and walked out onto the salt in person. Velsar went to meet him and was killed with a sword in his hand.

His children by Miretha were raised by her kin. Talven and Lethia stayed with their mother at Esharat as guests of the spared towns.

Velsar's reputation remained divided. Comrades remembered the commander who shared their food and died on the field he chose. Darsil remembered the gallows, the Gateway towns the Fourth Collection, and Caleran the Writing-In he had devised.
