# Darion Velsar

Darion Velsar (408–350 BR) was a commander of Teren Casvilar's armies, one of the Four at the Stores who governed Caleran after Casvilar's murder, and the principal eastern rival of Aurelius Thalion. He won the largest battle of the succession wars and lost the war to a man who never came to fight him. In 353 he accepted a Red Field of his own on the salt pans below Sarqen, and he died on it three years later.

## Soldier and companion

Velsar's standing grew through service under Casvilar. Soldiers knew his physical confidence, readiness to share food and ability to speak to a tired company without ceremonial distance. He enjoyed music and performance at a gathering and could reward a subordinate with conspicuous generosity.

Those qualities helped hold troops together. They also encouraged dependence on his personal attention. A commander who pleased him might obtain support quickly, while anger or distraction could leave a difficult request unanswered. He was capable of sustained military work when an immediate campaign concentrated his interests.

His wife Miretha Ardel managed property, received officers' families and maintained connections during his absences. Her political activity drew on resources of her own. Their household became an important centre for people whose prospects depended on continued military employment.

## Inheritance and public power

After Casvilar's assassination in 370 BR, Velsar possessed seniority, papers and the loyalty of experienced officers. He claimed that Casvilar's place on the Red Field had passed to him as senior lieutenant, and he went after the killers' friends at once. The seven themselves would not fight. In the summer of 369 Velsar broke the army their friends had raised at Darsil, the largest battle of the wars, and found none of the seven among the dead or the prisoners. He paid off two old companies on the field, and they were back under the standards by spring, having carried the war home to their own valley. No commander disbanded a company after that.

In 367 Seluc Varroth ruled that the Field could pass to no one and stood fouled while any of Casvilar's seven challengers lived. The victor of Darsil had closed nothing. Velsar joined Thalion, Varroth and the speaker Besral Venn as the Four at the Stores, governing from the store-house above the muster ground because armies that could not disband still had to be fed, and he took the eastern commands and the royal clients as his share.

The soldiers would not murder citizens. Velsar proposed the Writing-In: the partners' enemies were entered in the register of challengers, so that killing them on the muster ground counted as war on the fouled Field. Confiscated estates helped pay the armies. In 366 he asked for Venn's name, because Venn had opposed the device and kept the river stores. Venn walked down to the muster ground with a practice sword, and the squad sent to kill him would not do it. Venn kept the stores. From that winter the river barges reached Velsar's companies late and short, and he fed his armies from the eastern ports and from Sarqen.

## Darsil, Miretha and the Dawn Road

An army that could not disband could not be settled on land either, and Velsar quartered his victorious companies on Darsil. The town fed them through one winter, went short through the next and starved through the third, and rose in the spring of 366. His brother Tarsen, commanding the garrison, took the remaining grain into the citadel and hanged eleven organisers in the market. Velsar's wife Miretha Ardel came east that summer with carts of grain bought from her own estates. She came back every autumn for ten years and died at Darsil of the winter fever in 356, with nothing left to sell. Velsar sent neither the order nor the grain, and no countermand.

In the spring of 354 he marched on Avesh, the Vasharan road-city that had sheltered Caleran's fugitives and rebel captains since Vespen Harod's day, to seize the one of the seven who lived there. His veterans were deserting to Thalion because their general guarded Casvilar's murderer at the queen's table, and he meant to bring them the other murderer with his own hands. The Vasharan governor received him politely and regretted that the guest had left the day before. Velsar followed him up the Dawn Road through a whole summer. At every relay house the hearth was warm and the man a stage ahead, carried from post to post by riders with fresh horses, and Velsar turned back when the remounts he had bought on the way out were offered to him again at twice the price. He had fought no battle and lost no company. He had been outrun by a road, with an army watching, and the camps sang the stations in order.

## Samyra and the eastern settlement

Samyra's kingdom supplied grain, vessels and credit. Velsar offered military protection against her neighbours and against Thalion, who had married her elder sister Merath. From 358 the queen held Merath and Thalion's infant son in her palace, which kept Thalion a quiet partner for five years and suited Velsar well. After the Dawn Road he needed the queen more than she needed him. Their children Talven and Lethia were born in 353 and 351.

His veterans went on deserting. Many of them had served under Casvilar, and their general was protecting a queen who kept Mavren Seneth, Casvilar's murderer, at the spared towns' table.

## The Salt Field

In the autumn of 353 Velsar answered the deserters with the rite they understood. On the salt pans below Sarqen he dedicated his arms before an altar of the Red Star and named Thalion and the admiral Istran Melvo as his enemies. Seluc Varroth, First Celebrant of the Common Sky, came east from Caleran with the register of challengers to witness it, proclaimed that the Star had accepted him, and stayed. Velsar expected Thalion to be forced to meet him, since no Atherian commander, he believed, could keep an army after refusing a named enemy in person.

Thalion never came. His companies fought Velsar's outposts behind painted boards of his face, and Velsar, bound to a field whose named enemy still contested it, could not leave the salt. His army had to be fed where it stood. The queen's levies on the towns to feed it, which the towns called the Fourth Collection, turned the country against her. In the winter of 352 King Orvaz sent the Avesh fugitive's body down from the Dawn Road packed in salt, with a letter saying the man had fallen from a remount, and Varroth struck the sixth name on the Salt Field. It came two years too late to hold a single company.

In the spring of 350 the towns put Samyra's house out, and within a month nobody would feed Velsar's army. It scattered. He stayed on the salt with his brother Tarsen, the First Celebrant and a few officers who would not leave him. Thalion meant to leave him there until he renounced his claim before the Red Star's priests. Melvo, the other man Velsar had named, would not leave a soldier to that. He came ashore with forty crewmen and walked out onto the salt in person. Velsar went to meet him and was killed with a sword in his hand, and Tarsen and Varroth died beside him.

His children by Miretha were raised by her kin. Talven and Lethia stayed with their mother at Esharat as guests of the spared towns.

Velsar's reputation remained divided. Comrades remembered the commander who shared their food and died on the field he chose. Darsil remembered the gallows, the Gateway towns the Fourth Collection, and Caleran the Writing-In he had devised.
