# Atherian Imperial Age

The Atherian Imperial Age is the period when the Atherian city-state synthesis becomes continental empire.

## What It Inherits

The empire inherits:

- city-state law from [[Age of City-States]],
- star religion from [[Age of Stars]],
- roads, archives, and civic legitimacy from [[The First Atherian City-States]],
- stolen and purified Giant knowledge from [[Age of Chains]],
- distorted Exodus memory from [[Age of Exodus]].

## What It Creates

Atheria creates the imperial board that later cultures fight over: roads, provinces, archives, ruins, legions, star temples, imperial law, and the public stage on which the Redeemer appears.

Its oldest and densest infrastructure accumulated in the land now forming Thalmyria. The modern heartland is therefore not merely a successor state's territory but a vertical record of pre-Atherian cities, republican expansion, imperial monumental growth, conversion, occupation, and return. See [[The Layers Beneath Thalmyria]].

## Atlas narrative

The following account distinguishes established history from the received stories named within it.

## Empire from an older world

The unification around 350 BR gathered centuries of Atherian settlement, maritime rivalry, cities and republican experiment. Aurelius Thalion belongs to that later achievement. He did not lead the first departure from the Glass, invent the coastal cities or make imperial subjects one bloodline.

Imperial government enlarged a system which could admit outsiders while keeping them unequal. Service, language, citizenship, landholding and ancestry overlapped without becoming identical. A provincial family could supply officers for three generations and still be called foreign in the capital. Another could acquire civic standing through military settlement and lose its farm when a later governor changed the levy.

## The machinery people lived inside

Roads shortened some journeys and made armies more dangerous. Provincial granaries saved cities during failed harvests, but the officials who distributed food could demand land or labour in return. The Keeper of Borrowed Grain tradition recalls a minority administrator whose famine policy preserved lives while transferring farms to the ruler and turning independent households into dependants. Both outcomes belong to the story.

Markets connected wine, grain, wool, metals, timber, slaves and luxury craft. Veterans became settlers. Captives became domestic workers, quarry labourers, tutors and sometimes freed citizens. Rural obligations kept the cities supplied; civic festivals made that dependence seem like a natural harmony. The empire's celebrated order rested on work its monuments rarely named.

## The low door

The [[G-09 - The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door|Low Door Companions]] began in provincial service households. Their rise joined skill and friendship to hostage-taking, unlawful killing and the betrayal of a refuge. Later imperial Lives removed the blood from the ascent because successful outsiders made useful examples of imperial generosity.

The important historical pattern is larger than one company. Advancement was real. So were the people sacrificed to it. A cook could become a commander without the kitchen children becoming free; a hostage could learn the court's language and later use it to order hostages taken from another people.

## Imperial religion and its victims

The Star Pantheon provided civic ritual and a language of cosmic hierarchy. Imperial cults also standardized the dead ruler's face. Some ceremonies were theatre, some concealed fraud, and some opened genuine feeding routes for Idol Masks. An answering image was therefore not necessarily the emperor, even when it repeated his words.

Different provincial histories remembered the same victory differently. The capital praised a road and pacified frontier; local accounts recorded burned villages, forced labour and bodies beneath the embankment. These are not merely rival rhetorical emphases. A truthful history must preserve what the victorious inscription omitted.

## Inheritance beyond the empire

The Age of Glory left buildings, techniques and an idea of public order that later peoples could not simply discard. It also left fear that human empire was learning to resemble the Giants. Its achievements and cruelty became the setting into which [[The Age of the Redeemer]] entered.

Modern Thalmyrics and Eclessians do not correspond to two ancient biological halves of Atheria. They inherited different regions, imperial layers, conversions, occupations and mixtures. Their later quarrels over Rome-like legitimacy cannot be projected backward into the first war-settlement leagues.
