# Samyra Nerethan

**Samyra Nerethan** (392–333 BR) ruled the gateway kingdom of Sarqen during the wars that ended the Caleran republic. The spared towns' oath gave her the throne in 373 and took it away in 350. Between those years she made her country indispensable to Atherian armies, allied with Teren Casvilar and then Darion Velsar, kept her elder sister and her sister's son in her palace and Casvilar's murderer at her table. When the towns put her house out, she sat down at their table as a stranger, where no one could take her from it, and lived seventeen more years at Esharat.

## A country of surviving towns

Samyra belonged to the fifth generation of the House of Nerethan. Its founder had arrived from Delkos as an Atherian harbour commander and married Hadrima of Esharat in 497 BR. His family carried the naval and royal culture of the eastern Successor states. Hadrima's maternal grandmother Lemara had married the junior Qahuri prince Tazirun Zaheret around 548 BR. Samyra therefore inherited Sacred Basin kin and ceremonial customs through the women of Esharat. The Nerethan descendants obtained hereditary recognition and governed a country whose lesser towns had survived the destruction of its former republican capital.

Sarqen, the enlarged Second Harbour, was the royal seat. Food came from cultivated districts, storage towns and longer commercial connections. The kingdom's resources depended on maintaining relations with people who remembered their communities' survival before the dynasty arose.

Those people had their own oath, older than the crown. After Qarzeth fell in 520 BR, the survivors of the sack had eaten together at Sarqen's landing, each bringing what food they had, with no one served by rank and no seat kept empty. The rules of that meal became the **Table Oath** of the spared towns: *no child for the table; no seat kept empty; no guest served by worth.* Every Nerethan ruler since Arenes in 457 had eaten at the towns' unranked table and sworn it before the towns would recognise a king. Samyra swore it at seventeen on her father's death in 375, and again when the towns restored her.

Samyra used the Atherian court speech of her dynasty, the languages of the Gateway towns and the Qahuri ritual forms the house had kept since Hadrima's day. At Sarqen she appeared in harbour and spared-town regalia; before Qahuri embassies she used the forms of Hadrima's river kin; in Atherian diplomacy she presented the military inheritance of Delkos. She kept the towns close with festivals, household visits and grain in bad years, and she was said to know the elders of forty towns by name and their carters by their mules.

## Exclusion and return

Her father Olymes died in 375 BR. Samyra, then about seventeen, entered joint rule with her younger brother Nethar. The minister Vardek and part of the garrison supported Nethar and excluded her from the port government. Their older sister Merath gathered a separate following.

Samyra retained contacts among Esharat households and sought outside assistance. In 373 the old Atherian commander Marovan Veyr, whose eastern government had lived on the kingdom's grain for four years, sailed into Sarqen to ask for more, and Vardek, hoping to please Veyr's enemy Teren Casvilar, invited him to land and eat at the royal table. The table was laid with an empty seat. An Esharati elder at the landing warned Veyr, who turned his ship around and fled to sanctuary on Ilamar.

The empty seat cost Vardek the country. A minister who laid a table to kill a guest had broken the oath every ruler of the house had sworn, and the spared towns rose for Samyra. Casvilar, who needed Esharat's grain for his armies, backed the side that already held it. Town elders tried Vardek under the oath at Sarqen's landing, where it had first been sworn; Casvilar's officers carried out the sentence. Nethar's supporters fought on, and her brother died in 372.

The restoration cost lives and increased dependence on Atherian support. Samyra also recovered authority through people who had maintained her cause while she lacked the port, and she knew it: her throne had been given back to her by the towns' oath before it was secured by Casvilar's soldiers. Her relationship with Casvilar produced Hadram in 372. His acknowledgment gave the child a distinguished parentage; Casvilar's civic inheritance went separately to Thalion.

## Government and the guest at the table

Samyra's government maintained grain storage, port income and the movement of supplies. Favour to one town could draw resentment from another, particularly when military demands required exceptional contributions. Her household rewarded useful managers and kept the ceremonial obligations inherited from the spared towns.

In 362 Mavren Seneth, one of the seven men who had entered their names as Casvilar's challengers and killed him, came to Sarqen's landing on a carter's wagon and sat down at the towns' unranked table. The oath forbade anyone to sell a stranger from that table, and Samyra kept him. She had reasons besides the oath. While any of the seven lived, the Atherians believed the Red Field stood open and none of their commanders dared disband an army, and armies that could not disband bought her grain. When the Atherian partners demanded Seneth in 361 she answered with the oath. Her son Hadram, Casvilar's son, grew up watching his father's killer eat at his mother's table.

## Merath and the child

Samyra's elder sister Merath, passed over in 373, married Aurelius Thalion in 360 and gave Caleran's other great commander a claim on Sarqen. In 359 Merath went to Esharat to bear her child where the heirs of the house were born. Her son Meren was born there in 358, and before midsummer the queen's officers came with a litter and two hundred spearmen to bring mother and child to the palace. The women of Esharat would not hand the boy over at the door. The elders sat the officers down at the town's table and told them that the oath forbade a child for the table: the queen might keep her sister's son, and she would show him at the landing every spring, where the towns could see that he was whole.

Samyra kept the bargain for eight years. Merath lived in a wing of the palace with a guard on the stair, and Meren grew up in the royal household, where Hadram was already a youth and where Talven and Lethia later shared his nursery. Each spring he sat at the renewal, and the elders of Esharat looked him over as they looked over their sealed jars. Whether Samyra meant him for a hostage, an heir or a nephew is disputed. He stayed Thalion's hand for five years, which is what a hostage is for; the queen's partisans said she had also taught him his letters herself. Merath died in the palace of fever in the blockade winter of 351. People who had served her blamed the guarded stair as much as the hunger.

## Velsar and the Salt Field

Samyra supplied Velsar with food, ships and credit in exchange for protection against her neighbours and against Thalion. In 354 the treasury advocate Corven Ilar, whom most of Caleran took for Thalion's voice, asked the Caleran assembly to declare her an enemy, calling her heir of the child-burning city, keeper of Casvilar's murderer and gaoler of Thalion's wife and son. Spared-town envoys answered him before the assembly. They recited the Table Oath their queen had sworn, *no child for the table*, and then read out the names of the Gateway hostage children whom Caleran had killed on its own festival ground in 609 BR to win the Red Star's favour. Thalion stood while the names were read, and the assembly did not vote. The answer was remembered in the towns for centuries.

That same summer Velsar marched up the Dawn Road after one of Casvilar's killers and came back without him, and from then on he needed the queen more than she needed him. Their children Talven and Lethia were born in 353 and 351 and joined Hadram and Meren in the royal household.

In the autumn of 353 Velsar accepted a Red Field of his own on the salt pans below Sarqen and named Thalion and the admiral Istran Melvo as his enemies. Thalion would not come, and Velsar was bound to the salt with his army. The war the assembly had declined to vote arrived at the queen's door all the same, and her own ally had brought it.

## The Fourth Collection and the basin

An army that could not leave its field had to be fed on it. When Melvo closed Sarqen's anchorage in 352, the queen's officers took grain, animals and young men from the towns for the Salt Field, by what each household could be made to give, while the officers' tables on the field stayed full. The towns called it the Fourth Collection, after the collections of Qarzeth. That autumn the carters of Esharat and Belqara found their sealed jars a third empty and the wax bitten from beneath. The towns said the Provider had come back to a table that served by worth.

In 351 the queen's pilots secured a current through a sea bargain to bring grain hulls from the Qahuri river in past Melvo's line to Hazreth, at the price of surrendered cargo and a disused galley basin opened to flooding. The sea took a third of the grain over the side in sight of the mole. The wounded from the Salt Field and the harbour fights lay in the basin's sheds, and carts had been assigned to move them. Officers used the carts for the grain and left the wounded on the quay as the water rose. The grain went to the Salt Field and the officers' tables. The royal account described the passage without naming the people left behind, and the towns heard of them from the men who had lain on the quay.

## The filled seat and the landing

In the spring of 350 Hadram killed Seneth at the towns' renewal of the oath with the knife he had brought to cut bread. The towns would not harm a son of the house, but they put the house out. When the table was laid again, the queen's seat was filled by Tobal of Hazreth, a rower left on the basin quay. Without the towns Samyra had no grain, no carters and no gates. Hadram fled to sanctuary on Ilamar. Velsar's army melted away, and Melvo killed him on the Salt Field.

When Thalion's ships came to Sarqen that summer, the towns laid the boards at the landing for him, and Samyra walked down from the palace with Talven, Lethia and her sister's son and sat among the carters as a stranger. Thalion had meant to take her to Caleran. He sat three places from her, with a son he had never seen on the bench between them, ate, and swore the oath that forbade him to take her from the table. The kingdom ended, and Sarqen passed under an imperial governor. Meren went home with his father.

## Esharat

Samyra lived the rest of her life at Esharat, in the town of her ancestor Hadrima, as a guest of the towns. She raised Talven and Lethia there and came to the renewal at the landing every spring. For seventeen years she ate at the same boards as the rower her officers had left on the basin quay. She dictated an account of her reign that praised the towns, named Merath's ambitions and passed quickly over the basin. She died at Esharat in 333 BR.

Her memory divided as her reign had. Royal partisans said she had shamed the house by sitting down below a rower. Atherian accounts said she had hidden behind a child. In the towns' songs she was the queen who came home to the table, though the same songs remembered the guarded stair, the Fourth Collection, the grain poured into the sea and the men left on the quay.
