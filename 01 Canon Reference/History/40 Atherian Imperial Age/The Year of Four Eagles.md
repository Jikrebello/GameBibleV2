# The Year of Four Eagles

The Year of Four Eagles was the succession war of 247–246 BR which ended the House of Thalion and brought Samor Veloran to power. Four claimants controlled different parts of the imperial system. Their conflict lasted less than two years, but executions, requisitions and the transfer of settlements changed provincial relationships for a generation.

## The four claims

Avaren Mott, senior magistrate at Caleran, received recognition from the senate and part of the palace guard. He promised to restore appointments made before Olarion's purges. His authority depended on the capital and the impression that government continued there.

Daran Thalion, a relative of Cassara, held estates and veteran support in the western military districts. He claimed family right and accused Mott of concealing Olarion's murder. Households that had lost property under the last emperor joined him in expectation of restitution.

Aster Halven commanded ships and eastern garrisons. Provincial ports supported him because he could keep their routes open and promised to reduce Caleran's arrears. His strength came from long provincial service and command of the fleet.

Samor Veloran commanded the central river army and its grain corridor. His father had entered imperial service through a provincial municipal household. Samor promised pay, the confirmation of local officers and an end to court denunciations.

## War along the supplies

Mott attempted to govern before he possessed the means to compel the provinces. The palace treasury could pay the guard for a time. Grain convoys still passed through forces answering to Veloran or Halven, and both commanders withheld them.

Daran marched towards the Aurin and seized food from towns unwilling to declare for him. One town opened its stores after his men took the families of councillors as hostages. Veloran defeated him before he reached Caleran. Daran surrendered under a promise of safe custody and was executed several days later on Samor's order.

Halven moved against the river ports. His fleet won an initial engagement and burned military stores, together with a neighbouring working quarter. Melvo's earlier naval system had divided command among ports capable of continuing after one loss; local officers now used those arrangements against Halven. He died during a later battle, and his surviving captains negotiated separately.

## Caleran and the settlement

Food prices rose sharply at Caleran. Mott authorised searches for hoarded grain and allowed soldiers to requisition from suspected houses. Poorer districts experienced the searches most heavily. The death of demonstrators outside an inner market weakened his claim to civic legitimacy.

Veloran entered the capital after promising the guard its arrears. Mott was killed by soldiers while attempting to reach sanctuary. Samor punished some of the killers and rewarded their unit, actions serving different needs within the same week.

In 246, the senate and principal commands recognised Samor I. He buried the four claimants with unequal honours and married his daughter Tessa to a surviving member of the old imperial household. Towns that had supplied him received charters and tax relief; those associated with his rivals supplied settlements for soldiers.

## Later memory

The four eagles remained a common image for ambition feeding from one body. Plays made the claimants into recognisable types: the talkative magistrate, injured prince, proud admiral and patient soldier. Provincial accounts retained the names of people whose grain and relatives had become part of the contest.

All four claimants depended on the same roads, treasuries and cities. Their inability to govern those resources together produced the war, and Veloran's victory restored a single imperial command.
