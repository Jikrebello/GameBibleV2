# The House of Thalion

The House of Thalion was the first imperial dynasty of Atheria. It included the founder, his adopted successor Meren I, Empress Cassara I and Olarion. The house governed for slightly more than a century, turning a victorious military coalition into a court capable of surviving an ordinary succession. Its end showed that family descent alone could not command armies spread across several regions.

## Meren I, 318–286 BR

Meren was born in 358 BR to Evara Thalion and Darion Velsar. His childhood passed among the dependants of rival households. After 350, Evara also cared for Talven and Lethia, her husband's children by Samyra Nerethan. Shared tutors and ceremonies taught the children the language of reconciliation while guards and separate attendants preserved their different positions.

Thalion adopted Meren after Caelor's death. At his accession in 318, Meren already knew the officers, priests and household managers who sustained government. He confirmed military pay, retained Ilvara's experienced staff and gave Talven a supervised appointment at Sarqen.

Meren preferred meetings with a small number of prepared speakers. He enjoyed horse races and funded teams from provincial cities, creating a popular circuit whose rivalries sometimes ended in street violence. His government punished organisers of riots while continuing the contests that drew the crowds.

He reduced several emergency levies and confirmed town charters. Southern campaigning combined treaties with a succession of short punitive expeditions. Enslaved captives and confiscated land remained sources of reward. Meren died in 286 after naming his daughter Cassara as successor.

## Cassara I, 286–263 BR

Cassara had governed the Aurin stores and represented her father during illness. The army at Caleran recognised her, and provincial commands followed after receiving confirmation of their appointments. She became the first woman to hold the imperial command in her own name.

Her reign enlarged water storage, harbour granaries and the paved approaches to several provincial capitals. Cassara travelled with engineers and developed a reputation for asking workers to explain a problem before officials described the solution. Communities benefited from works that brought cleaner water and more reliable transport.

Construction drew labour and material from subject districts. A quarry revolt in 274 BR began after managers extended compulsory service through the harvest. Troops killed strikers and their families were expelled from housing attached to the works. Cassara dismissed two managers, retained the compulsory programme and completed the aqueduct.

Her household supported musicians, astronomers and comic performers. Provincial artists acquired commissions at Caleran and brought imperial styles home. Cassara died in 263, leaving the government to her son Olarion.

## Olarion, 263–247 BR

Olarion inherited a court rich in honours and suspicious of any commander who acquired independent popularity. He enjoyed mechanical birds, private performances and elaborate night processions through the palace. Craftspeople produced ingenious works for him while treasury officials struggled to meet the cost.

An eastern commander who returned without permission was executed. Olarion later ordered the deaths of two relatives after accusations that their household rites concealed a succession gathering. Confiscations rewarded informers and drove other families towards frontier armies willing to shelter them.

Food shortages at Caleran in 252 brought demonstrations. The palace released grain while soldiers killed people attempting to enter a closed store. Olarion's subsequent games quieted the city without restoring the families of the dead.

The emperor died suddenly in 247. Accounts variously blamed illness, poison and a frightened attendant. No explanation became conclusive. Four claimants received support before the funeral rites were complete.

## Dynastic memory

Later rulers retained Thalion's name within their titles and represented Meren and Cassara as peaceful continuators. Olarion became the wicked relative against whom the wisdom of the other three could be displayed. His government nevertheless used officers, tax arrangements and ceremonies built by his predecessors.

The dynasty possessed ordinary human ancestry. Adoption, marriage and public recognition made Meren a political son of Thalion; each man retained his complete human soul. The later imperial cult worked through portraits and ritual patterns, while the dead rulers remained distinct human persons.
