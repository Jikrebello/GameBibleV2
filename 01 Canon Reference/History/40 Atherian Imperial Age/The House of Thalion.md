# The House of Thalion

The House of Thalion was the first imperial dynasty of Atheria. Its emperors were Aurelius Thalion, Meren I, Valeran I and Olarion. It governed for slightly more than a century. Meren was Thalion's own son and succeeded him without adoption; one adoption kept supreme command in the household when Meren had no son of his own. Through Meren's mother the house also carried the blood of the Nerethan kings of Sarqen. Its end showed that even a recognised family claim could fail when armies and suppliers answered to rival patrons.

## Meren I, 318–286 BR

Meren was born at Esharat in 358 BR to Aurelius Thalion and Merath Nerethan, elder sister of Queen Samyra of Sarqen. His mother was executed at Samyra's request within days of his birth. The women of Esharat hid him in a grain bin, the town refused to give him up, and carters brought him west under a load of barley. His grandmother Lysara raised him at Caleran beside his half-brother Caelor and half-sister Lysara. At eight he crossed to Sarqen with his father and sat at the spared towns' table across from the women who had hidden him.

Caelor's death in 324 made Meren his father's heir. At his accession in 318, at the table on Sarqen's landing where his father died, he swore the Table Oath before he sailed home, and he already knew the officers, priests and household managers who sustained government. He confirmed military pay, retained his aunt Evara's experienced staff and gave his cousin Talven, Samyra's son, a supervised appointment in Sarqen's harbour administration. His half-sister's party, which had expected her children to inherit, never forgave him for being born.

Meren preferred meetings with a small number of prepared speakers. He enjoyed horse races and funded teams from provincial cities, creating a popular circuit whose rivalries sometimes ended in street violence. His government punished organisers of riots while continuing the contests that drew the crowds. The new stands at the Caleran course were among the first public works opened under Thalion's rule of the test: wardens loaded them with sandbags until a cross-beam split, the carpenters replaced it, and the city prefect sat alone in the front row through the first race before anyone else was allowed in.

He reduced several emergency levies and confirmed town charters. Southern campaigning combined treaties with a succession of short punitive expeditions. Enslaved captives and confiscated land remained sources of reward. With no son available to succeed him, Meren adopted Valeran Soreth, an officer from a harbour family and the husband of his daughter Cassara. Meren secured the principal commands' recognition of Valeran before his death in 286.

## Valeran I, 286–263 BR

Cassara had supervised the Aurin stores during her father's reign. That work made her a source of information about shortages and contracts for Valeran, who had served in harbour supply and held military command. His adoption joined a family with useful ties among the ports to Meren's household. The Caleran army acclaimed him and the provincial commands received confirmation of their appointments.

Valeran enlarged water storage, harbour granaries and the paved approaches to several provincial capitals. Cassara travelled with engineers and developed a reputation for asking workers to explain a problem before officials described the solution.

Construction drew labour and material from subject districts. Since Caelor's death every public work carried a Star's share, a portion built to be broken when the wardens loaded it before the Red Star, and Valeran's new aqueduct needed a breaking span in every run of arches. The quarries could not cut the extra stone in the working season. In 274 BR the managers extended compulsory service through the harvest, and the quarrymen struck rather than leave their fields unreaped to cut "stone for the Star to break". Troops killed strikers and their families were expelled from housing attached to the works. Cassara pressed for the removal of two managers whose conduct endangered the supply programme. Valeran dismissed them, kept the compulsory labour assessment and ordered the aqueduct completed. He also kept the test. His court said that a house which had buried an heir under an untested hall would not open an untested aqueduct; the quarry villages said that the house had found someone else to pay for its fear.

The imperial household supported musicians, astronomers and comic performers. Provincial artists acquired commissions at Caleran and brought imperial styles home. When Valeran died in 263, he left a recognised heir in Olarion, his son by Cassara and Meren's grandson.

## Olarion, 263–247 BR

Olarion inherited a court rich in honours and suspicious of any commander who acquired independent popularity. He enjoyed mechanical birds, private performances and elaborate night processions through the palace. Craftspeople produced ingenious works for him while treasury officials struggled to meet the cost.

Olarion made the accession portrait in the audience hall the centre of his court. His guards knelt to it at every change of watch, petitions were read aloud before it, and officials renewed their loyalty in its presence each year. His informers swore their denunciations before the same painted face. Families learned to fear being named in the hall more than being named in a court.

An eastern commander who returned without permission was executed. Olarion later ordered the deaths of two relatives after accusations that their household rites concealed a succession gathering. His informers also hunted copies of the history Amesar had finished on Ilamar, which called the founder a man who had other men killed for him, and owners of a copy were named before the portrait. Confiscations rewarded informers and drove other families towards frontier armies willing to shelter them.

Food shortages at Caleran in 252 brought demonstrations. The palace released grain while soldiers killed people attempting to enter a closed store. Olarion's subsequent games quieted the city without restoring the families of the dead.

The emperor died suddenly in 247. Accounts variously blamed illness, poison and a frightened attendant. No explanation became conclusive. Four claimants received support before the funeral rites were complete, and on the second night of the mourning the portrait before which Olarion's informers had sworn began to answer questions in the Emperor's voice. [[The Year of Four Eagles]] follows the war and the portrait.

## Dynastic memory

Later rulers retained Thalion's name within their titles and represented Meren and Valeran as peaceful continuators. Olarion became the wicked relative against whom the wisdom of the other three could be displayed. His government nevertheless used officers, tax arrangements and ceremonies built by his predecessors. The house left three habits that outlived it: the wardens' test of every public work; the portrait cult that grew from the founder's painted war board and answered in 247; and the spring meal at Sarqen's landing, where every governor of the province sat at the unranked table and swore the towns' oath.
