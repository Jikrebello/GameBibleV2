# The Spared Towns and the Queen's Country

The royal country later drawn into Atheria's imperial-founding struggles developed among the communities that survived the destruction of the [[The Gateway Republic|Gateway Republic's]] capital. Its farms, minor ports and inland towns preserved inhabitants and productive work after the final war of approximately 525–520 BR. Over the following generations, an enlarged harbour and a crown governing several communities replaced the destroyed city's republican leadership.

The later queen's territorial claims rested upon this developed country. Its inhabitants included descendants of older shore populations, proto-Ahdari migrants, refugees, freed captives and subsequent maritime settlers. The kingdom possessed a history of reconstruction and political accommodation well before the competing Atherian commanders whose struggles brought it greater prominence.

## What survived the war

The [[The Gateway Wars|final settlement]] spared lesser towns that accepted its terms. Working wells, cultivated districts, fishing landings and workshops remained in use. Some communities had already supplied food and passage to Atherian forces; others accommodated the victors after suffering losses. Protection from planned destruction did not remove requisitions, garrisons or obligations imposed after defeat.

Refugees carried skills and family relationships into these towns. They included people who had escaped before the siege and survivors later freed, ransomed or permitted to return. Many could not recover their former property. Local households absorbed relatives and strangers while dealing with shortages and competing claims to work.

The capital's salting damaged selected ground, while the demolition of waterworks and restrictions on rebuilding kept much of the city desolate. Food production continued beyond it. The survival of cultivated land and inhabited ports made a later kingdom possible without requiring the restoration of the old capital.

## A smaller harbour becomes a centre

Second Harbour, later known as Sarqen, was an existing lesser port that escaped the capital's destruction. Its sheltered landing was distinct from both the ruined capital and the later city of Qabir. Its enlargement drew upon resident pilots, craftspeople, storekeepers and cultivators, joined by newcomers seeking work and security. Repair of storage and water supply accompanied renewed shipping. A harbour could attract vessels only if it could provide food, equipment and labour between voyages.

Recovery remained unequal. Compulsory work and preferential grants gave some households the means to enlarge their holdings while others struggled to regain a livelihood. Return from captivity could reunite relatives without restoring the property or position they had lost. The growth of trade nevertheless supported apprenticeships, marriages and households whose prospects improved through the new centre.

Between about 500 and 400 BR, the port acquired greater political importance within a country still subject to Atherian supervision. Cultivated revenue, shipping and military protection became resources around which hereditary authority could consolidate. The surviving towns retained influential families and local institutions capable of supporting or resisting a royal household.

## Royal authority and Atherian influence

Nerethan of Delkos arrived as an Atherian harbour commander in 499 BR and married Hadrima of Esharat in 497. Her family controlled land and carrying interests in an inland town whose households had sheltered children during the final war. Their son Arenes obtained recognition as hereditary ruler in 457. [[The House of Nerethan]] developed through later marriages, religious patronage and arrangements with the towns supplying its port. Some obligations continued earlier practices; others followed conquest and the demands of the new government.

Atherian authorities retained influence through garrisons, payments, military assistance and the ability to favour rival claimants. They often depended upon local rulers to maintain supplies and order. The crown, in turn, needed sufficient support at home to meet those demands without surrendering every advantage to foreign patrons.

The resulting kingdom differed substantially from the old republic. Royal succession replaced the capital's former distribution of offices, and Second Harbour drew activity away from the ruined centre. Urban households, cultivators and foreign military families entered the new arrangements from unequal positions. Their descendants belonged to the same country while preserving different memories of how their families had acquired a place within it.

## The inheritance claimed by the queen

[[Samyra Nerethan]], daughter of Olymes and a descendant of Nerethan and Hadrima, inherited the kingdom's claim over the spared towns. Her position involved land, ports, protection and relationships with established communities. She entered a contested joint succession in 375 BR and recovered power with Teren Casvilar's assistance in 373–372. Her later alliance with Darion Velsar made the country a principal participant in the Atherian succession wars.

Religious memory mattered to that inheritance. Some towns had hidden children and assisted the destruction of the sacrificial establishment. Families also remembered relatives killed or enslaved during the sack. A royal claim to honour the country's dead therefore encountered traditions that distinguished opposition to sacrifice from approval of everything the victors had done.

The queen inherited food production, shipping, a royal following and access between cultivated country and desert approaches. Those resources had been rebuilt across generations. Thalion annexed the kingdom after her defeat in 350 BR, preserving its working harbour and negotiating with surviving town leaders. The dynasty ended while the country remained inhabited.
