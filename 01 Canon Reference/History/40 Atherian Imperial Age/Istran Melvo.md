# Istran Melvo

Istran Melvo, born in 391 BR, was the naval organiser and commander whose victories secured the sea routes for Aurelius Thalion. His career connected shipyard knowledge with the provisioning, training and political work needed to keep fleets operating over several campaigning seasons. He burned the moving city of Marelith and then refused to drag its survivors out of his own home harbour. In 350 he ended the succession wars by walking onto Darion Velsar's Salt Field in person, which Thalion had refused to do and never forgave him for doing.

## From the working shore

Melvo grew up among shipyard households at Pyrassa. Repair work required familiarity with timber, fastenings, weather and the different uses to which a vessel might be put. A sound hull could still fail through careless loading or an exhausted crew.

He entered Thalion's service during the struggles following Casvilar's assassination. The two men were close in age and brought different experience to their association. Melvo could explain why an attractive military proposal would fail at the anchorage, then identify the stores and time needed to make it possible.

He enjoyed discussing a difficult repair with experienced workers and had little patience for an officer who concealed a shortage. Those habits helped him learn from people below his military rank. His demands could also be severe when he believed delay endangered a campaign.

## Building a fleet

The maritime following of Ardel Veyr threatened supplies and offered refuge to the coalition's enemies, among them Corial Draven, one of the seven men who had killed Casvilar. Thalion needed a force able to remain at sea long enough to contest those routes. Melvo began with repair, crew training and agreements for provisions at cooperating ports.

His work brought more regular employment to some shipyards and heavy burdens to others. Officials seized timber and compelled labour when contracts and volunteers could not meet the required pace. Families lost income or working animals while public resources went towards the fleet.

Training joined rowers, sailors and fighting troops who often came from different communities. Melvo sought dependable routines for signals, loading and the distribution of food. Skill acquired through repetition gave him an advantage which the acquisition of additional ships alone could not provide.

## The sea-heir's defeat

In 355 BR Melvo destroyed Ardel's independent ally, the mobile harbour-city [[Marelith]], and defeated the sea-heir's nearby main force among the central Thalassian anchorages in one operation.

Marelith had a name among sailors older than Ardel's war. Since the gale of 466 BR, when it took in crews refused at the League's closed harbours, it had been "the harbour without a chain": a city that never anchored, kept no harbour chain and turned no hull away in a storm. Its founding covenant forbade it a permanent anchorage. Melvo had grown up in Pyrassa's shipyards, and he brought his own city's great harbour chain east in the holds of three transports. His crews laid it across the shallows where the city's living bearer fed and drew it tight between sunken hulks. Accounts differ over the prize ship admitted near the city with hidden attackers and sea-fire aboard. Held fast, the bearer fought the chain and tore the city's attachments apart while the fire ran through it. The wreck grounded as the [[Black Reef]], and Corial Draven died aboard.

Families fleeing the Writing-In had gone to Marelith for the same reason as the refused crews of 466. Melvo burned a refuge as well as a pirate base. Atherian accounts called Marelith a nest of pirates and slavers, which it also was. Its survivors, and the Elmarai pilots who had guided ships into it for a century, called the burning a second Kelarion, after the old harbour that had drowned for shutting out ships in a storm. Pyrassan sailors did not boast of whose chain it had been.

The victory opened routes and destroyed a refuge for displaced households. Some defeated crews were left beyond rescue as the victors concentrated on securing ships and prisoners. Captives subsequently faced pardon, sale or delivery to former enemies.

## The guests of Pyrassa

Ardel escaped the anchorages with eleven hulls, took aboard what survivors he could and ran before a rising wind for the nearest great harbour that kept a refuge fire. It was Pyrassa. Its chain lay under a burned reef in the central sea, and its wardens, who remembered how the closed harbour of 466 had shut out its own grain ships, lit the fire and let the hulls in. The people of Marelith were lodged in the boat sheds where Melvo had learned his trade.

Thalion ordered him to take Ardel off the quay. Melvo wrote back that he had carried his city's chain to sea and left it there, and he would not carry off its guests as well. If Thalion wanted Ardel, he could come to Pyrassa and fetch him. It was their first quarrel, and Thalion, who had no other admiral, let it lie. In 353 Calvena Casvilar made terms on the mole: Ardel gave up his remaining hulls and swore never to pass the mole towers, his captains sailed under Melvo or went home, and the survivors kept their freedom and their sheds, which became Pyrassa's Burnt Quarter. The captives Melvo's crews had taken from the water at the anchorages had already been sold.

## The royal war and the Salt Field

In 353 Darion Velsar accepted a Red Field on the salt pans below Sarqen and named two enemies, Thalion and Melvo. Melvo worked to separate the field from its supplies. He negotiated with coastal rulers, threatened reluctant ports and in 352 closed Sarqen's outer anchorage. There was no great sea battle. In 351 the queen's pilots bought a current that carried a convoy of Qahuri grain hulls in past his line to Hazreth, and his captains watched a third of the cargo go over the side as the sea's price. The blockade lasted one winter longer than he had planned.

In the spring of 350 the towns put the queen's house out and Velsar's army scattered for want of food. Velsar stayed on the salt, bound to a field whose named enemy still contested it. Thalion meant to leave him there until he renounced his claim before the Red Star's priests. Melvo thought it a shameful way to treat a soldier. He came ashore with forty of his crewmen and walked out onto the salt in person, and Velsar and his brother Tarsen were killed in the fight that followed. So was Seluc Varroth, the First Celebrant of the Common Sky, who had walked out beside Velsar with the register of challengers under his arm. One of Melvo's crewmen cut him down. The crew said the old man had held a blade, and the augurs said a book, and the register was never seen again.

Thalion never forgave him for that morning, and the sailors' songs never forgave Thalion. Melvo supervised the breaking-up of the war fleets after 350 and the distribution of useful hulls among public services and approved merchants. He was never given a land command. His reputation rested on vessels that could sail, stores available when promised, crews able to act together, a quay he would not clear and the morning he went to meet Velsar on foot.
