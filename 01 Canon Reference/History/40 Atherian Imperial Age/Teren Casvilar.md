# Teren Casvilar

Teren Casvilar (425–370 BR) was the Caleran commander whose southern conquests and victory in civil war brought the republic under his personal government. In 376 BR he accepted the Red Field, the war office from which no holder could walk away while a named enemy still stood against him, and marched on Caleran. Six years later the men who had entered their names as his challengers killed him on the muster ground before the contest had begun. His assassination began the succession struggle from which his nephew Aurelius Thalion emerged as emperor.

## Family and public life

Casvilar belonged to a civic family with old connections in Caleran and too little money for an expensive public career. He borrowed from Lethren Ostar, represented petitioners and cultivated friendships among officers and provincial households. People seeking an introduction found him willing to listen, and every favour he did was remembered on both sides.

He enjoyed conversation, public occasions and the chance to make an opponent look foolish without raising his voice. His speeches explained what a measure would put on the listener's table before asking anyone to trust the man proposing it. He could forgive a former enemy when forgiveness bought him room to move.

His sister Lysara managed the family's friendships and debts. Her son Thalion knew Casvilar chiefly as a famous patron and commander. Casvilar's daughter Calvena married Marovan Veyr in 386 BR. The match sealed a political partnership and grew into real affection, and for some years Calvena was the one person who could speak privately to both men.

## Partnership and conquest

Between 396 and 391 BR, Casvilar, Veyr and Ostar worked together to obtain commands and protect their separate interests. Veyr had victories and eastern clients; Ostar had money. Casvilar needed a command that could make him their equal.

His southern campaigns, principally between 390 and 379 BR, carried Atherian armies into the lives of separate Folk coalitions along the river and forest approaches. He negotiated with Morveth's followers, recruited local guides and fed his troops from conquered stores, so that the people who had grown the food went hungry while his army stayed strong.

At Kellovar in 383 BR his siege works defeated a coalition led by Drenoc. When the defenders expelled their noncombatants to save food, Casvilar refused to let them through his lines, and they died between the two armies. Drenoc surrendered when relief failed. Casvilar handed him to his ally Morveth as part of Morveth's reward, and Drenoc died in Morveth's hall that winter. The river halls never forgave Morveth, and his crossings were safe only while Atherian garrisons stood on them.

At Darneth in 380 BR the army's engineers loaded a siege frame to breaking under the Red Star's test, and the crack showed them where force would carry through the defenders' works. Their tunnels took the protected water and the ridge surrendered. Casvilar then ordered the hands of the captured fighters cut off, so that the men walking home would carry his warning into every neighbouring village. He told Caleran that the broken frame was heaven's confirmation of his command. The engineers' names survive only in local memory; the claim of heavenly approval became the foundation of everything he asked for afterwards.

## The Red Field

Casvilar's veterans began coming home in 379 BR, and he promised them land. The land he chose lay along the lines of the Noon Roll, the surveyors' record of the noon hour in 488 BR when, by the veterans' own tradition, every shadow in the lower Aurin had pointed along the old boundaries of the public land. For more than a century that land had been leased in great blocks to senatorial houses, and those houses were Marovan Veyr's friends. They demanded that Casvilar lay down his command before seeking further office. He refused to stand unarmed before courts his enemies controlled until his soldiers' grants were secure.

In 377 he offered Calvena a divorce from Veyr. She refused in public, before her father's own clients, and went east with her husband's household. Her refusal was remembered as the moment the partnership ended.

Ordered again to surrender his command in 376 BR, Casvilar dedicated his arms at an altar on the frontier road and read out the names of the senators who had demanded his surrender. By that rite he accepted the **Red Field**, the war office Caleran had made after its sacrifice of 609 BR and which Vespen Harod had held until his death. A Warden of the Field could not leave it while a named opponent still contested it. His enemies said he had invented a war in order to keep an army; the augurs who witnessed the dedication said the Red Star had accepted it. Either way, from that day Casvilar could not lay his command down without, in the language of the rite, fleeing the field.

He marched on Caleran. Veyr withdrew east. Near Velos in 374 BR their armies fought each other to a standstill. Veyr lost the war at sea instead: the eastern ports, remembering the harbour chains Caleran's senate had once raised against them, would not pay his ships.

## Rule after the civil war

In 373 Veyr sailed for the gateway kingdom and turned back from a royal table laid with an empty seat, taking sanctuary at Ilamar. Casvilar did not storm the island: the grain certificates that fed his armies were issued by its custodians' court. Veyr died there of fever in 371.

At Sarqen the spared towns rose against the minister Vardek, whose table had been laid to kill a guest. Casvilar needed Esharat's grain and backed the side that already held it, the cause of the young queen Samyra Nerethan. Their son Amesar was born in 372. Samyra's throne rested on her towns as much as on his soldiers.

Returning to Caleran, Casvilar settled veterans on the Noon Roll lands, revised appointments, cancelled part of the city's debts and filled offices with his own people. The Field explained much of what followed. A Warden with named enemies still alive could neither disband his army nor go home a private man, and so he kept his command long after the war that justified it had ended. Meanwhile at Ilamar the senator Arest Corven, who had followed Veyr into sanctuary, was writing the history of these years that Casvilar's heirs would one day have to answer. A pardoned opponent could speak in the senate again and still have no say in anything that mattered.

## Assassination and inheritance

Any Warden had to receive a challenger in person on the muster ground below the river stores. In 370 BR Mavren Seneth, Corial Draven and their fellows entered their names as challengers in the arms register kept by Deceran Tolm, Casvilar's trusted quartermaster. Tolm fixed the hour. When Casvilar came down to receive them, they stabbed him before the contest had been opened. The augurs said the Field had been fouled; the killers said they had freed the republic from a man who could never be made to stop.

Casvilar had designated Thalion as his civic heir. Amesar's royal parentage gave him a different claim, grounded in Samyra's country. Neither settled the allegiance of armies, and Darion Velsar commanded loyalties that no testament could transfer.

Later imperial honours joined Casvilar's victories to Thalion's foundation. Southern communities remembered lost fields, men without hands and the starving people between the lines at Kellovar.
