# Teren Casvilar

Teren Casvilar (425–370 BR) was the Caleran commander whose southern conquests and victory in civil war brought the republic under his personal government. His assassination began the succession struggle from which his nephew Aurelius Thalion emerged as emperor. Casvilar's career joined exceptional military achievement to an increasingly coercive use of public power.

## Family and public life

Casvilar belonged to a civic family with established connections in Caleran and limited means to sustain an expensive public career. He borrowed from Lethren Ostar, represented petitioners and cultivated friendships among officers and provincial households. People seeking an introduction could find him willing to listen; the resulting obligations also extended his influence.

He enjoyed conversation, public occasions and the opportunity to make an opponent appear foolish without interrupting the proceedings. His speeches explained practical benefits before asking listeners to trust the man proposing them. He remembered favours and could forgive a former enemy when reconciliation increased his freedom to act.

His sister Lysara managed important family relationships. Her son Thalion belonged to a younger generation which knew Casvilar chiefly as a successful patron and commander. Casvilar's daughter Calvena married Marovan Veyr in 386 BR. The marriage strengthened a political partnership and developed into an affectionate relationship. Her death in childbirth in 380 deprived the two men of a person able to speak privately to both.

## Partnership and conquest

Between 396 and 391 BR, Casvilar, Veyr and Ostar cooperated to obtain commands, secure decisions and protect their separate interests. Veyr possessed established victories and provincial clients; Ostar supplied money and influence among creditors. Casvilar needed a command capable of giving him comparable military standing.

His southern campaigns, principally between 390 and 379 BR, brought Atherian forces into the lives of separate Folk coalitions and other communities along the river and forest approaches. He negotiated with Morveth's followers, recruited local guides and used conquered stores to sustain further operations. These arrangements made his army effective while transferring food and labour away from the people who had produced them.

At Kellovar in 383 BR, his siege works defeated a coalition led by Drenoc. When the defenders expelled noncombatants to conserve food, Casvilar refused to admit them through his own lines. People died between the two armies. Drenoc's eventual surrender ended the siege but did not save him: he was carried north and executed during the celebration of victory.

Casvilar later ordered the hands of captured fighters cut off after the surrender of Darneth. He intended the surviving men to discourage further resistance. Their households continued to live with the injuries after the army had moved on. The campaigns also brought captives, land and wealth into Atherian hands.

## Rule after the civil war

The deaths of Ostar and Calvena, disputes over commands and the fear of prosecution helped destroy the partnership. Casvilar advanced on Caleran in 376 BR. Veyr withdrew east, lost the principal field struggle and was murdered in the gateway country in 373.

Casvilar's intervention there secured Samyra Nerethan's throne and access to supplies. Their son Amesar was born in 372. The queen's position depended on her own town relationships as well as the Atherian army.

Returning to government, Casvilar settled soldiers, revised appointments and dealt with debts and provisioning. He could make decisions which the divided republic had delayed. He also filled offices with dependants and extended his command beyond the circumstances used to justify it. A pardoned opponent could remain excluded from meaningful influence.

## Assassination and inheritance

Deceran Tolm, an officer whom Casvilar trusted, helped bring him to the meeting at which conspirators killed him in 370 BR. Mavren Seneth and Corial Draven shared the opposition to his rule, though their expectations of the government to follow differed.

Casvilar had designated Thalion as his civic heir. Amesar's acknowledged royal parentage gave him a different claim, grounded in Samyra's country. Neither arrangement settled the allegiance of armies. Velsar possessed military relationships which a testament could not transfer.

Later imperial honours joined Casvilar's victories to Thalion's foundation. Southern communities remembered lost fields, mutilated relatives and the siege of Kellovar. Both inheritances endured in the empire created after his death.
