# Teren Casvilar

Teren Casvilar (431–370 BR) was the Caleran commander whose southern conquests and victory in civil war brought the republic under his personal government. In 383 BR, besieging the hillfort of Kellovar, he accepted the Red Field, the war office from which no holder could walk away while a named enemy still stood against him. His named enemy vanished into a river ford, and the Field could not be closed. Casvilar carried it for thirteen years, through the ruin of his banker, a march on Caleran and a civil war. In 370 seven men who had entered their names as his challengers killed him on the muster ground before the contest had begun. His assassination began the succession struggle from which his nephew Aurelius Thalion emerged as emperor.

## Family and public life

Casvilar belonged to a civic family with old connections in Caleran and too little money for an expensive public career. He borrowed from Lethren Ostar, represented petitioners and cultivated friendships among officers and provincial households. People seeking an introduction found him willing to listen, and every favour he did was remembered on both sides.

He enjoyed conversation, public occasions and the chance to make an opponent look foolish without raising his voice. His speeches explained what a measure would put on the listener's table before asking anyone to trust the man proposing it. He could forgive a former enemy when forgiveness bought him room to move.

His sister Lysara managed the family's friendships and debts. Her son Thalion knew Casvilar chiefly as a famous patron and commander. Casvilar's daughter Calvena married Marovan Veyr in 389 BR. The match sealed a political partnership and grew into real affection, and for some years Calvena was the one person who could speak privately to both men.

## Partnership and conquest

Between 396 and 391 BR, Casvilar, Veyr and Ostar worked together to obtain commands and protect their separate interests. Veyr had victories and eastern clients; Ostar had money. Casvilar needed a command that could make him their equal. Ostar's own northern venture was destroyed at Rethmar in 392, and from then on the banker expected his loans to Casvilar to come back as captives.

His southern campaigns, principally between 390 and 379 BR, carried Atherian armies into the lives of separate Folk coalitions along the river and forest approaches. He negotiated with Morveth's followers, recruited local guides and fed his troops from conquered stores, so that the people who had grown the food went hungry while his army stayed strong. Each autumn the season's captives went north to Ostar's agents.

## The Field at Kellovar

At Kellovar in 383 BR his siege works shut in a coalition led by Drenoc. A relief host was gathering behind him and his command had a year to run. Casvilar dedicated his arms at the siege lines, named Drenoc and accepted the **Red Field**, the war office Caleran had made after its sacrifice of 609 BR and which Vespen Harod had held until his death. A Warden of the Field could not leave it while a named opponent still contested it, and for the same reason no senate could recall him. His enemies said he had sworn to the Star in order to keep an army. The augurs who witnessed the dedication said the Red Star had accepted it.

He expected Drenoc in chains within the month. When the hill's food failed, Drenoc sent out those who could not fight, and Casvilar refused them passage through his lines. They went down instead to the ford below the hill and crossed it on their household tokens, in the way of the river people. Drenoc followed them into the water and was not seen on either bank. Casvilar took Kellovar four days later, with a few hundred starved fighters in it. His augurs could not declare his named enemy dead or beaten, and the Field stayed open. He gave the ford to his ally Morveth, whose halls paid at it with Atherian silver. The river halls never forgave Morveth, and his crossings were safe only while Atherian garrisons stood on them.

At Darneth in 380 BR the army's engineers loaded a siege frame to breaking under the Red Star's test, and the crack showed them where the ridge's rampart would fail. Many of the fighters who surrendered were ford guides whose tokens had carried Kellovar's people over the rivers. Casvilar ordered their hands cut off, so that none of them should lead a file through a ford again and so that the men walking home would carry his warning into every neighbouring village. He told Caleran that the broken frame was heaven's confirmation of his command. The engineers' names survive only in local memory; the claim of heavenly approval became the foundation of everything he asked for afterwards.

## The open Field

Casvilar's veterans began coming home in 379 BR, and he promised them land. The land he chose lay along the lines of the Noon Roll, the surveyors' record of the noon hour in 488 BR when, by the veterans' own tradition, every shadow in the lower Aurin had pointed along the old boundaries of the public land. For more than a century that land had been leased in great blocks to senatorial houses, and those houses were Marovan Veyr's friends.

He could give his soldiers land. He could not give them their discharge. While the Field stood open the Warden could not lay down his command, his army could not be released from its oath, and no court could summon him. In 379 Lethren Ostar tried. The supply of captives had ended with the war, and the banker sued for his loans. Casvilar's augurs ruled that a man who summoned a Warden from his field had set himself against it, and they entered Ostar's name as a contestant. Ostar fled to Vorthara. His ledgers were forfeit to the Field, and Casvilar burned the pages that named himself, his officers and several thousand small debtors of the city.

The leaseholding houses demanded that he produce Drenoc or lay down his arms. Early in 377 he offered Calvena a divorce from Veyr. She refused in public, before her father's own clients, and went east with her husband's household. That spring the senate declared the Field at Kellovar void and named another man to the southern command. Casvilar's augurs entered every senator who had sealed the decree as a contestant, with Veyr first among them, and Casvilar marched on Caleran.

## The civil war

Caleran opened its gates. The senators he had named raised an army in the east from Veyr's old clients, and the two armies fought each other to a standstill near Velos in the autumn of 377. Veyr was not there. He had gone to bring the fleet, and the eastern ports, remembering the harbour chains Caleran's senate had once raised against them, would not pay for his ships. For four years the republic had two governments. Casvilar fed his on grain bought with certificates from Ilamar; the east lived on the grain of the gateway kingdom.

In 373 Veyr sailed for the gateway kingdom to secure that grain and turned back from a royal table laid with an empty seat, taking sanctuary at Ilamar. Casvilar did not storm the island: the grain certificates that fed his armies were issued by its custodians' court. Veyr died there of fever in 371.

At Sarqen the spared towns rose against the minister Vardek, whose table had been laid to kill a guest. Casvilar needed Esharat's grain and backed the side that already held it, the cause of the young queen Samyra Nerethan. Their son Amesar was born in 372. Samyra's throne rested on her towns as much as on his soldiers. With the gateway grain in Casvilar's hands the eastern army broke up, and its senators came to Caleran for pardon.

## Rule after the civil war

Returning to Caleran, Casvilar settled veterans on the Noon Roll lands, revised appointments and filled offices with his own people. The Field explained much of what followed. A Warden whose named enemy was unaccounted for could neither disband his army nor go home a private man, and so he kept his command long after the war that justified it had ended. He slept in a tent in the court of his own house. His veterans held their new plots as soldiers still under oath and could not leave them to their sons. Parties went south every year to look for Drenoc, and the augurs turned away three old men brought north under that name.

Meanwhile at Ilamar the senator Arest Corven, who had followed Veyr into sanctuary, was writing the history of these years that Casvilar's heirs would one day have to answer. A pardoned opponent could speak in the senate again and still have no say in anything that mattered. His name stayed in the augurs' book with a line through it.

## Assassination and inheritance

Any Warden had to receive a challenger in person on the muster ground below the river stores. In 370 BR seven men entered their names in the arms register kept by Deceran Tolm, quartermaster of the muster ground. Mavren Seneth had commanded the river line at Kellovar and had watched Drenoc go into the ford. Corial Draven was a ship-owner ruined when Ostar's ledgers burned. The seventh name belonged to a young man from the river halls, entered as the son of Drenoc of Kellovar.

Casvilar ordered his augurs to accept it. A Warden who beat his named enemy's son could close the Field before the whole city, and he was sixty-one and had waited thirteen years. Tolm fixed the hour. When Casvilar came down bareheaded to receive the challengers' arms, the seven stabbed him before the contest had been opened. The augurs said the Field had been fouled. Seneth said he had closed it the only way it could be closed.

Casvilar had designated Thalion as his civic heir. Amesar's royal parentage gave him a different claim, grounded in Samyra's country. Neither settled the allegiance of armies, and Darion Velsar commanded loyalties that no testament could transfer. While any of the seven killers lived, no commander dared disband an army, and the hunt for them lasted twenty years. The last of them, Mavren Seneth, was killed in 350 at the spared towns' table in Sarqen by Casvilar's own son.

Later imperial honours joined Casvilar's victories to Thalion's foundation. Southern communities remembered lost fields, men without hands and the autumn of hunger on the hill at Kellovar.
