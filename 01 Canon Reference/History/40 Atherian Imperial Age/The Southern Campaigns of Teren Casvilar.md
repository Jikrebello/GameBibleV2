# The Southern Campaigns of Teren Casvilar

Teren Casvilar's southern command extended Atherian power through a belt of forest settlements, hillforts and allied districts between about 390 and 379 BR. The principal fighting took place on the northern edges of Folk country adjoining the later Green March and Atherian provincial corridors. A separate voyage reached the cold outer coast. The campaigns increased Casvilar's wealth and military following while leaving conquered communities with captives abroad, damaged fields and obligations enforced by new garrisons. They also left Casvilar bound to a war office he could not close. At Kellovar in 383 he accepted the Red Field against the Folk chief Drenoc, and Drenoc walked into a river ford and was not seen again.

Atherian armies had entered Folk country long before his command. Casvilar inherited roads, treaties and local rivalries, together with communities that had resisted or accommodated earlier expansion. The societies he encountered possessed their own leaders, workshops, cultivation and arrangements for the winter watch.

## Allies and winter supplies

Casvilar first used existing allied settlements to establish stores and quarters. Some leaders welcomed his troops against neighbouring rivals. Morveth, head of a lesser river coalition, supplied boats and guides in return for protection of contested crossings. The crossings were fords, and the river people held that each ford belonged to a court of its own. Households that paid at a ford's spring offering carried its tokens and could lead others through the water; a contested crossing was one where two coalitions claimed the right to pay. Morveth's guides took Atherian columns over the fords his own halls paid for, and he wanted Casvilar's help to add Kellovar's. His alliance gave him an advantage over neighbouring halls and drew their hostility towards the Atherians.

Winter imposed costs that summer promises often concealed. Soldiers required stored grain and timber when households were already preserving food for the cold months. Farther south, contributions to the Grobi watch reduced what could be spared. Casvilar's officers extended requisitions after local leaders said their commitments had been met. Hostages provided assurance when promises of another delivery proved insufficient. Captives went north each autumn to the agents of Lethren Ostar, the financier whose loans had bought Casvilar his command.

Velcara, who organised stores among households around Kellovar, initially negotiated supplies to prevent seizure. She came to support resistance after repeated collections left families dependent on loans from military suppliers. Her knowledge of the remaining stores later sustained the hillfort's defenders and helped many families escape its fall.

## Expansion and the coast

Over several seasons, Casvilar established fortified positions, bridged river approaches and brought separate communities into unequal agreements. Some paid and retained local leaders. Others were defeated, lost hostages or had inhabitants sold. His reports emphasised the security offered by the roads and garrisons; the places paying for them preserved accounts of forced work and removal.

In 385 BR, he undertook a short voyage from the western coastal supply network towards a colder shore beyond his secure commands. Pilots and shipwrights from the Celion-facing ports made the venture possible. He obtained hostages and gifts from one shore community, but weather damaged vessels and the ships could not supply a lasting occupation. He returned with a claim to distant submission considerably larger than the authority he had left behind.

Coast tellers later gave his ships a part in the much older story of the Atherian dry fleet taken in Tidewood, so that a famous commander would have walked on their own shore.

## Drenoc's coalition

Drenoc of Kellovar assembled a coalition among hillfort leaders, dispossessed households and former allies who feared further demands. His strongest support came from communities whose grain and dependants had repeatedly been taken. Others joined to contain Morveth or recover influence lost to an Atherian-backed neighbour.

The coalition had considerable military skill and imperfect agreement. Drenoc demanded stores for a common army; some households regarded his collection parties as another threat. He ordered the burning of an exposed grain depot to deny it to Casvilar. Families whose winter food was held there suffered alongside the invading forces.

Casvilar moved quickly against the settlements sustaining the coalition. Capturing one landing separated defenders from river supply, while agreements with rival leaders provided information about inland routes. Drenoc withdrew into Kellovar, a defended height above a wooded tributary approach in the upper Weiss country. The fort held supplies and people from surrounding settlements but could not feed them indefinitely.

## The siege of Kellovar, 383 BR

Casvilar's army built works facing the hillfort and additional defences against the relief forces gathering beyond it. Timber cutting damaged the houses and winter stores of neighbouring communities. Supply parties travelled under guard, and a failed delivery could leave an entire section of the works short of food.

The relief coalition reached the district but could not coordinate its attacks across the forest approaches. Some leaders committed their men while others held back, expecting a clearer opening or fearing that a rival would acquire the advantage. Casvilar used reserves and local guides to reinforce threatened positions.

His command had a year to run, and the relief host was still growing in the forest behind his works. Some of his officers advised him to raise the siege while the roads north were open. He answered them at the altar in the siege lines. Before the assembled army he dedicated his arms to the Red Star, named Drenoc of Kellovar as his enemy and accepted the Red Field, the war office from which no holder could walk away while his named enemy held the ground. No Atherian had taken it since Vespen Harod. The army understood what it meant: there would be no retreat from Kellovar, and no successor would come from Caleran.

Within the fort the stores ran down through the autumn. The old and the newly born died first. When the ration could be stretched no further, Drenoc ordered out everyone judged unable to fight. Casvilar closed his lines to them, as he had said he would. Velcara, who knew every household on the hill, led them away from the Atherian ditch and down the river side, to the ford in the alder wood below Kellovar.

Kellovar's own households had paid at that ford every spring, and they carried its tokens, small pieces of iron, bone or plaited hair. A bearer with a token in hand could lead a file of people across by a rope or a belt, and through that night the households of the hill crossed in long files, each behind its token. The dispossessed families who had come to Drenoc from the stripped districts held no token of that river. They stood in the shallows through the next day with Atherian sentries watching from the bank above. Some tried the water alone, and it took them.

Drenoc came down from the hill at dusk. What he asked of the ford, and what it asked of him, only the river halls claim to know. Kellovar's descendants say the court wanted the man who had sent the people out, and that he gave himself: he walked in behind the last of the children and was not seen on either bank. Morveth's halls said he crossed with the rest and died an old man in the cold south under another name. Casvilar's report to Caleran said that the enemy commander had fled. All the tellings agree that the people without tokens crossed that night, and that they came out of the water far from where they had gone in, at fords several days' walk apart, among halls that did not know them.

The fighters left on the hill held the wall four more days to cover the crossing and then opened the gate. Casvilar took a few hundred starved men and those too sick to walk. Velcara was among them, because she had stayed with relatives who could not be moved. He distributed the captives and allowed selected leaders to retain authority on new terms. He held the hill and could not close his Field. His augurs could not swear that his named enemy was dead, and a man who left the ground on terms made with a river had not been beaten on it. They declared that the Field stood open. It stayed open for the rest of Casvilar's life.

Casvilar rewarded Morveth with the ford. At the next spring offering Morveth's halls paid at Kellovar's crossing with Atherian silver, and his token-bearers led Atherian columns over it by the rope. Velcara survived captivity through ransom arranged by her mother's kin. She returned to a smaller household and helped rebuild food stores, retaining the memory of who had contributed to her release and who had refused.

## The surrendered fighters of Darneth

Resistance continued after Kellovar, and it gathered where the people of the hill had gone. Darneth, a ridge settlement on a forest supply route south of the river, was held by ford-guide households whose tokens had carried Kellovar's families across and who would lead no Atherian column anywhere. Its fighters raided Casvilar's convoys and withdrew over crossings his cavalry could not follow. A cistern cut into the ridge kept its water safe through two dry months of siege.

Before the final effort, the army's engineers subjected a valuable siege frame to the dangerous load test they associated with Astaroth, the Red Star, loading it before the Star's priest until it broke. The break ran along a line that showed them where force would carry through the ridge's works. They moved their rams and stone-throwers to the place it indicated, and the eastern rampart came down in an afternoon. Darneth surrendered in 380 BR. Casvilar's account celebrated the broken frame as heavenly confirmation of the campaign. The engineers' own names survive imperfectly in the local accounts, and Atherian siege engineers repeated their test for centuries afterwards. The claim outlived the siege as well. When the senate declared his Field void in 377, Casvilar answered that the Red Star had confirmed it at Darneth, and his enemies never stopped saying that a cracked timber had been made to speak for heaven.

Casvilar ordered the hands of the captured fighters cut off. He had learned at Kellovar what carried the Folk across their rivers, a token held in a guide's hand, and he intended that no man of that ridge should lead a file through a ford again. The surviving bodies were also meant to carry his warning into neighbouring communities. Families thereafter supported people whose work and ordinary movement had been permanently changed. Several survivors learned tasks they could perform with assistance; others remained dependent on relatives impoverished by the same campaign. River people say that the maimed guides of Darneth went on crossing with the token bound to the forearm, and that the guides' custom of tying a token at the wrist began there.

## Withdrawal and continuing obligations

By 379 BR, Casvilar had acquired secure posts and a wider network of allied obligations. His troops carried wealth and captives north. Some officers remained to supervise contributions; others returned expecting rewards or new commands. Communities that had supported him sought confirmation of land and local authority before his political position changed.

Control remained uneven. Forest routes, winter conditions and the ability of households to move limited what garrisons could collect. Morveth's position depended on Atherian garrisons, because no hall that remembered whose silver had paid at Kellovar's ford would deal with him. The people of Kellovar were scattered among southern halls and rebuilt without a common leader able to replace Drenoc. Atherian citizenship and military service gradually connected some families to the conquerors. Other households preserved the stories of sold kin and the wounded people who had come home.

For Casvilar, the campaigns made surrendering command dangerous. Officers, creditors, veterans and allied leaders depended on decisions that his opponents could reverse. The open Field made it impossible as well. He could not be recalled, discharge his soldiers or go home a private man until his named enemy was produced, and for thirteen years search parties from Caleran went beyond the southern rivers asking after an old man of Kellovar. The contest over those commitments formed part of [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic|the final republican crisis]].
