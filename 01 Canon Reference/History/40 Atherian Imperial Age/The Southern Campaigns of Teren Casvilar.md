# The Southern Campaigns of Teren Casvilar

Teren Casvilar's southern command extended Atherian power through a belt of forest settlements, hillforts and allied districts between about 390 and 379 BR. The principal fighting took place on the northern edges of Folk country adjoining the later Green March and Atherian provincial corridors. A separate voyage reached the cold outer coast. The campaigns increased Casvilar's wealth and military following while leaving conquered communities with captives abroad, damaged fields and obligations enforced by new garrisons.

Atherian armies had entered Folk country long before his command. Casvilar inherited roads, treaties and local rivalries, together with communities that had resisted or accommodated earlier expansion. The societies he encountered possessed their own leaders, workshops, cultivation and arrangements for the winter watch.

## Allies and winter supplies

Casvilar first used existing allied settlements to establish stores and quarters. Some leaders welcomed his troops against neighbouring rivals. Morveth, head of a lesser river coalition, supplied boats and guides in return for protection of contested crossings. His alliance gave him an advantage over neighbouring halls and drew their hostility towards the Atherians.

Winter imposed costs that summer promises often concealed. Soldiers required stored grain and timber when households were already preserving food for the cold months. Farther south, contributions to the Grobi watch reduced what could be spared. Casvilar's officers extended requisitions after local leaders said their commitments had been met. Hostages provided assurance when promises of another delivery proved insufficient.

Velcara, who organised stores among households around Kellovar, initially negotiated supplies to prevent seizure. She came to support resistance after repeated collections left families dependent on loans from military suppliers. Her knowledge of the remaining stores later sustained the hillfort's defenders and helped some families escape its fall.

## Expansion and the coast

Over several seasons, Casvilar established fortified positions, crossed river approaches and brought separate communities into unequal agreements. Some paid and retained local leaders. Others were defeated, lost hostages or had inhabitants sold. His reports emphasised the security offered by the roads and garrisons; the places paying for them preserved accounts of forced work and removal.

In 385 BR, he undertook a short voyage from the western coastal supply network towards a colder shore beyond his secure commands. Pilots and shipwrights from the Celion-facing ports made the venture possible. He obtained hostages and gifts from one shore community, but weather damaged vessels and the ships could not supply a lasting occupation. He returned with a claim to distant submission considerably larger than the authority he had left behind.

Later accounts sometimes confused the expedition's vessels with the much earlier Atherian dry fleet taken in Tidewood. The stories gave familiar shores a part in the famous commander's career, although the voyages belonged to separate centuries and households.

## Drenoc's coalition

Drenoc of Kellovar assembled a coalition among hillfort leaders, dispossessed households and former allies who feared further demands. His strongest support came from communities whose grain and dependants had repeatedly been taken. Others joined to contain Morveth or recover influence lost to an Atherian-backed neighbour.

The coalition had considerable military skill and imperfect agreement. Drenoc demanded stores for a common army; some households regarded his collection parties as another threat. He ordered the burning of an exposed grain depot to deny it to Casvilar. Families whose winter food was held there suffered alongside the invading forces.

Casvilar moved quickly against the settlements sustaining the coalition. Capturing one landing separated defenders from river supply, while agreements with rival leaders provided information about inland routes. Drenoc withdrew into Kellovar, a defended height above a wooded tributary approach in the upper Weiss country. The fort held supplies and people from surrounding settlements but could not feed them indefinitely.

## The siege of Kellovar, 383 BR

Casvilar's army built works facing the hillfort and additional defences against the relief forces gathering beyond it. Timber cutting damaged the houses and winter stores of neighbouring communities. Supply parties travelled under guard, and a failed delivery could leave an entire section of the works short of food.

The relief coalition reached the district but could not coordinate its attacks across the forest approaches. Some leaders committed their men while others held back, expecting a clearer opening or fearing that a rival would acquire the advantage. Casvilar used reserves and local guides to reinforce threatened positions.

Within Kellovar, Drenoc expelled people judged unable to fight as provisions diminished. Casvilar refused them passage through his lines. People died between the two forces from hunger and exposure. Velcara used her relationships with neighbouring households to arrange the escape of some children before the last routes closed, then remained with relatives still inside.

Drenoc surrendered after the relief failed. Casvilar distributed captives and allowed selected leaders to retain authority on new terms. Drenoc was taken north and executed during the public celebration of the victory. Velcara survived captivity through ransom arranged by her mother's kin. She returned to a smaller household and helped rebuild food stores, retaining the memory of who had contributed to her release and who had refused.

## The surrendered fighters of Darneth

Resistance continued after Kellovar. At Darneth, a smaller ridge settlement along a forest supply route, defenders used protected water to endure a siege. Before the final engineering effort, military specialists subjected a valuable siege frame to a dangerous load test associated with Astaroth. The frame broke and exposed the direction in which force would carry through the opposing works.

The information helped the engineers direct tunnels and barriers against the protected water supply. Their work forced surrender in 380 BR. Casvilar's subsequent account celebrated the broken frame as heavenly confirmation of the campaign. The engineers' own names survive imperfectly in the local accounts.

Casvilar ordered the hands of captured fighters cut off. The punishment was intended to make the surviving bodies carry his warning into neighbouring communities. Families thereafter supported people whose work and ordinary movement had been permanently changed. Several survivors learned tasks they could perform with assistance; others remained dependent on relatives impoverished by the same campaign.

## Withdrawal and continuing obligations

By 379 BR, Casvilar had acquired secure posts and a wider network of allied obligations. His troops carried wealth and captives north. Some officers remained to supervise contributions; others returned expecting rewards or new commands. Communities that had supported him sought confirmation of land and local authority before his political position changed.

Control remained uneven. Forest routes, winter conditions and the ability of households to move limited what garrisons could collect. Morveth's position depended partly on outside support, while the people around Kellovar rebuilt without a common leader able to replace Drenoc. Atherian citizenship and military service gradually connected some families to the conquerors. Other households preserved the stories of sold kin and the wounded people who had come home.

For Casvilar, the campaigns made surrendering command dangerous. Officers, creditors, veterans and allied leaders depended on decisions that his opponents could reverse. The contest over those commitments formed part of [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic|the final republican crisis]].
