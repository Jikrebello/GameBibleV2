# Seluc Varroth

Seluc Varroth (414–350 BR) was one of the Four at the Stores who governed Caleran after Casvilar's assassination, and First Celebrant of the Common Sky. His ruling of 367 left the Red Field fouled for twenty years, and his augurs kept the register into which the partners wrote their enemies' names. In 353 he carried that register east to Darion Velsar's Salt Field, and in 350 he died on it. The register was never found, which is why the Common Sky's augurs held for the rest of the Age of Glory that the Field of 370 had never been closed, and why the Red Star's priests walked ahead of them.

## Supply and public standing

Varroth's family combined landed resources with service in the civic and religious institutions of Caleran. He developed a reputation for keeping a garrison supplied and arranging movement through difficult passages. Officers could rely on him to ask about pack animals, stored grain and the date at which a road became usable.

His household received associates with considerable ceremony. Varroth valued precedence and the recognition due to his family. He also attended to the practical work required to support that standing, including the estates and transport interests that financed public service.

During the disorder after 370 BR, troops under his authority occupied a connecting belt between the Aurin and eastern supply routes. Thalion and Velsar needed access to its stores. His inclusion in their agreement therefore reflected resources he could actually withhold.

He also held something neither of them could seize. As First Celebrant of the Common Sky he was the officer who could say whether Casvilar's Red Field still stood. Velsar claimed the Wardenship as Casvilar's senior lieutenant and had won the battle of Darsil for it; Thalion refused it. In 367 Varroth ruled. A Warden's acceptance belonged to the man who had stood bareheaded before the Red Star, he declared, and could no more be inherited than a wound; and a contest fouled with blood before it was opened could be closed by no one who had not fought it. The Field therefore stood fouled and unclaimed while any of the seven men who had killed Casvilar lived. Every commander in Atheria understood what that meant: none of them could safely disband an army until the seven were dead. The ruling bought Varroth his seat as surely as his magazines did.

## The Four at the Stores

Varroth joined Velsar, Thalion and the senate's speaker Belisar Venn in the store-house above the muster ground, where the four divided the country by what it could feed. His garrisons held the magazines along the passes while Velsar fought the eastern armies and Thalion paid the Aurin companies.

Tolm's register of challengers lay with Varroth's augurs. When Velsar proposed writing the partners' enemies into it, so that killing them would count as war on the fouled Field, Varroth's clerks wrote the names. Families paid his augurs to keep a name out and rivals paid to put one in, and his college grew rich and hated that winter. He consented to the writing-in of a cousin whose household had sheltered one of the seven, and to Venn's. When Venn walked down to the muster ground with a practice sword in 366 and the soldiers would not kill him, it was Varroth's clerk who wrote *stood* against the name, and Varroth who let the word remain.

## The burning of the magazines

The writing-in was supposed to bring the Field's closing nearer. The war spread instead, and it reached Varroth's own command. In the winter of 364 his pass garrisons, unpaid since the autumn, fought each other over the magazines at the head of the Aurin road and burned them. Old soldiers said the Red Star had been given helpless men instead of fighters and was taking its contests where it found them; Varroth's augurs called it mutiny. With the magazines went the stores on which his place among the partners rested.

He still had the book. Each time one of the seven died, in a forest village, behind a mountain door or on a quay, someone had to bring proof to the First Celebrant before the name could be struck, and commanders who no longer needed his grain still needed his pen. The Kept Doors of Vorthara sent a body down the pass on a mule for him to look at. He kept his seat in the store-house on the strength of five strokes of ink.

## The road to the salt

Through those years the priests of the Red Star blessed Thalion's painted boards and marched with his companies, and the Common Sky's augurs found themselves standing behind them at every camp altar. Varroth valued precedence, and he had ruled in 367 that only a man who fought could close the Field. Thalion would never fight. In the autumn of 353 Velsar sent to Caleran for a witness to his dedication on the salt pans below Sarqen, one whom no soldier could call a creature of his own. Varroth went himself, with the register in a chest, proclaimed that the Star had accepted Velsar, and did not come back. A Warden's field needed its Celebrant, he wrote to his college, and Caleran had only a store-house.

Some in Caleran said he had held to his principle, and others that Velsar had promised him the eastern magazines. He declared on the salt that a painted board could not contest a field. In the winter of 352 a mule train brought down from Avesh the salted body of the sixth of the seven, and Varroth struck the name there, in a tent, with Velsar's officers looking on.

## The death on the Salt Field

In the spring of 350 the spared towns put the queen's house out and stopped feeding Velsar's army, which scattered. Varroth stayed. When Istran Melvo walked out onto the salt with forty crewmen, Velsar and his remaining officers went to meet them, and the First Celebrant, sixty-four years old, went with them carrying the register, because a contest needed its witness. A crewman who had never seen a Celebrant's cap cut him down. Melvo's men said the old man had held a blade. The augurs said he had held a book.

Nobody could find the register afterwards. Melvo's crews swore they had left it lying on the salt. The Common Sky's college said the Red Star's priests had taken it and burned it. The salt-boilers of Hazreth say it is under the pans still, and children there are told not to dig.

When Thalion stood on the muster ground at Caleran that autumn, the Common Sky had no First Celebrant, its augurs would not overrule a dead one, and the seventh name had never been struck. The priests of the Red Star declared the Field closed and its charge taken into Thalion's command, and Thalion gave them first place in ceremonies of victory and command from that day. The Celebrant the college chose in Varroth's place presided over a calendar of festivals in which his own priests walked second. Celebrants of the Common Sky maintained for centuries afterwards that the Field had never been closed, only emptied.
