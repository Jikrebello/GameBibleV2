# The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion

Aurelius Thalion ruled the Atherian Empire from its settlement around 350 BR until his death in 318 BR. The institutions later associated with his name developed throughout these final decades. His government had to pay soldiers, restore movement, govern conquered Sarqen and persuade cities that permanent command would cost them less than renewed competition among armies.

## Paying for peace

Thalion inherited forces promised land and arrears. Istran Melvo supervised parts of the naval demobilisation and the distribution of useful ships among public services, military stations and approved merchants. Units judged essential remained under continuing pay. Others received settlement grants or were discharged with less than their commanders had promised.

Confiscated estates and conquered revenues supplied much of the settlement. In some districts, veterans occupied land whose cultivators had fled temporarily during the wars. The return of those families produced petitions, resistance and occasional killings. Thalion confirmed many grants because undoing them would have threatened the army on which his rule depended.

Regular assessments gradually replaced competing requisitions. Municipal councils were told in advance what grain, money, animals and labour they owed. Predictability allowed some households to plan. Officials still punished failure, and imperial assessments often remained unchanged during a bad local harvest.

## Sarqen and the eastern settlement

The gateway kingdom was annexed after Samyra's death. Thalion preserved Sarqen's working harbour and appointed an imperial supervisor alongside local town officers. Esharat and other cultivated communities kept their irrigation, storage and religious associations while supplying the new government.

Talven and Lethia, Samyra's young children by Darion Velsar, remained in Evara's household at Caleran. They received education appropriate to their birth and were displayed at occasions celebrating reconciliation. They were also hostages whose lives discouraged distant supporters from raising a royal restoration.

Talven later accepted an imperial appointment in Sarqen. Lethia endowed a house for women and children displaced by military settlement. Their work gave surviving royal relationships a place in the new province. Sarqen remained conquered, and their brother Amesar remained dead by Thalion's order.

## The ruler's household

Ilvara Deneth managed audiences, household finance and relationships among provincial visitors. Her influence became more formal as the palace expanded. She supported petitions concerning military widows and secured appointments for several relatives, combining useful access with family advancement.

Thalion and Ilvara's son Caelor was born in 363 BR. He served in provincial administration and was publicly treated as the likely heir. A collapse at a building inspection injured him severely in 326; he died two years later. Workmen and enslaved labourers also died, though imperial memorials concentrated on the prince.

Their daughter Lysara formed another branch of the household through marriage. Her supporters expected her children to receive precedence. Thalion instead adopted his nephew Meren, the son of Evara and Velsar, as his political heir. Meren's descent joined the ruling family to soldiers who had once followed Thalion's rival.

## Provinces, roads and public religion

Governors now required imperial appointment and could be removed before their personal commands became permanent. The treasury separated portions of military revenue from municipal funds. Road stations carried orders and supplied official travellers. These arrangements varied greatly by province and often relied on older local institutions.

Thalion placed Astaroth's priesthood prominently in ceremonies of victory and imperial command. Seluc Varroth retained the senior Common Sky office, which prevented the new order from appearing to abolish every republican dignity. Festivals presented the ruler as the guardian of a public harmony older than his own victory.

The emperor accepted titles and images linking him to celestial order. These political and religious honours magnified a living human ruler. Thalion retained an ordinary human soul, and responsibility for his victories and crimes remained human.

## Death and succession

Thalion's health declined during his later years. Meren increasingly received reports and presided over appointments while Ilvara and Evara managed competing household expectations. The army and senate recognised him before the emperor's death, reducing the opportunity for another open contest.

Thalion died at Caleran in 318 BR, probably after a prolonged illness. His body received the public rites of a founder. Later claims that a star took him bodily are unsupported by contemporary testimony or his burial tradition. Meren inherited a functioning government, unresolved land claims and a family whose members understood succession in different ways.
