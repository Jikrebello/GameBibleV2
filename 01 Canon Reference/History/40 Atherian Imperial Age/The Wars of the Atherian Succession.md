# The Wars of the Atherian Succession

The Atherian succession wars began with Teren Casvilar's murder on the muster ground below Caleran's river stores in 370 BR and ended twenty years later at a table on the landing of Sarqen. In between, no commander in Atheria dared disband an army. Casvilar had died as Warden of the Red Field, struck down by seven men who had entered their names as his challengers, and soldiers and augurs agreed that the Field stood open while any of the seven still lived. The wars became a hunt for seven men through every refuge of the inner sea, and every refuge that sheltered one of them paid for it.

[[Aurelius Thalion]] came out of the wars as founder of the empire without ever commanding in a battle. His companies carried his painted face into the fighting in his place. The war ended when he sat down to eat with carters and fishwives at a table whose oath he swore, and he stood on a field only once, on the day it was empty.

## The seven names, 370–367 BR

As Warden of the Red Field, Casvilar was bound to receive in person any challenger who entered his name in the arms register kept by his quartermaster, Deceran Tolm. In the spring of 370 seven men did so, led by Mavren Seneth and Corial Draven. They met him on the muster ground and stabbed him before the contest was opened. The augurs declared the Field fouled, and the camps round Caleran settled what that meant before any college could: a contest struck before it opened could not be closed while one of the seven lived, and until it closed, the war it had begun would go on spreading. Men who had followed Casvilar for twenty years would not go home and leave his killers walking.

[[Darion Velsar]], his senior lieutenant, held Casvilar's papers and the loyalty of officers who had served under both men, and claimed the Wardenship as his by seniority. Aurelius Thalion, Casvilar's sister's son and designated heir, reached Caleran four days after the killing and was taken to see the sand where his uncle had fallen. The next morning he refused the Field in the forum. It had killed his uncle, he said, as surely as the knives had, and he would not stand on it. Veterans jeered him. The senate's speaker, Belisar Venn, backed him all the same, reckoning that an heir who refused the army's rite would need the senate more than Velsar ever would. Thalion's mother Lysara sold the family's town houses to pay men her brother had promised land.

The seven had friends in Caleran and armies in the provinces. One of them was killed at his own door in the winter of 369 by veterans who had waited a week in the street for him; he was the last of them to die in Caleran, because the others had already gone. Deceran Tolm, who had chosen the hour of the killing, was taken on the coast road in 368 and killed.

In 367 [[Seluc Varroth]], First Celebrant of the Common Sky, gave the ruling every commander had been waiting for. A Warden's acceptance, he declared, belonged to the man who had stood bareheaded before the Red Star and could no more be inherited than a wound, and a contest fouled before its opening could be closed by no one who had not fought in it. Velsar's claim failed. So did any hope of a quick end. The Field stood open and unclaimed, and no commander could disband an army while it did.

## The Four at the Stores, 367 BR

Armies that could not go home still had to eat. Four men took charge of their bread: Velsar, who commanded the largest of them; Thalion, who had Casvilar's money and his veterans' promises; Varroth, who held the magazines and passes between the Aurin and the eastern roads; and Belisar Venn, who kept the river stores for the senate. They met in the store-house above the muster ground, among the grain bins and the tally clerks, and soldiers stood in the aisles of the assembly while it confirmed what the store-house had decided. Caleran called them the Four at the Stores.

They divided the country by what it could feed. Velsar's armies took the eastern ports and their client grain, Thalion's companies the Aurin and the outer-coast districts, and Varroth's garrisons the magazines along the passes. The river stores fed the capital and the camps around it. Every barge that came down the Aurin was counted twice, once by Venn's clerks and once by soldiers.

## The Writing-In, 367–364 BR

The partners needed money and they needed their enemies dead, and the soldiers balked. Men who had fought other Atherians in the field would not drag a senator out of his dining room. Velsar found a way round their scruple. The seven names stood in Tolm's register, which now lay with Varroth's augurs; a man named there was a challenger on the open Field, and killing him was war, not murder. Velsar proposed that they write in other names.

They did. Through the winter of 367 the augurs entered the names the partners brought them, and squads took the named men from their houses, their farms and the boats they hid in, and killed them on the muster ground so that the deaths would count as deaths on the Field. Each name struck, the soldiers were told, brought them nearer home. Boys were dragged down with their fathers. Families paid augurs to keep a name out of the book or to put a rival's in, and buyers bid for the estates of men not yet dead. Some enslaved attendants hid their owners in grain pits and drains; others led the squads to the door or used the disorder to escape. The Aurin below the stores ran red that season, and the river people would not draw water there for a generation.

Velsar asked for Belisar Venn's name. Venn had spoken against the writing-in in the store-house, and Velsar wanted the river stores out of the senate's hands. Thalion consented, and Varroth's clerk wrote the name. Venn did not wait to be fetched. He walked down to the muster ground in the morning with a practice sword borrowed from his grandson, stood bareheaded where Casvilar had stood and waited for the soldiers. There were nine of them, and he wounded one. The river stores passed to Thalion. Varroth, for his part, consented to the writing-in of a cousin whose household had sheltered one of the seven.

The writing-in was supposed to bring the Field's closing nearer. The war grew instead. Eastern cities that had paid both sides chose one; provincial commands that had sat still marched; two foraging columns sent into different valleys met on the same narrow bridge in the eastern hills and fought until neither could cross. In the winter of 364 Varroth's own pass garrisons, unpaid since the autumn, fought each other over the magazines at the head of the Aurin road and burned them, and Varroth lost the stores on which his place among the partners rested. Old soldiers said the Red Star had been offered helpless men instead of fighters and was finding its own contests. The Common Sky's augurs called it disorder and nothing more. Augurs and soldiers argued over it for centuries. No one wrote in another name after 364.

## Darsil, 363 BR

Mavren Seneth and Corial Draven understood the Field as well as anyone. While one of them lived, the armies hunting them could not disband, and armies that could not disband would eat the country that fed them and turn it against their masters. The seven did not give battle. They scattered to the refuges of the inner sea and left their officers and provincial friends to fight.

Those friends held the approaches around Darsil, an Atherian supply town on the mainland routes behind the Thalassian ports. Velsar broke them there in 363, after securing a road that let his own provisions through and threatened the defenders' stores. It was the largest battle of the wars. Afterwards his soldiers searched the dead and the prisoners for the seven and found none of them. Some defeated officers took service with the victors; others fled to the sea. Velsar's men had been told the Field would close when the republican army fell. They learned they would not be going home.

## The quartered winter, 362–360 BR

An army that could not disband could not be settled either. There were no veteran grants while the Field lay open, so Velsar quartered his victorious companies on Darsil itself. The town fed them through one winter and starved through the next. In the spring of 361 the bake-house women barred the ovens, and men of the lower town killed soldiers in the lanes at night. Velsar's brother Tarsen, who commanded the garrison, took what grain was left into the citadel and hanged eleven organisers in the market. The town gave in when its children began to die. Velsar's wife Miretha Ardel came east with carts of grain bought with her own estates, fed the town for a month and died there of the winter fever in 360.

Darsil remembered Tarsen's gallows and Miretha's carts for three hundred years, often in the same song. Velsar had sent neither the order nor the grain. He had sent no countermand either.

## The hunt through the refuges

The seven went where Atherian law could not follow. Refuge was older than the republic, and the Chain Peace of 461 had made it law at sea: every harbour must take in a ship in distress, and the custodians of Ilamar heard the complaints. The partners had to break a refuge or buy it before they could reach a man inside it, and each refuge answered differently.

One of the seven went south to Darneth, to the forest people whose men Casvilar had maimed after the siege of 380. They took in their enemy's killer gladly, and he died among them of a winter fever around 362. Another reached Vorthara, where the households of the Kept Doors had held their doors against Lethren Ostar's agents in 399. They held them again against Velsar's, and when the man died of age behind them in 356, they sent his body down the pass on a mule so that his name could be struck. Iren Sallas sat for eight years in the sanctuary precinct on Ilamar, where Marovan Veyr had died, while Thalion's men waited on the quay; the custodians' court wrote the certificates that fed Thalion's armies, and he would not storm it. In 359, having heard what Darsil had suffered, Sallas walked out of the precinct and down to the quay to them, so that he would not be the reason the war went on. Corial Draven went to sea, to Ardel Veyr's fleet and the moving city of Marelith. Another went east to Avesh, the Vasharan road-city that had sheltered Caleran's fugitives since Vespen Harod's day.

Mavren Seneth went to Sarqen. He came to the landing in 362 on a carter's wagon and sat down at the spared towns' unranked table, from which no stranger who reached it could be sold. The towns fed him. Queen [[Samyra Nerethan]] kept him, because the oath on which her crown stood forbade her to do otherwise, and because while he lived the Atherians could not disband the armies that bought her grain. When the partners demanded him in 361, she answered them with the oath.

## The painted board

Thalion went on no campaign. Through twenty years of war he stayed at the river stores, where he paid, wrote, remembered the names of men he had met once, and sent other men to command. The companies he paid marched behind a painted board of his face on a pole, so that soldiers who had never seen him could swear to him. In the eastern camps such a board was simply called a thalion. Soldiers mocked an heir who sent his picture to war, and kept swearing to it, because the man behind it paid on the day he promised.

## Merath and the child in the grain bin, 361–358 BR

Thalion's first wife, Ilvara Deneth, died in 361 giving birth to their daughter. Within the year he married again, and his choice made the war personal. Merath Nerethan was Samyra's elder sister, passed over when the towns restored Samyra in 373 and since then a claimant in search of a patron. She found one at Caleran. Velsar backed one daughter of King Olymes; Thalion now backed the other.

In 359 Merath went east to Esharat, the inland town of her ancestor Hadrima, to bear her child where the heirs of the house were born. Velsar returned in 358 from his campaign into the approaches of Avesh, the Vasharan road-city, having lost his convoys to Vasharan relay riders and his retreat to early snow, and he plundered a smaller ally on the way home. His brother Tarsen did not come back. Velsar needed Samyra's grain and ships more than ever. At her request his officers seized Merath at Esharat and executed her.

The queen's officers also asked for the child. The women of Esharat hid the newborn boy in a grain bin, as Hesara of Esharat had hidden children from Qarzeth's collectors in the same town a hundred and sixty years before, and the town elders told the officers that the oath said *no child for the table*. Carters took him west along the inland roads under a load of barley. Merath had named him Meren. Whether Samyra wanted the child dead or only in her keeping is still argued. The towns believed the first, and did not forget.

## Calvena's terms and Marelith, 357–354 BR

Ardel Veyr, Marovan Veyr's son, held the sea between the partners and their grain. He sheltered people who had fled the writing-in, Corial Draven among them, and seized merchant ships to feed his fleet. In 357 Calvena Casvilar, Thalion's cousin and Ardel's stepmother, carried terms between them, the one person both men would receive. The agreement protected some fugitives and recognised some of Ardel's possessions. It broke within a season over withheld grain and captured hulls.

Ardel hired [[Marelith]], the harbour-city carried on a living reefback, for ships and stores; its captains kept their own government. Sailors had called it "the harbour without a chain" since the gale of 466, when it took in the crews shut out of the League's harbours, and families fleeing the writing-in had gone there for the same reason. In 355 the city followed Ardel east of its ordinary circuit into the central Thalassian anchorages, where the water was shallow.

[[Istran Melvo]], Thalion's admiral, had grown up in the shipyards of Pyrassa, and he brought Pyrassa's great harbour chain with him. Marelith's founding covenant forbade it a permanent anchorage. Melvo's crews laid the chain across the shallows where the bearer fed and drew it tight between two sunken hulks. Accounts describe an apparent prize ship admitted near the city, carrying hidden attackers and the sea-fire whose making was a guarded Atherian secret. Held fast, the bearer fought the chain. The city's attachments tore, its lamps and cookfires spilled into the living works, and the fire ran along the shell. People died in the fire and smoke, were lost when structures tore away, or drowned when the vessels separated. The burned mass grounded as the [[Black Reef]]. Draven died aboard. Melvo's fleet broke Ardel's main force nearby in the same operation.

Ardel escaped east to Avesh. Its command had seen Velsar's columns in its passes four years before and wanted no second visit, so it gave up Ardel and the fugitive of the seven it had sheltered together, and Velsar's officers executed both in 354. Marelith's survivors were dispersed, pardoned or sold. Atherian accounts celebrated the end of a pirate nest. The survivors and the Elmarai pilots called the burning a second Kelarion, and sailors said that the harbour without a chain had been killed with one.

Six of the seven were now dead. The seventh sat at a table in Sarqen.

## The Salt Field, 353 BR

Velsar's power in the east now rested on Samyra. She supplied food, ships and credit; he supplied soldiers and protection against her neighbours' claims and against Thalion, whose wife she had had killed. Their children Talven and Lethia were born in 357 and 354 and joined Amesar, her son by Casvilar, in the royal household. Velsar's veterans liked none of it. Many had served under Casvilar, and their general was guarding Casvilar's murderer at the queen's table. They began to desert to Thalion, who paid on the day.

In 353 Velsar answered the deserters with the rite they understood. On the salt pans below Sarqen, where the towns boiled the salt that stood on their oath table, he dedicated his arms before an altar of the Red Star and named Aurelius Thalion and Istran Melvo as his enemies. His augurs proclaimed that the Star had accepted him. He had made himself Warden of a new field, and he meant it to force Thalion to come to him. A man who would not meet a named enemy in person, Velsar believed, could not keep an Atherian army.

Thalion did not come. He had grown up on the story of Vespen Harod, who lived twenty-five years in a tent on the muster ground while the enemies he had named lived comfortably at Avesh. Thalion's companies came to the coast behind his painted boards and fought Velsar's outposts. Velsar's priests declared that a board could not contest a field. Velsar was bound to the salt all the same, because the man he had named was still at war with him, and a Warden could not leave his field while his named enemy contested it. The towns called it the Salt Field.

That year the treasury advocate Corven Ilar told the assembly at Caleran that Samyra was queen of the child-burning city, heir of the Bronze-Faced Provider, keeper of Casvilar's murderer and killer of Thalion's wife. Envoys from the spared towns answered him in the same assembly. They recited the Table Oath, which every Nerethan ruler had sworn at the towns' unranked table since 457 BR: *no child for the table; no seat kept empty; no guest served by worth.* Then they read out the names of the Gateway hostage children whom Caleran itself had killed on its own festival ground in 609 BR to win the Red Star's favour against Zamaret. The names took some time to read. Thalion, it was noticed, stood while they were read. The assembly voted for war.

## The Fourth Collection, 352–351 BR

An army that could not leave its ground had to be fed on it. In 352 Melvo's ships closed Sarqen's outer anchorage, and the queen's officers turned inland. They took grain, oil, animals and young men from the towns for the Salt Field, by what each household could be made to give, and on the field itself the officers' tables stayed full while the Gateway levies went hungry. The towns gave it a name from the worst years of their history: the Fourth Collection.

That autumn the carters of Esharat and Belqara opened their sealed jars at the count and found them a third empty, every seal whole and the underside of the wax bitten, as their ancestors' jars had been in the years when the Provider's tables at Qarzeth never ran low. The queen's officers blamed thieves. The towns said the Provider had come back to a table that served by worth.

In 351 the queen's pilots made a bargain with something in the sea to bring the royal galleys out past Melvo's line to the landing at Hazreth. The price was cargo surrendered and a disused galley basin opened to the sea. The wounded from the Salt Field lay in the sheds of that basin, and places aboard the galleys had been assigned to them. The officers filled those places with the queen's treasury and left the wounded on the quay. The current carried the galleys through. Melvo's landing parties killed or took the men left behind. One of them, a rower from Hazreth named Tobal, who lost a leg on the quay, was sent home with the other captives the following spring, and the towns heard the story from them before they heard the queen's.

## The guest at the table, 350 BR

Every spring the towns renewed the oath at Sarqen's landing, on long boards with no high end, each family bringing what food it had. In the spring of 350 Mavren Seneth sat at that table for the twelfth time. Amesar, Samyra's son by Casvilar, was twenty-two and had watched his father's killer eat at his mother's table since he was ten. He stood up from the boards, walked down the table past the carriers and the fishwives, and killed Seneth with the knife he had brought to cut bread. He said afterwards that he had meant to end the war: Thalion had come for Seneth, and now there was nothing left to come for.

The towns had risen against a minister in 373 for laying a table to kill a guest. Now a son of the house had killed one at the table itself. Some elders wanted Amesar judged as Vardek had been. Others, descendants of Sapha of Esharat who had set the oath's rules, said the towns would not pay for their table with a child of the house, however grown. In the end they did not touch him. They put the house out instead. When the boards were laid again a week later, the seat where the queen had always sat was filled by Tobal of Hazreth, the rower left on the basin quay. No town in the Gateway country would seat a Nerethan as its ruler after that, and Amesar they would not seat at all. Without the towns the queen had no grain, no carters and no gates.

Amesar took passage on a fishing boat to Ilamar and entered the precinct disarmed, as Marovan Veyr had in 373. The towns sent to Thalion. The landing would be open to him, they said, if he came to it himself, sat at their table and swore their oath.

## The end of the Salt Field

Velsar's army was gone within the month, because nobody would feed it. He could not follow it. Thalion still contested his field, and a Warden may not flee. He stayed on the salt with a few officers who would not leave him. Thalion wanted him left there until he gave up his claim before the Red Star's priests, which would have ended the war with a confession. Melvo, whom Velsar had also named, thought it a shameful way to treat a soldier. He came ashore and walked out onto the salt in person with forty of his crewmen, and Velsar and his officers went to meet them. Velsar was killed with a sword in his hand on the field he had chosen. Thalion never forgave Melvo for it, and the sailors' songs never forgave Thalion.

## The table at the landing

Thalion crossed to Sarqen in the summer of 350 on Melvo's ships. Most people on the landing had never seen him: a heavy, short-sighted man of forty, plainer than his boards. He brought his son Meren, eight years old, whom the women of Esharat had hidden in a grain bin.

The towns laid the boards at the landing. Before he came ashore, Samyra Nerethan walked down from the palace with Talven and Lethia and sat at the table, not in the queen's seat, which Tobal held, but in a free place among the carters, as any stranger might. The oath forbade the towns to sell a stranger from their table, and it would bind Thalion too once he had sworn it. He had meant to take her to Caleran. He sat down three places from her, beside a salt-boiler from Hazreth, ate what was put in front of him and swore: *no child for the table; no seat kept empty; no guest served by worth.* The women who had hidden Meren sat across from him.

The terms were made at the same table. The kingdom ended, and Sarqen passed under an imperial governor, who was to come down to the landing every spring and swear the oath before the towns would supply him. Samyra and her children would stay in the Gateway country as guests of the towns, and no one would take them from it. Meren was recognised as Merath's son and an heir of the house. At the next count, the carters said, the jars at Esharat were full to their marks and the wax unbitten. Atherian writers called the meal the Concord of the Landing. The towns remembered it as the day the conqueror came to dinner.

## The settlement at Caleran

Thalion returned to Caleran through the eastern and highland routes. Every one of the seven was dead. He went down to the muster ground below the river stores, where Casvilar had died, and stood there bareheaded. It was the only field he ever stood on.

Seluc Varroth would not declare the Field closed. His own ruling of 367 said that only a man who had fought in the contest could close it, and Thalion had fought in nothing. The priests of the Red Star, who had blessed Thalion's painted boards for fifteen years, declared it closed and its charge taken into his command. Thalion gave them first place in every ceremony of victory and command from that day. Varroth kept his office, and his college walked second.

Thalion made no pretence that the republic had come back. He governed from the store-house above the muster ground, where the Four had sat in 367, and left the senate the markets, festivals and water of the city of Caleran. The armies that had not dared disband for twenty years were disbanded in four, onto the Noon Roll lands, the estates of the written-in and farms whose owners had fled the fighting. Soldiers received land and pay on the day promised. Families driven off that land petitioned the victors for generations.

For the Aurin farmers who had fed two armies a year, peace meant one collector instead of several and roads full of carts instead of columns. Those conditions formed the opening of [[The Atherian Empire – Age of Glory|the Age of Glory]].
