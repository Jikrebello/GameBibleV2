# The Wars of the Atherian Succession

The Atherian succession wars began after Teren Casvilar's assassination in 370 BR and ended with Aurelius Thalion's supremacy around 350 BR. The struggle passed through changing alliances, a public government of three commanders, renewed civil conflict and war in the gateway kingdom. Its participants inherited armies, debts and household promises that the killing of Casvilar had left unsettled.

Thalion emerged as founder of the empire through these wars. His success depended on colleagues, relatives, suppliers and cities whose interests he learned to join to his own. The settlement brought greater regularity to government while preserving the dispossession and political killing through which its leading families had acquired power.

## The competing inheritances

[[Darion Velsar]] had senior military standing, access to Casvilar's papers and the loyalty of officers who had served under both men. [[Aurelius Thalion]], about twenty at the assassination, possessed the dead ruler's designated inheritance. His mother Lysara, Casvilar's sister, connected him to family resources and people who remembered earlier assistance.

The senate's speaker Belisar Venn supported Thalion against Velsar, believing that the younger man's claim could divide the army. Thalion accepted the assistance while recruiting independently. His household sold property and borrowed to meet promised payments. Veterans who joined him were defending expectations created during Casvilar's commands as well as an attachment to his name.

The conspirators retained supporters in Caleran and provincial armies. They disagreed about whether to preserve Casvilar's grants, revoke his appointments or pay troops whose leaders now opposed them. Deceran Tolm, the trusted officer who had helped arrange the assassination, was captured and killed in 368 BR during these first struggles.

## The government of three, 367 BR

Thalion and Velsar fought and negotiated before accepting that neither could safely exclude the other. [[Seluc Varroth]] supplied a third concentration of troops, magazines and pass routes linking the Aurin with eastern supply. His resources made cooperation practicable.

In 367 BR, the three obtained public authority through an intimidated assembly and the support of armed followers. Their commission permitted appointments, military levies and confiscations for the stated purpose of restoring the republic. It differed from the earlier private partnership of Casvilar, Veyr and Ostar: the three commanders now claimed a collective office capable of overriding ordinary arrangements.

Their first agreement divided work among existing resources. Thalion took principal responsibility for the Aurin, the outer-coast districts and payments. Velsar commanded the main expedition against the remaining republican armies. Varroth held the connecting military belt, its magazines and important passages. Cities and local officers still had to be persuaded or compelled to supply them.

## Proscriptions and the price of agreement

The partners authorised lists of people to be killed and estates to be taken. Each protected some associates and abandoned others. Belisar Venn, who had helped Thalion rise, was surrendered to Velsar's demand and murdered. Varroth consented to the killing of a cousin whose household had supported the conspirators. Thalion approved confiscations needed to meet the soldiers' expectations.

Denunciation created opportunities for private enemies and purchasers of property. Families hid fugitives, paid for escape or turned relatives away in fear of punishment. Some enslaved attendants helped owners flee; others used the disorder to escape themselves or settle injuries. The surviving lists describe only part of the violence, since local killings also occurred under the protection of public orders.

The partners presented the measures as the means of paying troops and defeating Casvilar's murderers. Their decisions also destroyed people with little influence over the assassination. Property passed into the hands of families that would later celebrate the return of public order.

## Defeat of the republican armies, 366–363 BR

Mavren Seneth and Corial Draven gathered forces among eastern clients and provincial officers unwilling to accept the three-command government. They held the approaches around Darsil, an Atherian supply town on the mainland routes behind the Thalassian ports. Its stores and road connections made it important to both armies.

Velsar directed much of the fighting. Thalion's troops contributed, but illness and supply problems limited his presence during part of the campaign. In 363 BR, Velsar broke the opposing position after securing a route that allowed provisions to reach his own army and threatened the defenders' stores. Draven was killed in the defeat. Seneth took his own life rather than surrender.

Some defeated officers accepted service under the victors; others escaped to maritime refuges. Garrison submissions continued after the principal armies had fallen. The result increased Velsar's standing among soldiers while leaving Thalion in control of resources important to their rewards.

## New divisions and displaced households

The partners rearranged their responsibilities after victory. Velsar took the eastern military and royal relationships, with access to revenue from coastal cities. Thalion supervised veteran settlement and the western and central fiscal resources. Varroth's original connecting command was reduced to particular magazines and naval support districts around the eastern approaches.

Veteran settlements transferred cultivated holdings to soldiers. Some grants used available public land; others expelled resident families or confirmed purchases made under military pressure. An owner who had supplied the army could still lose land to its returning followers. The problem was especially severe where wartime damage had reduced the ground available for cultivation.

Miretha Ardel, Velsar's wife, used her property and household connections to support displaced families and maintain her husband's influence. His brother Tarsen joined armed resistance around Darsil in 362 BR. Miretha supplied food and money while arguing that Thalion's settlements were destroying the communities whose work had sustained victory.

The rebellion was besieged and defeated in 361 BR. Hunger killed inhabitants before surrender, and troops executed selected organisers afterwards. Tarsen escaped into exile. Miretha continued supporting surviving dependants and died in 360 BR. Velsar had offered encouragement from the east without bringing the army needed to save the town. His absence remained a source of accusation within his own family.

## Marriage and a fragile peace

In 359 BR, Velsar married Thalion's sister Evara. She brought household resources and a recognised channel through which the partners could negotiate. She arranged payments, received envoys and cared for dependants from Velsar's earlier household as well as their own children, Meren and Sovia.

The marriage supported a temporary accommodation. Its usefulness depended on decisions about commands and supplies which affection alone could not settle. Velsar's eastern campaigns required funds and recruits, while Thalion needed soldiers and ships to deal with the maritime opposition.

Velsar attempted a northern extension of his eastern command in 358 BR. Defended water, attacks on separated columns and inadequate provision forced a costly retreat. He subsequently seized people and stores from a smaller allied ruler whom he blamed for the failure. The campaign increased his dependence on the gateway kingdom and damaged trust among other clients.

## The sea-heir and the naval organiser

Marovan Veyr's son Ardel Veyr maintained a following among displaced officers, refugees and island communities. He offered shelter to people fleeing the proscriptions and took control of shipping that could support them. Merchants also faced forced payments and detention of crews. Gratitude for refuge and anger at coercion existed within the same coastal world.

Thalion relied on [[Istran Melvo]], a former shipyard worker and military organiser, to contest those routes. Melvo repaired vessels, trained crews and secured stores at cooperating ports. His campaign involved negotiation with residents whose harbours had already borne repeated levies. Seizure of timber and compulsory labour accompanied improvements in the fleet's ability to remain at sea.

An agreement with Ardel in 357 BR recognised some possessions and promised protection for fugitives. It broke down over withheld supplies, disputed returns of property and the continued seizure of vessels. In 355 BR, Melvo defeated the sea-heir's main force among the central Thalassian anchorages. Ardel escaped east and was executed in 354 BR by one of Velsar's officers. Refugee households were dispersed; some obtained pardon, while others were sold or surrendered to old enemies.

Varroth attempted to claim the surrendered forces and harbours during the victory. Thalion secured their allegiance through payment and promises of continued service. Varroth's troops followed, leaving him unable to enforce his demand. He relinquished military command but retained his estates and office as First Celebrant of the Common Sky.

## Velsar and Samyra

[[Samyra Nerethan]] supplied the food, ships and credit Velsar needed after his failed campaign. Their relationship was political and personal. She sought protection for her dynasty and recognition of claims that neighbouring rulers disputed. He wanted a dependable base from which to govern the east without accepting Thalion's control of resources.

Their children Talven and Lethia joined Amesar, Samyra's son by Casvilar, within the royal household. In 354 BR, Velsar publicly recognised titles for the queen and her children, including rights over districts not entirely under their control. The ceremony changed expectations among clients and rivals. It could not make absent governors obey or provide the forces needed to occupy contested land.

Evara returned to her own household as relations between the partners deteriorated. She continued to care for their children and some of Miretha's surviving dependants. Her exclusion became part of Thalion's public case against Velsar, although she continued seeking protection for people who had served her husband.

Samyra pursued enemies within her own dynasty. At her request, Velsar ordered the seizure and execution of her older sister Merath in 358 BR. Merath had supported a rival claim and sought foreign patrons. Her removal helped the queen while leaving families attached to her branch with reasons to oppose the reigning household.

## The final war, 353–350 BR

Thalion's supporters depicted Velsar as surrendering Atherian resources to a foreign royal house. The treasury advocate Corven Ilar sought to associate Samyra with the child-sacrificing capital destroyed generations before. Envoys from the spared towns answered by recounting their ancestors' resistance to that institution. The accusation nevertheless helped turn royal difference into an argument for war.

Actual interests were more immediate. Thalion and Velsar competed over appointments, payments and the ability to reward followers. Samyra sought the preservation of her kingdom and children. Coastal rulers assessed which alliance could protect their ports, and some changed sides after Melvo threatened the routes supplying them.

Melvo's fleet restricted access to Sarqen's outer anchorage in 352 BR while land forces contested its approaches. The royal coalition attempted to break the blockade and preserve a fleet for continued resistance. Part of its force escaped; other vessels were lost or surrendered. Samyra reached shelter with a reduced company, and Velsar withdrew towards the royal country.

During one withdrawal, the queen's pilots secured a safe current through a sea bargain. Cargo was to be surrendered and a disused galley basin opened to flooding. Officers substituted treasure for some of the wounded assigned places aboard. The current carried the chosen ships through, while people left at the basin were killed or captured. The queen's later account described the passage without naming everyone abandoned to obtain it.

The campaign continued through shortages, defections and the loss of supporting towns. In 350 BR, Thalion's forces entered Sarqen. Velsar, wounded and facing capture, killed himself. Samyra negotiated for her children, then took her own life before she could be carried to Caleran. Accounts differ about the means of her death.

Thalion ordered Amesar executed. The acknowledged son of Casvilar and heir of the royal house offered opponents a claim around which to gather. Talven and Lethia survived and entered Evara's household under imperial custody. Evara secured separate attendants and contact with people from their mother's country; their removal still ended the life for which the royal household had prepared them.

## The settlement at Caleran

After securing the gateway country's revenues and appointments, Thalion returned through the established eastern and highland routes to Caleran. Around 350 BR, he took final control of the capital and its military government. His earlier participation in the three-command administration had left rivals capable of contesting him. The new settlement concentrated supreme command in his household.

He organised continuing military service and payment, subjected provincial appointments to central approval and regularised taxation. Republican offices survived with civic and ceremonial duties, while the senate lost meaningful control of the army. Promises to soldiers became more dependable, and local governments acquired clearer channels through which to seek decisions.

Astaroth's priesthood received precedence in ceremonies of imperial victory and command. The Common Sky continued, and Varroth retained a religious office the ruler could not simply treat as vacant. Public honours joined Casvilar's conquests to Thalion's settlement while minimising disagreements about the killings and confiscations between them.

For many households, peace meant the return of trade, predictable duties and fewer armies competing for food. Others lived on land taken from people still seeking its return. Those conditions formed the opening of [[The Atherian Empire – Age of Glory|the Age of Glory]].
