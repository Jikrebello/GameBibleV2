# The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth

The **Years of Three Collections**, approximately 548–540 BR, name the most extensive child-taking campaigns conducted by the Gateway Republic. Military defeat, territorial loss and public fear enlarged an older sacrificial institution. Priests of the Bronze-Faced Provider promised security if Qarzeth offered what its people could not replace, and civic officials carried the demand into dependent towns which had previously offered animals or money.

The collections became a principal cause of Caleran's final war against Qarzeth. Internal opponents supplied testimony, guided rescuers and helped the besiegers. Caleran ended the sacrificial institution and then destroyed its city, murdering, raping and enslaving inhabitants during the sack. The lesser Gateway towns survived and inherited both histories.

## The civic sanctuary

The Provider's sanctuary stood in Qarzeth's upper district. Its estates owned workshops, gardens, stores and dwellings. Priests distributed food during festivals and financed public works. Leading households gained prestige by supporting it. These services made the institution part of civic life long before its last decades.

The cult taught that security required an irreplaceable return. Human sacrifice occurred at changing frequency across the Republic's history. Families offered infants in moments of fear; officials also took older captives, dependants and children purchased from poor households. Wealthy patrons sometimes surrendered their own children and sometimes bought substitutes. Soldiers enforced demands upon dependent towns.

The Bronze-Faced Provider was a dependent Gluttony Mask. It appeared in smoke, dreams and periods of collective fear. Good harvests following sacrifice strengthened the cult, even where weather and ordinary labour explained the plenty. Priests shaped the doctrine, magistrates authorised collectors, patrons paid and guards held the victims. The Mask exploited their choices and promised that each finite offering would require another.

## Ilya and Sapha

Collectors travelled with guards and household lists. Families hid children in orchards, cargo and relatives' homes. Neighbours informed for payment or to protect their own households. Punishments included confiscation, imprisonment and the seizure of another family member.

**Ilya of Qarzeth** worked in the sanctuary stores. Before the third collection, she copied the names of imprisoned children onto packing strips and escaped in a grain cart. Her list contained errors and omissions while identifying holding rooms, officials and districts with direct knowledge.

At Esharat, the midwife **Sapha** organised carriers, fishing households and rural kin into a rescue route. They moved children beneath produce and through irrigation channels. The network saved dozens and failed many others. Collectors killed Sapha's husband during a search and took her eldest daughter, Neral. Neral was sacrificed before the rescuers reached Qarzeth.

The opponents disagreed about their objective. Ilya wanted the sanctuary dismantled and the Republic preserved. Sapha wanted Qarzeth's authority broken. Belqara leaders sought independence, while exiles called for the city's destruction. Their testimony nevertheless established that the killings belonged to a public institution protected by the Republic.

## Debate in Caleran

Escaped families carried their evidence to Atherian ports. The elderly senator **Orsen Valeor** placed Ilya's list and survivors beside him while demanding that the child-taking city fall. He sincerely opposed the sacrifices and believed Qarzeth's recovery threatened every Atherian alliance.

Valeor's coalition included people seeking rescued children, safer shipping, land, profit and military distinction. Senator **Mera Castren** advocated inspection, permanent hostages and compulsory closure of the sanctuary. Gateway opponents supported parts of her proposal because annihilation would punish the families who had resisted. Valeor argued that the rooms would reopen after an Atherian withdrawal.

Qarzeth surrendered weapons and selected hostages. Caleran then demanded that the inhabitants abandon the city and rebuild inland. The assembly refused. Opponents of sacrifice defended their homes beside cult patrons, and some were killed trying to overthrow the priesthood during the siege.

## Siege and rescue

The blockade lasted several campaigning seasons. Belqara and Esharat supplied the Atherians under agreements promising survival. Sarqen admitted refugees and repaired allied ships. Hunger killed children, elderly people and enslaved labourers first in Qarzeth's crowded districts. Priests continued selected sacrifices and claimed that surrender would make every earlier death meaningless.

Ilya guided an Atherian detachment towards the holding rooms after the wall was breached in 520 BR. Soldiers killed the guards, broke the Provider's image and found living children beside cremated remains. Parents recovered some captives and identified the clothing or tokens of others.

Across the rest of the city, Atherian soldiers murdered residents, raped captives and organised survivors for sale. Some rescued children were separated again during the distribution of prisoners. Officers intervened unevenly, and commanders permitted looting and mass enslavement. Captured priests and officials were executed.

## Destruction and salting

Caleran's senate had decided that Qarzeth would never again become the capital of the Gateway country. Engineers marked the sanctuary, civic squares, elite gardens, store compounds and selected waterworks. Salt carried through the harbour was spread heavily over those districts. Cisterns were spoiled, channels broken and building stone removed.

This policy devastated the urban centre and left most of the surrounding country habitable. Villages, fields and lesser towns continued under new rulers. The salting required organised transport and labour and served as a deliberate statement that Qarzeth would never again govern the Gateway country.

The Gluttony Mask lost its sanctuary, public offerings and civic protection. Its Throne survived elsewhere. Fragments of the cult remained among exiles and fraudulent claimants without inheriting the authority of the Republic.

## Survivors and memory

Ilya spent her later life at Esharat matching rescued people with relatives. Atherian histories made her a grateful witness to Caleran virtue. Her surviving fragments named children lost after the breach and cursed soldiers who sold them.

Sapha refused office within the Spared Town League. She trained carriers to recognise marks used by traffickers. Esharat later honoured her family while omitting the households she had threatened into cooperation.

Valeor died before the final assault. Caleran raised a statue showing him with a rescued infant. Gateway survivors later broke its face. Mera Castren helped establish ransom funds and continued defending the senate's right to govern the conquered country.

The spared towns placed a prohibition against human sacrifice in their later agreements, and Nerethan kings repeated it at accession. The prohibition became a genuine moral inheritance and a source of royal legitimacy. It did not end slavery, compulsory labour or political violence within the successor kingdom.
