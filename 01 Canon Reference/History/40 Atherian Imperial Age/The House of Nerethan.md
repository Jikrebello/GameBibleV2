# The House of Nerethan

The **House of Nerethan** ruled the Gateway country from Sarqen between 457 and 350 BR, and through Merath's son Meren its blood passed into the first imperial house of Atheria. Its founder belonged to a Delkosi officer house descended from the maritime kingdoms created after Eryndor III's death. He married Hadrima of Esharat, whose family belonged to one of the towns spared when Qarzeth was destroyed. Their partnership joined Atherian military patronage and Successor naval culture to local land, shipping and kinship. The dynasty's last independent ruler was [[Samyra Nerethan]].

The royal family inherited a country already changed by [[The Gateway Wars]]. The capital's sacrificial establishment had been destroyed, while lesser towns retained working wells, farms and ports. Refugees and returning captives joined communities whose households had their own claims to survival and resistance. Royal power had to be established among them.

## Nerethan and Hadrima

Nerethan of Delkos was born around 525 BR and arrived as an Atherian harbour commander in 499 BR. His family belonged to the later officer houses of Delkos, where Serevan's former Successor kingdom had left a mixed naval and royal tradition. Nerethan inherited military titles, drill and patronage from that history. His appointment concerned coastal defence, military provisions and the supervision of dependent towns. Officers and specialists accompanied him, but local pilots, cultivators and transport households provided the means to remain.

Hadrima's family held cultivated land and a share in the storage and carrying trade of Esharat. The town lay inland from the coastal landings, close enough for its produce to reach them by cart and pack animal. Its households had sheltered children fleeing Qarzeth's collectors and had supplied passage during the final intervention. Some relatives prospered after the war; others lost people or property to the victorious army.

Hadrima's mother **Hesara**, then a young woman, had hidden children in Esharat's grain bins and irrigation culverts during the siege years of 526–520 BR, when the capital's collectors made their last sweeps through the towns. Hesara was the daughter of **Lemara of Esharat** and **Prince Tazirun Zaheret**, a younger son of a Qahuri royal house who settled in the Gateway country around 548 BR. The marriage connected Esharat to Sacred Basin kin and burial traditions. Later Nerethans used the relationship to strengthen their ceremonial standing; Qahuri genealogists continued to regard Tazirun as a prince who had left the Twin-Reed succession.

Nerethan and Hadrima married in 497 BR. She brought credit, kinship and knowledge of local obligations to the governing household. She also resisted his officers' attempts to assign recovered fields to their own dependants. A settlement protected several cultivated holdings while confirming grants elsewhere. Compulsory repair work continued, and households with weaker patrons obtained less protection.

Their residence at Sarqen drew together maritime officers, local families and displaced craftspeople. Sarqen occupied a sheltered landing. Esharat supplied part of its food, while longer caravan and coastal connections carried additional produce and materials. The city's growth depended on maintaining these relationships between sailing seasons.

## Repairs and unequal prosperity

The governing household repaired storage, reopened damaged water channels and enlarged the port's working shore. More dependable supply attracted vessels and workers. Marriages and apprenticeships joined incoming households to the older towns, and several languages remained in ordinary use.

Recovery also created opportunities for coercion. Nerethan's officers assigned unpaid labour to public works and gave favoured suppliers first access to stores. Hadrima's interventions protected particular families without ending the burdens. Their public reputation consequently differed between places where they had secured relief and districts which remembered the men sent to compel it.

Nerethan died around 462 BR and Hadrima around 459. Their son Arenes inherited relationships with both the incoming command and Esharat's households. He had served among the port garrison and supervised provisions inland. A dispute over the replacement of the Atherian command gave him an opportunity to seek hereditary recognition.

## From appointment to crown

In 457 BR, Arenes obtained support from town leaders who wanted continuity in supplies and protection against competing officers. Atherian patrons recognised him as ruler in return for payments, military assistance and secure access. The crown arose through bargaining among people who already possessed authority in the country.

The towns set one condition of their own. Before they would call Arenes king, he had to come down to Sarqen's landing, where the survivors of Qarzeth's sack had eaten together in 520, and eat at an unranked table among carriers, farmers and freedmen, served in no order of worth, with every seat filled. Then he swore the **Table Oath** the towns had renewed there every year since the sack: *no child for the table; no seat kept empty; no guest served by worth.* From then on the oath was read as covering suppliants as well as guests. Royal speakers liked to say that the house had brought order to the towns; the towns answered that they had made the house swear first.

Arenes governed from Sarqen while retaining local leaders in several towns. His son Tavren succeeded in 434 BR, ate at the same table and swore the same oath, and expanded royal patronage of storage, festivals and coastal defence. Marriage connected the family with further town households. Atherian influence remained substantial through military access and support for claimants who could challenge the reigning branch.

The next ruler, Olymes, swore the oath and succeeded in 408 BR. He cultivated eastern Atherian commanders and paid heavily for recognition during disputes within his own family. Those payments increased demands on cultivators and port revenues. His children inherited both a developed royal government and resentment towards the means by which he had maintained it.

## Religious and household inheritance

The court used Delkosi coin standards, military titles and royal ceremony alongside Esharati harbour customs and Qahuri funerary forms. These practices changed through five generations in the Gateway country. They gave the dynasty several languages of rule and exposed it to conflicting demands from soldiers, spared-town households and foreign allies.

The dynasty supported town cults and ceremonies that had survived the capital's destruction. Royal festivals commemorated rescued children and the communities that had sheltered them, while families in the same towns mourned relatives killed or enslaved by the Atherians during the sack.

Hadrima's position remained important to later accounts of the house. Royal speakers cited her ancestry when claiming the loyalty of Esharat and other surviving towns. Those towns could answer that their contribution preceded the crown and imposed responsibilities on its heirs, and they had the oath to prove it.

The oath also explains why the kings ruled from Sarqen and never from the ruins of Qarzeth a day's sail away. The old capital's cisterns were broken and its sanctuary ground salted, so it could not have fed a court. Gateway priests said a palace raised over the sanctuary would be a place kept for the Provider, and that such places do not stay empty for long. And the oath that made a Nerethan a ruler had been sworn at Sarqen's landing, over Sarqen's salt.

## Samyra's contested succession

Olymes's death in 375 BR brought Samyra and her younger brother Nethar into joint rule. The minister Vardek and members of the garrison backed Nethar, excluded Samyra from the port government and relied on foreign recognition to secure their position. Her older sister Merath gathered supporters of a different royal branch.

Samyra maintained relationships with Esharat households and sought allies outside the country. In 373 BR Vardek invited the fugitive [[Marovan Veyr]] to land at Sarqen and laid the royal table for him with an empty seat, meaning to kill him there and win [[Teren Casvilar]]'s favour. An Esharati elder warned Veyr, who fled to Ilamar. A minister who laid a table to kill a guest had broken the oath sworn by every ruler of the house, and the towns rose for Samyra. Casvilar, who needed Esharat's grain, backed the side that held it. The towns judged Vardek under the oath before Casvilar's officers executed him, and Nethar's claim ended when the younger ruler died in the fighting of 372.

Her subsequent relationship with Casvilar produced Amesar in 372 BR. Years later, her alliance with [[Darion Velsar]] produced Talven and Lethia. Her elder sister Merath married Velsar's rival, Aurelius Thalion, in 360 and was executed at Samyra's request in 358, days after bearing Thalion's son at Esharat; the town hid the child and would not give him up. The capital remained at Sarqen. The old sacrificial city was a ruined part of the country over which these families argued.

## End of the crown

The oath that had restored Samyra in 373 ended her house in 350. From 362 she kept Mavren Seneth, one of Casvilar's killers, at the towns' table, where the oath protected him. From 353 Velsar's army sat bound to his Red Field on the salt pans below Sarqen, and the queen's levies to feed it, which the towns called the Fourth Collection, fell hardest on the towns that had made her. In 351 her officers saved the treasury and left the wounded on the quay of a flooded galley basin. In the spring of 350 Amesar killed Seneth at the renewal of the oath. The towns would not harm a son of the house. They put the house out instead, and filled the queen's seat with a rower who had been left on the basin quay.

Thalion came to the landing that summer at the towns' invitation, sat at their unranked table and swore the Table Oath, the first ruler of the country who was not a Nerethan to do so. Samyra had already taken a stranger's place at the same table with Talven and Lethia, and the oath forbade anyone to take them from it. The kingdom was annexed and its revenues and appointments placed under an imperial governor, who swore the oath at the landing every spring. Amesar lived out his life in sanctuary on Ilamar. Samyra, Talven and Lethia lived at Esharat as guests of the towns.

The crown ended while the country remained inhabited, and the house's blood went on in an unexpected place. Merath's son Meren, carried out of Esharat in a grain bin, became the second emperor of Atheria and swore the oath at the landing at his accession in 318, as his mother's family had sworn it since 457.
