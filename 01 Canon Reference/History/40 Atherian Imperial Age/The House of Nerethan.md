# The House of Nerethan

The House of Nerethan ruled the gateway country from Sarqen, the enlarged port earlier histories call Second Harbour. Its founder came from the maritime Atherian city of Delkos and married into a family from Esharat, one of the towns spared when the former republican capital was destroyed. The dynasty's authority developed through that partnership, Atherian appointments and the recovery of cultivated land and shipping. Its last independent ruler was [[Samyra Nerethan]].

The royal family inherited a country already changed by [[The Gateway Wars]]. The capital's sacrificial establishment had been destroyed, while lesser towns retained working wells, farms and ports. Refugees and returning captives joined communities whose households had their own claims to survival and resistance. Royal power had to be established among them.

## Nerethan and Hadrima

Nerethan of Delkos was born around 525 BR and arrived as an Atherian harbour commander in 499 BR. His appointment concerned coastal defence, military provisions and the supervision of dependent towns. Officers and specialists accompanied him, but local pilots, cultivators and transport households provided the means to remain.

Hadrima's family held cultivated land and a share in the storage and carrying trade of Esharat. The town lay inland from the coastal landings, close enough for its produce to reach them by cart and pack animal. Its households had sheltered children fleeing the old capital's collectors and had supplied passage during the final intervention. Some relatives prospered after the war; others lost people or property to the victorious army.

Nerethan and Hadrima married in 497 BR. She brought credit, kinship and knowledge of local obligations to the governing household. She also resisted his officers' attempts to assign recovered fields to their own dependants. A settlement protected several cultivated holdings while confirming grants elsewhere. Compulsory repair work continued, and households with weaker patrons obtained less protection.

Their residence at Sarqen drew together maritime officers, local families and displaced craftspeople. Sarqen occupied a sheltered landing distinct from both the ruined capital and the later city of Qabir. Esharat supplied part of its food, while longer caravan and coastal connections carried additional produce and materials. The city's growth depended on maintaining these relationships between sailing seasons.

## Repairs and unequal prosperity

The governing household repaired storage, reopened damaged water channels and enlarged the port's working shore. More dependable supply attracted vessels and workers. Marriages and apprenticeships joined incoming households to the older towns, and several languages remained in ordinary use.

Recovery also created opportunities for coercion. Nerethan's officers assigned unpaid labour to public works and gave favoured suppliers first access to stores. Hadrima's interventions protected particular families without ending the burdens. Their public reputation consequently differed between places where they had secured relief and districts which remembered the men sent to compel it.

Nerethan died around 462 BR and Hadrima around 459. Their son Arenes inherited relationships with both the incoming command and Esharat's households. He had served among the port garrison and supervised provisions inland. A dispute over the replacement of the Atherian command gave him an opportunity to seek hereditary recognition.

## From appointment to crown

In 457 BR, Arenes obtained support from town leaders who wanted continuity in supplies and protection against competing officers. Atherian patrons recognised him as ruler in return for payments, military assistance and secure access. The crown arose through bargaining among people who already possessed authority in the country.

Arenes governed from Sarqen while retaining local leaders in several towns. His son Tavren succeeded in 434 BR and expanded royal patronage of storage, festivals and coastal defence. Marriage connected the family with further town households. Atherian influence remained substantial through military access and support for claimants who could challenge the reigning branch.

The next ruler, Olymes, succeeded in 408 BR. He cultivated eastern Atherian commanders and paid heavily for recognition during disputes within his own family. Those payments increased demands on cultivators and port revenues. His children inherited both a developed royal government and resentment towards the means by which he had maintained it.

## Religious and household inheritance

The dynasty supported town cults and ceremonies that had survived the capital's destruction. Royal festivals commemorated rescued children and the communities that had sheltered them. Families also mourned people killed or enslaved during the Atherian sack. A single public occasion could therefore bring together gratitude towards the intervention and anger at its conduct.

The rulers sought standing within maritime religious traditions as well as the customs of the gateway towns. Court display used inherited royal imagery and stories of protection. None established an unbroken Giant dynasty. The family's known descent was human, through households formed long after the Giant kingdoms had fallen.

Hadrima's position remained important to later accounts of the house. Royal speakers cited her ancestry when claiming the loyalty of Esharat and other surviving towns. Those towns could answer that their contribution preceded the crown and imposed responsibilities on its heirs.

## Samyra's contested succession

Olymes's death in 375 BR brought Samyra and her younger brother Nethar into joint rule. The minister Vardek and members of the garrison backed Nethar, excluded Samyra from the port government and relied on foreign recognition to secure their position. Her older sister Merath gathered supporters of a different royal branch.

Samyra maintained relationships with Esharat households and sought allies outside the country. The arrival and murder of [[Marovan Veyr]] in 373 BR brought [[Teren Casvilar]] into the struggle. His intervention restored her position, removed Vardek and ended Nethar's claim after the younger ruler died in the fighting.

Her subsequent relationship with Casvilar produced Amesar in 372 BR. Years later, her alliance with [[Darion Velsar]] produced Talven and Lethia and a wider programme of royal claims. The capital remained at Sarqen. The old sacrificial city was a ruined part of the country over which these families argued, rather than the seat of their government.

## End of independent rule

The final Atherian succession war reached Sarqen in 352–350 BR. After the defeat of Samyra and Velsar, Thalion annexed the kingdom and placed its revenues and appointments under his own officers. He preserved the working harbour and negotiated with surviving town leaders. Amesar was executed as a rival claimant. Talven and Lethia were removed into the protection and custody of Evara Thalion's household.

The crown ended while the country remained inhabited. Royal dependants, port workers and town families continued under another change of government. Sarqen's earlier survival and later enlargement made its resources valuable to the victor.
