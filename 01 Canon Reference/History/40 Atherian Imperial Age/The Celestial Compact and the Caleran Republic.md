# The Celestial Compact and the Caleran Republic

The Celestial Compact joined twelve leading Atherian cities around 800 BR in arrangements for a common calendar, arbitration and military contributions. Caleran became their principal meeting place. Over subsequent generations, assemblies, a senate of property-holding houses and annually chosen magistrates developed into the institutions of the Caleran republic. Their authority expanded through alliances and conquest until competition among military patrons undermined their ability to govern together.

## The city world before the Compact

The member cities inherited different histories of settlement and rule. Some had grown from river landings, others from hill towns, defended markets or coastal households. Their rulers depended on cultivators, craftspeople, transport workers and armed followers. Repeated cooperation against a danger could leave behind expectations about supplies and service which became difficult to abandon.

When Caleran's last strong king, Senavar, seized the Nerava boats, a coalition of councillors, boat owners and neighbouring rulers forced his garrison out. His family's supporters held on to some estates, no new dynasty could settle, and chosen officers took over the work of the king's household. The councillors had learned to act together.

The Compact placed this changing city within a wider association. Caleran's river mouth joined grain, shipping and the inland roads, and its festival ground could already seat visiting delegations at feasts they knew. Meetings came to Caleran, and so did the records of what was agreed at them.

## What membership required

The Compact gave its members a shared calendar for meetings and musters, arbitration before a quarrel reached the fields, and an army larger than any one of them could raise. Leading houses made the promises; cultivators, carters and sons paid for them. A city's promise of troops could leave its farms short of reapers at harvest, and a family with silver could buy its way out of a levy that took its neighbour's boys.

The twelve cities had distinct histories. Caleran's later claim that every member's founder had belonged to one original Caleran order was written after Caleran had become their master.

## The twelve seats

The roll of the Compact survives in Caleran's copies. Each seat brought something the others needed, and each city's later history ran through what it brought.

## Councils, magistrates and assemblies

Caleran's household council grew into a senate of men with land, lineage and friends among the suppliers and soldiers. Magistrates chosen each year ran public business and led armies. Assemblies of citizens voted, though money and standing decided which proposals reached them. A one-year term stopped one man from keeping an office; it did nothing to stop one family from filling it, year after year, through marriages, loans and old soldiers.

Public sacrifice, festival patronage and the reading of signs went with every appointment and campaign, and the temples employed enough people that a quarrel over ceremony was also a quarrel over wages and office. Caleran's augurs kept the common calendar, and their intercalary art, built around the old year in which the stars had stood still, let them lengthen a year by a month when the signs required. The families who supplied the augurs also held the longest leases on public land, and their tenants noticed which years grew longer.

Some member cities kept their own kings for generations; other communities joined later on terms of their own.

## Citizenship and incorporation

Republican expansion used conquest, alliance and negotiated submission, and citizenship came in as many grades. Some towns won full rights; others sent men and taxes and had no vote. A household could rise by service, manumission, marriage or grant while the family next door, resident for a century, stayed outside. The Enathi, Serathi and Vardeni entered the citizen body beside maritime peoples and communities beyond the Aurin, and being Atherian came to mean a language, a festival year and a place on the rolls as much as a bloodline.

Roads and colonies carried the expansion. Surveyors marked routes and holdings, officials assessed produce, and soldiers enforced the demands. A road could take a farmer's barley to a larger market and bring back the column that took his land. The [[01 Canon Reference/History/80 Myths and Legendary Histories/G-08 - The Road That Refused Its Last Stone.md|Road Without Milestones tradition]] remembers the labourers who died building it, the settlements destroyed to make way for it, and the one milestone a gang of its workers refused to dedicate, which undid none of it.

## Land and military service

Long campaigns took men from the farms. Their families borrowed seed against the land, wealthy neighbours bought up the failing holdings and worked them with captives, and conquest fed more land and more captives into the same estates. A veteran might come home to a prosperous household or to debt and a ruined field, and commanders who promised land won men whose families could no longer feed them. The land promised was often already being farmed by a subject community, which paid for the soldiers' reward.

Expansion also paid ordinary dividends. Better roads widened markets for tools, food and cloth; apprentices moved between workshops; families feasted when they were admitted to the citizen body and paid towards the temples and porticoes they were proud of. That pride held loyalty while the quarrels over burdens went on.

## The Ortheian hegemony and Caleran's recovery

The upland king Valeron II extended Ortheian power over several Compact cities between about 735 and 706 BR. He used marriage, hostage education, military pressure and a permanent infantry core to organise a common eastern campaign. Caleran supplied ships, grain and money while its assembly tried to preserve authority over appointments and taxation.

In 706 Valeron's own infantry killed him at a pay table when Olyssar's withheld silver left the count short. His son Eryndor III paid the army with Olyssar, which his soldiers sacked and burned in 704 BR. The remaining members then contributed to his eastern invasion under different degrees of compulsion. Their soldiers and ships helped defeat Arqesh, while eastern revenue and royal ceremony changed the officers who returned.

Eryndor's death in 684 divided his conquests. Demeran Kavos held Ortheia and the western army, but could not retain the former hegemony. Caleran admitted exiles and unpaid sailors, acquired ships from commanders unable to maintain them and restored meetings of the Compact. Land and revenue seized during the royal period remained in new hands. The recovered republic therefore combined renewed civic government with property and military networks created under the kings.

Delkos, Vashar and other Successor states remained active across the eastern sea. Their officers, coins, naval methods and dynastic practices entered Caleran life before the Gateway Wars.

## From league to hegemony

Caleran became the Compact's master by holding the things the members could not do without: the meetings, the calendar, the rolls and, at the last, the money for the fleet.

**The Olyssar seat, c. 680–672 BR.** Eryndor had burned Olyssar in 704 and given its fields to the infantry companies he owed. After his death Caleran bought ships cheaply from his unpaid commanders and used them to carry its own unpaid sailors to Olyssar's fields, where they drove off the Ortheian grantees and ploughed ground still black from the burning. Caleran then seated the settlement in the Compact as Olyssar. The Olyssaran exiles living in Caleran kept lists of their dead and their sold, and they read the lists aloud at the festival where the seat was disputed. The arbiters ruled that a seat went with land and a festival, and lists were neither. From about 672 Caleran spoke with two of the twelve voices.

**The unreckoned years of Verion, c. 652–649 BR.** Verion was ruled by heirs of the soldier whose emergency command had become a dynasty after the Bent-Key crisis. During Caleran's blockade of Tammur in the first Gateway war, its ruler withheld his levy. Caleran, as keeper of the common calendar, left Verion's festival and contract days out of the year. The Common Sky taught that an offering made on an unreckoned day went unseen, and Verion's sailors would not put to sea under a sky that could not see them. The festival fires stood unlit, cargo rotted on the quays, member harbours turned Verion's ships away and arbiters refused its merchants' suits. Within three seasons the city's ship-owning houses drove out the ruling family and sent envoys to ask that Verion be "restored to the sky". Caleran agreed, on condition that Verion's magistrates be confirmed at Caleran. Exclusion from the calendar became the Compact's heaviest sanction, and only Caleran could impose it.

**The rolls, c. 520–513 BR.** The Compact's rolls had always been kept in the archive valley, the highland league whose chief town housed the treaties, the boundary records and the copies of every founder's line. Around 520 that league destroyed one of its own districts in a war that Atherian annals cannot place, and the valley was lost for years. When it could be reached again, its archive had collapsed. The rolls were rebuilt from Caleran's copies around 513, and from then on every dispute between members went through Caleran's archive. Scholars have argued ever since about the gaps in the rebuilt rolls, which include the war records of the Gateway siege.

**The Oar's chains, 491–469 BR.** The League of the Eastern Oar, formed in 497 against Xarun of Vashar, was a separate alliance with its fleet at Velathene. Velathene's pilots learned the old Roll of Refused Hulls with their approaches, the list of ships the drowned harbour of Kelarion had shut out in a storm, and the League's first custom came from it: no member harbour closed its chain against a League hull in distress. After the Vasharan withdrawal of 491 the fleet stayed in service against Orvaz I, and Caleran's assessors fixed what each member owed. Thesra and Olveth, with no ships, paid in silver and sons. A city that fell behind learned it at sea. In an autumn blow around 480, a Pyrassan grain convoy running for Velathene found the chain up and the mole lined with watchmen under orders, and three hulls broke on the outer rocks in sight of the harbour while their cargo washed up for the beachcombers. Cities that refused outright lost their walls and magistrates. By 470 the money went into Caleran's treasury, and sailors called the League's chains "Caleran's purse-strings". The bill for that name came due in 469, in the [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic|war of the closed chains]].

## The maritime rival and the Gateway Wars

The [[The Gateway Republic|Gateway Republic]] controlled a cultivated maritime country at the approaches of later Qabir and Zanthir. Its leading port had developed among Ahdari migrants and older shore communities, with civic institutions and dependent towns of its own. Centuries of trade and local rivalry preceded the wars through which Caleran became committed to its defeat.

The [[The Gateway Wars|Gateway Wars]], approximately 660–520 BR, began over bread. After three dry springs on the Aurin, Compact cities bought Gateway grain at Tammur, where Qarzeth sold only to buyers who ate at its god's festival table. Caleran forbade the trade, blockaded the market and took it in 649. Caleran's position at the Aurin mouth required landward supply links and cooperating Arkenan and Saronikan harbours, and victory depended on crews, repair workers, transport households and cultivators well beyond the city that directed the war.

The second war, about 612–601, was Zamaret Ordan's invasion of Caleran's allied country. He offered wavering towns relief and Gateway grain, and the towns that ate it found their own sealed stores empty at the next count. After two Atherian armies were lost, Caleran killed Gateway hostage children and captives on its festival ground in 609 to give the Red Star a contestant. From that rite came the Warden of the Red Field, a commander bound to offer battle in person and forbidden to quit the field while his named enemy held it. Celor Marath, the first Warden, held the approaches below Thesra in 606, and "do not eat the Gateway's bread" held the alliance together until Zamaret withdrew.

The recovered rival remained a subject of senatorial disagreement, because it recovered faster than any harvest could explain. Opposition to its capital's child-sacrificing establishment became a major cause of the final war, alongside concerns about security, trade and renewed resistance. Internal opponents and threatened families assisted intervention. The final siege, around 525–520 BR, released children and destroyed the sacrificial institution; Atherian soldiers also murdered, raped and enslaved inhabitants. The capital was razed and heavily salted while lesser towns accepting the settlement escaped its planned destruction.

Those [[The Spared Towns and the Queen's Country|spared towns]] supported later royal recovery. Caleran acquired greater access, revenue and military prestige. The campaigns also enlarged debts, extended service and increased commanders' influence among the people dependent on their rewards.

## The Nerava supply dispute

Nerava, where the river boats met the pack trains, carried the Gateway Wars on its back. Between roughly 660 and 500 BR its boats and carts came home from one campaign to be requisitioned for the next before anyone had mended a hull or been paid for the last trip. Houses with friends in the senate got their own boats excused; smaller owners had theirs seized.

At last the crews would not embark, and loading stopped along the river. Army suppliers demanded force; boat families answered that one more season away would leave their own farms unsown. A partial settlement cut some demands and gave the established river associations a lasting voice in assigning service. Owners in the associations gained; hired boatmen and small carriers still went where the owners sent them. Nerava's songs celebrate the crews who came home and accuse the great families of buying relief for themselves.

## Recasting the founders

As Caleran's precedence grew, its officials rewrote the member cities' founders into one ceremonial order, dressed older heroes in Caleran titles and suppressed the stories that disagreed. Shrine custodians who resisted were imprisoned or killed, and some sanctuaries lost their endowments to new patrons. In one member city the recutting of a founder monument cost the lives of the custodians who stood in front of it, and one family kept a banquet vessel painted with the hostages of their city's defeat, to show at home what the public festival no longer admitted.

The result was a claim that Atherian expansion was the restoration of an ancient unity. Families who had come in through conquest, slavery or unequal admission kept other stories at home.

## Provinces and powerful commanders

The republic's wars reached the northern desert approaches, the southern forest marches, the eastern lands of later Eclessia and harbours of the later Sestaran world. Provincial revenue paid for Caleran's households and its elections. Commanders shared out spoils, looked after soldiers' families and promised land, and a soldier who had served one general for years owed him more than he owed an annual magistrate. Senate houses invested in transport, estates and supply, then sought the commands that would protect their investments. In the republic's last decades rival commanders' followers fought over land grants, offices and honours, and civic leaders found they needed soldiers to make a decision stick.

[[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] follows these conditions through the noon shadows at Sarven, the closed harbour chains of the allies' war and a Warden who governed from a tent. Casvilar's southern conquests and Ostar's failed northern expedition altered the balance among commanders. Casvilar accepted the Red Field in 376, drove Veyr into sanctuary and concentrated power in his own hands before his assassination in 370 BR.

[[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]] traces the continuing struggle through divided commands, confiscation and the eastern royal alliance. Aurelius Thalion emerged supreme around 350 BR. His seizure of Caleran and settlement of appointments, taxation and military pay established imperial government through institutions inherited from the republic.
