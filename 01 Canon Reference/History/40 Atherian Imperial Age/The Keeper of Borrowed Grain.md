# The Keeper of Borrowed Grain

The Keeper of Borrowed Grain was Neriah bar Sorek, a Jirahar administrator who directed imperial relief during the Raveth hunger that began in 119 BR. His purchases, transport orders and seed distributions saved towns from starvation. The terms attached to that assistance transferred large areas of farmland to the imperial household and made many former owners dependent cultivators. Raveth remembers the dispossession through two things: the Jirahar official who designed the relief, and the emperor's image before which many of its debts were sworn.

## Raveth before the failure

Raveth was an eastern river province supplying grain to towns, garrisons and inner-sea ports. Its farms ranged from small household plots to estates worked by tenants and enslaved labourers. Before it joined the empire, its towns paid canal duty to the Vasharan road command at Avesh, and under that duty every household sent men each spring to dig silt out of the flood channels. When the Arcelon road pulled Raveth's trade west, its councils negotiated entry into the empire late in Orent II's reign. The first imperial assessment was fixed in a good year.

Neriah belonged to a Jirahar diaspora household established near a provincial market. He learned agricultural reckoning through family trade and entered imperial service as a translator and store examiner. His ability to compare local measures exposed theft and helped him obtain promotion; it was said he carried the measures of three provinces in his head. He remained subject to suspicions directed at his community while acquiring authority over people with less access to government.

The new road levy took the same spring labour for the Arcelon stations, and the flood channels silted. Two harvests failed. The third was fair, and estate managers shipped it out under contracts already signed with the garrisons. Then blight took the fields. Villagers ate seed, sold tools and travelled towards towns whose stores were already failing.

## Relief

Emperor Elian II appointed Neriah after two earlier commissioners failed to coordinate river transport. His sister Lethara, who administered family estates and food contracts, had brought the Raveth stewards' accounts to court. Neriah purchased grain from other provinces, requisitioned vessels and opened military stores. Local women who knew which households had disappeared helped revise distribution lists. Bakers changed the mixtures used for public loaves so available grain would last.

Neriah required a countersignature from local witnesses before a store release, reducing diversion by officials. Deliveries still favoured towns on navigable routes. The boats reached the river towns first, and hill villages died waiting for the pack trains. Soldiers killed a crowd attempting to force entry into one store.

Relief grain and seed were issued as recoverable obligations. When households could not repay, their land passed to the imperial domain. Animals and tools were similarly absorbed into managed estates. Former owners could remain by accepting labour duties and a share of the crop. The worst was over within three seasons, and the relief was wound down by 113.

## The decision to continue

The first emergency conversion preserved fields from immediate sale to private speculators. Neriah then advised Elian to continue the policy across the affected province. He believed a single estate could repair the channels, distribute seed and secure future supply, and the emperor approved it.

Lethara's estate officers ran the new domain. They replaced the neighbours' countersignature with an oath, sworn by each debtor before the emperor's image set up in the store-house yard. Neriah would not administer that oath and resigned. He left copies of both the release accounts and the land transfers with his community.

Raveth noticed the difference within a season. Where debts had been countersigned by neighbours, households argued, delayed, petitioned and kept part of their land. Where they had been sworn before the image, families walked their animals to the estate office without being asked and stood waiting at the gate. Estate officers praised the sworn villages for their piety. The villages themselves called it "the loan before the Face" and said the portrait had taken the argument out of them.

The imperial estates recovered, and several towns survived. Families lost the right to decide what to plant, where their children would work and when they could leave. Debt servitude acquired the stability of public administration. Later imperial officials removed many local countersignatures when presenting the policy as the emperor's generosity.

## Memory

Raveth families called a measure taken in desperation "the Keeper's loan". The phrase could express gratitude for help or warn that assistance would claim the recipient's future. Neriah's own household preserved prayers for those who died before the grain arrived and the names of farms transferred under his seal.

Imperial histories praised his intelligence. Hostile accounts accused the Jirahar of planning the acquisition, obscuring Elian's approval, Lethara's officers and the benefits received by Atherian estate managers. Those accounts never explained why the sworn villages also blamed the image. Both the praise and the accusation gave little attention to the households whose lives and dependence followed from the same policy.
