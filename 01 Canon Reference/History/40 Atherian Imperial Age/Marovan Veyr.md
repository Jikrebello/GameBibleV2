# Marovan Veyr

Marovan Veyr (435–371 BR) was the leading Atherian commander of the generation before Teren Casvilar's southern conquests. His eastern campaigns secured ports, installed friendly governments and created loyalties that outlived his defeat. He became Casvilar's principal rival in the first civil war, refused a poisoned welcome at Sarqen and died of fever in sanctuary on Ilamar.

## The eastern commander

Veyr's victories between about 414 and 397 BR combined military pressure with settlements among coastal rulers. He wanted dependable winter harbours, passage for supplies and local lords willing to pay what they had promised. A ruler who kept his seat through Veyr's help became a source of troops, money and news.

Merchants sailed more safely along several routes because of him. The same campaigns brought executions, hostages taken from town councils and the burning of communities that resisted the terms offered. Every port had its own factions, and Veyr usually made one of them rich at the expense of the other.

He liked an orderly reception, knew the value of appearing unhurried and took pleasure in being recognised in the street by men who had rowed for him. His name could open a harbour before his ships arrived. At Caleran, though, an old victory did not guarantee that the senate would approve every bargain made overseas.

## Calvena and the partnership

Veyr joined Casvilar and Lethren Ostar in the informal partnership of 396–391 BR. Each could supply what the others lacked. Veyr wanted his eastern settlements confirmed and land for his soldiers; Ostar defended his money; Casvilar wanted the command that would make his own name.

In 386 BR Veyr married Calvena Casvilar, then nineteen. He was much older and already had children from an earlier union, Ardel among them. The marriage gave both households a way to talk and became an affectionate one. Calvena carried news between the two houses and could soften a quarrel before it reached the senate floor.

The partnership broke over land. From 379 Casvilar's returning veterans claimed the public land along the old noon-sighted boundaries, land that Veyr's senatorial friends had leased for generations. Veyr took his friends' side and supported the demand that Casvilar lay down his command first. In 377 Casvilar offered Calvena a divorce from her husband. She refused it in public and stayed with Veyr.

## The civil war

When Casvilar accepted the Red Field and marched on Caleran in 376, Veyr withdrew east to the clients, stores and recruits of his old commands. Casvilar took the capital; Veyr kept an army.

The war strained every client friendship he had. Rulers who had welcomed his protection now found themselves paying for an Atherian army fighting other Atherians. Near Velos in 374 the two armies fought to a standstill, and neither broke. Veyr lost the war on the water instead. The eastern harbours remembered how the senate's assessors had once had League chains raised against their own storm-driven ships, and they would not pay for the senate's champion's fleet. His galleys sat unmanned in their sheds while Casvilar's allies rowed.

The senator Arest Corven, who had spoken against the exceptional commands of both men, threw in his lot with Veyr after Velos and followed him to the end.

## The empty seat and Ilamar

In 373 BR Veyr sailed for the gateway kingdom, where King Olymes had owed him favours. The minister Vardek, ruling for the boy-king Nethar, invited him to land and eat. An elder of Esharat who had come down to the landing warned him that the royal table had been laid with an empty seat. In the spared towns no seat was ever kept empty at a shared meal, and Veyr understood what the place was for. He turned his ship around.

He took sanctuary on Ilamar, the island of witnessed oaths in the central sea. The custodians refused to give him up to Casvilar's envoys. Casvilar would not storm the island, because the custodians' court certified the grain that fed his armies. Calvena nursed her husband there, and Arest Corven began a history of the republic's last years that Casvilar's heirs would later have reason to fear.

Veyr died of fever on Ilamar in 371 BR, a year before his rival was killed. In the gateway country the story of the empty seat became a warning about royal hospitality. Esharati tellers made the elder the hero of it; Atherian ones remembered only that the old general had been too wise to come ashore.

## The sea-heir

Veyr's son Ardel gathered followers among displaced officers, island communities and refugees. His power at sea rested partly on his father's old friendships. He sheltered people fleeing the Writing-In, when the Atherian partners had their enemies' names entered in the register of challengers and killed them on Caleran's muster ground, and he took in Corial Draven, one of the seven who had killed Casvilar. He forced merchant shipping to serve his fleet.

Ardel's defeat in 355, when Istran Melvo burned Marelith with Draven aboard, and his execution the following year ended the family's military challenge. Refugees who had relied on him faced dispersal, slavery or submission to old enemies. Veyr's eastern settlements went on shaping coastal politics long after the imperial histories had turned his defeat into a step towards unity.

The room above Ilamar's harbour where Veyr died had one more famous tenant. In 350 Casvilar's son Amesar took sanctuary on the island and lived there for the rest of his life. He finished the history Arest Corven had begun at Veyr's bedside, and Corven's account of Veyr reached later readers through him.
