# Marovan Veyr

Marovan Veyr (435–373 BR) was the leading Atherian commander of the generation before Teren Casvilar's southern conquests. His eastern campaigns secured ports, reorganised dependent governments and created relationships which outlived his defeat. He became Casvilar's principal rival in the first civil war.

## The eastern commander

Veyr's victories between about 414 and 397 BR combined military pressure with settlements among coastal rulers. He sought dependable harbours, passage for supplies and local authorities willing to meet agreed demands. A ruler who retained office through his assistance became a source of troops, money and information.

His work gave merchants greater security along several routes. It also involved executions, hostages and the suppression of communities which resisted the terms offered. Ports had their own divisions, and Veyr often strengthened one group at the expense of another.

He liked an orderly reception, knew the importance of appearing unhurried and took pleasure in being recognised by men who had served under him. His reputation could attract cooperation before his army arrived. At Caleran, however, established eminence did not ensure approval of every decision made overseas.

## Calvena and the partnership

Veyr joined Casvilar and Lethren Ostar in the informal partnership formed between 396 and 391 BR. Each could provide something the others lacked. Veyr wanted recognition of his arrangements and provision for his soldiers; Ostar defended financial interests; Casvilar sought the command from which he could make his own reputation.

In 386 BR Veyr married Calvena Casvilar, then nineteen. He was considerably older and already had children from an earlier union, including Ardel. The marriage developed affection as well as political value. Calvena exchanged news between households and could soften a dispute before it became a public confrontation.

Her death in childbirth in 380 left Veyr bereaved and removed an important personal connection. Casvilar was approaching the end of campaigns that had made him an independent military power. Their relationship now depended increasingly on people who feared what the other commander might do.

## The civil war

Veyr supported measures intended to bring Casvilar's command to an end. When Casvilar advanced on Caleran in 376, Veyr withdrew east, relying on clients, supplies and recruits assembled through his earlier service. The withdrawal gave Casvilar control of the capital while preserving a substantial opposing force.

The war strained those client relationships. Rulers who had once welcomed protection now faced demands from an army fighting other Atherians. Veyr still commanded loyalty, but his allies differed over how much risk they would accept to restore him.

Defeat near Velos in 374 forced him to seek another base. Arest Corven, a senator who had opposed exceptional commands and eventually backed Veyr, took his own life after the collapse of that position. Others scattered among ports and royal households.

## Murder at the gateway

Veyr reached the gateway country in 373 BR expecting that former relationships would provide refuge. The minister Vardek, acting for the young ruler Nethar, instead ordered him murdered in the hope of pleasing Casvilar. His surviving wife and attendants escaped the reception.

The killing failed to secure the government. Casvilar had Vardek executed and intervened on behalf of Samyra Nerethan. The murdered commander consequently became a warning in later accounts of royal hospitality, although the political calculations behind his reception remained as important as the breach of trust.

## The sea-heir

Veyr's son Ardel assembled followers among displaced officers, island communities and refugees. His maritime power depended partly on the relationships his father had created. He offered sanctuary during the proscriptions and compelled shipping to support his forces.

Ardel's defeat in 355 and execution the following year ended the family's principal military challenge. Refugees who had relied on him faced dispersal, slavery or submission to former enemies. Veyr's earlier settlements continued to shape eastern politics even as the imperial account presented his defeat as a step towards unity.
