# Aurelius Thalion

Aurelius Thalion (390–318 BR) founded the Atherian Empire around 350 BR after twenty years of succession wars. He was the conquering commander Teren Casvilar's nephew and heir, and he refused the war-rite that had killed his uncle. He never commanded in a battle. His soldiers swore to a painted board of his face while he stayed at the river stores of Caleran and paid them, and the wars ended when he sat down at the spared towns' table on the landing of Sarqen and swore their oath. The Age of Glory begins with his settlement.

## Household and early prospects

Thalion's mother, Lysara Casvilar, was the conqueror's sister. His father, Aurel Thalion, died in 384 BR, leaving Lysara a household whose standing exceeded its ready money. Thalion and his elder sister Evara grew up among people who sought help through relatives, service and introductions.

He was heavy even as a young man, short-sighted and a poor rider. He held documents close to his face and listened more than he spoke. He remembered the name of a man he had met once, and veterans who gave him their names at twenty found that he still knew them at sixty. He grew up on Caleran's stories of Vespen Harod, the Warden of the Red Field who lived twenty-five years in a tent on the muster ground because the enemies he had named would not come to fight him, and he learned from them to fear the Red Star.

## The refusal, 370–367 BR

Casvilar was killed on the muster ground below the river stores in 370 by seven men who had entered their names as his challengers. Thalion, twenty years old and Casvilar's designated heir, reached Caleran four days later and was taken to see the sand where his uncle had fallen. The next morning he refused the Field in the forum. It had killed his uncle, he said, as surely as the knives, and he would not stand on it. Darion Velsar, Casvilar's senior lieutenant, claimed the Wardenship instead.

The refusal cost Thalion the army's respect and gained him the senate. The speaker Belisar Venn backed him against Velsar, expecting an heir without the army's rite to stay dependent on senatorial support. Lysara sold the family's town houses and borrowed from her brother's friends to pay veterans who had been promised land. In 367 Seluc Varroth ruled that the Field stood open and unclaimed and that no one could inherit it, and Thalion became one of the Four at the Stores, who governed Caleran from the store-house above the muster ground because armies that could not disband still had to eat.

## The Writing-In

The Four needed their enemies dead and their soldiers would not murder citizens. Velsar proposed writing new names into the register of challengers, so that killing those men would count as war on the open Field. Thalion agreed. Through the winter of 367 the named men were dragged to the muster ground and killed there, boys among them. When Velsar asked for Belisar Venn's name, Thalion consented. Venn walked down to the muster ground with a borrowed practice sword and was killed, and the river stores he had kept for the senate passed to Thalion. Thalion's later admirers dated the stability of his government from that winter. They did not dwell on how it had been bought.

## The man who was not there

Thalion went on no campaign. He stayed at the river stores, paid the companies that followed him on the day he promised, and sent other men to command them. The companies marched behind a painted board of his face on a pole, so that soldiers who had never seen him could swear to him; in the eastern camps a board of that kind was simply called a thalion. The joke against him was that his picture had seen more war than he had. The soldiers kept swearing to it.

His one indispensable commander was Istran Melvo, a shipwright from Pyrassa who could say why a plan would fail at the anchorage and what it would need to succeed. Melvo built the fleet, burned the moving city of Marelith in 355 and broke Ardel Veyr's sea power. Thalion ordered the attack on Marelith because Corial Draven, one of the seven, was aboard.

## Marriages and a son in a grain bin

In 365 Thalion married Ilvara Deneth, who managed his household's money and dependants during the worst of the Writing-In. Their son Caelor was born in 363. Ilvara died in 361 giving birth to their daughter, named Lysara after Thalion's mother.

Within the year he married Merath Nerethan, the elder sister of Queen Samyra of Sarqen, who had been passed over in her sister's favour and was looking for an Atherian patron. Velsar was already Samyra's ally. In 358, at Samyra's request, Velsar's officers seized Merath at Esharat, where she had gone to bear her child, and executed her. The women of Esharat hid the newborn in a grain bin and the town would not give him up. Carters brought him west under a load of barley. He was Meren, Thalion's second son, and Thalion's mother raised him at Caleran with Caelor and his sister.

## The Salt Field and the landing

By 354 six of the seven were dead. The seventh, Mavren Seneth, sat at the spared towns' table in Sarqen under their oath, and Samyra would not give him up. In 353 Velsar accepted a Red Field of his own on the salt pans below Sarqen and named Thalion and Melvo as his enemies, expecting that Thalion would have to come and meet him. Thalion did what Harod's enemies had done. He stayed away, kept the war going through his painted boards and left Velsar bound to the salt.

The war ended without him. In the spring of 350 Samyra's son Amesar killed Seneth at the towns' table, and the towns put her house out. Melvo, against Thalion's wishes, walked onto the Salt Field and killed Velsar in a fight. The towns sent to say that the landing was Thalion's if he came in person, sat at their unranked table and swore their oath. He came. The deposed queen had already sat down among the carters with her children, where the oath forbade anyone to take her. Thalion took a place three seats away, ate, and swore: *no child for the table; no seat kept empty; no guest served by worth.* Sarqen passed under an imperial governor, and Samyra and her children stayed in the Gateway country as guests of the towns.

## The settlement

Thalion returned to Caleran through the eastern and highland routes. Every man who had entered his name against Casvilar was dead. Thalion went down to the muster ground and stood there bareheaded, on the only field he ever stood on. Seluc Varroth, as First Celebrant, refused to declare the Field closed, since Thalion had never fought on it. The priests of the Red Star declared it closed into his command, and from that day they took first place in ceremonies of victory and command.

He made no pretence that the old republic had returned. He governed from the store-house above the muster ground and left the senate the markets, festivals and water of the city. He disbanded armies that had stayed under arms for twenty years, settled them on the Noon Roll lands and the estates of the written-in, and paid them on the day promised. Governors held their commands from him and could be recalled, and the companies that remained under arms answered to his treasury instead of to their generals. The painted board that had gone to war in his place hung in the audience hall, and later emperors were confirmed before it.

Farmers in the Aurin valleys stopped feeding two armies a year, and the roads filled with carts again. The men who had grown rich on the estates of the written-in kept them, and the families driven off those estates waited on the victors' pleasure.

## Later reign and death

[[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]] follows the discharge of the armies, the government of Sarqen and the family's quarrels over his heir. In 326 BR a new treasury hall on the forum at Caleran, blessed by the Red Star's priests but never loaded, fell during an inspection by Caelor, who died of his injuries two years later. Thalion could not disown the priesthood that had closed the Field for him. He kept its blessings and gave the trial to engineers: from then on no one entered a public work until rune-trained wardens had loaded it before the Red Star and broken the portion built to break, and the official who opened it stood under the arch first. His surviving son Meren became his heir.

Thalion kept the oath he had sworn at the landing and went back to Sarqen for the spring renewal whenever he could. He died there in 318 BR, aged seventy-two, at the table on the landing. The army and the governors had already recognised Meren, and the first imperial succession passed without open war.

Opinion about him divided early. Imperial writers praised the founder who ended twenty years of war without shedding blood in his own person. The history begun by Arest Corven and finished on Ilamar by Amesar called him a man who had other men killed for him, written in, burned or left on the salt. In the spared towns he was remembered as the conqueror who kept his word at their table.
