# Aurelius Thalion

Aurelius Thalion (390–318 BR) founded the Atherian Empire around 350 BR after two decades of succession wars. He inherited Teren Casvilar's public claim, overcame senior commanders and brought the army, provincial appointments and taxation under a continuing central authority. The Age of Glory begins with his settlement.

## Household and early prospects

Thalion's mother, Lysara Casvilar, was the conquering commander's sister. His father, Aurel Thalion, died in 384 BR, leaving Lysara responsible for a household whose influence exceeded its readily available wealth. Thalion and his elder sister Evara grew up among people accustomed to seeking assistance through relatives, service and personal introductions.

He learned to listen closely to reports and question the practical arrangements behind a promise. He was less accomplished in the field than several of his colleagues. Illness sometimes interrupted his service, and he depended on others to command where he could not. His enduring ability was to connect money, appointments and public expectations to a political purpose.

Casvilar's designation of him as heir made these relationships suddenly important in 370 BR. At about twenty, Thalion faced commanders with decades of military service. Lysara helped mobilise family property and supporters. The household borrowed and sold possessions to meet promises to veterans.

## Building a following

The speaker Belisar Venn supported Thalion against Darion Velsar, expecting the younger claimant to remain dependent on senatorial backing. Thalion recruited his own followers and developed a close working relationship with Istran Melvo, whose knowledge of vessels, supplies and training strengthened his forces.

In 365 BR he married Ilvara Deneth, whose earlier marriage had ended by agreement. She managed household expenditure, received requests from dependants and helped arrange supplies during his absences. Her position gave petitioners access to the ruling household while allowing its friends considerable advantages over people without introductions.

Thalion accepted the three-command government in 367 BR. He authorised confiscations and agreed to Belisar Venn's murder as part of the settlement with Velsar. The decision removed a former benefactor who had become inconvenient to the alliance. Subsequent imperial praise placed greater emphasis on restored order than on those abandoned to obtain it.

## War, settlement and personal alliances

Thalion supervised veteran settlement after the defeat of the republican armies in 363 BR. Grants included occupied farms, and displaced households joined resistance around Darsil. The suppression of that resistance brought starvation and executions.

His sister Evara's marriage to Velsar in 359 provided a period of cooperation. As their commands diverged, Thalion's supporters used Velsar's treatment of her to attack his alliance with Queen Samyra. Evara continued asking for the protection of people whose associations made them vulnerable to both sides.

Melvo's defeat of the maritime opposition in 355 strengthened Thalion's control of supplies. Seluc Varroth then lost his military following after an unsuccessful attempt to claim surrendered forces. Thalion obtained their allegiance through payment and continued service.

The final war brought him to Sarqen in 350 BR. Velsar and Samyra died before they could be taken to Caleran. Thalion ordered Amesar, Casvilar's acknowledged son, executed as a potential rival. The queen's younger children survived in Evara's custody.

## The imperial settlement

Thalion secured the gateway revenues and returned through the eastern and highland routes to Caleran. Around 350 BR he concentrated supreme command in his own household. Soldiers received more dependable arrangements for continued service and pay. Provincial governors required central approval, and taxation became more regular.

The senate retained civic and ceremonial work while losing effective control of the army. Astaroth's priesthood acquired precedence in imperial ceremonies of victory and command. The Common Sky remained part of public religion, including offices occupied by men who had once shared Thalion's power.

Trade and cultivation benefited as competing military levies diminished. The settlement also protected fortunes acquired through confiscation and left expelled families dependent on decisions made by the victors. These conditions formed the opening of imperial government and shaped the society which later celebrated Thalion as its founder.

## Later reign and death

[[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]] follows the demobilisation, provincial settlement and family decisions of his final decades. His son Caelor died in 324 BR after injuries received during a building inspection. Thalion adopted his nephew Meren, son of Evara and Velsar, as successor.

Thalion died after prolonged illness in 318 BR. The army and senate had already recognised Meren, allowing the first imperial succession to proceed without open civil war. Later bodily-ascent stories are unsupported; his complete human soul remained distinct from imperial office, portrait cult and the later dependent Pride Mask.
