# Historical Atlas of the Atherian Age of Glory

The Atherian Age of Glory extended from Aurelius Thalion's settlement around 350 BR to the world into which Isfir the Redeemer was born in 33 BR. Across those centuries, a government created from civil war became an empire of provinces, municipal cities, military roads and public star worship. Its inhabitants experienced long periods of peace within the central routes, repeated wars at their edges and striking inequalities between places joined under the same ruler.

Atherian writers called this period the Glory because its aqueducts, law, literature and armies seemed to prove that ordered civilisation could encompass the world. Provincial families could gain citizenship, education and office through the institutions of the empire. The same system transferred land to military settlers, moved enslaved people across seas and made distant households answer for the ambitions of Caleran.

## The settlement generation, 350–318 BR

[[The Later Reign of Aurelius Thalion]] began with disarmament, payment and the reassignment of commands. Thalion retained many municipal institutions while bringing provincial governors and the professional army under his household. Veterans received land, including farms taken during the succession wars. Sarqen remained a working port under imperial supervision.

Peace required further campaigns. Commanders secured roads between the Aurin and eastern ports, enforced settlements among former royal allies and negotiated with southern Folk leaders whose communities remained beyond direct occupation. Regular assessments gradually replaced indiscriminate military seizure as the usual means of supplying garrisons, though collection still involved force.

Thalion's son Caelor died before him. The emperor chose his nephew Meren, son of Evara Thalion and Darion Velsar, as successor. That decision joined descendants of the two principal founding rivals within one household. It also angered relatives who expected the succession to remain among Thalion's children.

## The House of Thalion, 318–247 BR

[[The House of Thalion]] governed through Meren I, Cassara I and Olarion. Meren continued demobilisation and confirmed the position of towns that had supplied the founding coalition. Cassara enlarged waterworks, harbour stores and road stations while becoming the first woman to exercise the imperial command in her own name.

The dynasty's government gradually acquired a ceremonial shape. An accession required army recognition, public confirmation at Caleran and the principal rites of the Common Sky and Astaroth. The separate ceremonies sometimes supported different candidates and gave powerful households several ways to contest a ruler.

Olarion used accusations, confiscation and execution against relatives and officers he feared. His death in 247 BR left no candidate able to command the capital, fleets and frontier armies together. [[The Year of Four Eagles]] carried fighting into the Aurin, eastern ports and western military districts before Samor Veloran prevailed.

## The Veloran settlement, 246–151 BR

[[The House of Veloran]] arose from a river-army household outside the old Caleran aristocracy. Samor I rewarded soldiers and punished cities that had supported his rivals. His daughter Tessa made those emergency arrangements more durable through roads, town charters and a clearer provincial treasury.

Under Tessa, the [[The Crown of Olveth|Crown of Olveth]] killed a general who tried to make a recognised shelter object into a victory trophy. Its return became one of the age's best-known demonstrations of a supernatural response whose meaning extended only to the purpose of that object. The event conferred no virtue upon the emperor.

Orent I pursued eastern campaigns and reorganised the roads behind them. [[The Two Roads of Arcelon]] preserves the victory account beside the work songs of people whose settlement was burned and whose labourers died building the route. Mariel I inherited the resulting revenues and presided over several comparatively peaceful decades of municipal building, travel and scholarship.

## Later dynasties, 151 BR–0 AR

[[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]] began with Elian Serrat, a provincial commander accepted after negotiation among the army, senate and eastern cities. The Serrat rulers depended heavily on municipal elites and professional administrators. Their reigns expanded citizenship among useful provincial households while concentrating great estates around the roads and river basins.

During the Raveth famine of 119–113 BR, the administrator Neriah bar Sorek organised grain imports and distribution that saved towns from starvation. His policy also transferred defaulting farms to the imperial domain and bound former owners to work them. [[The Keeper of Borrowed Grain]] follows the preservation, confiscation and later arguments over his responsibility.

The [[The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door - Imperial History|Low Door Companions]] rose during the last Serrat generations from service work into military and provincial office. Their abilities allowed people of modest origin to enter imperial government. Hostage-taking, an unlawful raid and the betrayal of a refuge accompanied their ascent.

A succession of later court houses preserved the empire through alliances with armies and provinces. Doran I restored authority after Velen Serrat's murder in 61 BR. Othran I succeeded through marriage and military support in 42 BR. His son Othran II became emperor in 24 BR and was still reigning when the Redeemer was executed at Caleran. His court possessed no knowledge that it stood at the end of a historical period.

## Provinces and frontiers

[[Provinces and Municipal Life in Imperial Atheria]] explains the layered administration beneath the rulers. Caleran governed most effectively through cities which collected taxes, maintained water and supplied transport. Local councils preserved languages, cults and influential families while extending imperial decisions.

The empire's eastern provinces included much of the country later inherited by Eclessia. Sestaran harbours occupied a changing range from direct provincial rule to alliance and commercial privilege. Sarqen connected the cultivated gateway country to inner-sea routes. Southern forts and treaty districts met independent Folk societies. Northern roads reached well kingdoms and mobile Tarkhan communities, while most of the dry belt remained outside imperial rule. Jirahar tower towns retained their own institutions inside and beyond eastern imperial territory.

[[The Imperial Army and the Frontiers]] follows recruitment, military families, auxiliary service and the work of maintaining forts. Recruits entered from subject and allied communities as well as the Atherian citizen body. Veterans carried language and institutions into new settlements, often upon land whose earlier inhabitants contested their title.

## Work, movement and prosperity

[[Food, Trade and Enslavement in Imperial Atheria]] follows the grain fleets, farms, mines, workshops and households sustaining the cities. More dependable routes encouraged specialisation and allowed people to travel farther for work or learning. Inns, baths, theatres and festivals made urban life attractive to visitors and migrants.

Enslavement remained part of the economy and the household. War supplied captives; markets separated relatives; mines and great estates consumed lives. Manumission gave some people and their children routes into citizenship. Those sold elsewhere and the families severed by the market received no remedy.

[[Schools, Arts and Imperial Magic]] describes the pleasures and knowledge of the period: theatre, satire, mosaics, medicine, navigation, philosophy, engineering and regulated rune craft. The empire preserved and exchanged immense bodies of knowledge. Patronage and censorship influenced which texts acquired multiple copies and which survived only in provincial quotations.

## Religion and imperial memory

[[Star Religion and the Imperial Cult]] developed the Common Sky into an imperial ceremonial system. Astaroth received precedence in rites of command; other Star Children governed festivals, occupations and local interpretations of the natural world. Thanatos remained a human misreading of the Boatman's signs.

Portraits, coins and praise formulae made successive rulers resemble one eternal imperial person. A dependent Pride Mask formed through the repeated attention given to these rites. [[Star Religion and the Imperial Cult#The Emperor after the Emperor|The Emperor after the Emperor]] describes its formation and the limits of its powers. No emperor's human soul entered it.

By 0 AR, imperial religion seemed inseparable from public life. Its temples employed workers, educated children and distributed food while also enforcing the celestial hierarchy of the state. The trial and execution of the Redeemer therefore occurred within institutions possessing centuries of confidence and habit.
