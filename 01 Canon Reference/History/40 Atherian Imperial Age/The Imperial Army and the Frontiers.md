# The Imperial Army and the Frontiers

The Atherian imperial army was a professional force supported by provincial taxation, military estates and a large civilian supply system. It guarded roads and ports, fought external wars and enforced imperial decisions within the provinces. Its units included long-service citizen formations, auxiliary troops recruited from allied peoples, fleets and specialist engineers.

## Service and households

Thalion's settlement placed essential units under continuing pay. A soldier served for years away from the place of birth and often formed a household near a garrison. Partners, children, traders, craftspeople and enslaved attendants travelled with or gathered around the forts.

Recruitment drew on the heartland and provinces. Allied leaders supplied contingents skilled in local terrain. Folk scouts, gateway sailors, eastern archers and northern camel handlers entered imperial service through agreements that left their wider peoples politically independent.

Pay arrived through coin, food and promised grants. Delay encouraged soldiers to compel local suppliers or support a commander who could meet arrears. The government therefore treated military finance as a condition of political survival.

## Forts and supply

A frontier fort required grain, animals, timber, cloth, metal and clean water. Roads and depots mattered more to continued occupation than a single battlefield victory. Civilians baked, repaired, carried and negotiated for the army.

Garrisons protected markets from some raiders and created demand for regional goods. They also requisitioned transport, punished suspected support for enemies and changed the balance among local rulers. A fort might preserve one town while drawing attack towards another.

Runes supported signalling, fire control, siege work and structural testing. Their use required trained people and material preparation. Every army still depended on delivered grain, reliable water and knowledge of the ground.

## Southern and northern frontiers

The southern frontier remained a belt of forts, allied districts and independent Folk country. Some clans served as auxiliaries or federates. Others raided provincial settlements, fought each other or withdrew beyond reliable imperial reach. Annual Grobi dangers required negotiated cooperation across the imperial boundary.

In the northern dry belt, armies depended on defended wells and caravan routes. Ostar's much earlier failure at Rethmar remained a lesson in the consequences of assuming water would submit to arrival. Imperial expeditions negotiated with well communities and employed camels. The eastern horse cultures of the later Tarkhan First Catch had not yet developed.

## Eastern and maritime commands

Eastern forces guarded mountain passages, river routes and cities with substantial institutions of their own. Their commanders possessed access to wealth and communication, which made them important during succession disputes.

Fleets escorted transport, pursued raiders and supported coastal campaigns. Ships were expensive to maintain. Timber cutting, compulsory harbour work and crew recruitment placed much of the cost on coastal households.

## War and return

Victories produced captives, land and public honours. Defeat produced disabled veterans, missing relatives and debts owed for equipment or ransom. Military cemeteries beside frontier roads contain people from many provinces.

Veteran settlement carried skills and savings into towns. It also displaced cultivators and gave armed newcomers authority over neighbours. The imperial army joined the world through marriages and markets as well as forts; its violence remained present within those relationships.
