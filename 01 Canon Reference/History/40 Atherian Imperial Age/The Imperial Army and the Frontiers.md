# The Imperial Army and the Frontiers

The Atherian imperial army was a professional force supported by provincial taxation, military estates and a large civilian supply system. It guarded roads and ports, fought external wars and enforced imperial decisions within the provinces. Its units included long-service citizen formations, auxiliary troops recruited from allied peoples, fleets and specialist engineers.

## Service and households

Thalion's settlement placed essential units under continuing pay. A soldier served for years away from the place of birth and often formed a household near a garrison. Partners, children, traders, craftspeople and enslaved attendants travelled with or gathered around the forts.

Recruitment drew on the heartland and provinces. Allied leaders supplied contingents skilled in local terrain. Folk scouts, gateway sailors, eastern archers and northern camel handlers entered imperial service through agreements that left their wider peoples politically independent.

Pay arrived through coin, food and promised grants. Delay encouraged soldiers to compel local suppliers or support a commander who could meet arrears. The government therefore treated military finance as a condition of political survival.

## Forts and supply

A frontier fort required grain, animals, timber, cloth, metal and clean water. Roads and depots mattered more to continued occupation than a single battlefield victory. Civilians baked, repaired, carried and negotiated for the army.

Garrisons protected markets from some raiders and created demand for regional goods. They also requisitioned transport, punished suspected support for enemies and changed the balance among local rulers. A fort might preserve one town while drawing attack towards another.

Runes supported signalling, fire control, siege work and structural testing. Military engineers had loaded siege frames and bridges before the Red Star since the late republic, and after 326 BR the civil [[Schools, Arts and Imperial Magic#Rune craft|wardens]] carried the same rite into every public work. Their use required trained people and material preparation. Every army still depended on delivered grain, reliable water and knowledge of the ground.

Units carried standards bearing a copy of the emperor's portrait, kept by priests of the Red Star, and soldiers swore their oath of service before it. Velen I's guards swore that oath, and in 61 BR they stood in their ranks facing the image while he drowned under his own festival banks. Doran I then had his officers enter their commands by a low door, stripped of insignia and carrying a plain bowl, in place of the oath before the image. The custom came from the [[The Companions Who Entered by the Low Door - Imperial History|Low Door Companions]], two of whom had supplied his march, and old command houses still keep their low lintels.

## Southern and northern frontiers

The southern frontier remained a belt of forts, allied districts and independent Folk country. Some clans served as auxiliaries or federates. Others raided provincial settlements, fought each other or withdrew beyond reliable imperial reach. Annual Grobi dangers required negotiated cooperation across the imperial boundary.

In the northern dry belt, armies depended on defended wells and caravan routes. Ostar's much earlier failure at Rethmar remained a lesson in the consequences of assuming water would submit to arrival. Imperial expeditions negotiated with well communities and employed camels.

## Eastern and maritime commands

Eastern forces guarded mountain passages, river routes and cities with substantial institutions of their own. Their commanders possessed access to wealth and communication, which made them important during succession disputes. Vashar's Dawn Road, with its relay stations and remounts, let Vasharan reserves reach a threatened bridge faster than any Atherian column; the Arcelon road of c. 190–182 BR was built to answer it. In the fever summer of 151 the river commands could agree on no one, and the towns of Sarqen and the eastern cities put forward Sarqen's governor, Elian Serrat.

Beyond the eastern provinces Vashar remained a sovereign peer. Under the late Serrats its civil war, the War of the Two Dawns, let Atheria take the sanctuary city of Qiryath under a charter, and Vashar accepted a yearly share of pilgrim dues in exchange. In the Qahuri basin the three crowns of Tenaret, Qemaret and Sakhret kept their thrones into the Redeemer's lifetime. Atherian contracts bound Qemaret's lower river and grain harbour, where a station stood, and the other two crowns ruled as clients.

Fleets escorted transport, pursued raiders and supported coastal campaigns. Ships were expensive to maintain. Timber cutting, compulsory harbour work and crew recruitment placed much of the cost on coastal households.

## War and return

Victories produced captives, land and public honours. Defeat produced disabled veterans, missing relatives and debts owed for equipment or ransom. Military cemeteries beside frontier roads contain people from many provinces.

Veterans brought savings, trades and a soldier's Atherian into the towns where they settled. They also took farms from the families already working them, and those families learned to be careful with armed neighbours.
