# Households of the Imperial Founding

The households of Atheria's founding generation connected military commands to the people who supplied them, carried terms between them and survived their defeats. Marriages could support cooperation across rival interests or turn a war into a feud. Property, upbringing and personal affection created obligations that outlasted every political partnership.

The principal families belonged to different societies. The Caleran houses drew upon citizenship, land and public service. The Nerethan dynasty governed a country formed by the survival of the gateway towns, whose oath made and unmade its rulers. Southern communities met these families chiefly through armies, requisitions and the fate of captives.

## Lysara and the younger Thalions

Lysara Casvilar, born in 421 BR, was Teren Casvilar's sister and the mother of Evara and Aurelius Thalion. Her husband Aurel died in 384. She maintained household property and relationships with people who had received help from either family.

Casvilar's assassination changed the demands placed on her. Thalion's inheritance gave him a public claim without the money to meet every promise attached to it. Lysara sold the family's town houses, borrowed from her brother's friends and received veterans who came to the door for reassurance. Her contribution kept the younger claimant in the contest.

Evara Thalion, born in 393, never married. She kept her brother's accounts with their mother through the war years and, after 350, ran the imperial household from the store-house above the muster ground: audiences, petitions, widows' pensions and the relatives who crowded the doors. Her favour could secure an appointment or a pardon. It also gave well-connected petitioners advantages over people who could not obtain an introduction.

## Ilvara, Merath and the grain bin

Ilvara Deneth, whom Thalion married in 365, managed the household's money and dependants during the Writing-In, when a word at the right door could keep a name out of the register. Their son Caelor was born in 363. Ilvara died in 361 giving birth to their daughter, named Lysara after her grandmother.

Thalion's second marriage, in 360, was a claim on a kingdom. Merath Nerethan, born in 394, was the eldest child of King Olymes of Sarqen and had been passed over when the spared towns restored her younger sister Samyra in 373. She came to Caleran looking for an Atherian patron. In 359 she went back east to Esharat, the inland town of her ancestor Hadrima, to bear her child where the heirs of the house were born. In 358, at Samyra's request, Darion Velsar's officers seized her there and executed her.

The queen's officers asked for the newborn as well. The women of Esharat hid him in a grain bin, as Hesara of Esharat had hidden children from Qarzeth's collectors in the same town, and the elders told the officers that the oath forbade a child for the table. Carters brought him west under a load of barley. Lysara raised him at Caleran with Caelor and the younger Lysara. He was Meren, and he became the second emperor. At eight he went with his father to Sarqen's landing and ate at the spared towns' table across from the women who had hidden him.

## Calvena between two commanders

Calvena Casvilar was born in 405 BR and married Marovan Veyr in 386. Her husband was thirty years older, had children from an earlier union, including Ardel, and a long record of military service. Their household joined people with different expectations of rank and inheritance.

The marriage became affectionate. Calvena arranged visits, passed on family news and spoke with both her father and husband about matters that public argument had made difficult. For a decade her dinners were where the two men still talked.

The quarrel that ended those dinners was about land. From 379 her father's veterans claimed public fields that Veyr's senatorial friends had leased for generations, and the friends demanded that Casvilar give up his army first. In 377 Casvilar offered his daughter a divorce, so that she need not be married to his enemy. Calvena refused it in public, before her father's own clients: she had married a man, she said, and would leave him when he gave her cause. Caleran repeated the words for years. Her refusal is remembered as the moment the partnership died, though the land and the armies had already killed it.

She went east with Veyr's household when the war began. In 373 she was aboard when he turned back from the royal table at Sarqen, and on Ilamar she nursed him through the fever that killed him in 371. A year later her father was murdered on the muster ground.

Calvena was now first cousin to Thalion and stepmother to Ardel Veyr, whose ships sheltered people fleeing the Writing-In. She became the one person both would receive. In 357 she carried the terms between them, and the short-lived agreement of that year, which protected some fugitives and recognised some of Ardel's possessions, was largely her work. It broke within a season over withheld supplies and captured ships.

In 350 her half-brother Amesar, Casvilar's son by Queen Samyra, whom she had never met, took sanctuary on Ilamar after killing his father's murderer at the spared towns' table. Thalion's officers waited on the quay. Calvena crossed to the island, went to her cousin afterwards and asked him to leave the boy where he was. Thalion did. She survived the wars; her later life belongs to the history of the Age of Glory.

Later accounts sometimes made her the reason the republic held together as long as it did. Others, especially in Ardel's fleet, said she had bargained for her cousin all along.

## Miretha, Tarsen and Darsil

Miretha Ardel, born in 407 BR, used her property and household connections to support Darion Velsar's military world. Officers' families sought her assistance during long absences. She could arrange provisions, loans and accommodation through people whose loyalty was personal to her.

While the Red Field stood open no army could be discharged, and after his victory at Darsil in 363 Velsar quartered his companies on the town. Darsil starved through the winter of 362–361 and rose in the spring. Velsar's brother Tarsen, commanding the garrison, took the remaining grain into the citadel and hanged eleven organisers in the market, and the town gave in when its children began to die.

Miretha came east with carts of grain bought from her own estates and fed the town for a month. She died there of the winter fever in 360. Darsil remembered her carts and Tarsen's gallows in the same songs. Tarsen died two years later in his brother's campaign into the approaches of Avesh. Miretha's children were raised by her own kin.

## The queen's household

Samyra Nerethan's children came from two Atherian alliances. Amesar, born in 372, was Casvilar's acknowledged son. Talven and Lethia, born in 357 and 354, were Velsar's. From 362 the household also shared its table, at the spared towns' renewal each spring, with Mavren Seneth, one of the seven who had killed Casvilar, whom the towns' oath protected.

In the spring of 350 Amesar, twenty-two, killed Seneth at that table with the knife he had brought to cut bread. The towns would not harm a son of the house, but they put the house out and filled the queen's seat with a rower her officers had abandoned on a flooded basin's quay the year before. Amesar went to Ilamar. When Thalion came to the landing that summer, Samyra walked down with Talven and Lethia and sat at the table as a stranger, where the oath forbade anyone to take them from it.

The three of them lived afterwards at Esharat as guests of the towns. Samyra died there in 333. Talven took a supervised post in Sarqen's harbour administration from his cousin Meren, the son of the aunt whose death his mother had requested. Lethia endowed a house at Esharat for children whose fathers had been taken in the Fourth Collection, her mother's levy for Velsar's army. Amesar never left Ilamar. There he finished the history of the republic that Arest Corven had begun in Veyr's last years, and he died on the island about 320.

## Hadrima's descendants

The royal house began with Nerethan of Delkos and Hadrima of Esharat. Their marriage in 497 BR connected an incoming military command to cultivated land, storage and local families. Hadrima's descendants invoked her standing when they asked the spared towns to support a crown, and every one of them ate at the towns' unranked table and swore the Table Oath before the towns would call them king or queen.

The relationship could be disputed from within the family. After Olymes died in 375, Merath, Samyra and Nethar pursued competing claims. Vardek and part of the garrison supported Nethar; Samyra retained supporters at Esharat; Merath looked for allies abroad and in the end found Thalion.

In 373 Vardek laid a royal table for the fugitive Marovan Veyr with an empty seat at it, a thing the oath forbade, and the towns rose for Samyra when they learned what the seat had been for. Nethar died in the fighting of 372. Merath was executed in 358. Her attendants and supporters thereafter remembered the dynasty through a loss inflicted by the reigning queen, and through a baby the reigning queen had looked for.

## Friendship, pardon and betrayal

Casvilar's assassins had different relationships with him. Deceran Tolm had served as a trusted quartermaster and kept the arms register of the muster ground, where anyone who challenged the Warden of the Red Field had to enter his name. Mavren Seneth had accepted pardon after fighting for Veyr. Corial Draven possessed naval contacts and ambitions frustrated by the distribution of commands. In 370 seven men wrote their names in Tolm's register as challengers, and Tolm chose the hour. They struck Casvilar down on the muster ground before the contest was opened.

The assassination joined their purposes briefly. Afterwards they could not supply a government capable of satisfying the armies and families whose expectations Casvilar had accumulated, and they did not try. They scattered to the refuges of the inner sea, knowing that while one of them lived no commander would dare disband an army. Their deaths came one by one over twenty years: at a door in Caleran, among the Folk of Darneth, on Ilamar's quay, behind the Kept Doors of Vorthara, in the burning of Marelith, at Avesh and at the spared towns' table.

Belisar Venn's association with Thalion followed a similar course of useful cooperation and abandonment. The speaker helped the young heir oppose Velsar and sat with him among the Four at the Stores. When Velsar asked for Venn's name to be written into the register, Thalion consented. Venn walked down to the muster ground with a practice sword borrowed from his grandson and was killed there.

## Households beyond the victors

At Kellovar, Velcara organised grain storage and helped sustain Drenoc's coalition against Casvilar. Her children escaped through maternal relatives. She remained, was captured and eventually returned through ransom arranged by those same connections. Her later work rebuilding storage belonged to a community that had lost people and land.

Families at Darneth supported men whose hands had been cut off after surrender. Work had to be divided differently, and impoverished households absorbed lasting responsibilities. The triumph celebrated at Caleran became an enduring change in meals, labour and dependence far from the capital. When one of Casvilar's killers came to Darneth in flight, those households fed him.

At Esharat, carters' families who had hidden one child of the house and fed the queen's army in the Fourth Collection kept both stories. Tobal of Hazreth, the rower left on the basin quay, sat in the queen's seat in the summer of 350, when the towns laid their table for Thalion, and came to the renewal every spring for the rest of his life.
