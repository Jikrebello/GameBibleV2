# Households of the Imperial Founding

The households of Atheria's founding generation connected military commands to the people who supplied them, negotiated between them and survived their defeats. Marriages could support cooperation across rival interests. Property, upbringing and personal affection also created obligations that continued after a political partnership ended.

The principal families belonged to different societies. The Caleran houses drew upon citizenship, land and public service. The Nerethan dynasty governed a country formed through the survival of gateway towns and subsequent maritime settlement. Southern communities encountered these families chiefly through armies, requisitions and the fate of captives.

## Lysara and the younger Thalions

Lysara Casvilar, born in 421 BR, was Teren Casvilar's sister and the mother of Evara and Aurelius Thalion. Her husband Aurel died in 384. She maintained household property and relationships with people who had received assistance from either family.

Casvilar's assassination changed the demands placed on her. Thalion's inheritance gave him a public claim without providing the money required to meet every promise attached to it. Lysara helped arrange sales and borrowing, approached relatives and received veterans seeking reassurance. Her contribution enabled the younger claimant to remain in the contest.

Ilvara Deneth, whom Thalion married in 365, brought experience of household management and connections of her own. She supervised expenditure, dealt with dependants and arranged supplies during his absences. Assistance through her household could preserve a family or secure an appointment. It also gave well-connected petitioners advantages over people who could not obtain an introduction.

## Calvena between two commanders

Calvena Casvilar was born in 405 BR and married Marovan Veyr in 386. Her husband had children from an earlier union and a long record of military service. Their household joined people with different expectations of rank and inheritance.

The marriage became affectionate. Calvena arranged visits, passed on family news and spoke with both her father and husband about matters that public argument had made difficult. These ordinary occasions helped sustain their relationship while Casvilar's growing command altered the balance between them.

She died in childbirth in 380. Her loss affected people beyond the two commanders, including attendants and relatives accustomed to relying on her. Later accounts sometimes made the death an explanation for civil war. The disputes over commands, prosecution and soldiers' land developed through many decisions after she was gone.

## Miretha, Tarsen and Darsil

Miretha Ardel, born in 407 BR, used her property and household connections to support Darion Velsar's military world. Officers' families sought her assistance during long absences. She could arrange provisions, loans and accommodation through people whose loyalty was personal to her.

Veteran settlement after 363 placed that network under strain. Families which had supplied victory lost fields to the returning soldiers. Miretha and Velsar's brother Tarsen supported resistance around Darsil in 362–361, hoping to defend those households and preserve their influence in the east.

Their movement brought armed confrontation into a town already burdened by campaigning. Siege produced hunger; surrender brought executions. Tarsen escaped into exile. Velsar's encouragement had failed to become military relief.

Miretha spent the remaining period before her death in 360 supporting dependants whose homes and prospects had been damaged. Some later joined Evara's household. Their survival depended on arrangements made between women whose marriages belonged to different stages of Velsar's career.

## Evara and the children of rival households

Evara Thalion, born in 393 BR, married Velsar in 359. She arranged meetings, payments and supplies during a period when the partners could still cooperate. Their children Meren and Sovia were born in 358 and 356.

Velsar's alliance with Samyra weakened the marriage and eventually left Evara maintaining a separate household. Thalion used his sister's treatment in the public campaign against Velsar. She continued seeking protection for dependants whom that campaign endangered.

After the conquest of Sarqen in 350, Samyra's surviving children Talven and Lethia entered Evara's custody. They were approximately seven and four. Evara retained attendants from their mother's country and helped preserve contact with familiar people. They grew up beside other children of Velsar while remaining under the authority of the man who had conquered their kingdom.

Amesar, Samyra's adult son by Casvilar, received no such protection. Thalion ordered his execution. The difference reflected the political danger attributed to an acknowledged royal heir who could command supporters in his own right.

## Hadrima's descendants

The royal house began with Nerethan of Delkos and Hadrima of Esharat. Their marriage in 497 BR connected an incoming military command to cultivated land, storage and local families. Hadrima's descendants invoked her standing when they asked the spared towns to support a crown.

The relationship could be disputed from within the family. After Olymes died in 375, Merath, Samyra and Nethar pursued competing claims. Vardek and part of the garrison supported Nethar; Samyra retained supporters at Esharat; Merath sought allies for another branch.

Nethar died in the fighting of 372. Merath was executed in 358 on Velsar's order at Samyra's request. Her attendants and supporters thereafter remembered the dynasty through a loss inflicted by the reigning queen. The royal household contained both affection for children and a willingness to kill relatives whose claims threatened power.

## Friendship, pardon and betrayal

Casvilar's assassins had different relationships with him. Deceran Tolm had served as a trusted quartermaster and could arrange access. Mavren Seneth had accepted pardon after fighting for Veyr. Corial Draven possessed naval contacts and ambitions frustrated by the distribution of commands.

The assassination joined their purposes briefly. Afterwards they could not supply a government capable of satisfying all the armies and families whose expectations Casvilar had accumulated. Their deaths came in separate stages of the ensuing wars.

Belisar Venn's association with Thalion followed a similar course of useful cooperation and abandonment. The speaker helped the young heir oppose Velsar. Thalion later consented to his murder when agreement with Velsar promised greater power.

## Households beyond the victors

At Kellovar, Velcara organised grain storage and helped sustain Drenoc's coalition against Casvilar. Her children escaped through maternal relatives. She remained, was captured and eventually returned through ransom arranged by those same connections. Her later work rebuilding storage belonged to a community that had lost people and land.

Families at Darneth supported men whose hands had been cut off after surrender. Work had to be divided differently, and impoverished households absorbed lasting responsibilities. The triumph celebrated at Caleran became an enduring change in meals, labour and dependence far from the capital.

These lives continued alongside the more extensively recorded marriages of rulers. The imperial founding altered who could govern, inherit and command; it also changed who prepared food for an injured relative, sheltered a child or could return to a former home.
