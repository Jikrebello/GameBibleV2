# Households of the Imperial Founding

The households of Atheria's founding generation connected military commands to the people who supplied them, carried terms between them and survived their defeats. Marriages could support cooperation across rival interests or turn a war into a feud. Property, upbringing and personal affection created obligations that outlasted every political partnership.

The principal families belonged to different societies. The Caleran houses drew upon citizenship, land and public service. The Nerethan dynasty governed a country formed by the survival of the gateway towns, whose oath made and unmade its rulers. Southern communities met these families chiefly through armies, requisitions and the fate of captives.

## Lysara and the younger Thalions

Lysara Casvilar, born in 421 BR, was Teren Casvilar's sister and the mother of Evara and Aurelius Thalion. Her husband Aurel died in 384. She maintained household property and relationships with people who had received help from either family.

Casvilar's assassination changed the demands placed on her. Thalion's inheritance gave him a public claim without the money to meet every promise attached to it. Lysara sold the family's town houses, borrowed from her brother's friends and received veterans who came to the door for reassurance. Her contribution kept the younger claimant in the contest.

Evara Thalion, born in 393, never married. She kept her brother's accounts with their mother through the war years and, after 350, ran the imperial household from the store-house above the muster ground: audiences, petitions, widows' pensions and the relatives who crowded the doors. Her favour could secure an appointment or a pardon. It also gave well-connected petitioners advantages over people who could not obtain an introduction.

## Ilmara, Merath and the child the towns counted

Ilmara Deneth, whom Thalion married in 365, managed the household's money and dependants during the Writing-In, when a word at the right door could keep a name out of the register. Their son Caelor was born in 363. Ilmara died in 361 giving birth to their daughter, named Lysara after her grandmother.

Thalion's second marriage, in 360, was a claim on a kingdom. Merath Nerethan, born in 394, was the eldest child of King Olymes of Sarqen and had been passed over when the spared towns restored her younger sister Samyra in 373. She came to Caleran looking for an Atherian patron. In 359 she went back east to Esharat, the inland town of her ancestor Hadrima, to bear her child where the heirs of the house were born. Her son was born there in 358, and that summer Samyra's officers brought mother and child to the palace at Sarqen.

The women of Esharat would not hand the newborn over at the door, and the elders let him go only on the queen's promise to show him at the landing every spring, under the oath that forbade a child for the table. He was Meren, and he became the second emperor. He spent his first eight years in the queen's household, latterly in the same nursery as her younger children, and each spring the carters' elders looked him over at the renewal. His mother lived behind a guarded stair in the same palace and died there of fever in the blockade winter of 351. In the summer of 350 he sat on the bench at the towns' table between his aunt and a father he had never seen, and went home with the father. Lysara raised him at Caleran from then on, with Caelor and the younger Lysara.

## Selvena between two commanders

Selvena Casvilar was born in 405 BR and married Marovan Veyr in 389. Her husband was thirty years older, had children from an earlier union, including Ardel, and a long record of military service. Their household joined people with different expectations of rank and inheritance.

The marriage became affectionate. Selvena arranged visits, passed on family news and spoke with both her father and husband about matters that public argument had made difficult. For a decade her dinners were where the two men still talked.

The quarrel that ended those dinners was about land. From 379 her father's veterans claimed public fields that Veyr's senatorial friends had leased for generations, and the friends demanded that Casvilar give up his army first. In 377 Casvilar offered his daughter a divorce, so that she need not be married to his enemy. Selvena refused it in public, before her father's own clients: she had married a man, she said, and would leave him when he gave her cause. Caleran repeated the words for years. Her refusal is remembered as the moment the partnership died, though the land and the armies had already killed it.

She went east with Veyr's household when the war began. In 373 she was aboard when he turned back from the royal table at Sarqen, and on Ilamar she nursed him through the fever that killed him in 371. A year later her father was murdered on the muster ground.

Selvena was now first cousin to Thalion and stepmother to Ardel Veyr, whose ships sheltered people fleeing the Writing-In. She became the one person both would receive. After Marelith burned in 355, Ardel and the city's survivors sat in Pyrassa under the old law of refuge, and Melvo would not take them from the quay. In 353 Selvena crossed to Pyrassa and made the terms on the mole: Ardel's life and the survivors' freedom for his remaining ships and his oath never to pass the mole towers. The agreement held, and Ardel lived out his life in the harbour.

In 350 her half-brother Hadram, Casvilar's son by Queen Samyra, whom she had never met, took sanctuary on Ilamar after killing his father's murderer at the spared towns' table. Thalion's officers waited on the quay. Selvena crossed to the island, went to her cousin afterwards and asked him to leave the boy where he was. Thalion did. She survived the wars; her later life belongs to the history of the Age of Glory.

Later accounts sometimes made her the reason the republic held together as long as it did. Others, especially in Ardel's fleet, said she had bargained for her cousin all along.

## Miretha, Tarsen and Darsil

Miretha Ardel, born in 407 BR, used her property and household connections to support Darion Velsar's military world. Officers' families sought her assistance during long absences. She could arrange provisions, loans and accommodation through people whose loyalty was personal to her.

While the Red Field stood fouled no army could be discharged, and after his victory at Darsil in 369 Velsar quartered his companies on the town. Darsil went short through its second winter, starved through its third and rose in the spring of 366. Velsar's brother Tarsen, commanding the garrison, took the remaining grain into the citadel and hanged eleven organisers in the market, and the town gave in when its children began to die.

Miretha came east that summer with carts of grain bought from her own estates and fed the town until the harvest. She came back every autumn for ten years, selling a farm for each bad season, and died at Darsil of the winter fever in 356. Darsil remembered her carts and Tarsen's gallows in the same songs. Tarsen rode with his brother up the Dawn Road in 354 and died beside him on the Salt Field in 350. Miretha's children were raised by her own kin.

## The queen's household

Samyra Nerethan's children came from two Atherian alliances. Hadram, born in 372, was Casvilar's acknowledged son. Talven and Lethia, born in 353 and 351, were Velsar's. From 358 her nephew Meren, Thalion's son, grew up beside them. From 362 the household also shared its table, at the spared towns' renewal each spring, with Mavren Seneth, one of the seven who had killed Casvilar, whom the towns' oath protected.

In the spring of 350 Hadram, twenty-two, killed Seneth at that table with the knife he had brought to cut bread. The towns would not harm a son of the house, but they put the house out and filled the queen's seat with a rower her officers had abandoned on a flooded basin's quay the year before. Hadram went to Ilamar. When Thalion came to the landing that summer, Samyra walked down with Talven, Lethia and Meren and sat at the table as a stranger, where the oath forbade anyone to take them from it.

Samyra, Talven and Lethia lived afterwards at Esharat as guests of the towns. Samyra died there in 333. Talven took a supervised post in Sarqen's harbour administration from his cousin Meren, who had shared his nursery. Lethia endowed a house at Esharat for children whose fathers had been taken in the Fourth Collection, her mother's levy for Velsar's army. Hadram never left Ilamar. There he finished the history of the republic that Arest Corven had begun in Veyr's last years, and he died on the island about 320.

## Hadrima's descendants

The royal house began with Nerethan of Delkos and Hadrima of Esharat. Their marriage in 497 BR connected an incoming military command to cultivated land, storage and local families. Hadrima's descendants invoked her standing when they asked the spared towns to support a crown, and every one of them ate at the towns' unranked table and swore the Table Oath before the towns would call them king or queen.

The relationship could be disputed from within the family. After Olymes died in 375, Merath, Samyra and Nethar pursued competing claims. Vardek and part of the garrison supported Nethar; Samyra retained supporters at Esharat; Merath looked for allies abroad and in the end found Thalion.

In 373 Vardek laid a royal table for Marovan Veyr with an empty seat at it, a thing the oath forbade, and the towns rose for Samyra when they learned what the seat had been for. Nethar died in the fighting of 372. Merath died in her sister's palace in 351, after seven years behind a guarded stair. Her attendants and supporters thereafter remembered the dynasty through a loss they laid at the reigning queen's door, and through a boy the towns had counted every spring.

## Friendship, pardon and betrayal

Casvilar's assassins had different quarrels with him. Dervan Tolm was quartermaster of the muster ground and kept its arms register, in which anyone who challenged the Warden of the Red Field had to be entered. Mavren Seneth had commanded the river line at Kellovar, fought for the senate at Velos and accepted the Warden's pardon. Corial Draven was a ship-owner who had held a third of Lethren Ostar's paper when Casvilar burned the ledgers. In 370 seven names went into Tolm's register as challengers, the seventh a young man of the river halls entered as Drenoc's son, and Tolm fixed the day. Whether he knew what the seven intended was never settled. They struck Casvilar down on the muster ground before the contest was opened.

The assassination joined their purposes briefly. Afterwards they could not supply a government capable of satisfying the armies and families whose expectations Casvilar had accumulated, and they did not try. They scattered to the refuges of the inner sea, knowing that while one of them lived no commander would dare disband an army. Their deaths came one by one over twenty years: at a door in Caleran, among the Folk of Darneth, on Ilamar's quay, behind the Kept Doors of Vorthara, in the burning of Marelith, at a relay station on Vashar's Dawn Road and at the spared towns' table.

Besral Venn's association with Thalion followed a similar course of useful cooperation and abandonment, and then outlasted both. The speaker helped the young heir oppose Velsar and sat with him among the Four at the Stores. In 366, when Velsar asked for Venn's name to be written into the register, Thalion consented. Venn, sixty-eight, walked down to the muster ground with a practice sword borrowed from his grandson. He put the first soldier on the sand, and the other eight walked away. He kept the senate's river stores for the rest of the war and fed the companies of the man who had agreed to his death. In 350, asked to close the Field as the one living man who had answered to his name on it, he sent Thalion the practice sword and no message. He died in his bed two years later.

## Households beyond the victors

At Kellovar, Velcara organised grain storage and helped sustain Drenoc's coalition against Casvilar. Her children escaped through maternal relatives. She remained, was captured and eventually returned through ransom arranged by those same connections. Her later work rebuilding storage belonged to a community that had lost people and land.

Families at Darneth supported men whose hands had been cut off after surrender. Work had to be divided differently, and impoverished households absorbed lasting responsibilities. The triumph celebrated at Caleran became an enduring change in meals, labour and dependence far from the capital. When one of Casvilar's killers came to Darneth in flight, those households fed him.

At Esharat, carters' families who had counted one child of the house every spring and fed the queen's army in the Fourth Collection kept both stories. Tobal of Hazreth, the rower left on the basin quay, sat in the queen's seat in the summer of 350, when the towns laid their table for Thalion, and came to the renewal every spring for the rest of his life. At Pyrassa the Burnt Quarter, where Marelith's survivors had been lodged in the boat sheds, kept a lamp for the drowned city on the night of the burning.
