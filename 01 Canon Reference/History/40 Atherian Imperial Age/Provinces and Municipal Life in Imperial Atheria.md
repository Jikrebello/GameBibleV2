# Provinces and Municipal Life in Imperial Atheria

Atheria governed its territories through provinces and cities whose institutions were older than the empire. Governors represented the emperor, commanded troops and supervised collection. Municipal councils maintained water, markets, streets and local worship. Between those levels stood treasury agents, military suppliers, temple estates and households able to translate an order into work.

## Provincial government

Thalion required governors to hold appointment from the imperial household and divided several commands that had supported independent warlords. A provincial term had no single uniform length. Effective governors depended on knowledge held by local clerks, town leaders, interpreters and transport households.

Taxes took the form of coin, grain, animals, materials and labour according to region. Assessments made obligations more predictable than wartime seizure. A figure based on an abundant year could become ruinous after flood, fire or failed harvest. Towns petitioned for reduction through patrons and delegations.

Governors heard appeals and settled disputes involving different local customs. Their decisions could protect a village against an abusive contractor or place an opponent's estate in the hands of a friend. Travel and communication limited direct supervision from Caleran.

## Municipal cities

Recognised cities possessed councils, magistrates, markets and public resources. Their households took pride in baths, fountains, theatres and festivals. Competition encouraged benefactors to repair a harbour or provide food during shortage.

Council membership carried expense. Members were expected to collect assessments and supply public works when ordinary revenue failed. Wealthy families sought the honour in prosperous periods and tried to escape it when imperial demands increased.

Citizenship expanded by grant, military service, manumission and the admission of whole communities. It opened access to contracts, courts and office. Residents outside the citizen body continued to contribute labour and rent. Local ancestry, Atherian civic identity and political loyalty overlapped in different proportions from one province to another.

## Regional variation

The Aurin heartland contained dense roads, old city institutions and estates close to Caleran. Eastern provinces adapted imperial government to river towns, mountain routes and long-established religious communities. Their later histories contributed heavily to Eclessia.

Sestaran harbours ranged from provincial cities to allied ports with negotiated privileges. The gateway country around Sarqen retained town relationships formed before annexation. In the south, military districts and client agreements met independent Folk communities. Northern roads ended among well kingdoms, caravan societies and mobile peoples the empire could influence more easily than rule.

Jirahar communities included tower towns, diaspora households and quarters within imperial cities. Some possessed collective privileges; others depended on arrangements with a governor. Their institutions remained their own, even where the surrounding province called every resident Atherian.

## A family's experience

One municipal household might include a council member, a daughter managing a workshop, an enslaved tutor, a veteran son and relatives farming outside the walls. An aqueduct improved their water. Its levy could also take animals needed for harvest.

Festival days brought processions, games and meals funded by local benefactors. Children learnt songs connecting their town to the stars and emperor. Market jokes about tax collectors circulated beside serious pride in civic buildings.

The empire endured because these local worlds could recognise themselves within it. It also depended on their inability to refuse every demand made in the name of the wider peace.
