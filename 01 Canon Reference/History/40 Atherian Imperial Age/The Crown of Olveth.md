# The Crown of Olveth

The Crown of Olveth is a woven metal circlet made to mark a recognised place of shelter in the Atherian uplands. During the campaign of 219 BR, the general Cavar Oren seized it and attempted to wear it as a trophy. The object burned him to death and injured attendants who knowingly helped prepare the display.

## The shelter

Metalworkers and household elders at Olveth made the circlet generations before the Veloran campaign. It hung above the entrance to a mountain refuge used during storms, raids and dangerous seasonal movement. People admitted beneath it had to surrender weapons and share available water until departure.

The Crown responded when the shelter's duties were being kept. Its surface became warm and gave a low light visible through smoke or snow. The response belonged to the made object's continuing purpose. The Crown contained no human soul or Menhir presence.

Olveth's refuge accepted imperial travellers as well as local families. Disputes arose when officers demanded precedence over people already inside. Custodians insisted that entry placed them under the same rule as everyone else.

## Capture

Cavar Oren campaigned against upland communities accused of sheltering raiders and withholding passage. His forces reached Olveth after defenders withdrew into higher country. Soldiers entered the refuge, removed the Crown and detained custodians who objected.

One custodian died after being beaten during questioning. Others explained that the object would answer only above a shelter whose obligations were observed. Oren regarded the warning as an attempt to deny imperial ownership.

He ordered the Crown fitted to his head for a public procession. The metal heated before the ceremony left the captured settlement. Oren died from the burns; two attendants handling its fastenings were permanently injured. Soldiers abandoned the object within the damaged refuge.

## Return and use

Empress Tessa sent specialists to observe the Crown in place. They found that it responded when the entrance again admitted endangered people under the shelter rule. It remained cold during military acclamation and when moved into a command tent.

Tessa ordered its return and compensated the surviving custodians. The army kept control of the surrounding road. Compensation could neither restore the dead nor return every displaced family. Olveth rebuilt the refuge and limited the Crown's display.

Later emperors cited Tessa's return as evidence of reverence. Upland accounts gave greater attention to the beaten custodian and the ruler's continued occupation. Both histories agreed that the object killed the general who tried to convert shelter into conquest.

## Present significance

Copies of the Crown appear above hospices and mountain shelters, although none has demonstrated a response. The original's location in 1360 remains known to a small custodial community. Its members admit people in danger and keep the object away from imperial and ecclesiastical testing.

A response proves only that the Crown's shelter conditions have been met. It authenticates no wider government, religion or military cause.
