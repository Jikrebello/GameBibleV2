# Lethren Ostar

Lethren Ostar (442–381 BR) was an Atherian financier and commander whose wealth sustained an influential partnership with Marovan Veyr and Teren Casvilar. His fatal expedition into the northern well country weakened that partnership and left surviving families with debts and captives which victory could no longer redeem.

## Wealth and public influence

Ostar's family had acquired property during the confiscations associated with Vespen Harod's seizure of Caleran in 448 BR. Born six years later, Ostar inherited the advantages of those purchases and expanded them through lending, supply and the acquisition of distressed estates.

His business required attention to harvests, transport and the reliability of people acting at a distance. He could recognise a capable manager and help a household recover when repayment seemed possible. He also enforced claims which transferred farms, workshops and dependants into the hands of creditors.

Ostar maintained a large receiving household where petitioners could seek money, recommendations or a place for a younger relative. Such assistance earned loyalty and provided information about the affairs of the city. He wanted military distinction commensurate with this civil influence.

## The suppression of 399 BR

A revolt among enslaved workers and fugitives threatened estates and military supplies in 399 BR. Ostar led its suppression. After defeating the principal force, he ordered captured rebels crucified along a supply road.

The executions discouraged immediate resistance and made his name inseparable from the punishment. Owners celebrated recovered property. Families searching for fugitives encountered bodies exposed where travellers had to pass them. Some survivors escaped into other districts, carrying accounts that differed sharply from the public celebration.

Ostar's command increased his standing without giving him the extensive overseas achievements associated with Veyr. He remained sensitive to the difference.

## The first partnership

Ostar supplied money and political support to Casvilar while cooperating with Veyr over appointments and provincial interests. Their partnership operated through personal agreement, marriage and influence in public bodies. It held no collective governing office.

Casvilar's southern command gave his creditor a useful ally and an increasingly independent competitor. Ostar sought a foreign victory of his own. Northern trade and the wealth moving between wells made the approaches beyond established Atherian control attractive to him.

His son Pel accompanied the expedition. Born in 407 BR, Pel had grown up expecting a public career supported by his family's means. Their shared ambition placed two generations of the household in the same vulnerable army.

## The disaster at Rethmar

In 381 BR Ostar advanced towards Rethmar, a winter gathering where Ahdari camel circuits and Tarkhan well communities met. Permanent gardeners, smiths and water keepers lived around its reservoirs. Visiting households occupied seasonal compounds and attended its market. Ostar expected local leaders to provide water, transport and passage for further operations.

Kharuz of the Stone Wells organised resistance among settlements whose livelihoods depended on controlling access to stored water. Tavisha, a keeper involved in distributing water between households and visiting caravans, helped move supplies away from the line of advance. Their followers used camels, foot archers and knowledge of the intervals between wells. These societies preceded the mounted Tarkhan formations of later centuries.

The Atherian columns became separated while trying to secure water and bring their baggage forward. Local forces attacked exposed parties and prevented them from rejoining. Pel died before his father. Ostar's attempt to recover the army through negotiation ended in his own death amid the collapse of the command.

Some soldiers escaped; others were killed or taken captive. Fever entered the northern camp before ransom could be arranged. Tavisha ordered sick captives and non-combatant water workers released with enough water to reach a southern station. Kharuz exchanged healthy officers for weapons and animals. Captives whose families sent no payment were exchanged, admitted to local households or enslaved.

The coalition dispersed after securing Rethmar. Tavisha's authority governed the reservoirs, while Kharuz commanded people who had followed him to war. Neither founded a northern kingdom. The loss damaged lenders and suppliers at home as well as military households. Northern communities remembered the preservation of their wells and the relatives lost in achieving it.

## Consequences

Ostar's death removed a wealthy intermediary from the relationship between Veyr and Casvilar. His resources passed to heirs and claimants with different loyalties. The surviving partners could no longer rely on him to finance agreement or restrain a dispute which threatened all three.

Later accounts used the expedition to illustrate greed. Its failure also depended on a practical misjudgement: Ostar treated local water and cooperation as resources that military arrival would make available.
