# Lethren Ostar

Lethren Ostar (446–c. 374 BR) was an Atherian financier whose wealth sustained an influential partnership with Marovan Veyr and Teren Casvilar. He never held a command. He sold the survivors of a slave rising north in lots, sent his son to take possession of desert wells he believed he had bought, and paid for Casvilar's southern war in return for its captives. In 379 the commander he had financed had him entered as a contestant on the Red Field. He died a fugitive at Vorthara, the refuge town whose doors his own agents had once tried to buy open.

## Wealth and public influence

The Ostars made their money on Caleran's muster ground. For the twenty-five years that the Warden Vespen Harod lived there in a tent, Ostar's grandfather sold bread, lamp oil and credit in the town of sutlers that grew up around it. Senators who waded out to see the Warden borrowed from him to keep their clients fed while they waited. When Harod died around 441 and the camp was cleared, the family held notes against half the senate. Ostar was born five years before the tent came down. He inherited those notes and enlarged them through lending, supply and the acquisition of distressed estates.

His business required attention to harvests, transport and the reliability of people acting at a distance. He could recognise a capable manager and help a household recover when repayment seemed possible. He also enforced claims which transferred farms, workshops and dependants into the hands of creditors.

Ostar maintained a large receiving household where petitioners could seek money, recommendations or a place for a younger relative. Such assistance earned loyalty and provided information about the affairs of the city. He believed that anything a commander took with an army could be had more cheaply with a contract.

## The corridor rising of 410 BR

In 410 BR enslaved workers on the estates along the corridor that supplied Marovan Veyr's eastern war rose, joined by fugitives from the road gangs, and burned the stores they had been made to fill. Several thousand of them set out for the mountain pass to Vorthara, the refuge town in and around the Black Spire, where no city magistrate could be made to hand anyone back. Most of the estates were mortgaged to Ostar. He raised no army. He lent the owners the money to hire companies, and the companies caught the rear of the column at the foot of the pass and broke it.

Ostar settled the affair as a financier would. He took the captives from the owners in payment of their debts, had them chained in lots of twenty and sold them north to the dealers of the well country, who paid in silver and in promises of water and passage on the northern roads. Owners who had lost workers were credited from the proceeds; the families of the sold never learned which well had bought them.

The head of the column reached Vorthara. Ostar's agents followed it up the road in what Vortharans remembered as **the Spire Pursuit**. They did not storm the town. They bought: fugitives taken by Vortharan captive traders were purchased out of the pens and marched to the Olveth ore workings. Several refuge households barred their doors to the buyers and swore to defend one another's thresholds, an agreement later called **the Kept Doors**. It saved the people behind those doors and no others.

The sales made Ostar's name hated among the enslaved and respected among owners. They also gave him friends among the northern well dealers, knowledge of the roads between the wells, and a belief that the north could be bought.

## The first partnership

Between 396 and 391 BR Ostar supplied money and political support to Casvilar while cooperating with Veyr over appointments and provincial interests. Their partnership operated through personal agreement, marriage and influence in public bodies. Veyr wanted his eastern settlements confirmed, and Casvilar wanted a southern command. Ostar wanted the senate's seal on a purchase.

The dealers who had bought his rebels had since sold him something larger: shares in the reservoirs of Rethmar and rights of passage on the roads beyond it. He kept the deeds in Caleran. In the well country water belonged to the keepers who maintained it and the households who drank from it, and no dealer had a share to sell. Ostar either did not know this or believed that a deed with enough soldiers behind it would serve.

His son Pel, born in 418 BR, had grown up expecting a public career supported by his family's means. Ostar gave him the venture to command.

## The disaster at Rethmar

In 392 BR Pel Ostar marched towards Rethmar, a winter gathering where Ahdari camel circuits and Tarkhan well communities met. Permanent gardeners, smiths and water keepers lived around its reservoirs. Visiting households occupied seasonal compounds and attended its market. Pel brought hired companies, a train of surveyors and bailiffs, and his father's deeds, and he expected local leaders to provide water, transport and passage on the strength of them. His father stayed in Caleran.

Kharuz of the Stone Wells organised resistance among settlements whose livelihoods depended on controlling access to stored water. Tavisha, a keeper involved in distributing water between households and visiting caravans, moved the stores away from the line of advance, drained the cisterns the deeds described and marked false camps with empty skins. Their followers used camels, foot archers and knowledge of the intervals between wells.

The Atherian columns became separated while trying to secure water and bring their baggage forward. Local forces attacked exposed parties and prevented them from rejoining. Most of the soldiers died between dry wells or were captured. Pel was taken alive beside an empty cistern with the deeds in his baggage.

Fever entered the northern camp before ransom could be arranged. Tavisha ordered sick captives and non-combatant water workers released with enough water to reach a southern station. Kharuz exchanged healthy officers for weapons and animals. Captives whose families sent no payment were exchanged, admitted to local households or enslaved.

For the financier's son Kharuz asked a thousand pack animals and the deeds burned before witnesses in Caleran's forum. Ostar offered the animals and kept the deeds, which were security for half the credit he had left. The bargaining lasted four years. By the time he gave way, Pel had been taken into a water keeper's household, was keeping its tallies and had a child there. He sent the animals back with a reckoning of what the house of Ostar owed the wells of Rethmar. Ostar did not speak of him in public again.

The coalition dispersed after securing Rethmar. Tavisha's authority governed the reservoirs, while Kharuz commanded people who had followed him to war. The loss damaged lenders and suppliers at home as well as military households. Northern communities remembered the preservation of their wells and the relatives lost in achieving it.

A Rethmar tradition holds that some of the water carriers who emptied the cisterns ahead of the columns were rebels Ostar had sold north eighteen years before. The well households tell it with relish. Ahdari genealogists answer that the carriers named in the story belong to known local families, and Atherian writers left it out of every account.

## The banker of the southern war

Rethmar cost Ostar an army's wages and his heir. He looked to Casvilar to make good the money. From 390 the loans that paid for the southern command were repaid each autumn in captives, whom Ostar's agents sold to estates and to the Olveth workings. For eleven years the arrangement suited both men.

It ended with the war. In 379 Casvilar's veterans came home wanting land, no more captives followed them, and Ostar called in what he was owed. He summoned Casvilar before the city's court for debt. Casvilar had been Warden of the Red Field since the siege of Kellovar, and his augurs ruled that a man who summoned a Warden from his field had set himself against it. They entered Ostar's name as a contestant. Under the rite he had now to come down to the muster ground armed or be held to have fled the field. He was sixty-seven and had never worn a sword.

He left Caleran at night with two servants and took the road up the pass to Vorthara. The camp declared him fled. His ledgers were carried to the Warden's tent, where Casvilar burned the pages that named himself, his officers and several thousand of the city's small debtors. The rest of the fortune passed to the Field and to claimants with different loyalties.

## Vorthara

Ostar lived about five more years in rented rooms above Vorthara's ascent market, among captive dealers who had once sold to his agents. Caleran's wits said the Kept Doors stayed shut against him. Vortharan households tell it otherwise. In their account one of the sworn doors took him in during his last winter, because the oath had no clause about deserving. He died there about 374.

His flight removed the one man both commanders owed, and it showed the senatorial houses what a Warden's register could do to a rich man. Later accounts used his life to illustrate greed. His failures also came from a practical misjudgement learned in 410: having once bought the north's goodwill, he believed its water, its people and at last his own safety could be had the same way.
