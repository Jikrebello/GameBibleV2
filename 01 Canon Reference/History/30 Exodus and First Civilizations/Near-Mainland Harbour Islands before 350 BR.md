# Near-Mainland Harbour Islands before 350 BR

Near-mainland harbour islands were Thalassian communities whose lives extended across narrow channels. An island household might keep a landing on one shore, fields or kin on the facing mainland, and graves or sacred obligations on both. Contact resumed soon after the Giant collapse. By 350 BR these islands were among the most contested places of the Elmaric-connected sea.

Fishing, seasonal cultivation, refuge and marriage joined island and mainland residents. Related Elmaric people, older shore communities, Folk-linked kindreds and Glass-descended settlers mixed differently from one crossing to another.

Grain, timber, customs and pilotage made the channels attractive to outside rulers. Some island communities bought food under treaty, while others held fortified grain ports or farms on land acquired by grant or force. Existing cultivators, settlers and enslaved workers could all work within a claimed holding. A blockade might cut residents off from fields and relatives they could see across the water. Claims to provide protection or to restore an ancient right often concealed a more immediate demand for stores and vessels.

Gateway-facing islands met Caleran and the Gateway successor world; eastern channels met Delkosi, Vashari and Atherian-speaking ports. A blockade there was no figure of speech. When Caleran closed Tammur to the Gateway's festival grain about 666 BR, channel families watched their own barley stand unharvested across water they were forbidden to cross. After 491, when the League of the Eastern Oar fixed its ship money, boats from islands behind on their payments were the first turned away from a closed chain in bad weather.
