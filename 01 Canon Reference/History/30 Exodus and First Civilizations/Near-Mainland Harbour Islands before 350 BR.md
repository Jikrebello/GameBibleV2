# Near-Mainland Harbour Islands before 350 BR

Near-mainland harbour islands were Thalassian communities whose lives extended across narrow channels. An island household might keep a landing on one shore, fields or kin on the facing mainland, and graves or sacred obligations on both. Contact resumed soon after the Giant collapse; later foreign forts and colonial charters did not begin their history. By 350 BR these islands were among the most contested places of the Elmaric-connected sea.

Fishing, seasonal cultivation, refuge and marriage joined island and mainland residents. Related Elmaric people, older shore communities, Folk-linked kindreds and Glass-descended settlers mixed differently from one crossing to another. A single fort or harbour office could not turn every inland farm or opposite-shore family into its subjects. Conversely, an island council could not assume authority over a mainland field merely because its residents used that field.

Grain, timber, customs and pilotage made the channels attractive to outside rulers. Some island communities bought food under treaty, while others held fortified grain ports or farms on land acquired by grant or force. Existing cultivators, settlers and enslaved workers could all work within a claimed holding. A blockade might cut residents off from fields and relatives they could see across the water. Claims to provide protection or to restore an ancient right often concealed a more immediate demand for stores and vessels.

Gateway-facing islands met Caleran and the Gateway successor world; eastern channels met Delkosi, Vashari and Atherian-speaking ports. These theatres did not follow one colonial timetable. By the opening of the imperial age, individual island–shore pairs still had to be understood through their own landings, households and rival claims rather than a blanket label of Elmaric possession or foreign dependency.
