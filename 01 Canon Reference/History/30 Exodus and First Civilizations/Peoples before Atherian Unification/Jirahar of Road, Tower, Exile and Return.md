# Jirahar of Road, Tower, Exile and Return

The ancient Jirahar developed from the Near-Glass camp continuum after the southern departure and the later northern fission. Their earliest communities named households, occupations, roads and towers before **Jirahar** became the broad communal name. Shared worship, mutual obligations and recognised admission joined settlements which possessed no common government.

## Road houses and towers

A road house consisted of related and adopted households which maintained a stage of travel, common stores and obligations towards guests and neighbouring houses. Some moved seasonally; others occupied permanent buildings beside a well, ford or pass. Tower communities combined refuge, storage, worship and signalling. A tower's authority depended on the people willing to provision and defend it.

Belonging passed through acknowledged descent and could also be acquired through public conversion and admission to a household. Marriage connected roads and occupations. Adoption incorporated displaced children and adults. A convert accepted communal worship and duties before recognised witnesses. Old families often possessed influence, though descent did not create a closed biological caste.

Occupational houses carried skills across the network. Judges travelled between disputes. Provisioners measured grain and protected pack animals. Scribes and name keepers recorded households separated by travel or violence. Smiths, herders, healers and water workers served several camps. Their different responsibilities prevented any single office from inheriting the Prophet's authority.

## Qiryathi civic belonging

Qiryath grew from a refuge, cistern, market and sanctuary into the largest Jirahar city. A **Qiryathi** could be a city resident, member of a recognised neighbourhood, sanctuary household or descendant of a family displaced from the city. These relationships carried different rights.

The city's councils regulated water, markets and defence. Village suppliers, road houses and foreign merchants sustained its population while retaining their own affiliations. Qiryath became a great centre of teaching and pilgrimage. It never encompassed the entire Jirahar people.

The Arqeshite destruction of First Qiryath in 742 BR killed inhabitants, broke the sanctuary and deported selected families. Those who survived in neighbouring villages, escaped by road or remained in foreign quarters preserved different versions of Qiryathi life. Claims to the lost city's property and authority became matters of conflict during the return.

## River exile

Arqesh distributed deportees among river cities, agricultural settlements, workshops, court households and canal gangs. Some skilled families received rations and limited property while remaining unable to leave. Others were sold, separated or assigned to lethal labour.

Exiles learned Arqeshite language, music, masonry and water administration. They formed burial fraternities, schools and trading partnerships. Service in an Arqeshite office did not itself erase Jirahar identity. A descendant could remain within Jirahar communal life, enter an Arqeshite household or hold both relationships. These were personal and household outcomes rather than an automatic result of deportation.

## Return and Second Qiryath

The fall of Arqesh allowed displaced families to return under the Vasharan Decree of Roads and Houses. Returners encountered people who had cultivated disputed land for generations. Marriages between survivors and exiles created households whose members possessed competing claims and memories.

Second Qiryath admitted families through instruction, acceptance of communal duties and public recognition. Severe restoration factions opposed spouses and children associated with Arqeshite settlers. The settlement reached around the rebuilt sanctuary preserved a living community at the cost of arguments that continued across generations.

Many Jirahar did not return. River families, western towers, sea companies and travelling houses remained part of the same dispersed people. By 350 BR Qiryath stood under Vasharan supremacy while Jirahar communities extended from the Glassward roads to Arqeshite cities and Atherian ports.
