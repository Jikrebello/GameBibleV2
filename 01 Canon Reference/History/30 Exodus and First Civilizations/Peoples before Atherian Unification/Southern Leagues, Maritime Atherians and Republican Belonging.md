# Southern Leagues, Maritime Atherians and Republican Belonging

Atherian identity developed over many centuries. It began among households which left the region of the Great Glass Desert, widened into a maritime language and culture, and acquired civic and political meanings as cities and the Caleran Republic grew. By 350 BR a person could be Atherian by upbringing and speech, by citizenship in an Atherian city, or by formal membership in the Caleran political system. These forms of belonging often coincided, though none required a single ancestry.

## The outward households, c. 2130–1900 BR

The southern migrants retained the language, worship, measures and memories of the One Glass People. Their separation followed the Prophet's death and developed through decades of argument about leadership, permanent settlement and the world beyond the refuge. No successor inherited the Prophet's commission.

The migrants did not possess a common national name. A travelling league usually named its captain, remembered camp, occupation or intended destination. Later accounts translate several of these expressions as **outward households** or **southern leagues**. The terms describe a family of migratory communities rather than a single government.

Membership rested on an acknowledged place within a household and participation in the movement. Marriage, adoption and attachment to a captain could admit newcomers. Local allies sometimes joined through sworn agreements. Captives and enslaved inhabitants also travelled with the leagues, worked their fields and defended their camps while remaining denied equal standing.

Commanders organised defence. Provisioners guarded the common stores. Craftspeople kept carts, tools and weapons serviceable. Teachers preserved the sacred account of liberation. Settlement altered these responsibilities. A store assembled for a journey became a granary controlling a neighbourhood's food; a temporary fort claimed fields; a captain who had led willing followers became a landlord commanding tenants and slaves. The resulting disputes helped divide the leagues into garrison households, river communities and harbour settlements.

## Maritime Atherians, c. 1900–1400 BR

The name **Atherian** acquired broad cultural use among the ship-owning households, harbour residents and armed fleets of the southern and eastern coasts. People still identified themselves by harbour, island and household in ordinary life. Foreign merchants used the wider name for speakers who shared voyage customs, stories and descent traditions but possessed no common state.

A large maritime household might own a vessel, maintain relatives in a store town, marry into a timber district and employ dependants drawn from several shores. Captains traded, escorted merchants, raided rivals and founded settlements. Their followers expected food and proceeds to be divided according to recognised custom. A captain who concealed cargo or abandoned a damaged ship could lose the loyalty on which his authority depended.

These communities were extensively mixed. Orphaned cultivators, pilots and craftspeople entered through alliance, coercion, marriage and adoption. Captives rowed ships and worked yards. Freed households raised children in Atherian speech while preserving memories of another people. Elite genealogies celebrated heroic southern captains and frequently concealed the enslaved workers and local spouses who had built the same houses.

Maritime religion preserved worship of the Lord and memory of the Prophet while absorbing the experiences of particular shores. Voyagers bargained with local powers, attended inland festivals and applied familiar names to manifestations encountered at sea. Shared stories connected distant ports; they did not make every local power one supernatural person.

## City peoples, c. 1400–800 BR

Growing cities made residence and public duty more formal. Citizens received rights to assemblies, landholding, protection and recognised cults according to local law. Free residents, protected foreigners, subject villagers, Compact allies, freed people and enslaved inhabitants held different positions. Birth within the walls did not always confer citizenship, while a wealthy foreign household could sometimes obtain it by grant.

People normally placed civic identity before the broad cultural name. A Caleran, Ortheian, Pyrassan or Irenai citizen belonged to a particular treasury, army and public cult. Shared Atherian speech made negotiation possible and supported common festivals, but it did not merge these cities. The [[The Celestial Compact and the Caleran Republic|Celestial Compact]] joined twelve of them through stated obligations while leaving many Atherian-speaking communities outside its membership.

Cities admitted people through recognised parentage, adoption, marriage arrangements, manumission, military service and public grants. Each route carried different rights. Families celebrated admission because it secured inheritance and public standing. Neighbours contested privileges obtained through forged ancestry, patronage or wartime favour.

## Caleran republican identity, c. 800–350 BR

Caleran citizenship became the most influential political form within the Atherian world. It remained distinct from Atherian culture. A Compact ally supplied ships or soldiers under treaty. A subject town paid revenue and obeyed a governor. A resident foreigner could trade and raise children in Caleran without voting or holding public land. Military dependants received protection through a commander and could lose it when his faction fell.

Admission widened as the republic required soldiers, taxpayers and loyal settlements. Manumitted households, provincial elites and veterans entered the citizen body unevenly. Other communities served for generations without receiving comparable rights. Caleran magistrates used the word Atherian for peoples governed by the republic, which encouraged later writers to mistake political control for common descent.

By 350 BR the term possessed three established meanings: a broad language and culture, affiliation with an Atherian city, and inclusion within Caleran's political order. An Enathi river family could possess Caleran citizenship and worship under the Common Sky while maintaining its own kinship and local customs. An Ortheian could speak Atherian and serve a kingdom hostile to Caleran. A freed household could become culturally and civically Atherian without claiming Glass ancestry. An enslaved Atherian speaker remained excluded by an Atherian owner.

This flexibility helped Atherian institutions spread. Its inequalities determined who received land, office and protection from that expansion.
