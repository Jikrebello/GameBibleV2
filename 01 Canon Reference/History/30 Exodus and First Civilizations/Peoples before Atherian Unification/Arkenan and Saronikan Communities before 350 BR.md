# Arkenan and Saronikan Communities before 350 BR

The ports and islands now grouped under the names **Arkena** and **Saronika** formed an ancient maritime region between the Atherian west and the eastern seas. These regional names are useful to modern readers. Their early inhabitants ordinarily named a city, island or harbour: Verion, Pyrassa, Irenai, Delkos, Kyrame, Naverion, Erenost or one of the smaller settlements dependent upon them.

The region contained older Orphaned shore communities, maritime Atherian settlers, freed captives, enslaved workers and families drawn by craft and trade. Their descendants formed durable mixed populations in several ports. They shared Atherian speech to varying degrees, though local languages remained in households and working districts for generations.

## Harbour and household

Public belonging centred on a city. An Irenai teacher, a Pyrassan store owner and an Erenost shipwright could recognise related speech and voyage customs while owing service to different councils. Citizenship governed land, harbour dues, military duty and protection abroad. Residence allowed work and worship without necessarily granting those rights.

Island households depended on cisterns, terraces and imported grain. Shipbuilding tied them to timber coasts and metal districts beyond their immediate waters. Marriage joined a ship-owning family to growers, rope-makers or storekeepers. Manumitted sailors could enter a harbour household, and several ports admitted veteran crews when they needed defenders. Enslaved rowers and yard workers remained essential to maritime wealth while being denied its honours.

Each port maintained its own public festivals and household rites. Atherian-speaking settlers carried worship associated with the Lord, the Prophet and the Common Sky, while older shore families preserved local observances. Civic patrons combined these traditions in different ways. Related divine names did not make the powers encountered at every harbour one being.

Material specialisation gave each city a character remembered in rival jokes and festival boasts. One kept unusually dependable famine stores; another trained pilots; another repaired vessels so quickly that competitors accused its shipwrights of bribing the tide. These reputations shaped marriage and patronage as surely as public decrees.

## Kings, fleets and changing service

Valeron's hegemony drew western cities into tribute and naval service. Eryndor III's eastern expedition then placed Ortheian garrisons and allied fleets across the region. After his death, the Delkosi Successor joined local island households with Ortheian, Arqeshite, Vashari and other military settlers.

A single person could therefore serve several governments within one lifetime. A harbour citizen might sail for a local assembly, carry supplies for an upland king, accept employment from a Delkosi commander and negotiate with a Caleran merchant. Such service altered wealth and allegiance. It did not automatically erase a person's city of origin or bestow Caleran citizenship.

Marriage and military settlement nevertheless created new households. Their children inherited several languages, burial customs and political memories. The name **Delkosi** widened beyond the city itself among families associated with its fleet and court, although many islanders retained older civic names.

## Position in 350 BR

By 350 BR the Arkenan and Saronikan ports belonged broadly to the Atherian cultural world and were deeply entangled with Caleran power. They remained politically diverse. Local citizenship continued to matter more than a regional label in taxation, inheritance and military command.

The same ports connected Caleran to Ortheia, the Delkosi Successor, Tasar, Ilyessa and the Vasharan Empire. Their pilots, interpreters and storehouses made them valuable to every claimant to the eastern sea. Their divided loyalties also ensured that no western government could treat them as a single inherited possession.
