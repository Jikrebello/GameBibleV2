# Vathra and the Great Unyoking

**Vathra** was a confederated Giant kingdom whose rulers carried their courts through the Material sky. Its crown-cities travelled above the northern dry belt during the last centuries of Giant rule. Human subjects maintained the wells, anchor towers, herds, workshops and roads that allowed those cities to move. The War of Altitude and the human-led Great Unyoking destroyed the kingdom between approximately 2300 and 2260 BR.

Vathra ranked among the important lesser crowns of the Giant world. It never formed a sixth Imperial Horizon. Its rulers traded with several horizons, married into other Giant houses and supplied cavalry, beasts, captives and mercenaries to neighbouring powers.

## The First Road-Mother

Vathran royal genealogy began with the **First Road-Mother**, a Titan-Firstborn conceived through a bounded manifestation of Hecatara and a human woman. Hecatara did not enter the conception in her full sovereignty. The Firstborn possessed Titan continuity rather than a human soul.

The oldest accounts credited the Road-Mother with opening a seasonal sky route, moving a royal herd during famine and defeating a Giant ruler of the western wells. The first two deeds preserve recognisable route effects. Several dynasties claimed the third under incompatible names. Her personal name and final fate are unknown.

Later kings turned her achievement into a doctrine of royal ownership. They taxed roads they had not built, demanded children from communities outside their protection and treated escape as theft from the crown. A route opening under its proper conditions gave them practical power; it supplied no moral title to land or people.

## The aerial economy

Vathran government travelled between subject districts. A crown-city descended at a prepared field, received tribute and hostages, settled disputes among client houses and departed before the surrounding country exhausted its stores. Smaller estates rose for a season or campaign. Stripped war-platforms carried soldiers, prisons and stone-dropping weapons.

Four systems sustained city flight.

- **Lift bodies** of hollow stone, Giant bone, timber and metal distributed the weight of the city.
- **Pressure mantles** of plant fibre, hair, metal thread and treated membrane caught an atmospheric pressure associated with Zephyraxis.
- **Route anchors** joined the city to marked fields, towers and mounds through Hecatara correspondences.
- **Human operators** read weather, tied the mantles, moved ballast, loaded water and repaired the foundations.

Repeated travel altered the country below. A road lengthened, a stream shifted or a familiar approach returned a traveller to the place from which they had set out. Astaroth-related structural knowledge helped builders identify stress within the lift bodies. These several supernatural relationships remained distinct.

Sky-carpets used the same system at a smaller scale. Courier cloths carried messengers. Tribute carpets moved goods and animals. War carpets carried raiders between platforms. Away from a prepared current, most became heavy textiles. A damaged carpet might retain its former destination and carry a rider towards a prison or ruined anchor.

## Rank and government

Vathran dynasties described themselves through storm, cloud and tether ranks. These were political and magical cultures within one Giant genealogy, not separate Giant species.

The **storm houses** occupied the highest cities and controlled the route calendar. Their courts cultivated weather sight, long hearing and lightning craft. The **cloud houses** governed tribute, diplomacy and most commerce from lower routes. **Tether houses** controlled landing fields, wells and pasture. Some tether houses were Giant dynasties; others were giant-blooded or ordinary human families elevated through service.

Human sky households contained pilots, weavers, animal handlers, healers, cooks, gardeners, water carriers, scribes and builders. Some held hereditary privileges. Many were captives or hereditary dependants. Their technical knowledge gave them influence and made coordinated revolt possible.

Vathran life included skill and pleasure alongside coercion. Gardeners raised herbs and fruit in protected courts. Children raced weighted cloth toys along safe currents. Musicians timed lifting ceremonies so that workers across a landing field could coordinate by sound. Royal art presented these achievements as gifts from the kings.

## The crown-cities

### Urzavad

**Urzavad** was the largest crown-city and the winter seat of the high kings. Its upper palace, weather galleries and lightning towers stood above crowded human workshops and reservoirs. The House of Ozhur held storm rank and claimed the closest descent from the First Road-Mother.

The last high king, **Ozhur IV**, could maintain Urzavad above ordinary storm height for short periods. He inherited failing routes and increased requisitions for food, ballast and workers. His policies killed subject communities before the final war began.

### Dazhur

**Dazhur** controlled herd exchange, tribute storage and much of Vathra's commercial flight. Its cloud-ranked rulers displayed suspended gardens, coloured awnings and captured animals. **Varakesh of Dazhur**, a Dynastic Giant and cousin of Ozhur, believed the lower courts could preserve the kingdom by displacing the high house.

Varakesh concealed shortages, purchased tether-house loyalty and seized war-platforms. His court treated dependant districts as material which could be discarded whenever their weight threatened the city.

### Kel-Orun

**Kel-Orun** contained the largest pressure-mantle workshops and trained pilots for several courts. Its rulers held a disputed place between cloud and tether rank. Human weaving families possessed unusual influence there because their craft could not be replaced quickly.

**Kelvara**, a Dynastic Giant of a junior line, supervised the descent grounds. She served Ozhur during the early crisis and allowed captive workers to be transferred to Urzavad. She later turned against the high court after witnessing the people killed in royal reprisals.

### The three uncertain cities

**Sairat** carried Vathra's mobile archive, foster genealogies and route accounts. **Molqara** grew from a war-platform around armouries and prisons. **Yazuran** belonged to a cadet royal line and attempted an unknown passage during the final war.

Sairat entered a route after its destination anchor had been destroyed. Molqara lost its steering while part of its lift remained active. Yazuran disappeared without reaching any known Material landing. Later claims concerning their survival remain unverified.

## The War of Altitude

Familiar sky roads became costly and unreliable during the wider Closing-Court crisis. Ozhur demanded greater tribute and tighter control over the route calendar. Varakesh challenged him with cloud-house wealth and lower-route support. Their followers destroyed anchor towers, withheld rain from rival districts and used false beacons to steer cities into dangerous air.

War-platforms dropped burning wreckage onto towns. An attack on a pressure mantle killed people in the city and those living beneath its route. Ground communities prepared tribute for one court and lost their remaining stores when a rival arrived unexpectedly.

Varakesh committed the conflict's most notorious act. During an assault upon Dazhur, he ordered the lower ward released. Houses, prisoners, animals and workers fell while families remained inside them. The ward struck a tether settlement and killed inhabitants who had never entered the aerial city.

Ozhur answered by destroying western landing fields and executing their crews. At Tal-Kesh, route workers were killed and their bodies thrown from an anchored platform. His order to clear the entire town drew hesitant ground households into open revolt.

## The human conspiracy

**Seyra Vatan** belonged to a privileged Kel-Orun weaving house. Her father had died repairing a war-platform during lightning. Seyra knew the complete knot vocabulary and trained captive workers whom the court treated as replaceable hands.

**Dorun of Tal-Kesh** managed Urzavad's ground routes. The court had divided his children between Ozhur's and Varakesh's households as security for his obedience. He knew when anchors received instructions and where a false confirmation could delay a city.

**Meya of Narash** governed three western wells through a maternal caravan house. She had profited from Vathran protection and supplied captives taken in local wars. Royal seizure now threatened the breeding camels and dependants on which her authority rested.

Seyra distributed altered knot patterns. Dorun changed route notices. Meya concealed food and animals beyond the tribute fields. Enslaved armourers made mooring knives, while cooks and waste crews carried messages between human districts which royal officers rarely searched.

Their plan aimed to ground Kel-Orun, abandon selected anchors and release the human trains. Local rebels pursued additional aims. Some sought escape. Others wanted the property of collaborators or revenge against Giant households.

## The Great Unyoking

The first coordinated uprising followed Ozhur's order against Tal-Kesh. Drivers cut animal traces and opened the royal corrals. Families killed overseers, seized stores and abandoned four anchors in one night. Giant clients massacred people at the eastern pens. Rebels later murdered human servants and children belonging to several tether-house families.

Kelvara reached Kel-Orun with orders to lift it beyond the revolt. She opened the lower gates and gave Seyra control of the mantle house. Royal guards fought through human neighbourhoods while the city descended. Ground crews held the western moorings, knowing a broken cable would strike them first.

The descent split two foundation chambers and killed hundreds. Most inhabitants survived. Kel-Orun's reservoirs, metal ribs and lower streets became the centre of several post-Vathran settlements.

Urzavad met Dazhur's forces above Tal-Kesh. Ozhur tried to climb before his water and breathable corridors were ready. A damaged mantle tore across the eastern foundation. Urzavad struck a platform and fell, killing its Giant court, human population and thousands below.

Dorun disappeared during the last days. One account places him beneath the crash; another says he entered Dazhur seeking his younger child. Meya led displaced families towards the Narashi roads. Seyra survived Kel-Orun's descent and spent her later life training people to dismantle royal cloth safely.

## Consequences

Vathra ceased to exist as a state. The destruction of its distribution system brought famine to people whom the court had previously supplied. Several liberated camps died during the first winters. Surviving humans quarried the cities, rebuilt herds and organised government around wells, settlements and seasonal assemblies.

Royal horse lines disappeared because they had been concentrated in exposed estates. Camels survived among dispersed human households. Animal medicine, weaving, fosterage and route knowledge became foundations of later Tarkhan and well-country life.

Objects acquired new meanings. Pressure mantles became tents and burial covers. Tether cables became marriage ropes. Fallen foundation stones became well curbs. Northern children continued to play an unyoking game in which the smallest player won by releasing the team before the cart owner returned.

## Historical limits

The Great Unyoking formed one regional theatre of the Great Unbinding. Its rebels did not cause the collapse of the Five Imperial Horizons, and no human leader inherited the Great Prophet's commission. A commissioned heavenly act may have bound one Titan or ended one recurrence system during the wider judgement. Human workers overthrew Vathra through their own knowledge, alliances and violence.

The First Road-Mother's fate remains unknown. Later sightings of Sairat, Molqara, Dazhur or Yazuran prove no surviving dynasty. Vathran flight occurred within the Material sky and had no connection to Nythalum's physical lunar programme.
