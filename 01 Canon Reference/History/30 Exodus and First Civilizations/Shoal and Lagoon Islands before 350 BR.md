# Shoal and Lagoon Islands before 350 BR

The shoal and lagoon islands formed a low-lying part of the ancient [[Elmaric Island Peoples before 350 BR|Elmaric]] heartland. Their inhabitants worked fisheries, shallow approaches and tidal channels rather than the same ground as the higher [[Elmarai Isles before 350 BR|Elmarai Isles]]. These were inhabited homelands with their own households and landing rights, not hazards existing only on a foreign captain's chart. The individual islands and named passages remain to be fixed on a precise map.

Local island survivors repaired small craft, water stores and fishery places after the Great Unbinding. Low ground constrained cultivation and made stored fresh water and sheltered buildings particularly important. Fishing sustained many households, but grain and ship materials still came from other islands or facing coasts. A storm or damaged store could leave an apparently well-supplied pilot community dependent on a convoy it had once guided.

Knowledge of shallow water gave these islanders political weight. A pilot could lead a grain vessel into safety, allow a raiding crew through a difficult approach or deny a foreign war fleet passage by refusing service. The knowledge lived with households and experienced crews; it was neither a magical immunity to storms nor a single hereditary guild shared by every shoal. Kinship, debt and captivity could divide pilots on neighbouring islands. A ruler might try to control a channel by holding a landing or a pilot family while remaining unable to govern the whole shallow-water region.

By 350 BR the shoal islands linked Ilamar, Elmarai and Seravon-facing routes. Their people could join an Elmaric convoy or league without surrendering local authority. Larger harbours needed their skill and food routes, while the pilots needed grain, timber and a safe place to sell their catch. That interdependence made them central to the island world even when their settlements were smaller than the great ports.
