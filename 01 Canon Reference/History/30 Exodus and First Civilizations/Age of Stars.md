# Age of Stars

The **Age of Stars**, approximately 800–350 BR, is the period in which rival Atherian cities developed a shared language of civic law, astronomy, roads, military service and public religion. The name comes from the star cults that supplied common festivals, divine genealogies and symbols of office. Older city loyalties remained powerful, but rulers and travellers could increasingly describe the Atherian world as one civilisation.

The synthesis drew upon Giant astronomy, memories of the Exodus, local civic law and the experience of maritime exchange. Caleran's [[The Common Sky and the Formation of Astratheon|Common Sky settlement]] had already joined maritime and inland worship in royal ceremony around 1000–950 BR. The age opens around 800 BR, when twelve cities that already met at Caleran's festivals swore to keep its calendar, arbitrate their quarrels and muster together as the Celestial Compact. Republican patrons expanded the settlement by funding shared festivals, arranging local founders within the Family of Stars and translating civic calendars into a common reckoning.

## Making a civilisation out of rivals

Older cities kept their own founders, rivalries and ceremonies throughout the Age of Stars, while scholars married those founders into kinship stories and seated incompatible gods at one celestial table.

The synthesis brought practical advantages. Travellers could move farther, merchants compare measures and rulers recruit beyond one household. The same systems also enlarged state coercion. Standard measures became quotas, surveys marked settlements for removal and roads built to carry food also carried punitive columns.

## The cost of the measured world

The Aurin trunk road absorbed older local paths between Caleran, Enathi, Serathi and Vardeni country. Compact, Ortheian and republican governments successively extended and militarised it for commerce, assessment, messages and troops, using unequal civic standing to impose labour and seize land. [[G-08 - The Road That Refused Its Last Stone|The Road That Refused Its Last Stone]] remembers one mixed gang of coerced Aurin workers and Atherian debt labourers withholding the dedication from a prepared milestone. The repentant surveyor, child-built false milestones and graves beneath every paving stone are later moral compressions of wider harm.

Along another inland corridor, prisoners' songs tell how a summoning operation placed mortal captives at the boundaries of an infernal map. In their account a map-lord opened a local wound in distance to erase a rival's territory, and both sides then needed enslaved bodies to hold their contradictory routes. The prisoners destroyed the marks sustaining the fortifications and contained the local disaster, but the wound remained. [[G-64 - The Prisoners Who Became a Border|The Prisoners Who Became a Border]] preserves its nightmare.

## Eastern exile and return

In 742 BR, the Arqeshite River Empire destroyed the First Qiryath and deported leading households. In the siege's first winter the altar's sign had already been withheld: offerings smoked and the stones stayed hot. Exile made portable teaching, skilled service and remembered kin more important. Arqesh fell around 690 BR; return followed changing political opportunity. The Second Qiryath was consecrated around 660 BR, and the royal consecration offering smoked. The sign returned around 654, under an offering carried by mixed households of the rebuilt city.

Restoration did not restore every dead person or every lost field. Returnees found neighbours who had survived by accommodation and households whose claims depended on later rulers. The memory of exile therefore contains gratitude, accusation and arguments over what return should mean, arguments that Atherian histories, writing of the east as territory awaiting unification, left out.

## Ortheia and the eastern conquest

Valeron II of Ortheia imposed a royal hegemony upon several Compact cities between about 735 and 709 BR. In 709 his own soldiers killed him at a pay table when Marenthe's withheld silver left the count short. His son [[Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest|Eryndor III]] left those soldiers at home, where they sacked Marenthe in 699–698, and crossed the eastern inner sea in 702 with newer men. The campaign joined Arezan Tiravan's Vashari rising against Arqesh and the revolts already spreading through the canal provinces. In 690 BR the capital's own dredging gangs, their rations cut to feed the king's soldiers, opened the gates of Arqesh to both armies.

Eryndor died in 673 aboard ship in the roads of Nemeru, below the conquered capital, as he turned for home. The [[Wars of the Inheritance]] killed the half-brother acclaimed in his place, left his eighteen-year-old daughter Mehrava a disputed claim in Vasharan keeping and divided his army among Vashar, Ortheia, Delkos, selected Qahuri crowns and farther eastern states. Caleran recovered its assemblies and fleets from the weakening Ortheian command. The city fought the Gateway Wars with officers, ships and eastern relationships gained in that recovery.

## Caleran's sky

Caleran took the [[The Celestial Compact and the Caleran Republic|Celestial Compact]] by holding what its members could not do without. Around 680–672 it bought ships from Eryndor's unpaid western commanders while the king lay wounded in the east, landed its own unpaid sailors on the burned fields of Marenthe and seated them in the Compact as Marenthe, while the Marenthan exiles read out their lists of the dead and the sold. Around 666 it struck Verion's festival and contract days out of the common year. The Common Sky taught that an offering made on an unreckoned day went unseen, and Verion's sailors sat on the quays until the ship-owning houses expelled their tyrant and begged to be "restored to the sky". After 520, when the highland valley that kept the Compact's rolls was lost, Caleran's copies became the only rolls there were.

## Bread, bronze faces and the Red Field

The [[The Gateway Wars|Gateway Wars]], about 674–520 BR, were fought over bread. After three dry springs on the Aurin, Compact cities bought grain at Tammur from Qarzeth, which sold only to buyers who ate at its god's festival table; Pyrassan merchants came home with small bronze faces for their store doors. In the second war, about 612–604, the Gateway captain Tashqar Ordan fed wavering Atherian towns with gifts of grain, and the towns that ate it found their own sealed jars empty at the next count, with tiny bite marks in the wax. His own city disowned him, and its Council Houses delivered their children to Caleran as hostages on the appointed day in 611 to prove they wanted peace.

Tashqar would fight no battle, and two armies wasted away behind him. Caleran answered with a sacrifice. In 609, on the festival ground of the Common Sky, it killed captured Gateway soldiers and the Gateway hostage children in its keeping to give the Red Star a contestant. The killing brought Qarzeth into the war. The augurs concluded that the victims had been the wrong kind and created in their place the **Warden of the Red Field**, a living commander bound to fight in person and forbidden to quit the field while the enemy he had named still held it. The office outlived the war by two and a half centuries. Tashqar himself was killed in 604 at the store doors of Erenost by townspeople who had eaten his bread for eight years.

The same war broke a city's covenant with Heaven, in the telling of its survivors. In the inner gulf around 608, an allied city that kept a refuge gate and a register of its guests read the register as a list of Gateway agents. Its magistrates executed registered foreigners in the square, seized their grain and drove whole households out to the besiegers, and its clerks rewrote the gate's covenant so that the dead seemed to have broken guest-law first. Survivors said the lamps of the refuge gate then went out one by one without smoke, and the register in the gatehouse turned to blank parchment. Defenders on the wall swore that white-gold wheels within wheels, ringed with moving wings, had passed from gate station to gate station in a fixed order before the light withdrew, and walls never breached in living memory were breached. The city fell within the season. Survivors carried a true copy of the covenant out through the siege lines, and later cities copied the order of the stations into a rite for unhousing a sanctuary gate. Church writers called it a heavenly withdrawal; Star priests called it a failed machine of the Giants; the city's name is disputed, and [[G-38 - The Houses Unhoused before the Gate Turns|The Houses Unhoused before the Gate Turns]] keeps the procession of roof tiles its survivors began.

In the Gateway towns' telling, Qarzeth's god paid for its city's recoveries with its subject towns' food, and the Gateway Republic paid for its god with children. The [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth|Years of Three Collections]], 548–540, brought Caleran's final war. Qarzeth's assembly first surrendered the collectors' books on which the children's names were entered. The god's granary doors would not open without them, and the hungry city killed the magistrates who had given them up and began the lists again. The capital fell in 520. Carriers from the towns Caleran had spared salted its sanctuary with their own salt and sealed its end with a meal at Sarqen's landing at which no one was served by rank; their oath outlived both republics.

In the same years, Caleran's siege levies passed through a highland league of nine valleys, the league that kept the Compact's archive. One district, stripped of men and grain, killed its collectors, and the league destroyed it. Atherian annals preserve the legend of a campaign that cannot be placed and a valley left off the maps. Allied soldiers marching home from Qarzeth went into that valley and were not seen again.

## Noon shadows and closed chains

Caleran's augurs read the sky for the republic, and in its last centuries the sky answered other people. Vashar's attempt on the Atherian coast ended at Ilyessa in 493, when the grain in Kazren I's anchored fleet rotted in sound hulls and his subject crews rowed for Velanthe's refuge fire ([[The Eastern Sea Wars]]). In 488 the veterans of that war, led by the magistrate Eshren Damar, asked the Sun at Sarven's old sighting stones to show the boundaries of the public land, and for one noon hour every shadow in the lower Aurin pointed along the old lines. The augurs declared that the day had fallen in a month they were only now adding to the year. The veterans walked onto the land all the same, because every steward in the valley had seen his own wall's shadow. The harvest of that land rotted on the stalk that autumn, and in the hungry spring the great houses bought the plots back for bread. In 486 the senate gave the augurs sole right to seek or record a sign over public things. Damar kept his own plot and worked it bareheaded for thirty years.

In 466, during the allies' rebellion against the naval league's ship money, Pyrassa closed its harbour chain against rebel ships in a gale on Caleran's order. Its own grain ships were shut out with them, and sailors swore the old dragon of drowned Kelarion rose under the breakwater until Pyrassa's wardens cut the chain. After that night no harbour warden would raise a chain for Caleran, and the war ended in a bargain. The peace of 461 gave every harbour its own chain and made refuge law for any ship in distress. That same winter the senator Vespen Harod had accepted the Red Field against the rebel captains. They never came to fight him and lived on in Vasharan exile, so he lived in a tent on the muster ground for twenty-five years and died in it, still Warden. The [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic|last republican generations]] inherited his tent and his open field.

## Stars, rulers and false ancestors

Scholars gave older powers tidy family histories, and rulers used those histories to make civic rank look like the order of the sky. Households whose founder was demoted in the Family of Stars lost their place in the festival procession and paid for someone else's. The elegant public images painted over older stories of gods who mutilated, imprisoned and made war on their own parents. In one cycle a profaned holy object killed the man who carried it rather than serve the conquest its ruler wanted it for.

## Towards unification

Citizenship became something a man could inherit, earn by service, receive as a grant or have imposed on his town by incorporation. Roads, colonies and provincial service let Caleran absorb communities on a scale the old war-settlement houses never managed, and every grade of citizen knew exactly which grade sat above him.

The northern well country stayed beyond Caleran's reach. Arqeshite and Qahuri rulers had sponsored competing Tarkhan and Ahdari clients there, and their garrisons demanded guides, animals and hostages; the client courts fell apart after Arqesh fell. In 402 BR the Caleran financier [[Lethren Ostar]], who had sold rebels north to the well dealers and believed the north could be bought, sent his son with a hired army to take possession of Rethmar's reservoirs, which he had bought on paper. Kharuz of the Stone Wells and the Ahdari water keeper Tavisha destroyed the expedition by dividing its columns and emptying the cisterns it expected to seize. Their coalition dispersed after the victory, and the financier's son stayed among the wells.

By 350 BR the Caleran republic possessed extensive roads, overseas territories, veteran clients and commanders whose wealth depended upon provincial war. Teren Casvilar accepted the Red Field in 383 at the siege of the Folk hillfort of Kellovar. The chief he had named walked into a river ford and was never found, so the Field could not be closed, and Casvilar carried it for thirteen years, through a civil war and into personal rule at Caleran. He was killed on the muster ground in 370 by men who entered their names as his challengers and struck before the contest opened; the wars that followed ran until no challenger was left alive. These institutions and their quarrels supplied the power used by Aurelius Thalion during the founding of the [[Atherian Imperial Age]]. The older cities and eastern states kept histories reaching far beyond Caleran's authority.
