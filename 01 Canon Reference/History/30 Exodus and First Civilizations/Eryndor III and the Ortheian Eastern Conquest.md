# Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest

**Eryndor III of Ortheia**, commonly called the **Far-Road King**, was an upland Atherian ruler whose campaigns transformed the eastern world between 706 and 684 BR. He inherited a trained army, a disputed hegemony over the Celestial Compact and an unfinished war from his father, Valeron II. He destroyed the city of Olyssar when it rebelled, crossed the eastern inner sea, joined Arezan Tiravan against the Arqeshite River Empire and carried his army beyond the conquered capital. He died at Arqesh in 684 BR in the middle of a Qahuri funerary rite meant to keep him. For nearly thirty years afterwards a voice in his tomb at Delkos claimed to be the living king, and the rivals who divided his conquests fought partly over what it promised them.

The failure of Eryndor's dominion taught later Caleran writers that conquest and government were separate achievements.

## Ortheia and Valeron II

Ortheia lay in the colder mountain country between the older Atherian city world and the lands later associated with Thalmyria. Its valleys produced grain and fruit; its pastures supported horses and pack animals; its forests and mines supplied timber, iron and silver. Ortheian speech, marriage and sanctuary practice belonged to the broad Atherian world, although the kingdom retained its own royal houses, upland assemblies and military traditions.

Valeron II inherited a crown threatened by neighbouring valleys and stronger cities. He drew the sons and daughters of subordinate houses into his household, maintained a permanent infantry core around the seasonal levies and employed engineers and clerks trained in the city world. When harvest contributions failed, the silver of the upland mines paid the soldiers. Around 730 BR his smiths began cutting that silver into weighed pieces stamped with the royal mark, so that a company's pay could be counted into soldiers' hands at a camp table. These were the first struck coins of the known world. Marriage, bribery, hostage education and war extended Valeron's influence into the twelve-city Celestial Compact between about 735 and 706 BR.

His war had a grievance to stand on. After Arqesh destroyed First Qiryath in 742 BR, Arqeshite governors on the eastern coast seized Compact ships lying in their harbours and held the crews against ransom. Between about 740 and 730 they marched the Atherian merchant quarter of **Terys** inland to the shipyards and estates of the river cities. Wives and parents of the taken crews petitioned at Compact festivals for a generation. Valeron used their grievance to win the cities' mandate for a common eastern war.

The cities retained their councils and festivals under this arrangement. They supplied money, ships, grain and soldiers for the campaign, much of the money struck as Ortheian silver. Some joined because Valeron restrained their local wars and promised protection from Arqesh. Others paid because Ortheian troops stood outside their walls. Caleran tried to preserve the authority of its assembly while meeting the king's demands.

Valeron was assassinated in 706 BR before the eastern expedition began. The killers and their immediate purpose remain uncertain. Eryndor secured the crown against adult relatives and commanders who had served his father. He also inherited a permanent army paid from the eastern mandate. Why he kept the war is the oldest argument about him. Ortheian songs say he went east to bring home the deported families of Terys. Caleran writers say an army that had to be paid would otherwise have chosen another king. Olyssaran lists say the war was made so that the cities would go on paying. Vashari stories say Arezan invited him.

His first great test came at Olyssar, a Compact city that repudiated Ortheian authority during the succession.

## Olyssar, 705–704 BR

Olyssar expected other cities to follow its revolt. Eryndor isolated it before that coalition formed. His soldiers cut the approaches, defeated a relief force and entered after a prolonged assault. They killed defenders and civilians, enslaved many survivors and divided land among loyal households. Exiles carried the city's cult objects, workshop skills and grievance to Caleran and the coast.

The destruction secured the league behind Eryndor. It also exposed the terms of his authority. Cities later celebrated the eastern war as liberation from Arqesh while omitting the Atherian city destroyed to make the campaign possible. Olyssaran descendants preserved lists of the dead and sold, and refused to honour Eryndor at festivals established after his victories.

## Crossing the eastern sea

Eryndor sailed in 702 BR with Ortheian infantry and cavalry, Compact contingents, siege engineers and allied ships. The cities had accepted the war mandate under different degrees of pressure. Their contribution lists remained a source of argument for generations because they showed which councils gave willingly, which delayed and which supplied troops only after royal seizure of their grain.

The army defeated Arqeshite field forces along the western approaches. Coastal cities then made separate choices. Some opened their harbours; others changed sides after neighbouring defeats; several fought because Arqeshite hostages or local rivalries gave them no safe neutrality.

Terys, the harbour from which the merchant quarter had been marched away, barred Eryndor from its offshore sanctuary and fleet anchorage. Between 697 and 695 BR his engineers built a causeway from the mainland with stone, timber and rubble pulled from abandoned buildings. Divers and haulers worked under missiles from the walls. Several attendants drowned when a deep-water attacker struck the observation bell in which the king had gone down to inspect the foundations, an incident later sung as an undersea journey.

When Terys fell, Eryndor's army massacred inhabitants, enslaved survivors and took the fleet. Ortheian songs call the killing reprisal for the deported families. Terysian survivors said that the households which had profited from the seizures sailed away before the causeway closed, and that the soldiers killed the people who stayed. The causeway altered currents and shoreline settlement long after the siege. Terys supplied ships for the remaining campaign and graves for families scattered across the eastern ports.

## Qahur and the Tiravan alliance

Eryndor entered divided Qahur in 695 BR because his army required grain, river craft and guides. The Qahuri crowns responded separately. One recognised him with a local royal title and admitted him to a renowned oracle; another supported Arqesh; a third withheld commitment until the military result became clearer. Witnesses associated a sign during the journey with Father Sun but disagreed about its form. Eryndor encouraged the claim that the sign revealed exceptional ancestry. Among the Qahuri who joined his army was **Neresh of Qemaret**, an officer from the lower Basin who knew the embalming houses of his city.

In 694 BR he allied with **Arezan Tiravan**, leader of the Vashari coalition fighting Arqesh from the east. Arezan commanded highland houses, mounted troops and relationships with rebel governors and forced workers. Eryndor supplied the larger siege army, allied fleet and money. Their treaty treated them as independent kings and divided campaigning responsibilities and captured revenue between their commands.

Eryndor married **Parena Tiravan**, adult daughter of Arezan's sister Mithara. Parena acted between the two courts, received Vashari officers and Arqeshite petitioners, and protected parts of her household from rival commanders.

Arqesh fell in 690 BR through their converging campaigns and the action of people inside the empire. Nemeru crews carried troops across a closed channel. Forced workers opened gates. Provincial governors withheld boats and food. Arezan seized eastern roads and water stations while Eryndor pressed the capital with the fleet and principal siege force. Both armies killed inhabitants and plundered districts during the capture.

The last Ubar king, Kassur III, escaped with several sons and the portable royal images, the gilded likenesses before which subject rulers had bowed. A provincial commander killed him in 688 BR and brought Eryndor the body and the images together, expecting a reward. Eryndor executed the killers and gave Kassur a royal burial, presenting himself as lawful successor to the king he had pursued. He kept the images. They were carried before him in procession and set up in his audience hall, and Arqeshite courtiers said that the presence they called the **Crowned Countenance** had followed them in. Petitioners spoke of a weight in the air when they bowed and of dreams in which the new king stood taller than his statues. Eryndor retained Arqeshite secretaries and court dress and accepted eastern officers.

## The farther roads

Eryndor campaigned east of Arqesh against fortresses and mobile opponents who had survived the fall of the capital. Hostage marriages, new garrisons, executions and burned villages accompanied the advance. The mixed court drew in Arqeshite officials, Jirahar interpreters, Vashari officers, Qahuri specialists and eastern nobles. Veterans expected the captured treasury and provincial commands as their reward.

Several marvels of these years entered romance: a reconnaissance flight on a frame drawn by Sky-Lions whose descent killed attendants, a grove of Root-Minds that named the place of the king's death, a pass sealed against a predatory host with prisoners and villages left on the far side, and a search for a restorative seep from which one traveller never returned. Singers later joined them, with the bell at Terys, into one voyage.

The army refused a farther march in 685 BR. Its veterans were exhausted, dependants had died along the supply roads and eastern recruits had no wish to carry the king beyond their own countries. No sign or royal speech broke the refusal. Eryndor began the return to Arqesh.

His personal rule also carried intimate bloodshed, and Kassur's images stood at the centre of it. Eryndor required his Ortheian officers to bow before the likenesses as Arqeshite governors had done. Veterans of Valeron's army called it kneeling to a dead enemy's furniture. An experienced officer who had served his father refused before the assembled army and told his men to stay on their feet; Eryndor had him condemned and executed. Later, at a feast in the palace, a companion who had once saved the king's life stood drunk before the images, mocked the bowing and would not bend. Eryndor killed him with his own hands. His remorse changed later behaviour and court ritual: Ortheians were afterwards excused the bow, though eastern subjects still made it. Arqeshite courtiers said that the Countenance had been insulted and the king had answered for it.

## Death and inheritance

Eryndor came back to Arqesh a sick man, with the seep lost and the grove's sentence travelling with the army. There he consented to the [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]], the Qahuri discipline that promised a disciplined founder a household beyond death. Neresh of Qemaret brought masters of the rite from the Basin, and **Serevan of Delkos**, commander of the allied fleet, paid for the gold, salts and resins of the regimen. A founder normally prepared for years. Eryndor had months.

He died at Arqesh in 684 BR while preparing another campaign and a more permanent government. The masters performed the final rite at his deathbed. What killed him was argued at once. Ortheian officers blamed the mineral draughts of the regimen; Vashari stories blamed the grove's sentence; camp rumour named fever, bad water and commanders who wanted the treasury. Parena was pregnant. An adult half-brother and her posthumous child served as nominal heirs, but neither controlled the army, treasury or provincial appointments.

The first settlement divided commands while retaining the language of one monarchy. Arezan secured Vashar, the eastern roads and much of the Ubar basin. **Demeran Kavos** took Ortheia and the western army. Serevan held islands, ships and eastern harbours. Neresh controlled selected Qahuri crown towns through garrisons and marriage.

Serevan also took the king. He diverted the embalmed body and Kassur's images from the road west to Delkos, and within months veterans admitted to the tomb-hall came out saying Eryndor had spoken to them from behind the curtain. The voice used his accent, called old soldiers by their camp names and declared that the king had not died and had no heir. A reigning king needed no half-brother and no infant; once Delkos proclaimed him alive, Demeran's royal hostages were worth nothing to him, and he killed them. The voice promised the same provinces to rival captains, and the [[Wars of the Inheritance]] ran on for a generation. Around 657 BR the Qemaret masters testified that their rite had failed at the deathbed. Serevan's grandson walled the body and the images into the tomb, and the living successors settled their borders by about 650 BR. The tomb's site was later lost.

Caleran recovered room to act as Demeran lost the old hegemony. It received exiles, sailors paid in sealed promises instead of silver, and officers from the divided armies. The ensuing Gateway Wars transformed this recovered city into a sustained overseas republic. Vashar became an imperial peer under House Tiravan, while Delkosi naval culture passed through later officer houses into the kingdom of Sarqen.

## The remembered king

Ortheian royal songs present Eryndor as the young king who inherited his father's unfinished war and went to bring home the families of Terys. Qahuri tales dwell upon the oracle, his failure to escape death and the embalmers who failed him. Arqeshite laments name cities and households destroyed by the campaign. Vashari stories give greater honour to Arezan, Mithara and Parena. Delkosi veterans' songs keep the voice in the tomb as the king himself. Jirahar travellers make an anonymous cook or guide, rather than the king, the finder of life-giving water.

These traditions share a ruler of courage, curiosity, generosity and extreme appetite. He crossed boundaries with his soldiers and learned from conquered courts. He also treated cities, companions and labourers as instruments when they obstructed his desired horizon. The unusual incidents surrounding his eastern journey have separate historical settings; their later union into one marvellous voyage belongs to [[The Five Limits of Eryndor]].
