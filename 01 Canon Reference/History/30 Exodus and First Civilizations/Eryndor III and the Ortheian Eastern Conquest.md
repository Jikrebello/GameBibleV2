# Eryndor III and the Ortheian Eastern Conquest

**Eryndor III of Ortheia**, commonly called the **Far-Road King**, was an upland Atherian ruler whose campaigns transformed the eastern world between 706 and 684 BR. He inherited a trained army and a disputed hegemony over the Celestial Compact from his father, Valeron II. He destroyed the city of Olyssar when it rebelled, crossed the eastern inner sea, joined Arezan Tiravan against the Arqeshite River Empire and carried his army beyond the conquered capital. His sudden death divided the territories among rival commanders and royal houses.

Eryndor belongs to an earlier political world than Teren Casvilar and Aurelius Thalion. He was a hereditary king who compelled Atherian cities to serve a royal campaign. Casvilar arose from the institutions of a mature republic, while Thalion converted victory in a civil war into lasting imperial government. The failure of Eryndor's dominion taught later Caleran writers that conquest and government were separate achievements.

## Ortheia and Valeron II

Ortheia lay in the colder mountain country between the older Atherian city world and the lands later associated with Thalmyria. Its valleys produced grain and fruit; its pastures supported horses and pack animals; its forests and mines supplied timber, iron and silver. Ortheian speech, marriage and sanctuary practice belonged to the broad Atherian world, although the kingdom retained its own royal houses, upland assemblies and military traditions.

Valeron II inherited a crown threatened by neighbouring valleys and stronger cities. He drew the sons and daughters of subordinate houses into his household, maintained a permanent infantry core around the seasonal levies and employed engineers and clerks trained in the city world. Mines paid the soldiers when harvest contributions failed. Marriage, bribery, hostage education and war extended his influence into the twelve-city Celestial Compact between about 735 and 706 BR.

The cities retained their councils and festivals under this arrangement. They supplied money, ships, grain and soldiers for a common eastern campaign. Some joined because Valeron restrained their local wars and promised protection from Arqesh. Others paid because Ortheian troops stood outside their walls. Caleran tried to preserve the authority of its assembly while meeting the king's demands.

Valeron was assassinated in 706 BR before the eastern expedition began. The killers and their immediate purpose remain uncertain. Eryndor secured the crown against adult relatives and commanders who had served his father. His first great test came at Olyssar, a Compact city that repudiated Ortheian authority during the succession.

## Olyssar, 705–704 BR

Olyssar expected other cities to follow its revolt. Eryndor isolated it before that coalition formed. His soldiers cut the approaches, defeated a relief force and entered after a prolonged assault. They killed defenders and civilians, enslaved many survivors and divided land among loyal households. Exiles carried the city's cult objects, workshop skills and grievance to Caleran and the coast.

The destruction secured the league behind Eryndor. It also exposed the terms of his authority. Cities later celebrated the eastern war as liberation from Arqesh while omitting the Atherian city destroyed to make the campaign possible. Olyssaran descendants preserved lists of the dead and sold, and refused to honour Eryndor at festivals established after his victories.

## Crossing the eastern sea

Eryndor sailed in 702 BR with Ortheian infantry and cavalry, Compact contingents, siege engineers and allied ships. The cities had accepted the war mandate under different degrees of pressure. Their contribution lists remained a source of argument for generations because they showed which councils gave willingly, which delayed and which supplied troops only after royal seizure of their grain.

The army defeated Arqeshite field forces along the western approaches. Coastal cities then made separate choices. Some opened their harbours; others changed sides after neighbouring defeats; several fought because Arqeshite hostages or local rivalries gave them no safe neutrality.

The fortified harbour of **Terys** barred Eryndor from its offshore sanctuary and fleet anchorage. Between 697 and 695 BR, engineers built a causeway from the mainland using stone, timber and rubble taken from abandoned buildings. Divers and haulers worked beneath attacks from the harbour. An enclosed observation bell allowed Eryndor and selected engineers to inspect the foundations, but a deep-water attacker struck the apparatus during one descent and killed several attendants. The king survived. Later stories made the incident a journey through an undersea kingdom.

When Terys fell, Eryndor's army massacred inhabitants, enslaved survivors and took the fleet. The causeway altered currents and shoreline settlement long after the siege. Terys supplied ships for the remaining campaign and graves for families scattered across the eastern ports.

## Qahur and the Tiravan alliance

Eryndor entered divided Qahur in 695 BR because his army required grain, river craft and guides. The Qahuri crowns responded separately. One recognised him with a local royal title and admitted him to a renowned oracle; another supported Arqesh; a third withheld commitment until the military result became clearer. A limited phenomenon associated with Father Sun occurred during the journey, but the surviving witnesses disagreed about its form. Eryndor encouraged the claim that the sign revealed exceptional ancestry. It gave him neither divine parentage nor authority over Qahur.

In 694 BR he allied with **Arezan Tiravan**, leader of the Vashari coalition fighting Arqesh from the east. Arezan commanded highland houses, mounted troops and relationships with rebel governors and forced workers. Eryndor supplied the larger siege army, allied fleet and money. Their treaty treated them as independent kings and divided campaigning responsibilities and captured revenue between their commands.

Eryndor married **Parena Tiravan**, adult daughter of Arezan's sister Mithara. Parena acted between the two courts, received Vashari officers and Arqeshite petitioners, and protected parts of her household from rival commanders. The marriage joined the alliance without removing the different ambitions of the two kings.

Arqesh fell in 690 BR through their converging campaigns and the action of people inside the empire. Nemeru crews carried troops across a closed channel. Forced workers opened gates. Provincial governors withheld boats and food. Arezan seized eastern roads and water stations while Eryndor pressed the capital with the fleet and principal siege force. Both armies killed inhabitants and plundered districts during the capture.

The last Ubar king, Kassur III, escaped. A provincial commander killed him in 688 BR and offered the body to Eryndor. Eryndor executed the killers and gave Kassur a royal burial, presenting himself as lawful successor to the king he had pursued. He retained Arqeshite secretaries and court dress, accepted eastern officers and permitted the royal Pride Mask known as the Crowned Countenance to appear around his ceremonies. The dead Ubar kings, Eryndor and the Mask remained separate persons and powers.

## The farther roads

Eryndor campaigned east of Arqesh against fortresses and mobile opponents who had survived the fall of the capital. Hostage marriages, new garrisons, executions and burned villages accompanied the advance. The mixed court drew in Arqeshite officials, Jirahar interpreters, Vashari officers, Qahuri specialists and eastern nobles. Veterans expected the captured treasury and provincial commands as their reward.

Several incidents from these years became the historical core of the Far-Road romances. Eryndor rose in a suspended frame carried by trained Sky-Lions to observe an eastern position; an uncontrolled descent injured handlers and killed attendants. A grove of Root-Minds answered questions through an interpreter and foretold the king's death. Engineers closed a local pass against a surviving predatory host, leaving villages and prisoners beyond the barrier. A final expedition sought a restorative seep connected with the Egg White. One lesser member of the party encountered the water and disappeared; Eryndor returned without it.

The army refused a farther march in 685 BR. Its veterans were exhausted, dependants had died along the supply roads and eastern recruits had no wish to carry the king beyond their own countries. No sign or royal speech broke the refusal. Eryndor began the return to Arqesh.

His personal rule also carried intimate bloodshed. He ordered the death of an experienced officer after the officer's son was accused of concealing a plot. During a drunken quarrel, Eryndor killed a companion who had once saved his life. His remorse changed later behaviour and court ritual; it could not restore either man.

## Death and inheritance

Eryndor died at Arqesh in 684 BR while preparing another campaign and a more permanent government. His cause of death remains unknown. Parena was pregnant. An adult half-brother and her posthumous child served as nominal heirs, but neither controlled the army, treasury or provincial appointments.

The first settlement divided commands while retaining the language of one monarchy. It postponed war. Arezan secured Vashar, the eastern roads and much of the Ubar basin. Demeran Kavos took Ortheia and the western army. Serevan of Delkos held islands, ships and eastern harbours. Neresh of Qemaret controlled selected Qahuri crown towns through garrisons and marriage. Their conflicts killed Parena, the child and the other nominal heirs and produced several independent states by about 650 BR.

Caleran recovered room to act as Demeran lost the old hegemony. It received exiles, unpaid sailors and officers from the divided armies. The ensuing Gateway Wars transformed this recovered city into a sustained overseas republic. Vashar became an imperial peer under House Tiravan, while Delkosi naval culture passed through later officer houses into the kingdom of Sarqen.

## The remembered king

Ortheian royal songs present Eryndor as the young king who inherited his father's unfinished war. Qahuri tales dwell upon the oracle and his failure to escape death. Arqeshite laments name cities and households destroyed by the campaign. Vashari stories give greater honour to Arezan, Mithara and Parena. Jirahar travellers make an anonymous cook or guide, rather than the king, the finder of life-giving water.

These traditions share a ruler of courage, curiosity, generosity and extreme appetite. He crossed boundaries with his soldiers and learned from conquered courts. He also treated cities, companions and labourers as instruments when they obstructed his desired horizon. The unusual incidents surrounding his eastern journey have separate historical settings; their later union into one marvellous voyage belongs to [[The Five Limits of Eryndor]].
