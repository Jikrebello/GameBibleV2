# The Glass Sojourn

The **Glass Sojourn**, approximately c. 2265–2165 BR, is the long refuge in which the mixed human remnant carried by the Glass Ark became one people. After the Prophet's death, that people first separates gradually into southern war-settlement leagues and a continuing Near-Glass camp continuum; the latter divides generations later into the ancestors of the Jirahar and proto-Ahl-e-Ahd. The date range is traditional and approximate; the exact number of years, camps and people remains unknown.

## Controlling Ruling

The Lord deliberately sends the principal remnant into the [[Great Glass Desert]] to shelter it from the continuing collapse of the Giant world. The Great Prophet leads and governs under commission but does not create the passage, provision or protection.

The Glass refuge is not the later failed sanctuary. It successfully preserves and forms the host. After the long sojourn, the people are commanded to leave and establish a southern sanctuary capable of holding their common life for three generations. The provision belongs to the refuge's commission and ends with it at the outward threshold (see The End of Provision below). The Prophet dies at or near the outward threshold around c. 2165 BR, while the rear companies are still inside the Glass and before that sanctuary is securely established. The sanctuary fails gradually as bounded camp offices contest movement, land, punishment, provision and command; no office inherits the Prophet's commission.

## Giant Non-Settlement

Before the Exodus, Giant powers had approached, surveyed and sometimes entered the Glass margins, but none established a permanent deep-Glass road, tax district, dynastic estate or self-sustaining city. Pursuers, temporary stations, instruments, bodies and marginal ruins may remain.

This is an empirical boundary, not an explanation of the Glass. No account may turn it into an anti-Giant weapon, hidden Menhir design, Titan poison, discoverable frequency or proof of the Glass's ultimate purpose.

## Provision and Human Work

The Lord supplies the necessities the host cannot secure by ordinary means: food, water, shade, orientation, endurance, preservation and protection at appointed crises and stages. The exact schedule and visible form of those signs remain open.

Human labour remains indispensable. People gather and distribute food, maintain animals and seed, repair carts and tools, dig drains and graves, isolate sickness, care for the injured, translate testimony, teach children, hear disputes, punish crime, defend the rear and carry those whom stronger households would abandon.

The signs are bounded to this commission. They cannot be summoned by descent, reconstructed from an anchor camp or converted into a modern Glass industry.

**What the host ate** (Causality Pass, 14 November 2026). The host ate provision at the appointed stages; milk and meat from its herds; and produce from gardens at the anchor camps, watered from provided springs. Seed was kept alive by token plantings, enough to keep each line viable and never enough to feed a camp. Three or four Glass-born generations could herd, repair, judge, count, bury and garden. Few had cleared a field, timed a flood or stored grain against a second bad year.

## The End of Provision

*Causality Pass 01.1, approved 14 November 2026.*

**Cause.** The provision belonged to the refuge's commission, not to the people. Grace in this world comes by commission, not on demand; when the commission was complete, the provision stopped on schedule.

**Sequence.**
- **c. 2180 BR.** The host is commanded south. The provision thins over about a decade (c. 2180–2170 BR): stages once met in full are met late or in part, and the provided springs at the anchor camps weaken, so the camp gardens fail with them.
- **c. 2170–2165 BR.** The crossing is staged over several seasons, company by company. Provision ceases for each company at the threshold. Drought strikes the rear camps, which begin eating the seed reserve.
- **c. 2165 BR.** The Prophet dies near the threshold while the rear companies are still inside. He died leading the people out; most of the people left after his death (this resolves Appendix A).

**Scale (author-only; reader texts keep "unknown").** About forty thousand people entered the Glass c. 2265 BR. With low mortality under provision, roughly 100,000–120,000 reached the threshold. No district south of the Glass could feed them.

**Why the sanctuary was commanded.** The three-generation southern sanctuary was the time needed to become farmers under protection. Its failure meant learning farming by taking farmers.

**Consequences.**
- **The Vacant Commission was famine politics.** Provisioners had distributed what was given; now their office decided who lived. The contested stores were chiefly the seed reserve.
- **The Five Staffs levy took seed.** The rear camps were eating seed to survive the drought; the southern expedition needed it to plant. The commander and the judge were both right, which is why the quarrel never closed ([[G-71 - The Five Staffs and the Empty Granary]]).
- **The Southern Seizures took farmers as much as land.** Forts seized fields together with their cultivators. Hostage-taking, adoption and marriage targeted farming households. Enslaved Orphaned farmers taught their captors the farming year. Atherian slavery and adoption law grew from the need for farmers.
- **Atherian religious drift has this root.** The southern camps' saying ran: *"The Lord who stopped feeding us; the stars that still answer."* Menhir correspondences answer any compatible rite; the Lord had answered by commission. A people fed daily for a century read a completed commission as abandonment. Astronax follows.
- **A camp layer survives in three languages.** Camp speech leaves a substrate under Atherian, Jirahar and Ahdari, densest in measure, oath, water, law, grave and name. All three keep the whole layer with drifting meanings: the provision-portion becomes an Atherian wage, a Jirahar portion owed to the dead and an Ahdari guest's share. The cognates are reserved for a later language pass.
- **The Ark–Orphaned grievance is about bread.** Folk saying: *"Heaven fed them a hundred years and sent them to eat our harvest."* Ark reply: *"They were not fed because they were not called."*

**The Prophet's death in public tradition.** Four incompatible tellings are canon as in-world traditions (Jirahar *Slow Road*, Ahdari *Unpaid Well*, Atherian *Herald's Ascent*, Folk and Aurin *Guest Who Died at the Door*); see [[Age of Exodus]]. Each ties his death to the end of the bread. The authorial truth of the death remains among the fixed unknowns below; no new sealed weird is created.

## The People of the Camp

The Ark enters as a convergence of households from many Giant horizons and lesser states, not as three later peoples travelling together. During approximately a century of common life, the host becomes one historical people through:

- common worship, recitation, fasts, feasts and rest;
- common law and delegated judgement;
- common ration, water and burden measures;
- household, birth, marriage, death and service registers;
- mixed duties, apprenticeship, adoption and intermarriage;
- a common public camp language;
- shared graves, enemies and memories of deliverance.

**People of the Camp**, **Glass People** and **Prophet's People** are authorial handles, not fixed endonyms. One people does not mean one appearance, pure descent, collective innocence or the erasure of household memory.

## Hard Edge

The refuge is sustained but not idyllic. Its cemeteries contain children, workers, criminals, victims and people who die in ordinary sickness or accident. Some recipients of provision hoard, murder, rebel, exploit emergency office, attempt to restore Giant rank or turn toward false sacred powers. Some leave the camp and perish. Deliverance does not prove the righteousness of every delivered person.

The exact rebellions, punishments and judgements remain separate planting work. They must preserve the distinction between attested consequence and later propaganda.

## The Civilizational Witness

The common life formed in the Glass becomes the Ark-derived world's first durable witness for free human civilisation after Giant rule:

- a human being cannot be owned as Giant property;
- ruler, judge, priest, commander and artisan hold differentiated offices rather than divine blood-right;
- names, graves, weights, rations and public measures answer to law;
- strangers, widows, orphans and dependants possess claims strength alone cannot erase;
- skilled work is transmitted through apprenticeship rather than guarded as a caste secret;
- rest, worship, mourning and teaching limit production and military necessity;
- emergency authority must account for its continuation;
- no miracle gives its human recipient unlimited authority.

Ark descent gives later societies a real institutional inheritance, not superior human nature or guaranteed obedience. Ark-descended rulers may betray the witness, while Orphaned peoples may independently recover true law, mercy and craft.

## The Last Witness

By his death, the Great Prophet is the last living human with direct memory of the Giant world. The Glass-born generations know it through testimony, objects, scars and records. His exceptional but finite longevity is a personal mercy and burden, not divinity, immortality or an inheritable prophetic trait.

His death therefore ends both a leadership and a mode of historical knowledge. Thereafter every account of the Giant world is mediated through record, tradition, archaeology or supernatural claimant.

## Departure and Division

The Glass is refuge and school, not a permanent homeland. The already-formed people leaves after the Prophet's death and fails to establish the commanded southern sanctuary in a stable common form.

Between approximately c. 2130 and 2050 BR, southern companies organised around captains, craftsmen, survey, captured works and permanent allotment separate gradually from the camps remaining near the Glass roads. Those southern leagues become proto-Atherian through settlement, conquest and mixture; they principally carry crafts, measures, military service, archives, public works and civic deliberation.

The continuing **Near-Glass camp continuum** is an authorial label, not the name of a forgotten nation and not another prophetic office. Between approximately c. 1950 and 1750 BR, water, distance, marriage, trade, raiding and sustained Tarkhan contact divide it into two later institutional formations:

- **Proto-Jirahar** principally carry commandment, names, portable courts, burial obligation, road memory and expectation.
- **Proto-Ahl-e-Ahd** principally carry oath, water allotment, guest protection, witness, restitution and enforceable obligation attached increasingly to defended wells and territorial protectors.

Neither fission occurs in one council, battle or migration. None of the three later traditions inherits the whole witness, and none possesses superior human nature through Ark descent.

Meanwhile, human communities outside the Ark survive through independent **Orphaned Reassemblies**. Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Nsizwa are principally Orphaned peoples. Sestaran harbour populations, Vesari and later Eclessians are **Braided peoples** formed through later mixtures of Ark-derived, Orphaned and other local populations.
