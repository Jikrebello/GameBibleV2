# The Glass Sojourn

The **Glass Sojourn**, approximately c. 2265–2165 BR, is the long refuge in which the mixed human remnant carried by the Glass Ark became one people. After the Prophet's death, that people first separates gradually into southern war-settlement leagues and a continuing Near-Glass camp continuum; the latter divides generations later into the ancestors of the Jirahar and proto-Ahl-e-Ahd. The date range is traditional and approximate; the exact number of years, camps and people remains unknown.

## Controlling Ruling

The Lord deliberately sends the principal remnant into the [[Great Glass Desert]] to shelter it from the continuing collapse of the Giant world. The Great Prophet leads and governs under commission but does not create the passage, provision or protection.

The Glass refuge is not the later failed sanctuary. It successfully preserves and forms the host. After the long sojourn, the people are commanded to leave and establish a southern sanctuary capable of holding their common life for three generations. The Prophet dies at or near the outward threshold around c. 2165 BR before that sanctuary is securely established. The sanctuary fails gradually as bounded camp offices contest movement, land, punishment, provision and command; no office inherits the Prophet's commission.

## Giant Non-Settlement

Before the Exodus, Giant powers had approached, surveyed and sometimes entered the Glass margins, but none established a permanent deep-Glass road, tax district, dynastic estate or self-sustaining city. Pursuers, temporary stations, instruments, bodies and marginal ruins may remain.

This is an empirical boundary, not an explanation of the Glass. No account may turn it into an anti-Giant weapon, hidden Menhir design, Titan poison, discoverable frequency or proof of the Glass's ultimate purpose.

## Provision and Human Work

The Lord supplies the necessities the host cannot secure by ordinary means: food, water, shade, orientation, endurance, preservation and protection at appointed crises and stages. The exact schedule and visible form of those signs remain open.

Human labor remains indispensable. People gather and distribute food, maintain animals and seed, repair carts and tools, dig drains and graves, isolate sickness, care for the injured, translate testimony, teach children, hear disputes, punish crime, defend the rear and carry those whom stronger households would abandon.

The signs are bounded to this commission. They cannot be summoned by descent, reconstructed from an anchor camp or converted into a modern Glass industry.

## The People of the Camp

The Ark enters as a convergence of households from many Giant horizons and lesser states, not as three later peoples travelling together. During approximately a century of common life, the host becomes one historical people through:

- common worship, recitation, fasts, feasts and rest;
- common law and delegated judgement;
- common ration, water and burden measures;
- household, birth, marriage, death and service registers;
- mixed duties, apprenticeship, adoption and intermarriage;
- a common public camp language;
- shared graves, enemies and memories of deliverance.

**People of the Camp**, **Glass People** and **Prophet's People** are authorial handles, not fixed endonyms. One people does not mean one appearance, pure descent, collective innocence or the erasure of household memory.

## Hard Edge

The refuge is sustained but not idyllic. Its cemeteries contain children, workers, criminals, victims and people who die in ordinary sickness or accident. Some recipients of provision hoard, murder, rebel, exploit emergency office, attempt to restore Giant rank or turn toward false sacred powers. Some leave the camp and perish. Deliverance does not prove the righteousness of every delivered person.

The exact rebellions, punishments and judgements remain separate planting work. They must preserve the distinction between attested consequence and later propaganda.

## The Civilizational Witness

The common life formed in the Glass becomes the Ark-derived world's first durable witness for free human civilization after Giant rule:

- a human being cannot be owned as Giant property;
- ruler, judge, priest, commander and artisan hold differentiated offices rather than divine blood-right;
- names, graves, weights, rations and public measures answer to law;
- strangers, widows, orphans and dependants possess claims strength alone cannot erase;
- skilled work is transmitted through apprenticeship rather than guarded as a caste secret;
- rest, worship, mourning and teaching limit production and military necessity;
- emergency authority must account for its continuation;
- no miracle gives its human recipient unlimited authority.

Ark descent gives later societies a real institutional inheritance, not superior human nature or guaranteed obedience. Ark-descended rulers may betray the witness, while Orphaned peoples may independently recover true law, mercy and craft.

## The Last Witness

By his death, the Great Prophet is the last living human with direct memory of the Giant world. The Glass-born generations know it through testimony, objects, scars and records. His exceptional but finite longevity is a personal mercy and burden, not divinity, immortality or an inheritable prophetic trait.

His death therefore ends both a leadership and a mode of historical knowledge. Thereafter every account of the Giant world is mediated through record, tradition, archaeology or supernatural claimant.

## Departure and Division

The Glass is refuge and school, not a permanent homeland. The already-formed people leaves after the Prophet's death and fails to establish the commanded southern sanctuary in a stable common form.

Between approximately c. 2130 and 2050 BR, southern companies organized around captains, craftsmen, survey, captured works and permanent allotment separate gradually from the camps remaining near the Glass roads. Those southern leagues become proto-Atherian through settlement, conquest and mixture; they principally carry crafts, measures, military service, archives, public works and civic deliberation.

The continuing **Near-Glass camp continuum** is an authorial label, not the name of a forgotten nation and not another prophetic office. Between approximately c. 1950 and 1750 BR, water, distance, marriage, trade, raiding and sustained Tarkhan contact divide it into two later institutional formations:

- **Proto-Jirahar** principally carry commandment, names, portable courts, burial obligation, road memory and expectation.
- **Proto-Ahl-e-Ahd** principally carry oath, water allotment, guest protection, witness, restitution and enforceable obligation attached increasingly to defended wells and territorial protectors.

Neither fission occurs in one council, battle or migration. None of the three later traditions inherits the whole witness, and none possesses superior human nature through Ark descent.

Meanwhile, human communities outside the Ark survive through independent **Orphaned Reassemblies**. Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Nsizwa are principally Orphaned peoples. Sestaran harbor populations, Vesari and later Eclessians are **Braided peoples** formed through later mixtures of Ark-derived, Orphaned and other local populations.

## Fixed Unknowns

- the complete provision cycle;
- the complete list of rebellions and judgements;
- whether the Prophet's death was failure, murder, exhaustion, judgement or mystery;
- the first post-Prophet faction leaders;
- why Giants could not hold the deep Glass;
- every protected Weird associated with the Glass.
