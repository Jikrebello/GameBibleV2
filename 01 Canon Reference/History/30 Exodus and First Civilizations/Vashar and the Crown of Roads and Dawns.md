# Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns

**Vashar** was a highland country east of the Ubar whose valleys, pasture roads and sanctuary towns were united by House Tiravan during the fall of Arqesh. Arezan I and his successors created the **Crown of Roads and Dawns**, an empire governed through royal relatives, road commanders, subject rulers and established city institutions. It protected the return to Qiryath, imposed tribute across former Arqeshite lands and remained an independent peer when Atheria unified in 350 BR.

## Vashar and its peoples

**Vashar** is the name for the high country east of the Ubar riverlands. Long cultivated valleys lie between dry ridges and colder upland pastures. Snowmelt feeds the western tributaries. Roads rise through narrow passes towards horse country, mining districts and farther lands beyond the known world's dependable surveys.

The **Vashari** developed from several Orphaned populations whose ancestors survived the Giant collapse outside the Glass refuge. Their villages, fortified estates and sanctuary towns existed before House Tiravan united them. Related languages, marriage customs and teachings concerning truthful order linked these communities. Political unity arrived much later through alliances, conquest and the royal service created by House Tiravan.

Three regions supplied the later imperial core. The **Kavresh valley** produced grain, fruit and the principal royal house. The high pastures of **Ruzhan** bred horses and supported mobile aristocratic households. The **Daryam approaches** contained copper, iron and old sanctuaries in which household fires were tended across generations. Villages commonly combined farming with seasonal movement. A household might cultivate valley land, send adolescents to summer pasture and maintain a relative in a market town.

The Vashari encountered Arqesh through trade, warfare and hostage exchange. Highland timber, stone, metal and horses descended to the canal cities. River grain and finished cloth travelled uphill. Arqeshite rulers tried to control the passes through allied lords and garrisons. Some Vashari houses prospered as intermediaries. Others lost children to hostage courts or land to collaborators.

## Before the imperial crown, c. 1300–760 BR

The oldest Vashari political unit was the **hearth domain**. Several villages and pasture households pledged service to a fortified landholding family or an important sanctuary in return for protection, access to winter stores and representation in disputes. The domain's ruler judged water, grazing, compensation for injury and military service with an assembly of household leaders. Larger coalitions formed for war and commonly dissolved when their leaders quarrelled over spoils or marriage alliances.

The sanctuary town of **Daryam** became influential during the Breaking of the Bronze Roads. Its keepers maintained a sheltered market and a fire used to light household lamps after winter. The fire was ordinary created matter, kept alive through skill and fuel. Keeping it alight through the closed season represented the community's obligation to preserve life and deal truthfully.

Teachers associated with Daryam called truthful speech, kept promises and the defence of cultivated land **the Right Order**. They called deliberate deceit and the abuse of a promise **the Lie**, treating it as human corruption rather than a second power of creation. Royal teachers later identified loyalty to the king with the Right Order. This allowed a ruler who had broken faith to condemn resistance as falsehood.

The oldest attributed teacher is **Ashara of the Three Questions**, placed uncertainly around 1190 BR. Her surviving sayings ask whether a gift remains generous when refusal brings punishment, whether a ruler speaks truth when his messenger cannot contradict him and whether a pure fire excuses stolen fuel. No reliable tradition gives her supernatural powers. Several later sects claim her.

House Tiravan first appears among the Kavresh valley rulers around 900 BR. Its estates controlled fruit terraces, a ford and the road to Ruzhan. Marriage with pasture houses supplied cavalry. Marriages with Daryam keepers supplied religious prestige. The house alternated between service to Arqesh and resistance against it.

## Arezan Tiravan and the coalition against Arqesh

**Arezan Tiravan** was born around 742 BR, the year Arqesh destroyed First Qiryath. His father **Vahrad Tiravan** had sent him to Durqesh as a political hostage after a failed rising. Arezan learned Arqeshite, trained with royal cavalry and formed relationships with provincial officers. His sister **Mithara** remained in Kavresh and managed the family estates during their father's illness.

Arezan returned after Vahrad's execution in 716 BR. He inherited a divided household. One uncle wanted submission; Ruzhan allies demanded war; tenant villages feared another Arqeshite levy more than they trusted the Tiravans. Mithara secured the valley by restoring the grazing and inheritance agreements her father had suspended.

The Arqeshite succession war beginning around 706 BR gave Arezan an opportunity to leave provincial service. Mardun Ubar, governor of Durqesh and Arezan's relative by marriage, sought Vashari support against Kassur III. Arezan accepted weapons and passage through Durqesh while negotiating independently with Nemeru, Qahuri rebels, Tarkhan riders and subject governors. When Mardun attempted to withdraw from their agreement, his own guards killed him and opened the city.

Arezan presented the coalition as a restoration of roads and truthful rule. Its members pursued different aims. Nemeru wanted freedom from Arqeshite tax officers. Tarkhan leaders wanted animals, pasture and the destruction of remount stations. Qahuri crowns wanted their eastern approaches returned. Vashari houses sought land, office and vengeance.

The western invasion of **Eryndor III of Ortheia** changed the scale of the war. Eryndor had inherited an Atherian-speaking upland kingdom and a hegemony imposed upon several city leagues. After crossing the eastern sea in 702 BR, he defeated Arqeshite coastal armies and took the fortified harbour of Terys. He entered divided Qahur in 695 BR, where rival crowns supplied his army and sought his recognition.

Arezan and Eryndor met in 694 BR. They agreed to destroy House Ubar while retaining separate commands. Arezan controlled the highland roads, provincial alliances and eastern approaches; Eryndor brought the larger siege army, fleet and treasury. The agreement was reinforced when Eryndor married **Parena Tiravan**, the adult daughter of Mithara. The marriage gave Eryndor a place within the Tiravan household and gave Arezan influence at the western king's court. Parena accompanied the royal household east and became an intermediary for Vashari officers, Ortheian commanders and Arqeshite families seeking terms.

The final campaigns included massacre and enslavement by both victorious armies. Arezan punished some killings when they deprived him of useful officials and ignored others committed by necessary allies. In 690 BR his forces seized the eastern gates and water stations of Arqesh while Eryndor's soldiers entered across a channel opened by Nemeru boat crews and forced workers. The two armies plundered districts assigned to one another after the surrender.

Kassur III escaped with several sons and the portable royal images. A provincial commander murdered him in 688 BR and offered the body to Eryndor. Eryndor executed the killers and buried Kassur with royal ceremony, presenting himself as the lawful successor of the king he had pursued. Arezan retained royal children and former Ubar officers in guarded households of his own. Vashari houses had recognised Arezan as king in 704 BR, fourteen years before Arqesh fell; he reigned until 671 BR.

## The Crown of Roads and Dawns

Arezan adopted the title **Keeper of the Roads and King at Dawn**. Later historians call the state he established the **Vasharan Empire** or **Crown of Roads and Dawns**. During Eryndor's lifetime, however, Arqesh and the conquered provinces had no single master. Eryndor occupied the High Palace and treasury. Arezan held the Gate House, eastern roads and much of the surviving provincial administration. Canal officials continued working because neither army could feed the city without them.

Eryndor died at Arqesh in 684 BR while preparing another campaign. Parena was pregnant. The Ortheian commanders acknowledged an adult royal half-brother and Parena's posthumous child as nominal heirs, then divided the army and treasury among themselves. Arezan used the crisis to secure the highlands, eastern roads and most of the Ubar basin.

The empire reused Arqeshite institutions. Canal governors retained their staffs. Local languages remained in administration. Subject rulers kept courts where their cooperation reduced the cost of occupation. Vashari relatives and allied houses received the highest military and provincial offices.

In 684 BR, during the first division of Eryndor's commands, Arezan issued the **Decree of Roads and Houses**. It authorised several displaced peoples to restore sanctuaries, recover approved sacred objects and return to property recognised by Vasharan officials. The decree served the king's interest by weakening Arqeshite military households and reopening neglected roads. It also allowed families to leave captivity and rebuild communal life.

Jirahar teachers later described the return decree as an act of providence. Vasharan inscriptions claimed that the Right Order had chosen Arezan to correct the Lie of Arqesh. The Jirahar accounts praised the release and return of captive households while continuing to record the killings committed by Arezan's coalition. Vasharan court histories used the decree to support broader claims about his right to rule.

The **Wars of the Inheritance**, fought from 684 to about 650 BR, divided Eryndor's conquests. Arezan held Vashar and most of the Ubar basin. **Demeran Kavos**, Eryndor's senior western general, took Ortheia and the western army. **Serevan of Delkos**, commander of the allied fleet, established a mixed court among the islands and eastern harbours. **Neresh of Qemaret**, a Qahuri-born officer, held southern garrisons and crown towns through marriage and military settlement. These rulers sometimes recognised one of Eryndor's heirs while fighting over the lands claimed in that heir's name.

Demeran eventually imprisoned Parena and her child and ordered their deaths. Vasharan accounts treated the killings as proof of western treachery; Ortheian histories blamed Arezan for withholding the forces that might have rescued them. Mithara preserved Parena's attendants and arranged marriages for two surviving members of her household. Their descendants carried rival accounts of Eryndor's final days into Vashari and maritime courts.

## Daryav I and imperial organisation, 671–625 BR

Arezan's eldest surviving son, **Daryav I Tiravan**, inherited a coalition held together by his father's relationships. Several provinces revolted at the succession. Daryav defeated the largest rising at Nemeru, executed its military leaders and deported neighbourhoods associated with the river fleet. He pardoned officials who delivered stores before the siege.

After victory, Daryav divided the empire into **road commands**, each governed from a fortified city on a principal route. A road commander collected tribute, raised soldiers, supplied relay stations and guarded the royal family against provincial revolt. Village assemblies, city councils and subject rulers continued to govern local affairs beneath him. The crown appointed the commanders, usually from House Tiravan or the families that had supported Arezan.

The **Dawn Road** connected Kavresh, Daryam, Durqesh, Arqesh and the western coast. Relay stations maintained horses, food, sleeping rooms and repair yards. Messengers could cross the core rapidly; merchants travelled more slowly and paid at bridges and stations. The road brought markets and news to some towns while exposing them to tax collectors and requisition.

Daryav funded the rebuilding of Qiryath and confirmed its sanctuary estate. In return, road houses supplied interpreters, maintained selected stations and carried recognised correspondence. The king's officials also used Jirahar household knowledge to assess communities that had previously escaped provincial counting.

At Kavresh, Daryav began a ceremonial terrace where delegations carried soil, tools, textiles, animals and vessels representing their provinces. The art described the empire as willing cooperation around the king. Tribute records, garrison letters and accounts of suppression reveal the force behind that procession.

## Religion and royal legitimacy

Vasharan kings adopted the language of Right Order and tended light. A royal flame was carried from Daryam to a new reign's first public audience. It represented household continuity and the king's promise to preserve the roads. Provincial rulers could retain their own worship while acknowledging the royal ceremony.

Three traditions later claimed that a sacred flame had accompanied Arezan's conquest. Daryam remembered a household lamp carried on a mule. An Arqeshite relief showed a royal brazier taken from Kassur's palace. Qiryath remembered a lamp lit when the return decree was read. Later writers fused these separate objects into one travelling relic called the **Threefold Fire**.

The three fires were ordinary lamps and braziers with separate histories. During Daryav's reign, an Idol Mask began to imitate the radiance of the royal ceremony. It appeared in mirrors, polished armour and dreams of a king surrounded by kneeling nations. Court diviners called it the **Witness of Dawn**. Its visions encouraged rulers to treat obedience as proof that their subjects recognised the truth.

Some priests resisted. **Mazhara of Daryam**, active under Daryav, taught that a king's lie remained a lie when carved in three languages. After she condemned the killing of surrendered rebels, the crown confiscated her family's orchards and sold their bonded workers. Mazhara remained alive and continued teaching in another household. Court histories later cited her survival as proof of royal tolerance and omitted the punishment inflicted upon her workers.

Highland springs and passes sometimes answered through established Menhir relationships or the actions of individual court persons. The songs of the White-Haired Fosterling describe an enormous bird, although they disagree about its home and purpose. Its identity as a court person, a Patron visitor or a poetic addition remains unknown.

## Kavran I and the western road, 625–584 BR

**Kavran I Tiravan**, Daryav's grandson, inherited a stable core and a contentious western coast. The coast contained mixed cities whose inhabitants spoke local, Vashari, Arqeshite and Atherian languages. Their ships connected the empire to the inner sea. Royal governors supported friendly rulers and demanded ships, taxes and soldiers.

Kavran concentrated upon the road between Arqesh, Qahur and the coastal commands. He replaced two rebellious governors, established permanent grain stations west of the Ubar and sent royal surveyors into districts whose customary pasture and canal rights were poorly understood at Kavresh. Resistance followed. Villages concealed stores, herders drove animals beyond the stations and one canal district drowned a tax camp by cutting its own embankment.

Kavran's new stations, garrisons and tax districts enlarged Vasharan authority along the western road. The Caleran republic was then occupied by the Gateway Wars and sent no unified army against him. Atherian merchants, mercenaries and individual cities nevertheless appeared along the Vasharan coast, where they traded, served rival rulers and learned the passages later used by fleets.

## Mithara I and the repaired empire, 584–536 BR

Kavran died after a short illness. His aunt **Mithara I Tiravan**, named for Arezan's sister and Parena's mother, was an experienced estate manager and provincial negotiator. She secured the throne against two adult nephews. One was blinded; the other accepted a highland command and later died during a suspicious hunt.

Mithara renegotiated ship levies, restored estates damaged by western resistance and regularised the crown's claims over the road stations. Her treaties allowed Atherian merchants into selected ports while forbidding those cities to shelter rebels.

She also confirmed Qiryath's internal courts after a governor attempted to appoint the chief sanctuary custodian. The confirmation required a larger annual payment and access to Jirahar route information. Later Qiryath tradition praises her restraint while remembering the price paid for it.

Mithara's treaties, tax agreements and marriages reduced the number of provinces that had to be held by a royal army. Provincial families entered the royal service, while Vashari officials married Arqeshite, coastal and Qahuri houses. Children of these unions governed cities and estates, although the oldest families at Kavresh continued to treat many of them as social inferiors.

## Xarun I and the eastern sea wars, 536–486 BR

**Xarun I Tiravan** inherited when the destruction of Qarzeth had released Caleran ships and commanders from the western Gateway struggle. The city of **Tasar** became the centre of revolt after Governor **Mazdan Aresh** intervened in the choice of a new harbour magistrate and increased the city's naval service. The magistrate **Liora of Tasar** sought aid from Atherian leagues in 514 BR. Atherian ships helped the rebels capture the lower city of **Avesh**, the western road-command capital, and burn its storehouses.

Vasharan forces recovered the coast by 508 BR. Tasar's walls were breached; leaders were executed; inhabitants were deported or enslaved. The city survived and supplied ships again within a generation. Xarun concluded that Atherian leagues would continue encouraging revolt while the coastal marches remained contested.

A first punitive expedition in 503 BR failed after poor reconnaissance and a storm separated its supply ships. A second, much larger invasion began in 497 BR. Subject fleets, coastal allies and road-command armies advanced by several routes. They won battles, burned resistant settlements and compelled neutral cities to choose sides.

The invasion failed to establish permanent rule over the principal Atherian leagues. Naval defeat in the **Narrows of Ilyessa** in 493 BR cut the army from regular sea supply. Atherian and allied forces held the crossings while disease and shortage spread through Xarun's camps. The king withdrew the main army in 491 BR, leaving garrisons and clients along the eastern coast.

Vasharan tradition remembers the campaign as the defence of order against oath-breaking cities. Atherian tradition makes every city a willing defender of freedom. Several Atherian cities submitted, supplied Xarun or waited to join the winning side. Subject peoples within the Vasharan army pursued their own interests.

## Later kings and provincial power, 486–431 BR

**Orvaz I Tiravan**, ruling from 486 BR, used money and grain more often than invasion. Atherian maritime alliances expanded after Xarun's defeat. They collected ship money, dismantled the walls of cities attempting to leave and installed friendly councils. Vasharan governors supported exiles from those same cities. Western governors recruited local troops and foreign mercenaries; families changed allegiance to preserve ships, estates or political enemies.

Orvaz financed rival Atherian leagues, hosted exiled rulers and offered naval timber to coalitions willing to recognise Vasharan coastal claims. Provincial governors acquired semi-hereditary influence, although the crown formally appointed them.

Road-command autonomy brought danger at home. The governor of Avesh joined three other commands in a revolt around 462 BR. Orvaz promised pardon, separated the coalition through private agreements and then executed two surrendered leaders whose safe conduct had been publicly proclaimed. **Rathava of Daryam**, called the Truth-Teller, condemned him in the palace garden. Orvaz spared Rathava but destroyed his family estate and sold its labourers. Royal historians recorded the spared priest and omitted the labourers.

## Tiraz III, Orvaz II and the Caleran crisis, 431–350 BR

**Tiraz III Tiravan** inherited a wealthy but decentralised empire. His western governors dealt increasingly with individual Atherian commanders. Marovan Veyr's campaigns between 414 and 397 BR secured winter harbours, suppressed raiding coalitions and established clients near the Vasharan sphere. Some local rulers played Veyr and the road-command governor against one another.

Tiraz died in 403 BR. His son **Orvaz II** spent the first years of his reign suppressing a cousin supported by eastern horse houses. This limited his ability to contest Veyr's settlements. He recognised several of them temporarily in exchange for Atherian withdrawal from Avesh's inland estates.

During the Caleran conflict between Casvilar and Veyr, Orvaz supplied money, pack animals and limited sanctuary to Veyr's clients. He did not commit the central army. Veyr's defeat near Velos in 374 BR therefore damaged Vasharan influence without destroying the empire.

Thalion's rise after 370 BR confronted Orvaz with a more coherent Atherian government. In the settlement of 350 BR, Thalion secures the Atherian-speaking ports and several western clients. Orvaz retains Avesh, the Ubar riverlands and the Vashari core. The agreement leaves two large states facing one another across the coastal marches, with neither able to dictate terms throughout the eastern sea.

## Government and society

The Great King ruled through the royal household, great Vashari houses, appointed road commanders, local rulers, temple estates and city assemblies. Authority varied by province. Arqesh required canal officials. Qiryath retained communal courts. Coastal cities defended civic privileges. Highland estates relied upon personal allegiance and mounted service.

Taxes arrived as grain, animals, silver, cloth, weapons or labour. Subject troops usually served under their own commanders. Royal inspectors travelled the roads and could hear accusations against governors, although access depended upon influence and distance.

Women of the royal and great houses managed estates, patronage and dynastic marriages. Mithara's reign established a royal precedent for a woman ruling in her own name, although most provincial offices remained in male hands. Women worked throughout the agricultural, textile, trading and religious economy; their control of property and choice in marriage differed sharply between regions and social ranks.

Slavery and bonded labour supported estates, mines, roads and households. War captives could be sold far from home. Some royal projects paid skilled crews through ration and wage systems. The same worksite could contain free specialists, estate dependants, conscripted villagers and enslaved prisoners.

## The eastern heroic inheritance

Vashari singers perform histories of great houses, flawed kings, fostered champions and wars between kin. Most surviving cycles were composed centuries after the periods they describe. They preserve family claims, local grievances and heroic memory rather than a consecutive record of reigns.

The **White-Haired Fosterling** concerns **Razana Tiravan**, exposed by an upland lord who feared an Idol had marked her. A great bird-shaped being raised her beyond the pasture roads. She returned as a physician and negotiator, refused the throne offered by her repentant father and supported a rival household whose tenants she had treated during famine. The identity of the fosterer remains unresolved.

The **Champion and the Unrecognised Son** belongs to a later western great house. A commander kills an Atherian-allied opponent at a ford before recognising a token from an earlier hostage marriage. Vasharan singers dwell upon the father's grief after the recognition. In the mother's city, the song begins with her removal as a hostage bride and ends by naming the officials who sent her husband and son to opposite armies.
