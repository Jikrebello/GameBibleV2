# Historical Atlas of the Elmaric Sea before 350 BR

The ancient Elmaric sea was a connected but divided world of islands and ports in the central Thalassian basin. Human communities occupied its islands before the Giant empires fell. Their descendants repaired landings and water stores, received migrants and captives, built rival harbours, and eventually formed a family of related [[Elmaric Island Peoples before 350 BR|Elmaric peoples]]. By 350 BR they shared routes, partly related languages and a sanctuary at [[Ilamar before 350 BR|Ilamar]], but no sovereign or common citizenship. Beyond the central heartland, foreign settlements, grain holdings and fleets made the same waters an arena of repeated conflict.

Two remarkable places belonged to this history without ruling it. [[Caelovar]] was a Nythalum-founded colonial city whose living marine infrastructure drowned during the Great Unbinding; people continued to inhabit its damaged margins. [[Marelith]] was a later, separate harbour carried by a living reefback, destroyed during the Atherian succession war in 355 BR. Inland from these maritime routes, [[Vorthara before 350 BR|Vorthara]] grew inside and around the Black Spire from a fugitive refuge into a town of traders, captive dealers and practitioners of dangerous arts. Grain, travellers, paid crews and captives connected these histories.

## The sea under Giant horizons

Before the first southern settlers arrived, human communities already worked reefs, fisheries, terraces, water stores and landings. Giant-linked powers could demand labour and supplies from a harbour while having little dependable authority over its interior or neighbouring fishing islets.

Nythalum founded one especially ambitious colonial harbour in the central island world. At [[Caelovar]], Giant-blooded houses and imported human dependants conquered existing human inhabitants, took the best water and harbour positions and subordinated older institutions. Native-descended people remained in dependent villages, service households and contested sacred places. Colonial rulers appropriated local reef husbandry and combined it with Giant fleshcraft. Living walls, fresh-water membranes, ship organisms, signalling systems and altered animals required constant human tending. Wealth and technical marvel depended on workers and living structures the ruling houses could not safely discard.

The senior houses eventually claimed parity with Nythalum as a sister power. The colony's marine craft took a biological path distinct from Nythalum's crystal and memory systems. Its internal divisions over control of the living harbour mattered when the Unbinding damaged the coast, disrupted supply and gave subjected communities an opening to rebel.

The Black Spire belonged to a different geography. Giants avoided it, while escaped human slaves and other outsiders used accessible shelter within or against it. Paths, warning places and food plots spread onto the ridges. The Spire's observed danger to Giants and people with strongly active Giant inheritance helps explain the refuge, but its builders and original use are unknown.

## Unbinding and local recovery, c. 2300–1900 BR

The Great Unbinding damaged Caelovar and much of its living foundation sank. Conflicting accounts assign responsibility to the ruling houses, revolt, the sea or divine judgement. Some people reached high ground or escaped by ship; others survived through dangerous transformations of their existing bodies. Camps, fishing settlements and salvage wharves grew beside the ruined site.

On other islands, survivors repaired cisterns, terraces, fishery equipment and short craft. One island could offer pasture but little timber; another held a protected landing yet could not feed every visitor. Families resumed seasonal crossings to exchange food, labour and news long before anyone could speak of one Elmaric state. The [[Shoal and Lagoon Islands before 350 BR|shoal-and-lagoon communities]] developed pilotage adapted to shallow approaches, while the [[Elmarai Isles before 350 BR|Elmarai archipelago]] connected specialised rocky islands and their separate households.

Southern Folk kindreds moving north settled coasts and nearby islands after the Ark dispersals began. Glass-descended voyagers also landed and stopped in some harbours rather than continuing to later Atherian centres. Such arrivals could be guests, spouses, rivals, armed settlers or captives. Their descendants entered particular island peoples over generations. Local Orphaned households remained the principal roots of the wider Elmaric family, while regional ancestry and speech became more varied. The nearby channel islands joined island and opposite-shore households through farming, fishing, marriage and violence before any later state called them dependencies.

Vorthara's old refuge expanded during and after this disorder. Displaced people joined the first Spire shelters and the ridge hamlets linked to them. The connected landscape offered concealment and routes to food, though neither made a newcomer safe at every door. Its later reputation as a place to disappear drew upon this longer continuity.

## Hearths, kinship and island difference, c. 1900–1400 BR

Over the Hearth ages, related speech and practice became recognisable across much of the central island network. People still named a home island, harbour, interior district or lineage before a broad family identity. The Elmarai formed one important heartland; other core communities lived among low shoals and on the island that became Ilamar's sanctuary. Larger [[Seravon before 350 BR|Seravon]] developed differently. Its coast, uplands and distant ends held related but increasingly distinct peoples and polities. A mainland writer's single island name could not describe their governments.

Fosterage, adoption, marriage, pilgrimage and seasonal crewing made relationships durable without creating uniform law. A child fostered across a strait could connect two ports at war. A pilot with kin on both shores could carry a ransom proposal when formal envoys could not safely land. The same networks transmitted food, craft and ritual knowledge. They also transmitted people against their will. Raiders seized captives for labour, sale, ransom and household incorporation. Captured women were often coerced into concubinage or marriage; children could be acknowledged as island kin while their mothers remained enslaved. Communities disagreed about redistribution, recognition and manumission. Shared Elmaric identity did not prevent one island from enslaving people another counted as kin.

At Ilamar, a resident sacred precinct developed into a meeting place for several island traditions. Custodians witnessed oaths, protected conduct and negotiated ransom. Because the island could not feed all visitors from its own ground, it also measured grain and heard convoy disputes. Its authority grew from others' need for credible exchange. An escapee reaching the precinct gained immediate protection but ordinarily still faced return, exchange or ransom. The sanctuary reduced some violence while serving an unequal captive economy.

## City-states, food and maritime power, c. 1400–800 BR

Harbours, island rulers and ship-owning groups grew more formidable during the City-State age. Denser populations made grain the heartland's strategic dependency. Fishing, orchards, vines, grazing and specialised crops mattered, but a poor harvest or closed channel could leave several busy ports short of bread. Some communities bought grain through treaties. Others held fortified fields, outer farming settlements or protected stores, sometimes on land already cultivated by other people. Existing farmers, Elmaric settlers, tenants and enslaved workers could labour within the same supply district under very unequal claims. Timber, pitch and masts brought another dependence on wooded shores.

These needs created convoy agreements, storehouses, cargo records and political competition over seemingly minor landings. A low island whose pilots knew one safe approach could determine whether a grain ship reached a great harbour. A ruler with fields but no vessels needed a captain; a captain with ships but no timber needed an outside supplier. Leagues formed for famine relief, seasonal convoy protection or war, and dissolved or changed partners when the immediate need passed.

Seravon's dynastic houses controlled different ports, grain stores and inland relationships. One harbour later absorbed into the Sermoran capital remembered a settler landing, stockade and seizure of shore as its founding. Older shore houses and their allies remembered its recovery by returned exiles, escaped captives, local fighters, defectors and pilots as a second founding. Farther west, councils and ship-owning associations were especially important in the communities later related to Saronika.

Near-mainland islands remained sites of divided claim. Their inhabitants might have fields and kin across a narrow channel while a foreign garrison held their landing. Mainland kings described those islands as natural dependencies; island leagues appealed to older settlement and sanctuary ties; settler houses praised their own fortified quarters as the beginning of civic life. All three claims could hide the work and residence of earlier communities.

Around 1000–900 BR, a Morlach follower of Thalassor reached the survivors near Caelovar after a tide-borne vision. Working with local knowledge and a wounded natural reefback altered by the old colonists, he helped create the mobile harbour of Marelith. Its living bearer and accompanying vessels crossed the central island circuit, with rarer long treks. This was a new polity among existing island peoples. Its later governments and tribute made it both a refuge and a dangerous neighbour.

Vorthara also acquired more specialised work over these centuries. Several waves of Qahuri mortuary households brought preparation, preservation and funerary contracts to the older refuge. Later practitioners adapted those crafts to a pass town that needed body handling, hazardous labour and guarded services.

## Raiding and the two outer theatres, c. 800–520 BR

Some Elmaric sailors made raiding a profession. Independent captains assembled crews for a venture, negotiated shares and found markets for cargo and captives. The same people might pilot a grain convoy, accept naval wages or conduct a raid in different seasons. Rulers and merchant houses also financed crews, but an independent share crew was a characteristic form. Warrants and claims of reprisal could protect a captain in one harbour while victims and their allies called the same attack piracy.

Two broad theatres drew islanders into the wars of mainland powers. The [[Gateway-Facing Outer Approaches before 350 BR|Gateway-facing approaches]] connected them with Qarzeth, Sarqen, Caleran and the southern coast. The [[Eastern Channels before 350 BR|Delkosi–Vashari-facing channels]] brought different claims from eastern kingdoms and the Arkenan–Saronikan ports. [[Near-Mainland Harbour Islands before 350 BR|Near-mainland islands]] in both theatres could have kin and fields across a narrow strait. Captives, credit and news crossed between these regions.

During the [[The Gateway Wars|Gateway Wars]], c. 674–520 BR, some Elmaric ships carried Caleran pilots, supplies or troops. Others served the Gateway Republic, its allies or themselves. Related captains could fight on opposing sides and later dispute captives, debts and lost cargo at Ilamar. The islands felt each stage. Caleran's blockade of Tammur about 666 BR turned back island grain buyers who had eaten at the Gateway's festival tables. In 608 Tashqar Ordan's war spread from the mainland into the islands and the inner gulf, and harbours changed sides within a season. About 551 Ilamar's custodians broke the seals on Esharati and Belqaran jars that had emptied without a thief, and put the Gateway capital's strange plenty on record. In the two years after the breach of 520 BR, Marelith's captains bought the rescued children whom Atherian soldiers had sold and resold many to Esharat's ransom funds. Once Qarzeth had fallen, Caleran pardoned or hired some useful former opponents and pursued less protected crews as pirates in waters it could reach. Its direct footholds were strongest toward the Gateway. In eastern channels, paid crews, treaties and isolated posts competed with other states. The republic did not occupy the Elmaric heartland.

## The late independent sea, c. 520–350 BR

Several maritime leagues and dynastic blocs pooled ships in the later Age of Stars. Their memberships shifted with rulers, marriages, grain seasons and foreign contracts. Seravon remained a large, predominantly Elmaric and politically divided island. Elmarai communities kept older councils, dialects and pilots while facing Atherian-speaking mainland ports. On the Saronikan side, gradual pre-imperial settlement had already changed some harbours; later conquest and institutional absorption were still to come.

The League of the Eastern Oar, formed against Vashar in 497 BR, brought the mainland's quarrels into island harbours. Its first custom came from the old Roll of Refused Hulls kept on Kelarion's reef: no member harbour closed its chain against a League ship in distress. After 491 Caleran's assessors fixed the League's ship money, and the chains began to rise against cities that had not paid. About 480 Pyrassan grain hulls broke on the rocks outside Velanthe's closed harbour, and about 475 the League held Elmarai pilot families as surety for Pyrassa's debt. In the autumn gale of 466, during the War of the Closed Chains, the harbours closed against the rebel hulls. Pyrassa's sailors swore that something rose under its breakwater that night, which coast people called the King beneath the Breakwater, until the city's own wardens cut the chain. Elmarai and shoal pilots led the refused ships to Marelith, which had no chain to raise. The Chain Peace of 461, sworn on Ilamar, gave every harbour's chain to its own wardens and made shelter in a storm the duty of every harbour, and a copy of the Roll was laid at Ilamar, whose custodians heard refuge cases from then on.

Marelith had meanwhile passed from its founding sacred dynasty to a covenant council and a captains' oligarchy. After hunger and a failed raiding season, residents threw the sacred ruling house into the sea. The council did not end the required human return; it transferred the cost onto people without civic standing, including captives and children. Later captains tied protection and political voice to recognised shares in vessels or indispensable installations. Fishing, escort, trade, salvage and repair coexisted with ransom, slavery and raids against other coasts. The moving city depended on a natural bearer and island provisioning relationships even while its white living works looked unlike any ordinary harbour. After 466 sailors called it the harbour without a chain, and refugees, deserters and proscribed families joined the raiders and slavers who had always used it.

At Vorthara, inhabited spaces inside the Spire and buildings against its foot joined the ascent market and ridge households in one substantial town. The Qahuri-rooted Pale Hand led a death-working quarter, and a distinct but adjoining group of cult courts housed competing claims on Menhir, Idols, Egg White patrons and other powers. Refuge families still sheltered fugitives; captive traders also found customers. Their conflict crossed routes and neighbourhoods. No common police, ruler or citywide law settled it. Gateway exiles opened a house of the Bronze-Faced Provider there about 505 BR, and it starved when the Pale Hand refused to feed its table with bodies. In 410 an Atherian financier's agents hunted fugitives from a mainland slave revolt up the Spire's paths, and several refuge households swore the **Kept Doors**, an oath to hold their thresholds together.

## The succession war and the 350 BR boundary

The Caleran civil war reached the islands first through Ilamar. In 373 BR Marovan Veyr, refused his ship money by the Oar ports and turned back from the royal table at Sarqen, took sanctuary there with his wife Selvena and the senator Arest Corven. The custodians refused to give him up, and Teren Casvilar, whose army bought its grain on Ilamar's certificates, did not attack. Veyr died there of fever in 371.

Ardel Veyr hired Marelith's independent captains during the Atherian succession wars. In 355 BR the city made an exceptional voyage east of its ordinary circuit to support his fleet among the central Thalassian anchorages. [[Istran Melvo]] destroyed Marelith and defeated Ardel's nearby main force in **one linked operation**. Melvo's crews held the city fast in the shallows with Pyrassa's harbour chain, and accounts describe a tempting prize ship admitted near the city before hidden attackers set its living works alight with sea-fire. Corial Draven, one of Teren Casvilar's killers, died aboard. Most residents died or entered captivity, among them families who had fled the Writing-In; island songs call the burning a second Kelarion. The torn-away mass grounded as the [[Black Reef]]; smaller surface and deep diasporas survived. Ardel reached Pyrassa with eleven hulls and the survivors he could take aboard, was admitted under the Chain Peace's law of refuge and lived out his life there under terms made in 353. The reefback's later fate remains unknown.

Around 350 BR, as Thalion concentrated imperial power, Atheria claimed routes around Vorthara and publicly called it a small lawful market. A temporary gathering accepted limited outside terms while local groups kept practical control of the inner town. In the islands, Caleran's older outer footholds, rival Elmaric blocs, Ilamar's precarious independence and contested mainland-facing harbours all remained.

## Households, belief and memory

An Elmaric household might include blood kin, fosterlings, adoptees, working crews, dependants and enslaved people. A recognised child of a captive mother could enter the island's civic body while the mother could not; another child might be denied acknowledgement. Women brought by force still transmitted knowledge, defended children, sought kin or escape and altered households that claimed to own them. Differences between local laws mattered more to these lives than a distant classification of all islanders as one people.

Island worship included household and harbour rites, local sacred places and Ilamar's plural precinct. Thalassor's followers were one presence among many. Marelith's pact had its own history and human abuses. Vorthara's cult courts were more openly heterogeneous still, with no citywide theology. A declared patron might be genuine, misidentified or invented.

Later songs and civic ceremonies remembered foundings, escapes, sea monsters, heroic raids and the brightness of Marelith. They could preserve real crossings or ruins while suppressing an enslaved mother, an abandoned worker or a burned household. Caelovar's transformations left dead and untransformed survivors beside those who entered the sea. Black Reef sightings preserved fears and possible encounters.
