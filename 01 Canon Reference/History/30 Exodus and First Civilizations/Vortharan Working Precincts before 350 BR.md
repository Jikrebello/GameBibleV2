# Vortharan Working Precincts before 350 BR

By 350 BR [[Vorthara before 350 BR|Vorthara]] contained recognisable working areas inside and around the Black Spire. They grew from refuge paths, workshops, markets and guarded premises. Boundaries shifted with the households and factions able to hold them, and several activities crossed the same street.

More travellers made a first market useful. Specialist crafts gathered where they could obtain clients and defend equipment. The death-working quarter and adjoining cult courts developed still later from immigrant expertise and ritual meeting places.

## Ascent market and inhabited Spire

The ascent and first market received grain, freight, travellers and people seeking a host. Carriers and sellers needed water and routes that could remain open even when neighbouring groups quarrelled. Passage onward became more conditional. Accessible Spire interiors held substantial habitation by 350 BR, as did buildings against its lower exterior and at its foot.

A buyer could reach a market route and still be refused a workshop. A fugitive might pass through an open ascent only to need a household's invitation for shelter. Specialist neighbours shared some main passages while controlling rooms, side routes and chambers. Deeper **occupied** thresholds could be guarded by a practitioner, gang or family that demanded payment, trust or a bargain.

The ridges and terraces supported plots, herds, medicinal plants and magical reagents. Repair yards and specialist workshops joined local crops to imported materials.

Some reagents came from plants native to the mountains; others required imported seed and skilled cultivation. Growers controlled particular beds, water and methods. Their produce moved through apothecaries and craft yards before reaching the mages who made Vorthara famous abroad. The work of carriers, gardeners, repairers and sellers tied more dangerous inner premises to the ordinary pass economy. A clash that blocked grain or water could bring a temporary coalition of people who otherwise would not share a court.

## The dead quarter and adjoining cult courts

The [[The Pale Hand before 350 BR|Pale Hand]] led a death-working neighbourhood whose Qahuri-rooted craft served funeral households, dangerous labour and body disposal. Mortuaries, preparation rooms and routes for corpse porters made it visible to traders and residents. Skilled outsiders joined or worked beside Qahuri-descended practitioners. Its control rested on clients, workshops and guarded entrances.

Adjoining courts offered ritual witnesses, lodging and access to many rival cults. Menhir-associated, Idol, Egg White and other houses occupied different rooms and loyalties. Most displayed a declared affiliation, though the claim on a sign might conceal the actual patron or a harmful practice. The meeting edge with the dead quarter was useful for funerary clients and brokers and therefore often contested. For a few years after about 505 BR a house of the Gateway's Bronze-Faced Provider stood on that edge, laying a nightly table under a bronze face; it closed when the Pale Hand's porters would not sell it bodies.

Older refuge rites gave some locations habitual meaning before paid witnesses and lodgings attracted additional houses. By 350 BR cult signs could be conspicuous. An Idol house, a Menhir-associated court or a patron room could seek clients openly, while withholding the true terms of its service. Houses competed over leases, offerings and adherents. A powerful one might drive out a weak neighbour; several rivals might cooperate briefly if one sought control of the whole quarter. Their balance arose from mutual deterrence.

## A conflict without a single district

The struggle between refuge households and captive traders ran through lodgings, markets and passages. Traders profited from a town without a general prohibition; defenders of refuge resisted them.

This was the town's most consequential spatial fault line because it could appear wherever routes and shelter met. A captive dealer might pay a gang for a warehouse, while a nearby family hid a person claimed by that dealer. Violence could spread to freight and food routes, drawing in otherwise uninvolved workshops.

The first line anyone swore along that fault was drawn in 410 BR. When an Atherian financier's agents came up the pass to buy fugitives from a mainland slave revolt, several refuge households barred their doors together and swore the **Kept Doors** on a single threshold. The oath bound those households only; the warehouses of the captive dealers stayed open a few doors away. [[Vorthara before 350 BR#The Spire Pursuit, 410 BR|The Spire Pursuit]] tells the story.
