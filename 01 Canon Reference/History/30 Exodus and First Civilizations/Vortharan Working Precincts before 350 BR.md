# Vortharan Working Precincts before 350 BR

By 350 BR [[Vorthara before 350 BR|Vorthara]] contained recognisable working areas inside and around the Black Spire. They grew from refuge paths, workshops, markets and guarded premises rather than a city plan. Their names here describe what visitors could find, not an official ward map. Boundaries shifted with the households and factions able to hold them, and several activities crossed the same street.

The earliest inhabited point lay within accessible Spire shelter or against it; ridge refuges spread from there. Repeated journeys for food, water and refuge gradually joined these clusters. More travellers made a first market useful. Specialist crafts gathered where they could obtain clients and defend equipment. The death-working quarter and adjoining cult courts developed still later from immigrant expertise and ritual meeting places. This sequence explains why areas stood together without requiring a founder to have laid out the whole town.

## Ascent market and inhabited Spire

The ascent and first market received grain, freight, travellers and people seeking a host. Carriers and sellers needed water and routes that could remain open even when neighbouring groups quarrelled. Passage onward became more conditional. Accessible Spire interiors held substantial habitation by 350 BR, as did buildings against its lower exterior and at its foot. A guarded inner room did not imply that anyone had explored or mastered the whole structure.

Arrival did not provide one public right of entry. A buyer could reach a market route and still be refused a workshop. A fugitive might pass through an open ascent only to need a household's invitation for shelter. Specialist neighbours shared some main passages while controlling rooms, side routes and chambers. Deeper **occupied** thresholds could be guarded by a practitioner, gang or family that demanded payment, trust or a bargain. The difference was one of local power and use, not a measured descent into the Spire's unknown original plan.

The ridges and terraces supported plots, herds, medicinal plants and magical reagents. Repair yards and specialist workshops joined local crops to imported materials. These areas helped the town live but did not free it from dependence on outside grain. Their users might defend a threshold together without accepting a common government.

Some reagents came from plants native to the mountains; others required imported seed and skilled cultivation. Growers controlled particular beds, water and methods. Their produce moved through apothecaries and craft yards before reaching the mages who made Vorthara famous abroad. The work of carriers, gardeners, repairers and sellers tied more dangerous inner premises to the ordinary pass economy. A clash that blocked grain or water could bring a temporary coalition of people who otherwise would not share a court.

## The dead quarter and adjoining cult courts

The [[The Pale Hand before 350 BR|Pale Hand]] led a death-working neighbourhood whose Qahuri-rooted craft served funeral households, dangerous labour and body disposal. Mortuaries, preparation rooms and routes for soulless corpse porters made it visible to traders and residents. Skilled outsiders joined or worked beside Qahuri-descended practitioners. Its control rested on clients, workshops and guarded entrances, not a city office.

The quarter's Qahuri origins lay in several arrivals of mortuary households, not an official colony from the Sacred Basin. The Pale Hand offered paid work and guarded space to capable outsiders as well as descendants of those migrants. Different crews prepared the dead, kept custody for families, studied residue, managed hazardous remains or animated empty bodies for labour. A family deciding to contract such work had reason to ask what would happen to a loved one's body; the association's public refusal to bind human souls mattered to that decision. Its authority remained local to premises and accepted contracts.

Adjoining courts offered ritual witnesses, lodging and access to many rival cults. Menhir-associated, Idol, Egg White and other houses occupied different rooms and loyalties. Most displayed a declared affiliation, though the claim on a sign might conceal the actual patron or a harmful practice. The meeting edge with the dead quarter was useful for funerary clients and brokers and therefore often contested. Neither side had a mandate over the other.

Older refuge rites gave some locations habitual meaning before paid witnesses and lodgings attracted additional houses. By 350 BR cult signs could be conspicuous. An Idol house, a Menhir-associated court or a patron room could seek clients openly, while withholding the true terms of its service. Houses competed over leases, offerings and adherents. A powerful one might drive out a weak neighbour; several rivals might cooperate briefly if one sought control of the whole quarter. Their balance arose from mutual deterrence, not a cult synod or neutral police. Claims of angelic, infernal or other patronage had to be judged individually; a sign did not prove that the named power had appeared.

## A conflict without a single district

The struggle between refuge households and captive traders ran through lodgings, markets and passages rather than between two neatly opposed wards. An escapee could find help at one threshold and be seized at another. Traders profited from a town without a general prohibition; defenders of refuge resisted them. Other factions tried to contain clashes that threatened access and business. No permanent settlement of that conflict, nor a fixed named ward map, existed by 350 BR.

This was the town's most consequential spatial fault line because it could appear wherever routes and shelter met. A captive dealer might pay a gang for a warehouse, while a nearby family hid a person claimed by that dealer. Neither faction could ask a general Vortharan court to settle the claim. Violence could spread to freight and food routes, drawing in otherwise uninvolved workshops. Their interventions might stop a fight without deciding the person's freedom. The later formal faction wards and Spire Truce cannot be read back from these shifting early protections.
