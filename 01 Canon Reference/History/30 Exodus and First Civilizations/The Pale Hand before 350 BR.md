# The Pale Hand before 350 BR

The Pale Hand was the leading association of death-workers in [[Vorthara before 350 BR|Vorthara]] by 350 BR. It developed from several arrivals of Qahuri mortuary households into a locally rooted faction serving clients who needed funerary care, preservation, hazardous body work and paid expertise. Its quarter stood beside, but was distinct from, Vorthara's plural cult courts. The Hand controlled its workshops and contracts; it held no city office and did not govern the Black Spire.

## Qahuri roots and Vortharan work

Ancient Qahur maintained professional funerary households, burial estates and different ways of preserving the dead. Workers, dependants, dissidents and specialists left that country at more than one time. Qahuri crises may have encouraged travel, but no recorded event dates the founding of the Vortharan quarter to a particular caravan. Migrants brought techniques and expectations about body custody into a town with its own older burial practices and a growing trade in dangerous services.

In Vorthara, preservation and funerary contracts became part of a broader craft. Crews prepared bodies for families, managed contaminated or hazardous remains, studied residue and supplied labour where living workers faced particular risks. Most ordinary animated porters were bodies moved without the deceased person's routed soul. Their movement could still offend relatives or violate a contract; soullessness did not settle who owned a body, who could sell its labour or what care a household had promised the dead.

The Pale Hand gathered equipment, protected premises and paying clients. Its Qahuri practitioners remained important, but useful skill mattered more than ancestry for entry or hire. Particular Folk druids and other Orphaned specialists brought non-identical burial and decay practices. An outside worker might find sanctuary from persecution, profit from Vorthara's market or seek distance from a former patron. The Hand could employ several methods on one job without making all members part of one religious school.

## Contracts, trust and neighbours

The faction publicly forbade the binding of a human soul. Bereaved families and ordinary paid members had reason to value a distinction between moving dead tissue and imprisoning the person who had died. Questions about procurement and custody could threaten the Hand's contracts even when its visible work met its own rule. Its promise gave clients a standard by which to challenge a worker who exceeded the agreed handling of a body.

Mortuaries, preparation spaces and defended entrances made the quarter recognisable. Corpse porters used marked working routes, while living carriers, witnesses and families moved through the same neighbourhood. The adjoining cult courts drew some of the same clients, including people seeking funerary patronage or witnesses to a bargain. They followed many incompatible powers and did not answer to the Hand. Cooperation at the shared edge could be profitable; disputes over a body or patron claim could be severe.

By 350 BR the Pale Hand was a durable exception to Vorthara's mostly informal faction landscape, not evidence that the whole town had become a council of magical schools. Its present-day ward, materials and hidden practices cannot all be assumed to have existed unchanged in this earlier period.
