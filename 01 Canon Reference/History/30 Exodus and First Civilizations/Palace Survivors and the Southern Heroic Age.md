# Palace Survivors and the Southern Heroic Age

The southern heroic age developed among communities living through the collapse of Giant government and the long struggles that followed it. Its earliest remembered figures belonged to the Orphaned peoples who remained outside the Glass refuge. Later generations included incoming Glass-descended settlers and the mixed households formed through their encounters with established societies. Between the Great Unbinding and the growth of maritime Atheria, the same coast could contain a ruined palace, a surviving Giant stronghold and human settlements seeking independence from both its heirs and their new rivals.

## The palace world

Giant kingdoms drew together estates, mines, workshops and ports through ruling households. Their power depended on the movement of food and materials. Cultivators supplied grain; transport households carried it to workers and soldiers; craftspeople maintained equipment whose production required knowledge acquired over generations. Enslaved people performed much of this work, alongside dependants and privileged servants with greater freedom within royal service.

Palaces concentrated stores and learning. Astronomical observation helped organise cultivation and voyages, while ceremonies made the same observations support claims of inherited rule. Captured chiefs and workers appeared in those ceremonies as evidence of royal power. Marriages joined ruling houses, redistributed access to productive districts and created competing claims among their descendants.

[[Arsuvan - The Giant Kingdom of the Aurin|Arsuvan]] provides a particularly clear regional history. Its river estates, upland connections and coastal trade supported Sarven and the houses of Iskaren and Velketh. Its neighbours and trading partners possessed governments of their own. The wider palace world included many kingdoms within the Five Imperial Horizons, with different relationships between inherited bodily power, military service and economic dependence.

## Collapse and survival, c. 2300–2165 BR

During the Great Unbinding, prophetic warnings and signs accompanied revolts, defections, Giant civil wars and heavenly intervention. The loss of a distant supplier could weaken a palace before an enemy reached its walls. Refugees carried news, skills and demands for shelter into districts already struggling to feed their inhabitants. Some royal houses seized seed grain to maintain troops and thereby damaged the following harvest.

Arsuvan disintegrated around 2260 BR. Arveth died in the fighting, and Lethra's boat crews helped carry families and seed beyond his control. Rival claimants burned stores and removed skilled captives before abandoning parts of Sarven. The [[Peoples of the Ancient Aurin|Enathi, Serathi and Vardeni]] developed through local survival and rebuilding in the generations that followed.

Elsewhere, detached royal houses retained smaller possessions. A Giant could continue to command a quarry or fortified landing after the kingdom that had appointed his ancestors ceased to exist. Such rulers demanded food and labour from a reduced population. Some human households entered their service to escape a more immediate enemy; others moved beyond their reach or allied with neighbouring settlements against them.

The Glass People remained in their refuge during much of this recovery. Their century of shared life ended with the Prophet's death around 2165 BR. Outside, several generations had already repaired boats, reopened fields, buried relatives and fought over abandoned works. The later southern settlers encountered the descendants of those survivors.

## Work beyond the palace

Agriculture continued wherever households could retain seed, animals, tools and access to water. The disappearance of royal supervision released some communities from exactions while leaving others without the labour needed for large repairs. A smaller cultivated area could feed a village even when the extensive works of its former rulers could no longer be maintained.

Craft knowledge travelled with people. Smiths and carpenters worked at new landings, taught relatives and adapted salvaged equipment. Bronze, iron and stone remained in use according to the supplies and skills available locally. A ruined workshop could provide material for a new one without preserving its former scale of production.

Households also retained ceremonies and observations detached from their royal setting. They used seasonal knowledge inherited from palace workers and changed the stories explaining who had discovered it. Songs could honour a human guide where an older ruler had claimed personal mastery of the route. Later Atherian traditions drew upon this surviving knowledge through teachers, marriages and travelling craftspeople.

## Settlers and the surviving rulers, c. 2130–1900 BR

Southern separation from the Near-Glass communities unfolded through repeated departures. New forts and maritime households sought fields, useful crossings and secure winter quarters. They entered a country inhabited by independent settlements as well as dependants of surviving dynasts. Local knowledge of channels, stores and mountain approaches often determined whether an incoming force could remain.

Alliances brought some households relief from royal collection parties. Other settlers seized the land or captives they had helped recover. A victory over a Giant could therefore replace an old ruler with human occupiers. Marriage, adoption and shared work created more durable communities alongside these conquests. [[Southern Settlement and Maritime Atheria]] follows that formation in greater detail.

The [[The Siege and the Returnings|siege of High Harbour]], approximately 1980–1960 BR, belonged to this later heroic world. Orphaned settlements, Glass-descended captains and mixed shore households assembled against a surviving High Giant court. Their quarrels and supernatural allies affected the war; the sack and subsequent voyages reshaped the victors' households as well as the defeated country. Its return stories preserve rescues, coercive hospitality, revenge and the choice to settle among strangers.

## Remembering the heroic age

Later Atherian tellers fitted older heroes into maritime genealogies and city histories. Some inherited genuine memories of settler victories. Others appropriated figures already honoured by Orphaned communities. Claims that a hero belonged to the Glass migration consequently require comparison with the people and circumstances of the particular tale.

Descriptions of monstrous rulers also vary. Some preserve encounters with genuine Giants; others enlarge the ancestry or bodily power of human dynasts. The death of one ruler could end a stronghold's command without ending every claim made in its name. At High Harbour, the supposed ancestral king continued to appear after the living ruler had fallen, urging the survivors to fight on.

## A later maritime society

Between about 1900 and 1400 BR, connected harbours and production districts sustained a growing Atherian maritime world. Families who had once depended on a single landing acquired partners farther along the coast. Voyages supported shipbuilding, feasts, apprenticeships and public work. Their fortunes remained vulnerable to the decisions of powerful patrons.

The disruptions around 1600–1400 BR arose within these later networks. Kassilon's seizure of Erenost's grain, around 1550–1500 BR, damaged an established system of supply and cooperation. It followed the principal Giant collapse by many generations. The history of [[Arkenan and Saronikan Cities before the Empire|the eastern cities]] traces how households responded to such failures and developed institutions that outlasted individual sea-kings.
