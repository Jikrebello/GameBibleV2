# Wars of the Inheritance

The **Wars of the Inheritance** divided Eryndor III's conquests between 684 and about 650 BR. The king had governed through personal loyalty, captured treasuries, existing provincial officials and agreements that did not outlive him. His death left two nominal heirs, several experienced commanders, allied rulers in possession of different parts of the army and state, and his embalmed body. Within months a voice in the king's tomb at Delkos claimed to be Eryndor alive. It disowned every heir and promised the same provinces to rival captains, and for a generation the living fought over what the dead king had promised.

The wars produced four principal centres in the known world: the Vasharan Crown of Roads and Dawns, Demeran Kavos's Ortheian kingdom, Serevan's maritime court at Delkos and Neresh of Qemaret's temporary southern dominion. A fifth, thinly held realm arose beyond the well-described eastern countries. Local cities, temples and dynasties survived among them and often determined which claimant could feed an army or collect revenue.

## The first settlement

Eryndor died at Arqesh in 684 BR during a Qahuri funerary rite performed at his deathbed. His Vashari wife, Parena Tiravan, was pregnant. Ortheian commanders recognised Eryndor's adult half-brother and Parena's unborn child as possible joint heirs. This arrangement allowed officers to issue commands and coin in the royal name while they divided troops, treasuries and provinces.

Arezan Tiravan controlled the eastern roads and much of the surviving Arqeshite administration. In the same year he issued the **Decree of Roads and Houses**, which allowed selected deported peoples to restore sanctuaries, retrieve approved sacred objects and return to recognised property. The decree weakened Ubar military households, reopened neglected routes and brought restored communities under Vasharan supremacy. It permitted the Jirahar return to Qiryath while leaving many Arqeshite families displaced by the conquest without a comparable restoration.

## The Voice at Delkos

Eryndor's officers prepared to send his embalmed body west to Ortheia. **Serevan of Delkos**, commander of the allied fleet, who had paid for the king's last rite, diverted the procession to Delkos. With the body went the portable images of the Ubar kings, which Eryndor had carried as the regalia of a lawful successor.

Within months, veterans admitted to the tomb-hall came out saying the king had spoken to them. They described a curtained room heavy with cedar oil and natron, Kassur III's gilded likenesses ranged along the wall, and a voice from behind the curtain in Eryndor's own upland accent. It called old soldiers by their camp names, remembered a horse drowned at a river crossing and asked after a dead companion's widow. It declared that the king had not died and had no heir. It promised commands, harbours and provinces, and Serevan's scribes wrote the promises down and sealed them in the king's name. Men who had marched with Eryndor for twenty years believed the voice because it knew things only his companions knew.

Every party had its own account of it. Serevan's court called it the king, kept by the Qahuri rite, and governed as his steward. The Demeranid camp called it a puppet play staged by Delkosi priests. Arqeshite courtiers in Delkosi service said they recognised the presence of the Crowned Countenance, which had attended Kassur's images under the river kings. The ports simply called it the **Voice at Delkos**. The corpse drew veterans, embassies and royal gifts, and possession of it made Serevan's claim to keep Eryndor's court the strongest in the east.

## Arezan and Vashar

Arezan converted a wartime coalition into the Vasharan Empire. His forces held the Gate House at Arqesh, the highland roads and most of the Ubar basin. Canal officials remained at work because the new rulers depended upon them for food and transport. Vashari houses received military commands, while subject cities retained councils or rulers where cooperation reduced the cost of occupation.

House Tiravan defended Parena's claims in public, but Arezan also governed in his own right. His sister Mithara preserved Parena's attendants and arranged marriages for surviving members of her household. The stories these families carried later became important sources for the king's final years.

## Demeran and the western army

**Demeran Kavos**, Eryndor's senior western general, controlled Ortheia and the largest body of soldiers able to return home. He claimed to act for the royal heirs while eliminating officers who resisted his appointments. Caleran and several Compact cities used his absences and financial difficulties to recover their assemblies and fleets.

The Voice made the heirs worthless. A reigning king needed no half-brother and no infant, and once Delkos proclaimed Eryndor alive, the heirs in Demeran's keeping could buy him nothing and might still rally his enemies. Demeran eventually imprisoned Parena and her child and ordered their deaths. Eryndor's half-brother and other nominal heirs were also killed during the struggle. Vasharan accounts made Demeran solely responsible for the extinction of the household; Ortheian histories accused Arezan of withholding the forces that might have rescued them, and Ortheian singers blamed the Voice that had disowned them. The murders removed the principal symbols around whom a general settlement could have formed.

Demeran retained Ortheia, but his authority over the city world contracted. Sailors paid in sealed promises rather than silver sold their ships or entered Caleran service. Exiles from Olyssar and other conquered cities returned with claims to land and revenge. Caleran restored meetings of the Celestial Compact and turned the recovery into the beginning of its republican predominance.

## Serevan and Delkos

Serevan governed through a mixed maritime court. His followers included Atherian sailors, Arqeshite clerks, Vashari officers, local island households and discharged soldiers. Delkos adopted court dress and ceremonial practices from several eastern kingdoms while preserving Atherian as the language of military command.

The kingdom moved grain, soldiers, royal brides and diplomatic missions through the eastern sea. Its fleets fought Demeran, protected friendly ports and compelled less powerful islands to supply ships. The Voice was generous with the eastern sea. It promised the same Qahuri approaches to Neresh and to a Delkosi admiral, and the same harbours to captains on opposite sides of a war. Every sealed promise gave a commander reason to keep fighting, and petitioners competed in praise of the dead king to win the next. Serevan's descendants lost territory through dynastic war, but their officer houses and naval customs survived the contraction of the royal state. Officer families kept the sealed promises as heirlooms long after they had lost force, and some carried them abroad.

Nerethan of Delkos descended from one of these later officer houses. His arrival at the Second Harbour in 499 BR brought an inherited Successor tradition into the Gateway country. The surviving genealogy traces House Nerethan through Delkosi officers.

## Neresh and the southern crowns

**Neresh of Qemaret**, a Qahuri-born officer in Eryndor's army, held selected Qahuri approaches and crown towns. He had brought the masters of the White Measure to Eryndor's deathbed, and he used garrisons, military marriages and Eryndor's local title to claim a place within the divided Basin. Temple and irrigation houses still controlled the land, grain and ritual knowledge needed for government.

Neresh's successors never ruled the whole of Qahur. Crown houses expelled some garrisons and absorbed others through marriage and service. Atherian coin forms, military colonies and court art entered the Basin, while Qahuri astronomy, funerary craft and royal ceremony influenced Delkos and Vashar. Prisoners, artisans, wives and soldiers carried this exchange alongside royal embassies.

## The sealing of the tomb

Around 657 BR the Voice refused Neresh a grant it had once promised him. Neresh answered by bringing the Qemaret masters who had performed Eryndor's rite before envoys of the eastern courts. They testified that the rite had failed at the deathbed, that the king had gone the road of all the dead, and that the speaker at Delkos was no more Eryndor than the speaking tomb of **Nemeret the Pure**, a notorious counterfeit of their own country. Delkosi loyalists called the masters bribed. Their testimony still emptied the sealed promises of force, because no captain could now show one to a garrison and be obeyed.

Around 655 BR Serevan's grandson walled the corpse and Kassur's images into the tomb. The living successors settled their borders by about 650. The walled tomb drew veterans' families for a time; later wars obscured its location, and it has never been found.

## Consequences

The wars ended the prospect of one eastern empire. Vashar became the strongest continuous successor and protected the rebuilding of Second Qiryath. Ortheia retained its monarchy but lost mastery of the Compact. Delkos created an enduring maritime inheritance. Qahur remained divided among native crowns strengthened or weakened by foreign marriages and garrisons. The embalmers of the Basin carried a reputation for speaking counterfeits into every court of the eastern sea.

By about 650 BR, with Caleran already fighting the first of the Gateway Wars over the grain market at Tammur, sailors, coin, siege knowledge, sealed promises and royal claims moved through an eastern sea permanently altered by Eryndor's conquest and its failure to remain whole.
