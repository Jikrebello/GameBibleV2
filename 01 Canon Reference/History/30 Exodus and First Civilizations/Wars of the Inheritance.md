# Wars of the Inheritance

The **Wars of the Inheritance** divided Eryndor III's conquests between 684 and about 650 BR. The king had governed through personal loyalty, captured treasuries, existing provincial officials and agreements that did not outlive him. His death left two nominal heirs, several experienced commanders and allied rulers in possession of different parts of the army and state. None could command the whole inheritance.

The wars produced four principal centres in the known world: the Vasharan Crown of Roads and Dawns, Demeran Kavos's Ortheian kingdom, Serevan's maritime court at Delkos and Neresh of Qemaret's temporary southern dominion. A fifth, thinly held realm arose beyond the well-described eastern countries. Local cities, temples and dynasties survived among them and often determined which claimant could feed an army or collect revenue.

## The first settlement

Eryndor died at Arqesh in 684 BR. His Vashari wife, Parena Tiravan, was pregnant. Ortheian commanders recognised Eryndor's adult half-brother and Parena's unborn child as possible joint heirs. This arrangement allowed officers to issue commands and coin in the royal name while they divided troops, treasuries and provinces.

Arezan Tiravan controlled the eastern roads and much of the surviving Arqeshite administration. In the same year he issued the **Decree of Roads and Houses**, which allowed selected deported peoples to restore sanctuaries, retrieve approved sacred objects and return to recognised property. The decree weakened Ubar military households, reopened neglected routes and brought restored communities under Vasharan supremacy. It permitted the Jirahar return to Qiryath while leaving many Arqeshite families displaced by the conquest without a comparable restoration.

The funeral became another contest. Eryndor's officers prepared to send his embalmed body west to Ortheia. **Serevan of Delkos**, commander of the allied fleet, diverted the procession to Delkos. The corpse remained there for several generations and attracted veterans, embassies and royal gifts. Later wars obscured its location. Possession of the body strengthened Serevan's claim to preserve Eryndor's court, but the corpse contained neither the king's soul nor his authority.

## Arezan and Vashar

Arezan converted a wartime coalition into the Vasharan Empire. His forces held the Gate House at Arqesh, the highland roads and most of the Ubar basin. Canal officials remained at work because the new rulers depended upon them for food and transport. Vashari houses received military commands, while subject cities retained councils or rulers where cooperation reduced the cost of occupation.

House Tiravan defended Parena's claims in public, but Arezan also governed in his own right. His sister Mithara preserved Parena's attendants and arranged marriages for surviving members of her household. The stories these families carried later became important sources for the king's final years.

## Demeran and the western army

**Demeran Kavos**, Eryndor's senior western general, controlled Ortheia and the largest body of soldiers able to return home. He claimed to act for the royal heirs while eliminating officers who resisted his appointments. Caleran and several Compact cities used his absences and financial difficulties to recover their assemblies and fleets.

Demeran eventually imprisoned Parena and her child and ordered their deaths. Eryndor's half-brother and other nominal heirs were also killed during the struggle. Vasharan accounts made Demeran solely responsible for the extinction of the household; Ortheian histories accused Arezan of withholding the forces that might have rescued them. The murders removed the principal symbols around whom a general settlement could have formed.

Demeran retained Ortheia, but his authority over the city world contracted. Unpaid sailors sold ships or entered Caleran service. Exiles from Olyssar and other conquered cities returned with claims to land and revenge. Caleran restored meetings of the Celestial Compact and turned the recovery into the beginning of its republican predominance.

## Serevan and Delkos

Serevan governed through a mixed maritime court. His followers included Atherian sailors, Arqeshite clerks, Vashari officers, local island households and discharged soldiers. Delkos adopted court dress and ceremonial practices from several eastern kingdoms while preserving Atherian as the language of military command.

The kingdom moved grain, soldiers, royal brides and diplomatic missions through the eastern sea. Its fleets fought Demeran, protected friendly ports and compelled less powerful islands to supply ships. Serevan's descendants lost territory through dynastic war, but their officer houses and naval customs survived the contraction of the royal state.

Nerethan of Delkos descended from one of these later officer houses. His arrival at the Second Harbour in 499 BR brought an inherited Successor tradition into the Gateway country. The surviving genealogy traces House Nerethan through Delkosi officers and contains no blood descent from Eryndor or Serevan.

## Neresh and the southern crowns

**Neresh of Qemaret**, a Qahuri-born officer in Eryndor's army, held selected Qahuri approaches and crown towns. He used garrisons, military marriages and Eryndor's local title to claim a place within the divided Basin. Temple and irrigation houses still controlled the land, grain and ritual knowledge needed for government.

Neresh's successors never ruled the whole of Qahur. Crown houses expelled some garrisons and absorbed others through marriage and service. Atherian coin forms, military colonies and court art entered the Basin, while Qahuri astronomy, funerary craft and royal ceremony influenced Delkos and Vashar. Prisoners, artisans, wives and soldiers carried this exchange alongside royal embassies.

## Consequences

The wars ended the prospect of one eastern empire. Vashar became the strongest continuous successor and protected the rebuilding of Second Qiryath. Ortheia retained its monarchy but lost mastery of the Compact. Delkos created an enduring maritime inheritance. Qahur remained divided among native crowns strengthened or weakened by foreign marriages and garrisons.

The new states also preserved the violence of their birth. Parena and the heirs were murdered. Cities were besieged, households deported and fields granted to soldiers. At the same time, new ports, workshops, libraries and mixed households developed under the Successors. By the opening of the Gateway Wars around 650 BR, sailors, coin, siege knowledge and royal claims moved through an eastern sea permanently altered by Eryndor's conquest and its failure to remain whole.
