# Wars of the Inheritance

The **Wars of the Inheritance** divided Eryndor III's conquests between 684 and about 650 BR. The king had governed through personal loyalty, captured treasuries, existing provincial officials and agreements that did not outlive him. His death left an adult half-brother and an unborn child as nominal heirs, several experienced commanders, allied rulers in possession of different parts of the army and state, and his embalmed body, which was already aboard his fleet commander's flagship. Within months a voice in the king's tomb at Delkos claimed to be Eryndor alive. It disowned every heir and promised the same provinces to rival captains, and for a generation the living fought over what the dead king had promised.

The wars produced four principal centres in the known world: the Vasharan Crown of Roads and Dawns, Demeran Kavos's Ortheian kingdom, Serevan's maritime court at Delkos and Neresh of Qemaret's temporary southern dominion. A fifth, thinly held realm arose beyond the well-described eastern countries, among garrisons a closed wood had cut off from the king. Local cities, temples and dynasties survived among them and often determined which claimant could feed an army or collect revenue.

## The first settlement

Eryndor died in 684 BR aboard Serevan of Delkos's flagship in the roads of Nemeru, during a Qahuri funerary rite performed in the ship's hold. He had been waiting for a wind to take him home to Ortheia. His Vashari wife, Parena Tiravan, was at Arqesh and pregnant. Ortheian commanders recognised Eryndor's adult half-brother, who was with the regent Demeran Kavos in the uplands, and Parena's unborn child as possible joint heirs. This arrangement allowed officers to issue commands and coin in the royal name while they divided troops, treasuries and provinces.

Arezan Tiravan controlled the eastern roads and much of the surviving Arqeshite administration, whose canal officials had served him since the last Ubar king sealed the Gate House's water books over to him at Durqesh in 688. In 684 he issued the **Decree of Roads and Houses**, which allowed selected deported peoples to restore sanctuaries, retrieve approved sacred objects and return to recognised property. The decree weakened Ubar military households, reopened neglected routes and brought restored communities under Vasharan supremacy. It permitted the Jirahar return to Qiryath while leaving many Arqeshite families displaced by the conquest without a comparable restoration.

## The Voice at Delkos

Eryndor's officers expected the body to go west to Ortheia. **Serevan of Delkos**, commander of the allied fleet, who had paid for the king's last rite and on whose ship he had died, waited for the wind and sailed for his own harbour. With the body went the portable images of the Ubar kings, which had answered in Eryndor's voice since 688 and which he had carried as the regalia of a lawful successor, and the descendants of the Terys families whom the king had meant to bring home.

Within months, veterans admitted to the tomb-hall came out saying the king had spoken to them. They described a curtained room heavy with cedar oil and natron, Kassur III's gilded likenesses ranged along the wall, and a voice from behind the curtain in Eryndor's own upland accent. It called old soldiers by their camp names, remembered a horse drowned at a river crossing and asked after a dead companion's widow. It declared that the king had not died and had no heir. It promised commands, harbours and provinces, and Serevan's scribes wrote the promises down and sealed them in the king's name. Men who had marched with Eryndor for twenty years believed the voice because it knew things only his companions knew.

Every party had its own account of it. Serevan's court called it the king, kept by the Qahuri rite, and governed as his steward. The Demeranid camp called it a puppet play staged by Delkosi priests. Arqeshite courtiers in Delkosi service said they recognised the presence of the Crowned Countenance, which had attended Kassur's images under the river kings and had learned the king's voice while he lived. The ports simply called it the **Voice at Delkos**. The corpse drew veterans, embassies and royal gifts, and possession of it made Serevan's claim to keep Eryndor's court the strongest in the east.

## Arezan and Vashar

Arezan converted a wartime coalition into the Vasharan Empire. His forces held the Gate House at Arqesh, the highland roads and most of the Ubar basin. Canal officials remained at work because the new rulers depended upon them for food and transport, and because the water books they kept now bore Kassur's seal over to the Vashari king. Kassur himself lived as Arezan's guarded guest at Kavresh until about 676. Vashari houses received military commands, while subject cities retained councils or rulers where cooperation reduced the cost of occupation.

Parena was carried up the Dawn Road to her mother Mithara at Kavresh and there, in the late spring of 684, bore a daughter, **Mehrava**. House Tiravan defended the child's claims in public, but Arezan also governed in his own right and never sent an army west in her name. Mithara kept mother and daughter in her valley household and arranged marriages for Parena's surviving attendants. The stories these families carried later became important sources for the king's final years. Around 668 Arezan's son and successor, Daryav I, married Mehrava, and every later Tiravan king who descended from her could call himself heir of the Far Road.

## Demeran and the western army

**Demeran Kavos**, regent in the west since 704, controlled Ortheia, the mints and the largest body of soldiers already at home. He claimed to act for the royal heirs while eliminating officers who resisted his appointments. Caleran and several Compact cities used his absences and financial difficulties to recover their assemblies and fleets.

The Voice made the heirs worthless. A reigning king needed no half-brother and no infant, and once Delkos proclaimed Eryndor alive, the half-brother in Demeran's keeping could buy him nothing and might still rally his enemies. Demeran had him killed. He sent agents up the Dawn Road after Parena and her daughter; Arezan's road guards took them at Durqesh and hanged them in the gate. Other nominal heirs were killed during the struggle. Vasharan accounts made Demeran a would-be killer of children. Ortheian histories called Mehrava a Vashari pretence, citing the Voice, which had declared the king heirless, and the White Measure's fasts, under which, they said, the king could have fathered no child that year. Vashari households answered that Parena had been with the king at the healing spring in the summer of 685, before the regimen began. The killings removed the principal symbols around whom a general settlement could have formed.

Demeran retained Ortheia, but his authority over the city world contracted. Sailors paid in sealed promises rather than silver sold their ships or entered Caleran service. Exiles from Olyssar and other conquered cities returned with claims to land and revenge. Caleran restored meetings of the Celestial Compact and turned the recovery into the beginning of its republican predominance.

## Serevan and Delkos

Serevan governed through a mixed maritime court. His followers included Atherian sailors, Arqeshite clerks, Vashari officers, local island households, discharged soldiers and the Terys families from the Nemeru yards, who had been promised Ortheia and were given Delkos. Delkos adopted court dress and ceremonial practices from several eastern kingdoms while preserving Atherian as the language of military command.

The kingdom moved grain, soldiers, royal brides and diplomatic missions through the eastern sea. Its fleets fought Demeran, protected friendly ports and compelled less powerful islands to supply ships. The Voice was generous with the eastern sea. It promised the same Qahuri approaches to Neresh and to a Delkosi admiral, and the same harbours to captains on opposite sides of a war. Every sealed promise gave a commander reason to keep fighting, and petitioners competed in praise of the dead king to win the next. Serevan's descendants lost territory through dynastic war, but their officer houses and naval customs survived the contraction of the royal state. Officer families kept the sealed promises as heirlooms long after they had lost force, and some carried them abroad.

Nerethan of Delkos descended from one of these later officer houses. His arrival at the Second Harbour in 499 BR brought an inherited Successor tradition into the Gateway country. The surviving genealogy traces House Nerethan through Delkosi officers.

## Neresh and the southern crowns

**Neresh of Qemaret**, a Qahuri-born officer in Eryndor's army, held selected Qahuri approaches and crown towns. He had brought the masters of the White Measure to Eryndor's deathbed under the pledge the king had given in 695 to the House of the Returning Cargo, whose grain had fed his army up the river. He used garrisons, military marriages and Eryndor's local title to claim a place within the divided Basin. Temple and irrigation houses still controlled the land, grain and ritual knowledge needed for government.

Neresh's successors never ruled the whole of Qahur. Crown houses expelled some garrisons and absorbed others through marriage and service. Atherian coin forms, military colonies and court art entered the Basin, while Qahuri astronomy, funerary craft and royal ceremony influenced Delkos and Vashar. Prisoners, artisans, wives and soldiers carried this exchange alongside royal embassies.

## The garrisons beyond the wood

The garrisons Eryndor had placed beyond the wooded country east of the Vashari highlands were cut off in 686, when the road behind his army closed. No relief, pay or orders reached them. Their commanders married into the households they ruled, struck coin in the dead king's name for a few years and then in their own, and treated with the mobile peoples of the farther roads as equals. Traders who reached them by the long way round reported Ortheian drill, Arqeshite clerks and a horror of felling trees. Their history survives only in such reports and in their coins.

## The sealing of the tomb

Around 657 BR the Voice refused Neresh a grant it had once promised him. Neresh answered by bringing the Qemaret masters who had performed Eryndor's rite before envoys of the eastern courts. They testified that the rite had failed in the ship's hold, where they had no house to close the king into; that the king had gone the road of all the dead; and that the speaker at Delkos was no more Eryndor than the speaking tomb of **Nemeret the Pure**, a notorious counterfeit of their own country. Delkosi loyalists called the masters bribed, and some said that the dead lender of Qemaret had sent them because her share in the king had never been paid. Their testimony still emptied the sealed promises of force, because no captain could now show one to a garrison and be obeyed.

Around 655 BR Serevan's grandson walled the corpse and Kassur's images into the tomb. The living successors settled their borders by about 650. The walled tomb drew veterans' families for a time; later wars obscured its location, and it has never been found.

## Consequences

The wars ended the prospect of one eastern empire. Vashar became the strongest continuous successor and protected the rebuilding of Second Qiryath. Its kings kept the water books of the last Ubar king and, from Mehrava's marriage onward, the blood of the Far-Road King by their own reckoning. Ortheia retained its monarchy but lost mastery of the Compact. Delkos created an enduring maritime inheritance. Qahur remained divided among native crowns strengthened or weakened by foreign marriages and garrisons. The embalmers of the Basin carried a reputation for speaking counterfeits into every court of the eastern sea.

By about 650 BR, with Caleran already fighting the first of the Gateway Wars over the grain market at Tammur, sailors, coin, siege knowledge, sealed promises and royal claims moved through an eastern sea permanently altered by Eryndor's conquest and its failure to remain whole.
