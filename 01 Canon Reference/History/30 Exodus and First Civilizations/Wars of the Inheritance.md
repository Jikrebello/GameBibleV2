# Wars of the Inheritance

The **Wars of the Inheritance** divided Eryndor III's conquests between 684 and about 650 BR. The king had governed through personal loyalty, captured treasuries, existing provincial officials and agreements that did not outlive him. His death left a half-brother ruling Ortheia under a borrowed seal, a seven-year-old daughter in a Vashari valley, several experienced commanders, allied rulers in possession of different parts of the army and state, and his embalmed body, which the grandchildren of the Belyra deportees sailed away while his commanders argued ashore. Within months a voice in the king's tomb at Delkos claimed to be Eryndor alive. It disowned every heir and promised the same provinces to rival captains, and for a generation the living fought over what the dead king had promised.

The wars produced four principal centres in the known world: the Vasharan Crown of Roads and Dawns, Demeran Kavos's Ortheian kingdom, Serevan's maritime court at Delkos and Neresh of Qemaret's temporary southern dominion. A fifth, thinly held realm arose beyond the well-described eastern countries, among garrisons a closed wood had cut off from the king. Local cities, temples and dynasties survived among them and often determined which claimant could feed an army or collect revenue.

## The death at Nemeru

Eryndor died in 684 BR aboard the flagship of the allied fleet in the roads of Nemeru, during a Qahuri funerary rite performed in the ship's hold. He had been waiting for a wind to take him home to Ortheia. His Vashari wife, Parena Tiravan, was at Arqesh; their daughter Mehrava, born in the camp before Arqesh in 691, was with her grandmother at Kavresh. No settlement followed. In Ortheia the upland assembly acclaimed the elder half-brother who had kept the royal seal since 702. In the east each commander went on issuing orders and coin in the dead king's name while he secured the troops, treasuries and provinces within his reach.

Arezan Tiravan controlled the eastern roads and much of the surviving Arqeshite administration, whose canal officials had served him since the last Ubar king sealed the Gate House's water books over to him at Durqesh in 688. In 684 he issued the **Decree of Roads and Houses**, which allowed selected deported peoples to restore sanctuaries, retrieve approved sacred objects and return to recognised property. The decree weakened Ubar military households, reopened neglected routes and brought restored communities under Vasharan supremacy. It permitted the Jirahar return to Qiryath while leaving many Arqeshite families displaced by the conquest without a comparable restoration.

## The Voice at Delkos

Eryndor's officers expected the body to go west to Ortheia. They were ashore at Nemeru, quarrelling over the treasury, when the wind came round on the twelfth night after his death. The flagship had been built in the Nemeru yards by the descendants of the Belyra deportees, and they crewed it. With the king who had promised them a home dead, they expected the yards and the rope again. They cut the cables and sailed, and the transports carrying their families followed. Aboard were the body, the Qemaret masters and the portable images of the Ubar kings. They steered for Delkos, the fleet's home port, reckoning that no harbour would chain out a king's funeral ship. **Serevan of Delkos**, commander of the allied fleet, who had paid for the king's last rite, followed in a dispatch galley and found his harbour already keeping a king. The Belyra families became the tomb's doorkeepers, and Serevan its steward.

Within months, veterans admitted to the tomb-hall came out saying the king had spoken to them. They described a curtained room heavy with cedar oil and natron, Kassur III's gilded likenesses ranged along the wall, and a voice from behind the curtain in Eryndor's own upland accent. The images, which had mocked the king in a dead companion's voice for three years, stood silent. It called old soldiers by their camp names, remembered a horse drowned at a river crossing and asked after a dead companion's widow. It declared that the king had not died and had no heir. It promised commands, harbours and provinces, and Serevan's scribes wrote the promises down and sealed them in the king's name. Men who had marched with Eryndor for twenty years believed the voice because it knew things only his companions knew.

Every party had its own account of it. Serevan's court called it the king, kept by the Qahuri rite, and governed as his steward. The Demeranid camp called it a puppet play staged by Delkosi priests. Arqeshite courtiers in Delkosi service said they recognised the presence of the Crowned Countenance, which had attended Kassur's images under the river kings and had learned the king's voice while he lived. The ports simply called it the **Voice at Delkos**. The corpse drew veterans, embassies and royal gifts, and possession of it made Serevan's claim to keep Eryndor's court the strongest in the east.

## Arezan and Vashar

Arezan converted a wartime coalition into the Vasharan Empire. His forces held the Gate House at Arqesh, the highland roads and most of the Ubar basin. Canal officials remained at work because the new rulers depended upon them for food and transport, and because the water books they kept now bore Kassur's seal over to the Vashari king. Kassur himself lived as Arezan's guarded guest at Kavresh until about 676. Vashari houses received military commands, while subject cities retained councils or rulers where cooperation reduced the cost of occupation.

Parena rode up the Dawn Road to Kavresh, where her mother Mithara had kept **Mehrava** since the king marched east. House Tiravan defended the child's claims in public, but Arezan also governed in his own right and never sent an army west in her name. Mithara arranged marriages for Parena's surviving attendants, and the stories these families carried later became important sources for the king's final years. Around 670 she married Mehrava to Arezan's younger son Vahrad. The Tiravan kings from their son Kavran I onward descended from her and called themselves heirs of the Far Road.

## Demeran and the western army

**Demeran Kavos**, speaker of the standing companies that Eryndor had left at home, controlled the mints and the largest body of soldiers in the west. The companies had killed one king at Pelatha and sold Marenthe for their arrears, and they wanted no second king counting their pay. Caleran and several Compact cities used his absences and financial difficulties to recover their assemblies and fleets.

The Voice did Demeran's work for him. If Eryndor lived and reigned at Delkos, the half-brother acclaimed in Ortheia was a usurper, and in 683 Demeran had him killed in the living king's name. A season later the Voice promised the upland mines to a Delkosi captain, and Demeran declared it a puppet. He sent agents up the Dawn Road after Parena and her daughter; Arezan's road guards took them at Durqesh and hanged them in the gate. Other royal kin were killed during the struggle. Vasharan accounts made Demeran a would-be killer of children. Ortheian histories called Mehrava a camp child whom no upland assembly had seen, and cited the Voice, which had declared the king heirless. Vashari households answered that the king had named her before two armies under the walls of Arqesh. The killings removed the people around whom a general settlement could have formed.

Demeran retained Ortheia, but his authority over the city world contracted. Sailors paid in sealed promises rather than silver sold their ships or entered Caleran service. Exiles from Marenthe and other conquered cities returned with claims to land and revenge. Caleran restored meetings of the Celestial Compact and turned the recovery into the beginning of its republican predominance.

## Serevan and Delkos

Serevan governed through a mixed maritime court. His followers included Atherian sailors, Arqeshite clerks, Vashari officers, local island households, discharged soldiers and the Belyra families from the Nemeru yards, who had been promised a home in the west and made one at the door of the tomb. Delkos adopted court dress and ceremonial practices from several eastern kingdoms while preserving Atherian as the language of military command.

The kingdom moved grain, soldiers, royal brides and diplomatic missions through the eastern sea. Its fleets fought Demeran, protected friendly ports and compelled less powerful islands to supply ships. The Voice was generous with the eastern sea. It promised the same Qahuri approaches to Neresh and to a Delkosi admiral, and the same harbours to captains on opposite sides of a war. Every sealed promise gave a commander reason to keep fighting, and petitioners competed in praise of the dead king to win the next. Serevan's descendants lost territory through dynastic war, but their officer houses and naval customs survived the contraction of the royal state. Officer families kept the sealed promises as heirlooms long after they had lost force, and some carried them abroad.

Nerethan of Delkos descended from one of these later officer houses. His arrival at the Second Harbour in 499 BR brought an inherited Successor tradition into the Gateway country. The surviving genealogy traces House Nerethan through Delkosi officers.

## Neresh and the southern crowns

**Neresh of Qemaret**, a Qahuri-born officer in Eryndor's army, held selected Qahuri approaches and crown towns. He had brought the masters of the White Measure to Eryndor's deathbed under the pledge the king had given in 695 to the House of the Returning Cargo, whose grain had fed his army up the river. The loan had put Ortheian garrisons at the House's store courts until the grain was repaid, and Neresh commanded them. He held those courts and the crown towns around them as surety for a debt the king had died owing, and added to them by military marriages. Temple and irrigation houses still controlled the land, grain and ritual knowledge needed for government.

Neresh's successors never ruled the whole of Qahur. Crown houses expelled some garrisons and absorbed others through marriage and service. Atherian coin forms, military colonies and court art entered the Basin, while Qahuri astronomy, funerary craft and royal ceremony influenced Delkos and Vashar. Prisoners, artisans, wives and soldiers carried this exchange alongside royal embassies.

## The garrisons beyond the wood

The garrisons Eryndor had placed beyond the wooded country east of the Vashari highlands were cut off in 686, when the road behind his army closed. No relief, pay or orders reached them. They went on writing to the king. Their dispatches came out by the long way round in traders' packs, reported musters and deaths and asked for four, nine and then twenty years of pay. After 684 some were addressed to Delkos, and the Voice answered them with grants of land it had never seen. The last known dispatch is dated by a regnal year of Eryndor that would fall around 646. Traders reported that the garrisons had given the valley terraces back to the wood, that their commanders had married into the households they ruled, and that nobody there would fell a living tree. Their history survives only in those letters and reports.

## The sealing of the tomb

Around 657 BR the Voice refused Neresh a grant it had once promised him. Neresh answered by bringing the Qemaret masters who had performed Eryndor's rite before envoys of the eastern courts. They testified that the rite had failed in the ship's hold, where they had no house to close the king into; that the king had gone the road of all the dead; and that the speaker at Delkos was no more Eryndor than the speaking tomb of **Nemeret the Pure**, a notorious counterfeit of their own country. Delkosi loyalists called the masters bribed, and some said that the dead lender of Qemaret had sent them because her share in the king had never been paid. Their testimony still emptied the sealed promises of force, because no captain could now show one to a garrison and be obeyed.

Around 655 BR Serevan's grandson walled the corpse and Kassur's images into the tomb. The living successors settled their borders by about 650. The Belyra doorkeepers kept the wall for generations. When Delkos's court shrank, keeper families carried their office to other islands, and three harbours now show a walled house and call it the king's.

## Consequences

The wars ended the prospect of one eastern empire. Vashar became the strongest continuous successor and protected the rebuilding of Second Qiryath. Its kings kept the water books of the last Ubar king and, from Mehrava's marriage onward, the blood of the Far-Road King by their own reckoning. Ortheia retained its monarchy but lost mastery of the Compact. Delkos created an enduring maritime inheritance. Qahur remained divided among native crowns strengthened or weakened by foreign marriages and garrisons. The embalmers of the Basin carried a reputation for speaking counterfeits into every court of the eastern sea.

By about 650 BR, with Caleran already fighting the first of the Gateway Wars over the grain market at Tammur, sailors, coin, siege knowledge, sealed promises and royal claims moved through an eastern sea permanently altered by Eryndor's conquest and its failure to remain whole.
