# Marelith

Marelith was a mobile harbour-city of the central Thalassian sea, carried by an immense living reefback and accompanied by a changing flotilla. It arose around 1000–900 BR among survivors of the older drowned city of [[Caelovar]] and people drawn into a new maritime settlement. Its ordinary range joined central island routes; an exceptional eastern voyage brought it into [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession|the Atherian succession war]], where it was destroyed in 355 BR.

A Morlach sea-kindred follower of [[Thalassor]] is remembered as Marelith's founder. Accounts say he followed a tide-borne vision, worked with survivors and deep negotiators, and entered a bargain around a wounded natural reefback altered in the Giant age. The animal bore an inhabited shell and living structures while the city fed and defended it. The founder went into the sea as the first living return required by the bargain. What happened to him below is unknown; later claims speak of death, transformation or continuing service.

## The living harbour and its route

The bearer was a natural reefback that the earlier colonists had enlarged and altered. Old grafts and injured living works complicated its movement. Survivor knowledge and negotiation with deep peoples were necessary to make an inhabited settlement possible. The resulting harbour included cultivated shell, living structures, landing places and a changing collection of vessels. A visitor might reach a working quay and still find that the whole settlement had moved by the next season.

Marelith's ordinary movement followed a broad central-island circuit shaped by the bearer, food, repairs and existing provisioning relationships. A harbour might expect a seasonal visit but could not command a particular day or anchorage. Rarer long treks took the city beyond that circuit. Mobility gave residents options against a besieging land power, yet it created its own vulnerabilities: the city had to feed both its people and its bearer, maintain attachment points, keep vessels together and obtain supplies from shores it could not occupy permanently.

The founding covenant forbade a permanent anchorage or an immovable maritime capital. It also required a living human return to the sea. The founder's disappearance fulfilled the first payment in the stories preserved by later residents. Traditions disagree about the experience that led him there. His vision of a magnificent inhabited moving island may explain his resolve.

## Rule and livelihood

The first sacred ruling household claimed authority from the founding bargain. After a failed fishing and raiding season left the city hungry, residents and crews cast that dynasty into the sea rather than yield more lives from poorer families. A covenant council followed, then an oligarchy dominated by ship captains. The change restrained hereditary monopoly but did not end human tribute. Councillors protected recognised citizens by transferring its cost to captives, enslaved foreigners and condemned people, including children who could not consent. The city's rulers made those choices.

The dynasty depended on ritual prestige, stores and the crews needed to move and defend the city. When catches failed and raids brought back neither food nor saleable cargo, those supports came apart. Hungry residents could see that the ruling household demanded further lives while controlling provisions. The revolt destroyed the dynasty's hold but was also a violent sacrifice of its members. The later captains' ascendancy followed the material power of vessels, routes and mobile neighbourhoods.

In the later city, a recognised share in a ship or essential installation gave protection and a civic voice. Residence and years of labour alone did not. Captains could grant a share for exceptional service, but existing owners judged whose service counted. Fishing, trade, salvage, escort and repair sustained households alongside smuggling, ransom, labour capture and slavery. Marelith's warrants distinguished paid war and authorised reprisals from piracy in its own law; people on raided coasts had reason to reject the distinction.

A vessel share represented a stake in the system by which the settlement moved, ate and fought. That made citizenship materially useful as well as honourable. An enslaved worker could keep a living wall functioning without obtaining a vote over its use. A resident foreigner could trade for years without the protection given to a recognised owner.

Warrants named targets, waters, time, division of prizes and treatment of captives. A captain might use one to enforce a claimed debt, retaliate for an attack or fulfil a foreign contract. Other attacks remained unlicensed even under Marelith's law. The same flotilla needed ordinary trade and escort contracts to live. Its reputation as a pirate city concealed the work of fishing crews, repairers, carriers and families who had no equal share in captains' decisions.

Surface and deep communities met around the city's living works. Outsiders often flattened diverse peoples into “fishmen” or “Deep Ones”. Some residents descended from transformed Caelovar survivors, but other deep peoples had separate histories. Child-taking belonged to particular covenants and crimes, not to every deep visitor. Marelith could shelter a fugitive from one shore while buying a captive from another.

## Destruction in 355 BR

During the succession wars, Marelith's independent captains accepted Ardel Veyr's hire as an allied harbour, supplier and source of ships. The city remained self-governing. Its final trek carried it east of its ordinary circuit toward Ardel's force among the central Thalassian anchorages. In 355 BR Istran Melvo attacked the city during the same linked operation in which his fleet defeated Ardel's nearby main force. Accounts of the deception differ; several describe a prize ship admitted too close before hidden attackers and an incendiary weapon set the living works alight. The exact substance and its making were guarded Atherian secrets.

Ardel's offer gave the captains payment, access to friendly stores and a chance to defend privileges threatened by a victorious Thalion. It also carried the city beyond the circuit in which its routes and relationships were strongest. Survivor and Atherian accounts differ over who authorised the apparent prize ship's admission and how much warning reached the attached neighbourhoods. The deception exploited a city accustomed to dividing valuable captures among its own share-holders. When attackers ignited the wet shell, rigging and cultivated works, the same attachments that had held a mobile harbour together helped carry fire and panic through it.

The fire blackened Marelith's once pale structures. Most residents died or were taken captive; smaller surface and deep diasporas survived. The burned urban mass grounded as the [[Black Reef]] along the eastward route from the anchorages. Ardel escaped east and was executed the following year. The reefback's ultimate fate is uncertain. Elmaric raiding continued after Marelith's loss.

Some people died in the fire and smoke; others were lost when structures tore away or vessels separated. Captured residents faced different later fates, including enslavement. Reports of movement below Black Reef may preserve an escape or may misread storms, salvage and other sea life.
