# Marelith

Marelith was a mobile harbour-city of the central Thalassian sea, carried by an immense living reefback and accompanied by a changing flotilla. It arose around 1000–900 BR among survivors of the older drowned city of [[Caelovar]] and people drawn into a new maritime settlement. Its ordinary range joined central island routes; an exceptional eastern voyage brought it into [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession|the Atherian succession war]], where it was destroyed in 355 BR.

A Morlach sea-kindred follower of Thalassor is remembered as Marelith's founder. Accounts say he followed a tide-borne vision, worked with survivors and deep negotiators, and entered a bargain around a wounded natural reefback altered in the Giant age. The animal bore an inhabited shell and living structures while the city fed and defended it. The founder went into the sea as the first living return required by the bargain. What happened to him below is unknown; later claims speak of death, transformation or continuing service.

## The living harbour and its route

The bearer was a natural reefback that the earlier colonists had enlarged and altered. Old grafts and injured living works complicated its movement. Survivor knowledge and negotiation with deep peoples were necessary to make an inhabited settlement possible. The resulting harbour included cultivated shell, living structures, landing places and a changing collection of vessels. A visitor might reach a working quay and still find that the whole settlement had moved by the next season.

Marelith's ordinary movement followed a broad central-island circuit shaped by the bearer, food, repairs and existing provisioning relationships. A harbour might expect a seasonal visit but could not command a particular day or anchorage. Rarer long treks took the city beyond that circuit. Mobility gave residents options against a besieging land power, yet it created its own vulnerabilities: the city had to feed both its people and its bearer, maintain attachment points, keep vessels together and obtain supplies from shores it could not occupy permanently.

The founding covenant forbade a permanent anchorage or an immovable maritime capital, and it required a living human return to the sea at intervals. The founder's disappearance was the first payment. Residents' traditions disagree about what he saw that made him willing to pay it; the commonest says he had been shown the moving island alive with lamps.

## Rule and livelihood

The first sacred ruling household claimed authority from the founding bargain. It held the stores and the rites, and it kept demanding lives for the sea from poorer families while it controlled the food. After a season when the catches failed and the raiders came home with neither food nor saleable cargo, hungry residents and crews threw the whole dynasty into the sea: a revolution and a sacrifice at once. A covenant council followed, then an oligarchy of ship captains whose power was the plain power of vessels, routes and mobile neighbourhoods. The sea still had to be paid. Councillors protected recognised citizens by passing the cost to captives, enslaved foreigners and condemned people, including children who could not consent.

In the later city, a recognised share in a ship or essential installation, a stake in the means by which the settlement moved, ate and fought, gave protection and a civic voice. Residence and years of labour did not: an enslaved worker could keep a living wall alive for a lifetime without a vote over its use, and a resident foreigner could trade for years without an owner's protection. Captains could grant a share for exceptional service, but existing owners judged whose service counted. Fishing, trade, salvage, escort and repair sustained households alongside smuggling, ransom, labour capture and slavery. Marelith's warrants distinguished paid war and authorised reprisals from piracy in its own law; people on raided coasts had reason to reject the distinction.

Warrants named targets, waters, time, division of prizes and treatment of captives. A captain might use one to enforce a claimed debt, retaliate for an attack or fulfil a foreign contract. Other attacks remained unlicensed even under Marelith's law. The same flotilla needed ordinary trade and escort contracts to live. Its reputation as a pirate city concealed the work of fishing crews, repairers, carriers and families who had no equal share in captains' decisions.

Surface and deep communities met around the city's living works. Outsiders often flattened diverse peoples into “fishmen” or “Deep Ones”. Some residents descended from transformed Caelovar survivors, but other deep peoples had separate histories. Child-taking belonged to particular covenants and crimes, not to every deep visitor. Marelith could shelter a fugitive from one shore while buying a captive from another.

## Children for sale and the harbour without a chain

Marelith's captains came late to the Gateway Wars and profited from their end. After the breach of Qarzeth in 520 BR, Atherian soldiers sold many of the children their comrades had just pulled from the holding rooms. Between 520 and 518 Marelith's ships bought such children cheaply in the camp markets and along the outer approaches, carried them to the moving city and sold many of them back to the ransom funds of Esharat at a profit. Some never came back at all. [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth|Ilya of Qarzeth]], matching names to children at Esharat, cursed the buying captains one by one in a fragment her descendants kept; Marelith's councillors answered that a child bought and resold to its own people had been carried home.

The same city also became a refuge. Marelith never anchored, so it had no harbour chain to raise. In the gale of 466 BR, when League harbours closed their chains against the rebel hulls of the War of the Closed Chains, Elmarai pilots led the refused ships through the weather to the moving city, and Marelith took the crews in. Sailors afterwards called it **the harbour without a chain**. The name was praise on some coasts and an accusation on others. Over the next century it drew mutineers, debtors, runaways and the families of men Caleran had marked for death, alongside the raiders and slavers who had always used it.

## Destruction in 355 BR

Families fleeing the Writing-In of 367, when the Atherian partners had their enemies' names entered in the register of challengers and killed them on Caleran's muster ground, had already found their way to the moving city, and Ardel Veyr's fugitives followed. Among them was Corial Draven, one of the seven men who had killed Teren Casvilar; while any of the seven lived, the Atherians held, the war could not end. During the succession wars Marelith's captains accepted Ardel's hire as an allied harbour, supplier and source of ships, while keeping their own government. His offer gave them payment, friendly stores and a chance to defend privileges a victorious Thalion would take away. It also carried the city on its final trek, east of its ordinary circuit toward Ardel's force among the central Thalassian anchorages, into shallow water beyond the routes and friendships where it was strongest.

In 355 BR Istran Melvo attacked the city in the same linked operation in which his fleet defeated Ardel's nearby main force. Melvo came from Pyrassa, and he brought Pyrassa's great harbour chain east in the holds of his transports. His crews laid it across the shallows where the bearer fed and drew it tight between sunken hulks, so that the city that had never anchored was held fast. Several accounts describe an apparent prize ship admitted too close, in a city used to sharing out valuable captures among its own share-holders, before hidden attackers set the living works alight with a sea-fire whose making was a guarded Atherian secret. Survivors and Atherian writers still disagree over who let the prize in, who told Melvo where the bearer fed, and how much warning reached the attached neighbourhoods. The bearer fought the chain. Its struggles tore the attachments that had held the mobile harbour together, spilled lamps and cookfires into the works, and carried the fire through the wet shell, rigging and cultivated gardens. People died in the fire and smoke, were lost when structures tore away, or drowned when the vessels separated. Draven died aboard.

The fire blackened Marelith's once pale structures. Most residents died or were taken captive, and many of the captives were sold; smaller surface and deep diasporas survived. The burned urban mass grounded as the [[Black Reef]] along the eastward route from the anchorages. Ardel escaped east and was executed the following year. The reefback's fate is uncertain. Some survivors said the founding covenant, which forbade the city a permanent anchorage, had been broken the moment the chain held, and that the bearer had been freed of the city rather than killed. Elmaric raiding went on without Marelith.

Melvo's dispatches called Marelith a nest of pirates and slavers, and the city had been both. It had also been the last open harbour for families with nowhere else to land. Marelith's survivors and the Elmarai pilots who had once led the refused hulls to it called the burning a second Kelarion, after the old harbour that let its suppliants drown at the chain; the name stuck in island songs and never entered an Atherian record. Sailors on every coast said that the harbour without a chain had been killed with one. Reports of movement below Black Reef may preserve an escape or may misread storms, salvage and other sea life.
