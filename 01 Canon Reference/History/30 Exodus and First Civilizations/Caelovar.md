# Caelovar

Caelovar is the name by which a drowned Giant-age colonial harbour is known in the central Thalassian islands. Founded under Nythalum, one of the Giant powers of the [[Age of Chains]], it rose through the conquest of existing human island communities and the construction of living marine works. Much of the city sank during the Great Unbinding, but people survived on nearby high ground and along its damaged margins, centuries before Marelith or the [[Black Reef]].

The colony's Giant-blooded houses and imported human dependants took harbour and water sites from local inhabitants. Older shore families remained in dependent villages, service households, marginal islands and contested sacred places. The colonial elite later called itself a sister power to Nythalum, or its heir; the claim was ambition dressed as pedigree.

## Colonial society and living work

The best protected quays, fields and fresh water served the ruling houses first. Older residents continued to fish, farm, repair and worship around them under changed conditions. Some were enslaved; others lived in dependent settlements or households whose access to the harbour could be withdrawn. Imported human stock also occupied varied ranks beneath the Giant-blooded elite.

Distance from Nythalum and the growth of local wealth gave the colonial dynasties room to contest their founder's direction. Their claim to be a sister civilisation rested on mastery of the inner-sea harbour and its routes, and on native husbandry and dependent labour they preferred to leave out of the story.

Caelovar's harbour depended on living marine structures. Older human reef husbandry was appropriated by rulers who joined coral, shell, sponge, kelp and altered animals to their own fleshcraft. Living walls, water systems, floating works and vessels required sustained cultivation and repair. The city's wealth rested on dependent workers and on organisms its governors treated as property. Its biological craft followed a different path from Nythalum's crystal and memory technologies.

An engineered reef could shelter a landing only while people fed, cleaned and tended it. A living fresh-water structure needed careful maintenance as much as a cistern did. Workers familiar with currents and organisms held practical knowledge the ruling ideology often treated as subordinate craft. Different dynastic households held different parts of the complex living system, giving them leverage over one another and over the human communities that kept it alive.

The city's ambitions depended on exchange as well as craft. Its growing population required food, materials and vessels from other islands and shores. A ruler could conscript the reef-tenders, but a damaged living wall would heal for its caretakers and for no one else, so when supply failed and the tenders revolted, the harbour began to die with them.

## Drowning and unequal survival

The Great Unbinding struck amid damaged foundations, disrupted supply and revolt. Workers and native-descended residents fought. Some survivors escaped by boat or over high ground. Others used living chambers that changed their bodies for survival at sea. Some reached the chambers with the ruling houses' permission, others fought their way in, and many never reached them at all.

The accounts disagree because people stood in different places during the collapse. A royal narrative presents a planned descent as rescue; workers' descendants remember an escape route closed in their faces and neighbours left behind; later religious tellings describe judgement upon a proud dynasty. Survivors who could live in the water entered new relationships with deep communities. Sailors still tell of a whole city turned into dolphins, a story kinder than the drowned, the untransformed and the people who stayed beside the ruin.

## The occupied margins

Fishing places, salvage wharves and refuge quarters endured beside the dangerous ruin. Their residents retained some knowledge of tides and safe approaches. The relationships between transformed survivors and other deep peoples remain disputed. At 350 BR Caelovar was an old, partly inhabited ruin within the island world.

Native-descended islanders, former workers, lesser Giant-blooded households and later refugees occupied remaining high ground and usable shore. Some recovered materials from the damaged city and kept approaches known to their families. Dangerous collapsed works, contested salvage and the absence of a restored central authority limited what they could reclaim.

When the founder of [[Marelith]] reached this region centuries later, he met surviving communities with their own interests and knowledge. Their help shaped the new mobile harbour.
