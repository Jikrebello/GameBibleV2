# Caelovar

Caelovar is the name by which a drowned Giant-age colonial harbour is known in the central Thalassian islands. Founded under [[The Lost Civilization of Nythalum|Nythalum]], it rose through the conquest of existing human island communities and the construction of living marine works. Much of the city sank during the Great Unbinding, but people survived on nearby high ground and along its damaged margins. The inhabited ruin is older and separate from both [[Ilamar before 350 BR|Ilamar]] and the later [[Black Reef]].

The colony's Giant-blooded houses and imported human dependants took harbour and water sites from local inhabitants. Older shore families remained in dependent villages, service households, marginal islands and contested sacred places. The colonial elite later claimed to be a sister power or successor to Nythalum. This expressed ambition, not equal antiquity or a shared constitution.

## Colonial society and living work

The best protected quays, fields and fresh water served the ruling houses first. Older residents continued to fish, farm, repair and worship around them under changed conditions. Some were enslaved; others lived in dependent settlements or households whose access to the harbour could be withdrawn. Imported human stock also occupied varied ranks beneath the Giant-blooded elite.

Distance from Nythalum and the growth of local wealth gave the colonial dynasties room to contest their founder's direction. Their claim to be a sister civilisation drew on mastery of the inner-sea harbour and its routes. It also obscured the native husbandry and dependent labour without which their distinctive technology could not operate.

Caelovar's harbour depended on living marine structures. Older human reef husbandry was appropriated by rulers who joined coral, shell, sponge, kelp and altered animals to their own fleshcraft. Living walls, water systems, floating works and vessels required sustained cultivation and repair. The city's wealth rested on dependent workers and on organisms its governors treated as property. Its biological craft followed a different path from Nythalum's crystal and memory technologies.

An engineered reef could shelter a landing only while people fed, cleaned and tended it. A living fresh-water structure needed careful maintenance as much as a cistern did. Workers familiar with currents and organisms held practical knowledge the ruling ideology often treated as subordinate craft. Different dynastic households held different parts of the complex living system, giving them leverage over one another and over the human communities that kept it alive.

The city's ambitions depended on exchange as well as craft. Its growing population required food, materials and vessels from other islands and shores. A ruler controlling one harbour could interrupt those relationships or conscript workers but could not make damaged living structures obey without their caretakers. This dependence helps explain why supply failure and revolt mattered during the Unbinding.

## Drowning and unequal survival

The Great Unbinding struck amid damaged foundations, disrupted supply and revolt. Accounts differ over the royal houses' decisions and whether the final descent was judgement, failed escape or a disaster beyond their control. Workers and native-descended residents fought. Some survivors escaped by boat or over high ground. Others used living chambers that changed their existing bodies for survival at sea; access was unequal and many died.

The surviving accounts disagree in part because people stood in different places during the collapse. A royal narrative could present a planned descent as rescue; a worker's descendants could remember the closing of an escape route and the abandonment of neighbours. Later religious tellings described judgement upon a proud dynasty.

Some residents reached the chambers with elite permission, and others fought their way in; many had no access at all. Survivors who could live in the water entered new relationships with deep communities. Sailor stories of a city turned entirely into dolphins survive because they offer an image of escape. They cannot account for the dead, the untransformed and those who remained beside the ruin.

## The occupied margins

Fishing places, salvage wharves and refuge quarters endured beside the dangerous ruin. Their residents retained some knowledge of tides and safe approaches. The relationships between transformed survivors and other deep peoples remain disputed. At 350 BR Caelovar was an old, partly inhabited ruin within the island world.

Native-descended islanders, former workers, lesser Giant-blooded households and later refugees occupied remaining high ground and usable shore. Some recovered materials from the damaged city and kept approaches known to their families. Dangerous collapsed works, contested salvage and the absence of a restored central authority limited what they could reclaim.

When the founder of [[Marelith]] reached this region centuries later, he met surviving communities with their own interests and knowledge. Their help shaped the new mobile harbour.
