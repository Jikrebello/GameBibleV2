# Gateway-Facing Outer Approaches before 350 BR

The Gateway-facing outer approaches were islands, channels and facing coasts linking the Elmaric sea to the [[The Gateway Republic|Gateway Republic]], its Sarqeni successor and Caleran. Older island residents, mixed harbours, settler quarters and foreign stations held overlapping claims. A power controlling one customs quay or fort could influence a route without governing the whole island behind it. By 350 BR Caleran's strongest direct footholds in Elmaric-connected waters lay in these approaches, while the central heartland remained independent.

These positions mattered because ships needed food, repairs, pilots and shelter. Grain came by treaty from some shores and by contested farming holdings from others. Local cultivators could remain under rent or tribute while settler households and enslaved workers cultivated neighbouring ground. Timber and repair materials gave a modest port value far beyond its area. An armed ruler seeking one landing might therefore provoke a war among people whose houses and fields lay elsewhere.

The [[The Gateway Wars|Gateway Wars]], c. 660–520 BR, divided the connected island world. Some Elmaric captains carried Caleran supplies, troops and pilots. Others served the Gateway or its allies, or raided without a lasting allegiance. Kin at different ports could fight on opposite sides and then dispute a captured relative or vessel. Their shared ancestry supplied channels for negotiation.

The approaches saw each stage of the wars at close quarters. Caleran's ships blockaded Tammur here about 652 BR, turning back grain buyers who had eaten at the Gateway's festival tables. After the peace of 646 the hostage ships passed through every seventh year, carrying the children of Qarzeth's Council Houses to Caleran. The last of them sailed in 611. In 608, when Tashqar Ordan's war spread from the mainland, island harbours here changed sides, burned and changed back within a season. In 520 the salt that covered Qarzeth's sanctuary came by boat along these coasts from the pans of Hazreth and Sarqen, and in the two years after the breach captains from [[Marelith]] cruised the same approaches buying the children Atherian soldiers had sold.

After Qarzeth fell in 520 BR, Caleran used its new leverage selectively. A captain with useful pilotage, strong patrons or ships might receive a contract or amnesty. A less protected former opponent could be pursued as a pirate for comparable wartime acts. This policy worked where Caleran could reach ships, harbours and markets. Local rulers and rival states continued to shelter people whom Caleran condemned.

At 350 BR the approaches remained a mixture of direct Caleran positions, clients, independent ports and disputed island–shore communities.
