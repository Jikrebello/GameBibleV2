# The Eastern Sea Wars

The **Eastern Sea Wars** were a series of conflicts between Vashar and the Atherian maritime leagues from 514 to 491 BR, followed by decades of client rivalry. They began when the harbour city of Tasar resisted a Vasharan governor's attempt to choose its magistrate and enlarge its naval levy, and they widened when the Atherian port of Velanthe took in Tasar's fugitives and would not give them up. Kazren I lost one fleet at anchor off Ilyessa in 506 BR, hired the island's pilots and launched a western invasion that won its battles and starved before Velanthe. At Ilyessa in 493 BR the grain that fed his army spoiled in the holds of a second anchored fleet, and his own subject crews rowed for an Atherian refuge fire. The king died in his camp. The League of the Eastern Oar, which took the deserters in, preserved the leagues from Vasharan rule and within a generation was raising its harbour chains against its own members.

## Two powers approach the same coast

The eastern sea wars grew from the expansion of Vasharan and Caleran power along the same coast. A Vasharan road commander governed several districts from a fortified station, collecting tribute, raising troops and guarding the route to the royal heartland. Subject rulers and city assemblies continued beneath him. The Caleran republic assembled naval leagues whose members supplied ships, money and protected anchorages. Vasharan governors punished a city's refusal as rebellion; Caleran commanders treated withdrawal from a league as defection.

Several old communities occupied the contested coast. **Tasar** was a cultivated harbour city with its own assembly, landholding families and ship crews. **Avesh**, farther inland, controlled the stores and road stations joining the coast to Arqesh and Vashar. The islands and channels of **Ilyessa** provided pilotage, fishing and sheltered passages, and its pilot families kept old terms with the water of the Narrows. Villages in the hills supplied timber, pitch and food to the nearest station and suffered requisition whenever its ruler changed.

Qiryath lay inland from the main fighting. Its road houses carried travellers across both political spheres, while Jirahar residents lived in Vasharan, coastal and Atherian cities. War therefore divided related households. One cousin might provision a Vasharan station, another serve as an Atherian interpreter and a third try to move children away from both armies.

## The successor coast, 684–650 BR

The coast inherited an earlier division from the death of Eryndor III of Ortheia. Eryndor's conquest had joined Ortheian infantry, city levies and allied ships under his personal command. When he died aboard ship at Nemeru in 684 BR, that army possessed no accepted ruler. A half-brother was acclaimed in distant Ortheia, the king's small daughter was in a Vashari valley, and generals and royal relatives seized the provinces, fleets and treasuries within their reach.

**Serevan of Delkos**, commander of the allied fleet, took the islands and several eastern harbours. His court combined Ortheian officers, local pilots, former Arqeshite officials and households attached to the conquered ports. It preserved Eryndor's claim to kingship while rewarding cities that supplied ships. **Demeran Kavos** controlled Ortheia and the western army. **Arezan Tiravan** held Vashar and most of the Ubar basin. **Neresh of Qemaret** established southern garrisons through agreements and marriages with Qahuri crown towns.

Their Wars of the Inheritance moved fleets and soldiers through the coast until about 650 BR. Demeran killed the king's half-brother and sent agents after Parena and her daughter at Kavresh; Arezan's road guards hanged them at Durqesh. Serevan sheltered some of Parena's former attendants and used their testimony against Demeran, although his own tomb-voice had disowned her child. Coastal cities learned to bargain with commanders who claimed the same royal inheritance and issued incompatible grants of land, citizenship and harbour privilege.

The strangest grants came from Delkos. The king had died on the flagship of the allied fleet, and its Belyra crews had sailed the body to Serevan's harbour. Veterans swore that the dead king still spoke in the dim tomb-hall, from behind a curtain that smelled of cedar oil, in a voice that remembered their horses' names. Serevan's scribes wrote down and sealed what the voice promised, and it promised the same harbours to more than one captain. The voice fell silent around 657 BR, when Qahuri embalmers testified that their own rite had failed, and Serevan's grandson walled the corpse in its tomb a year or two later. The sealed grants outlived it. Harbour houses kept them in chests and brought them out whenever a magistracy, a berth or an anchorage changed hands. [[Wars of the Inheritance]]

The successor kingdoms' people and practices remained. Delkosi officers entered maritime republics and royal courts; Ortheian veterans held land among eastern cities; mixed ship crews carried Atherian, Qahuri, Vashari and Arqeshite techniques. The Gateway Wars began within this already connected sea. Later rulers remembered the successor age whenever they claimed that a fleet commander could preserve order after a king's death.

## The western coast during the Gateway Wars, c. 660–520 BR

Caleran's long war with Qarzeth shaped the later eastern conflict. The republic learned to maintain large fleets, collect naval contributions and keep distant crews supplied. Its commanders acquired clients among island cities and merchant households. Qarzeth's destruction in 520 BR released ships, veterans and political ambition into an eastern sea already disturbed by Vasharan expansion.

Vasharan kings had meanwhile incorporated the Ubar riverlands and strengthened Avesh. **Tiraz II Tiravan** established grain stations between Arqesh and the coast and made Avesh the seat of a single western road command under the Aresh house. His officials demanded animals, timber and labour along routes whose communities possessed older rights of pasture and water. Resistance remained local because the coastal cities distrusted one another as much as the royal governor.

Trade continued throughout these tensions. Atherian captains bought Vashari horses and river grain. Vasharan officers hired Atherian shipwrights and physicians. Coastal young people entered foreign service and returned with money, spouses, injuries and political opinions. Such relationships later made intervention possible.

## The revolt of Tasar, 514–511 BR

The immediate crisis began with a succession dispute at Tasar. The harbour magistrate supervised the civic warehouses, assigned public berths and negotiated the ships and money owed to Vashar. Two civic households claimed the vacant office. One had the assembly's support. The other held a sealed Delkos grant, more than a century old, which named its ancestors masters of the public berths in the dead king's voice. Governor **Mazdan Aresh** recognised the grant, which no Tasaran assembly had honoured in living memory, and supported the weaker claimant in exchange for an increased ship levy and the surrender of three hostages. Tasar's assembly rejected these terms. In 514 BR Mazdan came down from Avesh with his guard to install his candidate in person, and fighting spread from the council house through the harbour quarter.

The rebels went first for the governor's counting-house on the quay, where the ship-levy rolls and the hostage lists were kept, and burned it. Mazdan surrendered in the council house on the promise of **Liora of Tasar**, a magistrate from a ship-owning family, that he would be taken alive. Rebels killed him on the steps. Vasharan families in Tasar were attacked, and several hundred people fled inland.

Liora crossed to the Atherian port of **Velanthe** and asked for a fleet. She promised harbour access, timber and membership in a defensive league. The Caleran Senate refused. The Compact's war rolls had been coming apart since the erasure of Orthea, and no senator could say with confidence which allies owed which ships. Liora came home with eleven hulls hired on her own family's credit under **Eryon Telmar**, a captain discharged after the last Gateway campaign, and with Ilyessan pilots who wanted protection from Avesh. For two seasons they ran grain into the harbour past the royal squadron.

**Ardash Aresh**, the dead governor's brother, took the Avesh command and the siege. Pack trains brought grain from Arqesh; highland estates supplied horses; coastal subject cities provided ships. The Vasharan lines isolated Tasar by land while smaller vessels wore down Telmar's runs. Disease spread through the crowded city. Liora ordered household grain seized for the garrison, and poor districts suffered first.

In 511 BR the net-makers and porters of the Salt Gate quarter, the poorest in the harbour, opened their gate to the besiegers in return for bread. Ardash executed the principal leaders and sold many defenders and inhabitants. On Kazren's order he sent the neighbourhoods associated with the revolt, the Salt Gate quarter among them, up the Dawn Road to keep the royal relay stations. Harbour families who had never sat a horse fed the king's remounts in the snow of the high passes, and their descendants kept the stations for generations. Liora disappeared during the final fighting. Atherian sources place her death at the gate; a Tasaran family later claimed that she lived in exile. The conquest restored Vasharan administration and ended the belief that coastal privileges were secure under negotiated tribute.

On the night the gate opened, Telmar's ships and every hull that could float rowed west with the people who expected the executioner, guided through the Narrows by their Ilyessan pilots. Velanthe had sent Tasar nothing. Its harbour wardens had learned Kelarion's Roll of Refused Hulls with their approaches, and they raised no chain against a hull in distress. Kazren's envoys demanded the fugitives. The wardens read them the Roll.

## The fleet that sank at anchor, 506 BR

Kazren could not yet reach Velanthe. He could reach the pilots. In 506 BR he appointed **Zadran Vezh**, an Aveshi noble with experience in coastal levies, to take the islands of Ilyessa, seize the pilot families and hold the Narrows. The expedition assembled ships from several subject ports. Their hulls, rigging and crew practices differed, and Zadran filled the benches with conscripts from the coast, Tasarians among them.

The pilot families took to their boats and the inner channels. Zadran anchored his fleet in the lee of the islands to starve them out and kept it there through a calm summer. The ships began to ride low. Carpenters found dry bilges and sound seams. Zadran would not believe that a tight hull could sink, and he kept the subject crews bailing empty holds while his Vashari crews shifted to the newest ships. By autumn more than half the fleet had gone down at its moorings. Crews drowned below decks at the pumps; others swam to the islands and gave themselves up to the people they had come to seize. Coastal allies accused Zadran of buying his own crews' lives with theirs. He returned to Avesh with fewer than half his ships and was executed after a royal inquiry, which found that Ilyessan sorcery had sunk the fleet. His household lost its estates, although ship captains who had slipped their cables early escaped punishment by blaming the dead commander.

Kazren read the inquiry and drew his lesson from it. If the pilots of Ilyessa could sink a fleet, then the pilots commanded the water, and a king who commanded the pilots would command it too. He built coastal stores, took hostages from subject ports and placed the land army and naval transports under separate commands. His admiral, **Sharvan Medh**, hired Ilyessan pilots for the Narrows at royal wages and lodged their children at Avesh. Kazren's inscriptions at Avesh now called him heir of the Far Road, through Parena's daughter Mehrava, and claimed the Atherian cities as the hegemony Eryndor had held. Atherian envoys answered that the voice at Delkos had disowned Mehrava, and that Mazdan Aresh had found that voice good enough to cite at Tasar.

## Kazren's great invasion, 497–491 BR

The invasion aimed to recover the fugitives and break the Atherian alliance system. Kazren expected frightened cities to withdraw once a royal army stood on their land. His forces included Vashari cavalry and household troops, Arqeshite engineers, Qahuri and Ubar grain carriers, coastal marines, subject fleets and hired companies. The host moved west along the coast in 497 BR while the fleet carried heavy stores, siege material and most of the army's bread.

The Atherian response was organised through the **League of the Eastern Oar**, an alliance of Caleran commanders, island cities and threatened ports formed at Velanthe that year. Eryon Telmar commanded the land defence from Velanthe. **Nerica Aven**, a ship-owner's daughter who had commanded convoy escorts during the last Gateway war, directed the combined fleet. League membership required ships or money. The League's first custom came from Kelarion's Roll of Refused Hulls, which Velanthe's pilots learned with their approaches: no member harbour closed its chain against a League hull in distress. Cities which tried to withdraw faced confiscation, the dismantling of walls and the installation of friendly councils.

Kazren won the early campaigns. His army burned two settlements which refused provisions and enslaved survivors from a fortified island taken after a short siege. Several cities submitted. Others supplied both sides while waiting for the outcome. Atherian accounts later presented a unanimous defence of liberty; surviving contribution lists reveal coercion, evasion and sharply unequal burdens.

### The terms of the Narrows

In 495 BR the royal fleet came to the **Narrows of Ilyessa**, the channel of islands and cross-currents between the Vasharan coast and the Atherian approaches. Its Ilyessan pilots took it through on their families' terms with the water. The pilot **Ebris of Ilyessa** gave the admiral those terms in three sentences: keep moving; never ask the water to hold you still; never call the sea yours. What the current carried, she said, the sea could take back. For two seasons the fleet obeyed. Transports shuttled between the coastal stores and the army's camps, and the royal army ate.

In the spring of 493 the last cities between Ilyessa and Velanthe submitted, and Kazren's court diviners dreamed of nations kneeling around a radiant king, the vision they called the **Witness of Dawn**. Kazren had a new title cut on the mole at Avesh and over the door of every station on the coast road: **Keeper of the Roads by Land and by Water**. He brought the army before Velanthe and ordered Sharvan to lay the transports at anchor in the lee of the Ilyessa islands as a standing granary for the siege. Sharvan reminded him of Zadran's fleet. The king answered that Zadran had held no pilots' children. Ebris refused to take the transports in. Pilots with sons and daughters at Avesh did it.

### The holds that took the tide

Through the summer of 493 the holds took the tide. Crews opened sound hatches to find water-lines drawn on the inside of dry planking and salt crusted on the undersides of the hatch covers. The ships settled at their moorings, a hand's breadth a week, with no seam opening and no water in the bilges. Grain in sealed jars swelled, sprouted and rotted. Oil turned to brine in stoppered jars. Sailors who had ridden out the worst gales of the Gateway coast believed their ships were sinking under them in a flat calm.

The subject crews broke first. They were Tasarians, Ilyessans and Aveshi coasters pressed onto the benches, many with kin in the Dawn Road stations or the hostage houses at Avesh, and they were hungry, because the Vashari marines had taken the sound stores for the army. At night they cut their cables and rowed for the refuge fire that Velanthe kept on its outer mole. Nerica Aven's captains wanted the chain kept up against the king's hulls. Velanthe's harbour wardens had learned Kelarion's Roll as children and would raise no chain against a hull in distress, League or not, and Nerica kept the fire lit. Each crew that came in was disarmed, fed and offered the League's oar. Most took it.

In the autumn of 493 the League fleet, half of it rowed by men who had come west under the king's banners, went out against the anchored transports. The Vashari marines fought for two days for ships that were settling under them. The granary fleet was taken, burned or sunk at its moorings. Atherian crews killed many of the marines in the water and sold the rest. Marines who blamed Ebris for the holds drowned her before the fighting ended, and her children, hostages at Avesh, were sold.

### The withdrawal

Without the granary the army before Velanthe ate the country and then went hungry. Telmar kept to the walls and attacked foraging parties. Kazren ordered requisitions from towns which had submitted, causing some to revolt behind him. Flux spread through the royal camps, and in the winter of 492–491 it killed the king.

His son **Orvaz**, eighteen years old and in command of the household horse, was acclaimed at the bier by the Vashari regiments. The road commanders did not wait for the funeral. Each broke camp with his own nation and took his own road, because the throne would be settled at Kavresh by whoever arrived first with soldiers. The army that had marched west as one host went home as a dozen.

The overland road through Avesh was the only one with bread on it, and its governor, **Ardash Aresh**, kept the grain stations shut. The hungry lower town of Avesh and the first stragglers of the army stormed the granary gates together. Ardash's house troops burned the lower town to clear the approaches, and warehouses, workshops and several hundred homes went with it. When Orvaz reached the gate, Ardash's envoys asked that the western command pass in his house by right. Orvaz swore it there, with the lower town still smoking behind him, and received bread and remounts enough to reach Kavresh before his uncles. The rest of the army came home through 491 BR as it could. Wounded soldiers and dependent camp families died on the road when the animals failed. Atherian pursuers killed stragglers and sold captives.

Vashar retained Avesh and the roads east of it, at the price of a road command that now belonged to a family. The invasion had failed to conquer the Atherian leagues, yet it prevented them from taking the Vasharan western command. Both powers proclaimed victory over different parts of the outcome.

## The two liberations of Tasar

Tasar's later civic calendar contained two liberation days. The older marked the expulsion of Mazdan's soldiers in 514 BR. The newer marked the reduction of royal control after Kazren's failed invasion. Political factions celebrated one and neglected the other.

The city's poorer families preserved a different account. They remembered grain seizure under Liora, deportation under Kazren and ship levies under the Atherian league. Their winter song names all three governments and asks which of them brought bread. A verse added after 493 names the Salt Gate families on the Dawn Road and the Tasaran rowers who came home in League ships. In this tradition, a liberation earns its name when households can eat, return home and keep their children from another levy.

Later tellers joined the two festivals, their rival processions and the winter song into the **Tale of the Two Liberations**. Families chose different heroes according to the quarter, fleet or exile community from which they descended. The tale preserves the divisions within Tasar after the banners of both great powers had left its streets.

## Orvaz I and the war of clients, 491–439 BR

Kazren's successor **Orvaz I Tiravan** abandoned plans for a direct conquest of the principal Atherian leagues. He used money, grain, marriage and sanctuary to divide them. Exiled city rulers received houses at Avesh. Vasharan timber reached coalitions opposing Caleran leadership. Atherian commanders supported provincial rivals within the western road command.

The League of the Eastern Oar survived the invasion that had brought it together. Caleran senators kept the fleet in service to protect trade and prevent another royal attack. Member cities increasingly paid ship money for decisions made at Caleran. A council that refused its assigned contribution could lose ships, walls or its chosen magistrates. Within a generation of the night Velanthe's wardens let the king's deserting crews in under the Roll, cities that fell behind found the League's chains raised against their hulls even in storms. In 469 Velanthe and the shipless allies refused the assessment, Orvaz sent them timber and money and housed their leaders at Avesh, and the war of the closed chains ran until the Chain Peace of 461. The captains Vespen Harod named as his enemies in 466 lived out their lives at Avesh. The peace left Caleran the fixed ship money and took the chains out of its hands.

Vasharan road commanders used local troops and taxes to defend the empire and accumulated enough wealth to treat their commands as family possessions; the Aresh house at Avesh held its command by the oath Orvaz had sworn at its gate. In 462 BR the governor of Avesh joined three commands against him. The king offered safe conduct to divide them, then executed two surrendered leaders, the Aresh governor first, and gave Avesh to the dead man's nephew. **Rathava of Daryam** condemned the broken promise in the palace garden. Orvaz had Rathava's labourers brought into the garden in ropes, and Rathava unsaid his words before the court to keep them from the slave market. His students quarrelled for generations over whether he had failed the truth or kept faith with his household.

No treaty settled the whole coast. Agreements recognised particular cities, returned named hostages and fixed tolls for a few years at a time. Sailors and caravan masters learned which seals mattered at each harbour. Mixed coastal households often possessed property or relatives under both governments and moved what they could before the next breach.

## Marovan Veyr and the eastern commands, 414–397 BR

The later Atherian commander **Marovan Veyr** built his early reputation among ports whose alliances had been shaped by this older struggle. He secured winter harbours, suppressed raiding coalitions and established clients near the Vasharan sphere.

Veyr's conquests ended along the coast and the routes approaching Velos. The Vashari highlands and Ubar basin remained beyond his control. Vasharan governors accepted temporary boundaries when they needed freedom to deal with rival houses inland. Some coastal rulers held Atherian civic titles and Vasharan court ranks at the same time.

Jirahar road families carried messages, ransomed prisoners and moved dependants through the unsettled marches. Other Jirahar served openly in the armies. Some houses concealed refugees, some sold information to both governments and others used military contracts to ransom relatives. Later traditions that describe the road houses as neutral observers omit these divided choices.

## The Caleran civil wars and Orvaz II

**Tiraz III Tiravan** died in 409 BR and left the throne to a grandson of nine, **Orvaz II**. For ten years the keepers of Daryam withheld the royal flame from the child's audiences, and a king without the flame could summon no highland levy. The regency restricted Vasharan action during Veyr's final eastern commands.

When Teren Casvilar and Veyr went to war, Orvaz supplied money, pack animals and limited sanctuary to Veyr's clients. He withheld the central army. The indecisive battle near Velos in 377 BR, fought without Veyr because the ports would not pay for his ships, damaged the western network while leaving the empire intact. For four years Veyr held the east on Gateway grain. His sanctuary at Ilamar from 373 and death there in 371 ended his personal command; former clients divided between Casvilar, local rulers and Vasharan patrons.

Casvilar's assassination opened another Caleran succession struggle. Orvaz traded with several parties and delayed a commitment until Darion Velsar allied with Queen Samyra. If they prevailed, their two governments would control the southern approaches to Vasharan ports. Orvaz then supplied credit, animals, refuge and intelligence while keeping the central army near Kavresh. In 354 Velsar marched on Avesh after a fugitive and was outrun all summer by the Dawn Road's relay riders, who carried the man from station to station ahead of him. He went home without a battle, and Orvaz II came to the settlement of 350 the stronger. This limited intervention preserved his own succession and allowed him to bargain with the eventual victor.

## The balance in 350 BR

Thalion's victory and the annexation of Sarqen produced a strong Atherian government at the western and southern end of the old contest. In the settlement of 350 BR, Thalion secures the Atherian-speaking ports and recognises selected coastal clients. Orvaz retains Avesh, Arqesh, Qiryath, the Ubar riverlands and the Vashari core.

The agreement leaves Atheria with the fleets, island obligations and coastal clients assembled during the wars. Vashar keeps the inland roads and river provinces, although its strongest governors can bargain with the king. Second Qiryath remains under Vasharan supremacy and continues to govern its sanctuary and communal affairs.

Independent harbours and difficult inland tracks leave routes outside the firm control of either state. Merchants cross through negotiated ports, exiles find patrons on the opposite side and border villages change masters without moving. By 350 BR, the coast contains mixed families, graves from several armies and communities that celebrate incompatible liberations.

## What the wars ran on

Vasharan armies marched on Ubar grain, Qahuri barley, highland animals and the stores of the road stations. Atherian fleets ate from island granaries, coastal markets and the ship money of league members. That is why Ilyessa mattered: an army inland ate faster than the country it crossed could feed it, and its bread came by sea. When that bread rotted in sound hulls at Ilyessa, the army before Velanthe was finished, whatever its cavalry could do. The royal fleet was a gathering of subject squadrons with different hulls, rigging and habits, strong in numbers and hard to command as one; its crews had kin in the king's stations and hostage houses and little else binding them to his war. The Atherian crews had learned common signals and repair in the Gateway Wars. Vashari cavalry won battles inland and starved on a stripped coast.

Free citizens, allied crews, debtors, prisoners and slaves built the ships, mended the roads, carried the stores and dug the siege works. Victory inscriptions credit kings and commanders. Pay lists name rowers, porters, carpenters and drovers, and burial pits hold the people the inscriptions left out. Pilots, translators, traders, hostages and road-house travellers carried knowledge between the powers. A false report wrecked an expedition as surely as a storm, and governments punished villages accused of guiding the enemy, often on the word of their neighbours.

The sea and the sky took sides in the songs. Kazren's court diviners dreamed of ordered nations kneeling around a radiant king and called it the Witness of Dawn; it told the king what he already believed, that submission proved truth, and the sea had submitted too. Atherian crews dedicated weapons and campaign rites to **Astaroth**, and their commanders treated what the Red Star revealed about how force would travel through a hull, a bridge or a formation as plain military intelligence. Coastal people said the water of the Narrows kept its own terms. Vasharan histories blamed Ilyessan sorcery for the holds; Atherian victory songs say the sea itself fought for free cities; Ilyessan pilots say the king called the water his and asked it to stand still.

## Received memories

Vasharan palace histories describe a defence of truthful order against rebellious governors and oath-breaking cities, lost to sorcery and an ungrateful governor. Atherian civic histories describe free cities repelling eastern monarchy. Tasaran songs remember two liberations and three forms of requisition. Ilyessan pilot families remember Ebris. Jirahar accounts follow divided households, rescued captives and the routes which armies failed to close.

Vasharan inscriptions record the road commands and royal victories while omitting some surrendered leaders and punitive sales. Atherian contribution lists reveal which allied cities supplied ships under threat. Destruction layers, burial grounds, household correspondence and road-house memories preserve the losses experienced in the coastal settlements.
