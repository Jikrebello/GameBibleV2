# Proto-Jirahar Tower Towns

The proto-Jirahar tower towns emerge from communities that refuse to let the Exodus become merely a heroic origin myth. They preserve route, law, genealogy, lament, and prophecy as living obligations.

## Why Towers

The tower town is both shelter and statement:

- a visible marker on caravan roads;
- a storehouse for scrolls, witnesses, and route memory;
- a court for disputes among travelers;
- a refuge during raids and famine;
- a vertical symbol of waiting for the true promised land.

The visible structure is only the crown of the settlement. Cisterns, dwellings, workshops, stores, schools, burial galleries, and refuges expand below ground over generations.

## Social Pattern

Proto-Jirahar life combines movement and rooted memory. Families travel by caravan, but the tower towns anchor law, archives, marriage records, food storage, ritual calendars, and judges.

## Claim to the Prophet

The Jirahar preserve the strongest claim to the Great Prophet's original teaching. That does not mean every Jirahar person is holy or every later institution is pure. It means their culture is built around the conviction that forgetfulness is a form of betrayal.

## Future Consequence

Qiryath becomes the greatest early tower, is destroyed by Arqesh in 742 BR, rebuilt, and destroyed again by Atheria in 70 AR. The broader tower pattern survives because it never depends on one kingdom. Later towers become deep cities and fixed anchors for the Prophet's Roads.
