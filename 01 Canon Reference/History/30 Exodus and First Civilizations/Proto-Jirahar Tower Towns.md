# Proto-Jirahar Tower Towns

The proto-Jirahar tower towns emerge after the northern fission, from one branch of the continuing Near-Glass camp continuum. These communities answer water scarcity, distance, raiding and dispersed settlement with portable courts, translocal registers and protected route shelters, refusing to let the Exodus become merely a heroic origin myth. They preserve route, law, genealogy, lament, and prophecy as living obligations.

## Why Towers

The tower town is both shelter and statement:

- a visible marker on caravan roads;
- a storehouse for scrolls, witnesses, and route memory;
- a court for disputes among travellers;
- a refuge during raids and famine;
- a vertical symbol of waiting for the true promised land.

The visible structure is only the crown of the settlement. Cisterns, dwellings, workshops, stores, schools, burial galleries, and refuges expand below ground over generations.

## Social Pattern

Proto-Jirahar life combines movement and rooted memory. Families travel by caravan, but the tower towns anchor law, archives, marriage records, food storage, ritual calendars, and judges. This is one later answer to the shared northern inheritance.

## Claim to the Prophet

The Jirahar preserve the strongest claim to the Great Prophet's original teaching. Their culture is built around the conviction that forgetfulness is a form of betrayal. Their road-singers tell his death as *the Slow Road*: commanded to go no faster than the last cart, he turned back along the column to the rear camps where the bread had already stopped, and died among the people the captains had outpaced. Tower towns kept the habit that story teaches, waiting for the slowest caravan before closing the gate.

## Future Consequence

Qiryath becomes the greatest early tower, where guardians who carried the offering beside the altar's cool stones came to rule like kings until a widow asked whom the stones answered. It is destroyed by Arqesh in 742 BR, rebuilt, and destroyed again by Atheria in 72 AR. The broader tower pattern survives because it never depends on one kingdom. Later towers become deep cities and fixed anchors for the Prophet's Roads.
