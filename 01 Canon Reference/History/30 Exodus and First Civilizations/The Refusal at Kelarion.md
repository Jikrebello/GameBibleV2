# The Refusal at Kelarion

Around **1160 BR**, a storm drove a fleet of merchant hulls, pilgrim craft, fishing boats, and refugee transports toward [[Kelarion]]. The harbor could not admit every vessel safely, but the ruling harbor council ordered the gate chain kept closed even after pilots identified a sequence that could have brought many inside.

The council feared disease, disorder, and political rivals among the refugees. It also intended to purchase wreck cargo cheaply after the storm. Guards turned away private rescuers and extinguished outer signals so incoming ships would stop approaching the chain.

## The Wound

Hundreds drowned within sight of shelter. The decisive refusal was not limited capacity or a failed rescue. It was the choice to convert sanctuary into an instrument, deny the personhood of everyone outside, and destroy the practical signs by which help could still reach them.

That layered act opened the wound from which [[The Shoreless King]] manifested. The dragon broke the harbor, raised drowned walls, and made every surviving route lead back into the storm.

## The Sealing

Fisher crews, rival pilots, surviving refugees, and defecting harbor guards reopened the refuge route. They cut the chain, relit an outer fire, and named the refused ships from every surviving manifest and testimony. The work bounded the wound long enough to seal the dragon beneath the collapsing breakwaters.

Three practices continue the seal: the chain remains broken, the Roll of Refused Hulls remains distributed, and a refuge beacon remains lit on the surviving reef.

## Public Memory

Later traditions remember a proud harbor drowned after denying ships in a storm. Most omit the council's salvage motive, disagree on the city's name, and treat the dragon as divine punishment rather than the active shape of the refusal.
