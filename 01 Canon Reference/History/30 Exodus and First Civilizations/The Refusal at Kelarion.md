# The Refusal at Kelarion

Around **1160 BR**, a storm drove a fleet of merchant hulls, pilgrim craft, fishing boats, and refugee transports toward [[Kelarion]]. The harbour could not admit every vessel safely, but the ruling harbour council ordered the gate chain kept closed even after pilots identified a sequence that could have brought many inside.

The council feared disease, disorder, and political rivals among the refugees. It also intended to purchase wreck cargo cheaply after the storm. Guards turned away private rescuers and extinguished outer signals so incoming ships would stop approaching the chain.

## The Wound

Hundreds drowned within sight of shelter. The decisive refusal was not limited capacity or a failed rescue. It was the choice to convert sanctuary into an instrument, deny the personhood of everyone outside, and destroy the practical signs by which help could still reach them.

That layered act opened the wound from which [[The Shoreless King]] manifested. The dragon broke the harbour, raised drowned walls, and made every surviving route lead back into the storm.

## The Sealing

Fisher crews, rival pilots, surviving refugees, and defecting harbour guards reopened the refuge route. They cut the chain, relit an outer fire, and named the refused ships from every surviving manifest and testimony. The work bounded the wound long enough to seal the dragon beneath the collapsing breakwaters.

Three practices continue the seal: the chain remains broken, the Roll of Refused Hulls remains distributed, and a refuge beacon remains lit on the surviving reef.

## Public Memory

Later traditions remember a proud harbour drowned after denying ships in a storm. Most omit the council's salvage motive, disagree on the city's name, and treat the dragon as divine punishment rather than the active shape of the refusal.

## The Roll's Later Descent

*Added by the Causality Pass, 14 November 2026; see [[Causality Pass - 02 Gateway Wars and the Late Republic]], items 02.1 and 02.6.*

**Into a charter, 497 BR.** The distributed copies of the Roll travelled with pilots. Velathene's pilots, from the nearest Compact port to the reef, learned it with their approaches as a list of names recited at the outer marks. When the League of the Eastern Oar formed at Velathene against Xarun I in 497 BR, its first custom came from that recitation: no member harbour closed its chain against a League hull in distress. This is the earliest written descendant of the seal's second anchor.

**Into a purse, 491–469 BR.** After Ilyessa Caleran kept the League fleet in service and fixed its ship money. Refuge became a privilege of cities that had paid. A city behind on its payments found League chains raised against its hulls in any weather; about 480 Pyrassan grain hulls broke on the rocks outside Velathene's closed harbour. This was Kelarion's refusal repeated as policy, and it weakened the seal on each occasion, because each was refuge knowingly withheld from people the harbour could have admitted.

**The partial recurrence at Pyrassa, 466 BR.** In the War of the Closed Chains Caleran ordered League harbours to close their chains against rebel and defaulting hulls. In the autumn gale of 466 Pyrassa, loyal because the League held its Elmarai pilots' families as surety, obeyed. The Shoreless King partly recurred under Pyrassa's breakwater, in its own signs: the harbour admitted no one, not even Pyrassa's own grain ships, which circled on the same wave whatever their heading; shore signals contradicted one another; the watch on the mole saw every hull outside as an enemy. Pilots who knew the Roll ran for the refuge fire on Kelarion's reef and lived. Pyrassa's harbour wardens, against Caleran's order, cut their own chain, which is the remedy the seal allows (opening shelter during a genuine and dangerous crisis). The recurrence ended that night. The same night Elmarai and shoal pilots led the refused rebel hulls to [[Marelith]], which never anchored and had no chain, and which sailors afterwards called the harbour without a chain. Pyrassan sailors' name for what rose under the breakwater, the King beneath the Breakwater, is the coast's regional name for the dragon; reader prose uses only that name.

**Into law, 461 BR.** The Chain Peace that ended the war made shelter law for any vessel in distress, of any allegiance, and deposited a copy of the Roll with the custodians of [[Ilamar before 350 BR|Ilamar]], who heard refuge disputes from then on. This made the second anchor a public institution for the first time and strengthened the seal. The descent from the Roll through the Oar charter and the Chain Peace is the ancestry of later Thalassian refuge law.

**Marelith, 355 BR.** Melvo's burning of Marelith destroyed a refuge as well as a pirate harbour, and island songs call it a second Kelarion. Authorially it was a massacre by fire, not a refusal at a chain; no wound opened and the King did not stir.
