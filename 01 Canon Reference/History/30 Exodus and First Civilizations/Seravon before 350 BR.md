# Seravon before 350 BR

Seravon was a populous great island of the central Thalassian sea, in the zone where the later [[The Sermoran Republic Overview|Sermoran Republic]] developed. It lay east of the western Thalmyric and Saronikan maritime region, west of a neighbouring archipelago, and west or north-west of most Eclessian territory. Its coasts, uplands and harbours belonged to several ancient communities and rulers. The later republic's capital and jurisdiction cannot be projected back over the whole island.

Locally rooted [[Elmaric Island Peoples before 350 BR|Elmaric peoples]] remained the island's largest cultural family at 350 BR. Southern and Glass-descended settlers, traders, refugees and captives entered its ports over a long period. Their influence was uneven. Some settler houses controlled fortified quays and grain stores despite governing no majority of island households. Older shore communities, pilots and inland dynasties continued to negotiate, resist and rule in their own places.

## An island of several polities

Seravon's size and varied ground gave its communities different routes to food and power. A coastal house could command a landing and the ships using it while lacking reliable authority over an inland valley. An upland ruler might collect from farms and pasture but need coastal pilots to reach the neighbouring chain. The long island history therefore produced different dialects, cults, tenure rules and dynasties among peoples related to the wider Elmaric family. A foreign treaty with one port did not surrender the rest of the island.

The older populations survived the Giant collapse locally. Later southern and Glass-descended houses entered occupied shores. Some obtained land or marriage ties through negotiation; others seized stores, built stockades and forced older families into dependency. Captives and freed people added further ancestry. These layers did not disappear when a strong harbour called itself the island's representative. By 350 BR Elmaric speech and family connections remained widely present even where settlers controlled a disproportionate share of ships or walls.

Food and shipping linked Seravon to its neighbouring chain and to the wider Elmaric circuit. Coastal fisheries, inland cultivation, stores and convoy access gave rival harbours different kinds of leverage. Dynastic houses were prominent in this zone, but no house spoke for every port. One harbour later absorbed into the Sermoran capital remembered two foundations: a settler landing and seizure of shore, followed by a recovery led by older shore houses, returned exiles, escaped captives, local fighters and defectors. The competing accounts preserve different parts of that conflict; neither founded all Seravon.

## The harbour founded twice

Settler-descended houses dated the predecessor harbour from a damaged convoy's landing, a seasonal quarter, the building of a stockade and the taking of grain, shore and repair facilities. Their story made the risk of settlement and the continuity of their houses the beginning of civic life. Older Elmaric shore families remembered a later return: exiles and escaped captives joined local fighters, pilots and settler defectors to defeat a punitive fleet and recover the harbour. Their story treated possession won by conquest as a break to be overcome rather than a foundation.

Neither memory was wholly invented. The first neglected people whose work made a harbour possible before the convoy arrived. The second could obscure those who had remained through the occupation or who defected from the settlers. As the later Sermoran capital incorporated this harbour, the two memories competed over precedence. The event remained the history of one important place, not a charter for the whole great island or an account of all Elmaric origins.

## Supply, sanctuary and rivals

Island food did not make each port self-sufficient. Dynasties needed grain shipments, repair materials, ship timber and pilots through neighbouring channels. A house with a fortified store could lend during shortage and demand allegiance in return; a harbour without safe access to outside fields was vulnerable even if its own walls held. Treaty purchase and contested outer grain holdings linked Seravon to older inhabitants of other shores, some of whom cultivated under rent or tribute and others under coercion.

Seravon houses attended [[Ilamar before 350 BR|Ilamar's sanctuary]] for oaths, ransom and grain records, but no dynasty appointed all its custodians. A league could pool Seravon ships with archipelago allies for a season, then fall apart over captives, tolls or a foreign contract. The island's neighbouring chain retained pilot communities and politics of its own. This divided setting explains both Seravon's maritime weight and its inability to act as a single ancient Sermoran state.

Seravon's relations with [[Ilamar before 350 BR|Ilamar]] and the [[Elmarai Isles before 350 BR|Elmarai Isles]] involved trade, kinship, sanctuary and warfare. Its harbours could join a convoy or foreign campaign without binding the rest of the island. By the opening of Atheria's imperial age, Seravon remained a multi-polity Elmaric island. Its later conquest and republican institutions belong to a subsequent history.

The contrast with its later republic matters because the ancient zone leaned towards dynastic houses while the later Sermoran capital became a republic of captains and merchant institutions. That was a change produced by subsequent conquest and civic struggle, not an original constitution waiting to emerge. Precise coastlines, the neighbouring chain's individual names and the positions of Seravon's early courts remain to be established; its location relative to Saronika, the central Sestaran zone and Eclessia is fixed only in broad terms.
