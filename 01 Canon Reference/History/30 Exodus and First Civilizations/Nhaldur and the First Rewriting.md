# Nhaldur and the First Rewriting


**Nhaldur** was a southern Giant penal crownland operating before and during the Great Unbinding. Six principal ring-fortresses controlled scores of mine prisons, labour camps, road towers, hostage houses, harbour compounds and geothermal estates near the polar country. It was never a sixth Imperial Horizon. The Five Imperial Horizons and many lesser Giant crowns sent prisoners there. The southern cold-country crowns used it most heavily because their roads could reach Nhaldur and because they wanted defeated princes, rebellious officers, dangerous relatives and skilled human captives removed from their own courts.

The name survives in Kharadi road copies and in later southern translations. It may originally have meant the jurisdiction of a chain or locked road rather than one bounded country. Nhaldur's practical territory extended only as far as its stores, patrols and heating systems could support a garrison. Older fishing, hunting and farming communities remained beyond those reaches, sometimes trading with the prisons and sometimes losing people or land to them.

The Crownland developed during the last centuries of Giant power. A Kharadi cadet house first fortified the polar road as an iron and stormglass concession. Qianglong and Ishkatari courts then paid to confine prisoners there. Xolotl, Nythalum and Anshara added compounds and client houses. Lesser southern crowns built most of the outlying prisons and supplied much of the daily population. By c. 2450 BR, Nhaldur possessed a recognised inter-court government, an evacuation harbour and several generations of people born under sentences imposed upon their parents.

## The Council of Keys

Nhaldur was governed through five **Key Seats**. Each contributing house held one key to the central stores, archive and warded road. Three keys could appoint a **Crown Gaoler**, authorise ordinary transfers and divide revenue. Opening every deep cell, changing the heat allotment or executing a foreign Giant prisoner formally required all five.

The Key Houses were prison dynasties, not free provincial governors. Each had been banished, condemned to hereditary service or lodged in Nhaldur as security for a foreign court. Its members lived in fortified apartments, retained servants and commanded guards, but could not return home without the court which owned its sentence lifting the key. They governed people whose confinement was harsher while remaining prisoners themselves. This produced the Crownland's characteristic hierarchy: one captive could hold another captive's cell key and still be killed for crossing the outer road.

The constitution was designed to prevent one house from seizing the prisoners of its rivals. In practice, stores and force mattered more than procedure. A house which controlled winter grain could obtain a signature from a hungry councillor. Keys were copied, withheld and inherited through disputed Giant genealogies. Human clerks made the system function by identifying bodies, sentences, work skills, dependants and ration claims across several scripts.

Five houses dominated the last generation:

- **House Vorzhal**, descended from Kharadi road officials, controlled the Crown Gaoler's guard, the hostage archive at Ossar and most transport warrants. It expected iron, fees and political leverage over every court which sent a prisoner.
- **House Nemek**, a mixed Qianglong–Kharadi bodily workshop, managed infirmaries, breeding compounds and punitive alteration. Its rulers promised soldiers and labourers able to survive cold, injury and reduced food.
- **House Rathkar**, connected to Ishkatari estate administration, supervised mines, debt sentences and corpse accounting. It sought metal and evidence that property claims survived removal.
- **House Ulthara**, a Nythalum engineering exile elevated into local office, maintained storm instruments, pressure galleries and parts of the harbour fleet. It exchanged technical work for reduced sentences and proposed berths which Nythalum never guaranteed.
- **House Salkesh**, formed from Xolotlan and Ansharan clients, supplied soldiers, sacrifice specialists and captive brokers. It used the Crownland to remove political enemies while building an armed household far from either patron.

These were Giant political houses rather than five creaturely kinds. Their marriages, concubinage, adoptions and service households crossed older imperial and lesser-crown ancestry. Some members were genuine Dynastic Giants; others were giant-blooded humans with complete human souls. The majority of people under their rule were human.

## Prisoners, workers and older inhabitants

Nhaldur's registers divided human beings according to the authority which claimed them. **Sentence people** were condemned individuals and their inherited dependants. **Term hands** theoretically owed a fixed period of labour, although fines and ration debt often extended it. **Hostage households** guaranteed the conduct of a distant city, estate or Giant prisoner. **Crown workers** possessed wages and legal standing while remaining forbidden to leave. **Free carriers** supplied the fortresses and could depart when weather and road permits allowed. Separate Giant rolls listed exiled dynasts, failed cadets, politically dangerous children and condemned guards from the lesser southern crowns.

The categories concealed as much as they explained. A child born to a sentence household could be entered as part of the original offence. A free carrier who lost a sledge became a debtor. A skilled prisoner might receive better food, rooms and authority over other captives without acquiring freedom. Human overseers could beat, sell or protect people beneath them. Giant prisoners sometimes owned human servants and continued that ownership inside their own confinement.

Older southern communities entered the system through land seizure and compulsory supply. The coast villages near Avarn owed fish, oil, pilots and hull repair. Sheltered-valley households around Suldren supplied grain and maintained warm-water channels. Molkar took miners from upland communities and then required their relatives to deliver charcoal. People fled these obligations, but winter travel without food and permission was often fatal.

Nhaldur also created mixed neighbourhoods. Prisoners learnt one another's work words, married across origin and raised children who understood the Crownland as home. Some guards formed friendships or households with captives while continuing to benefit from the prison. Such attachments later enabled rescues and betrayals. They did not turn confinement into consent.

## Six ring-fortresses

The six rings were the Crownland's administrative and supply centres, not its only prisons. Each governed smaller sites: shaft cages around Molkar, heatwork barracks around Suldren, island cells and quay compounds under Avarn, road towers below Veyrun, hostage houses around Ossar and punishment camps supplied from Kharvos. A minor prison might hold one Giant claimant and two hundred human workers, or a single condemned household guarding another set of prisoners. When the rings lost food, heat or orders, most of their satellites failed first.

### Ossar Ring

Ossar held the Council of Keys, foreign envoys, the central hostage archive and the largest legal prison for Giant dynasts. Its wall contained offices, apartments, guard rooms and narrow cells in successive arcs. The open centre held storehouses and an assembly ground on which transfer lists were read.

The ring depended upon grain from the north and heat from Suldren. During the War of Open Keys, rival councillors loaded its eastern and western arcs with incompatible ward commands. The wall cracked through occupied rooms. Survivors quarried it after the collapse, while later visitors mistook the two broken crescents for separate fortresses.

### Molkar

Molkar controlled iron mines and hard-stone quarries in a valley where winter smoke could remain trapped for days. Human miners, charcoal burners, lifters and smiths made up most of its population. House Rathkar maintained severe body and corpse tallies because dead workers' ration debts were charged to their households.

Miners took the lower shafts during the revolt and cut the lifting cages. Rathkar guards fired timber supports while attempting to smoke them out. The fire entered old seams and continued beneath the snow for nine winters. Some galleries remain sealed; no surviving Titan fragment or immortal furnace is established there.

### Suldren

Suldren enclosed hot springs, pipe chambers, bath infirmaries and glasshouses which produced greens and medicinal plants through winter. Its heat moved through stone conduits to nearby barracks and settlements. Control of the valves gave human engineers unusual bargaining power.

Vaska Ren organised the **Measured Fuel stoppage** after wardens demanded heat for military workshops while cutting nursery rations. Her crews marked protected pipes and shut industrial branches in sequence. A Giant counterattack broke a retaining wall and flooded the lower heatworks. Parts of the system survived as local warm pools; most deep galleries mineralised or froze.

### Avarn

Avarn was a harbour prison, shipyard and supply customs post. Its ring opened towards a protected inlet whose boom could trap a ship after inspection. Free pilots, enslaved rowers, hull workers, fishing households and foreign guards lived close enough to form common work crews and separate legal communities.

When northern command failed, the wardens reserved the sound sea-going vessels for Giant houses and locked human dependants behind the quay gate. Sorna Vale opened the river barges and smaller fishing craft instead. Her flotilla carried people and seed along the coast, but it could not take everyone. The last Giant ships left hundreds on the quay, and fighting over the remaining dried fish killed people whom no vessel had room to carry.

### Veyrun

Veyrun guarded the eastern mountain road. Its tuned horns, air shafts, bridge chambers and speaking galleries formed a relay system. A caller spoke an exact command into a shaped wall mouth; the chamber strengthened the words and carried them to the next tower, where another crew repeated them. In this way a message crossed a ravine or reached a gate during a blizzard which would have swallowed an ordinary shout. Separate pressure phrases could close a vent, tilt a suspended load or strike a work gang. The relay did not summon lightning from the sky.

Human callers learnt more than their assigned phrases. They tested intervals during repair, altered echoes and passed warnings through labour songs. During the revolt, callers opened the eastern bridge against House Ulthara's order and broke a pursuit gate behind the refugees. The escaped vocabulary became one root of the [[The Deep Voice|Deep Voice]]. Veyrun supplied a technique and a violent institution, not a hereditary magical bloodline.

### Kharvos

Kharvos combined grain vaults, barracks, punishment courts and House Nemek's bodily workshops. Its wall was broad enough to hold entire training streets. The lower complex had separate water, ration and ventilation systems so that captives could be isolated for trials.

The fortress became strategically valuable when the Crownland armies began recruiting from their cells. Crown Gaoler Urdazar Vorzhal intended to turn the largest human prisoner population into a dependable cold legion. A dependent Wrath Mask hidden among the Xolotlan prison staff turned that project towards Furor's larger purpose. Kharvos became the site of the First Rewriting and the earliest lasting Grobi refuge.

## The War of Open Keys

News of the wider Unbinding reached Nhaldur in contradictory pieces. A Qianglong command ordered military prisoners sealed. An Ishkatari letter demanded the return of estate claimants to a river system already failing. Nythalum agents tried to recall engineers while denying places to their dependants. Each local house suspected the others of concealing a viable escape route.

Urdazar Vorzhal used three keys to renew himself as Crown Gaoler and declared the missing seats forfeit. He opened selected Giant cells, recruited their retainers and took the children of human officers as hostages. Kharoth Rathkar seized Molkar's output and demanded food in metal's name. Nashara Nemek promised an army which could settle the dispute before the next winter.

Human resistance joined existing work relations. Ilyen Marr copied transfer rolls at Kharvos and issued orders moving threatened households away from Nemek selection. Vaska Ren coordinated the heat stoppage. Sorna Vale carried messages between the coast and river road. Miners, cooks, guards, clerks and local farmers attacked at different times for different purposes. Some wanted every cell opened. Others feared armed Giant prisoners and defended the doors.

The war also divided imprisoned Giants. **Zarven Voss**, a bodily surviving Dynastic Giant engineer of House Ulthara, agreed to keep Veyrun's eastern bridge open for escaping households in exchange for the release of her human crew. She honoured the agreement after northern berth news gave her no advantage in doing so. House Salkesh killed her during the retreat. Her act saved people and did not grant her a human soul, sainthood or innocence for her earlier administration.

## The Kharvos levy

Urdazar's final prisoner levy was an administrative act before it became a supernatural catastrophe. Kharvos guards collected four registered groups:

| Registered group | People | Reason for selection |
|---|---:|---|
| Molkar mine prisoners and dependants | 611 | strength, quarry experience and collective punishment for the shaft revolt |
| mutinous or deserting human soldiers | 487 | existing weapons training and exemplary reconditioning |
| Suldren heatworkers and children | 532 | cold-work skill and retaliation for the fuel stoppage |
| hostages from older southern communities | 384 | route knowledge and pressure upon outside suppliers |
| **Total** | **2,014** | population confined in Kharvos's lower barracks |

The roll counted children and adults as bodies available to the project. It did not record consent, family integrity or the people who died during transfer. Ilyen copied the final list before falsifying an order which allowed thirty-seven additional prisoners to avoid the lower barracks. Those thirty-seven remained human and later supplied testimony. They are not part of the 2,014.

Nashara's method joined controlled starvation, brief overfeeding, forced combat, cold exposure, blood and gland preparations from Giant breeding work, and resonant commands intended to bind appetite to obedience. A **Red Reckoner**, received as a Xolotlan casualty clerk, altered her tables and taught her to sort the prisoners by appetite, feed victors with the flesh of the defeated and reserve the final grain opening for bodies which had killed to reach it. These instructions were not neutral Giant science. They prepared a living army for Wrath at the moment when Giant power was leaving much of the world without its former masters.

Nashara understood that the regimen was cruel and chose it because it promised results. The surviving evidence does not establish whether she recognised the clerk as a Mask. Urdazar approved the punishments without investigating their source. Neither Giant craft nor the Reckoner itself could create or remove the Image-bearing human soul. Wrath alone possessed the power and intent to complete the categorical act.

The lower barracks were sealed for nineteen days. Fighting cut one water gallery. Guards executed prisoners at the ration doors when the confined population tried to break out. Urdazar then ordered the full food store opened to the survivors so that Nemek observers could select the strongest bodies. This was the final action specified in the Reckoner's altered programme. Killing, humiliation, hunger and anticipated violence gave Wrath the Material pattern it intended to seize.

## The First Rewriting

Wrath performed the categorical act. The 2,014 human souls separated and routed towards judgement. Furor then tore down and rebuilt the vacated flesh. Hunger governed growth, consumed tissue became rapid repair, victory opened the path to greater size and physical defeat brought reversion or death. Wrath intended a self-reproducing Material army which could expand without the workshops, roads and pay systems of the fallen Giant states. Every successful attack would feed both the bodies and the Idol's wider dominion.

The event created no damned human race and no collection of Masks. It began a true biological lineage capable of reproduction, language, memory, cunning, suffering and death. Wrath did not give that lineage the human moral conscience which apprehends justice, mercy or another person's dignity as goods. Grobi could choose whom to follow, how to hunt, when to bargain and which victim to attack; they could not become moral neighbours by deciding that domination and consumption were wrong. Pack attachment and care for young were real animal and social bonds ordered towards survival, possession and strength.

The first Grobi emerged in different bodily sizes because the prisoners had received different food, punishment and preparations. That variation became the basis of the later food-driven stages. It did not create five species or hereditary castes. The new beings retained some trained movement, sounds and material traces of memory in altered brains. None was the human person whose soul had departed. Later Grobi inherited Wrath's predatory nature, not the personal guilt or memory of the human victims.

The newly awakened population broke the ration doors and killed Nashara. Urdazar escaped the lower court, tried to seal the ring and died when Grobi opened the grain lift beneath him. The violence spread into upper Kharvos. Human prisoners outside the lower barracks remained human; those killed by the first Grobi followed ordinary human death. No second automatic Rewriting followed contact, blood or captivity.

Later Wrath cults called Kharvos the **Table Under Stone** and claimed Urdazar had served the Red Appetite knowingly. The claim exaggerates what the surviving records establish. Wrath planned the Rewriting and directed Nashara's regimen through a Red Reckoner. Nashara and Urdazar chose to carry out the atrocities because those acts served their political purposes; neither became Furor's prophetic founder merely because the Idol used their work.

## Registers and memories

Ilyen's copied list became the principal human record. Descendant communities read the names at winter gatherings under the title **Names Beneath the Ninth Door**. The reading commemorated the human victims, including criminals, soldiers, captives and children without claiming that every person had been innocent of every earlier act. The wrong remembered was their reduction to bodies and the destruction of their embodied future.

Grobi traditions preserved other memories. Kharvos packs spoke of waking under a shut roof, food above the head and prey at every door. Ash-Marrow priests called the first slaughter the proof that eating and mastery were the same law. Narrow-Bowl recitations used the event to justify preserving enough young to survive after the strongest fighters had fed. Grobi did not identify the human register as their ancestry because the human persons and the new living continuity were categorically distinct.

The two histories can therefore refer to the same bodies without becoming the same genealogy. Archaeological evidence includes Ilyen's roll fragments, altered lower-barrack bones, Nemek preparation vessels, breached ration lifts and early Grobi birth rooms built after the event. None by itself establishes a complete witness account.

## Historical inheritance

Nhaldur scattered people and knowledge without founding every southern society. Avarn refugees strengthened some coastal settlements. Suldren workers carried greenhouse and pipe craft into sheltered valleys. Veyrun callers helped create eastern resonance schools. Molkar miners entered upland iron communities. Exiled lesser Giants and giant-blooded humans emerged as war leaders where their strength, endurance or inherited craft outmatched ordinary fighters. Some established short-lived tyrannies; others entered local households through marriage, concubinage or captivity. Most later descendants in those populations were human, possessed complete human souls and could inherit particular Giant bodily or magical traits. Rare children born as true Giants retained Giant creaturely continuity and belonged to a separate classification. Former guards and client households also carried weapons, ownership claims and habits of punishment which outlived the crown.

The Crownland's ruins acquired separate histories. Human communities occupied some rings, quarried others and avoided Kharvos. Grobi hearths used warm galleries and defensible walls. Later rulers claimed prison keys as titles even when the locks no longer existed. No intact Nhalduran central office, Giant continuity project or common royal successor survived.

## Authority and memory

### Authorial history

- Nhaldur, the Council of Keys, six principal fortresses, their satellite prisons and their material functions;
- the five imprisoned ruling houses, the Imperial Horizons and the lesser southern crowns which used the system;
- the War of Open Keys and named human revolt leaders;
- the registered 2,014 people subjected to the First Rewriting at Kharvos;
- Wrath's deliberate plan, direct intervention through a Red Reckoner and sole authorship of the categorical transformation;
- the separate fates of human souls, new Grobi life and human victims outside the lower barracks.

### Received histories

- the Names Beneath the Ninth Door as a later human memorial with a genuine copied register;
- Grobi Table Under Stone and Narrow-Bowl songs as partial, interested traditions;
- later claims that one surviving key grants sovereignty over all ring ruins.

### Protected or excluded claims

- no new Imperial Horizon;
- no human-to-Grobi transformation after the First Rewriting through ordinary blood, adoption or captivity;
- no Grobi soul route to the Vestibule;
- no Giant technique capable of authoring Grobi continuity;
- no human moral conscience within Grobi continuity; intelligence, cunning, memory, suffering and pack attachment remain real;
- no surviving Titan-Firstborn or exact present-day state assigned to the deepest ruins;
- no equation of Veyrun resonance with innate dragon speech, Menhir sovereignty or later Sermoran Harmonic institutions.
