# Southern Cold Belt and Grobi Frontier

The known world is southern-hemisphere oriented. South means colder. The deep south is the cold belt: taiga, dark woodland, permafrost, frozen rivers, hard passes, long winters, and survival corridors.

## Climate Rule

When designing maps, campaigns, migrations, wars, and calendars:

- do not assume north equals cold;
- southward travel should increasingly mean frost, snow, taiga, permafrost, short growing seasons, and harder logistics;
- southern coasts and highlands should have cold seas, fog, storm tracks, and dangerous winter crossings;
- summer is a campaigning window, not a guarantee of comfort;
- winter is a strategic force.

## Peoples of the Cold Belt

The Folk and Ättar are distinct southern cold-frontier peoples. Their cultures are shaped by:

- forest survival,
- clan halls,
- winter stores,
- ancestor law,
- Menhir pact territories,
- moon and beast rites,
- permafrost burials,
- cold-road hospitality,
- raids and counter-raids,
- distrust of southern expansion by church, bank, and crown.

Among the Folk, this pressure produces longhouses, ringworks, hidden granaries, beacon chains, refuge roads, and the [[Frost-Road and the Winter War|Frost-Road]]. Among the Ättar it produces fortified mead halls, chief-sized sally doors, beacon ranges, the annual [[Winter War and the Grobi Frontier|winter muster]], and the disastrous firstborn bargain made during the [[War of the Empty Halls]].

## Grobi Pressure

The Grobi frontier belongs strongly to the deep southern cold belt. The Grobi are not simply a generic border threat; they are tied to seasonal scarcity, hunger, migration, forest warfare, and frozen terrain.

Key pressures:

- winter migration out of exhausted hunting grounds;
- raids through frozen passes and river corridors;
- hunger-driven attacks on granaries, herds, and isolated villages;
- summer growth and warband expansion;
- cold-adapted camps, caves, and forest holds;
- border cultures built around watches, beacon fires, stockades, and evacuation roads.

For the Folk, frontier clans bear the direct obligation while people from safer regions travel to the fighting as a path to honor, followers, marriage standing, and eligibility for office. Winter service includes stores, transport, refuge, healing, and scouting as well as battle.
