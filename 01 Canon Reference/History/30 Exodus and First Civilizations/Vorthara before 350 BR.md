# Vorthara before 350 BR

Vorthara grew from a human refuge inside and around the Black Spire into a substantial pass town by 350 BR. The Spire stood at a mountain knot before Atherian rule, and its original builders and purpose remain unknown. Escaped slaves and other outsiders occupied accessible shelter there while Giant powers avoided close approach. Later ridge households, traders and magical specialists joined the settlement. No founding charter, single ruler or common law made it a city at one recorded moment.

The Spire's effects appear to have made approach especially dangerous to Giants and people with strongly active Giant inheritance. Ordinary humans could use parts of it. Refugees first survived through concealment, water, small plots and warning paths. Over centuries, occupied interior spaces, buildings at the Spire's foot, the ascent market and neighbouring ridges became one lived settlement.

## From hidden refuge to pass town

The oldest known habitation was a shelter within accessible Spire spaces or immediately against them. Escaped slaves could recognise a destination avoided by their pursuers and enter it with help from people already there. They still needed food, water, places for the sick and ways to warn one another when a master sent human agents. Small refuges spread onto the ridges as the population grew. Their paths and shared stories tied them to the first shelter.

The Unbinding ended much Giant control elsewhere but did not end the need for refuge. Displaced dependants, people pursued by new rulers and workers whose skills attracted dangerous patrons continued to arrive. Some families defended escapees; others profited by selling labour or information. A household could shelter one stranger and refuse another. An old refuge path could later carry traders into the same inhabited area.

Across the Hearth and City-State ages, terraces, herds, repair places and exchange on the pass approaches made the settlement more than a hiding place. Accessible interiors became occupied rooms and routes, while buildings gathered against the lower exterior and at the Spire's foot. Freight, water and repeated movement between these areas made the clusters one town in practice. A dispute could cross several defended thresholds, each with a different host and means of enforcement.

## Work and shelter

By the Age of Stars, the town grew medicinal and magical plants on nearby terraces, raised some food and still imported grain. Repairs, medicine, freight, lodging and services other cities restricted brought customers. A stranger's safety depended on a host, a faction or the ability to leave; there was no citywide police force.

The ascent market joined ordinary provisioning to rare services: a visitor might seek a guide, healer, instrument, reagent, discreet lodging or forbidden craft. Repair workers depended on freight; growers depended on buyers and protected water; a powerful mage still needed food carried up the road. These mutual needs encouraged limited agreements to keep a passage usable even among groups that feared one another.

Vorthara's reputation drew people wishing to disappear, including fugitives from slavery and practitioners avoiding particular authorities. It also drew captive traders.

## Refuge, captivity and local power

A fugitive needed a household, gang, practitioner or other group willing and able to defend a door. A person who reached one refuge could be unsafe on the next public route. This made Vorthara attractive to people fleeing claims from outside while leaving them exposed to bargains or enslavement made inside. An imperial officer, creditor or former owner could try to seize someone, but had no general local institution obliged to enforce the claim and could face several armed opponents.

Captive sellers found a market for labour and for services other cities forbade. Those fleeing captivity had descendants and allies within the same town. Rescue, purchase of freedom, abduction and reprisal could occur along the same paths used for freight. Traders and refuge households sought help from stronger practitioners according to the case, which meant a dispute over one person could become a quarrel over a market route. Other factions intervened when that threatened their own work. Such intervention kept business moving more often than it freed captives or abolished trade.

Vorthara's autonomy was therefore uneven. A group defended its own premises, and strength, reputation, payment and relationships determined whether neighbours respected a boundary. A vulnerable resident could not appeal to one city magistrate. Conversely, an outside king could not assume that threatening the pass would make every faction submit; even rival practitioners had reasons to resist a power demanding authority over all their doors.

## Death-work and cult courts

Several waves of Qahuri mortuary workers contributed preservation, funerary contracts and estate craft to Vorthara. Once in the town, workers adapted instruments and body custody to pass hazards, contracts and clients who could not seek a public court elsewhere. By 350 BR a Qahuri-rooted association called the [[The Pale Hand before 350 BR|Pale Hand]] led a recognisable death-working quarter. Other specialists entered its crews. Preserved and animated bodies performed hazardous work. Families and clients disputed the handling of bodies and the terms under which the dead could be put to labour.

A distinct adjoining area of cult courts grew from older refuge rites and paid services to travellers. Menhir-related courts, Idol houses, Egg White patron rooms and other human cults displayed incompatible claims. Small houses could rent a room, claim a patron, gather adherents and disappear. Menhir-associated courts might offer place-bound bargains, Idol houses human devotion to predatory powers, and Egg White rooms access to particular patrons or intermediaries.

## The Provider house, c. 505–498 BR

Some of Qarzeth's exiles came up the pass after their city fell: kin of its priests, sanctuary servants and patrons who had lost their gardens to Atherian salt. About 505 BR they opened a house of the Bronze-Faced Provider at the edge of the cult courts, hung a bronze face over the door and laid a long table every evening for anyone who would eat. Hungry porters and fugitives did. Ridge children said the crumbs on that table crawled.

Refuge households, several of them descended from Gateway families who had hidden children from the collectors, carried salt up from the market and poured it across the threshold, as they had heard the Atherians had done at Qarzeth. The table was laid the next night, and the night after. The house closed only when its supply failed. It needed bodies for its table and its back rooms, and it asked the [[The Pale Hand before 350 BR|Pale Hand]]'s crews to sell it the dead they carried. The Hand refused. Its porters stopped calling at the bronze door, and within a season, around 498, the table stood empty and the house's keepers went back down the pass. Vortharans told the story afterwards to show that salt on a doorstep keeps nothing out, and that a corpse porter's refusal can.

## The Spire Pursuit, 410 BR

In 410 BR enslaved workers and fugitives rose along an Atherian supply corridor, burning estate stores and seizing carts meant for the armies. Companies hired by the estate owners on the credit of the financier [[Lethren Ostar]] broke the rear of their column at the foot of the pass. The captives taken in the field were roped together and sold north in lots to the well trade, and the owners he had credited praised the price. A band of survivors fled the other way, up the pass to the Black Spire, which fugitives had used for longer than anyone could count.

Ostar's agents followed them with money instead of soldiers. Captive traders inside the town sold them every fugitive they could find, and the agents sent their purchases down the pass in chains to the ore workings of Olveth. Several refuge households refused to give up the people in their rooms. They barred their doors, fed the hunted inside, and swore on one threshold to hold their doors together: an attack on one house would bring the others. This oath, the **Kept Doors**, bound a handful of households and no one else. The traders kept their warehouses and their customers, and most fugitives without a door were taken. Those who had one lived, and their descendants still show visitors the threshold where the oath was sworn.

Thirty-one years later Ostar came up the same pass as a fugitive, entered against his will as a contestant on a Caleran war-rite and stripped of his ledgers. He lived about five years in rented rooms above the ascent market and died there around 374. Caleran said the Kept Doors stayed shut against him. Some of the sworn households say one of them opened.

## The Atherian boundary

Around 350 BR, Atheria claimed the surrounding routes and publicly treated Vorthara as a small lawful market. A temporary gathering of households, pass keepers and influential practitioners reached a limited external understanding that left internal premises in local hands.

The official description was convenient to both parties. Atheria could watch the approaches and purchase services without taking responsibility for every cult, corpse yard or captive dispute. Vortharan groups could keep their own premises while avoiding a war over land the empire valued mainly as a route and destination for expertise. The arrangement rested on a temporary ability to negotiate externally. Atherian expansion would later produce more formal contracts and perimeter infrastructure.
