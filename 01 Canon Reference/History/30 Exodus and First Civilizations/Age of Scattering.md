# Age of Scattering

The Age of Scattering begins after the Prophet's death around c. 2165 BR, in the hungry years after the provision of the Glass stopped. One people formed during [[The Glass Sojourn]] divides through two gradual, geography-driven fissions, while older human communities continue independent reassembly outside the Ark and later populations braid those histories together. The southern companies, fed for a century and never taught to farm, took fields and farmers from the people already living south of the Glass, and many of them turned towards stars that still answered. This is the hinge between sacred prehistory and recognisable cultures.

## Ark Scattering

### Vacant Commission, c. 2165–2130 BR

The Prophet leaves commanders, judges, archivists, provisioners and household elders with real but bounded responsibilities. None inherits his universal commission. With the provision ended, the provisioners, who had only ever handed out what was given, hold the one store that matters: the seed reserve, kept alive through the Glass years in sealed jars. The rear camps, struck by drought, are eating it; the southern companies need it to plant. The attempted common southern sanctuary fails gradually over that store and over movement, land, punishment and command, and deaths from hoarding, requisition, failed scouting and factional violence follow.

### First Fission, c. 2130–2050 BR

Southern captains, craftsmen, surveyors, specialists and younger household coalitions pursue permanent settlement, repaired Giant infrastructure and offensive security. Camps remaining nearer the Glass maintain seasonal roads, graves, registers and older stations. Families, judges, workshops and trade continue to cross the growing divide for generations.

The southern leagues become recognisably **proto-Atherian** through the movement itself. Their inherited measures, records, crafts and military organisation aid survival.

### Southern Seizures, c. 2050–1900 BR

Proto-Atherian forts spread along rivers, hills, road junctions and coasts already inhabited by Orphaned villages, harbour communities, Giant-client remnants and separate Folk clans. Fields were useless to settlers who did not know the farming year, so the forts took farmers with the land. Their expansion includes massacre, slavery, hostage-taking, expulsion and land seizure as well as treaty, local alliance, defection, adoption, intermarriage and the destruction or absorption of failed Atherian forts. Hostage-taking, adoption and marriage fell hardest on farming households.

These contacts began the early harbour braids and the first lasting exchanges among recovering towns. Local pilots, smiths, farmers, healers and route-keepers remained indispensable.

### Northern Fission, c. 1950–1750 BR

Historians use **Near-Glass camp continuum** for the related camps that remained close to the desert after the southern separation. Water scarcity, distance, marriage, trade, raiding and reciprocal contact with older desert communities and Orphaned Tarkhan gradually made one camp system unworkable.

- **Proto-Jirahar** develop portable courts, translocal registers, protected burial obligation, route memory and later tower towns whose legitimacy does not depend upon owning all surrounding land.
- **Early Ahdari communities** develop defended wells, territorial coalitions, guest protection, blood-price, rescue debt and public enforcement. Older desert and Tarkhan people join them. The broad Ahdari name becomes common during the later Bronze-Road centuries.

The two later northern formations share real ancestry. The northern fission begins late in the formal Scattering and completes during the [[Age of Hearths and First Kings]].

## Orphaned Reassembly

Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Nsizwa are principally descended from communities that survive the Giant collapse outside the Ark. Their ancestors include subject villages, local revolts, failing client states, slave-soldier bands, hidden enclaves, mountain and forest refugees, island survivors and people born later among Giant ruins.

Their histories of liberation are local and uneven. Some know the Prophet only as rumour, foreign law, adopted story or not at all.

In the northern dry belt, the flying Giant kingdom of [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking|Vathra]] broke apart during the Unbinding. Giant civil war destroyed its command system while human tether crews, camel keepers, soldiers and captives seized wells and landing grounds. Some crown-cities crashed; others were grounded, cast loose or carried beyond human pursuit. The human survivors rebuilt among wells, seasonal lakes and eastern grasslands. Their communities became the principal ancestors of the Tarkhan.

In the southern cold world, [[Nhaldur and the First Rewriting|Nhaldur's]] prison roads and ring-fortresses fell separately during the War of Open Keys. Human captives, lesser Giants and giant-blooded families escaped into older coastal, forest, river and upland communities. These mixtures contributed to the later Folk and Ättar. At Kharvos, some two thousand captives, by the Nhalduran count, were rewritten into the first Grobi, whose descendants formed a separate living lineage.

## Braided Peoples

Sestaran harbour populations and Vesari emerge later through distinct mixtures of Ark-derived migrants, Orphaned communities and other local populations. Later Eclessians are a further Braided ethnogenesis of Atherian provincial, Folk, Markish, Vesari and other regional layers.

## What Gets Lost

No Ark-derived people keeps the whole Exodus inheritance intact. Orphaned peoples preserve different truths and errors about the fallen Giant world. Braided peoples inherit several memories that do not always agree. Some lose doctrine but preserve ritual; some preserve law but become hard-hearted; some keep practical survival skills inside false history; some turn Giant remnants into prestige.

## What Gets Made

The Scattering produces:

- ancestor houses,
- clan marks,
- oasis covenants,
- caravan routes,
- first holy roads,
- settlement taboos,
- early calendars,
- memory specialists,
- oral law,
- sacred suspicion of slavery and false gods.

## Inherited Wound

Every human culture descends from people who survived the end of Giant rule, but not every culture descends from the Glass Ark.

## One people, several directions

Scattering was a long separation of households which continued to trade, marry and quarrel. After c. 2165 BR, no office could make the whole Glass People move together. A commander could defend a camp but could not inherit the Prophet's voice. A keeper of the old routes could preserve testimony but could not feed every settlement. Arguments about fidelity became arguments about where children would live through the next season, and what they would eat.

The hunger had a shape. For a century the host had eaten what was given; now the provisioners' jars held the only food that could become more food. Rear camps in the drought ground seed corn into gruel and watched the jars go down. Southern captains counted the same jars as next year's fields. Each side called the other's bread theft.

Before his death, the Prophet commissioned a scout-captain to reconnoitre the inhabited lands beyond the Glass and guide the first safe movement. His repeated circuits—scouting, escorting and returning—helped establish the mobile discipline later inherited by Jirahar road companies and courts. [[G-56 - The Empty Seat Facing Outward|The Empty Seat Facing Outward]] remembers the dispute over this narrow mandate; the captain held no revelation, universal government or right to succeed the Prophet.

The tradition of [[G-71 - The Five Staffs and the Empty Granary|the Five Staffs]] remembers a rival commander taking grain before the rear camps' dependants had been counted. In the camps' memory that grain was seed: the rear camps were living on it, and the southern expedition needed it in the ground before the last rains. The first expedition succeeded, and its council repeated the levy while calling the stores seed, marching provision and settlement allotments. Two poorly connected camps suffered the clearest famine deaths; families boiled harness and buried children without shrouds. A judge ruled the policy unlawful and ordered removal and restitution, but the southern council refused both. The commander had taken the rear's bread, and the same seed had planted the fields that fed the south. Each side could prove its case with graves, which is why the quarrel never closed. Three historical staff-bearers became five in later song.

Movement also ended for reasons which no common council controlled. [[G-04 - The Seven Who Stopped Seeking Home|The Seven Who Stopped Seeking Home]] combines many unrelated parties which abandoned, lost or ceased following the migrating camps. Some returned to old worship; others remained through marriage, exhaustion, fear, error or attachment to a new home. Their survivors entered several existing communities, and later descent claims cannot identify their separate paths.

## The wrong seasons

The fallen Giant world went on working after its masters died. In valleys where the courts had kept artificial seasons, plague gardens and famine works, the old machinery ran on with nobody left to tend or halt it. Settlers and Orphaned villages met frost in high summer that kept the dead uncorrupted in their houses and killed every sprouting field; a garden of sickness that flowered out of season and emptied a hamlet; heat that followed a refugee column from valley to valley. Faewild powers moved in on the broken works, some to finish what the Giants had begun, some to halt it, some to deepen it, and none of them asked who deserved to live.

Later kings sang of a War of Unfinished Seasons, in which their ancestors had sided with the good season against a wicked one, and they counted a fine harvest among their allies. The farmers' memory is plainer. A valley survived when someone found the one local task that ended one wrong season: an engine-stone dug out of a field and broken, an old work calendar read back from tree rings and grave layers, a field left fallow on the day the Giant calendar had forbidden it. In some valleys four funeral calendars still overlap, because nobody could agree which season had won.

## The southern departure

Between c. 2130 and 2050 BR, captains, craftsmen and displaced households increasingly chose permanent settlements south of the Glass. They wanted fields, captured infrastructure and defensible crossings. Their relatives nearer the refuge preferred portable institutions and familiar routes. Neither side left all at once. Southern settlers still sent marriage partners and gifts north; camp families used forts they claimed to distrust.

The Southern Seizures, c. 2050–1900 BR, entered inhabited land, and the settlers soon learned that land without farmers fed nobody. Their first sowings rotted in wet ground or burned in the wrong month. Forts were taken through battle, betrayal and negotiated admission. Victors massacred defenders, enslaved families and seized cultivated land, and they took the ploughmen with the fields, the oxen with the ploughmen, and the daughters of farming households as wives and hostages. Enslaved Orphaned farmers taught their captors when to sow, how to read a flood and how to keep a second year's grain. Settler households came to count a captive who knew the farming year among their most valuable property, and the earliest Atherian customs of slavery and adoption grew around that need. Hosts also defeated settlers, took their children as hostages, adopted useful strangers or married into new ruling houses. Some settlements vanished after one generation. Others became genuinely mixed towns whose descendants could not separate conquest from belonging.

The Folk of the conquered country said: *"Heaven fed them a hundred years and sent them to eat our harvest."* Ark households answered: *"They were not fed because they were not called."* The quarrel was about bread, and it never closed. Folk land claims around Grenzburg still call the Seizures the time "the fed came for the farmers".

[[G-57 - The Gate Opened during Supper|The Gate Opened during Supper]] compresses several settler betrayals under guest-right and the later shared-meal peace intended to prevent them. [[G-58 - The Harbour Founded Twice|The Harbour Founded Twice]] distinguishes an ancestral Sermoran harbour's occupying first settlement from the mixed coastal victory which won its independence. [[G-72 - The Hostage Children of Oak and Glass|The Hostage Children]] remembers captives regularised as fosterlings, the execution of a fort-born man made Folk by marriage and adulthood rites, an unauthorised reprisal and the fosterling evacuation which saved children from both sides.

## Stars that still answer

The southern camps carried a saying out of the hungry years: *"The Lord who stopped feeding us; the stars that still answer."* For a century the Lord had fed the host daily. When the bread stopped on the threshold of a strange country, many could not tell a finished task from abandonment. In the south, offerings to the stars and to the powers of field and sea seemed to bring rain and safe harbour to whoever made them properly, and southern households brought their offerings there. Singers turned the Prophet into Astronax, the Star Herald, who had not died but ascended. Households nearer the Glass called the saying apostasy; southern priests called it experience. The road from that saying to the [[The Common Sky and the Formation of Astratheon|Common Sky]] of later Atheria was long, and every Atherian astrologer walked it.

Camp speech outlived the camps. Under Atherian, Jirahar and Ahdari lies an older layer of shared words, thickest where people measure, swear, draw water, judge, bury and name. The three languages kept the same words and let their meanings drift apart. The camp word for a provision-portion became an Atherian wage, a Jirahar portion owed to the dead and an Ahdari guest's share.

## The later northern fission

The Near-Glass continuum endured after the southern departure. Its later division, c. 1950–1750 BR, grew from water scarcity, distance and sustained contact with active Tarkhan societies. Some communities invested in travelling judgement, remembered routes and households able to cross political boundaries. Others invested in wells, walls, territorial oaths and restitution that could be enforced without moving away.

These tendencies contributed to proto-Jirahar and proto-Ahl-e-Ahd formation. [[G-59 - The Walking Court and the Wall That Stayed|The Walking Court and the Wall That Stayed]] remembers relatives making opposite choices during a raid: each saved some people and failed others. The surviving funeral custom lays one staff along the road and one stone facing the danger.

[[G-60 - The Bride of the Dry Road|The Son-in-Law and the Stolen Gods]] belongs to the same Near-Glass contact world. Its route-wise son-in-law left an exploitative Tarkhan-facing coalition with two wives, dependants, protected fugitives and allotted stock. His favourite wife secretly carried away her father's cult images; their later discovery turned religious fear and marital favouritism into a succession dispute within one travelling household.

## The city of the second hearth

One refugee city of these centuries survives only in a wedding custom. Its founders raised it on the edge of the Egg White from carried stone, builders' labour and one founder's remembered childhood streets. Tellers say the streets held wherever people walked them every day, while lovely remembered lanes that nobody used became dead ends. The founder neglected a living family to keep the remembered city whole, and later citizens refused to let the founder's memories decide who belonged.

Refugees who came afterwards built a second hearth beyond the old wall, repaired the drains, married their neighbours and buried their children in ground everyone had once called temporary. When the founder's heirs returned from exile, they remembered only the first hearth. Officials stopped carrying water to the outer district and withdrew the ward stones during a night attack. Houses burned, and people who had kept the city alive died outside gates they had mended with their own hands. Survivors carried a coal into diaspora, and [[G-19 - The Hearth Carried Twice|The Hearth Carried Twice]] keeps their rule that a home is carried twice, once in memory and once in work. Restoration songs blame a military emergency; diaspora versions remember deliberate abandonment; Patron traditions add that an old anchor shard punished the district.

## The world beyond those divisions

Folk clan worlds and the emerging Ättar halls were independently rebuilding southern landscapes. Tarkhan well communities and hunters were remaking dry-country routes. Nsizwa ancestors joined plateau, cattle and burial communities in the far southwest.

Braided peoples emerged through later mixture. Harbour populations which eventually entered the Sestaran world, the Vesari and later Eclessians inherited several lines of experience. Language, political citizenship, worship and ancestry often changed at different speeds.

## Memory after separation

Founding marriages made slaughter easier to forget. [[G-63 - The Peace Feast at Nine Hearths|Nine Hearths]] preserves the darker reverse: an assault upon a refugee woman, deceptive admission, collective killing and enslavement later remembered as a peaceful union.

Somewhere within these overlapping centuries falls the Year of Standing Stars, when, all the oldest calendars agree, the stars did not move. Every people patches the gap differently. Atherian astronomers built a whole theology of the added year around it; the Jirahar road-chronicle passes over it with the words "and of that year, the road only"; Folk king-lists count it as a winter with no name. Everyone agrees it happened. No two peoples agree what it was.
