# Age of Scattering

The Age of Scattering begins after the Prophet's death around c. 2165 BR. One people formed during [[The Glass Sojourn]] divides through two gradual, geography-driven fissions, while older human communities continue independent reassembly outside the Ark and later populations braid those histories together. This is the hinge between sacred prehistory and recognizable cultures.

## Ark Scattering

### Vacant Commission, c. 2165–2130 BR

The Prophet leaves commanders, judges, archivists, provisioners and household elders with real but bounded responsibilities. None inherits his universal commission. The attempted common southern sanctuary fails gradually as those offices contest movement, land, punishment, stores and command; deaths from hoarding, requisition, failed scouting and factional violence make the dispute more than constitutional theory.

### First Fission, c. 2130–2050 BR

Southern captains, craftsmen, surveyors, specialists and younger household coalitions pursue permanent settlement, repaired Giant infrastructure and offensive security. Camps remaining nearer the Glass maintain seasonal roads, graves, registers and older stations. Families, judges, workshops and trade continue to cross the growing divide for generations; no meeting or battle creates it.

The southern leagues become recognizably **proto-Atherian** through the movement itself. Their inherited measures, records, crafts and military organization aid survival but grant no divine title to occupied land.

### Southern Seizures, c. 2050–1900 BR

Proto-Atherian forts spread along rivers, hills, road junctions and coasts already inhabited by Orphaned villages, harbor communities, Giant-client remnants and separate Folk clans. Their expansion includes massacre, slavery, hostage-taking, expulsion and land seizure as well as treaty, local alliance, defection, adoption, intermarriage and the destruction or absorption of failed Atherian forts. No conquest is a commanded holy war, and no policy describes every league.

These contacts begin early harbor braids and several connected post-Giant recovery networks. Local pilots, smiths, farmers, healers and route-keepers remain indispensable; Atherians neither discover empty lands nor invent civilization for them.

### Northern Fission, c. 1950–1750 BR

The continuing **Near-Glass camp continuum** is an authorial label, not a lost nation. Water scarcity, distance, marriage, trade, raiding and reciprocal contact with older desert communities and Orphaned Tarkhan make one camp system increasingly unworkable.

- **Proto-Jirahar** develop portable courts, translocal registers, protected burial obligation, route memory and later tower towns whose legitimacy does not depend upon owning all surrounding land.
- **Proto-Ahl-e-Ahd** develop defended wells, territorial oath-coalitions, guest-law, blood-price, rescue debt and public enforcement. Older desert and Tarkhan people join these communities without becoming Ark-derived by that fact.

The two later northern formations share real ancestry without either possessing the whole Prophet's inheritance. The northern fission begins late in the formal Scattering and completes during the [[Age of Hearths and First Kings]].

## Orphaned Reassembly

Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Nsizwa are principally descended from communities that survive the Giant collapse outside the Ark. Their ancestors include subject villages, local revolts, failing client states, slave-soldier bands, hidden enclaves, mountain and forest refugees, island survivors and people born later among Giant ruins.

Their histories of liberation are local and uneven. Some know the Prophet only as rumor, foreign law, adopted story or not at all. They are neither rejected humanity nor a single related remnant people.

## Braided Peoples

Sestaran harbor populations and Vesari emerge later through distinct mixtures of Ark-derived migrants, Orphaned communities and other local populations. Later Eclessians are a further Braided ethnogenesis of Atherian provincial, Folk, Markish, Vesari and other regional layers. None is a fourth direct branch of the Glass camp.

## What Gets Lost

No Ark-derived people keeps the whole Exodus inheritance intact. Orphaned peoples preserve different truths and errors about the fallen Giant world. Braided peoples inherit several memories that do not always agree. Some lose doctrine but preserve ritual; some preserve law but become hard-hearted; some keep practical survival skills inside false history; some turn Giant remnants into prestige.

## What Gets Made

The Scattering produces:

- ancestor houses,
- clan marks,
- oasis covenants,
- caravan routes,
- first holy roads,
- settlement taboos,
- early calendars,
- memory specialists,
- oral law,
- sacred suspicion of slavery and false gods.

## Inherited Wound

Every human culture descends from people who survived the end of Giant rule, but not every culture descends from the Glass Ark. Direct inheritance, adopted liberation memory and later Braided ancestry must not be collapsed into one claim. No people carries survival without contradiction, sin or loss.
