# Age of Exodus

The Age of Exodus begins when the Great Prophet's commissioned movement becomes the center of the [[The Great Unbinding|Great Unbinding]]. The Prophet gathers the largest deliberate convergence of liberated human communities and leads that representative remnant toward and across the [[Great Glass Desert]]. The host becomes the principal ancestral convergence of the later Scattering, but it is not the only place human communities survive the end of Giant rule.

## The Liberation

The Prophet does not merely defeat tyrants. He exposes them. Giant kings who call themselves gods are shown to be corrupt, contingent, and breakable. Their cities fall through a providential convergence of prophetic warning, plagues, human revolt, client defection, Giant civil war, divine judgement, internal rot, failed escape, and the collapse of the systems used to bind human beings.

The Prophet announces, interprets, intercedes, prepares, and leads. He is not the metaphysical source of the plagues and cannot deploy Heaven as a personal army. Giant rulers remain responsible for the wars they choose, while human liberation depends upon the work of households, scouts, burial fraternities, artisans, soldiers, officials, and subject communities as well as the Prophet at their head.

Four Imperial Horizons are broken directly in liberation memory. Anshara destroys itself concurrently in the Drinking Wars and receives no authorially fixed prophetic plague cycle.

## The Great Glass Desert

The Glass predates the Prophet, his plagues, and the Giants' recorded interest in approaching it. The Exodus crosses it through a bounded survivable passage where no imperial road had been established. The ordered camp itself becomes a dry ark: people, animals, seed, tools, medicine, records, law, and living crafts survive through both high-sign provision and ordinary material labor.

The crossing leaves real anchor camps and threshold corridors but does not explain what the Glass is or make its miracles repeatable. Later peoples remember the passage differently:

- the Jirahar remember commandments, names, routes, and failed obedience;
- the Ahl-e-Ahd remember water, oath, provision, and covenant;
- the Folk remember coldward survival, the first common hearths, dangerous roadside powers, and the duty not to trade freedom for easy protection;
- Atherian descendants remember liberation but often sand away the Prophet's harder demands;
- Sestaran memory treats the crossing as the first great archive problem: which testimony survived, and who profited by preserving it?

## The Prophet's Failure and Death

The Prophet's death is one of the great disputed wounds of history. Some traditions call it punishment for refusing the sanctuary meant to shelter the people for three generations. Others call it murder, tragic exhaustion, or a mystery no nation has the right to simplify.

## Inherited Wound

Humanity is freed before it is formed. Liberation happens, but unity collapses before shared law, institutions, and memory can mature. Every later culture inherits both deliverance and fracture.

Human communities outside the principal host are not rejected humanity. Some revolt after the central columns pass, remain to preserve a failing city, survive in isolated regions, or enter separate successor histories. Later cultures may claim exclusive descent from the Exodus, but no one surviving genealogy contains the whole end of the Giant Age.
