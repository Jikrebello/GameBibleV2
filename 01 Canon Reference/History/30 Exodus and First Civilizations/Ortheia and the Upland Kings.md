# Ortheia and the Upland Kings

**Ortheia** was an Atherian-speaking kingdom in the colder mountain country beyond the principal city leagues. Its valleys, forests, mines and pasture roads supported royal households able to maintain soldiers for longer periods than the seasonal forces of many lowland cities. Under Valeron II and Eryndor III, Ortheia imposed a temporary hegemony upon much of the Celestial Compact and led the first great Atherian campaign across the eastern inner sea.

The kingdom survived the division of Eryndor's conquests but lost control of Caleran and the western cities. Its remaining influence lay among upland roads, eastern ports and the households established by Ortheian officers abroad. By Thalion's generation, Ortheia was one of several frontier powers competing with Caleran.

## Country and settlement

Ortheia occupied a chain of cultivated valleys separated by wooded ridges and high pasture. Snow and seasonal rain fed streams descending towards the older Atherian cities. Roads through the passes carried ore, timber, wool, animals and travellers. Winter could close a high route for weeks, giving the household that maintained a lower crossing influence over merchants and armies.

Villages cultivated grain and fruit on terraces and valley floors. Herding households moved sheep, goats and horses between seasonal grazing. Miners, charcoal burners and smiths worked in settlements connected to royal or sanctuary estates. Market towns brought these occupations together and supplied the fortified residences of the great houses.

Ortheians shared language, festival forms and marriage relationships with other Atherian societies. Kings governed with upland assemblies, military households and sanctuary estates; Caleran's civic magistracies held little authority in their valleys. Later Thalmyric communities inherited parts of this mountain world through settlement, marriage and political succession.

## Royal houses and assemblies

An Ortheian king depended upon the support of valley lords, military companions, sanctuary estates and assemblies able to promise local levies. Great houses sent children to the royal household for education and service. The practice created friendships and marriages across the valleys while placing potential rivals under watch.

Royal income came from estates, mine dues, passage payments and contributions imposed for war. A permanent body of infantry guarded the household and trained seasonal levies. Mounted nobles supplied horses, scouts and pack animals. City-trained clerks recorded stores and service, while engineers supervised roads, bridges and fortifications.

Assemblies could refuse an extraordinary demand when the crown lacked the force to compel it. A successful king therefore spent as much effort maintaining households and roads as winning battles. Gifts, shared hunting, marriage and the education of hostages bound the kingdom together between campaigns.

## Valeron II

Valeron II inherited a divided kingdom around 735 BR. Rival valleys controlled important mines and passes, and several great houses maintained alliances with Atherian cities. Valeron brought younger members of those houses into his service, enlarged the permanent infantry and used mining revenue to pay soldiers during poor harvests. His engineers improved the routes by which the army could descend towards the lowlands.

Paying a standing army in sacks of grain and lumps of ore was slow and quarrelsome. Around 730 BR Valeron's smiths began cutting the silver of the upland mines into weighed pieces stamped with the royal mark, so that a company could be paid by count at a camp table. These were the first struck coins of the known world. Compact cities soon paid their contributions in the same silver, and after 684 the Successor courts carried coined money across the eastern sea.

He then entered the rivalries of the Celestial Compact. Some cities accepted his arbitration and military protection. Others received garrisons or surrendered hostages after defeat. Caleran supplied ships, grain and money while defending the authority of its assembly. The Arqeshite seizures gave him his cause. Governors on the eastern coast had taken Compact ships in their harbours, held the crews for ransom and, between about 740 and 730 BR, deported the Atherian merchant quarter of Terys to the river cities. Valeron's proposed eastern campaign promised to bring those families home, offered his supporters Arqeshite wealth and gave threatened cities a common enemy.

Valeron ruled the cities through a hegemony. Their councils continued to collect revenue and conduct worship while supplying him with ships, grain, money, soldiers or hostages. Contribution lists preserve the difference between willing allies and cities compelled after defeat.

Valeron died in 706 BR, before the army crossed the sea, at the pay table of the muster camp at Pelatha. Olyssar had withheld its contribution the year before, the silver came up short, and men of his own standing infantry killed him in the press around the table. Who began it remains disputed. His death left Eryndor facing relatives who possessed plausible claims, commanders loyal to his father, cities willing to test the succession and an army that had learned what it could do at a pay table. It also left him a standing army that the eastern mandate alone could pay. The sources still disagree about why he went east. Ortheian songs speak of vengeance for the deported families; Caleran writers, of an army that had to be paid; Olyssaran lists, of a war made so that the cities would go on paying; Vashari stories, of an invitation from Arezan. Ortheian soldiers kept a saying from Pelatha: *count it in front of us.*

## Eryndor and Olyssar

Eryndor III secured the royal household, hanged the men who had struck at the table and demanded confirmation of the eastern war. He owed the companies their arrears and paid them with Olyssar. The army blockaded the city through the winter of 705; no neighbour came to its relief, and in 704 BR its council opened the gates on terms and offered its treasury. The treasury was short. The soldiers killed and enslaved inhabitants, burned the city and sold the survivors against their arrears, and the infantry companies were settled on its territory. The sack compelled most other members of the Compact to contribute to the expedition. Eryndor left Demeran Kavos in the uplands as regent, with the settled companies, the home garrisons and the mints.

Olyssaran exiles settled in Caleran, island cities and foreign ports. They preserved cult objects, workshop traditions and lists of people killed or sold. Their refusal to honour Eryndor remained an important dissenting voice within the later Far-Road festivals.

After Eryndor's death Caleran bought ships from his unpaid commanders, landed its own sailors on Olyssar's fields, drove off the Ortheian grantees and seated the settlement in the Compact as Olyssar (c. 680–672 BR). The exiles' lists did not win the seat back.

The eastern campaign made Ortheian command familiar across the inner sea. Soldiers encountered Arqeshite water administration, Vashari cavalry, Qahuri royal ceremony and the mixed households of the eastern ports. Officers returned with wealth, captives and new ambitions. Many remained abroad in the Successor kingdoms.

## Demeran's succession

Eryndor died in 684 aboard ship in the roads of Nemeru, during a Qahuri funerary rite, while he waited for a wind to carry him home. Demeran Kavos, regent in the west for twenty years, took command of the Ortheian army and claimed to govern for the royal heirs. He held the upland homeland, where the army's families and estates gave him practical authority. Caleran and other cities recovered their assemblies while he fought over the eastern inheritance.

The king's body never came home. Serevan of Delkos, on whose ship he died, sailed it to his own harbour, and within months a voice in the tomb there claimed to be Eryndor alive and without heirs. Ortheian veterans who heard it divided bitterly; Demeran called it a Delkosi puppet. Once the dead king had disowned his heirs, the half-brother in Demeran's hands was worthless as a hostage, and Demeran had him killed. He sent agents up the Dawn Road after Parena Tiravan and the daughter she had borne at Kavresh; Arezan's road guards took them at Durqesh and hanged them. Other nominal heirs died during the conflict. The killings removed the household around which the divided armies might have reunited, and the attempt gave Vashar a permanent grievance against the Ortheian succession. Ortheian court histories called Parena's daughter a Vashari pretence.

Returning troops required land and pay. Demeran settled some on estates confiscated from rivals and confirmed others in property taken under Valeron and Eryndor. Olyssaran returners found much of their former land occupied. Violence, purchase and negotiated restoration produced different settlements from valley to valley.

## Ortheia after the hegemony

Ortheia remained a strong upland monarchy after losing the Compact. Its soldiers served at home, in Delkos and in the rival Successor courts. Mines and pasture routes continued to finance royal households. Caleran merchants sought its timber and metal, while Ortheian rulers needed access to ports increasingly controlled by the republic.

The Far-Road cult became part of royal ceremony. Court singers celebrated the conquest, the Sky-Lion ascent and the king's night beneath the sea at Terys, and said the king's body had been stolen. Ortheian silver long remained a trusted coin in the eastern ports. Families affected by Olyssar, the succession murders and the failed return preserved contrary histories. The resulting tradition could honour Eryndor's courage while refusing his claim to universal rule.

By 350 BR, Ortheia occupied a position between the Caleran state and Vashar's eastern empire. Thalion inherited relationships with its kings and military houses.
