# Ortheia and the Upland Kings

**Ortheia** was an Atherian-speaking kingdom in the colder mountain country beyond the principal city leagues. Its valleys, forests, mines and pasture roads supported royal households able to maintain soldiers for longer periods than the seasonal forces of many lowland cities. Under Valeron II and Eryndor III, Ortheia imposed a temporary hegemony upon much of the Celestial Compact and led the first great Atherian campaign across the eastern inner sea.

The kingdom survived the division of Eryndor's conquests but lost control of Caleran and the western cities. Its remaining influence lay among upland roads, eastern ports and the households established by Ortheian officers abroad. By Thalion's generation, Ortheia was one of several frontier powers competing with Caleran.

## Country and settlement

Ortheia occupied a chain of cultivated valleys separated by wooded ridges and high pasture. Snow and seasonal rain fed streams descending towards the older Atherian cities. Roads through the passes carried ore, timber, wool, animals and travellers. Winter could close a high route for weeks, giving the household that maintained a lower crossing influence over merchants and armies.

Villages cultivated grain and fruit on terraces and valley floors. Herding households moved sheep, goats and horses between seasonal grazing. Miners, charcoal burners and smiths worked in settlements connected to royal or sanctuary estates. Market towns brought these occupations together and supplied the fortified residences of the great houses.

Ortheians shared language, festival forms and marriage relationships with other Atherian societies. Kings governed with upland assemblies, military households and sanctuary estates; Caleran's civic magistracies held little authority in their valleys. Later Thalmyric communities inherited parts of this mountain world through settlement, marriage and political succession.

## Royal houses and assemblies

An Ortheian king depended upon the support of valley lords, military companions, sanctuary estates and assemblies able to promise local levies. Great houses sent children to the royal household for education and service. The practice created friendships and marriages across the valleys while placing potential rivals under watch.

Royal income came from estates, mine dues, passage payments and contributions imposed for war. A permanent body of infantry guarded the household and trained seasonal levies. Mounted nobles supplied horses, scouts and pack animals. City-trained clerks recorded stores and service, while engineers supervised roads, bridges and fortifications.

Assemblies could refuse an extraordinary demand when the crown lacked the force to compel it. A successful king therefore spent as much effort maintaining households and roads as winning battles. Gifts, shared hunting, marriage and the education of hostages bound the kingdom together between campaigns.

## Valeron II

Valeron II inherited a divided kingdom around 735 BR. Rival valleys controlled important mines and passes, and several great houses maintained alliances with Atherian cities. Valeron brought younger members of those houses into his service, enlarged the permanent infantry and used mining revenue to pay soldiers during poor harvests. His engineers improved the routes by which the army could descend towards the lowlands.

He then entered the rivalries of the Celestial Compact. Some cities accepted his arbitration and military protection. Others received garrisons or surrendered hostages after defeat. Caleran supplied ships, grain and money while defending the authority of its assembly. Valeron's proposed eastern campaign offered his supporters Arqeshite wealth and gave threatened cities a common enemy.

Valeron ruled the cities through a hegemony. Their councils continued to collect revenue and conduct worship while supplying him with ships, grain, money, soldiers or hostages. Contribution lists preserve the difference between willing allies and cities compelled after defeat.

Valeron was assassinated in 706 BR before the army crossed the sea. The manner of his death left Eryndor facing relatives who possessed plausible claims, commanders loyal to his father and cities willing to test the succession.

## Eryndor and Olyssar

Eryndor III secured the royal household and demanded confirmation of the eastern war. Olyssar refused and expected a wider rising. Eryndor isolated the city, defeated its relief and took it after a prolonged assault in 704 BR. His soldiers killed and enslaved inhabitants and divided its territory. The destruction compelled most other members of the Compact to contribute to the expedition.

Olyssaran exiles settled in Caleran, island cities and foreign ports. They preserved cult objects, workshop traditions and lists of people killed or sold. Their refusal to honour Eryndor remained an important dissenting voice within the later Far-Road festivals.

The eastern campaign made Ortheian command familiar across the inner sea. Soldiers encountered Arqeshite water administration, Vashari cavalry, Qahuri royal ceremony and the mixed households of the eastern ports. Officers returned with wealth, captives and new ambitions. Many remained abroad in the Successor kingdoms.

## Demeran's succession

Eryndor died at Arqesh in 684. Demeran Kavos, his senior western general, took command of the Ortheian army and claimed to govern for the royal heirs. He could hold the upland homeland, where the army's families and estates gave him practical authority. Caleran and other cities recovered their assemblies while he fought over the eastern inheritance.

Demeran imprisoned Parena Tiravan and her child and ordered their deaths. Other nominal heirs also died during the conflict. The killings removed the household around which the divided armies might have reunited. They also gave Vashar a permanent grievance against the Ortheian succession.

Returning troops required land and pay. Demeran settled some on estates confiscated from rivals and confirmed others in property taken under Valeron and Eryndor. Olyssaran returners found much of their former land occupied. Violence, purchase and negotiated restoration produced different settlements from valley to valley.

## Ortheia after the hegemony

Ortheia remained a strong upland monarchy after losing the Compact. Its soldiers served at home, in Delkos and in the rival Successor courts. Mines and pasture routes continued to finance royal households. Caleran merchants sought its timber and metal, while Ortheian rulers needed access to ports increasingly controlled by the republic.

The Far-Road cult became part of royal ceremony. Court singers celebrated the conquest, the Sky-Lion ascent and the burial of Kassur III. Families affected by Olyssar, the succession murders and the failed return preserved contrary histories. The resulting tradition could honour Eryndor's courage while refusing his claim to universal rule.

By 350 BR, Ortheia occupied a position between the Caleran state and Vashar's eastern empire. Thalion inherited relationships with its kings and military houses, but no settlement made Ortheia the birthplace or natural possession of the later Empire. Its precise boundaries and royal seat remain open pending the revision of the regional map.
