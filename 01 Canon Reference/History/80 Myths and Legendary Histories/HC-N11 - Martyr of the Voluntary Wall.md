# Martyr of the Voluntary Wall

**c. 744–752 AR; later correction c. 811 AR.** This fixes the local human history and the explicitly described commission, not the identity of every later claimant or relic. The title is a received epithet rather than an additional heavenly rank.

## Earthly life

The future Saint began as a ditch digger and conscripted stone hauler in a frontier water-city. The mortal learned fortification from maintaining drains, not from noble schooling, and rose through militia command after exposing an officer who sold repair stone. Earlier in command, however, the future Saint authorized beatings, hostage seizure and one lethal exemplary punishment against workers who deserted a floodwall project. Later Lives remove these acts or transfer them to a deputy; the orders were personally given.

During a combined siege and structural failure, the commander devised a plan requiring thousands to hold an outer retaining wall while families crossed the inner causeway. Some defenders freely remained. Others were debtors, prisoners, foreign laborers or conscripts threatened under emergency law. The commander knew the distinction and used the coerced stations anyway because the inner city would otherwise fall. Before taking a place on the wall, the commander confessed the earlier killing, revoked inheritance penalties against deserters’ children and ordered the volunteer and compulsory rolls kept separately. The wall failed. The commander and most of those stationed there died; the city survived. Repentance did not make the coercion voluntary or restore the dead.

## Later active history

In c. 811 AR, a successor confraternity used the old emergency law to seize minority labor for an unnecessary prestige wall. A genuine manifestation of the Saint received one bounded commission: stop the confraternity from invoking the Saint’s name, reveal the separate rolls and require the living institution to hear the coerced households. The Saint did not demolish the wall, govern the city or dictate compensation. The manifestation ended when the seizure order was publicly revoked; restitution remained human work and remained incomplete.

## What people remember

flawless savior, necessary tyrant, repentant martyr, fabricated worker rebellion, Saint who legalized sacrifice or later apparition invented to defeat a building faction.

## What remains

descendants still demand that public memorials name the compulsory stations separately. The city pays a reduced orphan levy whose original purpose is politically contested.

## What remains unknown

city, gender, exact number, public recognition, compensation, cause of the structural failure and authenticity of relic claims. Within this nursery lead, reception as a redeemed Saint after judgement is fixed; coerced deaths do not become voluntary through that ruling.
