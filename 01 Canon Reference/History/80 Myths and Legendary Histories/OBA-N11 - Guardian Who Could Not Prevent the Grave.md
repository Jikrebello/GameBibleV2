# Guardian Who Could Not Prevent the Grave

**c. 1327–1331 AR; disputed custody active in 1360.** This fixes the local human history and the explicitly described commission, not the identity of every later claimant or relic. The title is a received epithet rather than an additional heavenly rank.

## Historical aperture

In the assurance era, a baptized tallyman falsified warehouse measures for personal gain. Several poor households lost grain, and a guard died during a riot the shortage helped provoke. The tallyman later confessed part of the fraud, returned what money remained and supplied evidence against a larger broker. He was still convicted, indebted for restitution and sent to a workhouse infirmary. Repentance, if genuine, did not erase the dead guard, hungry households or lawful punishment.

The workhouse ignored repeated warnings that the prisoner was dying. After death, a clerk exchanged the corpse tag with that of another debtor bearing a similar name. A contract broker intended to treat the wrong identity as proof that one infernal assurance claim had matured, transferring liabilities to innocent dependants. The tallyman’s personal Guardian Angel had not prevented the crime, imprisonment, illness or death. Its final visible act was narrower: throughout one night a pale, small-winged figure repeatedly displaced the false tag, woke a washer and caused the baptismal name to be spoken before both bodies were sealed.

The bodies were eventually identified through ordinary records and testimony. The identity fraud was exposed, but the infernal office still disputes whether the corrected register defeats its claim. The Angel did not reveal the tallyman’s judgement, declare him innocent or advocate a false name to save him from consequence.

## What people remember

personal Guardian, hospital saint, guilt dream, trained bird, clerk’s cover story, infernal interference against a rival office or compassionate fabrication by the washer.
