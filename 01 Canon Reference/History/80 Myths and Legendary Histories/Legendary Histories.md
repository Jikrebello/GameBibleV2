# Legendary Histories

Alongside the established chronology in [[The Ages of the World]], the world carries seventy-five and more **Garden traditions**: stories that particular communities tell about their own past. Their existence, their tellers and whatever physical or customary remainder survives them are established facts. What the stories claim happened inside them — a miracle, a founder's identity, a god's intervention — is a separate question, and each article keeps the two apart.

That distinction is marked by an **authority layer**. Most legends on this shelf carry the layer **stories told**: the telling is certain, its events are not automatically so. An article will say plainly where tellers disagree, what physical or customary evidence remains, and what stays permanently uncertain — a name, a site, a cause of death. None of that uncertainty makes the tradition placeless or the community that keeps it unreliable; it just means a sung version and a verified history are different kinds of document.

## Where to start

**[[G-60 - The Bride of the Dry Road|The Son-in-Law and the Stolen Gods]]** is the clearest single example of how these articles work: a Near-Glass household cycle with a firm historical setting, a plainly stated historical basis, the popular tale itself, where tellers disagree, and what physical evidence survives. Read it first to see the whole method in one place.

From there, four more traditions spread across the ages show the range of what this shelf holds:

- **[[The Wolf's Two Sons]]** — Caleran's founding family, a wolf's purpose and the murder between brothers, from the early [[Age of City-States]].
- **[[G-73 - The Oar-King without a Country|The Oar-King without a Country]]** — a Folk coastal captive's rise to kingship, a murder, a prophet's exposure and a magistracy born from confession, from the [[Age of Hearths and First Kings]].
- **[[G-10 - The Road of the Empty Purse|The Road of the Empty Purse]]** — mercy and betrayal among ordinary households sheltering the persecuted, from [[The Age of the Redeemer]].
- **[[G-17 - The Company with No Charter|The Company with No Charter]]** — a still-unfinished present-day mystery with living participants, from [[The Age of Held Proofs]].
