# The Four Deaths of the Prophet

**The Four Deaths of the Prophet** is the scholars' name for the four irreconcilable accounts of how the Prophet of the Exodus died, around 2165 BR, near the outward threshold of the Glass. He died in the season the bread stopped. The provision that had fed the host for a century had thinned for some ten years, coming late, then short, then not at all, and it ended company by company at the threshold while the rear camps were still inside the Glass grinding their seed corn. Every people that came out of the Glass, and one that watched it come out, tells his death as an account of who was fed. Each gives him a different last command, a different death, a different saying and a different person to blame.

## The Slow Road

Jirahar road-singers give his last command as "Go no faster than the last cart." By their account the captains of the forward companies were already through the threshold with the herds and the seed when the old man turned back. He walked the column alone, against its current, to the rear camps where the morning portion no longer came, and sat with them through the hungry weeks, naming their dead. He died of exhaustion among the people the captains had outpaced, and they buried him among their own under a name and no marker.

His saying in this telling is "A name is the last thing a person owns." The fault is the people's, because they walked too fast. Road-singers end the song by naming the rear before the front, as the rear camps' own Last Cart taught them to count.

## The Unpaid Well

Covenant jurists and Ahdari well-keepers give his last command as "Take no water you have not answered for." At the edge of the refuge the host's water failed, and he opened a well kept by Orphaned keepers without their consent and watered the people from it. The Lord held him to his own law, and a storm took him beside the well.

His saying here is "Water owes its well, the well its keeper, the keeper the thirsty." The fault is his own. Covenant jurists call the story the Unpaid Well and count it as their first case: the man who gave the law was the first person held to it.

## The Herald's Ascent

Atherian temple drama gives his last command as "Measure the land and divide it." On the road south the sun darkened at midday, and the Prophet, now Astronax the Star Herald, climbed a height above the camp and was taken up among the stars. That is why no one can show his grave.

His saying is "What is measured cannot be stolen." The fault is no one's. The Ascent took shape in the hungry years after the provision stopped, when the southern companies carried the saying *"The Lord who stopped feeding us; the stars that still answer."* It asked little of the people who performed it, which southern priests found convenient: it turned the surveyor's rope into a sacred commission and the seized fields into a measured share. Later image-makers inherited its raised arms and darkened sun.

## The Guest Who Died at the Door

The Orphaned peoples whose fields lay south of the Glass tell the death as a joke with an edge. Folk and Aurin-valley satirists say his captains killed him because he forbade them fields other people had sown. No captain is ever named, and tellers who are pressed for one name the whole forward column. A comic variant has him starve instead, "the first winter the bread stopped, like any of us".

Its saying is "Heaven's guest ate for a hundred years and starved in a week." The fault is the captains'. The same tellers call the years that followed the time "the fed came for the farmers", when settlers who had never learned the farming year took the farmers along with the fields.

## No one is owned

One line survives in all four tellings: **no one is owned.** The road-singers put it in his mouth at the rear camps, the jurists in the preamble to the Unpaid Well, the temple dramatists in the Herald's last speech before the sun goes dark, and the satirists among the things he said that his captains could not stomach. Scholars in Caleran, in the Covenant schools and in the Jirahar courts, who agree on almost nothing else about the Prophet, cite that one shared line as the best evidence that a real teacher stands behind the four deaths.

The rest they leave argued. Some traditions call the death a punishment for refusing the southern sanctuary in which the people were to shelter for three generations. Others call it murder, tragic exhaustion, or a mystery no nation has the right to simplify.

## What remains

- **The empty seat.** The seat that faces the road in Jirahar assemblies is, in the Slow Road's telling, kept for the man who turned back. [[G-56 - The Empty Seat Facing Outward|The Empty Seat Facing Outward]] tells its older history.
- **The Covenant's first case.** Jurists cite the Unpaid Well whenever a ruler claims that the law does not bind the one who made it.
- **The Ascent's iconography.** Raised arms, a darkened sun and a height with no grave passed from Atherian temple drama into the image-making of later churches.
- **The Guest in Grenzburg.** Folk satirists around Grenzburg still bring the Guest out in land quarrels, where Folk claims call the Southern Seizures the time the fed came for the farmers.
