# The Two Songs at the Wall

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**612–884 AR; Long Frontier.** Atherian–Covenant frontier districts. This tradition belongs to [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]; its cultural bearers are mixed provincial, Ahdari, Velian and Jirahar communities.

It is told in one Long-Frontier city where a fallen wall became both victory shrine and place of protest. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

When siege engines reached the eastern wall, the city called volunteers. Too few came. Officials opened debt prisons, chained prisoners in the breaches and threatened their dependants if they ran. Foreign labourers were promised citizenship after battle but not permitted to refuse service.

The wall held for three days. Free defenders died beside coerced debtors. One prisoner killed the officer holding his chain and then stayed because his daughter was inside the city. On the last morning the parapet collapsed over both groups. Relief arrived before the enemy crossed the rubble.

Rulers composed a single victory song saying every dead person had freely offered life for the city. Widows of the prisoners answered from the opposite end of the street. Their song named beatings, threats and the chains found beneath the stones. Volunteer families feared the second song dishonoured their beloved dead; coerced families answered that honour built on a lie dishonoured everyone.

The city eventually required both songs. They are never blended. As one choir finishes, the other begins from far away, so listeners must turn their bodies to hear the second account.

Some listeners always leave after the victory song. Others arrive only for the widows. Children who stay for both learn that one wall can have been worth defending, one defence can have been coercive, and the same dead can be loved without assigning them the same choice. The lesson requires both melodies and the uncomfortable turn between them.

## Why it is remembered

It lets people mourn a shared defence without erasing the difference between choice and coercion. The second song changes the emotional meaning of the first rather than disproving that the city was saved.

## How tellers differ

Official versions say chains were removed before combat. Excavated links and ankle injuries contest this. Some debt families sing the killer as liberator; officer descendants call him traitor.

## What remains

Paired wall songs are sung from opposite ends of one street. The audience turns between them; no choir receives the final note.
