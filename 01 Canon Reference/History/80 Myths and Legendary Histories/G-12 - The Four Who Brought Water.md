# The Four Who Brought Water

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**541–612 AR and the first frontier settlements.** Atherian–Covenant frontier districts. This tradition belongs to [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]; its cultural bearers are mixed provincial, Ahdari, Velian and Jirahar communities.

It is told in Covenant frontier country, among well diggers, former captive families and soldiers who swear public promises. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

A conquered town's only deep well collapsed during summer. Its governor promised freedom to any four prisoners who could reopen it. A thief, a physician, a deposed well keeper and a captured enemy captain descended together, each convinced the others would steal the reward.

They crawled through mud, old bones and poisonous air. The captain held a falling beam while the thief crossed beneath it. The physician cut the well keeper's crushed foot free. At the lowest chamber they found that the governor's own illegal quarrying had diverted the spring. They could restore water only by breaking the wall of his private cistern.

Water returned to the whole town. The governor freed the captain alone, claiming the others had supplied skill rather than victory. The four seized the public cup, climbed the well tower and refused to lower water until the promise was spoken again before citizens and soldiers. Thirst forced the governor to yield.

Years later his successor broke a related treaty with an allied household. Retaliation became massacre; to settle the delayed blood-price, descendants of the promise-breaker were executed. The four cups thereafter carried both memories: prisoners winning freedom and an oath preserved through the deaths of people who had not made it.

One survivor refused to drink from them and smashed his cup against the tower. Well keepers repaired rather than replaced it, leaving the fracture visible whenever later rulers praised the beauty of kept promises. The repaired cup always leaks.

## Why it is remembered

The descent is an adventure about mistrust becoming dependence. Its afterlife prevents the satisfying victory from teaching that every sworn debt can be made just merely by being paid.

## How tellers differ

Well-keeper families say all four survived. Military songs say the captain died under the beam. Some Covenant preachers omit the later executions; descendants of the executed begin there.

## What remains

Four mismatched cups are lowered together into a dry well before repair begins. None may be raised until every worker has drunk.
