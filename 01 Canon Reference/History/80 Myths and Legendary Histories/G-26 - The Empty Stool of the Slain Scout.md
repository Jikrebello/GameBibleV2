# The Empty Stool of the Slain Scout

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**c. 708–781 AR.** Atherian–Covenant frontier districts. This tradition belongs to [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]; its cultural bearers are mixed provincial, Ahdari, Velian and Jirahar communities.

It is told in late Long-Frontier route villages along a dense woodland belt. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

A hunter hired to find a path through Shutwood saw a bootprint turned toward the expedition's camp. Convinced a scout had sold the route to raiders, he followed and killed the man before asking a question. In the dead scout's boot he found a strip of bark marked with the warning that the eastern ford was poisoned.

The hunter hid the body and used the warning to lead the caravan safely west. People praised his woodcraft. Settlers gathered around the road he found, and his painted sign appeared on inns and watch posts. He accepted gifts meant for the founder who had saved them.

The scout's sister arrived years later carrying the matching half of the bark. She recognised her brother's cut bootlace nailed above the hunter's chair. The hunter confessed, but by then families feared that exposing him would destroy trust in the road. He offered blood-price. She refused it and demanded only that her brother's place remain present wherever the founder was praised.

The hunter continued guarding the route. Some say he died saving the sister's grandchildren; others say she never permitted him near them. He did not stop being useful, and usefulness did not make the first killing necessary.

On his last known journey he walked behind every scout and never interpreted a sign before the person who made it could speak. That changed his craft and saved lives, but the empty stool remained when his own chair was removed.

## Why it is remembered

The audience knows the founder is guilty before the settlement does. Momentum comes from waiting for the dead man's kin to enter the feast and change the meaning of every earlier success.

## How tellers differ

Hunter guilds say he mistook a real traitor's trail. Scout families say fear and pride were enough. Neither version removes the body beneath the road.

## What remains

Route hearings and founder feasts keep an empty stool with a cut bootlace across its seat. Nobody may sit there, even in winter.
