# The Houses Unhoused before the Gate Turns

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**c. 800–350 BR; Age of Stars.** Atherian roads and city leagues. This tradition belongs to [[Age of Stars]]; its cultural bearers are Atherian workers and later provincial inheritors.

It is told in civic threshold rites whose oldest form may reach from the Age of Stars into later imperial use. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

A city seized a refuge house during war and copied its guest marks into a list of suspected enemies. Soldiers dragged families from beds, killed some in the square and sold others. The gate that had once opened for fugitives was fitted with murder holes and praised as the city's holy shield.

Defenders reported a great winged brightness above the gate during the next siege. Priests declared that Heaven had accepted the conversion of shelter into fortress. Yet when soldiers prepared to burn prisoners beneath it, the brightness withdrew. Hinges split, roofing tiles fell and the defenders were exposed to arrows. The prisoners escaped through the same passage that had trapped their families.

After the war, officials wanted the gate reconsecrated without returning stolen homes. Survivors refused. They climbed onto every participating house, stripped one roof tile and carried the tiles beyond the walls. Only after dependants were rehoused and the guest marks destroyed did they allow the old threshold to serve again.

The procession is remembered more clearly than the reported heavenly sign. Even tellers who deny any angel agree that the city learned protection could depart when rulers used refuge as bait.

One confiscated house was never restored because every lawful claimant had died in the purge. The city made it a night shelter governed by former guests. That imperfect solution, rather than the falling gate, is what descendants point to when asked whether repentance changed anything material.

## Why it is remembered

The story matters because a place beloved for welcome can become an instrument of murder while keeping its honourable name. Restoration requires visible loss and repair, not a cleaner inscription.

## How tellers differ

Church tellers call the brightness an angel under a brief commission. Secular versions call it lightning and rotten hinges. Victim families say the explanation matters less than who was under the falling roof.

## What remains

Before a disgraced sanctuary can be reused, roof tiles are carried out in public and replacement shelter must already stand.
