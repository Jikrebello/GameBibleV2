# The Returners' Second Sack

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**905–1052 AR; Fall and Long Reconquest.** Thalmyric refuge mountains and reconquered provincial towns. This tradition belongs to [[The Fall and the Long Reconquest]]; its cultural bearers are refugees, occupation-born households and returners.

It is told in one provincial Reconquest town, especially by hospital families and people whose winter graves lie beyond its old boundary. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

A claimant army returned carrying keys and titles from before occupation. The town opened after negotiation. Its stewards included collaborators who had enriched themselves, mixed families born during occupation, and workers who had kept wells, hospitals and grain mills alive for everyone.

The returners erased the distinctions. They executed stewards in the square, killed people who tried to hide them and expelled families into winter. A water-court book recording maintenance and refuge rights was burned. Soldiers issued backdated titles to their supporters and used occupation parchment because no other material remained.

The hospital stopped receiving water after the purge. A returning mason discovered that the conduits had been repaired years earlier by two of the executed households. He broke into the sealed channel and restored it with stones pulled from their confiscated homes. Each stone still bore a family mark on the hidden side.

Later victory histories called the winter deaths enemy propaganda. A collapsed ward exposed a grave of children wrapped in both occupation cloth and returner blankets. The hospital wall then became the town's second account: it literally stood through work done by people the first account had removed.

Returner descendants repaired the exposed grave beside expelled families. They quarrelled over prayers and did not reconcile their title claims, but none could again say the winter dead were invented by an enemy who had buried them in both peoples' cloth. The shared work lasted one winter and outlived no political quarrel.

## Why it is remembered

It does not condemn return or excuse collaboration. It shows how restoration becomes another sack when victors prefer categories to the lived differences among the people already there.

## How tellers differ

Claimant families say the executed men poisoned wells. Hospital descendants preserve patients treated by them. Some expelled households later fought the occupation elsewhere, complicating every clean lineage.

## What remains

The hospital wall contains stones with two household marks, one scraped from the public face and preserved toward the water channel.
