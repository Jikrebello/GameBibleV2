# Great Saints and Sinners — Lives and Afterlives

These fourteen lives are now historical entries rather than a promised future brotherhood. The older four and younger four of the Long Frontier overlapped, but never formed a permanent company. Some rivalries began in life; others began only through the particular later encounters recorded in their articles. There is no common Heresy.

The public traditions remain distinct from the authorial certainty of judgement. A person may be genuinely redeemed despite a guilty mortal past; a cult may still lie about that life. A Sinner may preserve considerable ability without retaining any right to rule.

## Mortal relationships and later meetings

The Hundred-Wells Clerk and Bitter Reaper disputed frontier policy in life. The Sealed-Door Reader and Refuge-Bell Smith shared a friendship damaged by the warning breach. Other principal pairings belong to later commissioned or infernal encounters: the Red-Passage Witness against the Chain-Bearer and Glass General; the Broken-Hands Smith against the Glass General; the Last-Redoubt Warden against the Ninth-Bastion Mason; the Shutwood Hunter against the Black-Eaves Judge. Their dates do not imply mortal meetings across centuries.

At least the Red-Passage Witness leaves no surviving lawful order. Legacies elsewhere are equally uneven: a medicine, ruined workshop, false relic market or hated legal phrase can outlast the organization that once used the founder's name.

## Erased lives and unused comparisons

Two late-frontier people suffered different attacks on memory. One was erased by human authorities after a catastrophe; the other was attacked supernaturally through names and images. Neither soul was destroyed or divided. Their identities, guilt and final judgement remain protected, and Heaven is not uncertain about them.

The four unused source comparisons are reconsidered in [[Additional Mortal Lives and Frontier Traditions]]. They do not enlarge this constellation or become a second hidden generation.
