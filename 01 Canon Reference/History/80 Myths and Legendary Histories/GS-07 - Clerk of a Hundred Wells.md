# Clerk of a Hundred Wells

> **What happened — authorial history.** The earthly core and the expressly described later activity are established. Public cult claims, relic pedigrees and the final list of personal names are not thereby authenticated. This person is separate from every other life in the constellation.

## Earthly life

The future Clerk begins copying well allocations for wages low enough to require sleeping in the archive. During drought and siege the Clerk devises a common register that prevents elite households from taking all available water. The emergency system saves thousands but also confiscates private wells, compels labour and converts several independent households into permanent dependants of the court. After being warned that several households will resist, the Clerk personally signs armed seizure warrants. Household defenders and impressed labourers die during enforcement, and one petitioner is executed under an emergency procedure the Clerk wrote.

When peace returns, the Clerk initially defends every measure as necessary. A dispute with the Bitter Reaper exposes people still trapped under health and water restrictions long after danger passed. The Clerk spends the remaining career restoring hearings, returning land where possible and writing expiration clauses that later copyists often omit. Some victims accept compensation without forgiving the original coercion.

## Later activity

The Saint later manifests during one Long Rent dispute in which a copied Hundred-Wells manual is being used to classify kidnapped people as infrastructure contributors. The commission preserves testimony, suspends one seal and compels a hearing; it does not legislate for every city. The Preacher of the Second Gospel offers a rival law based on effective wonders, while the Bitter Reaper argues that dependency is the only reliable form of protection.

A Saint's work ends with the commission; no institution owns or summons the person. A damned Sinner's office, image, body and copied likeness are not the soul. Defeat of such a manifestation does not reverse judgement.

## What the life cost

Confiscation, compelled labour, household dependency, armed seizure, execution under the Clerk's own procedure, deaths during enforcement and reforms arriving too late for the victims.

## The surviving inheritance

a family of water codes, expiration clauses, a clerkly feast and several governments invoking only the emergency powers.

Courts still ask whether a coercive order contains “the Clerk's ending,” meaning a date after which it must justify itself again.

## What people remember

Perfect lawgiver, colonial administrator, repentant bureaucrat, founder of Banu Mizan law or later saint invented by clerks.

## What remains unknown

The private name, city, faith, exact deaths, judgement chronology, relic seal and which codes descend directly.
