# Golden Last-Watch

**c. 653–668 AR.** This fixes the local human history and the explicitly described commission, not the identity of every later claimant or relic. The title is a received epithet rather than an additional heavenly rank.

## Historical aperture

A frontier town changed hands under a negotiated withdrawal whose written terms protected soldiers and taxable households but omitted debtors, servants, the wounded, mixed families and children claimed by both sides. The departing commander ordered the last carts stripped of those who could not keep pace. Cart crews obeyed at first. Several abandoned people were killed by scouts; others hid in drainage cuts while the column moved on.

When one cart crew turned back, a Warden Angel appeared at the rear as a tall hooked shadow made of gold-white pinions. It did not carry the carts or kill the pursuers. For the length of one march it confused hostile range-finding, held one collapsing ford and made the names on the neglected household roll audible whenever someone was about to be left. Human guards still fought and died. The column crossed only because porters shared loads, soldiers surrendered mounts and families refused the commander’s second abandonment order.

## What people remember

Warden Angel, golden Menhir bird, atmospheric mirage, soldiers’ collective oath, later refugee saint or legal fiction created to shame commanders.

## What remains

some caravan courts require evacuation treaties to append a **dependants’ annex** naming people who cannot be excluded by military convenience.

## What remains unknown

town, belligerents, numbers, commander’s later trial, named households, angelic title and whether any current last-watch practice descends directly.
