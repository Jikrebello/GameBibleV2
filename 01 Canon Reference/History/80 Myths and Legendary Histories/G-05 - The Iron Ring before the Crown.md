# The Iron Ring before the Crown

**The Iron Ring before the Crown** is a Hartheni foundation cycle about a cattle-defence company which became the ruling war-band of an early middle-forest kingdom. The company first earned authority by protecting settlements which could not defeat raiders alone. Its later leaders used a genuine guest-right murder and refused wergild to justify collective vengeance, the seizure of women and permanent kingship.

## Historical setting

**c. 1900–1400 BR; Hearths and First Kings.** Hartheni middle forests. This tradition belongs to [[Age of Hearths and First Kings]]; its principal cultural bearers are Hartheni and neighbouring Folk communities.

It is told in forest and marsh country where cattle protection, personal followings, public compensation and assembly law shaped early authority. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## Historical basis

One mixed company of low-status fighters, widowed herders, outlaws and displaced dependants became famous after recovering cattle for several mutually suspicious settlements. Their success gave the captain and his companions continuing claims upon food, followers and judgment. A dependant woman associated with the company was later raped and killed after receiving formal guest-right in a neighbouring household. The killer's house refused proper wergild and manipulated her status to offer an insulting valuation.

The company's grievance was lawful; its retaliation was not limited to the offenders. Its fighters participated in collective killing and the seizure of women, after which their cook or provisioner accepted a crown. The resulting kingdom brought safer roads and stronger cattle defence while converting voluntary support into taxes, service and dynastic obedience. It failed within several generations as successors repeated the founding coercion. Rival assemblies and descendants of the seized women preserved the counter-song which later strengthened Hartheni suspicion of permanent war-kings.

## The tale

The company began when raiders stole cattle from settlements that distrusted one another. An outlaw cook, widowed herders, household dependants and landless fighters recovered the herd by following smoke no trained warrior had noticed. Their success made them protectors. Soon people called them whenever assemblies failed to raise a common defence.

Then a low-ranking woman attached to the company was raped and murdered beneath the protection of a neighbouring house. Her killer's people refused wergild, arguing that her dependant status made her life worth less than the protection they had publicly granted. The insulting offer gathered fighters more effectively than any captain's summons.

The company joined the vengeance and carried it beyond the guilty house. Its men killed armed opponents and people who had neither committed nor defended the crime. They seized women from defeated households and later used a feast to take more. Some followers objected; others argued that new wives and labour were necessary if the company was to become a lasting people rather than disperse after victory.

The outlaw cook fed the abducted women before he accepted acclaim as the first ruler. He carried his plain iron cooking ring before the crown to show that he had once served the hungry. His victims said it proved only that a man could remember humility while exercising domination.

Protection brought roads, safer herds and coercive taxes. The company that had answered failed law became a court capable of overriding every assembly beneath it. The first kingdom collapsed after later rulers repeated its seizures without the founding generation's personal loyalty. Royal singers preferred to end the story at the coronation. The women taken during the vengeance taught their daughters a longer ending.

Those daughters did not agree about the company. Some remembered rescuers who later became oppressors; others remembered only the armed men beside their abduction. Their counter-song made the crown hear not one founding judgement but the households that its victory had treated as repair material.

## Why it is remembered

It refuses to make liberation morally simple. People may be genuine protectors and still commit crimes that no later peace cleans away.

## How tellers differ

Royal versions say the iron ring represents service. Women's laments call it the cooking ring of a household stolen before it was founded.

## What remains

Several coronations carry a black iron ring ahead of the crown. Someone strikes it once before the cheering begins.
