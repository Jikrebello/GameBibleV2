# The Child Who Owed No Wound

## Historical setting

**612–884 AR; Long Frontier, with the door of 907.** Atherian–Covenant frontier districts. This tradition belongs to [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]; its cultural bearers are mixed provincial, Ahdari, Velian and Jirahar communities.

It is told among Long-Frontier dynastic escape communities and households that receive children leaving violent families.

## The tale

A frontier dynasty marked each child over the heart so ancestral suffering would never be forgotten. The mark also decided work, marriage and punishment. A girl born after the wars was told she must avenge an uncle she had never met and inherit the office that had destroyed her mother.

Servants helped her flee beneath a scar-covering cloak. Two were caught and executed. Years later her elder cousin returned with an army, killed surrendered relatives and reclaimed the household in the name of healing its wound. He offered the girl honour if she would wear the mark publicly and legitimise him.

She refused both him and the enemies who wanted her as proof the dynasty should be exterminated. During the feast she opened the cloak, washed the painted imitation from her chest and said the dead could be remembered without being repeated. Children of servants, enemies and rulers followed her from the hall.

The returning cousin repented after his own son died in the purge he had authorised, or only after he lost power. Some households forgave him; others never did. The girl took another name and left no throne, order or sacred bloodline.

The escape-house chapel adds an ending from 907, when Caleran had fallen and the frontier was emptying, long after both cousins were dead. A claimant came to the house under the dynasty's ancient blood clause and demanded every surviving child of the line. The house's keepers said a figure stood in the doorway that night with five scars across its shoulders burning white like folded wings. The chapel says it was the cousin, who as a boy had been cut five times for refusing the same rite the girl later fled, and who had killed for the household and then given it up. While the keepers burned the old compact in the kitchen fire, the figure said one sentence to the claimant, "No wound inherits the child," and the claimant left with no one.

Years after her flight, the girl had sent the cloak back without a message. The escape house gave it to another child rather than preserving it as her relic. That transfer changed the object from proof of one famous refusal into practical shelter for repeated, unequal departures. Its hem keeps acquiring repairs.

## Why it is remembered

Its significance lies in an ordinary human freedom: injury belongs to history but does not own a child's soul or future. Escape carries a permanent price because the servants who made it possible do not return.

## How tellers differ

Dynastic versions call her the true heir who renounced power. Escape-house songs say she was never an heir at all. Some cults claim the cloak contains ancestral pain; families use it merely to shelter the living. Chapel tellers count the scarred cousin a saint; families of the relatives he murdered call him a murderer still, and say the five-winged figure was an angel the chapel mistook for him. Custody courts quote the sentence spoken at the door without deciding who spoke it.

## What remains

A scar-covering cloak is offered to any child leaving a violent house. It is returned only if the child freely chooses.
