# The Child Who Owed No Wound

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**612–884 AR; Long Frontier.** Atherian–Covenant frontier districts. This tradition belongs to [[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]; its cultural bearers are mixed provincial, Ahdari, Velian and Jirahar communities.

It is told among Long-Frontier dynastic escape communities and households that receive children leaving violent families. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

A frontier dynasty marked each child over the heart so ancestral suffering would never be forgotten. The mark also decided work, marriage and punishment. A girl born after the wars was told she must avenge an uncle she had never met and inherit the office that had destroyed her mother.

Servants helped her flee beneath a scar-covering cloak. Two were caught and executed. Years later her elder cousin returned with an army, killed surrendered relatives and reclaimed the household in the name of healing its wound. He offered the girl honour if she would wear the mark publicly and legitimise him.

She refused both him and the enemies who wanted her as proof the dynasty should be exterminated. During the feast she opened the cloak, washed the painted imitation from her chest and said the dead could be remembered without being repeated. Children of servants, enemies and rulers followed her from the hall.

The returning cousin repented after his own son died in the purge he had authorised, or only after he lost power. Some households forgave him; others never did. The girl took another name and left no throne, order or sacred bloodline.

She later sent the cloak back without a message. The escape house gave it to another child rather than preserving it as her relic. That transfer changed the object from proof of one famous refusal into practical shelter for repeated, unequal departures. Its hem keeps acquiring repairs.

## Why it is remembered

Its significance lies in an ordinary human freedom: injury belongs to history but does not own a child's soul or future. Escape carries a permanent price because the servants who made it possible do not return.

## How tellers differ

Dynastic versions call her the true heir who renounced power. Escape-house songs say she was never an heir at all. Some cults claim the cloak contains ancestral pain; families use it merely to shelter the living.

## What remains

A scar-covering cloak is offered to any child leaving a violent house. It is returned only if the child freely chooses.
