# General of the Glass Court

> **What happened — authorial history.** The earthly core and the expressly described later activity are established. Public cult claims, relic pedigrees and the final list of personal names are not thereby authenticated. This person is separate from every other life in the constellation.

## Earthly life

The future General begins as a camp tailor and armour repairer whose ability to make defeated people appear dignified earns access to a claimant's household. The tailor becomes a commander through logistics, theatre and personal courage. A fellow craftsperson and loyal field officer remains the one person able to criticise the General honestly.

As victory approaches, the General turns reconstruction into aesthetic cleansing: damaged veterans are hidden, mixed households expelled and surrendered rivals displayed before execution. During a private confrontation, the loyal officer threatens to reveal sexual abuse of office, diverted refugee funds and the cleansing programme. The General personally murders the officer, then fabricates assault orders and a field-death record so the body can be placed among soldiers abandoned in an unsupported action. A court offering perfected replacement bodies first acts as consolation, then becomes the chosen means of ruling without unwelcome witnesses.

## Later activity

The damned General presides over a mobile Glass Court in the Crown-Market of Shadows. It offers descendants flawless versions of dead heroes in exchange for surrendering contradictory records and bodily remains. Later contests involve the Red-Passage Witness, whose martyr-copy demands vengeance, and the Broken-Hands Smith, whose order is offered perfected bodies that erase disability and institutional guilt.

A Saint's work ends with the commission; no institution owns or summons the person. A damned Sinner's office, image, body and copied likeness are not the soul. Defeat of such a manifestation does not reverse judgement.

## What the life cost

Sexual abuse of office, personal murder of the beloved critic, execution of prisoners, expulsion of mixed families, theft from refugees and bodies used as patterns without consent.

## The surviving inheritance

a courtly school, military pageants, descendant claims, several constructed “returned” officers and no legitimate order founded by the General.

Formal portraits repaint wounds and mixed marriages out of the victorious generation; restoration reveals one repeated medium-and-contract signature beneath several faces. That signature supports coordinated revision but does not prove that every portrait belonged to one supernatural court.

## What people remember

Civilising hero, decadent traitor, victim of possession, several generals combined or beautiful Patron sovereign.

## What remains unknown

The private name, claimant, exact campaign, Patron, Mask involvement, current Court and whether any constructed person has acquired independent moral standing.
