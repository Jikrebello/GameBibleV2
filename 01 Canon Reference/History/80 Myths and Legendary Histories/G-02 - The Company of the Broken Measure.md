# The Company of the Broken Measure

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**c. 2400–2260 BR; Late Chains and Unbinding.** former Giant estates and Glassward approaches. This tradition belongs to [[Age of Chains]]; its cultural bearers are slave-descendant communities.

It is told in the Glassward marches and among peoples descended from Late-Chains estate slaves. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

A Giant estate measured food, sleep, marriage and punishment with one black rod. The rod allotted half a ration to anyone too sick to work and no medicine to anyone receiving half a ration. When a kitchen girl fell feverish, the steward's commands required that she be both alive for the tally and dead for the stores.

Six household strangers carried her between those two orders. A cook hid her in a flour chest. A stable thief changed the work marks. A bastard of the Giant house—human despite the blood that made him unnaturally strong—held the door while the others crawled through a channel the estate could not acknowledge without admitting its measure had contradicted itself.

The escape succeeded, but its strongest member did not become clean through victory. During a rage supernaturally distorted by the household powers, he killed people he loved. The masters found him beside the bodies and offered an impossible bargain: labour for them and perhaps his guilt would become useful. They sent him against beasts, rebel slaves and rival Giant kin, praising every victory as payment toward a debt that could never resurrect the dead.

At last he returned to the measuring house, snapped the rod and opened one route for the remaining slaves. Some followed. Some spat at him. The family of those he killed refused every gift.

When later singers called the final labour an atonement, that family stood up and left. Their absence became part of the performance: a row of places no triumph could fill, even though other captives owed their freedom to the guilty man's return.

## Why it is remembered

The company proves that an unjust order can break upon its own contradictions. The strongman's story warns that useful service does not transform murder into innocence.

## How tellers differ

Courtly versions make the strongman a flawless liberator. Household songs give the final word to the woman who refuses to forgive him.

## What remains

Children balance seeds on a snapped stick while chanting, “The measure broke first.” Three shrines claim pieces of the original rod.
