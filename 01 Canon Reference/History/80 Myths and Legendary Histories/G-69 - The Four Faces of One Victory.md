# The Four Faces of One Victory

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**c. 2400–2260 BR; Late Chains and Unbinding.** Sarrakai canal country. This tradition belongs to [[Age of Chains]]; its cultural bearers are slave-descendant communities.

It is told along old Sarrakai canals and among descendant boat households. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

Two river crowns fought over a canal first dug by prisoners. The upstream king seized the sluices during planting season and promised safe surrender to the boat families who defended them. When they laid down their hooks and bows, his commander killed them on the mudbanks.

The first victory relief showed the king towering over the dead. After soldiers complained that allied boat crews had been erased, stonecutters recarved the scene and placed the allies behind him. A later dynasty shaved them away again and added chains, presenting prisoners as property lawfully won. Generations after that, priests cut grieving faces into the margins and taught displaced descendants that every later tenant was an heir of the original murderers.

Thus one victory offered four different meals. Wrath took the massacre. Pride took the triumph. Greed took the sale of families and water rights. Envy took a true grievance and taught it to hunger for the homes of people born centuries later. The human rulers and commanders chose the crimes; no Hunger forced their hands.

Boat households eventually stopped singing the triumphal verse. Before opening the irrigation gates, they began naming those who had surrendered and were killed.

One conquered family refused the new recitation because the same crown still occupied its land. Another joined it so later tenants would not inherit the rulers' lie. The rite never produced agreement, only a moment when water could not flow without the victory revealing whom it had drowned.

## Why it is remembered

The relief shows how a crime changes meaning without ceasing to be a crime. It also warns that truthful grief can be taught to consume innocent inheritors.

## How tellers differ

Royal chronicles compress the recuttings into one restoration. Canal workers say each dynasty revealed the Hunger it preferred.

## What remains

The levee relief still bears four depths of carving. During the boat song, one verse is left wordless while the sluices rise.
