# The Peace Feast at Nine Hearths

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**c. 2165–1750 BR; overlapping Scattering and early Hearths.** one southern Scattering refuge basin, separate from the first gate and harbour. This tradition belongs to [[Age of Scattering]]; its cultural bearers are Ark-derived and neighbouring Orphaned communities.

It is told among descendants of one Scattering refuge and the settlement that received it. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

A refugee people asked winter shelter from a rich settlement of nine hearth districts. The ruler admitted them beyond the cattle ditch, but his son took a refugee woman by force. Her brothers demanded judgement. The ruler offered grain, cattle and marriage; the brothers publicly accepted an admission rite that required the settlement's fighting men to lay aside weapons and remain indoors.

On the appointed night the refugees went from hearth to hearth with hidden blades. They killed the ruler's house, then men who had taken no part in the assault, then boys old enough to avenge them. Houses that resisted were burned. Women, children and field workers were bound and divided among the victors. Some refugee elders tried to stop the killing; others seized grain while claiming they had always opposed it.

The assaulted woman vanished from the earliest songs after the first demand for justice. Later genealogies made her the willing bride of the dead prince and called the massacre a wedding battle. Yet the enslaved survivors taught the conquerors how to keep the canals alive. Their words entered lullabies; their daughters became ancestors of the people who denied them.

Generations later, a famine council discovered that the strictest rule against punishing a household for one member's crime had been preserved in the speech of those enslaved after the feast.

The council adopted the restraint while refusing to name its source. Survivor families began teaching it beside the ninth hearth, making the community's best protection speak in the accent its founding story had tried to erase.

## Why it is remembered

The story will not let sexual violence justify collective murder, nor let the horror of retaliation erase the first victim. It matters because the community's best restraint came from people its founders treated as spoil.

## How tellers differ

Official marriage songs say the prince and woman loved one another. Hearth laments call that the second assault. Some refugee descendants insist only the ruler's fighters were targeted; the ash line beneath children's rooms contradicts them.

## What remains

At wedding dances the circle stops at the ninth hearth and leaves one place empty. No bride or groom may be pulled across it.
