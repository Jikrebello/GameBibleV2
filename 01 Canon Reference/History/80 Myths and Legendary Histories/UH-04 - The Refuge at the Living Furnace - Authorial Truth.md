# The Refuge at the Living Furnace — Authorial Truth

> **What happened.** The central event and surviving evidence are established; no living witness knows the whole account. Its current discovery remains unfinished. This history is separate from its nearest thematic neighbours, as recorded in the integration ledger.

## What Actually Happened

**c. 740–810 AR; a small Fire-littoral refuge and its Material forge approach.** Exact coordinates and private names remain open; this local placement does not move the controlling chronology.

During the Long Frontier, several hundred people fled the destruction of a fortified river district. A route broker offered passage to a refuge where no frontier ruler could reach them. The destination was a small constructed littoral inside the Fire quadrant of the Elemental Shell: one fortified furnace platform of kilns, metal galleries, cooled barracks, and pressure conduits built around an Astral anchor and tethered to a great Material furnace. It was never a metropolis, free port, or capital and failed within its founding generation. The surrounding Shell was sterile state-of-matter pressure. It had no native people, king, will, or moral claim.

The refuge's owner was an Anchor-Lord with `astral-pattern`, acting as Patron through ownership of the anchor and route. Its human residents retained human soul. Native Astral servants likewise had `astral-pattern`; later Driftkin workers had `drift-rewritten-life`; cooling frames and adaptive furnace systems used `shell-pattern` or constructed copy and were not persons. No one became an elemental by residence. The Patron initially provided real shelter, water condensed through imported apparatus, and a return route. It also required every admitted household to supply maintenance labor.

The first compact limited service to seven years. After a conduit failure killed experienced workers, the Patron's magistrates reclassified their outstanding shifts as household debt. Children inherited the missing labor. Criminal sentences, insolvency, and unpaid passage could all be converted into dangerous furnace duty. The government placed slaves, condemned prisoners, and debtors nearest the failure points because replacing them was cheaper than shutting the industrial galleries. People burned, suffocated, or died when cooling chambers failed. Refuge was genuine; so was the hereditary slavery built out of its upkeep.

The platform survived by releasing waste heat through the Material furnace that anchored its return. That furnace stood inside an old forge district whose workers still understood its ordinary mechanisms. Refugee families in the Material world received smuggled maintenance tallies and learned what the refuge had become. They did not cast a universal spell. They extinguished the furnace, shattered two heat-sink collars, and opened its emergency vents at the same hour that enslaved workers inside the refuge closed the industrial galleries and jammed the labor registers into the pressure valves.

With no Material heat sink, the platform began to melt from its outer galleries inward. The Patron could preserve its private hall and treasury only by releasing stored route pressure and reopening the return gate. It chose itself. Hundreds reached the gate, but guards and privileged households fought for passage. Some laborers killed magistrates; some guards killed refugees; several families were crushed or burned in the galleries. The Patron escaped with a smaller anchor fragment and some clients. The outer works failed completely. A few sealed service vaults may have remained habitable for days or months, but no continuing polity or present route is established.

Returnees concealed the full history for incompatible reasons. Frontier rulers did not want proof that they had driven subjects into extrarealm bondage. Merchant houses did not want hereditary labor contracts tested against their own passage debts. Some survivors defended the refuge because it had saved their families before enslaving them. Others feared that admitting any sealed vault had outlasted the revolt would invite another extraction campaign. The Patron's personal name, later anchor, and the fate of those who remained are not recoverable from known evidence.

## What Later Traditions Say

- **Frontier sermons** describe a false paradise destroyed when refugees worshipped its fiery ruler.
- **Merchant records** call the refuge a voluntary industrial colony whose workers rioted after refusing lawful debts.
- **Survivor households** disagree over whether the Patron was rescuer, slaver, or both at different stages.
- **Shell prospectors** claim the melted platform belonged to a native fire civilization. Its tools actually repeat human and Astral workshop measures.



## Discovery Interface

- **Knowledge state:** `suppressed`, `misclassified`, and partly `unknown`.
- **Limited contemporary witnesses:** a refugee admission clerk knew the seven-year compact but not later furnace deaths; a labor forewoman knew the inherited debt system and revolt but not the Material sabotage; a forge worker recorded the synchronized shutdown but never entered the platform; the Patron's surviving proxy knows private-hall decisions only after the first collapse.
- **Primary remainders:** heat-altered human tools, multilingual labor tallies, household debt contracts, death lists, shattered Material heat-sink collars, one melted platform, and one emergency doorway mechanism.
- **Independent evidence classes:** material, documentary, procedural, testimonial, comparative, and negative.
- **Transmission damage:** merchant houses split contracts by language; frontier governments confiscated survivor testimony; prospectors looted the galleries; later copies converted labor categories into moral castes; the Patron's proxies removed its personal name from every portable instrument.
- **1360 AR access point:** repair of the old Material forge uncovers the emergency collars and a sealed register naming living descendant households as still owing furnace shifts.
- **Reproducible test:** when the two surviving collars are mounted in their original order and the furnace reaches the recorded temperature, the emergency doorway appears for less than a minute onto the same dead outer gallery. Reversing either collar prevents the opening. The test proves a working historical correspondence, not a safe route or the Patron's survival.
- **Plausible false reading:** the refugees willingly colonized a native elemental fortress and destroyed it through religious rebellion; present descendants therefore inherit the refuge's unpaid debt.
- **Corroboration threshold:** the seven-year admission compact, inherited tallies, death distribution, Material sabotage record, human workshop measures, and doorway test must agree before refuge-turned-slavery is established.
- **What remains unknowable:** the Patron's name and later fate, the exact population, how long the sealed service vaults remained habitable, the fate of those left behind, and whether any individuals escaped by another route. No current Shell settlement is presumed to descend from the platform.
- **Protected Weird check:** the history does not explain the Held Breath or any elemental anomaly in the Weird register.
