# Smith Who Broke His Hands

> **What happened — authorial history.** The earthly core and the expressly described later activity are established. Public cult claims, relic pedigrees and the final list of personal names are not thereby authenticated. This person is separate from every other life in the constellation.

## Earthly life

The future Smith begins as a foundry bondservant whose unusual resistance to heat becomes the workshop's excuse for giving dangerous tasks to other children. After manumission the Smith teaches that weakness must be exposed and overcome. During a test, a gifted apprentice rings the stop bell and begs release. The Smith personally holds the mechanism closed to prove that fear can be mastered, and the apprentice dies. Several maimed witnesses are expelled because their injuries supposedly prove unfitness.

The Smith eventually recognizes that the school has made injury into shame and strength into entitlement. In a public act neither miraculous nor painless, the Smith breaks the celebrated artificial gauntlets, confesses responsibility, sells the workshop interest for victim restitution and spends the remaining years designing tools usable by maimed workers. Former followers resist; some attack the new workshop and kill another apprentice. Repentance changes the Smith but cannot restore those already dead.

## Later activity

During one Closed-Door crisis, the Saint manifests before leaders of a later strength-order that has begun replacing healthy flesh and abandoning injured members. The manifestation names victims, breaks the chapter seal and protects its records, then withdraws without selecting new leaders. A later conflict with the Glass General concerns perfected replacement bodies offered as a way to erase culpability and grief.

A Saint's work ends with the commission; no institution owns or summons the person. A damned Sinner's office, image, body and copied likeness are not the soul. Defeat of such a manifestation does not reverse judgement.

## What the life cost

Child labour, the founder's knowing refusal to stop a lethal test, expulsion of maimed witnesses, founder culpability and retaliatory murder after reform.

## The surviving inheritance

a divided craft tradition, accessible tools, a coercive strength-order and no single lawful successor institution.

Paired broken gauntlets are displayed in rival cities; neither pair need be original for the reform charter to be authentic.

## What people remember

Patron of strength, patron of disabled artisans, hypocrite, late penitent, fabricated reformer or saint captured in the broken gauntlets.

## What remains unknown

The private name, craft culture, exact injuries, death, relics, order and outcome of the later correction.
