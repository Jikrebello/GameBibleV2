# The Company with No Charter

> **Stories told.** This is an unfinished 1360 tradition. Current rumours and the observable practice are established; no future discovery, victory, judgement or fate is implied.

## Historical setting

**1297–1360 AR; Reformation and unfinished present.** known-world towns and Anchor-facing harbours. This tradition belongs to [[The Age of Held Proofs]]; its cultural bearers are living townspeople and conflicting current witnesses.

It is told in 1360 AR taverns, caravan yards and neighbourhoods that have begun arguing whether the group exists at all. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

A failed apprentice found an injured stranger beneath a collapsed sign. Carrying him to safety drew the attention of a debt collector, a shrine keeper and two siblings searching for someone who had vanished. None trusted the others. Each expected to leave after one night.

The injured stranger possessed a token claimed by three institutions. The collector could have paid a family debt by surrendering it. The keeper believed it should be burned. The siblings recognised a mark used by the people who took their kin. While they argued, armed claimants arrived and set the lodging on fire.

The five escaped through a laundry yard with an old cook who refused to leave her neighbour's caged birds. They saved the birds, lost most of their money and discovered the token was not what any claimant had described. At dawn, an official offered protection if they signed a charter placing their finds, testimony and future service under one patron.

They refused to sign. That is as far as the shared story has reached. Some have already lied to one another. One may still sell the token. The vanished person may be dead. The company exists only because each has, for now, chosen not to walk away.

The cook fed them again without asking for a promise. At sunrise, the rescued birds returned to the burned roof instead of flying free. Nobody agreed whether that was loyalty, confusion or merely the nearest perch.

## Why it is remembered

This root plants forward hooks without converting them into foretold history. Its significance is intimate: debt, a missing sibling, injured trust, a neighbour's birds and the frightening first moment strangers become responsible for one another.

## How tellers differ

Because the events are recent, versions differ by gossip rather than tradition. Officials call them thieves. Children call them the Bird Company. None of the names has authority over what they become.

## What remains

A scorched bird cage hangs above the repaired lodging. Locals place unsigned scraps inside when they need help but do not know whom to trust.

## History and belief

The practice, landscape or physical object described here has a place in the present world. Statements that it is the original relic, that a voice is the original dead person, or that a victory proves divine approval belong to the tradition unless a separate history establishes them.

The tale stops at the present.
