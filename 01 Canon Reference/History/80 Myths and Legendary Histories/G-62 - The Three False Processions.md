# The Three False Processions

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**c. 2400–2260 BR; Late Chains and Unbinding.** a separate lesser court and its Egg White pursuit routes. This tradition belongs to [[Age of Chains]]; its cultural bearers are slave-descendant communities.

It is told along ruined Late-Chains pursuit routes and in the contradictory memories of copied Giant ancestors. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

Another lesser Giant court escaped by a subtler cruelty. Its rulers copied the speech, gait and household memories of honoured ancestors into processional bodies. They dressed these copies in royal colours, gave each a different itinerary and sent all three toward the passages most likely to draw the Hunt.

The copies believed they were the true evacuation. One remembered a childhood orchard that had never existed. Another knew the private name of the ruler's mother. A third begged the servants to hurry because the palace children were already aboard. Human tenants, expendable Giant kin and condemned soldiers marched among them, unaware that they had been chosen to make the deception convincing.

The Hunt found every procession. Route stones split beneath its passage. Bodies, wagons and copied memories were torn apart. While the hunters followed three royal scents, the actual caravan crossed elsewhere after stripping its dependants of food and shelter.

Centuries later, recovered ancestor-patterns still accuse one another of being the decoy. Each remembers watching the others depart. None remembers the hidden fourth road. The escaped court calls the manoeuvre a heroic rescue; the route dead call it a procession held for people who never intended to attend their own funeral.

One copied grandmother repeatedly asks after a living grandchild abandoned with the tenants. The court keeps silencing her because the question survives every restoration. Whether love in the copy is memory, performance or something stranger, the child was real and no procession returned for him.

## Why it is remembered

It asks whether memory makes a victim less real and whether believing oneself royal excuses the ruler who made that belief into bait.

## How tellers differ

Some hunters' songs claim one copy understood and chose sacrifice. Surface traditions insist that this is later propaganda meant to make abandonment look voluntary.

## What remains

Three routes contain palace hinges, royal cloth and ancestor-patterns that cannot all describe the same childhood. The fourth itinerary has never been found.
