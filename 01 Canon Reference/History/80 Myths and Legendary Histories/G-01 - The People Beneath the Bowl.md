# The People Beneath the Bowl

> **Stories told.** The local telling and the material or customary remainder below are established. A claimed original object, miraculous response, famous identity or supernatural explanation is not authenticated merely by this article.

## Historical setting

**c. 2400–2260 BR; Late Chains and Unbinding.** Ansharan household estates and their dispersed descendants. This tradition belongs to [[Age of Chains]]; its cultural bearers are slave-descendant communities.

It is told among descendants of Giant-household slaves across several former imperial horizons. Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account of the event.

## The tale

A kitchen child once dropped a golden bowl at the feet of a Giant steward. The steward struck him, wrote his name among the dead and ordered the cooks to replace him before supper. But the boy was not dead. A washerwoman pulled him beneath the preparation table, covered him with an overturned mixing bowl and told the steward that rats had carried the body away.

The mistake should have ended by morning. Instead, the servants discovered that the dead were less closely watched than the living. A dead scullion could carry bread to a bricked cellar. A dead groom could sleep behind the feed bins. A dead nurse could move through the children's wing after dark. Soon every household had two populations: the numbered servants seen by the masters and the unnumbered people who lived in walls, culverts, ovens gone cold and rooms too small for Giant hands.

They stole food, buried bodies the reclamation works wanted, moved children from cruel households and sometimes opened service doors for whole families. They did not save everyone. When one refuge was exposed, the masters boiled its grain, sealed its passages and left the hidden people to suffocate. Some escaped toward the Glass gathering. Some remained and disappeared during the Drinking Wars. Others betrayed neighbouring refuges to preserve their own.

The tale never names a commander because there was none. It begins with one frightened child and becomes a people only because hundreds of servants decide, separately, to continue the same mercy.

## Why it is remembered

It makes the smallest act of concealment the beginning of resistance. Children love the cleverness; adults hear the cost of surviving through compromises nobody celebrates.

## How tellers differ

Some say the first hidden child later led an Exodus column. Others insist he died under the same bowl and that the game remembers only the lie that kept later children alive.

## What remains

In the game *Beneath the Bowl*, one child hides while one place at the table remains empty. Nobody may claim the empty portion until the hidden child is found.
