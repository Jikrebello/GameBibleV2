### Overview

The Serpent Kingdom of Ishkatar was a river civilisation of flood calendars, monumental tomb-estates, divine kingship, and concentrated wealth. Along the fertile **Serpent's Spine**, its Naga Giants built an imperial religion around ownership, hierarchy, and descent from **Avarix, the Idol of Greed**. The oldest Naga houses possessed genuine Titanic ancestry, including lines descending from Titan-Firstborn conceived through bounded Idol manifestations associated with Avarix. Court genealogy turned that remote and intermingled ancestry into the stronger claim that every reigning Pharaoh-Serpent was Avarix's direct, undiluted child. Later Idol patronage and bodily alteration further shaped the dynasties without making that claim universally true. Ishkatar's decline was swift and brutal, leaving its cities beneath the desert.

### Geography

The Serpent Kingdom of Ishkatar was situated along the banks of the **Serpent’s Spine**, a mighty river that wound its way through the vast desert. The river was the lifeblood of the kingdom, its annual floods bringing the fertile silt that allowed for the cultivation of crops in the otherwise barren landscape. The kingdom’s cities, including the capital **Nagtara**, were built along the river, with towering pyramids and monumental statues dominating the skyline. Beyond the river’s fertile banks lay the **Sea of Sand**, a seemingly endless desert that protected the kingdom from outside threats but also isolated it from the rest of the world.

### Ruling Giants: The Naga Giants

The ruling class of Ishkatar, the **Naga Giants**, were massive serpentine beings with humanoid upper bodies and colossal coiling lower bodies. Their scales carried metallic and jewel-like colouration shaped by inherited Titanic traits, generations of Giant blood craft, and Avarix patronage. Their subjects were required to revere each Pharaoh-Serpent and royal house as Avarix's immediate offspring. That formula exaggerated a real but remote ancestry into an endlessly renewed proof of rule. The Naga Giants ruled through force, temple estates, and absolute demands upon their human subjects.

Ishkatar's continuity project attempted to make title and property outlive the ruler. Estates, offices, guardians, records, and ownership claims were built to reconstruct authority after bodily death. The system could preserve records, residue, and imposed office; it did not preserve the dead Giant person without a separately established uninterrupted survival, and it could neither create a human soul nor grant the Image.

The Naga Giants were also the spiritual leaders of Ishkatar, acting as intermediaries between the mortal world and the divine. Each Naga Giant governed a specific aspect of life, such as fertility, death, war, or commerce, and their temples were the centres of both religious and political power. The most powerful of the Naga Giants held the title of **Pharaoh-Serpent**, ruling over all others and presiding from the **Golden Pyramid of Nagtara**, the largest and most opulent structure in the kingdom.

### Culture and Religion

Ishkatar was a deeply religious society, with its culture centred around the worship of the Naga Giants as living deities. The people of Ishkatar believed that the Naga Giants were the chosen ones of Avarix, tasked with ruling over the mortal world and accumulating the wealth of the earth in his name. The kingdom's religious practices were elaborate and often brutal, with human sacrifices playing a central role. The blood of slaves and captured enemies was believed to appease the Naga Giants and ensure the prosperity of the kingdom.

The most significant religious events were tied to the cycles of the Serpent’s Spine. The **Festival of the Flood** was the most important celebration, marking the annual inundation of the river that brought life to the kingdom. During this festival, massive sacrifices were made, and the riverbanks were lined with offerings of gold, jewels, and food, all meant to secure the favour of the Naga Giants and Avarix.

Architecture and art were also deeply intertwined with religion. The pyramids of Ishkatar were not just tombs but massive religious complexes where the Naga Giants were worshipped. The interiors of these pyramids were adorned with intricate carvings, depicting the Naga Giants' divine deeds, their victories in battle, and their endless pursuit of wealth. The walls of these structures were often inlaid with gold and precious stones, making them both places of worship and symbols of the kingdom's immense wealth.

### Technology and Achievements

The Naga Giants were master architects, engineers, and artisans, responsible for some of the most impressive structures in the ancient world. They developed advanced irrigation systems to control the floods of the Serpent’s Spine, turning the desert into a thriving agricultural hub. The kingdom's engineers constructed vast canals, dikes, and reservoirs, ensuring that even the most arid lands could be farmed.

The pyramids of Ishkatar were the most iconic achievements of the Naga Giants. These towering structures, built from massive blocks of stone, served as both tombs for the Naga Giants and temples for their worship. The largest of these, the **Golden Pyramid of Nagtara**, was a wonder of the ancient world, its apex capped with a massive block of pure gold that gleamed in the desert sun.

The kingdom was also known for its wealth, accumulated through both conquest and trade. The Naga Giants amassed vast treasures of gold, jewels, and precious artifacts, which were stored in vast underground vaults. These treasures were not just symbols of power but were also believed to hold magical properties, enhancing the Naga Giants' control over the kingdom.

### Decline

> [!note] Amended 14 November 2026
> [[Causality Pass - 01 Deep Past and the Ancient East|Causality Pass 01.2]]: Giant dilution is the root of the collapse, and the Giant ritual that dried the Serpent's Spine is now survivor legend. The river failed because nobody kept it.

Ishkatar began to die in its tombs. Its continuity project made every dead Pharaoh-Serpent an owner: each tomb-estate held river fields, granaries, barges, oxen and the households who worked them, so that title and property would outlive the body. The first Naga houses, close to their Titanic ancestors, could wake and command those estates with their own blood. Their diluted heirs could not. The estates answered thinner blood only when more was poured into them from outside: first labour to keep the offering-halls and guardian works turning, then captives for the halls, then the grain of living districts, and at the end children entered on the tomb rolls at birth.

By the Closing-Court years the dead held much of the best land along the Serpent's Spine, and the living fed them. The dike gangs of the middle reaches were marched off the river to haul facing-stone for new tomb-works. The towpath crews who had dragged silt-sledges along the regulators every dry season were sent to quarry. Nagtara's granaries were emptied into offering-halls while the reach villages ground seed grain and stripped the date palms. Dikes went a season unmended, then two. Each estate's overseers swore that the river could wait one more year and the dead could not.

The plagues of the Unbinding struck through ownership. Offerings rotted in sealed halls, tomb guardians stopped answering their marks, and households on the estate rolls found that nothing held them to the land. The canal and dike crews rose first, on the towpaths where the overseers came to take them. They walked off the works, burned estate rolls in the reach villages and carried their children upriver at night. As the Great Prophet's warnings, gathering columns and road toward the Glass made release imaginable, enslaved households and subject cities followed. The uprisings spread through burial, canal and refuge networks before, during and after the principal host's departure; the later sanctuary beyond the Glass still failed near its threshold.

The Naga Giants answered with massacre on the riverbanks, and then turned on one another. Their civil wars were fought over the regulators: the sluice-works that decided which reach received the flood. Each house held a few sluices and shut them to starve a rival's fields, or opened them to drown a rival's camp, while claiming the throne of the Pharaoh-Serpent and the treasures of the Golden Pyramid for itself.

No one was left to keep the river. In a high flood year the Spine rose against dikes that had not been mended in a generation, overtopped them above Nagtara, broke into an older and lower bed, and never returned to its course. The royal reach and its tomb-estates dried in a single season; the Golden Pyramid stood above a channel of cracked mud. Major cities emptied as people fled or perished, while some canal towns, delta crowns, oasis clients and mobile communities entered separate successor histories along the new bed. The [[Lesser Crowns of the Giant Age|Reed Crowns of Sarrakai]] found the flood running past their own doors. Ishkatar ceased to exist as an imperial order; its wealth and many royal archives disappeared beneath the sands.

**Survivor legend.** The delta boat-households tell it otherwise. In their songs a Naga lord, desperate to hold the Golden Pyramid against his kin, performed a ritual that drank the river into his coils, and the Spine has run dry along its old course ever since. Reach villages add that the lord burst; delta priests say he still lies under the dry bed, full of water. The legend compresses a generation of neglected dikes, stolen crews and sluice wars into one villain, and later tellers preferred it because it named a single guilty Giant. No ritual dried the Spine.

### Legacy and Myth

The ruins of the Serpent Kingdom of Ishkatar lie buried beneath the shifting sands of the desert, their secrets hidden from the world. The pyramids, now crumbling and half-buried, stand as silent sentinels to a bygone era of opulence and cruelty. The treasures of the Naga Giants, said to hold immense power, are believed to still lie within the labyrinthine vaults beneath the ruins, waiting to be discovered by those brave—or foolish—enough to seek them out.

The legend of Ishkatar has become a cautionary tale of the dangers of greed and the pursuit of power at any cost. The Naga Giants, once revered as gods, are now remembered as tyrants whose insatiable lust for wealth led to their downfall. The kingdom’s fall serves as a stark reminder of the fragile nature of civilisation and the destructive power of unchecked ambition.

The estates did not all forget their owners. Ishkatari metal kept its title marks, and some marks remained joined to dormant recovery works: [[Gilded Wardens]], processional coils and estate dead. When later ruin traders opened deeper estate-vaults (c. 1450–1325 BR), linked Wardens woke and walked the old ceremonial roads recovering marked metal from caravans and palace stores. That episode, the Reclaiming, helped break the Bronze Roads and is why later traders preferred iron that no dead owner claimed. See [[Causality Pass - 01 Deep Past and the Ancient East|Causality Pass 01.7]].

The legacy of Ishkatar also lives on in the myths and legends of the world, with tales of the Naga Giants and their treasures inspiring adventurers and treasure hunters. The lost civilisation is often depicted in art and literature as a place of immense beauty and horror, a kingdom where the line between the divine and the monstrous was blurred, and where the price of power was ultimately too high to bear.
