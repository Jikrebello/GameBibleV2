# Lesser Crowns of the Giant Age

**c. 3400–2260 BR, with different peaks inside that span.** These are established secondary political traditions inside the Five Imperial Horizons. Their familiar names are archaeological or translated labels. They do not fix modern borders, exact native endonyms or an uninterrupted royal genealogy.

A smaller crown might change overlord, marry into another house and preserve its own people. The following accounts establish those political histories without declaring every later ancestral voice genuine.

## Arsuvan, the Aurin kingdom

[[Arsuvan - The Giant Kingdom of the Aurin|Arsuvan]] joined river agriculture, upland metal production, workshops and maritime exchange in the Aurin basin. Iskaren and Velketh rulers governed through inherited Giant power, military households and human labour. Their palace at Sarven maintained observation terraces and ceremonies which presented the royal houses as celestial descendants.

The kingdom disintegrated during the Great Unbinding. Arveth's seizure of seed grain provoked resistance; Lethra's crews carried families and stores away by night. Arveth died during the subsequent fighting, and rival claimants damaged the palace before unified government ceased. The related Enathi, Serathi and Vardeni developed among its human survivors and neighbouring remnants. Their Orphaned history remained distinct from the Glass migration.

Arsuvan is an additional regional kingdom alongside the eighteen secondary polities originally gathered in this register. Its remote Giant ancestries are classified in [[Caleran Foundations - Authorial History]]. Kharad, the Copper Stair kingdoms and Ulmaris retain independent histories and are connected here only through indirect exchange and knowledge.

## Kharad, the Bronze Meridian

A seven-generation road empire founded by the marriage of an Ishkatari serpent claimant and a Qianglong cadet, then enlarged by capturing an Ansharan processional corridor. Recognized as a peer only at its height; treated as a rebel province before and after.

Successive rulers wear one Meridian office and are later compressed into the “King of Three Fathers.” Bronze route rods still recognize bounded road authority while disproving the official biological genealogy.

## Sarr-Vey, the Twin Diadem

Two peer God-Kin houses with incompatible blood laws fight to exhaustion, exchange hostages, intermarry, and govern through two crowns. Alternately courted and invaded by Anshara and Qianglong; never fully absorbed while the diadem functions.

The shared instrument works only when two lawful rulers voice opposed advice before reaching judgement. Later cults disagree over which house conquered the other; escaped ancestors may still share one hostile Egg White anchor.

## Vathra and the Crown-Cities

[[Vathra and the Great Unyoking|Vathra]] was a confederated Giant crown whose principal cities moved physically through the Material sky. Urzavad, Kel-Orun, Dazhur, Sairat, Molqara and Yazuran carried palaces, workshops, gardens, prisons and dependent neighbourhoods. Their seasonal circuit allowed concentrated force to appear above country incapable of supporting a permanent royal court.

Human settlements supplied nearly all food, water, fibre, timber, animals and ordinary labour. The cities flew through crafted lift bodies, Zephyraxis pressure mantles, Hecatara route anchors and ground crews who operated the system. Royal cavalry and chariot horses remained concentrated among Giant and client houses, while human camel networks sustained the roads beneath them.

The War of Altitude and the [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking|Great Unyoking]] broke the crown between c. 2300 and 2260 BR. Urzavad fell, Kel-Orun was deliberately grounded and Dazhur escaped after discarding a dependent ward. Sairat, Molqara and Yazuran passed beyond reliable Material history. Their current state remains unresolved.

## The Ulmaris Sea-Crown

An archipelagic thalassocracy formed from Nythalum engineering houses, older island God-Kin, and Xolotlan maritime war families. Controls the sea between Nythalum's island and several continental extraction coasts; alternates between partner, client, and enemy.

Ulmaris provides copper, crystal, timber, food, captives, and Nythalum-directed launch-support sites to Ascension; it possesses no rival lunar programme. It revolts when promised lunar berths are withdrawn. Its last fleets pursue fallen spires across the physical ocean rather than entering the Egg White.

## The Reed Crowns of Sarrakai

A delta league of flood cities whose crown rotates among serpent grandmothers, giant-blooded houses, and late human temple dynasties. Ishkatar claims permanent overlordship; Anshara treats its rulers as marriage-worthy border crowns.

Canal access follows adopted kinship as much as force. Rival flood records preserve several simultaneous Pharaoh-Serpents and make Ishkatar's supposedly seamless chronology impossible.

## The Copper Stair Kingdoms

Many small mountain states controlling mines, passes, terraces, and smelters; their cadet houses repeatedly marry conquerors. Lie between Qianglong-facing valleys and Ansharan or Ishkatari roads; suzerainty changes without ending local kingship.

Every stair preserves layers of tax marks in different imperial scripts. One kingdom supplies Qianglong vault bronze while its sister furnishes Ansharan throne-wax vessels during the same war.

## The Seven Furnace Cities of Orqad

A neutral industrial league built around seven separated pieces of a dismembered Titan-Firstborn whose living body-heat powers the furnaces. Arms every great horizon and survives by making any conqueror fear that rivals will receive the other six cities.

The league's real constitution keeps the Titan divided. A royal attempt to collect four fragments begins a reconstitution crisis; later weapons may still turn toward the missing body pieces.

## The Glassward Crownlets

Human-majority buffer kingdoms, slave depots, oasis courts, and fortified estate towns along Anshara's southern approaches and the then-existing Glass margin. Pay shifting tribute to Anshara, Ishkatar, and lesser road empires.

They are the first states forced to choose when the Prophet's movement reaches the frontier. Some open wells, some sell refugees, and some join the Exodus; no single choice becomes the ancestry of every later people.

## The Hundred Wells of Narash

A chain of matrilineal caravan crowns whose rulers control water, bride exchange, sanctuary, ransom, and safe-conduct. Survives between Ishkatar, Anshara, Vathra, and Kharad through marriage diplomacy.

Its maternal books preserve direct Titan-Firstborn births more accurately than later paternal king lists. Several wells still enforce ancient sanctuary without morally approving the ruler who purchased it.

## The Black Orchard Courts

Ansharan cadet kingdoms built around paradise-gardens, blood orchards, and specialized mortal vampire houses. Formally satrapies, practically independent whenever the Court of Courts weakens.

Some close themselves during the Drinking Wars; others become minor Egg White courts or counterfeit ancestral patrons. Their surviving bloodlines disagree over whether secession was rebellion, self-rescue, or theft.

## The Cloudstep Principalities

Qianglong mountain clients founded by inter-caste marriages and local weather or fertility correspondences. Alternately protected, purged, and annexed by the currently dominant Qianglong caste.

Their very existence refutes claims that the four castes never mixed. Hidden terraces preserve medicines and crops created by lineages the imperial genealogy declared impossible.

## The Ash Calendar Cities

Older Xolotlan highland and jungle city-states, each with its own captive calendar, observatory, and war patron. Incorporated unevenly into Xolotl; their priests are forced to synchronize sacrifice with the Blood Sun.

Their rejected calendars preserve eclipses and rebellions the imperial count erases. One city's refusal to supply a “missing day” creates a gap in Blood Sun upkeep without altering cosmic time.

## The Moon Harbors of Caelene

Mainland and island treaty ports that feed Nythalum's launch economy while remaining locally ruled. Nythalum promises berths, technical education, and survival quotas in exchange for material and labor.

When the core retracts the berths, some harbors revolt, seize unfinished Nythalum vessels, or attempt doomed launches with stolen plans and defecting Nythalum engineers. They never possess an independent lunar programme. Every route, wreck, receiving frame, and surviving claim remains physical and moonward, never an Egg White current.

## The Ossuary League of Urum

Mortuary cities and burial fraternities governed by human keepers, diluted Death-linked Giants, and neutral hostage judges. Tolerated because every empire needs corpse exchange, plague burial, and custody of dangerous ancestor remains.

Urum distinguishes a human grave, Giant body, Titan fragment, office relic, and empty royal reconstruction more carefully than any imperial theology. Its sealed keys may be the only proof that a claimed ancestor is a copy.

## The Empty Throne of Meret

A city-kingdom whose last embodied God-Kin sovereign was divided and whose throne refuses every permanent replacement. Repeatedly occupied by Ishkatar and Anshara, neither of which can make its preferred claimant pass the throne's bounded test.

A mortal council governs while the throne recognizes temporary judges, war leaders, or petitioners for one commission at a time. Recognition proves actual access, not divine descent, general virtue, or a right to empire.

## The Dam Kingdom of Ezhar

A hydraulic choke-state whose mixed dynasty controls a mountain dam, reservoir, and the only reliable passage between two agricultural basins. Changes allegiance among Qianglong, Ishkatar, and Kharad while its engineers remain indispensable.

Conquest replaces crowns but not water administration. Its final breach may have been sabotage, liberation, or a failed attempt to carry the reservoir's law into an ancestral refuge.

## The Nine Bearings

A league of navigator isles, observatories, pilot houses, and neutral repair harbors. Trades with Ulmaris, Xolotl, and Nythalum while resisting annexation.

Its pilots supply observational and navigational support to Nythalum but never build lunar vessels or pursue colonization independently. Their star tables understand the Moon as a material destination with orbital windows, tides, mass, and distance, and can expose later mystical claims that Nythalum “ascended out of creation”.

## The Thorn-Spear Confederacy

Seasonal jungle and upland leagues of human communities, lesser Giant houses, defectors, and fostered war leaders. Xolotl lists them as delinquent tributaries; local traditions remember repeated negotiated independence.

Authority lives in witnessed oath-spears rather than one throne. Heroes can save one valley without becoming emperors, and later Xolotlan art falsely depicts every defeated confederate as the same monster people.

## The collapse did not have one outcome

Cloudstep terraces and Ash Calendar cities could defect without becoming one human nation. Glassward wells admitted, sold or refused refugees. Orqad's fragment-gathering crisis ended in heavenly binding of the selected Titan, not an ordinary killing or a repeatable method for assembling a god. Urum's practical distinctions survived in burial craft. Several Black Orchard rulers escaped; others consumed their dependants or died in the Drinking Wars. No one is identified as Anshara's final sovereign.

A repentant Giant defector at a Glassward crossing received temporal mercy after surrendering captives and breaking a pursuit engine. The defector did not acquire a human soul or become a Saint. A counterfeit escaped ancestor later claimed the deed through copied memories; that claimant is not the same person. The two figures, Orqad's bound Titan, a bodily escaped Giant and the lunar Continuant remain five separate ancient actor histories, not one biography spread across realms.
