# Creation and the First Rebellions

Before any human calendar there was a war in heaven, and not all of its losers went down with the Adversary. Some forced themselves into the yolk of the world and became the powers later called Menhir and Idols; their fusion and its aftermath broke the Faewild in the Schism. Every later empire that claimed divine authority, beginning with the Giant horizons of the [[Age of Chains]], mistook that creaturely power for the Lord's uncreated authority.

## Before kingdoms

There was a world before there were human calendars. The Lord is uncreated; everything which later receives the language of godhood is creaturely. The oldest songs often reverse that order because a people encounters a mountain, a hungry image or an ancestral tyrant before it learns to distinguish power from divinity.

Some of the rebels deserted the defeated Adversary and chose the Yolk. That choice was culpable. Their fusion made the future Menhir and Idols something other than ordinary fallen angels: powers distributed through place, law, appetite and repeated manifestation. Later religion remembers these transformations as marriages, wounds, births, descents and the eating of forbidden food. No one surviving mortal account tells the entire sequence.

## The first divisions

The Menhir's fusion bound them into the world's natural ordering while destroying their capacity to understand moral obligation. They can remember an insult, cherish a dependency and keep an exact bargain without recognising that fulfilling it may be evil. The Idols retained that knowledge. They knowingly cultivate the human acts through which their Hungers feed.

After fusion, the Faewild Schism broke the historical unity associated with Trium. Its aftermath produced relationships later summarised as Circles. Courts abandoned places; others inherited duties without receiving every former inhabitant's loyalty. A scar in a mountain may therefore outlast the court which claims to have made it.

## What later peoples made of it

Giant houses claimed descent from these older powers. Human priests later arranged incompatible local stories into family trees. Atherian scholars made the stars into relatives, turning conflicts among cities into quarrels among heavenly households. Folk tellers instead remembered the conditions under which a stranger could cross a threshold or survive a hunt.

No mortal witnessed the Faewild Schism. Human knowledge of it begins with remains, inherited errors and encounters with powers who have reasons to misrepresent themselves.

## Inheritance

The war also left wreckage on the ground. Old battlefields kept their banners after their armies were gone, and some of those standards still sort anyone who comes within their reach onto sides in a fight nobody remembers. Giant courts later dug them up and carried them into their own wars; the slaves driven under them learned that lowering a standard ended the battle when killing the enemy only restarted it ([[Age of Chains]]).

The later world inherits different kinds of danger. A Menhir correspondence may make a harvest fail without punishing a sin. An Idol may answer a cruel rite precisely because the cruelty feeds it. A heavenly judgement follows a commission. A human ruler can misunderstand all three and still command an army.

These distinctions shape [[Age of Chains]], when supernatural ancestry becomes a political weapon. The Glass and the Bird-Marked Sites remain unexplained.
