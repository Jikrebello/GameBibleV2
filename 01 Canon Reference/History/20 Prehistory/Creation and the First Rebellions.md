# Creation and the First Rebellions

## Atlas narrative

The following account distinguishes established history from the received stories named within it.

## Before kingdoms

There was a world before there were human calendars. Creation, humanity's fall and the rebellion of created powers are not competing accounts of the same event. The Lord is uncreated; everything which later receives the language of godhood is creaturely. The oldest songs often reverse that order because a people encounters a mountain, a hungry image or an ancestral tyrant before it learns to distinguish power from divinity.

The defeated Adversary did not become the secret maker of the world. Some of the rebels deserted him and chose the Yolk. That choice was culpable. Their fusion made the future Menhir and Idols something other than ordinary fallen angels: powers distributed through place, law, appetite and repeated manifestation. Later religion remembers these transformations as marriages, wounds, births, descents and the eating of forbidden food. No one surviving mortal account tells the entire sequence.

## The first divisions

The Menhir's fusion bound them into the world's natural ordering while destroying their capacity to understand moral obligation. They can remember an insult, cherish a dependency and keep an exact bargain without recognizing that fulfilling it may be evil. The Idols retained that knowledge. They knowingly cultivate the human acts through which their Hungers feed.

After fusion, the Faewild Schism broke the historical unity associated with Trium. Its aftermath produced relationships later summarized as Circles, not a tidy succession of divine families. Courts abandoned places; others inherited duties without receiving every former inhabitant's loyalty. A scar in a mountain may therefore outlast the court which claims to have made it. That does not make every strange mountain a battlefield of the Schism.

## What later peoples made of it

Giant houses claimed descent from these older powers. Human priests later arranged incompatible local stories into family trees. Atherian scholars made the stars into relatives, turning conflicts among cities into quarrels among heavenly households. Folk tellers instead remembered the conditions under which a stranger could cross a threshold or survive a hunt. Neither tradition is a complete authorial cosmology.

There are no mortal eyewitness biographies to add to the Faewild Schism. Nor is there a complete first-family epic waiting behind every later tale. The events are real, but human access begins with remains, inherited errors and encounters with powers who have reasons to misrepresent themselves.

## Inheritance

The later world inherits different kinds of danger. A Menhir correspondence may make a harvest fail without punishing a sin. An Idol may answer a cruel rite precisely because the cruelty feeds it. A heavenly judgement follows a commission, not the independent appetite of a luminous creature. A human ruler can misunderstand all three and still command an army.

These distinctions shape [[Age of Chains]], when supernatural ancestry becomes a political weapon. The corresponding otherworld histories are told in [[Menhir Courts through the Ages]], [[Idol Kingdoms and the Hunger Wars]] and [[Heavenly Campaigns and the Human Dead]]. The Glass and Bird-Marked Sites remain unexplained; none is assigned to this sequence as a convenient missing piece.
