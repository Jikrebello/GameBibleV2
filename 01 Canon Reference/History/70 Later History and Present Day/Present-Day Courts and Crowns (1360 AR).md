# Present-Day Courts and Crowns (1360 AR)

The courts and crowns of 1360 AR govern a world divided among successor empires, confessional states, city republics, mobile coalitions and dispersed communal institutions. This survey identifies the principal rulers, the governments through which their authority operates, and the recent history that put each of them where they sit. [[Present-Day Political Board]] explains their immediate conflicts, while [[Named Figures of the Known World]] provides the wider register of living and historical people. The road from 1225 to this board is traced in [[The Age of Held Proofs]].

## Eclessia

Eclessia is split three ways: Sanctum-loyal realms, reformed duchies protected by [[The Korrheim Accord]], and the 345-year schism with Veridun. The Grey Sweat (1232–1247) made the question of who sends the dead the question of the age; the assurance trade built on that fear provoked the [[Freeflame Reformation]] in 1297; the Ashenmen Risings and the princes' Reform Wars ran until the Accord was sworn on the River Weiss in 1343.

### The Primarchal State of Aurelia

**Lord Primarch Alistair III** rules Aurelia directly. The Primarchy is also a temporal crown, which is why the **Crown of Embers** vault, the Sanctum Divinitatis, and the reunification mandate all sit in one man's keeping. Elected in 1354 after Celestin IX, he is a reformist: enforcing the Council of Veridance's abolition of routing assurances against a thriving gray market, curbing noble corruption, and quietly reading Freeflame pamphlets to understand rather than merely burn them. Against him stand **Cardinal Marcellus Vrenne** and the Council of the Eternal Creed; **Grandmaster Henri d'Montesse** of the Order of the Phoenix; and more cautious critics such as **Archbishop Ysabel de Braxford**. The **Inquisition of Saint Malveris** is weaponized by all sides, while the **Order of the Lantern** answers to the Primarch alone. Alistair's private nightmare is the Unburnt Psalter in his own vault.

*How it came to this.* The Primarchy's present weakness was bought in two instalments. In 1281 Medori credit elected Alessior VI, whose treasurer Silvio Quarne turned assurance letters into an industry and sold the unsung ones on. When Alessior died in debt in 1296 the Medori took the Chancery of Sendings' registers as surety, and the Sanctum still has to buy back copies to sing the rites they record. Celestin IX's Council (1337–1352) abolished the sales and saddled the Church with the Great Arrears, the backlog of rites owed for letters sold and never sung; the Lantern was founded to walk the haunted parishes singing it. Alistair inherited the Arrears, the Psalter (unburnt at Aldersbruck in 1305), the unruled Vessing apparition (1352), the Closed House hearing against the Malverine Inquisitors (from 1357), and a mandate the Accord deliberately left unmentioned.

### The Kingdom of Virelia

**King Gregor IV Radomir**, former Grand Marshal, was elected by the council of sabre-right nobles. He follows the old Church, but every royal death becomes a confessional auction: reformed nobles hold perhaps a third of the council, and [[The Korrheim Accord]] gives each magnate's estates their ruler's rite. Virelia is where the Accord is most severely tested.

*How it came to this.* Virelia's grain wealth rests on the bond of the clean village. After the Sweat the magnates, who held the patronage of most parishes, sealed villages whose priests had died and moved the survivors onto the demesne under bond in exchange for a priest and burial in rite. The second serfdom has hardened every generation since, and its runaways walk south to Grenzburg and to Freeflame towns where elders bury anyone. At every election the Patriarchate of Veridun offers to return the Virelian Lamp.

### The Ironvale Dominion

**Grand Minister Aldric Weyn** governs with the Council of Iron. The Dominion is a child of the [[Freeflame Reformation]]: the last duke, **Rainald**, sheltered Doctor Ferro, armed the Ashenmen's heirs, and broke his house through blood, debt, and Church embargo, leaving the guilds to fill the vacuum. Ironvale is now the strongest Freeflame polity. Saint Menos the Chain-Breaker is its patron, congregational hymn-casting its norm, and the Church's regulation of black-powder experiments is read as the old enemy's newest leash.

*How it came to this.* Rainald watched Ansgar burn at Aldersbruck as a boy of fourteen. In 1329 he burned the Sending letters of his duchy in Brenholt market and turned the oath-bound Ashenmen into his army. His last son died at Falkendorf in 1340 and he died in 1341; the guild ministries chartered the Grand Ministry, which swore the Accord in 1343 as keeper of a realm without a prince. The Preacher's Water runs through its country.

### The Rest of the Confessional Map

**Caldria** — **Grand Duke Alric IV von Caldria**, staunchly old Church and pushing military modernization against his zealot old guard. **Veridancia** — **Duke Adrien III de Veridancia**, balancing Church obligation, Lantern seminaries, and merchant money. **Brauvalis** — **Duke Reinhardt von Brauvalis**, old Church but bruised by its conflicts, his settlement one succession from changing. **Korrheim** — the **Council of Heldenheim**, formed from rotating canton representatives and still scarred by the Falkendorf Rebellion. **The Sylvan Marches** — **High Warden Elias Dreymann**, alongside frontier lords such as **Baroness Katarina Wolfsden**, governs territory that is nominally old Church, heavily shaped by Folk inheritance, and difficult to rule. Old-rite Enaidfae and Chapel-Folk communities retain distinct claims. **Grenzburg** is officially Sanctum-loyal, with Folk practice beneath public observance and Freeflame preachers among the winter labor camps.

*How it came to this.* Caldria fought its Reform War campaigns in 1310–1324 and emerged the old Church's sword. Veridancia hosted the Council and its seminaries. Brauvalis holds the Weiss's middle course and the abbey that once claimed the Ossuary Crown. Korrheim's Falkendorf crisis of 1340 forced the Accord, which was drafted at Heldenheim. The Marches took in the Sweat's runaways and the Unsworn congregations the Accord left outside its protection.

**Marker of the age:** in Markish reform country the register runs Ansgar's way; in the Latinate territories the Sanctum holds; Virelia's folk piety remains eastern in texture beneath a Sanctum-aligned crown. See [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/10 Foundations/The Making of the Eclessians.md|The Making of the Eclessians]].

## Thalmyria

### The Imperial Throne

**Emperor Caedros IV Aetherion** rules in Veridun, claiming the Nikandran restoration line: Ash-Blood, past sixty, widowed, silent. His chancellery and the three-way succession are detailed in [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Thalmyric Empire/60 Realms and Peoples/Kingdom of Veridun/The Court of Veridun (1360 AR).md|The Court of Veridun (1360 AR)]].

*How it came to this.* Nikandros I the Restorer (1225–1263) ended the post-Qarath civil wars, refused in 1227 to let the Sanctum try the Great Lamp as the price of Eclessian soldiers, and recovered half the lost districts when Covenant garrisons marched home in the plague. His son Evandros the Sower (1263–1290) resettled them; Evandros's son Stavren the Patient (1290–1329) kept the peace on Sestaran credit and founded the war college at Vatrin. When the Covenant's Iron-mounted night raids reached the chapel-forts, a Vatrin-trained provincial Aetherion named Caedros won at Orinel (1318), led the Crimson Pilgrimage (1319–1322) and took Zehrim (1324–1325). The summer flux of 1327 killed Stavren's sons; Stavren adopted Caedros in 1328 and died the next year. Caedros's claim to the Nikandran line rests on that adoption and on a grandmother who was the Restorer's daughter. Since Vasilia's death in 1357 his silences before the Great Lamp have become a court matter, and since Prince Nikandros's death in 1358 the succession has been open.

### The Patriarchate

**Patriarch Sophron the Unsleeping**, enthroned in 1351, holds the historical title Patriarch of Veridun while residing at Caleran. A contemplative formed in the mountain monasteries, he presides over the **Holy Synod of Veridun**, commonly called the Council of Flame. The Synod is divided among frontier militants, doctrinal administrators, provincial bishops, and contemplatives. Sophron has refused to authenticate a widely circulated vision of *a lamp carried out of a burning archive*, frustrating every succession faction that claims it.

### The Provincial Crowns and Governments

- **Saronika** — **Queen Thaleia IV Saromen** rules the maritime kingdom through its Admiralty, port councils, and an uneasy dependence on Sestaran credit. Its customs were pledged to Sermoran and Medori lenders for the Crimson Pilgrimage.
- **Vlastrava** — **King Dragovan II Vlastar** leads the great fortress and veteran kingdom and supports stronger frontier expenditure without yet endorsing total war. It was the late empress's homeland and is Princess Alexane's power base.
- **Elenara** — **Duchess Mirena Ielen** balances monastery estates, river cities, abandoned land, and rival archive claims.
- **Arkena** — **Governor-General Kyrion Arkenos** governs the military province alongside civic councils that guard older city privileges.
- **Kedrava** — **Prince Radovan Kedrin** speaks for mountain-refuge traditions, peasant militias, and monasteries that distrust both court centralization and reckless reconquest. Kedravan families resettled the interior under Evandros.
- **Zanthir** is not a Thalmyric province. It is an autonomous emirate claimed by Thalmyria and pressured by the Covenant.

### The Court's Standing Wounds

The ransom-shame of Markellos encourages leaders to treat negotiation as humiliation, and since him no emperor signs a Covenant instrument. The Nikandran grievance of 1227 makes cooperation with Eclessia politically costly. Caedros IV's succession, Sestaran debt, depopulated interior districts, and the disputed legal inheritance of occupation-era communities, including the Zehrim folk whom Iqashar still counts as bound, now threaten the peace more immediately than any single foreign army.

## The Covenant (Qahirid Suzerainty)

### The Sublime Threshold at Iqashar

**Sultan-Caliph Marzuq III**, ruling since 1349, is clever, indolent, and holds a throne bought through the Cull of Brothers. The real government includes:

- **Sayyida Nurhan, "the Veiled Ledger"** — the Sultan-Caliph's mother, controlling the household treasury, the marriage market, and through both half the court.
- **The Unnamed Custodian** — chief of the Vault of Names, who surrendered his own true name upon investiture; the only officer the Guard cannot bind and the court cannot blackmail.
- **Guard-Master Temuz** — commander of the Ahdanite Guard, king-broker, and veteran of the last frontier war, loyal to the institution of the Guard rather than any one throne.
- **Princes Kavus and Ardavan** — the two surviving sons. The Cull awaits Marzuq's death; both princes are recruiting, and every appointment is secretly a wager on which brother survives.

*How it came to this.* The Covenant's present shape was made by its witnesses. In the Grey Sweat, which Covenant chroniclers call the Emptying of the Benches (1236–1243), the witnesses who released the names of the dead and held garrison oaths died faster than the bound; frontier forts opened and Thalmyria walked in. When Suhrab the Lawbinder's most gifted son swore wavering soldiers to himself to hold them, Guard officers testified to it, and Suhrab lodged the prince's name in the Deep Vault, where he died within the year. Suhrab's last book (1243) let a sealed register stand where no witness survived and named the Guard's officer bench a competent witness for the Threshold's own instruments; every accession since has been witnessed by it. In 1249 a lodged prince whose register page had been lost in the plague walked free under the law and raised the Basin's military households; after his death in 1252 the Cull turned from the lodge to the knife and the blinding needle. Tahmur (1243–1268) and Marzuq I (1268–1301) paid the Black Wind; Marzuq II (1301–1349) hired the Iron Horde and lost Zehrim. Temuz's bench witnessed Marzuq III in 1349 while his brothers died. The "last frontier war" is the war of Orinel and Zehrim (1318–1325); no treaty ended it.

### The Vassal Thrones

- **Al-Qahur** — **Caliph Uwais the Custodian-of-the-Wells** holds spiritual precedence in the holy city and oasis of the 4,444. **Sword-Regent Nadir ibn Haraz** governs through competing military households. Uwais's acknowledgment legitimizes Marzuq; his silence would wound both Marzuq and Nadir. The Basin's canal councils, strengthened when the Sweat left them holding the water survivors needed, contest the military households' expired leases.
- **Qazmarid** — **Caliph-Wasi Zahran of the Sealed Blood** rules an independent counter-caliphate across the western strait. He denies Qahirid universal rule, claims descent from Ruqaiya bint Mujalib, and keeps what he says is the Counter-Instrument beneath his throne. See [[The Disputed Fourth Seal]].
- **Zanthir** — **Emir Badis al-Zanthiri**, ruling from Qabir, holds the autonomous remnant of the Banu Mizan inheritance. He pays tribute toward Iqashar and Veridun in alternating seasons, protects the Velian Church and Jirahar quarters, and plays every court against the expectation that his realm will be the next war's first battlefield.
- **Mazrakan** — the **Council of Nine Measures** governs the northern chartered market under an imperial Gate-Prefect. Its commercial peace converts Tarkhan pressure into customs, patronage, and licensed danger without ending the raids beyond its wells.

## The Seven Sestaran Seals

Sestara has no common court. **Duke Gaius Medori** rules Fiorenzia beside its senate, with **Lord Matteo Medori** directing investments and Factors Gianni and Livia active in Grenzburg. **Doge Terenzo Vescari** balances Sermora's Admiralty and merchant houses. **Duke Marcus Soran** and **General Aurelia Soran** command Aversia. Vorthara has no ruler; **Pass-Keeper Salvi Corren** holds only its external seal. **First Speaker Eliana Corvesi** heads Castara's Merchant Princes. **Harbor Rector Sabina Valeris** governs Orsena. **First Burgess Tomas Bellafor** presides over Vardessa's guild benches.

The Medori carry hidden Ahl-e-Ahd lineage and contract technique; this remains author-only and does not make Fiorenzia a Covenant proxy. The seven authorities cooperate automatically only under [[The Seven-Seal Defensive Muster]]. Fiorenzia still dreams uneasily of Frate Severin.

*How it came to this.* The Medori bought a seat in Fiorenzia's Golden Book in 1238, when the Sweat had emptied a third of it, cleared the estates of the plague dead, bought Alessior VI's Primarchy in 1281 and took the Sanctum's assurance registers as surety in 1296. A Sermoran pamphlet calling their vault "the Bank of the Dead" brought Frate Severin to power in 1346; the family returned in 1349 and hanged him. Castara became the seventh seal in 1332, four years after the *Naris's Hand* crossed the Boiling Sea on a Breath day, and bought the Ash Road Peace in 1334.

## The Tarkhan Lands (the Four Hordes)

Fifty-seven years after the Black Wind's death, his great-grandchildren rule four regional coalitions. **Khan Batuqai** of the White Horde directs the principal raiding and slaving threat against the Nine Wells. **Khan Tegrul** of the Iron Horde embeds contracted horse-and-camel companies in Iqashar's frontier institutions. **Khatun-regent Sorqai** of the Moon Horde keeps the Nine-Mane Standard and the prohibition against searching for Qarayel's grave. **Khan Chilgir** of the Ash Horde sells the annual road peace to Castara while struggling with foreign debt. No full kurultai can be called without Sorqai's Standard; her exceptional regency does not establish unrestricted female khanship. See [[Four Hordes of the Partition]].

*How it came to this.* Qarayel's power rested on horses descended from a black colt a captive Velian farrier blessed in 1249, which stood before the Covenant's bound wardens. The Partition of the Herds in 1304 divided those horses by bloodline: White took the numerous herds, Iron the carefully bred lines the Covenant hires because they stand beside its bound things, Moon the Standard braided from the nine founding stallions' manes, Ash the poorest lines and the eastern road. The grace is thinning again in every Horde's foals.

## The Jirahar

**High Keeper Zerahiah**, first among the Keepers of the Path at [[Migdal Qesheth]], embodies post-Meshalem orthodoxy: ferocious scrutiny of prophecy, guarded coordination between independent courts, and refusal to let any host ruler purchase gate access. His standing ruling presumes every claimant to the Mesakha false until the Keepers rule otherwise, and the Keepers have never ruled otherwise. He is first in dignity, not king of the towers.

*How it came to this.* Meshalem's return movement of 1288 sold half the tower towns' goods on roads the Black Wind had emptied of Covenant garrisons; his conversion under Marzuq I in 1291 ruined them, and Sestaran creditors bought cheap.
