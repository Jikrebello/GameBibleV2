# The Grey Sweat

From roughly 1232 to 1247 AR, recurring waves of a sweating fever killed about a third of eastern Eclessia and a fifth of its western territories. No surviving authority agrees on where it began. The disease itself has passed; the society it made has not.

## Demographic Consequences

Labor scarcity broke old obligations in opposite directions. Across much of Eclessia, surviving tenants demanded wages, fixed rents, inheritance rights, and freedom of movement. Towns grew, guild labor strengthened, and abandoned holdings were consolidated or offered on favorable terms.

In Virelia, the sabre-right nobility answered scarcity by binding survivors more tightly to the demesne estates. The resulting second serfdom made Virelia a dependable grain exporter and created a permanent population of runaways moving toward river towns, the southern marches, and Grenzburg's seasonal funnel.

## Religious Consequences

The plague produced the Ash-Walker processions: penitents who traveled from town to town attempting to turn the fever through public suffering. When the mortality ended, their spiritual descendants split. Some sought silence and exhaustion in teachings that later fed [[The Apathetics]]. Others pursued increasingly desperate apparitions and relics, giving the [[The Weeping Courts|Weeping Courts]] a vast field of grief to cultivate.

The false apparition at Bruckmoor was exposed in 1243 AR after feeding on that grief for decades. Its destruction became the founding trauma of Eclessia's modern relic courts.

## Legal and Political Consequences

Parish registers break across the plague years. Land titles, marriages, inheritances, and clerical appointments from the gaps remain disputed. Noble houses, monasteries, guilds, and villages still litigate property created by deaths no surviving witness recorded.

The plague also weakened trust in routing assurances and institutional promises that had failed visibly during mass death. That wound later gave the [[Freeflame Reformation]] an audience.

## Present-Day Legacy

Eclessian families still speak of events as before or after the Sweat. Wage labor, bound Virelian estates, relic-court procedure, migration into the southern frontier, and several Left Hand networks all carry its inheritance.

## The Torque: The Years the Ferry Queued

On Earth a plague is biology; here it was a **soul-logistics catastrophe**. The Vestibule's intake overwhelmed; burial rites collapsing faster than bodies could be blessed; the **Undertow's harvest of the century** (their oldest standing routes date from the Sweat, bought in bulk from the unburied). Church quarantine doctrine was written about **rite-integrity, not contagion**: a sealed village with a living priest was "clean"; a healthy village with a dead one was not. And the Ash-Walkers were not merely hysterics — where they walked, the records show, the thin places closed. Nobody has ever wanted to study why. (Per [[The Divergence Pass — Torque Register]].)
