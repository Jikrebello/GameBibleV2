# The Korrheim Accord

The **Korrheim Accord** is the confessional peace of 1343 AR that ended open war between the Sanctum and the recognised Freeflame princes after the [[Freeflame Reformation]]. It was drafted at Heldenheim, the council seat of the Korrheim cantons, and sworn with the signatories' hands in the River Weiss on Ironvale's reach, the water into which Ansgar of Brenholt's ashes had been shovelled. It settled who may set a territory's public rite and left the theology that caused the war exactly where it was. It has held for seventeen years because nobody who swore it dares break an oath made in that river.

Its governing formula was sworn in the plain speech of the river country: **every roof under its lord's lamp**. Each recognised ruler sets the public confession of his territory, controls its church property and guarantees limited passage to displaced clergy and households. Sanctum canonists, copying the Accord into their law-books in the court Latinate, rendered the formula more coldly as *whose the realm, his the rite*, and that is the form lawyers now quote.

## The road to Heldenheim

By 1340 Eclessia had been at war over the Reformation for thirty-five years: first the Ashenmen Risings, then the princes' war that followed Duke Rainald's burning of the Sending House letters in 1329. Caldria and Aurelia had fought Freeflame towns and merchants; Ironvale had taken Church estates up and down the Weiss. In 1338 a forged no-quarter order turned a prisoner exchange between Freeflame and old-Church companies into a massacre. In 1340 the Freeflame-majority canton of Falkendorf resisted an old-Church council settlement, and Korrheim came close to civil war. Rainald's last son died in that fighting, and Rainald himself died in 1341 without an heir, leaving Ironvale to its guild ministries.

Everyone was tired and nobody trusted anyone's oath. The negotiators at Heldenheim needed a guarantee stronger than relics, which each side accused the other of faking, and stronger than seals, which the assurance trade had taught Eclessia to despise. They found it in the Weiss. Since the Risings, oaths sworn on the Preacher's Water had held with a consistency nobody could explain. The Sanctum's relic court tested the water in 1342; the result was declared inconclusive and the file sealed, and the old-Church delegates took that as reason enough to fear it. In the spring of 1343 they waded in beside reformed princes, canton councillors and the Grand Ministers of Ironvale, and swore.

## What it settled

- Old-Church and recognised reformed governments may coexist within Eclessia.
- A ruler may establish the territory's public rite and appoint its approved clergy.
- Church property follows the recognised territorial settlement rather than automatic Sanctum claim.
- Armed conversion across a signatory border is a breach of the peace.
- Each ruler's rite buries that ruler's dead, and each cemetery follows its territory.

The "ruler" turned out to be a broad word. Ironvale's Grand Ministry signed as keeper of a realm without a prince, and the Korrheim cantons signed one by one, so the formula covers ministries, cantons and chapters as well as dukes.

## What it refused to settle

- Individual freedom of conscience.
- Mixed marriages and children raised across two rites, and where their dead may lie.
- Radical Freeflame congregations without princely sponsorship.
- The status of itinerant preachers, exiles and clandestine parishes.
- Eclessia's reunification mandate towards Thalmyria.
- Whether any ruler may define doctrine merely by owning land.

## The Unsworn

The radical congregations refused to put their hands in the water. An oath on a heretic's ashes, their elders said, made the river a relic and the Accord an idol. They are called the Unsworn, and the Accord protects none of them. Princes of both confessions hunt their preachers, and both churches cite them as proof of what the other side becomes.

## The silence on reunion

The Accord never mentions the reunification mandate, the Sanctum's standing duty to restore one Church and one imperial crown with Thalmyria. Sanctum legates at Heldenheim would not put the mandate into an oath sworn on water that holds oaths. Some feared being bound to a reunion no one could achieve; others feared being bound to give it up. The silence was deliberate, and Veridun's chroniclers have enjoyed it ever since.

## Present-day effects

Every succession is a possible religious crisis. Virelia's elective crown makes each royal death a confessional auction, and its serfs follow their lords' rite. Border towns keep parallel registers and feast calendars, and quarrel over cemeteries. Nobles use the settlement to acquire land, while sincere clergy use its protections to keep another generation from war. Every few years someone proposes amending the Accord, and nobody will go back into the river to do it.

Grenzburg remains formally within the old-Church settlement, but frontier villages, migrant labourers, Folk converts and Freeflame influences make actual practice less tidy than the ducal register suggests.
