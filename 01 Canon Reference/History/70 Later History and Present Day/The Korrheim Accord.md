# The Korrheim Accord

Signed in 1343 AR after decades of reform war, the Korrheim Accord ended open confessional campaigning between the Sanctum and recognized reformist princes without resolving the theology that caused it.

Its governing formula is simple: **whose the realm, his the rite**. Each recognized ruler determines the public confession of the territory, controls its church property, and guarantees limited passage to displaced clergy and households.

## What It Settled

- Old-Church and recognized reformed governments may coexist within Eclessia.
- A ruler may establish the territory's public rite and appoint its approved clergy.
- Church property follows the recognized territorial settlement rather than automatic Sanctum claim.
- Armed conversion across a signatory border is breach of the peace.

## What It Refused to Settle

- Individual freedom of conscience.
- Mixed marriages and children raised across two rites.
- Radical Freeflame congregations without princely sponsorship.
- The status of itinerant preachers, exiles, and clandestine parishes.
- Eclessia's reunification mandate toward Thalmyria.
- Whether any ruler may define doctrine merely by owning land.

## Present-Day Effects

The Accord turns every succession into a possible religious crisis. Virelia's elective crown makes each royal death a confessional auction. Border towns keep parallel registers and feast calendars. Nobles use religious settlement to acquire land, while sincere clergy use its protections to prevent another generation of war.

Grenzburg remains formally within the old-Church settlement, but frontier villages, migrant laborers, Folk converts, and Freeflame influences make actual practice less tidy than the ducal register suggests.
