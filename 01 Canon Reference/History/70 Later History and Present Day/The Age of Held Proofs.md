# The Age of Held Proofs

The Age of Held Proofs runs from the Reformation's beginning in 1297 to the present year, 1360 AR. In it, institutions which once argued chiefly from ancestry, office or conquest now also face evidence capable of testing their claims: authenticated relics, conditional miracles, sealed instruments, disputed successions and records preserved by rival custodians. Such evidence rarely supplies a simple verdict. A successful test may vindicate one claim while exposing another abuse, so rulers and churches increasingly govern not only territory but access to proof.

The age remains unfinished. The settlements which ended one phase of religious war have not reunited the churches, resolved the great successions or determined how distant discoveries will alter the known world.

## Reform after catastrophe

The Reformation began in 1297 when Ansgar of Brenholt challenged the assurance trade. Its context included real abuses, fiscal need, fear for the dead and a Church which had developed increasingly elaborate claims about access to sacred aid. Doctor Lucian Ferro gave the movement an enduring ecclesiology; Duke Rainald's protection made that thought politically consequential.

Ansgar's burning in 1305 did not end the dispute. The Ashenmen Risings of 1306–1329 exposed grievances which neither doctrine nor princely protection fully controlled. Reform could inspire costly faithfulness and permit new coercion.

## The settlement that settles too little

The Council of Veridance, 1337–1352, abolished assurance sales and codified the five tests. The Order of the Lantern arose within that correction. The Korrheim Accord of 1343 protected particular territorial confessions while excluding unaffiliated radicals and leaving the reunification mandate unresolved.

The resulting world is full of people for whom a settlement is real but insufficient. A family can be protected by its ruler's rite and unwelcome across the next bridge. A genuine spring can heal without settling the claims of every shrine. An object can survive a test while its custodians conceal a crime.

## Present inheritances

Caedros IV's frontier reputation, campaigns and crown belong to recent history. Vasilia's death in 1357 and Prince Nikandros's death in 1358 intensify the current succession problem without deciding it. Marzuq III's Covenant is layered, guard-brokered and challenged by the Qazmarid counter-caliphate. Sestara finances rivals while Castara's sea routes broaden the world its merchants imagine they understand.

The Tarkhan partitions after Qarayel's death in 1303 produced rival successor hordes. Castara's annual Ash Road payment began in 1334. Its expeditions make distant exploitation and discovery possible.

## Unfinished local histories

The Company with No Charter has rumours and living participants. The Nameless Castellan's dispute has evidence and claimants. The two workhouse tags place families before a real question in 1360 that no investigation has yet decided.
