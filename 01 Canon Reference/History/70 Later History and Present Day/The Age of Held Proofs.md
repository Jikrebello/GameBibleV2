# The Age of Held Proofs

The **Age of Held Proofs** runs from Ansgar of Brenholt's *Hundred Grievances* in 1297 to the present year, 1360 AR. Its name comes from a habit every great power learned in it. In a world where sainthood can be tested, kindling witnessed and contracts examined, a decisive proof can vindicate a church or destroy it, and nobody can be sure which in advance. So the proofs are held: a psalter in a lead vault, a vision no synod will authenticate, an apparition the Sanctum has not ruled on for eight years. Rulers and churches govern territory, and they also govern access to evidence.

The age opened with a reformer who asked to be tested by fire and was burned, and with a nomad empire whose horses were losing their grace. It closes on an emperor who kneels in silence before a lamp and a court that watches the flame instead of asking him what it means.

## How proof became dangerous

The lesson was a century in the learning.
- **The Refusal of the Lamp (1227).** When Nikandros I of Thalmyria asked the Sanctum for soldiers, the price was that the Great Lamp of Veridun be carried east and tried by the Sanctum's relic court. He refused: *a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned.* Thalmyric liturgy still has a line most worshippers take to mean *the East sold us for a doctrine.*
- **Bruckmoor (1243).** The relic courts tested the weeping Lady and destroyed her shrine, rightly; a whole region's piety went with it.
- **The Ossuary Crown (1263).** They tested the crown that spoke with the voices of the plague dead and ruled that *the dead do not testify through things*, and the people who had heard their mothers in it never forgave them.
- **Aldersbruck (1305).** Ansgar challenged the Sanctum to prove him wrong with Fire. They burned him, and his psalter came out of the ashes unburnt.
- **Sarnwell (1311).** A spring that rose where the Mother appeared to a crippled washerwoman has passed every test since, and showed that a test could also win.
- **The Council of Veridance (1337–1352)** wrote the five tests down, and closed in the year of Vessing.

By 1360 the great powers treat a test the way a mason treats a keystone: they may admire it, but they do not pull it out to see what happens.

## Reform after catastrophe

The [[Freeflame Reformation]] grew out of the [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|Grey Sweat]]. After the plague the Sanctum sold sealed letters promising that Sending Houses would sing rites for named dead. Under Alessior VI and his treasurer Silvio Quarne the letters were sold far faster than they could be sung. Ansgar counted, in his own parish, more than ten letters for every rite and found the unsung dead still walking. His burning in 1305 began the Ashenmen Risings, twenty-three years of wagon-fort war fought by bands whose oaths, sworn in the River Weiss where his ashes lay, never broke. Duke Rainald of Ironvale ended the Risings in 1329 by burning the Sending letters of his duchy, and turned the bands into an army. Doctor Lucian Ferro, who had been the notary at Ansgar's trial, gave the movement its order: *the Fire keeps no landlord.*

The old Church answered with the Council of Veridance under Celestin IX. It abolished assurance sales, ordered the Great Arrears sung for every unsung letter, and founded the Order of the Lantern under Father Ivo the Lamed to sing them. The [[The Korrheim Accord|Korrheim Accord]] of 1343, sworn on the same river as the Ashenmen's oath, let each ruler fix his realm's rite and left out everyone who would not swear.

## The steppe after the Black Wind

[[Qarayel and the Black Wind|Qarayel]] died in 1303, undefeated, and was buried where no one has found him. His power had rested on horses descended from a black colt that a captive Velian farrier blessed in 1249; they stood before the Covenant's bound well-wardens as the First Catch horses once had. By 1300 the grace was thinning, Iqashar's wardens held again, and the escort payments stopped. In 1304 his descendants divided the herds by bloodline, and the White, Iron, Moon and Ash Hordes took their characters from what each inherited. The Iron Horde's carefully bred lines were the only horses on the frontier that would stand beside the Covenant's own bound things, and from 1304 Iqashar hired them.

## The frontier and the adopted emperor

Iron-mounted companies riding with devil-mantled escorts began raiding Thalmyric chapel-forts on the Velian frontier by night. At **Chapel-Fort Orinel** in 1318 the seven signal braziers failed one by one during a night assault, and a provincial commander of House Aetherion named Caedros led the dawn counterattack after the last went out. Songs say the bound riders smothered the fires and a great bird of fire rose from the smoke; a Saronikan quartermaster's ledger found a generation later records the fort's oil bought from a contractor who cut it with water. Caedros's **Crimson Pilgrimage** (1319–1322) reopened roads through the Velian districts and returned stolen parish registers, and his siege of **Zehrim** (1324–1325) ended with the garrison spared and the fort's oath-ledgers burned on its altar. The Covenant's Court of Breaches ruled in 1326 that the people those ledgers had bound remained bound in the registers at Iqashar. Thalmyria calls them freed.

In 1327 the summer flux killed both sons of the old emperor Stavren the Patient within a month. In 1328 Stavren adopted the hero of Zehrim, over the objections of the Caleran houses, and died the next year. Caedros IV was crowned at Virelos. He codified frontier amnesties, strengthened provincial audits and has not drawn his sword Ashreaver since. No treaty ended the war: Thalmyric emperors will not put their names into a Covenant instrument, and the Covenant makes no peace without one. The frontier has been an armed peace ever since, raided by Tarkhan-mounted and Velian companies, while Zanthir between the two empires pays tribute to each in alternating seasons.

## The Covenant's witnesses

The Sweat had left the Covenant with a court that could not act without witnesses and a Guard that supplied them. Since 1243 the officer bench of the Ahdanite Guard has witnessed every accession at Iqashar, and since 1252, when a lodged prince whose register page had been lost in the plague walked free under the law and raised the Basin, the Cull of Brothers has used the knife and the blinding needle rather than the lodging of a rival's name in the Vault. Marzuq II ruled for nearly half a century with Iron Horde cavalry and Guard counsel. When he died in 1349 the Guard bench witnessed Marzuq III, and his brothers were killed, blinded or lodged.

## The sea opens

The grass-tolls of the Black Wind had pushed Sestaran trade to the sea, and the minor port of Castara on the Boiling Sea grew with it. In 1328 the Castaran salt-hauler *Naris's Hand*, under the pilot Ottavia Sele, was caught at sea on one of the rare days when the hurricane belt falls flat calm, and crossed to the Shattered Isles. She came home with green crystal, Expanse hardwood and a chart-case of old Viscaran surveys from a wreck, and with a log that disagrees with the calendar. The University of Castara rebuilt the Viscaran storm-keel from the wreck's lines, and by the early 1330s ships crossed in the hurricane season's windows. Castara joined the Sestaran Concord as its seventh seal in 1332. When its surveyors staked wells across Ash Horde pasture for an inland road in 1333, the Ash banners burned the camps, and in 1334 Castara bought the first [[Castaran Long Road|Ash Road Peace]].

## Fiorenzia's fire

The Medori had held the Chancery of Sendings' registers since 1296, as surety for Alessior VI's debts. In 1345 a Sermoran pamphlet printed extracts naming Fiorenzian dead whose Sendings had never been sung and called the family vault **the Bank of the Dead**. The friar Frate Severin preached that the Medori held the city's dead in pawn and that the carnival masks of the merchant houses had become doors for the Hungers. In 1346 the city expelled the Medori and burned its masks, mirrors, paintings and gowns in the cathedral square, the **Pyre of Masks**; witnesses still argue about what they saw standing in the fire. A Medori-restored government hanged and burned Severin in 1349. Nicanor Vetti's banned *Mirror for Sovereigns*, written in exile around 1340, is on every ruler's private shelf.

## Vessing and the Closed House

In 1352, the year the Council of Veridance closed, three shepherd children at **Vessing**, a hill parish of the southern marches, reported a figure of banked fire standing in a sloe-field. Their testimonies cannot be reconciled. One child said the figure told them the Fire dwells where it is welcomed, which is Veridun's teaching. One said it is carried by the hands of the Church, which is the Sanctum's. The third said the figure spoke no word, wept and pointed towards the churchyard, and reformed preachers read that as *sing for them*. Captured papers of the Weeping Courts claim the apparition as their work. The Sanctum has not ruled.

From 1357 a hearing has examined a prison-house of the Inquisitors of Saint Malveris, where laundresses, guards, clerks, a physician and gravediggers kept evidence of torture, seizure and execution on fabricated evidence. Witnesses say Saint Malveris, or something using the name, appeared in the sealed house, named the victims and refused to give the order a sign that it alone was right. The Inquisitors have answered to the Order of the Lantern since the Council; the hearing could reform them or split them. See [[Saints and Sinners of the Chronicles]].

## The last years

Empress Vasilia of Vlastrava, wife of Caedros IV for forty years, died in 1357. After her fortieth-day memorial the emperor began keeping silences of a day or more before the Great Lamp of Veridun, and courtiers report that the flame stands still while he kneels. In 1358 his elder son Prince Nikandros died in a skirmish with Iron-mounted raiders in the Velian marches. Princess Alexane, Prince Sophios and the adoption of a proven commander, the road by which Caedros himself came to the throne, now divide the court. Lord Primarch Alistair III, elected in 1354, holds the Crown of Embers, the Unburnt Psalter and the Vessing file in one set of vaults. Marzuq III's sons keep rival households. The Moon Horde's Sorqai is old. In every capital the most dangerous object is a piece of evidence nobody wants opened.

## Unfinished local histories

The Company with No Charter has rumours and living participants. The Nameless Castellan's dispute has evidence and claimants. The two workhouse tags place families before a real question in 1360 that no investigation has yet decided, and brokers holding dead paper press claims of the same kind in other towns.
