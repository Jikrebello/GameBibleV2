# High Noon Provinces and the Qarath Aftermath

This article follows the provinces and realms of the High Noon from 1104, when the Crown of Embers entered the Sanctum's custody, to 1231, the year before the Grey Sweat. It describes how ordinary districts lived under district levies, mine customs, Sestaran credit and Covenant deeds, and what Qarath's Well and the civil wars that followed did to them. The wars and wonders of the period are told in [[High Noon and the Qarath Disaster]].

## A world without one crown

The lands called Eclessia were governed by competing kings, dukes, cantons and a Primarch, with the empire's crown locked in a vault at Aurelium. Thalmyria kept a different imperial claim and a crown that was worn, though in 1167 its wearer was chosen by a widow's lamp and an army's acclamation, which no law allowed. Sestaran houses lent to both and to the Covenant. The noon was bright and crowded, and few stood in all of its light.

Its strength showed in repaired walls, new fairs and terraces cut back out of the thorn. So did its cost. The ruler who defended a farm against raiders took its sons for road-work and its grain for the garrison, and every year the frontier held made the burden look more permanent.

## Life in a district

A Thalmyric **military district** was a valley or a run of terraces resettled after the Two Winters, divided into family holdings around a walled granary, a chapel and a threshing floor where the men drilled on feast days. A holding owed a lance in war and a lamp: every year at the Rekindling the family lit its door-lamp from the relay and hung it by the door, and the lamp was inherited with the land. Widows held for their sons; daughters inherited the lamp when there were no sons, and married men who would ride for it. The district towns were raw, loud and pious, full of young families and stonemasons.

The settlers were not the valleys' only claimants. Occupation-born families who had kept terraces and channels working through the years of Covenant rule found an imperial grant counted for more than their years of maintenance. Some became district holders by marriage; others moved north to the Velian belt and the Covenant's courts, carrying their grievance and their deeds.

The district families saved the frontier in the War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139), when their door-lamps kindled as they held their houses against the mercy towers. Their reward was a generation of prosperity and then two generations of levies. After the Unanswered Funeral of 1167 the districts made their own commander emperor, and Andrion the Widow's Emperor counted them for his levies more thoroughly than any emperor before him. By 1210 a district holding owed sons for the frontier, grain for the garrisons, carts for the roads and labour for the forts, and many holdings were held by widows. Kolonna keeps the Widow's Lamp in its church, and on the anniversary of the funeral the district families carry their door-lamps to it.

## The Thalmyric provinces

**Elenara**, the river province of vineyards and monasteries, was rich in grain, wine and records. Its prince's attempt to make himself a king through a census that seized dependants ended at the threshing floor of Kremna in 1171–1173, and Elenara has been a duchy since. Its monasteries kept the census schedules that survived the fire, and their archives still settle and unsettle titles.

**Vlastrava**, the fortress and veteran kingdom of the northern frontier, supplied the district captains and the hardest soldiers. In the civil wars its king, Stanimir, refused to murder his enemy's surrendered children and his own brother killed the mediator who had offered them. Vlastrava's word became the one men trusted, and it backed Nikandros.

**Kedrava** kept the mountain refuges, militia valleys and the monasteries that had sheltered the rebellion after the Fall. Its monasteries kept a rule from the occupation years that forbade drinking from any well sworn under a Covenant instrument. Their contingents obeyed it at Qarath's Well and came home.

**Saronika** carried district grain and imperial soldiers in its ships, and its admiralty borrowed from Sestaran houses to build them. In the civil wars it fought for the side that would pay its debts.

The **Velian belt** beyond the frontier was the Covenant's southern march: Velian cities under amirs and Covenant deeds, Lamp congregations protected and taxed, Southern House families who had left the reconquered heartland with their deeds, and towns of Jirahar and mixed households. The Ransomed Standard worked its roads recovering bodies and pledged prisoners; around 1201 a Velian amir's coalition besieged a district town until the Woman Who Returned the Keys broke it.

## Work beneath prosperity

Aversian ore became ploughshares, lance-heads and credit in towns that had never seen a shaft, and a stoppage in the high mines meant hunger in the valleys below. Shield-House officials used that hunger against strikers. At the mine remembered through the Three Knocks they cut the pale rib for quota, sent prisoners and strikers into the deadliest workings and executed the interpreters who accused them. The compact belonged to that one mine and the grey folk of its seam. The surface families who buried its dead went on needing ore, and went on sending their sons up the same mountain.

In the Heldenberg silver mines the slaves won their own valleys in 1116–1124 and swore the Oath of the Shafts. Korrheim's canton towns were mining camps first: shaft-heads, smelting sheds, wash-floors on the streams, and assemblies held at the pithead where every free miner had a voice. They read their dead each year the morning after their liberation feast.

In Sermora the harbour chain was the city's wall. It was closed against famine ships in 1102 and lowered by a foreign-born chain-tender, whom the city hanged; when the city was besieged in 1166 his figure was seen above the harbour wall. Sermora's harbour law since then requires the chain to be lowered for one foreign or refugee vessel each feast, and the Admiralty's lawyers have spent two centuries narrowing what counts.

## The eastern realms under the vault

**Aurelia** passed from Aldwin's line to the Primarchs when the last of that line died in 1166. Its estates were taken into the same trust as the Crown of Embers, and its bishops became its magistrates.

**Virelia** had elected its kings in the Council of Sabres since 1103, when the Radomir heirs died in the Two Winters. Its villages kept icon-corners and standing vigils under Sanctum bishops, its hearth-houses had been purged, and at every election Veridun's envoys offered to send the Virelian Lamp home if the candidate of the old Vesari east won.

**Caldria** was a duchy of fortress-orders and frontier captains facing the Thalmyric watershed. In 1217, while Thalmyria tore itself apart after Qarath, its captains crossed the watershed and took three rite-changing valleys.

**Brauvalis** became abbey-and-guild country in the Two Winters, when its monasteries fed their neighbours and took their land. **Veridancia** bought Sestaran grain in the same famine and has owed Sestaran houses money ever since; its towns kept the ducal compact of the 1050s and their schools. Brauvalis, Caldria and Veridancia were the duchies whose children walked west in the Walking of the Small around 1180.

**Ironvale's** ducal house ruled the northern mines and forges, and the **Sylvan Marches** held a frontier that stopped at Wolfsmere, with old-rite clans recovering from the Great Southern Purge of 1158–1163 and Chapel-Folk communities sheltering under the Bent Oak precedent.

## Qarath and the transferred districts

In 1212 the Covenant offered the district families on the old Southern House lands confirmation of their farms under Covenant deed, with no levy owed. Families who had given two generations of sons to Andrion's levies listened. Some registered at the Covenant's frontier bench beside Qarath's Well; their neighbours called them collaborators, and some burned their barns. Markellos marched in 1214 to stop it, and lost his army at the sworn well.

Suhrab's courtesy to his captive and the exaction of the ransom were separate acts. Markellos was entertained like a guest of honour; the districts were taken all the same. Blinding the returned emperor was a third act, done to void the ransom by a borrowed doctrine, and it did not defend a single family who had already borne the defeat. Covenant garrisons occupied the transferred districts in 1215.

In the transferred districts the old imperial tax-collector, the new Covenant owner and recruiters for three Thalmyric factions could knock on the same door in one season. A family that fled lost its farm; a family that stayed was called collaborator. Those who had registered in 1212 kept their land under Covenant deed; those who had refused lost it or walked south into the shrunken empire. Repair crews kept the walls and channels working while the chronicles argued about the crown. In one lesser city a poor wall-worker stopped a pumping order that would have burst the cisterns under a crowded district; officials took the credit and her wages, and engineers still cut her notch before a pressure test.

Civil wars lasted until 1225. Nikandros stabilized a diminished inheritance and sought aid from Eclessia. In 1227 the Sanctum named its price: the Great Lamp of Veridun, tried by the Sanctum's relic court, and with it Veridun's submission in the Kindling dispute. His refusal and partial recovery left the unequal provincial losses where they lay.

## Before the Grey Sweat

By 1231 some districts had resumed trade while others stood empty, disputed or pledged against the civil war's debts. Three watershed valleys flew Caldrian banners. Eclessia's realms were crowded, their younger sons hungry for land and their rents rising. When the Grey Sweat came it found Thalmyric villagers who would sing over their own dead and Eclessian villagers who waited for a priest, and that difference decided which villages were haunted.
