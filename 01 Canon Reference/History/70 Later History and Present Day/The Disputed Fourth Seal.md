# The Disputed Fourth Seal

The Disputed Fourth Seal is the succession crisis that divided the Covenant after the death of the third Rightly-Sealed Caliph. Rival instruments named General Uthar ibn Qays and Husan ibn Raiq, Mujalib's grandson, as the lawful heir. Uthar secured the caliphate, while Husan and his household were killed after accepting safe conduct to arbitration. Their deaths at Bir Qassim gave a dynastic dispute its enduring theological and communal force.

## The Two Instruments

Mujalib left succession seals bearing his witness-mark. After the third Rightly-Sealed Caliph died in 597 AR, two instruments appeared:

- The scribal college at Al-Qahur produced the **Fourth Seal**, naming General Uthar ibn Qays and bearing the college's attestation.
- Ruqaiya bint Mujalib's household produced the **Counter-Instrument**, naming her son Husan ibn Raiq and bearing what they held to be Mujalib's true final witness-mark.

The college condemned the Counter-Instrument. Ruqaiya's party condemned the Fourth Seal. Uthar took the caliphate; Husan took the desert.

## Bir Qassim, 603 AR

Husan and seventy-two members of his household accepted safe conduct to arbitration. Flood damage forced their escort from the named road. A travelling court ruled the protection void because the route was an essential term. Husan's party was killed at the dry wells of Bir Qassim.

The annual **Mourning of the Wells** recites the safe-conduct clause by clause, answering each with: *And yet the well was dry.*

## The Seal on the Frontier

In 597 the Covenant's armies were halfway through the conquest of the Atherian north, and every breach-court that voided a town's charter did so in the caliph's name. From then on the towns had an answer. Defenders shouted it from their walls and lawyers wrote it into their pleadings: *your court sits under a seal nobody may open.* Sealed-Blood jurists refused to sit in Uthar's name. In 598 Uthar dismissed his great general Qatada Sayf-al-Ahd, and the field advance stopped. Before the march town of Tamsar in 611 a Sealed-Blood jurist climbed onto a wagon and told the Covenant army that its court sat under a seal nobody dared open. Neither army gave battle, and the peace of 612 followed.

The Velian cities were taken under Uthar's seal, and their pre-conquest charters still wait in fireproof chests. If the Fourth Seal were ever proved forged, every judgement made in Uthar's name would be open to challenge, the Velian ones among them. Qahirid jurists count that among the reasons the Seal stays shut.

## The Divide

[[The Witnessed Seal]] teaches that authority continues through public attestation and the community's jurists. [[The Sealed Blood]] teaches that succession continued through Ruqaiya and the Veiled Wasiyin.

Both instruments reportedly survive. The Fourth Seal rests in the Vault of Names at Al-Qahur, in the Deep Vault, which since 1059 has been a separate house from the Living Register at Iqashar; Qazmarid claims the Counter-Instrument lies beneath the Caliph-Wasi's throne. Opening either for forensic examination would void the succession it might prove. The dispute is therefore decidable in principle and undecidable in practice.

## Present Day

Iqashar and Al-Qahur are Witnessed-Seal. Qazmarid is Sealed-Blood. Zanthir is Witnessed-majority with protected Sealed-Blood communities. Every Qahirid succession, especially Marzuq III's expected Cull of Brothers, reopens the wound.
