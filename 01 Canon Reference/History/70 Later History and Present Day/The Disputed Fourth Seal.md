# The Disputed Fourth Seal

The Covenant's defining internal division began as a succession dispute from which centuries of theology grew.

## The Two Instruments

Mujalib left succession seals bearing his witness-mark. After the third Rightly-Sealed Caliph died in 597 AR, two instruments appeared:

- The scribal college at Al-Qahur produced the **Fourth Seal**, naming General Uthar ibn Qays and bearing the college's attestation.
- Ruqaiya bint Mujalib's household produced the **Counter-Instrument**, naming her son Husan ibn Raiq and bearing what they held to be Mujalib's true final witness-mark.

The college condemned the Counter-Instrument. Ruqaiya's party condemned the Fourth Seal. Uthar took the caliphate; Husan took the desert.

## Bir Qassim, 603 AR

Husan and seventy-two members of his household accepted safe conduct to arbitration. Flood damage forced their escort from the named road. A travelling court ruled the protection void because the route was an essential term. Husan's party was killed at the dry wells of Bir Qassim.

The annual **Mourning of the Wells** recites the safe-conduct clause by clause, answering each with: *And yet the well was dry.*

## The Divide

[[The Witnessed Seal]] teaches that authority continues through public attestation and the community's jurists. [[The Sealed Blood]] teaches that succession continued through Ruqaiya and the Veiled Wasiyin.

Both instruments reportedly survive. The Fourth Seal rests in the Vault of Names at Al-Qahur; Qazmarid claims the Counter-Instrument lies beneath the Caliph-Wasi's throne. Opening either for forensic examination would void the succession it might prove. The dispute is therefore decidable in principle and undecidable in practice.

## Present Day

Iqashar and Al-Qahur are Witnessed-Seal. Qazmarid is Sealed-Blood. Zanthir is Witnessed-majority with protected Sealed-Blood communities. Every Qahirid succession, especially Marzuq III's expected Cull of Brothers, reopens the wound.
