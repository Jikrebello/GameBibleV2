# The Age of Held Proofs — Authorial Control

This control preserves the private structural model behind the reader-facing [[The Age of Held Proofs]]. It defines how present-day powers manage decisive evidence without turning that evidence into a predetermined future. The causal road to the 1360 board is [[Causality Pass - 09 The Road to 1360]], with the Reformation, Fiorenzian, Meshalem, Twin Cardinals, Alessior II and Qarayel's-burial sequences as bent in [[Causality Pass - 10F Bending the Road to 1360]] and bent again, in skeleton and dates, in [[Causality Pass - 11F Second Bend - The Road to 1360]]. Where the three disagree, 11F governs.

## The Structure

The 1360 board has sometimes been described as a semi-cold war, but cold war is an Earth structure built around deterrence by weapons. This world's present has a structure Earth never had: **deterrence by unopened proof**.

Because this cosmology is testable — sainthood has protocols, kindling has witnesses and contracts have forensics — every major power possesses at least one object or case whose public test would either vindicate it completely or shatter it completely. Because each holder knows the result is not certain, nobody tests. The world's peace is not the absence of war; it is a mutual deterrence of verification.

## How the engine was made

The habit has a history, and reader articles should tell it as history.

**The root: Tamsar and the Velian chests (611–612).** At Tamsar a breach-court's ruling failed against a march town re-sealed in the Fire, a Caleran reliquary burned yellow before both armies, and neither side gave battle. The instrument of 612, which Covenant law calls the Suspension, held every disputed charter "neither void nor in force": the first deterrence by unopened proof. The Velian cities' pre-conquest charters, locked in fireproof chests c. 610, wait on it still. See [[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]]. The habit then grew through these steps:

1. **The Refusal of the Lamp (1227).** Nikandros I refused the Sanctum's price, a trial of the Great Lamp of Veridun, for the Crown of Embers and Eclessian soldiers, offered unasked: "a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned." The second refusal of that test, after Makarios's nails in 1015, and the one that made refusal state policy.
2. **Bruckmoor (1243).** A test that destroyed what it tested, rightly.
3. **The Ruling of the Hollow Mouth (1263).** A test that was correct and that nobody believed (the Ossuary Crown).
4. **Aldersbruck (1299).** A test that answered in public and was voided by the people who set it. The last note in Ansgar's psalter asked for Fire; the synod put the psalter to the brazier and it did not burn; the legate declared the trial void, vaulted the book and charged the Weiss country a second fee for its funerals. Ansgar himself was never tried. He had drowned in the Blackwater two years earlier, a Sending House chaplain in good standing, so the Sanctum has a condemned book and no sentence against its author.
5. **The Preacher's Water (from 1306).** A proof nobody can own: oaths on it hold, and the 1342 test was inconclusive.
6. **Sarnwell (1311).** A test that vindicated, and became the model for the five tests. Its witnesses were eleven oath-bound Ashenmen, so the old Church's proudest proof rests on its enemies' river.
7. **The Council of Veridance (1319–1324).** The tests codified, drafted so that none can be applied to a saint without remains, by a council that answered Ferro's book with Ferro in the room.
8. **The Night Count (1342).** A test used as a land survey. The Accord's commissioners assigned disputed parishes by whether the churchyard walked, and whichever choir quieted a walking yard took it. The Schedule of Yards is therefore a list of several thousand test results that nobody wants repeated.

By 1352, when Vessing was reported, every great power already behaved as this control describes.

## The Held Proofs

- **The Unburnt Psalter** (Sanctum vault, 1299): test it and a chaplain who died in the Sanctum's own service is a saint whose margins are the Freeflame's first text, or the tests themselves are exposed as riggable. Either result breaks the old Church's spine.
- **The Vessing apparition** (pending, eight years): three grown witnesses at one grave, a Sanctum sexton, a Freeflame carter and a Virelian mother of Veridun-leaning piety; three testimonies, each matching one party's doctrine (Veridun, the Sanctum, the Freeflame) and each telling against the witness's own church. Confirmation inflames Veridun and the Freeflame; condemnation breaks the marches' piety; everyone remembers Bruckmoor.
- **The Saint-Emperor's hymns** (Thalmyria): field-testable any day, with results maddeningly conditional. Both churches have quietly decided this is worse than either clean answer. The Council's five tests of 1324 cannot reach Caedros I; Veridun took that as a verdict.
- **The two succession instruments** (the Covenant): the Fourth Seal and the Counter-Instrument, each capable of settling the Sealed-Blood schism and each legally impossible to open because examination would itself breach the instrument.
- **The Virelian Lamp** (Veridun): still burning and formally offered home at every Virelian election. Accepting it would prove what the east chose, and under Vesari hearth-law a fire handed back from another's hall is a lent fire, so taking it would make the king Veridun's client; no Virelian king dares.
- **The Empty City's dead gate** (Qiryath Reqa): if it flickers, the Mesakha question stops being theoretical for every power simultaneously. Since Meshalem (1288–1291), who twice went down the gate stair before the whole return and twice came back without touching it, the Keepers let no claimant near the gate and guard the question harder than the gate.
- **Wolfsmere's sleep** (the Lakekeepers): the one proof no one wants opened, held shut by an annual price that is rising.
- **The Creditor's three name-fragments:** the assembled true name would be the greatest contract-handle in history. Its assembly is the one event the Concord of Weights, the Vault of Names and the Church would unite to prevent, each for different reasons.

**Pressures that are not held proofs.** The Weiss water-test file (1342), the Schedule of Yards and its common grounds, the Brenholt tally board, the Ossuary Crown's custody, Silvio Quarne's missing ledgers, Gavrin's cairn, Ferro's unwritten refutation, the wax face Severin was buried in, the moulds in the mask-makers' cellar, the lodged prince's page in the Deep Vault and the emperor's flame-silences are live pressures. None is a ninth held proof; promote one only by deliberate decision.

## Why this is the engine

Every faction's strategy in 1360 is legible as **proof-management**: acquiring rivals' proofs, protecting its own, funding permanent postponement wherever a test threatens and building deniable test conditions. Wars are dangerous not only for territory but because battlefields are uncontrolled test environments — a siege can settle a doctrine by accident.

This is what the Restoration Doctrine means politically: the end of days is, among other things, the day everything gets tested. Every institution that would fail its test is therefore, knowingly or not, on the Adversary's side of the schedule.

## Forward Rule disruptors

The 1360 board resembles the 1570s. Each trajectory that real history would put on rails has a world-native disruptor. These are pressures; none chooses an outcome.

| Trajectory a reader might predict | World-native disruptor |
|---|---|
| The Korrheim Accord collapses into a long confessional war | No prince chose his rite: the map was drawn by the dead, yard by yard, and the quarrel now runs through funerals. A yard unsent for a year and a day is forfeit, hauntings are staged to open a parish and hidden to keep one, and a rerun of the Night Count could unmake any border. The peace is sworn on the Weiss. Whoever breaks it breaks an oath on water that holds oaths, and nobody knows what that does. The Unsworn, who bury in that water, sit outside it. |
| Virelia's elective crown drifts into paralysis | The crown has no royal graves, so every election reopens whose lamp the crown lands stand under. The Virelian Lamp is offered home at every election, and a reformed third of the council has its own reasons to want it accepted or refused. |
| The Covenant declines through palace succession | The succession is a contest of registers. The Cull needs the Guard bench and the Unnamed Custodian to witness it; the Custodian's own name lies in the Deep Vault at Al-Qahur, where Prince Mihrzad's name was released in 1243 and whose canal councils and military households are at odds. A brother of Marzuq III, lodged in 1349 as the price of Al-Qahur's acknowledgment, is alive in the walled garden there, and his page lies in the same vault: whoever the shrine's custodians un-lodge is a claimant the Guard never witnessed. ## Writing rule

When plotting anything set in the present, identify which held proof the story walks toward. Every power in the room should be more afraid of certainty than of the enemy. That fear is the setting's signature.

[[Unknown Histories - Authorial Accounts]] may establish central crimes without making every witness omniscient. The Forward Rule still holds: no control chooses the next emperor, resolves Vessing, decides what the flame-silences are, opens the distant embassy or rewards the first person who believes the right legend.
