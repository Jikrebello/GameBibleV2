# The Grey Sweat - Authorial Truth

This note contains the creator-facing truth behind [[The Grey Sweat]]. The causal chain that runs from the Sweat to 1360 is in [[Causality Pass - 09 The Road to 1360]], as bent in [[Causality Pass - 10F Bending the Road to 1360]] and [[Causality Pass - 11F Second Bend - The Road to 1360]].

## The Hidden Catastrophe

The Grey Sweat was a disease and a **soul-logistics catastrophe**. The fever was biological; its mortality was not changed by rites. It killed about a fifth of eastern Eclessia and an eighth of the west, and it killed priests, gravediggers and ferrymen at several times that rate because they went to the dying. Deaths arrived faster than the Vestibule's damaged routes and Eclessia's burial ministries could process them. Interrupted rites did not cause the fever, but they multiplied the number of souls left exposed between death and safe passage. The walking dead of the reader article were real: residue and unsent souls at thin places, in the proportions the Bestiary and soul-road controls allow.

Church quarantine doctrine was written about rite-integrity rather than contagion. That was a reasonable response to what people could see. It was also the rule that western tenants turned into the clean-road charters, by walking to wherever a priest lived, and the legal tool Virelia's magnates used to seal priestless villages and bind their survivors (the bond of the clean village). One rule produced both settlements; which one a region got depended on who held the patronage of its parishes.

## The Undertow Harvest

The [[The Undertow|Undertow]] bought, diverted, and trapped unguarded routes in bulk. Several of its oldest standing passages date from these years. Modern Undertow claims on plague cemeteries, drowned charnel pits, and extinct parishes are not metaphors: they are inherited pieces of stolen soul-infrastructure.

## Ash-Walkers, Gerlind and Thin Places

Ash-Walker processions carried the ash of the unblessed pyres to churchyards that still had a priest, and sometimes closed thin places in their wake. Their fasting and their burdens did not purchase salvation, and the ash itself carried nothing, but shared repentance, truthful naming of the dead, and the presence of genuine sanctity stabilized places abandoned by formal institutions. This is why some processions worked and others became feeding grounds for [[The Weeping Courts]].

Saint Gerlind of Lindenfurt (c. 1228–1279) worked by the same principle, and her sanctity was genuine. Neither her voice nor any rite owned the result. The Thalmyric lay singers who sang their own dead out worked the same way where they were righteous; where they were not, Thalmyric villages were haunted too, and Thalmyric memory has smoothed that over.

## The Twin Cardinals

Aimery of Corvel died of the Sweat the night of his election, unsung. The figure the sacristans saw at the conclave garden wall in 1242 was his unsent soul at a thin place, and it went when it was named and sung. Aubert's motives were mixed and both real: he feared the conclave would be sealed in with the fever, and he wanted the chair. Funerals sung under his Ferry Rule worked as Gerlind's did, where the singers named the dead truthfully; neither his false authority nor Gelasin's later confirmation changed what had happened to any soul. See [[Causality Pass - 10F Bending the Road to 1360]].

## Beyond Eclessia

- **The Covenant (the Emptying of the Benches).** Every death of a bound person required a witnessed release in the Living Register. Deaths outran witnesses; unreleased names piled up; debts of the dead rolled onto heirs; garrison instruments were widowed and frontier forts opened. Custody without a name-cage does not touch routing, so the Unreleased were a bookkeeping catastrophe and not a soul trap. The Vault cannot know that, and its living and dead registers have never balanced since. Neither the Vault nor the relic courts knows that the other's books also fail to balance.
- **Sestara.** The emptied Roll of Faces and the Medori clearing house. No special soul-road effect. The wax faces Fiorenzian families began to keep of their dead are ordinary likenesses; what spoke through some of them a century later is recorded in [[Causality Pass - 11F Second Bend - The Road to 1360]].
- **The steppe.** Light mortality. Wind-speakers read death residue but cannot recall routed souls; their claim that the steppe's dead "go with the wind" is a belief, and the authorial record does not rule on it.

## The Sending and the Paper of the Dead

The post-Sweat Sending Houses worked where rites were sung, because intercession is real. A sealed assurance letter never touched judgement, and Alessior II's Bull of the Sufficient Seal (1283) was false in every particular, though he believed it. An **unsung** letter is an unresolved sealed promise over a named soul, and that makes it a lien that a Patron collecting soul-liens, the Undertow or an infernal broker can press to delay or contest passage. This is what Silvio Quarne sold. The full ruling is in [[Causality Pass - 09 The Road to 1360#Silvio Quarne, the Bridge-Seller]].

The four hundred thousand letters Alessior had sealed without charge in 1287 for Aurelia's plague dead were liens like any other unsung letter, and they are the largest single block of the Great Arrears. Parishes that stopped singing for their poor because the seal sufficed are the reason the dead walked again in the loyal south by the 1310s.

Alessior's own Sending was sung. What the night clerks saw at the signing desk in 1296 was residue at a thin place, made in a room where hundreds of thousands of unsung promises over named souls had been sealed; the choir's singing closed it. The blank letters found on the desk in the mornings were written by living clerks, who had signed in his hand for years and were afraid to say so. Neither the Sanctum nor the Freeflame knows this.

## The Ossuary Crown

The crown that spoke in the plague dead's voices (1251–1263) is the coherence-piece of a mature Ossuary Mind, a full-agency Astral person assembled from wrecked plague refuges and abandoned route offices in the Egg White. It is not the dead and not their collective soul. Its voices are accurate copies, which is why the Ruling of the Hollow Mouth was right and unbelieved. Whether the crown is the Mind's core, whether any soul is separately trapped in it, and what returning its remains would do stay open.

## The Quiet Ninth

The end of the Sweat is held in [[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]] and is unexplained here as well. This file attaches no cause, interval, discrepancy or mechanism to it. Covenant registers record the date and no cause.

## Institutional Knowledge

The highest relic courts know that plague-year routing records do not balance. The Order of the Lantern holds copies of the Chancery registers it bought from the Medori to sing the Great Arrears and, through that work, knows more than any other body about where the dead were and were not sent; it does not know about liens as such. The Medori hold the master registers and, through the family's inherited contract craft, understand what an unsung letter is worth. Undertow agents, a few Left Hand scholars and the authorial record know the larger truth.

## Unresolved

Whether the Sweat's first outbreak had a deliberate mortal agent is deliberately left open, even here. Its origin is unknown in every surviving source.

## Public-Facing Boundary

Public notes may establish missing souls, failed rites, walking dead, contradictory registers, haunted plague sites, theological panic, the Ossuary Crown's voices and the Hollow Mouth ruling, the Twin Cardinals and the Ferry Rule, the Sending Houses, the sightings at Alessior II's signing desk as reports, and in-world claims about dead paper. They must not identify the Undertow harvest, the lien nature of unsung letters, the Ossuary Mind, or the precise soul-route failure as settled public knowledge, and nothing anywhere explains the Quiet Ninth.
