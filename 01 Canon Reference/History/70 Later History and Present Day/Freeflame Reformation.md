# Freeflame Reformation

The **Freeflame Reformation** is Eclessia's continuing religious fracture over who may send the dead, who may kindle the White Flame, and whether the Church's routing assurances ever had the power claimed for them. It began in 1297, when the friar Ansgar of Brenholt posted the *Hundred Grievances* against the assurance trade, and it produced a peasant war, twenty-three years of wagon-fort fighting, a princes' war, a reforming council in the old Church and, in 1343, the [[The Korrheim Accord|Korrheim Accord]] that froze the map. The Freeflame, or Order of the Open Flame, govern Ironvale and much of the Markish north in 1360.

The quarrel was sharper than a dispute about money. In this world prayer for the dead does something, and so does its absence. Both sides of the Reformation had seen the dead walk.

## The assurance trade at its height

After the [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/The Grey Sweat.md|Grey Sweat]], the Sanctum's Chancery of Sendings sold sealed letters promising that a Sending House would sing named rites for a named soul. Where the rites were sung, the trade kept its promise, and families who had watched their dead return after the plague paid gladly. The trouble was arithmetic. By the 1280s the Chancery was selling letters far faster than any House could sing them.

**Lord Primarch Alessior VI** (r. 1281–1296), a Malvora of Sestara elected with the credit of Bartolo Medori, needed money for his wars and for a new dome over the Sanctum Divinitatis. Under him the trade became an industry. Its engineer was his Cardinal-Treasurer.

### Silvio Quarne, the Bridge-Seller

**Silvio Quarne** (c. 1238–1299) was a Fiorenzian notary's son left alive by the last wave of the Sweat with a ledger and a gift for figures. He entered the Malvora household as a clerk, joined the Chancery about 1265 and made it efficient. He introduced the printed letter; the schedule of rates by age and estate, with its cheaper rate for a child; the blank letter sold with the name left open; and the household bond that covered a family for three generations. He became Cardinal-Treasurer in 1282. In 1284 he put the newly canonised Saint Gerlind's face on the letters, and the Gerlind seal outsold every other. In 1285 an apparition of Gerlind condemned the singers of Aurelium's Saint-Leon quarter, who sang the dead out for nothing, and a riot burned the quarter. Lantern investigators later traced the apparition to his office.

His own audits told him by 1288 that the Houses sang perhaps one rite in ten of those sold. He sold the unsung letters too. Brokers in Vorthara paid well for bundles of what the trade came to call **dead paper**, and the money went to Alessior's wars and the dome. When the Freeflame later found dead paper in brokers' houses, it bore his office's countersign. Quarne fled Aurelium in 1297 with his private ledgers and died two years later in a rented room in the Black Spire wards of Vorthara. The ledgers were gone when the room was opened. Sestaran historians call him a gifted administrator blamed for a Primarch's appetite. The Freeflame named him after Ansgar's First Grievance: the **Bridge-Seller**. Vortharan tellers say his ledgers still add up at night.

## Ansgar's Count

**Ansgar of Brenholt** (1264–1305) was a marches friar and a lecturer on the doctrine of the Vestibule, a miner's son from the iron country of the upper Weiss. When he was seven, Saint Gerlind had come to Brenholt and sung his father's name after a collapse at the mine-head. In 1296 he set the letters sold in Brenholt parish against the rites its Sending House had actually sung, and found more than ten letters for every rite. He then counted the dead still seen at the mine-heads and the ford and found them among the unsung names. His friars sang those names themselves. The walking stopped, and several of them swore that a woman's voice joined the hymn.

In the autumn of 1297 he nailed the *[[The Hundred Grievances — Selections|Hundred Grievances]]* to the chapel door at Brenholt, over the Sending House's printed table of rates. *"No seal of wax weighs anything at the Weighing. You have sold the poor a bridge that is not yours."* The Grievances read as an audit as much as a sermon, and they named the dead Alessior eleven times. The Hundredth ended with a challenge: *prove me wrong with Fire, fathers, and not with fees.*

## Aldersbruck

The Synod of Aldersbruck tried him in 1305. Ansgar would not recant and asked again for the Fire. They burned him on the riverbank. His psalter, thrown onto the pyre after him, came out of the ashes unburnt, and his ashes were shovelled into the Weiss so that no one could keep them. Two of the witnesses mattered later. The Synod's junior notary was **Lucian Ferro**, a twenty-six-year-old jurist from the Latinate south, who wrote the record. Among the lords watching from the bank was the fourteen-year-old heir of Ironvale, **Rainald**.

The Sanctum vaulted the Unburnt Psalter in lead and has denied it exists ever since. Ironvale's reach of the Weiss became the **Preacher's Water**. Oaths sworn on it hold strangely well.

## The Ashenmen Risings, 1306–1329

In the spring of 1306 miners and farmers from Brenholt and the upper Weiss waded into the river below Aldersbruck, smeared their faces with its grey silt and swore to fight until the last false seal was burned. Their enemies called them the Ashenmen, after the plague penitents; they took the name for Ansgar's ashes.

The oath held. In twenty-three years of fighting, no Ashenmen band is recorded breaking its word, deserting or selling out a neighbour. Behind wagon-forts chained in rings, with literate quartermasters and field preachers, they were the hardest infantry in Eclessia, and they were impossible to bargain with, because their oath named an end no prince would grant. Bond-labour, tithe, Sending fees and old grudges fed them. They burned Sending Houses and church estates; old-Church armies burned their villages in return. They also smashed images of the Mother across the Ironvale country, a guilt the movement's second generation still handles badly: reformed congregations keep her feast under other names.

In 1311, in a valley the Ashenmen had stripped of images, the Mother appeared to a crippled washerwoman at **Sarnwell**, and the spring that rose there has passed every test the relic courts have devised. The old Church read it as a rebuke to the image-breakers. Reformed preachers called it bait. It is the Sanctum's proudest modern proof.

The Risings ended by the letter of their oath. In 1329 Rainald, now Duke of Ironvale and converted, seized the chests of every Sending House in his duchy and burned their sealed letters in Brenholt market square. The Ashenmen bands of Ironvale held their oath fulfilled, came down from the wagon-forts and took service under his banner.

## Ferro and the Open Flame

Ferro reached Ironvale about 1312, carrying his record of Aldersbruck. About 1316 he published *The Order of the Open Flame*. Its argument starts from the Grievances and from the plague years: the Lord's Flame kindles wherever real sanctity gathers, *the Fire keeps no landlord*, and so no hierarchy can mint it or sell it. Freeflame congregations are governed by tested elders. They read scripture aloud, cast hymns together, refuse the assurance trade and keep relic devotion to a minimum, and the congregation sings the Sending for anyone who dies among them, whatever the dead could pay. Ferro's reformed towns became famously orderly, literate and merciless. He died in 1352, and his students govern.

## The Reform Wars and the end of a ducal house

From 1329 the war belonged to princes. Rainald armed the heirs of the Risings, confiscated Church estates and fought old-Church neighbours along the Weiss. Caldria's campaigns of 1310–1324 and Aurelia's trade war of 1310–1313 against Freeflame-linked merchants had already shown how quickly doctrine became a land war. Princes on both sides took Church property they had wanted for generations. In 1340 the Freeflame-majority canton of Falkendorf resisted an old-Church settlement and brought Korrheim to the edge of civil war; Rainald's last son died in the fighting. Rainald died in 1341 without an heir. His house had spent its blood, its credit and its standing with the Church, and Ironvale's guild ministries chartered the Grand Ministry to govern in its place.

## The old Church reforms

Lord Primarch **Celestin IX** (r. 1330–1354) convened the **Council of Veridance** in 1337. It abolished assurance sales, which by then was the easier half of the problem. Millions of letters had been sold and never sung, so the Council ordered every one of them performed: **the Great Arrears**. The first work of the **Order of the Lantern**, founded under the Council by Father Ivo the Lamed, a crippled veteran of the Zanthiri border wars turned mystic, was walking haunted parishes with lanterns and singing names out of the old registers. The registers had been in the Medori vault since 1296, held as surety for Alessior's debts, and the Council had to buy copies sheet by sheet.

The Council also rebuilt seminary education and codified the **five tests** of sainthood, drafted around Sarnwell. Veridun noticed that none of the five can be applied to a saint who left no remains, which rules out Caedros I. The reform kept the old Church standing and did not restore its credit with the people who had bought the letters.

## What it left

Freeflame reform found a real fraud against the dead and made villages sing their own Sendings again. Its congregations taught farm children to read the Grievances aloud and disciplined corrupt clergy in front of them. It also let Rainald and his neighbours strip Church estates they had wanted for generations, broke the Mother's images and the people who kept them, and raised children who called every grandmother's custom a corruption. Old-Church writers point out that the Freeflame's own most famous object is a river and its most famous saint is a woman they forbid anyone to pray to; Freeflame writers answer that the Sanctum's most famous object is a book it says it does not have.

## The frozen settlement

The [[The Korrheim Accord|Korrheim Accord]] of 1343, sworn on the Preacher's Water, ended open war by letting each recognised ruler fix the confession of his territory. Radical congregations who would not swear on the river were left outside it. Conscience, mixed families and border parishes were left unresolved.

## Present-day fault lines

- Ironvale is the strongest Freeflame polity, governed by guild ministers.
- Veridancia trains both old-Church reformers and some of their most effective opponents.
- Virelia's elective crown could change the confessional balance with one succession.
- Grenzburg is formally old Church but exposed to migrants, pamphlets and reform-minded labourers.
- Dead paper still circulates, and brokers still press claims on the named dead.
- The Hollow Flame exploits every abuse while offering severance rather than reform.
