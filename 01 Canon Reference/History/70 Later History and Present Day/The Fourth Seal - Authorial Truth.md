# The Fourth Seal - Authorial Truth

Which succession instrument began genuine remains deliberately unresolved.

[[The Creditor]], wearing the mask of Malak al-Ahd, witnessed both instruments and manipulated their preservation, legal framing, and institutional custody so neither could be conclusively privileged. It encouraged the loophole that enabled Bir Qassim and has profited from duplicated allegiance, fear, pilgrimage, rebellion, and metaphysical rent ever since.

The Creditor's interference does not make both traditions equally true, nor prove that every believer or institution knowingly serves it. The original human dispute may have had an innocent claimant, a forger, two partial authorities, or a succession Mujalib himself failed to resolve.

## The Three Seals

Bent on 14 November 2026 ([[Causality Pass - 11D Second Bend - Covenant Rise and the Fall]]). The sequence of four Hand-Sealed Caliphs succeeding the Prophet (572–601) is retired.

- **Mujalib** (c. 510–596) never held office. In 563 he sealed three founding instruments and gave each to a holder: Water to the guard of the wells under Zuhayr ibn Qays, Names to the college of scribes at Al-Qahur, and the Roads to Qatada Hafiz-al-Turuq. "The Hand-Sealed" are those three first holders. Nobody bore the title of caliph before 597. The "Fourth Seal" is the fourth of the Prophet's instruments, the one that would set a holder over the other three.
- **Why he divided it.** He was sincere, and he divided his authority on purpose. The guard of the wells had taken the Nine Wells in 558 without any instrument of his, and he feared what a single holder would do. Whether he ever sealed a fourth instrument is the unresolved question. The Creditor, as Malak al-Ahd, witnessed all three Seals and both claimed fourths.
- **The kept wells.** The wells Mujalib sealed between 541 and 549 were kept by the first of the 4,444, bound one at a time. The Covenant of the 4,444 (c. 549) closed the number. Reader prose says only that something kept the well.
- **The roll and the Given.** The Seal of Names bound the Covenant to feed every name in a failed year, and the Clause of the Given lets the bound carriers lift only what has been given. The Unsealing of 568 followed from those two terms meeting a thin flood. The Creditor's rent grows with the roll. Whether it designed the pair of terms or only profited from them is **left open**.
- **The finite number.** The 4,444 are all the bound offices the founding covenant holds. By about 610 every one was set at a well, on a road or in the oasis, which is one true reason the Unsealing stopped.
- **The heirs in 1360.** The Seal of Water descends to the caliph of Al-Qahur, the Custodian-of-the-Wells. The Seal of Names descends to the Vault of Names and its Unnamed Custodian. The Seal of the Roads lay in the college's keeping from 604 to 881, when the caliph gave it to Qahir ibn Ruzbeh with the new title of Sultan; the Qahirids hold it and claim the fourth as Sultan-Caliphs.

## Bir Qassim

Bent on 14 November 2026 ([[Causality Pass - 10D Bending the Long Frontier and the Covenant]]) and again the same day ([[Causality Pass - 11D Second Bend - Covenant Rise and the Fall]]). Hilal lives; the massacre falls on the dying and on the drovers who gave water.

- **The keeper.** The station wells of the North Well Road were kept by bound offices of the 4,444 under the Seal of the Roads, to draw "for those who travel under the Covenant's protection" by the road measure. The keeper of Bir Qassim is a devil, bound to the letter and not blind to it. When the travellers left the named road at Bir Nadim it took the cruellest reading its instrument allowed, which is the only latitude a bound devil has, and stopped drawing for the fifty-eight before any court spoke. Jarud ibn Sulayt's ruling confirmed what the keeper had already done; he could have ruled the flood a necessity and chose the letter. Whether the Creditor, which brokered the binding of the 4,444 as Malak al-Ahd, prompted the keeper is **left open**; this is the loophole the Creditor "encouraged".
- **The escort and Qatada.** The escort's refusal was its own, and the sergeant's words to the bailiff are reported accurately. Qatada rode west because his name was on the instrument. He was not poisoned. He refused the kept wells for most of the ride back and died of a fever at Qafra in the spring of 604, so both in-world accounts of his death are partly true.
- **Zuhayr** did not order the killing. His order was to arrest the escort and deliver the travellers alive. The column's commander applied Jarud's ruling on his own reading of it, and Zuhayr punished nobody. In 604 he had the keeper recalled to end the pilgrimage of mourners to the well. The unkept shaft silted within the year. "Dry by dawn" is the mourners' compression of that year. Zuhayr died in 609.
- **The count.** Fifty-eight named. Sixteen died of thirst: twelve before the escort left and four of the nine left behind, Rumaitha among the four. The column killed the other five. Thirty-seven were carried west and became the core of the Sealed-Blood households at Qazmarid. Fifteen drovers stayed; fourteen were killed and one boy escaped. The Witnessed registers' five and the mourning houses' thirty-five are each honest about what that side chose to count.
- **Hilal** lived at Qazmarid until 631. Rumaitha did order him to go. His veil began in shame and became a vow, and the Veiled Wasiyin follow it.
- **The Ledger.** Suhrab's diggers in 1194 found the shaft dry because the keeper had held a deep shaft open in country the Basin's channels had been drawing down since the 550s, and six centuries of that drawing had left the water below any depth men could dig. Nothing supernatural keeps it dry.

## The Frontier Stake

The deadlock also holds a border. Covenant breach-courts under the Banu Mizan voided the Velian cities' charters under Zuhayr's seal between 598 and 610, and the cities kept the voided charters in fireproof chests. If the Fourth Seal were proved forged, every service made in Zuhayr's name would fall and those charters would revive. Qahirid interest in never opening the Seal is therefore territorial as well as dynastic, and Qazmarid has a standing interest in the chests.

The plea of the disputed seal halted the First Unsealing twice over: Qatada stopped his own army on it in 598, and at Tamsar in 611 it frightened a Covenant army into standing still. The Suspension of 612, kept by both sides because each feared to test what it held, is the root of the Age of Held Proofs. The Creditor did not need to engineer the Suspension; fear did the work. Its interest in keeping both instruments undecidable reaches the frontier through the chests. See [[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]].

## Secrecy Rule

Public notes may describe contradictory evidence, impossible examination, dreams, accusations, and the spiritual fruits of the schism. They must not identify the Creditor as Malak al-Ahd or state that it engineered the permanent deadlock. They may report what witnesses saw at Bir Qassim (the bucket that came up empty for the named and full for the drovers) and must not say why the keeper chose as it did. They must not say that the kept wells of 541–549 were kept by bound devils, only that something kept them.
