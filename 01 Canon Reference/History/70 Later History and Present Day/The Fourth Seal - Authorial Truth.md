# The Fourth Seal - Authorial Truth

Which succession instrument began genuine remains deliberately unresolved.

[[The Creditor]], wearing the mask of Malak al-Ahd, witnessed both instruments and manipulated their preservation, legal framing, and institutional custody so neither could be conclusively privileged. It encouraged the loophole that enabled Bir Qassim and has profited from duplicated allegiance, fear, pilgrimage, rebellion, and metaphysical rent ever since.

The Creditor's interference does not make both traditions equally true, nor prove that every believer or institution knowingly serves it. The original human dispute may have had an innocent claimant, a forger, two partial authorities, or a succession Mujalib himself failed to resolve.

## Bir Qassim

Bent on 14 November 2026 ([[Causality Pass - 10D Bending the Long Frontier and the Covenant]]).

- **The keeper.** The station wells of the North Well Road were kept by bound offices of the 4,444 under Mujalib's road instrument, to draw "for those who travel under the Covenant's protection". The keeper of Bir Qassim is a devil, bound to the letter and not blind to it. When the travellers left the named road at Bir Nadim it took the cruellest reading its instrument allowed, which is the only latitude a bound devil has, and stopped drawing for the fifty-eight before any court spoke. Jarud ibn Sulayt's ruling confirmed what the keeper had already done; he could have ruled the flood a necessity and chose the letter. Whether the Creditor, which brokered the binding of the 4,444 as Malak al-Ahd, prompted the keeper is **left open**; this is the loophole the Creditor "encouraged".
- **Uthar** did not order the killing. He did order the keeper recalled in 604 to end the pilgrimage of mourners to the well, and punished nobody. The unkept shaft silted within the year. "Dry by dawn" is the mourners' compression of that year.
- **The count.** Fifty-eight named; seven dead of thirst; thirty-four killed at dawn; seventeen women and children sent west with the drovers, who became the core of the Sealed-Blood households gathering at Qazmarid by 611. Both in-world counts are honest about what each side chose to count.
- **The Ledger.** Suhrab's diggers in 1194 found the shaft dry because the keeper had held a deep shaft open in country the Basin's channels had been drawing down since the 550s, and six centuries of that drawing had left the water below any depth men could dig. Nothing supernatural keeps it dry.

## The Frontier Stake

The deadlock also holds a border. Covenant breach-courts voided the Velian cities' charters under Uthar's seal between 598 and 610, and the cities kept the voided charters in fireproof chests. If the Fourth Seal were proved forged, every service made in Uthar's name would fall and those charters would revive. Qahirid interest in never opening the Seal is therefore territorial as well as dynastic, and Qazmarid has a standing interest in the chests.

The plea of the disputed seal helped halt the First Unsealing, and the Suspension of 612, kept by both sides because each feared to test what it held, is the root of the Age of Held Proofs. The Creditor did not need to engineer the Suspension; fear did the work. Its interest in keeping both instruments undecidable reaches the frontier through the chests. See [[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]].

## Secrecy Rule

Public notes may describe contradictory evidence, impossible examination, dreams, accusations, and the spiritual fruits of the schism. They must not identify the Creditor as Malak al-Ahd or state that it engineered the permanent deadlock. They may report what witnesses saw at Bir Qassim (the bucket that came up empty for the named and full for the drovers) and must not say why the keeper chose as it did.
