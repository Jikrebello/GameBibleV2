# The Hollow Flame Cult

The Hollow Flame Cult is the principal counter-church devoted to the Adversary. Eclessian and Thalmyric authorities condemn it by name because it rejects communion, judgement and every enduring obligation of love. Its preachers seek people already injured by religious institutions and offer severance as a form of freedom.

## Doctrine

The cult teaches that the White Flame is a cage, that communion dissolves the self beneath divine ownership and that the Weighing is the final judgement of a corrupt system. Its “flame that casts no light” represents a will owing nothing and warming only its bearer. Freedom, in this doctrine, requires the severing of every claim that another person or the Lord might possess.

Its rites invert familiar forms of worship. During the **Quenching**, an initiate writes a baptismal name in water and drinks it away. The **Unlit Vigil** gathers a cell around a cold brazier into which members place precious objects that will never burn. Admission culminates in the **Sacrament of the Closed Hand**, when the initiate renounces an obligation of love. The loss of a marriage, friendship or family bond is the rite's intended price.

## Structure

The cult is organised in cells. Initiates are called **Wicks**, cell leaders **Tapers** and regional organisers **Pyres**; perhaps thirty Pyres are alive. Interrogated Tapers also refer to **the Unburning**, although no surviving evidence establishes whether this is a person, office or useful fiction.

Itinerant preachers known as **the Unlit** form the cult's missionary arm. They usually arrive after a church has failed someone: a widow ruined by assurance sellers, a veteran abandoned after a crusade or a Folk convert shunned by a parish. The injury may be entirely real. The preacher interprets it as proof that every obligation is a form of ownership and encourages the wounded person to destroy the relationships that remain.

## Signs

Investigators associate the cult with cold braziers in recently occupied rooms, bowls and dry pens left after the name rite, and clusters of broken marriages or abandoned oaths. Congregations may cease gathering without a public dispute. Eclessian inquisitors place particular weight on converts who will no longer argue for their choice, because the final discipline of a cell teaches members to treat disagreement itself as a renewed claim upon them.

## Methods and Relations

Recruitment has grown during the wars and scandals of the Reformation era. Cells sometimes exploit communities already injured by Envy, debt or other Idol networks, while regarding those networks as another form of dependence. The Idols gain little from a soul that refuses every attachment and commonly oppose the cult in turn. The Undertow deals with Hollow Flame agents more readily than most supernatural traffickers do.
