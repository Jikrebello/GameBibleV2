# The Atlas of the Left Hand

The master index of the world's hostile networks — the named answer to the setting's rightward institutions. Rule of use: **villainy gets the same specificity as sanctity.** Every network below has a dossier; every dossier follows the same shape (mask/layer, doctrine, structure, signs, methods, relations, hooks).

## How the Left Hand Is Organized (authorial doctrine)

Evil in the World Egg is **plural and competitive**, not a pyramid. Four distinct supply chains, often mistaken for one another in-world:

1. **The Adversary's project** (refusal): counter-churches and false prophets — [[The Hollow Flame Cult]].
2. **The Idols' feeding** (Shadowlands predation): seven networks, one per engine — [[The Weeping Courts]] (Envy), [[The Devoted]] (Lust), [[The Red Tally]] (Wrath), [[The Under-Ledger]] (Greed), [[The Diadem]] (Pride), [[The Long Table]] (Gluttony), [[The Apathetics]] (Sloth). The Idols do not command the Adversary's church and it does not command them; they interlock the way markets interlock.
3. **The patron economy** (Astral rent): [[The Concord of Weights]] (the rent cartel) and its greatest member, [[The Creditor]] — the spine's biggest secret.
4. **Soul-route predation** (the leftward road): [[The Undertow]] — the counter-Boatman.

Standing creature-side infrastructure cross-references: the vampire bloodlines (Pride's escrowed souls), the ghoul-cities (Gluttony's logistics), the Nightmares (Sloth's cavalry), and the Hollow-Kings, whose place in these supply chains remains unknown.

## The Interlock Rule

When writing any villain plot, identify **which supply chain profits** — refusal, feeding, rent, or interception — because their goals genuinely differ: the Hollow Flame wants souls to *refuse*; the Idols want them to *produce*; the patrons want them to *owe*; the Undertow wants them to *arrive elsewhere*. A community can be farmed by all four simultaneously, and the four will sabotage each other doing it. That competition is the party's best weapon and the setting's best irony.
