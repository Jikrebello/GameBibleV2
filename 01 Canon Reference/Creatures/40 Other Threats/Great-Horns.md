# Great-Horns

**Great-horns** are massive migratory grassland bovines whose herds cross the northeastern Tarkhan country. They are mundane animals rather than leakspawn, court beasts, or sacred manifestations, though some clans attach inherited ritual to their migration.

## Encounter Role

A lone great-horn is dangerous; a moving herd is terrain. Stampede, defensive circles, rutting bulls, frightened calves, grass fire, and human hunters create most encounters. Predators and raiders follow the same movement.

## Ecology and Lifecycle

Herds follow rain-grown grass, mineral licks, calving grounds, and winter shelter. Cows and juveniles form the main body; mature bulls separate outside the rut. A failed migration damages hunting clans and can strip pasture needed by domestic animals.

Some Grass-Tarkhan follow the herds as their primary economy. Others hunt only at agreed seasons. Pastoral clans may protect a herd as future food or divert it away from horse pasture. Great-horns do not define all Grass-Tarkhan life.

## Signs

- a dark moving line at the horizon;
- ground vibration before the herd is visible;
- broad cloven tracks and churned mineral soil;
- stripped grass, dung fields, circling scavengers, and broken scrub;
- falcons and hunters moving parallel rather than toward the herd.

## Materials

Meat, fat, hide, sinew, bone, horn, glue, containers, bow components, shields, and winter coverings support camps and trade. Horn from a dangerous bull has prestige but no inherent magical property.

## Faction Use

Ash road-builders try to steer migrations away from depots. Moon wind-speakers arbitrate old hunting grounds. Castaran merchants buy hide and horn; Aversian buyers seek standardized supply. Poachers can trigger feud or famine without invoking supernatural taboo.

## Story Uses

- a depot has blocked the only safe calving route;
- a herd moves weeks early because something follows it;
- rival clans claim the same legal first strike;
- a stampede threatens an annual peace gathering;
- diseased animals reach a horse pasture before scouts recognize the signs.
