# Gluttony Masks and Feast-Beasts

Gluttony's forms do not represent ordinary hunger. They arise where consumption is taught to have no proper end and where those denied nourishment become part of the spectacle.

## Feastlings

Feastlings are hand-sized Shadow fauna resembling knuckled hands with lamprey mouths in their palms. They work in chattering swarms, setting tables, seasoning food, dragging scraps into patterns, and marking who will eat and who will be eaten.

- **Origin and agency:** animal Idol fauna with no moral agency.
- **Ecology:** they hatch from leftovers deliberately displayed beside deprivation. A brood prepares a site for larger forms by turning every object into food, utensil, guest, or course.
- **Signs:** cutlery arranged around sleeping people, bite marks on sealed jars, crumbs moving against the slope, and meals that smell richer while nourishing less.
- **Threat pattern:** spoil carried provisions, fasten victims to seats, strip armor as tableware, swarm anyone who shares food outside the assigned order, and flee into drains or serving vessels.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** distribute the hoarded nourishment to its intended recipients, clean the feast site without preserving trophies, and destroy the first serving dish. Starving the swarm while guests remain excluded creates cannibal broods.
- **Materials:** dried palms retain flavor without nutrition and are used in illicit appetite tonics.
- **Regional names:** table hands, crumb mouths, little hosts, and plate-biters.
- **Story uses:** prepare a noble feast whose missing servant is the final course; infest an army ration train; lead investigators from a luxury market to a hidden famine store.

## Gut-Maws

Gut-Maws are large Masks whose bodies are traveling digestive chambers wrapped in borrowed architecture. One may crawl as a tavern on too many legs, a barrel-bellied hunting beast, or a procession of mouths under a banquet cloth.

- **Origin and agency:** cunning Mask with limited agency, anchored to an appetite engine such as an invitation, recipe, drug supply, corpse market, or sacrificial table.
- **Ecology:** it consumes more than flesh. A Gut-Maw may digest memory, craftsmanship, fertility, warmth, or the capacity to feel full, leaving bodies alive but emptied of the chosen good.
- **Signs:** drains exhale meal steam, missing goods return as grease, victims describe themselves as ingredients, and tracks contain teeth rather than toes.
- **Threat pattern:** swallows terrain and people, regurgitates partly digested servants, changes its appetite to exploit what the group values, and retreats through any active serving route.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** identify what appetite the Maw embodies, cut its supply and invitation routes, recover named remains or stolen qualities, then break the organ-object at its center. Physical killing leaves the organ able to grow another body.
- **Materials:** stomach pearls contain compressed traces of consumed skills or memories. Using one transfers fragments without restoring the victim.
- **Regional names:** walking kitchens, belly houses, feast boars, and the second table.
- **Story uses:** consume a town's ability to preserve food rather than its stores; serve a cult as both temple and execution site; hold the only surviving memories of an erased craft.

## Banquet Tyrants

Banquet Tyrants are autonomous Archmasks that present as monumental hosts: many-robed monarchs, smiling abbots, beloved innkeepers, or immense figures whose ribs form a dining hall. Their courts make being consumed feel like admission to greatness.

- **Origin and agency:** sapient Archmask with full moral agency. It understands sufficiency and deliberately makes limit a mark of inferiority.
- **Ecology:** a Tyrant organizes producers, guests, excluded populations, suppliers, addicts, and lesser forms into a self-renewing feast. It prefers living systems to a single massacre.
- **Signs:** social status tracks what one can waste, invitations arrive before harvest totals, servants lose personal names, and public hunger is treated as proof of the host's abundance.
- **Threat pattern:** assigns guests and courses, weaponizes appetite, turns consumed strengths into temporary court powers, and re-forms from any ceremonial place kept empty for it.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** feed those systematically denied, end the supply chain that treats persons as courses, restore names and burial where possible, and destroy the host-seat while a shared meal is completed without hierarchy of worth. Mere fasting leaves the court's victims hungry and its architecture intact.
- **Materials:** a host-bone records every supplier and victim in the last feast cycle, making it invaluable evidence.
- **Regional names:** great hosts, belly kings, table fathers, and the never-sated.
- **Story uses:** end a siege by feeding defenders from the besiegers' future harvests; rule an addiction network through genuine hospitality; invite heroes to save one village by choosing another as the winter course.

## Boundary with Natural Decay

Feast-beasts can exploit rot, infestation, or plague, but they do not cause those ecologies by default. [[Veradia]] governs infection and proliferating life; [[Morvalis]] governs bodily death and decomposition; Material hunger remains a physical condition before it becomes an Idol architecture.
