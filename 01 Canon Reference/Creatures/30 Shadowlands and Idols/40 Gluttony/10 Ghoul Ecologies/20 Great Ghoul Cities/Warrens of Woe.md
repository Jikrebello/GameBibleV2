# Warrens of Woe

The **Warrens of Woe** occupy caverns and burial systems beneath the Merewick fens and eastern [[Wanemoor District]]. Brauvalian grave-watch folklore calls them **the Fever Deeps**.

The name describes a regional threat, not a publicly mapped state. Most officials believe the Fever Deeps are an explanation assembled from unrelated grave breaches. They are wrong about the city's existence and right that no surviving map proves its full extent.

## Material Body

The Warrens grew where fen sinkholes, iron workings, plague burials, dissolved monasteries, and older tomb routes repeatedly crossed. Warm groundwater, peat gas, abundant animals, and generations of unrecorded poor burials made a durable subterranean ecology.

Its physical districts include:

- fever nurseries where living infection is cultivated;
- training cuts built for grave-runners and surface hunters;
- drowned ossuaries accessible only during low fen water;
- corpse chutes beneath abandoned parish and manor sites;
- workshops producing grave-wax, contaminated bait, and false burial goods;
- contested roads toward the lower Weiss and Wanemoor mines.

## Feast-Shadow: The Fever Cradle

The corresponding Shadowlands district is organized around the **Fever Cradle**, a pulsing architecture of first feedings, infection dreams, and repeated burial panic. Material passages correspond to stages of appetite rather than distance.

The Cradle can project fever-dreams and guide infected victims toward openings. It cannot capture or reroute their human souls. Living victims remain treatable until mature transformation.

## Rule and Rivalry

Fever-keepers, hunting lineages, and corpse-route elders compete over how quickly the city should expand. One faction wants controlled predation that preserves surface supply. Another treats every epidemic as a mandate for immediate harvest.

The city wars with Nihil over stolen plague records and with the Under-Cairns over elite Grave-Runner bloodlines. It is not subordinate to the Devourer, although its institutions reproduce Gluttony's feeding economy.

## The Maw-Road and Wane-Warren

The [[The Seal of the Shut Mouth|Maw-Road]] is a damaged western feeder of the Fever Deeps ecology. It does not provide a direct open march into the great city. Collapses, seals, hostile lineages, and displaced Shadowlands correspondence interrupt the route.

[[The Wane-Warren]] descends from lineages that broke away from Fever Deeps hunting stock. It is politically independent, locally adapted, and not a province awaiting orders. Some Wane elders deny the connection; others use it to claim prestige they cannot enforce.

## Access and Signs

- fever cases share dreams of the same warm stair;
- peat pools release grave wax and worked bone after low water;
- cemetery subsidence forms a sequence rather than one collapse;
- grave-runners carry marks from training cuts not found locally;
- burial roads on old maps end at parishes that never existed;
- mourners hear feeding noises beneath flooded ground during frost.

## Threat Pattern

The Fever Deeps cultivates supply. It targets sextons, burial registers, hospitals, plague pits, mine deaths, and communities willing to conceal missing bodies. Surface agents create dependency by offering disposal, forbidden medicine, or protection from rival ghouls.

## Permanent Defeat

Permanent destruction requires collapsing the warm-water corpse routes, destroying the Material fever nurseries, and ending the Fever Cradle in the corresponding Shadow district before surviving keepers carry an inoculated anchor elsewhere.

Sealing one Maw-Road protects one region. It does not destroy the city, and the city's survival does not predetermine the outcome of [[The Dead Press Upward — Full Adventure]].

## Story Uses

- grave-watch orders discover several isolated outbreaks share one training doctrine;
- a healer has been suppressing fever by feeding the Cradle names instead of bodies;
- the Long Table and Fever Deeps both hunt a defector who knows how appetite was cultivated as pity;
- draining a fen would expose a city entrance and destroy the living communities above it.
