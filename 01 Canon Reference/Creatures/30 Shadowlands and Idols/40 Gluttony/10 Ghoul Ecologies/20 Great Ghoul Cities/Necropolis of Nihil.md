# Necropolis of Nihil

The **Necropolis of Nihil** is the largest of the three great ghoul cities. Its Material body occupies a salt-mirror ruin basin eleven days east-northeast of [[Qasr Ilyun]] by a dry-year track. It is separate from [[Bitter Crown Basin]] and the Palace of the Last Audience.

Qahuri ruin-workers call the legend **the Hollow City**. Jirahar warning records call it **the City After the Last Name**. Neither name is a reliable map reference.

## Material Body

Nihil is built through the mortuary levels of a pre-Exodus human subject-city that served an unknown Giant imperial client. Plague, predation, and administrative abandonment destroyed the living settlement before the Great Exodus reached the Glass frontier. The site is not a lost Giant capital.

Salt has preserved bodies, inscriptions, sealed chambers, and civic archives with terrible effectiveness. Ghoul lineages entered through burial shafts over many generations and turned the dead city's processional avenues into corpse markets and feeding precincts.

The physical city includes:

- salt galleries containing named and unnamed dead;
- dry wells descending into habitation caverns;
- archives sorted by family, disease, and remembered taste;
- surface sinkholes concealed beneath mirror crust;
- ruin workshops that extract glass, grave metal, and preserved tissue;
- outer lineages that guide expeditions toward Qasr Ilyun and caravan graves.

## Feast-Shadow: The Last Name

Nihil's Shadowlands body is an enormous district where a person's name appears as a place setting. Every stolen register, erased lineage, and consumed memory adds another street to the Feast-shadow.

Its central organ is the **Table of the Last Name**, a distributed archive-feast corresponding to thousands of Material tablets and preserved remains. The table does not contain whole human souls. It contains residue, attachments, stolen records, and Gluttony-patterns organized into a predatory memory system.

## Rule and Rivalry

Nihil is governed by competing elder tables rather than one monarch. Status follows access to old names, controllable corpse-roads, preserved lineages, and the ability to feed without exhausting a source.

The city regards the Fever Deeps as reckless breeders and the Under-Cairns as provincial hunters. Both rivals regard Nihil's claim to antiquity as theft from the civilization whose corpses it occupies.

The Devourer shapes the Feast-shadow's appetite but does not issue daily commands. Some elders worship it; others insist the city has surpassed religion while reproducing its logic exactly.

## Access and Signs

No reliable route remains open year-round. Access appears through:

- salt crust collapsing over a dry well;
- a recovered funerary tablet that points toward its missing archive shelf;
- caravans following tracks made by their own dead animals days earlier;
- a Shadowlands overlap after mass death on the Glass frontier;
- ghoul guides offering water that preserves thirst rather than satisfying it.

Approach signs include names vanishing from manifests, graves drying instead of decaying, salt figures set like diners, and a horizon that reflects ruins not present on the Material plain.

## Threat Pattern

Nihil prefers acquisition to open conquest. Its agents steal registers, genealogists, grave maps, plague records, and persons whose memories unlock old districts. Surface cults may believe they purchase ancestral truth. The city treats every recovered identity as food, leverage, and infrastructure.

## Permanent Defeat

Nihil can recur while either the Material archive or the Table of the Last Name survives. Permanent destruction requires breaking the salt-preserved archive network and the Feast-shadow table during the same collapse window, then restoring or truthfully recording the names that can still be recovered.

Burning the surface ruins alone seals some entrances and destroys evidence while leaving the deeper city intact.

## Story Uses

- an expedition returns with accurate memories belonging to families no one sent it to study;
- the city steals a tower register to open a Jirahar burial route that never physically reached the Glass;
- Qasr Ilyun must evacuate a sinkhole district without revealing why names are disappearing from its court rolls;
- a lost pre-Exodus history can be recovered only from the table that has been eating it for centuries.
