# The Militis

Militis are the banner bloodline: retained-soul vampires whose Giant inheritance specializes in endurance, predatory war-forms, and obedience carried through blood. They may possess real courage and loyalty. Those goods become corrupt when used to defend blood hierarchy, enforce human servitude, or excuse atrocities as duty.

## Origin and Lifecycle

A Militis is made through near-death, a sire's blood, and a vigil beside a weapon, standard, gate, or person the recipient is charged to defend. The waking lien ties appetite to service. When threatened, the vampire can thicken bone, plate skin, extend limbs, and redirect blood into short bursts of speed or strength.

Militis houses feed like other vampires and often organize supply as military ration. Their lineage-anchors are battle standards, muster rolls, fortress stones, weapons carried through several generations, or the living commander to whom a cohort has bound itself.

## Signs

- sentries keep impossible watches without eating, sleeping, or changing post;
- old blood appears in weapon grooves after each new battle;
- a garrison's wounded recover while prisoners grow steadily weaker;
- footprints deepen abruptly where an apparently human soldier entered a war-form;
- veterans obey an officer whose face appears unchanged across a century of portraits.

## Society and Agency

Militis form cohorts, household guards, wandering companies, and solitary oath-keepers. Some genuinely protect communities abandoned by mortal rulers. Protection does not confer ownership, and a freely accepted watch can become predatory when departure, dissent, or ordinary mortality is forbidden.

## Threat Pattern and Tactics

They reconnoiter, establish fallback lines, and force opponents to fight through marked ground. Militis use disciplined mortal troops, sudden bodily transformation, blood-scent coordination, and injury transfer within a prepared cohort. They are least comfortable when separated from the person, object, or place their lien defines as the objective.

## Weakness and Permanent Defeat

Sunlight makes war-forms unstable and exposes every inherited wound. White Flame severs false duty from genuine obligation. Breaking command, freeing bound troops, and removing the defended anchor deprive a Militis of reinforcement. Destroying the body alone permits return through standard or muster. Permanent defeat breaks the blood lien and releases the retained soul.

## Materials and Remains

Militis bone plates, blood-hardened leather, and weapons can retain command impulses after death. Cleansed material makes durable armor or forensic evidence; uncleansed trophies can mark a bearer as the cohort's next objective or commander. Muster rolls often contain the names required for release.

## Regional Names

Eclessian hunters call them **banner-bloods**; Thalmyric soldiers say **red lances**; frontier speech uses **night wardens**. Militis is the creator-facing bloodline name.
