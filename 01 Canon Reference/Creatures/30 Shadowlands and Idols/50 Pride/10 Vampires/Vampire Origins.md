# Vampire Origins

## The Ansharan Source

Vampirism began in the Imperial Court of Anshara. Its Vampire Giants cultivated blood-memory, bodily continuity, and coercive life-binding under the influence of [[Pride|the Sovereign]]. They were not Pride's offspring, and no Idol created a new soul-bearing lineage. Their embodied Giant continuity and their blood craft predated the mortal bloodlines that survive today.

Ansharan court theology presented Giant blood as proof of divine right. Authorially, it was a predatory technology and inherited bodily condition: it could preserve memory, impose obedience, transfer vitality, and hold a recipient's human soul inside a body that should have died. Pride strengthened the hierarchy and fed upon its chosen domination, but the Giants built and maintained the system themselves.

## The First Human Vampires

The Ansharan Giants created human vampires for service, punishment, intimacy, succession, and experiment. Some recipients consented in pursuit of rank or longevity; others were forced. Consent did not make the act benign. Giant blood established a lien that prevented the recipient's genuine human soul from routing at bodily death.

A human vampire therefore remains a person with a `retained-human-soul`. This is the heart of the condition's horror. The vampire is neither a corpse residue nor a new soulless species: the original person remains present, morally accountable, and capable of suffering while appetite and blood-law continually pressure choice.

The blood does not compel every vampire to identical conduct. It creates hunger, inherited commands, and an affinity for hierarchical possession. Feeding on unwilling people, making new vampires, and enforcing blood servitude remain chosen evils even when refusal is painful.

## The Five Bloodlines

Centuries of descent, deliberate breeding, and adaptation divided the mortal inheritance into five stable bloodlines. Their specialized gifts are diminished portions of the broader Ansharan condition.

- **Regalis:** command, rank, household dominion, and inherited legitimacy.
- **Nocturnis:** concealment, intelligence work, hidden movement, and guarded memory.
- **Sanguine:** blood rite, appetite, vitality transfer, and ritual lineage.
- **Eclipsis:** appearance, reputation, social perception, and cultivated absence.
- **Militis:** violence, endurance, protection of hierarchy, and martial obedience.

These are blood inheritances rather than moral destinies or separate peoples. Individual vampires may resist the social role associated with a bloodline, but none escapes the retained-soul condition merely through good intention.

## The Drinking Wars

Anshara did not end in one outside conquest. Mortal vampire houses rebelled, Giant courts drank rivals and their own servants, and competing bloodlines tried to inherit the hierarchy that had made them. [[The Fall of Anshara]] controls the full authorial history; public tradition establishes only that the empire consumed itself and the final sovereign's fate remains unknown.

The Drinking Wars spread mortal vampires beyond Anshara while destroying most reliable knowledge of the first transformations. Later houses often call their inheritance a gift, curse, sacrament, disease, or noble blood. None of those claims changes its authorial continuity.

## Death and Release

Destroying a vampire's body does not annihilate its soul. A true permanent defeat must break the active Giant blood lien so the retained human soul can resume its route toward judgement. White Flame, correctly performed release rites, and destruction of a bloodline's controlling anchor can accomplish this in different circumstances. A merely dispersed vampire may rebuild through blood, earth, a prepared vessel, or a surviving lineage-anchor.

No mortal rite can promise the vampire salvation or reverse final judgement. Release restores the soul-road; it does not decide its destination.
