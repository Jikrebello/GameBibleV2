# Vampires in the Present Age (1360 AR)

The synthesis dossier: what vampires are in World Egg terms, where the five bloodlines stand on the present board, and why every weakness in the folklore is actually a law. Builds on [[Vampire Origins]] and [[Bloodlines Overview]]; family placement in [[Bestiary Framework — Creatures of the World Egg]].

## What a Vampire Is (cosmologically)

A vampire is **a human soul held out of routing by a Giant-derived blood lien**. Ansharan blood, cultivated beneath Pride's influence and divided through five lines, anchors the recipient's soul to the body past death. Vampires are therefore not undead in the ghoul's sense, where appetite and residue remain after the soul is gone. They are the opposite horror: **the human soul remains entire and imprisoned**. Every vampire is a continuing violation of the death-route, which is why the Church's hunt is doctrine rather than simple monster-fear and why elder vampires dread the Weighing they have postponed.

## Why the Folklore Works (weaknesses as rival law)

- **Sunlight** burns them because it is **Father Sun's jurisdiction**: exposure makes the hidden blood-claim answer to a stronger natural law. (The Ättar, who know Solzareth's character, consider vampire-burning the one unambiguously kind thing the day-law does.)
- **Thresholds and invitation:** an actively kept hearth carries old Faewild boundary law. Ansharan blood cannot cross that claim at full strength until a resident admits it. Vampires therefore cultivate charm, dependence, fear, and despair because each can produce an invitation without making it morally free.
- **White Flame, consecration, sincere prayer:** coherence against the lien. The Flame does not merely hurt a vampire; it exposes the imprisoned human soul and weakens what holds it.
- **Running water:** this is regional rather than universal. Where local Thalassor law strongly governs passage, old blood may need a vessel, bridge, or invitation to cross at full strength. Elsewhere the custom has no effect beyond what the vampire expects or exploits.

## The Five Bloodlines on the 1360 Board

- **The Regalis** (rule) — old-money apex predators of court and lineage. Thickest in **Sestara and the Latinate Eclessian south**: patient shares in Medori instruments, marriages into indebted noble houses, and a three-century whisper (never proven, never quite killed) about one Fiorenzian gallery patron who has "retired to the country" eleven times.
- **The Nocturnis** (secrets) — the unseen hand. **Sermora's espionage market** is their garden; the Admiralty's best rumor-brokers have never met their ultimate client. In the schism-borderlands they sell to both churches, which both suspect it and both keep paying.
- **The Sanguine** (blood-rite) — lineage ritualists, physicians, and keepers of blood-memory. They maintain the **Memory of Anshara**, the strongest surviving internal tradition concerning the fifth empire. Vorthara's Red Covenant would trade a wing of the Citadel for one honest examination of it.
- **The Eclipsis** (masks) — the masquerade's wardens: perception-weavers managing the great concealment. Their present crisis is the **Freeflame**: congregational churches with tested elders and no fixed rite are far harder to infiltrate than hierarchies with predictable liturgy. Reformed duchies are quietly becoming vampire-hostile terrain, and no one mortal knows why the Eclipsis lobby (through many removes) *against* reform.
- **The Militis** (war) — the lines' sword: dueling codes, night-war doctrine, terror-transformations. Some sell discipline to mercenary companies under mortal masks; an old Militis under-cult persists in **Aversia's** officer schools as a "hard tradition" nobody examines closely.

**In Covenant lands** all five lines fare badly for a beautifully bureaucratic reason: **the Vault of Names has no entry for the dead who walk.** A vampire under Covenant law is an unregistered party — contract-incapable, protection-ineligible, and prey to any binder who learns its true name. The Qahirid breach-courts treat elder vampires as *found assets*. Vampires call the Covenant "the land of open ledgers" and mostly stay out.

## History in Brief (wired to the AR spine)

Survivors of Anshara's self-consumption scattered with the Scattering, hiding in the courts of every age. The Atherian centuries were their golden age — Star Pantheon courts, provincial villas, the long peace. **The Redeemer was their apocalypse**: the White Flame could expose and break the blood lien, and the Underground Centuries were a two-front war the mortal chronicles never saw — elder vampires burned by saints whose names the Church remembers for other reasons. The Fall of Caleran (905) and the Reconquest fed the lines for a century; the Reformation is starving the Eclipsis now. The bloodlines' own reckoning dates from Anshara's fall and is kept by the Sanguine alone, who will tell you the current year in it only if you are family or dinner.

## Signs

A household's debts consolidating quietly toward one distant creditor; servants with old, parallel scars and serene loyalty; a noble line whose portraits repeat a face every third generation; cellars renovated by outside masons; a district where despair is *organized*; and the Church-sign the hunters trust most — **hymns going flat in one house**: ordinary clergy's mint-hymns failing for no visible reason, because righteousness is present but *jurisdiction* is contested.

## Loot and Materials

Vampire ash is potent, illegal, and theologically dangerous because it is the remains of a body that held a genuine human soul. Heart's-blood taken at destruction is valuable to both the Sanguine and the Fleshweavers. Ansharan grave-goods and court regalia preserve rare evidence of the fifth empire. Most important, a captured vampire is a **human soul that can still, in principle, be released to the proper route**. Exorcism-restoration is the hardest mercy in the hunter's manual, the Church's official preference, and almost never what happens in a torch-lit cellar.
