# Pride Masks and Court-Beasts

Pride's ecology feeds upon recognition detached from truth. Its forms make an existing hierarchy feel metaphysically inevitable and then remove the duties that once justified it.

## Acclaim Swarms

An Acclaim Swarm is a flock of hand-sized glass throats, jeweled eyelids, and bright feather-like shards. It circles a favored figure and reproduces the praise, applause, salutes, or devotional responses of nearby witnesses.

- **Origin and agency:** reflexive Idol fauna with animal intelligence and no moral agency.
- **Ecology:** swarms feed where recognition is required to flow toward one person regardless of deed. They nest in portraits, medals, balconies, pulpits, and ceremonial uniforms.
- **Signs:** applause heard in empty rooms, compliments repeated in unfamiliar voices, portraits brightening as collaborators fade, and birds refusing to land above a dais.
- **Threat pattern:** blinds critics with reflected attention, magnifies a leader's commands, drowns contrary testimony, and peels acclaim from one person to feed another.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** restore correct attribution publicly, separate office from occupant, and shatter the nesting object while witnesses name the overlooked contributors. Mockery and humiliation merely reverse the swarm's attention.
- **Materials:** acclaim glass replays the loudest praise it consumed and can expose staged crowds or coerced testimony.
- **Regional names:** praise flies, mirror birds, bright mouths, and court starlings.
- **Story uses:** make an incompetent commander irresistible; reveal whose labor a saintly reputation concealed; migrate from a deposed tyrant to the hero who defeated him.

## Mirror Heralds

Mirror Heralds are Masks with polished faces and ceremonial bodies assembled from the symbols of an office. They announce rank, certify succession, and edit the memories of witnesses until ceremony appears to prove moral worth.

- **Origin and agency:** cunning Mask with limited agency, anchored to a title, portrait cycle, coronation object, or repeated acclamation.
- **Ecology:** a Herald requires an audience and an office that has stopped accepting correction. It can speak real law accurately while concealing limits on the office.
- **Signs:** witnesses remember the same flattering detail, mirrors show regalia absent from the room, seals authenticate documents they never touched, and subordinates forget who gave a successful warning.
- **Threat pattern:** commands through borrowed office, redirects attacks as breaches of protocol, makes allies perceive correction as treason, and steps between reflective surfaces.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** establish the office's true jurisdiction, restore erased witnesses, obtain correction from someone the office must hear, and break the ceremonial reflection anchor. Killing the office-holder does not end the Herald.
- **Materials:** a face shard shows which details it added to a witness's memory. It cannot reveal what truly happened without other evidence.
- **Regional names:** silver heralds, rank mirrors, bright confessors, and the second chamberlain.
- **Story uses:** validate two mutually exclusive heirs; preserve a beloved ruler's authority after death; turn a church inquiry into proof of the accused prelate's sanctity.

## Throne-Born

Throne-Born are autonomous Archmasks that arise already seated in an office they claim has always existed. Their forms range from radiant monarchs to faceless judges and military saints. A Throne-Born builds a court around the proposition that relation to it determines every person's worth.

- **Origin and agency:** sapient Archmask with full moral agency. It understands delegated authority and deliberately substitutes itself as the source.
- **Ecology:** it cultivates beneficiaries, invisible labor, ritual opposition, and carefully permitted critics. Even rebellion can become evidence that the throne defines the world.
- **Signs:** maps acquire a capital before the ruler arrives, people kneel at empty thresholds, older laws gain the Archmask's title, and public works lose the names of their makers.
- **Threat pattern:** grants and revokes social reality, embodies its palace or command post, turns acclaim into armor, and returns through any claimant who accepts its throne as the source of legitimacy.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** expose the true source and limits of authority, restore diminished persons, let rightful office accept correction, and destroy or vacate the throne without creating a new object of self-worship. A mere coup feeds succession back into the same ecology.
- **Materials:** throne dust reveals every person treated as invisible support for the regime.
- **Regional names:** unmade kings, first judges, crown ghosts, and the seated ones.
- **Story uses:** stabilize a collapsing state more effectively than its lawful rulers; offer a church perfect unity around a counterfeit office; survive deposition by making the revolutionaries define themselves through its throne.

## Relation to Vampires

Vampires are human soul-holders created through Ansharan Giant blood, not court masks. Pride finds their retained identity and predatory sovereignty especially fertile, but every vampire remains accountable as a human person under a distinct continuity.
