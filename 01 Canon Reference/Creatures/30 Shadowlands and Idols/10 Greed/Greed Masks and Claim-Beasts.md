# Greed Masks and Claim-Beasts

These forms grow where possession has been separated from use and obligation. Their presence does not prove that every disputed claim is false. Greed succeeds by hiding predation inside real ownership, real scarcity, and valid institutions.

## Claim-Mites

Claim-Mites are coin-sized Shadow fauna with ivory shells, key-shaped legs, and mouthparts that print tiny ownership marks. They gather where an object, room, body of water, or inheritance has been left unused but fiercely withheld.

- **Origin and agency:** reflexive Idol fauna with animal intelligence and no moral agency.
- **Ecology:** mites feed on the gap between control and use. A colony reproduces by dividing a disputed thing into smaller exclusive claims until no one can act without trespass.
- **Signs:** duplicate seals, gnawed keys, hairline property marks on skin, stores counted more often than opened, and small objects migrating into locked containers.
- **Threat pattern:** swarms mark equipment as belonging elsewhere, jam access, lead guards toward apparent thieves, and make allies perceive one another as possessors of stolen goods.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** open the hoard to its proper use or settle the true custody under witnesses, then burn or wash away every mite-mark. Killing the insects while the withheld good remains creates a new brood.
- **Materials:** intact shells hold one temporary claim-mark and are used by honest investigators as tracers. Under-Ledger agents prize live colonies for sabotage.
- **Regional names:** tally lice, key-ants, miser's teeth, and little claimers.
- **Story uses:** trace a hidden warehouse; complicate a legitimate inheritance; expose relief supplies diverted by a respected official.

## Hoardlings

Hoardlings are Masks assembled from the things a hoard prevents others from using. A mine Hoardling may be plated in unused tools; a granary form wears grain sacks as flesh; a collector's form speaks through the mouths of stolen portraits. It calls itself keeper, heir, curator, or protector.

- **Origin and agency:** cunning projected Mask with limited agency, anchored to a specific hoard and the mortal practice maintaining it.
- **Ecology:** it catalogs, cleans, defends, and enlarges the hoard. It can offer accurate provenance and may expose ordinary thieves, which makes it useful before its definition of theft expands.
- **Signs:** objects arranged by value rather than function, a caretaker heard behind sealed walls, protective traps that spare only the recorded owner, and dust-free goods no one has touched.
- **Threat pattern:** animates possessions, locks exits, separates bearers from equipment, offers a needed item at the price of placing another person inside the collection, and flees by redistributing itself through the hoard.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** identify the goods whose denied use anchors the Mask, return or lawfully distribute them, and break the keeper-token. Random looting disperses the body but strengthens Greed's claim.
- **Materials:** keeper-tokens preserve provenance without proving ownership. Their testimony is useful but metaphysically biased.
- **Regional names:** brass keepers, vault children, tool saints, and sealed cousins.
- **Story uses:** guard medicine during a plague; reveal a museum's stolen foundation; become an apparently helpful guide through an occupied mine.

## Gilded Claimants

Gilded Claimants are autonomous Archmasks that appear as tall, beautifully dressed persons whose skin is layered with deeds, brands, keys, and hammered gold. Each claims a jurisdiction rather than one object: all salvage from a war, all water below a road, all children of a purchased household, or every memory made inside a palace.

- **Origin and agency:** sapient Archmask with full moral agency. It chooses its claims, understands suffering, and cultivates dependence because ownership delights it.
- **Ecology:** a Claimant establishes a court of beneficiaries, dispossessed people, Hoardlings, and mortal agents. Every accepted privilege makes the jurisdiction more Material.
- **Signs:** strangers use the same possessive phrase, maps gain borders no surveyor drew, gifts return bearing liens, and doors bow inward when the Claimant approaches.
- **Threat pattern:** separates people from equipment or legal standing, weaponizes genuine debts, embodies claimed architecture, and cannot be permanently injured while witnesses continue treating its central claim as real.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** expose the chain by which custody became domination, restore named people and goods, secure truthful witness from at least one beneficiary, and destroy the central claim-medium while the Archmask is embodied. A Claimant driven away without restitution re-enters through its oldest unchallenged asset.
- **Materials:** claim-gold remembers every hand that accepted benefit from the regime. It is powerful evidence and a dangerous relic.
- **Regional names:** gilt lords, asset kings, last buyers, and the owners beneath.
- **Story uses:** rule a company town more effectively than its human founders; purchase the battlefield dead from both armies; offer refugees perfect safety as inheritable property.

## Faction Use and Response

The Under-Ledger uses mites as probes, Hoardlings as deniable warders, and Claimants only when prepared to become a junior partner. Churches distinguish restitution from indiscriminate destruction of property. Secular courts often need supernatural proof that a valid title has become an Idol anchor.
