# Grave-Runners

Grave-Runners are animalistic, quadrupedal ghoul-corrupted dead created when a corpse or dying victim is exposed to concentrated ghoul-fray and then physically mauled. They translate the festrogs of *Hungry Are the Dead* into the World Egg bestiary.

## Encounter Role

Grave-Runners are fast pursuit predators. They break a defensive line, knock down an isolated target, and drag bodies toward a warren or breach. They are most effective in grave rows, forest roads, mine galleries, and ruined interiors where speed lets them attack from an unexpected direction.

They should make an undead encounter move. They are not stationary hit-point obstacles.

## Creation

A Grave-Runner forms through three conditions:

1. a person or corpse is saturated by ghoul-fever or intense soul-route fray;
2. a ghoul, scavenger animal, or deliberate handler tears and reshapes the body;
3. residue animates the damaged form around hunger, pursuit, and the last physical struggle.

This process does not recall the person's soul. What rises is corrupted residue in a corpse altered for predation. A living victim exposed early enough can still be treated for ghoul-fever; death and completed animation mark the ordinary point beyond which saving the person is no longer possible.

## Form

The spine arches, shoulders thicken, and limbs turn outward until the creature can run on hands and feet. Hair and loose skin are often worn away. Swollen joints and disease-filled lesions split when the creature is struck. Human clothing, work gear, or burial cloth may remain caught around the torso.

Despite their animal posture, many Grave-Runners retain practical fragments: opening a familiar latch, following a work path, hiding where the body once sheltered, or responding to a name for an instant. These are residue habits, not proof that the person remains trapped.

## Intelligence and Society

Most Grave-Runners possess hunting intelligence and can coordinate a pack, recognize traps, and retreat. They do not maintain independent communities. Ghoul warrens use them as scouts, corpse haulers, pursuit hounds, and expendable surface hunters.

Rare elder specimens recover speech and tool use. These are usually called **grave-runners** as a lineage rather than **Grave-Runners** as a common creature, with [[Mournfang]] as the known Wanemoor example.

## Signs and Lair

- human handprints alternating with deep toe gouges;
- graves or bodies dragged low and fast rather than carried;
- lesions smeared against knee-height stone;
- familiar doors opened from outside during an attack;
- a bitten victim left alive as a disease carrier;
- pack routes that circle toward a corpse chute or warren entrance.

They do not build true lairs. Packs shelter in drainage works, empty graves, mine cuts, collapsed houses, or the outer runs of a ghoul warren.

## Threat Pattern

- sprint on all fours to cross open ground;
- charge and knock a target prone;
- bite or hook clothing, then drag toward darkness;
- rupture diseased lesions when struck at close range;
- feed briefly on a fallen body to restore damaged residue;
- retreat when light, consecration, or formation denies isolation.

Handlers use one pack to force movement and another to wait along the retreat.

## Countermeasures

- narrow stakes, caltrops, and waist-high barriers break their charge;
- polearms and shields deny the first knockdown;
- bright maintained light makes coordinated ambush harder;
- immediate washing, burning of contaminated cloth, and trained treatment reduce ghoul-fever risk;
- consecrated cemetery boundaries repel them when properly maintained;
- destroying a Grave-Runner does not harm the soul of the body it uses.

## Materials and Loot

Grave-Runner remains are dangerous evidence rather than valuable treasure. Lesion fluid can help identify a parent warren or recent feeding source, but transporting it is a public-health risk. Grave-script collars, drag harnesses, or marked teeth may identify handlers.

Church and civic authorities usually require the remains to be burned or reinterred after examination. Fleshweavers and illegal corpse-workers may pay for intact specimens, creating a secondary moral and containment problem.

## Quest Hooks

- a runner pack follows the same road at the same hour each night because the bodies once worked that route;
- a living bite victim must be reached before the fever completes its course;
- handlers use runners to steal only recently buried members of one family;
- a speaking elder claims to preserve a victim's memory but demonstrates only residue fragments;
- runners escaping a closed warren lead grave-watch to an unknown secondary route.
