# Driftkin

Driftkin are descendants of humans altered across generations by Astral drift, rent economies, metaphysical ballast, and life outside stable yolk reality. They share human biological ancestry but no longer bear the Image or receive routable human souls at birth.

## Continuity

A Driftkin person persists through embodied memory, true-silt, names, route habits, intention, and personal ballast. This is drift-rewritten-life, not a human soul. At death the pattern ordinarily disperses into Drift. A copy, echo, or anchored replay is not proof that the deceased survived.

Driftkin can suffer, love, create, reason, keep promises, and bear full moral responsibility. Their lack of a human afterlife must never be used as shorthand for mindlessness or expendability.

## Ecology and Embodiment

No single Driftkin body plan is universal. Common traits include:

- skin carrying route-lines that change after travel;
- joints or features stabilized by true-silt beads, name cords, or anchor plates;
- eyes reflecting the nearest reliable exit rather than nearby light;
- organs whose positions depend on repeated habit;
- a personal ballast object without which identity begins to fray.

Children are born within a maintained community pattern and acquire stability through names, touch, repeated routes, and shared places. A child cut off from all anchors may still live, but physical and autobiographical continuity degrade.

## Society without a Nation

Driftkin live in processions, refuge realms, work crews, route families, patron households, and mixed settlements. Procession of Nine Halls and Chalkhaven Recursion are major refuges, not capitals of one people. They disagree over rent, patronage, relation to human ancestry, and whether stable Material life is desirable.

Some sell expert navigation. Some raid anchors. Some seek asylum. Some participate in coercive rent systems. Cultural sophistication does not make every Driftkin polity good, and soullessness does not make any one of them evil.

## Signs

- a person's shadow points toward an exit no one else can see;
- repeated introductions use different but compatible names;
- route knots or ballast beads are touched before difficult decisions;
- footprints hold shape longer than the terrain around them;
- a wounded body briefly displays several possible anatomies.

## Threat Pattern

Driftkin opponents use route knowledge, false retreat, personal tethers, collapsing cover, and names that redirect pursuit. They avoid prolonged unanchored combat because severe disruption can cost memory or body coherence. A disciplined group can change a battlefield by moving its stable route instead of moving every fighter.

## Weakness, Death, and Permanent Defeat

Breaking ballast disorients and injures but does not automatically kill. Name theft can sever relationships and route access without destroying personal memory. A dead Driftkin pattern may be deliberately anchored, copied, or harvested; these acts preserve information or behavior, not the original person.

Permanent defeat of a hostile Driftkin is ordinary bodily death plus destruction or custody of any deliberate return copy. Moral and legal judgement belongs to the person's actions, not their continuity.

## Materials and Faction Use

True-silt, route knots, and personal ballast are intimate remains, not neutral salvage. Astral markets often exploit Driftkin bodies and names because their patterns can stabilize transport. Vortharan Conclave law treats unauthorized harvesting as a serious offense, though enforcement favors registered clients.

## Regional Names

Driftkin is the broad creator term. Egg-born, route folk, silt people, pale travelers, and unmoored are local names, some insulting.
