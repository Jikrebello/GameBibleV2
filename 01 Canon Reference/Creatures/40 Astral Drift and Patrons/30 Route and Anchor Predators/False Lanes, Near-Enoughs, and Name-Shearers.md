# False Lanes, Near-Enoughs, and Name-Shearers

These native Astral patterns prey on the acts by which travelers establish destination, correspondence, and identity. They are not malicious roads, mischievous genies, or fragments of a universal route god.

## False Lanes

False Lanes are predatory paths that imitate a known current and survive by being chosen. Their visible form may be a bridge, wind, stair, procession, familiar road, or sequence of correct navigational signs.

- **Origin and agency:** cunning astral-pattern with no moral agency.
- **Ecology:** a Lane grows from abandoned tethers, copied route instructions, and repeated near-misses. Every traveler who accepts it makes the imitation more stable.
- **Signs:** all landmarks are correct but arrive in the wrong emotional order, outbound markers face inward, and route memories become easier the less they are checked.
- **Threat pattern:** separates travelers by presenting a personally convincing path, loops movement into upkeep, and deposits victims near a predator or failed realm once their anchors weaken.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** compare two independent route methods, stop moving when the Lane rewards confidence, trace its first divergence, and sever the dead tether it inhabits. Merely turning around may become another branch.
- **Materials:** lane skin stores a single reliable correspondence after the predator is closed.
- **Regional names:** sweet roads, easy currents, homeward lies, and the nearly straight.
- **Story uses:** hijack a rescue route; lead smugglers somewhere safer but owned; reveal that a famous shortcut has been feeding on missing pilgrims.

## Near-Enoughs

Near-Enoughs are destination mimics that build a place from what arriving travelers expect to find. A copied inn, city gate, family home, refuge, or patron hall is accurate enough to invite rest and wrong enough to keep the traveler correcting it.

- **Origin and agency:** cunning Astral pattern with limited agency. It adapts to expectation but cannot invent details no visitor supplies.
- **Ecology:** it feeds on arrival certainty, personal corrections, and the metaphysical mass travelers spend making the copy feel right.
- **Signs:** a familiar person knows only anticipated answers, repaired details worsen elsewhere, private rooms have public geometry, and every exit leads back to a more accurate copy.
- **Threat pattern:** isolates the person whose expectations provide the strongest map, creates helpful inhabitants, consumes return tethers during sleep, and weaponizes attempts to correct the environment.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** introduce an independently verified detail no traveler expected, refuse to repair the copy, locate the blank space behind its most accurate room, and collapse the imitation from there.
- **Materials:** near-stuff can make a temporary prop perfectly convincing to one named witness.
- **Regional names:** almost homes, kind arrivals, second inns, and close-enoughs.
- **Story uses:** shelter refugees who know it is false but have nowhere safer; copy a city from a spy's biased expectations; convince a party they completed an expedition before they reached its destination.

## Name-Shearers

Name-Shearers are long, jointed route predators with scissor-like heads and bodies composed of dangling address fragments. They hunt the attachments between a being and the names by which routes, people, and institutions recognize it.

- **Origin and agency:** animal-to-cunning astral-pattern with limited agency.
- **Ecology:** they gather around stolen aliases, overcrowded registries, mass renaming, and routes carrying many people under one identity.
- **Signs:** companions know a person but cannot call to them, doors accept an old title and reject the living bearer, signatures split at the first letter, and labels fall from containers holding valuable anchors.
- **Threat pattern:** cut social and navigational attachments, herd nameless prey off stable routes, wear severed addresses as camouflage, and flee through any destination that no longer recognizes its owner.
- **Weakness and permanent defeat:** establish a name through living witnesses and embodied memory rather than a single document, trap the Shearer between two valid forms of address, and break its jaw while returning the severed attachments.
- **Materials:** address joints can reconnect one object to a known destination. They cannot create a true name or human soul.
- **Regional names:** name scissors, address wolves, uncallers, and route shearers.
- **Story uses:** strip a diplomat of every title but not memory; attack a soul-route without annihilating its travelers; expose a patron who has registered hundreds of servants under one collateral name.

## Soul Boundary

Name-Shearers can obscure, misdirect, or strip attachments from a human soul in transit. They cannot annihilate the soul, reverse judgement, or make the person cease to exist. Soul-road intervention belongs to the Boatman's commission and properly commissioned heavenly service.
