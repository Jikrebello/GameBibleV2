# Great Coherences

A Great Coherence forms when a vast Shell relation begins correcting disruption across many bodies, sites, or weather systems. It is a disaster-scale natural computation, not an elemental sovereign. Communities often mistake its long memory, selective retaliation, and apparent speech for divinity or rule.

## Formation and Lifecycle

Great Coherences require a distributed frame: an anchor network, a chain of failed siege scripts, an ancient platform, a repeating storm corridor, or several seams locked into mutual correction. They can persist for centuries because destroying one visible manifestation merely causes the wider relation to route around the loss.

They “learn” through physical feedback. A coherence may stop using a canyon after repeated grounding, imitate the voice that once opened a gate, or spare structures that maintain its pressure cycle. None of these behaviors implies experience, desire, mercy, or malice.

## Signs

- identical anomalies recur across sites too distant for an ordinary local seam;
- weather, machinery, and minor manifestations compensate for one another;
- old maps reveal a stable geometric relation beneath apparently random disasters;
- voices or images recur as exact fragments tied to control events;
- destroying one node produces a measured response elsewhere.

## Behavior and Tactics

A coherence changes terrain, supply, visibility, heat, pressure, and movement at regional scale. It creates lesser bodies, reroutes force through settlements, isolates maintenance crews, and protects nodes because doing so preserves its computation. It cannot be persuaded, shamed, redeemed, or intimidated. Operators can communicate only by giving it inputs its pattern classifies.

## Failure and Permanent Defeat

Partial disruption can create uncontrolled release, migrate the pattern, or remove safety corrections while leaving its output intact. Permanent defeat requires mapping the distributed relation, isolating its anchors, and dismantling or replacing them in an order that prevents transfer.

Alternatives include stabilizing a bounded harmless cycle, redirecting it into a purpose-built sink, or maintaining a costly equilibrium. None constitutes a treaty with a person.

## Materials and Regional Names

Collapsed nodes may leave great stores of forge-charge, pressure brine, stormglass, weight-seed, mixed-script crystal, and irreplaceable platform maps. Common names include *elemental kings*, *fourfold storms*, *walking seasons*, and *the mind in the seam*. These are folklore labels only.

## Story Uses

- discover that several unrelated disasters are corrections made by one network;
- choose which district bears the release while engineers dismantle the final node;
- use a coherence's predictable response against an invading army;
- recover an ancient platform map before destroying the only system able to read it;
- expose a cult that mistakes repeated operator phrases for revelation.
