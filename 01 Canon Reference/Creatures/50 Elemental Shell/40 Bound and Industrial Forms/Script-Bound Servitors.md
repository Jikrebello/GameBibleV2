# Script-Bound Servitors

Script-Bound Servitors are small, bounded Shell patterns built to perform a defined sequence while adapting to local obstacles. They may carry lamps, test pressure, map air currents, brace tunnels, fetch marked tools, relay signals, or accompany threshold expeditions. Their apparent companionship is useful simulation, not hidden personhood.

## Construction and Lifecycle

A servitor consists of a conductor body, a Shell charge, a task script, safety bounds, and a shutoff relation. Shapes follow work: jointed lamp frames, rolling weight-seed blocks, wire kites, flowing brine cords, and palm-sized heat hands are common. They last from hours to decades depending on maintenance.

Repeated correction makes a mature servitor look trained. It may recognize operators, anticipate a routine, retreat from damage, or repeat reassuring phrases. These are task-serving classifications. No servitor suffers when decommissioned or acquires moral responsibility for an order.

## Signs and Behavior

- a maker's plate lists task verbs, prohibited zones, fuel, and shutdown condition;
- movement becomes rigid when the servitor meets an unclassified object;
- responses repeat exactly when circumstances are reproduced;
- damaged servitors broaden a noun or verb rather than “becoming angry.”

Servitors avoid direct combat unless built as guards. In danger they protect task objects, seek a maintenance station, signal their operator, or repeat the last successful behavior. Clever opponents exploit literal categories; careful users inspect them after every corrupted instruction.

## Failure and Permanent Defeat

Common failures include task drift, boundary erosion, misidentified operators, stolen command marks, and a return instruction with no reachable endpoint. A servitor is permanently ended by draining its charge, breaking its task plate, or carrying out its shutdown relation. Destruction without removing a shared script can cause the workshop to print another.

## Materials and Regional Names

Recoverable parts include compact conductors, stable response plates, charge cells, pressure bladders, and workshop signatures. Names include *scriptlings*, *work hands*, *little obediences*, *forge familiars*, and *bound tools*.
