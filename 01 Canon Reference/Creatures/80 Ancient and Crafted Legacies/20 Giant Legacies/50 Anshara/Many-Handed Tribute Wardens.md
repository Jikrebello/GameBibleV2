# Many-Handed Tribute Wardens

Many-Handed Tribute Wardens are Ansharan court constructs built to receive gifts, inspect petitioners, and punish anyone who violates ceremonial rank. Their broad stone or metal torsos carry four to twelve articulated arms, each fitted for a distinct court function: receiving bowl, censor, measuring cord, restraining hand, ceremonial blade, seal press, or execution hook.

They are crafted machines animated by blood-responsive court architecture. They contain no soul, possess no interior life or moral agency, and are not victims awaiting release.

## Signs

- wall niches shaped for a torso with many radial arms;
- grooves at several heights where hands repeatedly crossed a floor;
- offerings sorted by material, giver, and blood trace rather than value;
- one clean processional lane amid dust and rubble;
- hand-shaped bruises or cuts arranged symmetrically on remains;
- a repeated sequence of bell tones before the Warden moves.

## Court Logic

A Warden first identifies the apparent rank of each entrant. It expects a petitioner to kneel, an envoy to present a gift, a servant to use the side lane, and a conqueror to submit weapons. Contradictory costume, title, and behavior make it pause while its arms attempt separate classifications.

This pause is a weakness, not proof that the Warden's hierarchy is legitimate. Unmarked service passages, interruption of its bell sequence, and presenting several incompatible rank signs can divide its attention.

## Tactics

- receiving arms pull carried objects away and mark them as tribute;
- restraining hands separate the highest-ranked intruder from companions;
- long lower arms sweep legs and drive petitioners toward kneeling positions;
- ceremonial weapons strike only after a refused instruction or detected theft;
- a Warden beside a processional mechanism pins intruders for a moving wall, throne, or gate rather than killing them directly;
- damaged Wardens reassign working arms and become simpler but more violent.

They do not pursue indefinitely. Most remain bound to one audience function unless directed by the Spear Cup or a recognized court officer.

## Weaknesses

- joints are accessible from the undecorated servant side, not the formal approach;
- each arm moves in a repeated order announced by bells, gears, or light;
- blood-responsive surfaces can be grounded by warlock instruments or blinded with mineral ash;
- [[White Flame Doctrine|White Flame]] does not exorcise the construct but can break the Pride-shaped authority that lets it command living bodies;
- destroying or severing the Spear Cup prevents palace-wide coordination.

## Materials

- **gesture rings:** encode one court action and can open a matching maintenance fitting;
- **blood-glass lenses:** identify warmth, blood, and court marks, but remain dangerous outside the original site;
- **joint bronze:** holds repeated motion scripts useful to engineers and warlocks;
- **tribute seals:** prove what the court received without proving that the giver consented.

## Story Uses

- a museum Warden begins sorting visitors by social rank learned from donors;
- a breach court uses one immobilized hand as a seal press until the other arms wake;
- a vampire claimant seeks a gesture ring that forces old Ansharan machines to recognize lineage;
- explorers find a Warden carefully preserving worthless personal objects while discarding gold.
