# Lunar Menagerie

The Lunar Menagerie is the family of purpose-grown organisms Nythalum engineered for low pressure, hard radiation, long dormancy, and machine service. It includes pale tracking hounds, membrane-winged maintenance gliders, vacuum barnacles, cold-blood couriers, and larger guardian beasts without counting each morph as a separate bestiary form.

## Nature and Suffering

Menagerie organisms are living nonhuman animals. They possess no routable human soul, but they feel pain, fear, attachment, and bodily need. Nythalum treated them as replaceable components. Modern handlers remain morally responsible for needless suffering even when a dangerous specimen must be killed.

## Signs and Ecology

- nests woven through cable channels rather than natural shelter;
- translucent tissue, silver capillaries, and sealed breathing sacs;
- feeding behavior keyed to machine heat, mineral paste, or lichen beds;
- hibernation triggered by particular signal tones.

Escaped populations cannot usually reproduce in ordinary Material conditions. A functioning brood cistern or adapted ruin can sustain local generations.

## Behavior and Tactics

Different morphs track heat, carry repair organisms, cling to hulls, mark intruders, or protect a machine station. They exploit darkness, vertical surfaces, vacuum pockets, and unfamiliar gravity. Command tones can redirect trained lines; feral descendants respond only to the old signals as environmental cues.

## Failure and Permanent Defeat

Destroying a brood is temporary while its cistern, eggs, or tissue printer remains. Permanent removal requires sterilizing the reproductive system and closing the habitat. Nonlethal capture, sedation, and habitat transfer are possible when time and skill permit.

## Materials and Regional Names

Useful remains include pressure membranes, radiation-dark pigment, cold enzymes, sealant mucus, and highly unethical living graft stock. Names include *moon beasts*, *silver hounds*, *star vermin*, and *cold menagerie*.

## Story Uses

- rescue living specimens from a failing return craft without letting them loose near a town;
- track a Continuant by the engineered animals maintaining its route;
- expose a fleshweaver selling menagerie tissue as miraculous medicine;
- calm a guardian line through recovered husbandry records;
- discover that a “machine infestation” is a suffering biological colony.
