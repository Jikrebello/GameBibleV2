# Covenant Siege Golems

The Qahirid Covenant maintains three distinct great-engine technologies. Outsiders commonly call all of them “demon golems,” but only one line contains captured Hell fauna. The distinction determines what suffers inside, how the engine can be stopped, and what must be released afterward.

## Depth-Core Siege Golems

Depth-Core engines imprison a captured native beast from mapped Hell inside a runic chassis. “Demon” is soldier and foundry slang, not an authorial species. The core is a living nonhuman animal capable of pain, fear, and appetite; the armor and command lattice exploit those responses.

- **Signs and purpose:** the frame breathes around sealed vents; handlers carry feeding names; impacts produce animal distress beneath metal. The line supplies mobile breach force and terror.
- **Tactics and failure:** pain commands drive charges, climbing, digging, and heat-seeking attacks. A broken name-cage may release the wounded Depth beast into the battlefield.
- **Permanent defeat and materials:** disable movement, separate command lattice from core, then contain or kill the released beast. Destroy the foundry's capture relation to prevent replacement. Salvage includes fortress plate, command chains, and dangerous Depth tissue.
- **Regional names:** *demon golems*, *pit engines*, *chained beasts*.

## Slave-Soul Siege Golems

Slave-Soul engines bind a genuine human soul. The victim is ritually executed during the foundry rite, and a name-cage intercepts the soul before routing. Consent, sentence, debt, or military oath cannot make this legitimate. The person remains a prisoner.

- **Signs and purpose:** a human name is hammered beneath every command plate; the engine dreams aloud; White Flame reveals a person-shaped obstruction inside the chassis. These engines provide adaptive response and command recognition unavailable to pure patterns.
- **Tactics and failure:** the lattice forces the prisoner to aim, move, remember routes, and recognize human deception. Cracking the cage may produce contradictory movement as the captive resists.
- **Permanent defeat and materials:** immobilize the frame, sever the command layer, and open the name-cage through White Flame, true-name work, or careful physical unbinding. Release sends the soul onward toward judgement; it does not restore the dead body. Salvage excludes the cage, which must be destroyed or sanctified.
- **Regional names:** *name engines*, *martyr irons* in Thalmyric speech, *speaking golems* among Covenant troops.

## Surrender Engines

Surrender Engines are pure strategic constructs built to break organized resistance. They contain neither soul nor animal core. Their voices, offers, hostage calculations, and apparent cruelty are optimized simulations inherited from Qahirid siege doctrine.

- **Signs and purpose:** proclamations adapt to local titles; drums predict civilian flight routes; marked stones appear where defenders will see them. Engines coordinate bombardment, false mercy, panic, and capitulation.
- **Tactics and failure:** they model morale, select symbolic targets, broadcast counterfeit orders, and command lesser frames. Damaged models may repeat an obsolete ultimatum while continuing bombardment.
- **Permanent defeat and materials:** isolate observation, corrupt its campaign model, destroy the strategic core and all field mirrors, or capture the command key. Salvage includes survey engines, signal arrays, and siege tables.
- **Regional names:** *mercy towers*, *kneeling engines*, *the speaking walls*.

## Historical Register

**Before 885.** For three centuries the Covenant fought without bound powers. The recited Covenant of the 4,444 binds its offices to dig, lift, carry, keep and guard, and says the bound "shall take nothing that is not given": the **Clause of the Given**, a translation convention whose native form belongs to the locked Covenant register. Witnessed-Seal jurists read it as barring the 4,444 from conquest, so from 568 to 885 the Covenant fought with human armies, contract logistics, pure-pattern siege engines and breach-courts.

**The Ruling on Parties, 885–886.** Qahir ibn Ruzbeh obtained from the college at Al-Qahur a ruling that the Clause binds parties, not powers. A captured Depth beast from mapped Hell was never sealed into anything. A condemned person's name is forfeit by sentence, by the jurists' claim; canon holds that no sentence makes soulbinding lawful. A pure construct has no name. The dissenting *people of the Clause* lost and are still taught at Al-Qahur. Being party to nothing, the engines cannot be voided by a breach-court either, so Atherian chanceries had no legal answer to them, only Vortharan counter-scripts.

**The foundry system, 885–902.** Qahir ibn Ruzbeh's foundry reforms between **885 and 902 AR** standardized the three lines on an industrial base:

- capture companies working route-registries of mapped Hell, compiled through the Long Frontier by Banu Mizan scholars and breach-assessors;
- name-cage foundries fed by a docket of breach-court death sentences. The first cages held Second Crusade prisoners Caleran never ransomed, condemned under the 884 Ruling of the Broken Suspension, which makes the Thalmyric *martyr irons* literal. One of the two great Slave-Soul engines at the Fall held such a prisoner;
- Shell extraction from Covenant-held anchors;
- coerced Ahdanite levies trained as engineers and foundry crews;
- supply contracts sealed in the Vault of Names.

In 905 the Surrender line existed only as lesser engines and siege tables travelling with the breach-court train. They read each district's title and offered each its own terms. No great Surrender frame yet existed; the first followed after 905.

**The Fall and Crown Gate.** Five great golems took the field during the invasion of Caleran: three Depth-Core and two Slave-Soul. They came to one span of Caleran's land wall, the Star's share, only after the Surrender train had split the city. The siege tables' span, located in a dead augur college's archive, was checked against a bounded answer from Astaroth, priced at a lesser engine frame broken under the test load ([[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]]). At the [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)|Battle of the Shattered Engines]], Vortharan specialists destroyed one Depth-Core and one Slave-Soul engine, crippled a second Depth-Core, and forced the remaining engines from the field. Their counter-scripts drew on generations of Corvenid court contracts under which Vortharan warlocks had been paid to study Covenant script.

**Afterwards.** Every replacement engine reopens the Clause. The foundry that replaces the engines lost in 907 works under the 885–886 ruling, and the people of the Clause contest each new capture.

**The great-engine ledger, 905–1190.**

| Date | Great engines | Event |
|---|---|---|
| 905 | five (three Depth-Core, two Slave-Soul) | the Fall of Caleran |
| 907 | two | three lost or abandoned at Crown Gate |
| 931 | two | the surviving Depth-Core frame walks loose through Iqashar's dyers' quarter in the Widowed Seals and is afterwards called **the Elder** |
| c. 944 | three | the first great mercy tower (Surrender Engine) finished at Iqashar by overdrawing the Chain Furnace |
| 956 | two | the last great Slave-Soul engine, sent south by sea in 955, lost before Caleran when Thalmyric war-lamps open its name-cage |
| c. 1000 | three | the second mercy tower |
| 1080s | four | the third mercy tower |
| 1131–1139 | three | one mercy tower lost in the War of the Southern Deeds |
| 1190 | three | the Elder and two mercy towers |

In 905 the Covenant had three engine yards. The Basin yard passed to Al-Qahur's military households in 938 and the forward yard in the south was lost with Caleran, so from about 960 only the Iqashar foundry builds great frames, at roughly one a generation. See [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]].

In **1360 AR**, the Qahirids retain:

- three operational great engines;
- two dormant great frames requiring major cores and rebuilding;
- one foundry capable of slowly replacing a great golem.

This is a reduced but active strategic arm, not a vanished wonder.
