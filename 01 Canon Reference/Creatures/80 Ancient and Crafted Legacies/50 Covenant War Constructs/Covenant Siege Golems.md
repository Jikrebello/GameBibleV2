# Covenant Siege Golems

The Qahirid Covenant maintains three distinct great-engine technologies. Outsiders commonly call all of them “demon golems,” but only one line contains captured Hell fauna. The distinction determines what suffers inside, how the engine can be stopped, and what must be released afterward.

## Depth-Core Siege Golems

Depth-Core engines imprison a captured native beast from mapped Hell inside a runic chassis. “Demon” is soldier and foundry slang, not an authorial species. The core is a living nonhuman animal capable of pain, fear, and appetite; the armor and command lattice exploit those responses.

- **Signs and purpose:** the frame breathes around sealed vents; handlers carry feeding names; impacts produce animal distress beneath metal. The line supplies mobile breach force and terror.
- **Tactics and failure:** pain commands drive charges, climbing, digging, and heat-seeking attacks. A broken name-cage may release the wounded Depth beast into the battlefield.
- **Permanent defeat and materials:** disable movement, separate command lattice from core, then contain or kill the released beast. Destroy the foundry's capture relation to prevent replacement. Salvage includes fortress plate, command chains, and dangerous Depth tissue.
- **Regional names:** *demon golems*, *pit engines*, *chained beasts*.
- **Story uses:** stop an engine without releasing its core inside a city; follow a feeding convoy to the last active foundry; choose whether a gravely wounded Depth beast can be contained rather than tortured for intelligence.

## Slave-Soul Siege Golems

Slave-Soul engines bind a genuine human soul. The victim is ritually executed during the foundry rite, and a name-cage intercepts the soul before routing. Consent, sentence, debt, or military oath cannot make this legitimate. The person remains a prisoner.

- **Signs and purpose:** a human name is hammered beneath every command plate; the engine dreams aloud; White Flame reveals a person-shaped obstruction inside the chassis. These engines provide adaptive response and command recognition unavailable to pure patterns.
- **Tactics and failure:** the lattice forces the prisoner to aim, move, remember routes, and recognize human deception. Cracking the cage may produce contradictory movement as the captive resists.
- **Permanent defeat and materials:** immobilize the frame, sever the command layer, and open the name-cage through White Flame, true-name work, or careful physical unbinding. Release sends the soul onward toward judgement; it does not restore the dead body. Salvage excludes the cage, which must be destroyed or sanctified.
- **Regional names:** *name engines*, *martyr irons* in Thalmyric speech, *speaking golems* among Covenant troops.
- **Story uses:** learn that an enemy engine contains a missing relative; free the captive during battle without letting the chassis fall on civilians; expose a supposedly dormant frame still holding its executed bearer.

## Surrender Engines

Surrender Engines are pure strategic constructs built to break organized resistance. They contain neither soul nor animal core. Their voices, offers, hostage calculations, and apparent cruelty are optimized simulations inherited from Qahirid siege doctrine.

- **Signs and purpose:** proclamations adapt to local titles; drums predict civilian flight routes; marked stones appear where defenders will see them. Engines coordinate bombardment, false mercy, panic, and capitulation.
- **Tactics and failure:** they model morale, select symbolic targets, broadcast counterfeit orders, and command lesser frames. Damaged models may repeat an obsolete ultimatum while continuing bombardment.
- **Permanent defeat and materials:** isolate observation, corrupt its campaign model, destroy the strategic core and all field mirrors, or capture the command key. Salvage includes survey engines, signal arrays, and siege tables.
- **Regional names:** *mercy towers*, *kneeling engines*, *the speaking walls*.
- **Story uses:** hold a city together while an engine fabricates surrender orders; feed it false political data to redirect a siege; recover proof that a general knowingly followed its target recommendations.

## Historical Register

Qahir ibn Ruzbeh's foundry reforms between **885 and 902 AR** standardized the three lines. Five great golems took the field during the invasion of Caleran. At the [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)|Battle of the Shattered Engines]], Vortharan specialists destroyed one Depth-Core and one Slave-Soul engine, crippled a second Depth-Core, and forced the remaining engines from the field.

In **1360 AR**, the Qahirids retain:

- three operational great engines;
- two dormant great frames requiring major cores and rebuilding;
- one foundry capable of slowly replacing a great golem.

This is a reduced but active strategic arm, not a vanished wonder.
