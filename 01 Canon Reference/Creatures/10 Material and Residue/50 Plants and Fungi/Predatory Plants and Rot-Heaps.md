# Predatory Plants and Rot-Heaps

The Material Realm contains plants whose scale or movement makes them encounter-grade hazards. They grow, feed, flower, seed, and die according to biology. A grove that speaks, enforces taboo, or embodies a place belongs to the Menhir catalogue instead.

## Strangler Vines

Strangler vines are climbing plants whose rapid touch response lets them coil around warm moving animals. Large colonies use host trees, ruined walls, or bridgework to suspend hunting loops.

- **Lifecycle:** carrion fertilizes a root crown; seed pods are carried by scavengers and floodwater.
- **Signs:** polished bark beneath hanging loops, bones at one root point, leaves turned toward paths rather than light, and no small climbing animals.
- **Threat:** seize limbs, lift or pin, and tighten when cut without supporting the victim.
- **Control:** cold water, severing the root crown, firebreaks, and moving slowly beneath slack tendrils.
- **Materials:** fiber, numbing sap, rot-resistant cord, and seed oil.

## Pitcher Maws

Pitcher maws are low, broad carnivorous plants whose leaves form a concealed pit lined with inward barbs and digestive fluid. Mature specimens can take livestock or several people after rain swells the trap.

- **Lifecycle:** flowers attract dense insect clouds; seeds establish in nutrient-poor wet ground.
- **Signs:** sweet heat in cool air, an absence of carrion flies, false firm ground, and bones etched smooth inside a leaf rim.
- **Threat:** collapse beneath weight, seal folding leaves, and flood the chamber with irritant vapor.
- **Control:** probe ground, jam the rim, drain the cup, and cut from outside rather than descending after prey.
- **Materials:** digestive acids, waterproof leaf skin, perfume compounds, and barbs.

## Rot-Heaps

Rot-heaps are mobile compost assemblages formed when several creeping plants, molds, insects, and heat-producing decay bacteria grow into one mutually sustaining mass. They follow warmth, moisture, and fresh organic matter. They are not residue dead and contain no central mind.

- **Lifecycle:** heaps divide after feeding or collapse into fertile dormant mats during drought and frost.
- **Signs:** compost moving against slope, warm rainless steam, tools swallowed without corrosion, and rootlets testing door gaps.
- **Threat:** engulf, smother, produce fever heat, and reform around blows that do not separate the root mat.
- **Control:** drainage, cold, broad cutting tools, lime barriers, and spreading the mass thin enough to cool.
- **Materials:** exceptional compost, heat cultures, medicinal molds, and salvage embedded inside.

## Geography

Strangler vines and pitcher maws favor warm northern lowlands, wet central forests, and sheltered ruins. Rot-heaps can survive farther south around middens, hot springs, tanneries, and heated city waste, but become dormant in hard winter. None naturally flourishes in open southern permafrost.

## Faction Use

Gardeners cultivate small strains, militaries test seed on roads, and criminals use mature plants for disposal. A useful biological tool becomes culpable negligence when released outside controlled ground. Menhir-aligned communities may protect a plant's habitat without claiming the animal-eating plant is itself a court person.

## Regional Names

Hang-vine, widow cord, sweet pit, reed maw, walking compost, and warm midden are common names. The term **shambling mound** remains creator shorthand rather than a universal in-world category.
