# Predators and Oversized Fauna

Most large predators in the Material Realm are ordinary bears, wolves, boars, cats, crocodilians, and sharks. They do not require a supernatural explanation. Two widespread lineages are unusual enough to deserve the delver trade's attention while remaining wholly biological: **wargs** and **marsh-jaws**.

Neither family is a Menhir beast, Grobi creation, Shell manifestation, or moral adversary. Hunger, territory, injury, and breeding season explain their behavior.

## Wargs

Wargs are heavy forest canids descended from cold-country pack hunters. A mature warg stands high at a horse's chest, has a broad skull built for holding prey, and can travel for days through snow or wet woodland. They are clever animals, not a speaking people. Apparent speech in stories usually means trained signals, a skinwalker, or something using the pack as cover.

### Ecology and Signs

Warg packs follow deer, great-horn stragglers, livestock, and winter carrion. Large packs divide when food improves and fuse during hard cold. Their southern range reaches the Grey-Wolf taiga, Bearshield forests, Folk country, and the wooded approaches to Grenzburg.

- prints as broad as a person's spread hand, with the outer toes splayed under weight;
- kills dragged toward cover and opened first at the chest;
- several resting hollows facing different approaches;
- ordinary wolves leaving a valley before the larger pack is seen;
- low contact calls that carry beneath wind rather than above it.

### Threat Pattern and Defeat

Wargs test a line, harry mounts, and isolate whoever runs. They break off when badly hurt unless defending young or trapped by hunger. Fire, noise, defended livestock circles, and denying cover work better than chasing them into forest. Killing or driving off the breeding pair disperses a local pack; there is no supernatural recurrence to close.

Hide, teeth, sinew, and rendered fat have practical value. March lords sometimes maintain trained wargs, but breeding does not make them obedient and escaped kennels create recurring frontier problems.

### Regional Names and Hooks

Folk hunters call them **broad-wolves**; Ättar scouts often distinguish hall-wargs from wild winter-wargs; Brauvalian foresters use **black-muzzle** for the smaller northern strain.

- a trained ducal pack has joined wild animals after its keeper's murder;
- wargs abandon rich winter ground because a deeper predator has arrived;
- a bounty pays per jaw and quietly rewards the destruction of ordinary wolves as well;
- starving deserters trail a pack and scavenge the same kills.

## Marsh-Jaws

Marsh-jaws are immense semi-aquatic reptiles of warm northern rivers, deltas, and reed seas. Old natural histories call them **marsh-drakes**, but they have no relation to [[Drakes Overview|Menhir drakes]]. They bask, ambush, nest, and defend feeding water like animals.

### Ecology and Signs

Females build reed-and-mud nests above flood level. Adults feed on fish, waterfowl, livestock, carrion, and animals crossing familiar channels. Cold winters restrict them to northern lowlands, hot springs, and deep slow water; they do not naturally occupy the southern permafrost belt.

- parallel belly furrows between water and a sunning bank;
- broken reeds pressed inward rather than cut;
- slides ending beneath floating weed;
- fish traps emptied without damage to their stakes;
- bird colonies shifting to branches over shallow water.

### Threat Pattern and Defeat

A marsh-jaw waits beneath opaque water, strikes once, and turns with its prey. It avoids groups on firm ground but attacks boats low enough to overturn. Raised walkways, pole-testing channels, hooked lines, and denying deep-water retreat are standard answers. A slain animal stays dead; destroying eggs controls a dangerous population but can also collapse a scavenger niche and increase diseased carrion.

Hide, teeth, oil, and stomach stones enter local craft and medicine. Claims that a stomach stone wards drake-law are false but profitable.

### Regional Names and Hooks

Qahuri boatmen use **reed-crown**; northern Sestaran pilots use **mud admiral**; foreign soldiers commonly preserve the misleading name marsh-drake.

- dredging diverts a breeding channel beneath a poor riverside quarter;
- a supposedly man-eating animal is feeding on bodies dumped by a human killer;
- competing ferry houses move warning stakes to damage one another's traffic;
- flood refugees must cross the only bank holding a guarded nest.
