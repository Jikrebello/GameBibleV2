# Harridans

Harridans are carrion-bird flocks grown large and bold around old death — the harpy of frontier folklore, minus the folklore. They are Tier One vermin: no cosmological meaning, no master, no message, just big fauna fattened on residue and unafraid of people. In [[The Broken Tower]] a flock has colonized the cracked roost of the [[The Kestrel Tower]], and clearing them is the delve's central vermin lesson. This dossier collects them as a bestiary family, since they haunt nearly every exposed ruin on the frontier.

## What They Are

Where death pools and stays — battlefield edges, plague pits, cracked crypts, sealed forts newly opened — the carrion birds that feed there grow past their natural size and lose their fear. A harridan is not a cursed thing or a fey thing; it is an ordinary scavenger scaled up by an unnatural abundance of the dead, exactly as grave-rats and corpse-beetles are. The folklore that makes them women-faced omens is just that; the trade knows them as loud, bold, dangerous birds.

They arrive within weeks of a ruin opening, which makes a harridan flock a reliable sign that a sealed site has recently cracked — and that its residue is fresh enough to still be animating.

## Signs

- oversized droppings and scattered bone-litter around a ruin's open floors;
- a wheeling, shrieking flock that does not scatter at a shout the way ordinary birds do;
- picked-clean remains arranged nowhere in particular — they are messy, not ritual;
- nests built high in broken walls, from fort debris, rope-ends, and carried scraps;
- a roost that reacts to noise, not to intrusion — they hear a loud party long before they see one.

## Encounter Role

Harridans are the trade's bread: a flock fight that rewards a quiet, prepared party and punishes a loud, careless one. They are the classic "big vermin set-piece" of a starter delve — genuinely dangerous to a level-1 crew, wholly handleable with tactics, and free of any theology. A party learns crowd-control, ranged priority, and noise discipline on a harridan flock.

## Behavior and Tactics

- nest high and dive; they fight from the air and hate being pinned to the ground;
- react to noise above all — a loud delve draws them, a quiet one can pass beneath the roost;
- mob a single loud or wounded target rather than spread evenly;
- scatter when badly hurt or when their nest is threatened, then re-form if the party lingers;
- flee fire and confined space, which is why torches and doorways beat open floors.

## Handling — the Trade's Way

The proverb is *iron for bones, silver for shades, run from anything that talks* — and harridans are firmly in the "iron" column, if you can reach them. Ranged weapons, a controlled choke-point, a torch, and quiet approach are the standard answers. A flock that has fed a while is fat and slow; a hungry one is bold. Either way they are killable vermin, and killing them is pest-control, not a moral event.

## Canon Boundary

Harridans are Tier One vermin, not a fey court, not undead, and not an omen of anything but fresh death nearby. They are the harpy of folklore with the folklore filed off — a naming grievance the trade lost long ago. Nothing about a harridan flock requires a priest, a warlock, or a second thought beyond "clear the roost and mind the noise."

## Quest Uses

- a harridan flock is the first sign a sealed ruin has newly cracked open;
- a roost must be cleared before a salvage crew can work a site;
- a march town posts a vermin bounty on a flock harrying its livestock or its dead;
- the noise of fighting a flock warns whatever else lives in the ruin that visitors have come;
- a fat, established flock marks a site that has been open — and animating — longer than anyone admitted.
