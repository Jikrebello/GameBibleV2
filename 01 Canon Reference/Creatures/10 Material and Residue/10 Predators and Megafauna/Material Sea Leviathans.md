# Material Sea Leviathans

**Leviathan** is a sailor's measure of consequence, not a single lineage. The ordinary seas contain whales, sharks, crocodilians, and giant fish; three rarer biological families are large enough to change routes, fishing law, and naval behavior without requiring Menhir or Abyssal ancestry.

## Sea Serpents

Sea serpents are long-bodied air-breathing reptiles of warm northern currents and western straits. They follow schooling fish and migrate between isolated breeding coves.

- **Signs:** repeated parallel wakes without sail, floating shed scales, fish driven into shallows, and breath heard at night beyond the lamps.
- **Threat:** coil rudders, strike low decks, and use a vessel as leverage while feeding. They retreat from sustained hull noise and deep injury.
- **Control:** protect the steering gear, scatter fish away from the hull, use fire only downwind, and avoid breeding coves during the marked month.
- **Materials:** scale laminate, oil, vertebral ivory, and scent glands used by pilots to mark forbidden water.

## Great Cephalopods

Great cephalopods include several cold-water squid and octopus lineages large enough to attack boats or occupy drowned masonry. Their problem-solving is impressive animal cognition, not proof of speech or a human soul.

- **Signs:** sucker scars on pilings, neatly opened traps, shell middens, sudden ink-dark water, and missing metal objects near a den.
- **Threat:** seize from below, immobilize oars and limbs, extinguish sight with ink, and withdraw through gaps no pursuing human can use.
- **Control:** barbed poles, bitter oils, multiple escape lines, bright surface light, and denying the den its narrow retreat.
- **Materials:** ink, beak, oil, lens tissue, and edible flesh that spoils within hours.

## Reefbacks

Reefbacks are ancient giant turtles whose shells support weed, shellfish, birds, and occasionally enough soil to be mistaken for an islet. They are usually peaceful, but a feeding dive or panicked roll can destroy small craft.

- **Signs:** an “island” changing orientation, regular deep bubbles, fresh shell beneath broken coral, and birds lifting before an apparent tide.
- **Threat:** living terrain rather than hunter; danger comes from movement, suction, defensive forelimbs, and people refusing to abandon cargo or camp.
- **Control:** recognize the animal, leave no anchors through living shell, evacuate before a dive, and keep fire away from accumulated dry growth.
- **Materials:** shed scutes and attached shellfish may be harvested without killing; hunting an adult is wasteful, politically explosive, and likely to drown the hunters.

## Distribution and Regional Names

Sea serpents favor warmer northern and western routes. Great cephalopods dominate cold southern shelves, deep Sestaran channels, and the Saltborn sea roads. Reefbacks follow broad warm currents but can drift far south in summer.

- Saltborn sailors distinguish **long-wakes**, **many-hands**, and **walking reefs**.
- Sestaran insurers group all three as **route leviathans** and care more about season than species.
- Ättar crews call the largest cold-water cephalopods **keel-wives**, without implying human sex or personhood.

## Permanent Defeat and Moral Boundary

These animals die when killed and leave no soul-road event. A dangerous individual can be hunted, driven, or avoided. Killing an entire breeding population for route convenience remains ecological vandalism even though the animals possess no moral agency.

## Story Hooks

- a charted islet carries an expedition camp away overnight;
- a sea serpent breeding cove lies inside a privateer's only safe pursuit route;
- a great cephalopod collects bells from wrecks and has taken one that must be recovered intact;
- insurers conceal a seasonal migration to keep premiums low;
- a supernatural attack is blamed on a leviathan whose actual behavior proves someone staged the wreck.
