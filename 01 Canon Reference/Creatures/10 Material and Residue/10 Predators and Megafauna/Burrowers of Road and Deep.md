# Burrowers of Road and Deep

The Material Realm supports several large subterranean animals whose tunnels make roads, farms, mines, and winter camps dangerous. Their anatomy is unusual but coherent: pressure-sensing organs, reinforced forelimbs or mouthparts, slow growth, and diets based on roots, carrion, mineral-rich soil, or large prey.

Shell-scarred and Giant-bred burrowers exist elsewhere. The four forms here require no such origin.

## Road-Borers

Road-borers are ox-sized colonial insects that cultivate pale root-fungus in warm soil. Workers undermine packed roads and fields; soldiers erupt when vibration threatens the nursery.

- **Signs:** regular vent holes, warm crumbly spoil, missing roots, and road surfaces that sound hollow under a pole.
- **Threat:** workers seize and drag; soldiers hold a breach while the colony closes deeper passages.
- **Control:** flood empty galleries, dig intercept trenches, move vibration away from the nursery, or relocate the queen comb. Burning a surface hole drives the colony sideways.
- **Materials:** shell plates, resin, edible fungus, and acid glands.
- **Names:** road-borer, root mason, Qahuri clay-ant.

## Stonebacks

Stonebacks are low, plated ambush predators that swim through scree and soft rock using wedge-shaped skulls and broad forelimbs. They detect regular footfall and prefer herd trails, quarry floors, and canyon roads.

- **Signs:** a shallow moving ridge, polished stones, sudden sink lines, and carcasses crushed before feeding.
- **Threat:** breach beneath the heaviest target, roll to expose plates, retreat under loose ground, and return from another angle.
- **Control:** irregular movement, bedrock, suspended walkways, pit sounding, and attacks at the softer neck after emergence.
- **Materials:** armor plates, vibration nodules, quarry-grade teeth.
- **Names:** stoneback, road shark, gravel bull.

## Frostmaws

Frostmaws inhabit the deep southern cold belt. Their insulated bodies store heat through violent digestion and release white vapor through dorsal vents. The warmth is chemistry, not Shell fire. They follow reindeer, Grobi migration waste, refuse pits, and heated foundations beneath permafrost.

- **Signs:** melted tunnels under hard frost, sulfurous steam, circular ice collapse, and scavengers cooked rather than eaten.
- **Threat:** surface beneath shelter, vent scalding vapor, then dive before exposed skin freezes.
- **Control:** scatter heat sources, use deep-frozen ground as a barrier, plug vents with wet hide, and never cluster around one fire.
- **Materials:** insulating fat, heat bladders, drilling teeth.
- **Names:** frostmaw, steam-worm, hall-eater.

## Deep Worms

Deep worms are the rare apex of the family: immense blind tunnelers that follow mineral salts, subterranean water, and sustained vibration. A mine may work for generations above one without knowing it exists.

- **Signs:** wells changing taste, distant rhythmic grinding, warm stone, vanished ore seams, and passages too smooth to be cut.
- **Threat:** collapse terrain, swallow anything near the breach, and withdraw through a route that continues failing behind it.
- **Control:** redirect vibration, poison a mineral lick, lure it into old workings, or evacuate. Killing one normally requires an expedition or military engine.
- **Materials:** cutting rings, acid reservoirs, pressure pearls, and enormous quantities of dangerous meat.
- **Names:** deep worm, earth keel, the mine beneath the mine.

## Regional Logic

Road-borers favor warmer northern soils and irrigated country. Stonebacks range across central uplands and dry northern roads. Frostmaws belong to southern taiga and permafrost. Deep worms can occur beneath any old mountain chain. This distribution follows the world's southern-hemisphere climate rather than an imported northern model.

## Story Hooks

- road repairs conceal a road-borer colony because the contractor wants one more payment;
- a stoneback follows an army's marching rhythm toward a refugee column;
- a frostmaw has learned that heated watchtowers mean winter food;
- miners strike a deep-worm nursery and mistake the eggs for valuable stone;
- a town can save its road only by diverting a burrower toward someone else's fields.
