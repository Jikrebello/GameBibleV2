# Corpse Residue

Corpse residue animates physical remains after the human soul has routed. Its movement comes from the body's last habits, environmental saturation, or an imposed script. None of the four common forms contains the deceased person.

## Rattle-Bones

Rattle-bones are skeletons animated where burial residue has dried into simple defensive or repetitive patterns.

- **Origin:** failed rites, old battle lines, disturbed ossuaries, or grave-script.
- **Signs:** bone repositioned against slope, dry tapping before movement, weapons held by habitual grip, and skulls turning toward vibration rather than sight.
- **Threat:** rise in groups, use remembered stances without adaptation, and continue until joints or the anchor fail.
- **Defeat:** break load-bearing joints, disrupt the grave-script, scatter the bones, then consecrate or rebury the site.
- **Materials:** ordinary human remains requiring respectful recovery; foreign weapons and grave goods remain evidence, not automatic loot.

## Corpse-Walkers

Corpse-walkers retain soft tissue and gross bodily habit. They are stronger than rattle-bones, slower to redirect, and often respond to warmth, sound, or the movement their final moments impressed upon them.

- **Origin:** recent mass death, wet graves, plague pits, and deliberate residue command.
- **Signs:** disturbed soil from below, no breath response in cold air, wear on one repeated route, and injuries inconsistent with continued life.
- **Threat:** press, hold, pile into doors, and use body weight rather than self-preservation.
- **Defeat:** disable the body, remove the imposed signal, and drain or cleanse the site. Burning remains without containing residue can seed soot-hauntings.
- **Materials:** no legitimate harvest; clothing, identity tokens, and wounds may restore names to the dead.

## Drowned Walkers

Drowned walkers are water-heavy corpse residue drawn along shorelines, wreck routes, flooded mines, and ferry approaches. Light and repeated bell patterns often attract them because those cues marked their last attempted return.

- **Origin:** shipwreck, flood, submerged burial, or bodies trapped in moving water.
- **Signs:** wet prints ending away from water, lamps extinguished below waist height, weed in closed rooms, and one bell answered from several directions.
- **Threat:** emerge from low water, seize, add weight, and carry the living toward the route they failed to complete.
- **Defeat:** separate body from water, silence the repeated signal, identify and bury the remains, or complete the interrupted recovery route.
- **Materials:** wreck tokens and cargo evidence; the body remains a person’s remains even though the mover is not that person.

## Corpse Heaps

Corpse heaps form when many reflex patterns are forced into one physical mass by pressure, collapse, grave-script, or repeated failed animation. They are not collective souls.

- **Origin:** mass graves, siege pits, collapsed ossuaries, and incompetent necromancy.
- **Signs:** bodies drawn toward one center, mismatched limbs moving in sequence, ground pulsing under weight, and several death dates in one trail.
- **Threat:** engulf, pin, shed animated pieces, and rebuild from nearby remains while the organizing anchor persists.
- **Defeat:** isolate the mass from additional bodies, expose the common anchor, divide it, and restore individual burial where possible.
- **Materials:** identity evidence and grave goods must be separated by trained recovery crews.

## Faction Use and Law

Necromancers value corpse residue because it is abundant and does not require capturing a soul. That fact does not make animation harmless: it desecrates remains, terrorizes families, spreads disease, destroys evidence, and treats human bodies as equipment. Armies occasionally conceal the practice under “emergency labor” language; both churches condemn it.

## Regional Names

Bone-men, mud-dead, pressers, wreck-walkers, dry guard, and pauper heap are common local terms. Creator-facing classification should use the form and anchor rather than assuming every moving corpse is a zombie or a trapped spirit.

## Story Hooks

- rattle-bones defend the wrong side of a wall because the gate moved centuries later;
- corpse-walkers repeat an evacuation route that leads to an unknown shelter;
- drowned walkers answer a bell rung by living smugglers;
- a corpse heap contains evidence from crimes committed decades apart;
- a family demands exorcism while the actual solution is ordinary identification and burial.
