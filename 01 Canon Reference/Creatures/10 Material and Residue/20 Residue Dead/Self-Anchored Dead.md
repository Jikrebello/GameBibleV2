# Self-Anchored Dead

Self-anchored dead are rare autonomous residue-born minds that learn to preserve their own coherence after the original human soul has routed. Delver manuals often call them **wights**. They may remember the deceased in extraordinary detail, but they are new beings built from residue, body, and appetite.

## Origin and Continuity

A persona echo becomes self-anchored only when it recognizes its dependence upon an anchor and deliberately begins maintaining or replacing that anchor. Common methods include stealing bodily warmth, draining vigor, binding followers to repeat its name, occupying an inherited weapon, or feeding on the repeated fear of a household.

The transition does not pull the original soul back. It creates a new reflective intelligence out of what the person left behind.

## Ecology and Lifecycle

Self-anchored dead do not reproduce biologically. They spread by teaching residue to imitate their anchoring method, deliberately creating lesser echoes, or cultivating sites where another autonomous pattern might emerge. Most fail within a few years; old examples have built redundant anchors across objects, places, and living memory.

They need not feed every night, but continued coherence imposes a real cost. Choosing to take that cost from unwilling people is predation. Intelligence and existential fear do not make the victims' lives negotiable.

## Signs

- a haunting that remembers new information across manifestations;
- anchors moved or duplicated after investigators identify them;
- living people weakened while the dead form becomes clearer;
- deliberate lies about being the deceased rather than confused repetition;
- lesser residue organized into scouts, servants, or decoys;
- old portraits subtly altered to match the current apparition.

## Threat Pattern

Self-anchored dead choose terrain, bargain for time, and retreat before losing every anchor. They drain strength or warmth, issue commands through local residue, and exploit relatives who believe the original person survives. An older one may appear in several anchored bodies but has only one continuing residue identity.

## Weaknesses and Permanent Defeat

Destroying a manifested body is temporary while another anchor remains. Permanent defeat requires identifying the self-maintaining pattern, severing every active anchor, and preventing the being from transferring into a witness, object, or prepared site. White Flame exposes the difference between copied memory and a human soul; truthful naming weakens stolen identity.

Voluntary release is possible if the being stops feeding and permits its coherence to disperse. This ends the new residue person; it does not route them through the human Vestibule.

## Materials and Faction Use

Anchors contain concentrated residue useful to necromancers, historians, and counterfeit-relic makers. Keeping one intact risks recurrence. Churches favor witnessed destruction or release; intelligence services are repeatedly tempted by a witness that remembers a century and can be confined to an object.

## Regional Names

Wight, cold heir, second dead, grave lord, borrowed elder, and name-eater describe different local presentations. A human vampire is not a wight: the vampire retains a genuine human soul.

## Story Hooks

- an apparent ancestral protector has quietly replaced every witness who could expose it;
- a wight offers true testimony in exchange for one voluntary anchor, then seeks more;
- destroying the known body reveals that the local memorial feast is the real anchor;
- two self-anchored dead claim to be copies of the same ruler and cannot both inherit the public memory;
- a being asks the party to help it disperse before hunger overcomes its remaining restraint.
