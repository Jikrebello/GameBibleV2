# Ruin Vermin and Swarms

Ruin vermin are ordinary small animals thriving where carrion, damp, shelter, and few predators coincide. Residue-rich sites often make them larger, bolder, or more fertile, but their continuity remains biological. They are not tiny undead, omens with opinions, or servants of whatever else occupies the ruin.

## Grave-Rat Swarms

Grave rats are broad-jawed rats adapted to bone pits, sewers, siege tunnels, and charnel ground. A swarm follows food and escape routes rather than command.

- **Signs:** paired gnaw marks on bone and lead, oily runways, cloth pulled into wall gaps, and sudden silence when a lantern enters.
- **Threat:** flood ankles and low ground, bite trapped targets, carry fever, and flee through the party's presumed retreat.
- **Control:** smoke toward a prepared exit, raised stores, terriers, lime, traps, and sealing water access. Fire in an enclosed crypt usually harms people and records before the rats.
- **Use:** fat, hides, disease samples, and their movement as a map of hidden voids.

## Tomb-Spider Colonies

Tomb spiders are several related cave and cellar spiders whose shared webs can blanket rooms. Individuals are rarely exceptional; the colony's sight-lines and venom make the site dangerous.

- **Signs:** dust suspended in horizontal web, drained insects arranged by air current, shed skins near warm masonry, and threads crossing door hinges.
- **Threat:** blind lights, divide movement, drop onto immobilized prey, and retreat into cracks while venom works.
- **Control:** slow poles, wet cloth, controlled smoke, removing anchor strands, and protecting eyes and mouths. Loud burning scatters the colony deeper.
- **Use:** silk, venom, preserved specimens, and web patterns that reveal recent passage.

## Corpse-Beetle Clouds

Corpse beetles are thumb-sized carrion beetles that mass around fresh residue and unsealed dead. When disturbed, adults rise as a dense biting cloud while larvae retreat deeper into remains.

- **Signs:** dry clicking beneath a corpse, pinhole damage in leather, polished bone, and a sweet rancid smell before movement.
- **Threat:** extinguish exposed flame, enter clothing and armor, obscure vision, and strip an unconscious body with alarming speed.
- **Control:** oilcloth, cold, water mist, sealed lamps, and removing the larval food source. Killing adults without cleaning the nest only postpones the next cloud.
- **Use:** cleaned bone, forensic timing, rendered oil, and bait for larger scavengers.

## Ecology and Failure Modes

All three families reproduce quickly after war, plague, flood, or a sealed chamber opening. A delver crew that clears monsters but leaves food, water, and entry points has not cleared the site. Poison can contaminate wells, archives, and burial soil; indiscriminate extermination can drive vermin into inhabited walls.

## Regional Names

Names are occupational rather than cultural: bone-rats, ledger spiders, bell beetles, red crypt-rats, sail-cellar spiders, and plague clickers. The same common name may cover unrelated local species.

## Story Hooks

- rats emerge carrying cloth from a chamber absent from every plan;
- a spider colony's broken web proves someone entered after the official sealing;
- corpse beetles date a hidden death later than the court's testimony;
- a bounty campaign poisons the district cistern;
- vermin flee an apparently safe tunnel before a deeper danger arrives.
