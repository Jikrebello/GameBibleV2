# Natural Oozes and Slimes

Natural oozes are colonies of simple cells, digestive films, and cooperative microbial bodies. They sense heat, vibration, dissolved chemicals, or metal ions rather than seeing and thinking. Their forms can look impossible while remaining Material biology.

Alchemical runoff, Shell seepage, Astral consumption, and black-mud anti-coherence are separate origins and do not belong in this family.

## Carrion Slimes

Carrion slimes digest soft tissue in damp places while leaving hair, bone, and most worked material behind.

- **Chemistry and food:** flesh, fat, leather, and fungal mats; the colony exudes a numbing digestive gel.
- **Signs:** polished bone, damp trails against gravity, flies avoiding a carcass, and leather softened at the edge.
- **Threat:** spread over fallen or restrained prey and contract when struck, holding weapons and limbs.
- **Control:** salt, dry ash, sustained heat, drainage, and denying carrion. Cutting spreads live fragments unless the blade is cleaned.
- **Use:** controlled bone cleaning, tanning, wound research, and criminal disposal.

## Clear Slimes

Clear slimes suspend nearly invisible sheets across cisterns, flooded stairs, and smooth stone. They digest small animals and organic sediment, leaving water deceptively clean.

- **Chemistry and food:** protein and suspended waste; strong spirits cloud the body and interrupt adhesion.
- **Signs:** bubbles stopping at one plane, pebbles hanging above the floor, a clean patch amid algae, and ripples that do not reach the wall.
- **Threat:** envelop the face, seal air, and use a victim's struggle to fold more body around them.
- **Control:** thrown dust, dye, hooked line, alcohol, and draining below the colony.
- **Use:** water clarification when properly screened, optical gel, and concealed disposal pits.

## Iron-Eaters

Iron-eaters are rust-colored cave colonies that dissolve oxidized metal and concentrate cleaner iron nodules in their cores. They feed slowly until vibration or blood salts trigger rapid adhesion.

- **Chemistry and food:** rust, iron salts, certain pigments, and blood; they leave stone and precious metal largely untouched.
- **Signs:** bright-edged pits in old tools, orange condensation, nails failing together, and smooth dark pellets in drains.
- **Threat:** seize armor joints and weapon edges, heat while digesting, and split when washed with ordinary water.
- **Control:** oil, lime, ceramic tools, isolation from metal, and freezing. Acid usually feeds the reaction.
- **Use:** ore recovery, scrap processing, sabotage, and fraud involving “pure” nodules seeded into poor mines.

## Lifecycle and Permanent Defeat

Oozes reproduce by budding and fragmenting. A visible body is only the local colony; permanent clearance requires removing the food source, drying or treating the substrate, and cleaning tools that carry fragments. This recurrence is biological contamination, not metaphysical resurrection.

## Faction Use

Miners, tanners, sewer crews, alchemists, and delvers all cultivate or exterminate oozes. A useful colony becomes a public hazard when an owner conceals escape, changes its feed, or dumps it into shared water.

## Regional Names

Carrion soap, glass water, rust hunger, bone-wash, cistern veil, and miner's orange are common trade names. Scholars group them for convenience; locals care which substance stops the one in front of them.
