# Heavenly Commission and Manifestation

A heavenly intervention is defined by **who is sent, what is authorized, where the commission applies, and what ends it**. Raw power does not grant universal jurisdiction.

## Commission Before Rank

A smaller servant with a precise commission may possess authority that a greater servant does not have in that matter. A local saint can direct a host of angels if the Lord places the saint over the mission. A greater angel can destroy a powerful intrusion yet lack authority to decide a human succession, forgive a crime, or revise a saint's testimony.

Angels can command other angels within an assigned host. Angels do not command saints by nature. Saints do not control angels through personal prestige; their authority exists only where the divine commission grants it.

## Petition, Not Summoning

Prayer, liturgy, relics, icons, hymns, fasting, charity, confession, and repaired rites may prepare people and places to receive aid. They cannot compel an appearance. Repeatable “angel summoning” is either false advertising, a patron compact, a bound created spirit, or a dangerous attempt to imitate Heaven's signs.

An answered petition may produce no visible servant. Providence, courage, truthful evidence, a delayed enemy, a mortal healer, or the sudden failure of corruption can fulfill the mercy without spectacle.

## Commission-Bodies

True angels and manifested saints normally enter Material conditions through temporary bodies shaped for the work. The form is real enough to touch, suffer, fight, carry, and be resisted. It is not the servant's entire continuity.

- True angelic bodies reveal a stable strange anatomy beneath cultural vestment: avian geometry, many eyes, wheels or rings of motion, impossible scale, clean flame, or living wind.
- Saintly bodies preserve the person's human face, voice, wounds, clothing tradition, and testimony, translated so witnesses can recognize them. Avian marks may express heavenly service without erasing humanity.
- Road mechanisms manifest only the form required by a bounded function.

Breaking a commission-body ends local action and may strand people the servant was helping. It does not kill the spirit or soul. A servant may also withdraw when the commission is completed, refused, displaced, or rendered impossible.

## Rank and Scale

Bestiary `rank` measures encounter consequence, not holiness or authority.

| Scale | Typical rank | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Local | standard or specialist | one crossing, household, witness, or immediate protection |
| Mission-bearing | specialist or elite | settlement, expedition, institution, or extended struggle |
| Great | apex or unique | army, kingdom, church, or metaphysical frontier |
| Phoenix-grade | unique | regional judgement, purification, and restoration |

## Recognition

A heavenly claim should be tested by source, commission, coherence with revealed truth, refusal of worship, treatment of the vulnerable, and the fruit of its action. Beauty, wings, healing, fire, accurate secrets, or effective protection are not sufficient proof.

True servants do not:

- demand worship owed to the Lord;
- sell salvation or revise judgement;
- create human souls;
- make evil good by command;
- flatter an institution into confusing its victory with divine approval;
- submit to a mortal binding as proof of holiness.

## Falling

True angels retain full moral agency. A fall is an intelligible act of refusal in which the angel abandons a commission or allegiance. It does not occur because a spell “corrupts” angelic blood. A fallen angel remains a created spirit but no longer belongs to Heaven's active orders.

Redeemed saints cannot fall after judgement and fulfilled communion. Their signs can be forged, relics can be abused, and mortal accounts can lie; the saint is not thereby turned against the Lord.

## Encounter Use

Heavenly servants may aid, test, warn, bar passage, demand release of a bound soul, protect someone the protagonists oppose, or refuse a request. Opposition must stay legible and tied to commission. Players can resolve it by correcting the violation, completing the truth, accepting the warning, or knowingly resisting the authorized work.
