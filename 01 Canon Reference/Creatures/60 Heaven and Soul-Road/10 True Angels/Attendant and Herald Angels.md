# Attendant and Herald Angels

Attendants and heralds are the true angels most likely to be encountered without an open cosmic battle. They are not minor because their work is unimportant. Their commission is simply bounded enough to fit inside a household, journey, warning, or conversation.

## Attendant Angels

Attendants sustain one local work: keeping a lamp through a siege, guiding a child from a burning house, carrying a prayer to its proper witness, holding a thin place closed for one night, or strengthening a mortal who must act without replacing that person's choice.

- **Core form:** small nested wings around one eye, hand, flame, bell, or seedlike center; movement often resembles a flock behaving as one body.
- **Signs:** a repeated ordinary sound becomes perfectly clear, falling objects miss one narrow path, a lamp burns without consuming fuel, or a frightened person can suddenly distinguish the next necessary act.
- **Behavior:** assist the commission and refuse unrelated demands. They rarely explain the larger plan.
- **Threat pattern:** obscure pursuit, block one threshold, carry a protected person, or expose the hidden object sustaining corruption. If opposed, they evade and preserve rather than punish.
- **End of manifestation:** completion, deliberate refusal by the intended beneficiary, destruction of the local path, or breaking the commission-body.
- **Remains:** no harvestable corpse. A featherlike trace may persist until the witnessed mercy is completed, then become ordinary ash, light, or air.

Attendants are not familiars. A mortal who tries to retain one after the commission ends is attempting angelic imprisonment.

## Herald Angels

Heralds carry a bounded truth, warning, command, invitation, or answer. Some address one person; others speak before courts, armies, or peoples. A herald does not add private doctrine to its message and does not answer every question merely because it knows more than the hearer.

- **Core form:** a tall avian geometry of opening wings and many forward-facing eyes, with a voice that may come from every feather or from the space between sounds.
- **Signs:** spoken language becomes mutually intelligible, concealed writing appears legible, lies become difficult to finish, and every witness later agrees on the essential message despite differing descriptions of the speaker.
- **Behavior:** identify source and commission, deliver the message, permit enough understanding for genuine response, then withdraw.
- **Threat pattern:** halt hostile motion through overwhelming attention, reveal disguises, separate a speaker from rehearsed lies, and make the consequences of refusal plain. Violence is secondary to communication.
- **End of manifestation:** the message is faithfully delivered and freely received or refused. Killing the body cannot erase what was heard.
- **Remains:** sometimes a written phrase, scorched feather-shape, or shared memory with evidentiary value; never a reusable summoning component.

## Cultural Vestments

Eclessian witnesses favor robed or armored winged figures, bells, books, and clear human speech. Thalmyric accounts emphasize wheels of pinions, fire, eyes, and sung command. Folk witnesses may describe a white bird too large for the sky or a traveler whose cloak opened into migration. These are vestments around stable profiles.

## Regional Names

Attendants are called **little witnesses**, **house lamps**, **near wings**, or **helpers at the edge**. Heralds become **high voices**, **road trumpets**, **white messengers**, or **birds before the word**. These titles describe recognition traditions, not separate choirs.

## Recognition and Failure

An attendant or herald refuses worship and mortal ownership. A “herald” that revises salvation, sells protection, demands secrecy from all testing, or accepts a binding contract is more likely a patron or counterfeit. Angels can fall, but an actual act of refusal ends the heavenly commission; it does not turn the same commission into a malicious one.

## Story Hooks

- an attendant protects a person everyone believes to be the villain, forcing the party to discover the actual commission;
- a herald's warning is public, but three factions disagree over the action it requires;
- a false summoner has trapped the trace left by an attendant and claims to own the angel;
- witnesses describe different bodies but one exact sentence;
- breaking a commission-body silences the guide while refugees remain inside the closing route.
