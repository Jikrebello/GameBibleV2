# Local and Mission Saints

Local and mission saints are the most intimate forms of posthumous heavenly service. Their manifestations are rare, brief, and attached to a concrete need rather than a permanent supernatural office.

## Local Saints

Local saints return to a people, parish, monastery, household, battlefield, or trade whose suffering they knew in life. They may warn a village, identify a hidden survivor, defend a sanctuary for a single night, or correct a community that has turned their memory into vanity.

**Signs:** a familiar voice heard by several sober witnesses; an old tool or martyr's wound made luminous; birds gathering without panic; warmth around a neglected shrine; an exact recollection known only to trustworthy contemporaries.

**Conduct:** local saints speak plainly and remain concerned with persons rather than their own cult. They do not demand new worship, enrich custodians, or contradict the Lord for the sake of local custom.


## Mission Saints

Mission saints are commissioned beyond the communities that knew them. They accompany conversion, relief, reconciliation, translation, or dangerous witness across cultural frontiers. Their earthly identity remains visible, but the commission may require them to speak languages they never knew or appear in vestments legible to those receiving the message.

**Signs:** shared understanding across languages; a road made passable for one company; a lamp that burns through hostile weather; hostile thresholds becoming briefly traversable; courage without frenzy among frightened witnesses.

**Conduct:** mission saints invite and testify. They do not compel belief through domination. Their presence may be formidable, but its purpose is service under truth rather than conquest of attention.


## Continuity, Defeat, and Remains

These manifestations are commission-bodies around redeemed human souls. Violence can disperse the body, profane the site, or prevent the immediate work, but it cannot kill or corrupt the saint. No harvestable heavenly corpse remains. At most, an ordinary object used during the manifestation may retain a temporary blessing whose use remains subordinate to the original commission.

## Regional Names and Institutional Use

Eclessian records distinguish **local patrons** and **mission saints**; Thalmyric usage favors **near intercessors** and **road-bearing elders**; Freeflame communities usually say **the faithful witness** without endorsing invocation. Churches investigate such appearances, preserve testimony, organize relief around them, and sometimes compete to claim them. Recognition does not create the saint or grant custody over later manifestations.
