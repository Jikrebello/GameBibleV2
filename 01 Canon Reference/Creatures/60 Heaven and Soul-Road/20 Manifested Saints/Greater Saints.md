# Greater Saints

Greater saints are redeemed human persons entrusted with commissions whose effects cross kingdoms, layers, or generations. The category describes exceptional service, not a separate kind of soul. They remain the people they became through earthly life, judgement, redemption, and communion with the Lord.

## Manifestation

A greater saint may appear in a stable human form carrying unmistakable cultural identity while also expressing signs too large for ordinary space: wings made from remembered prayers, wounds shining across a battlefield, a voice heard at several threatened thresholds, or vestments that contain the history of a people without consuming their individuality.

Their authority may exceed that of powerful true angels when the commission requires it. Conversely, a greater angel may command the same saint under another commission. Authority belongs to the Lord's purpose, not to a fixed ladder of species or force.

## Uses and Limits

Greater saints may coordinate hosts, confront a catastrophe that threatens the soul-road, preserve a people through a decisive passage, or bear witness before rulers who could not otherwise survive the encounter. Their appearance should alter an age, not merely solve a difficult fight.

They cannot be summoned, inherited, captured in a relic, or compelled by possessing their earthly remains. They do not found private heavens or accept worship. They cannot fall after final communion.

## Opposition and Defeat

Hostile powers can obstruct a greater saint's commission-body, obscure mortal cooperation, profane necessary sites, or force the manifestation to spend itself protecting lives. They cannot destroy the redeemed soul. A thwarted commission can still leave grave earthly consequences, which is why mortal participation matters.

No harvestable heavenly material remains. A touched banner, gate, weapon, or book may become a relic only insofar as White Flame continues to serve the commission; possession never grants command of the saint.

## Regional Names and Institutional Use

Cultures remember greater saints as **fathers or mothers of a people**, **witnesses before kings**, **keepers of an age**, or **great intercessors**. Churches build calendars and political claims around them, but no patriarch, synod, or relic court can attach a greater saint to a new campaign by decree.

## Story Uses

- A kingdom receives a warning whose political cost makes obedience uncertain.
- Several choirs of angels act under a saint whose earthly culture their mortal allies distrust.
- A counterfeit claims the title of a greater saint and is exposed by its demand for worship.
- The heroes must complete a Material task while a greater saint holds back a layer-wide breach.
