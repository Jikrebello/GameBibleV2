# Manifested Saints Overview

Manifested saints are redeemed human souls sent from Heaven under a particular commission. They are not a species of angel, and living people acclaimed as saints are not automatically included among them. A true posthumous manifestation presupposes judgement, redemption, and final communion with the Lord.

Saints retain a recognizable relation to their earthly identity. Their speech, clothing, scars, tools, and cultural manner may remain visible, though glorified and sometimes accompanied by avian features, fire, impossible light, or signs associated with their earthly witness. These signs express the commission; they do not turn the saint into another kind of being.

## Authority and Limits

- A saint acts by divine commission, not by personal ownership of a place, relic, bloodline, or congregation.
- A saint may command true angels when the commission grants that authority. Angelic origin does not create automatic rank over a redeemed human soul.
- Saints cannot fall after final communion. A cruel, corrupting, or self-exalting apparition claiming sainthood is a counterfeit, a mistaken haunting, or a hostile power using a revered name.
- Mortals may pray for aid, but no rite can summon, bind, or repeatedly deploy a saint as a magical service.
- Destroying a manifested body ends that appearance. It does not injure or dislodge the redeemed soul in Heaven.

## Recognition

A sound claim of manifestation normally combines moral coherence, known signs, reliable witnesses, and fruits consistent with the Lord's character. Familiar appearance alone proves little. Residue can repeat a dead person's habits, Idols can counterfeit longing, and patrons can borrow symbols.
