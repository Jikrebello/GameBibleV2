# Stone-Eyes and Many-Necks

These predators demonstrate two different Menhir pressures carried by biological life: form fixed too rigidly and form multiplied faster than injury can contain it.

## Stone-Eyes

Stone-Eyes are squat reptilian or avian predators whose gaze and breath force living tissue toward mineral stillness. They arise near calcifying springs, exposed strata, and places where Hecatara's route-law and Father Sun's fixing exposure overlap.

- **Lifecycle:** leathery eggs are buried in mineral mud; hatchlings begin with stiffening venom before the adult gaze develops.
- **Signs:** small animals preserved in lifelike mineral poses, eye-level scrape marks, pale dust downwind, and water leaving a rigid film on skin.
- **Threat:** hold one target in sight, drive others into reflective surfaces, and bite partly stiffened prey.
- **Weakness:** the effect requires a clear sensory path and degrades outside the creature's mineral territory. Indirect sight, moving water, opaque screens, and breaking its line of fixation protect better than polished mirrors.
- **Permanent defeat:** bodily death. Petrified victims can sometimes be restored before mineral replacement reaches the brain and heart; later statues are remains, not imprisoned souls.

## Many-Necks

Many-Necks are marsh and river predators whose growth law treats severe injury as a branching instruction. Most have three to seven feeding heads emerging from one broad body. Typhonius-shaped recombination, not malice or divine punishment, explains the anatomy.

- **Lifecycle:** juveniles begin with one head. Injury, abundant food, and warm water trigger branching; old specimens may become locally catastrophic.
- **Signs:** several bite patterns on one carcass, feeding trails converging on one body drag, shed tooth clusters, and reed beds flattened in a spoke pattern.
- **Threat:** control several approaches, pin prey between necks, sacrifice injured heads, and retreat into water while regrowing.
- **Weakness:** starvation, cold, destruction of the central heart-lung mass, and sealing a neck wound before it branches.
- **Permanent defeat:** destroy the central body and every viable bud. Severed heads die unless a substantial portion of the core accompanied them.

Shell-fire variants are conditions applied to a Many-Neck, not a separate Faewild species.

## Materials and Regional Names

Stone-Eye glands supply controlled calcifying reagents. Many-Neck bud flesh supports dangerous regeneration research. Both trades are heavily regulated where authorities possess the capacity to regulate them.

Names include stone-eye, cockatrice, quarry basilisk, still-gaze, many-neck, reed hydra, delta crown, and seven-mouth.

## Story Hooks

- A quarry crew sells statues as art before learning they are recent victims.
- A Many-Neck grows beneath a warm city drain where repeated culling only increases its heads.
- A healer needs a Stone-Eye gland before a noble's calcification becomes irreversible.
- Warlocks deliberately Shell-scar a captive Many-Neck for siege use.
- A village protects a juvenile Stone-Eye because its territory suppresses a worse burrower.
