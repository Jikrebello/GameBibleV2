# Giltlings

Giltlings are small sapient scavengers found around old gold-drake courts, sun-cliff thresholds, abandoned shrines, and caches of repeatedly polished metal. They are court-fauna whose social memory and tool use crossed into language long before any surviving human record.

They are not young drakes, mortal children transformed by magic, or a branch of the Grobi. Their bodies are narrow, heat-tolerant, and covered in dull ochre scales with reflective markings around the eyes, tail, and hands.

## Ecology

Giltlings eat insects, eggs, roots, carrion, grain, and mineral salts. They prefer warm cracks near water and establish branching warrens with several exits. Polished metal serves practical purposes: directing warmth, marking routes, signalling danger, and displaying remembered obligation.

A settled host normally contains twelve to forty adults with young, elders, gatherers, watchers, and craft specialists. Hosts can survive independently of drakes. Those attached to an elder court gain safety and rich scavenging but risk becoming servants whose patron treats them as replaceable.

## Social Intelligence

Giltlings speak compact clicking languages supplemented by hand reflections and tail-ring patterns. They learn neighboring mortal languages readily. They remember favors, deaths, routes, and humiliations with precision but do not possess supernatural memory.

They are morally responsible people, not vermin driven by alignment. Individual hosts may trade, steal, hide, serve, raid, defect, or make restitution. Fear and court pressure shape choices without erasing them.

## Signs

- triangular tool marks around locks, mortar, and mirror frames;
- narrow warm-air passages fitted with polished scraps;
- small footprints that avoid direct shade at midday;
- rings, washers, bell fragments, or foil used as route signs;
- food taken in regular portions rather than indiscriminate spoilage;
- quick reflected flashes where no human-sized observer could stand.

## Weaknesses and Permanent Defeat

Giltlings are small living people rather than recurring manifestations. Matte coverings, smoke, cold flooding, blocked warm-air passages, and removal of prepared reflection lines defeat their usual tactical advantages. Food, safety, amnesty, and proof that a patron cannot protect them may break a host's willingness to fight.

Bodily death is permanent. Destroying a host means killing a community, not dismissing court-fauna back to another plane. A defeated but surviving host can scatter, surrender, negotiate withdrawal, or establish a new warren if it retains young, tools, and access to warmth.

## The Hellerbruck Host

[[The Bright-Tail Host]] serves [[Sovarath-of-the-Open-Noon]]. Kezra Seven-Rings embraces the promised rank of mortal-court steward. Nirr Ash-Eye leads a faction that recognizes Sovarath's indifference and can abandon the campaign.

The host's role does not establish all Giltlings as followers of gold drakes. It establishes one community divided by fear, ambition, guilt, and the possibility of choosing differently.

## Materials and Law

Giltling tools are clever small-scale craft, not magical treasure by default. Their route mirrors and glare powders are useful to scouts and thieves. Captured hosts should be judged for particular acts rather than hunted as monsters. A drake's claim over them has no inherent legal or moral authority.

## Regional Names

Giltling, bright-tail, sun-warren folk, mirror-tail, ochre hand, and warm-crack people. Host names are normally more important to Giltlings than the broad human family label.
