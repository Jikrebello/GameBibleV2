# Hags and Counterfeit Kin

`Hag` is a human folklore category, not one species or continuity. This dossier distinguishes two recurrent forms. Idol-grown mimics, patron servants, and other beings called hags remain in their actual origin sections.

## Cradle-Hags

Cradle-Hags are native court-law persons that feed on kinship made ambiguous, denied, or transactional. They enter through an accepted domestic role: midwife, aunt, fosterer, bereaved child, nurse, or returned relative. Their bodies are assembled from borrowed resemblance, cradle wood, hair, milk teeth, and the household's unspoken expectations.

- **Continuity:** `court-law`; no human soul lies beneath the disguise.
- **Domain:** kinship names, replacement, inheritance, and care given without truthful recognition.
- **Compulsion:** occupy an empty or contested family role and make the household reorganize around it.
- **Taboo:** a complete genealogy spoken by people who accept both duty and truth.
- **Bargain:** they can provide fertility, survival, concealment, or a convincing replacement for someone lost.
- **Price:** a living relationship is displaced, a child is raised under a false identity, or one branch of kin is erased from memory and inheritance.

Cradle-Hags may love particular people. Affection does not make replacement, child theft, or murder permissible. They possess full agency and can choose to release a claim.

**Permanent defeat** requires exposing the assumed role, recovering or destroying its cradle-token, restoring the displaced person's name and claims, and forcing the Hag outside every accepted kinship threshold. Killing the body without repairing the household lets the law grow another mask.

## Skinbound Hags

Skinbound Hags are human beings who chose a Faewild bargain that rewrote body and appetite while retaining the human soul. Common bargains promise beauty, fertility, revenge, safety from age, authority over a household, or freedom from a hated obligation.

- **Continuity:** `retained-human-soul`; the person remains human and routable once the lien is broken.
- **Compulsion:** enact the benefit by reproducing the harm concealed in its price.
- **Anchor:** a skin, mirror, comb, cauldron, name-strip, or living beneficiary holds the bargain in force.
- **Moral condition:** coercion and deception may reduce culpability, but the original choice and later acts must be judged separately.

Restoration is possible but never a routine dispelling. The Skinbound person must confront the actual bargain, surrender its benefit, release every captive or transferred burden, repair concrete harms where repair remains possible, and accept lawful consequences. White Flame can reveal and support this work; it cannot manufacture repentance.

If the Skinbound body is killed while the lien remains intact, the soul stays caught in the bargain's anchor until released. Destroying the anchor carelessly may scatter manifestations while leaving the person held.

## Signs and Threat Pattern

- family portraits changing one face at a time;
- children using a relative's private phrase before meeting them;
- milk souring only in the presence of one accepted caregiver;
- a human shadow fitting an older or younger body;
- gifts that solve one household problem by transferring it to another;
- a home whose records contain perfect agreement and no witnesses.

Hags isolate relatives, redefine obligations, weaponize shame, and use the household as terrain. They prefer a victim who cannot prove who belongs.

## Materials and Regional Names

Cradle-tokens retain dangerous kinship handles. Skinbound anchors are evidence and prisons, not salvage. Churches, Folk seers, and secular courts disagree sharply over custody, but destroying either without identifying its continuity is reckless.

Regional names include cradle-hag, old mother, skin-wife, borrowed aunt, tooth nurse, hedge widow, and counterfeit kin.

## Story Hooks

- A family begs the party to remove an aunt whom every record says has always lived there.
- A Skinbound healer can cure a plague only by transferring each fever into an unwanted child.
- Two adults both remember being the infant stolen from the same cradle.
- A repentant Hag has surrendered her beauty but cannot identify the final person carrying her bargain's harm.
- An Idol servant imitates Hag signs to conceal a different predatory economy.
