# Hearthlings and Glimmerfolk

Small court folk arise where repeated human habits overlap Faewild law. They do not form a hidden kingdom. A household, lane, workshop, or shrine may support one little lineage for generations while the next valley has none.

## Hearthlings

Hearthlings take form from maintained domestic rhythms: swept thresholds, banked fires, shared bread, tools returned to their places, and labor acknowledged by those who benefit from it. They are hand-high figures of soot, lint, grain husk, wax, or polished wood.

- **Domain:** household continuity, useful routine, and reciprocal labor.
- **Compulsion:** finish interrupted domestic work and expose concealed neglect.
- **Taboo:** public praise addressed directly to them, stolen household food, and claiming another person's labor as one's own.
- **Bargain and price:** they trade quiet help for regular food, an unused corner, and honest acknowledgment among the household. They may demand the exposure of an abuser or thief before resuming work.
- **Signs:** swept floors no one remembers cleaning, tools sorted by purpose, porridge missing one spoonful, and soot footprints inside locked cupboards.
- **Threat pattern:** jam locks, spoil lies by moving evidence, trip violent occupants, hide necessities, or turn every neglected task against the household.
- **Permanent defeat:** end the domestic rhythm, lawfully release the Hearthling under witness, or destroy its hearth-token. Burning the building kills the relationship but is rarely the best answer.

Hearthlings possess full agency. A grievance can explain sabotage without excusing the decision to injure a child, poison food, or trap a household in a burning room.

## Glimmerfolk

Glimmerfolk form along attended paths, boundaries, and moments of exchanged notice. They appear as moving sparks, tiny winged figures, reflective eyes, or a voice traveling between lamps.

- **Domain:** attention, route-marking, invitation, and concealment through distraction.
- **Compulsion:** redirect whoever follows a sign without understanding it.
- **Taboo:** a light deliberately extinguished after accepting its guidance.
- **Bargain and price:** safe passage in exchange for carrying a message, leaving a lamp at another threshold, or surrendering knowledge of the route afterward.
- **Signs:** lights moving against wind, repeated reflections without a source, knot-marks on hedges, and footsteps stopping where the road continues.
- **Threat pattern:** split groups, imitate a companion's lamp, lead pursuers into loops, and expose hidden travelers to the wrong witness.
- **Permanent defeat:** identify the true route, close the invitation that admitted them, or break the local chain of reflected signs.

## Materials and Regional Names

Hearth-tokens and glimmer scales retain a weak tendency toward order or misdirection for several days. Harvesting them from a living court-being is mutilation, not ordinary salvage.

Regional names include ashkin, little keepers, lamp-folk, lane sparks, hedge stars, brownies, sprites, and pixies.

## Story Hooks

- A Hearthling stops serving an inn because the owner has concealed a servant's death beneath the floor.
- Glimmerfolk guide refugees safely but erase the route from every adult who travels it.
- A magistrate prosecutes a household for theft after its Hearthling moves evidence into public view.
- A false lamp-line leads tax collectors toward a sanctuary village.
- Two families claim the same hearth-token during an inheritance dispute.
