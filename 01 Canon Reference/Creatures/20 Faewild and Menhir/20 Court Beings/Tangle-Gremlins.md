# Tangle-Gremlins

Tangle-Gremlins arise where repeated motion, neglected maintenance, and Lyraxis-shaped disruption overlap. They resemble narrow people assembled from cord, filings, hair, bent pins, and scraps of the machine they inhabit.

## Law and Conduct

- **Domain:** coupling, feedback, useful disorder, and the unexpected consequence of rigid systems.
- **Compulsion:** connect two mechanisms or routines that were meant to remain separate.
- **Taboo:** a machine opened, named part by part, and repaired in public silence.
- **Bargain:** a Gremlin may spare one mechanism if allowed to alter another or if given the first broken part from a new work.
- **Price:** the alteration always leaves an unplanned dependency that must later be understood.

Tangle-Gremlins know the difference between a nuisance and a murder. Their law encourages sabotage; it does not absolve a being that cuts a lift cable full of workers or ruins a winter mill.

## Signs and Ecology

Gremlins nest in warm cavities near motion: gear housings, ship bilges, bell frames, looms, mills, and siege engines. They eat grease, glue, skin oil, and the attention generated by repeated repair.

Signs include knots that re-form after cutting, matched failures in unrelated machines, missing left-handed tools, a mechanism working only when watched, and tiny diagrams scratched where no mechanic can reach.

## Threat Pattern

They switch control lines, reverse signals, release stored force, and make one repair trigger another failure. They flee through moving assemblies and prefer a crisis that keeps victims too busy to pursue them.

Worked iron does not poison them. A correctly fitted iron brace can stabilize a boundary they are exploiting, while loose nails merely become ammunition.

## Weaknesses and Permanent Defeat

- stop the mechanism and lay out its parts in their true operating order;
- recover the Gremlin's first stolen component and return it to function;
- isolate every coupled system so failure cannot travel;
- force the Gremlin to name the consequence it intended, breaking its shelter in plausible accident;
- destroy the nest while the machinery is fully still.

Gremlin wire holds a temporary sympathetic link between two moving objects. Engineers value it for testing; saboteurs value it for obvious reasons.

## Regional Names

Tangle-Gremlin, mill-knot, rigging mite, bell imp, gear brat, and the second mechanic.
