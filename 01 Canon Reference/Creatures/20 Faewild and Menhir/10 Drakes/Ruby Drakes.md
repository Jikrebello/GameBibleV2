# Ruby Drakes

Ruby drakes are furnace, ash-field, and battlefield scavengers among the six drake lineages. Like all drakes, they are Faewild court-fauna whose bodies organize around a crystallized law-gem. Their ruby alignment draws them toward concentrated heat, exposed metal, violent contest, and places repeatedly altered by fire.

They are animals with court-shaped instincts, not lesser dragons and not natural military servants. A hungry ruby drake can learn routines, recognize a handler, or return to a profitable battlefield. It does not become obedient merely because someone wounds, feeds, or collars it.

## Signs and Range

- slag pulled into low windbreak nests;
- iron tools found tooth-marked, warmed, or sorted by brightness;
- livestock killed near the chest and partly cauterized;
- stone split by repeated heating and sudden night frost;
- red scale dust in chimney crowns, charcoal pits, and ruined siege lines;
- small animals abandoning warm burrows before a drake settles nearby.

Ruby drakes prefer volcanic slopes, foundry districts, burned fortifications, Shell-scarred battlefields, and ridges where sun-warmed stone overlooks prey. They can survive cold country if they have charcoal pits, mine vents, hot springs, or regular access to worked metal and blood. A breeding pair typically controls several miles of ridge rather than a single cave.

## Creature Role

- **Encounter shape:** fast aerial skirmisher, territorial hazard, siege complication, or regional predator.
- **Opening behavior:** circles downwind, tests exposed metal or animals, then strikes at isolated heat sources.
- **Tactics:** short diving attacks, wing-driven ash, heated bites, and sudden retreats toward broken ground. A wounded drake grows less predictable rather than more submissive.
- **Lair:** high ledge or masonry cavity with two exits, a heat source, and a midden of wire, coins, nails, armor scraps, and charred bone.
- **Retreat:** an uncollared drake abandons poor prey when badly hurt. A nesting adult may instead draw pursuers away from its eggs.

## Ecology

Ruby drakes eat meat but also seek heated mineral salts and traces of worked iron. Their habit of clearing carrion and small predators can temporarily benefit a burned region. Their arrival in settled country remains dangerous: livestock, smithies, cremation grounds, and marching armies all present concentrated attractions.

Females lay one to three thick-shelled eggs in a heated midden. Young drakes are ground-running scavengers for their first season and can be mistaken for unusually large red lizards. Captive breeding usually fails because removing court conditions produces sick or malformed hatchlings.

## Weaknesses and Permanent Defeat

Cold saturation, wet ash, broken launch lines, and removal of concentrated heat sources reduce a ruby drake's speed and orientation. A frightened animal can be diverted toward an empty heat beacon, but a nesting adult is unlikely to abandon its midden.

Bodily death permanently ends the individual. A ruby law-gem can continue storing heat and contest-pattern after death, but it does not contain a routable soul or reconstruct the animal. A freed collared drake may still need treatment, distance, and a viable retreat route before it ceases attacking.

## Law-Gems and Materials

- **Ruby law-gem:** holds a pattern of heat-seeking, contest, and rapid release. It can stabilize fire scripts, furnace instruments, or heat-resistant relic settings. Extraction from a living drake is fatal.
- **Scales:** resist sparks and brief radiant heat when properly cured; prolonged fire still chars them.
- **Gland salts:** used in foundry flux, signal flares, and dangerous military incendiaries.
- **Eggshell:** valuable to heat-alchemists but legally treated as evidence of a disturbed nesting site in several realms.

Most authorities distinguish salvage from hunting. Grenzburg permits collection from a drake killed in defence, but deliberate nest-raiding requires ducal leave. Folk courts often treat the law-gem as part of the land's court ecology and demand that it be returned, buried, or exchanged under witness.

## Rune-Collar Coercion

Siege-warlocks can fit a ruby drake with a script collar keyed to pain, heat, and orientation. Such a collar does not command thought. It burns when the animal turns away from a marked bearing, rewards approach with relief, and can trigger a panic-flight toward a prepared heat source.

Collared drakes show scorched neck scales, repetitive circling, bleeding around rune studs, and attacks that continue after ordinary retreat behavior should begin. Breaking the control gem or grounding its script ends the coercion, but the freed animal remains frightened, wounded, and dangerous. Removing a live collar without cooling and interrupting its pattern can discharge the stored heat into the drake or everyone nearby.

The Alderway attacks demonstrate applied cruelty and warlock skill, not a natural bond between warlocks and drakes.

## Regional Names

Ruby drake, furnace wing, slag-back, cinder jaw, red scavenger, and battle ember. Southern cold-belt communities often distinguish vent-dwellers from migratory battlefield animals, while warmer northern foundry towns rarely make the distinction.
