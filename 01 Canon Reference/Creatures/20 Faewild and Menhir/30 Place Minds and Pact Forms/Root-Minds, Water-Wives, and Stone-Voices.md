# Root-Minds, Water-Wives, and Stone-Voices

Place minds arise when a durable landscape pattern becomes capable of attention, memory, and choice under concentrated Menhir law. They do not require worship, a dead witness, or a human oath. That distinguishes them from [[Oathwood Wardens]], which form around a specific broken compact and a human persona echo left in residue.

## Root-Minds

Root-Minds inhabit connected groves, fungal networks, hedgerows, or a single ancient tree with enough dependent life around it.

- **Domain:** succession, nutrient exchange, shade, and territorial memory.
- **Compulsion:** fill ecological vacancy and incorporate unattended matter.
- **Taboo:** severing root connections and leaving the cut ground unused.
- **Bargain and price:** safe woodland passage for seed dispersal, removal of blight, or surrender of a cultivated strip to regrowth.
- **Signs:** roots crossing fresh cuts overnight, several plants turning toward one listener, tools returned wrapped in fungal thread, and paths closing behind harvested wood.
- **Threat:** path editing, root restraint, coordinated animals, and growth through worked timber.
- **Defeat:** isolate the network, move the contested use outside its living boundary, satisfy a sustainable exchange, or destroy the sustaining grove.

## Water-Wives

Water-Wives are minds of springs, river bends, wells, mill races, or flood basins. Their apparent gender is a recurrent human reading of a form that often resembles flowing garments, hair, hands, or a face under water.

- **Domain:** flow, replenishment, carrying, and the difference between shared water and captured water.
- **Compulsion:** keep water moving through its remembered course.
- **Taboo:** taking the last usable water while concealing downstream need.
- **Bargain and price:** water, warning, or passage in exchange for clearing a channel, returning a carried object, or accepting a seasonal flood.
- **Signs:** reflected faces moving before the witness, dry vessels filling with local water, channels restoring erased bends, and one voice heard through every well on the same course.
- **Threat:** undertow, redirected channels, reflected doubles, drowning isolation, and contamination concentrated against one user.
- **Defeat:** restore a viable course, lawfully divide the water, dry the source, or break the Faewild pressure that gives it personhood.

## Stone-Voices

Stone-Voices awaken in cliffs, standing stones, caverns, fault faces, and quarries whose repeated pressure has acquired memory.

- **Domain:** load, endurance, fracture, burial, and the truth preserved by layers.
- **Compulsion:** make weight and consequence physically legible.
- **Taboo:** a hidden void beneath a public claim of safety.
- **Bargain and price:** stability, mineral guidance, or shelter in exchange for accepting a load, exposing a fault, or leaving one stratum untouched.
- **Signs:** tapping answered from unworked stone, dust arranging itself by layer, cracks spelling loads or names, and quarry echoes arriving before the blow.
- **Threat:** local gravity, closing fissures, stone resonance, and the release of old structural failure.
- **Defeat:** redistribute the load, expose the concealed void, satisfy the protected layer, or shatter the speaking formation.

## Agency and Materials

Place minds possess full agency once they can understand alternatives and choose among them. Protecting a spring or grove does not justify drowning children or crushing laborers who never knew the dispute.

Living root-script, wife-water, and voice-stone remain aligned to their place. Removing them can make useful wards or divination tools, but it may also mutilate a person or destabilize the site.

## Regional Names

Green mothers, grove minds, water-wives, well daughters, river brides, stone-voices, cliff fathers, speaking seams, dryads, and naiads.

## Story Hooks

- A Water-Wife floods an illegal mill but traps the tenant families who did not build it.
- A Stone-Voice knows why a fortress wall will fail and demands that its sealed foundation be exposed.
- Two Root-Minds contest an abandoned battlefield through incompatible successions.
- A town has mistaken an ordinary spring for a person while ignoring the real Water-Wife in its sewer channel.
- A quarry can remain open only if one lucrative stratum is never cut.
