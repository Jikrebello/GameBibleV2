# Demiplane Atlas

A demiplane is a bounded made place with an **anchor**, **governing law**, **upkeep cycle**, and **return tether**. This atlas records fourteen substantial examples without fixing future adventure outcomes.

## Operating Rules

- A local law is engineering, not moral authority.
- Ownership of the anchor does not prove ownership of inhabitants.
- Destroying a realm can kill or displace dependents even when its ruler is evil.
- A speaking place may be a person, proxy, construct, residue intelligence, or mere response pattern. Continuity must be stated.
- Human soulbinding remains evil even when used to keep a refuge or archive stable.
- Every entry's failure state is actionable geography, not flavor.

## Tiny Pockets and Containers

Extradimensional containers are object-anchored micro-demiplanes. A sound tether ejects contents into the nearest stable space when deliberately closed. A damaged or destroyed tether may instead lose contents to Drift. Placing one pocket inside another makes both return tethers oscillate; warning signs include duplicated weight, delayed retrieval, inward shadows, and objects returning to the wrong container.

## Mortal-Made Realms

### Quiet Index

- **Navigational regime:** Index-Shelves of Knots.
- **Anchor:** a human archivist's true name bound into a black catalog spine.
- **Governing law:** nothing properly indexed can be lost, but nothing may leave without a replacement reference.
- **Upkeep:** nightly cataloguing, surrendered memories, and repaired cross-references.
- **Access:** a shelf mark written in the visitor's own hand and opened through a dark archive.
- **Owner and continuity:** the Quiet Indexer, a bound-human-soul. The same human person is imprisoned in the anchor; the arrangement remains evil despite original consent.
- **Inhabitants:** mortal scholars, Proxy Bodies, index automata, and visitors held as missing references.
- **Exports:** provenance, lost records, memory copies, and routes to forgotten collections.
- **Failure state:** the Index expels unreferenced matter while its bound owner finally routes; incomplete references manifest as Lacuna Rays.
- **Story uses:** release the Indexer without erasing an archive; recover a censored genealogy; decide what living reference must replace a rescued prisoner.

### Orrery of Unspent Days

- **Navigational regime:** Knot-Sleeper Basin.
- **Anchor:** a brass orrery containing seven unrealized calendar days.
- **Governing law:** a declared action may be rehearsed but not made final until the visitor exits.
- **Upkeep:** novel choices, precise clockwork, and one day of ordinary Material time each year.
- **Access:** align the orrery with a decision not yet acted upon.
- **Owner and continuity:** a rotating circle of living human chronographers; the orrery itself is a constructed-pattern without personhood.
- **Inhabitants:** researchers, rehearsal copies, Causality Crabs, and staff responsible for ending abandoned simulations.
- **Exports:** tactical rehearsal, evidence about branching consequence, and carefully bounded delayed effects.
- **Failure state:** rehearsals become competing returns, each claiming to be the action actually taken.
- **Story uses:** prepare for a lethal assault; prove that a supposed prophecy is only one rehearsal; rescue a copy whose memories make it plead to become the final version.

## Artifact Interiors

### Shuffled House

- **Navigational regime:** Breathwaste routes through Lantern Registry.
- **Anchor:** a deck-like artifact whose drawn sequence assigns rooms and exits.
- **Governing law:** every opened threshold changes the adjacency of all unopened thresholds.
- **Upkeep:** use, shuffled uncertainty, and the continued integrity of the complete artifact.
- **Access:** draw or expose an entry face while naming a destination.
- **Owner and continuity:** no living owner is inherent. The House is a constructed-pattern; any apparent master must be classified separately.
- **Inhabitants:** stranded visitors, room-bound fauna, old custodial patterns, and creatures admitted by earlier draws.
- **Exports:** impossible adjacency, temporary shortcuts, and artifact fragments that can reassign one door.
- **Failure state:** damaged cards or faces strand rooms as separate pockets or eject them unpredictably into Material buildings.
- **Story uses:** map a changing dungeon; retrieve one missing face before a neighborhood inherits its room; discover a stranded community that regards shuffling as apocalypse.

### Midnight Glass

- **Navigational regime:** Index-Shelves by way of the Mnemonic Sargasso.
- **Anchor:** a black mirror whose silver remembers every reflected threshold after midnight.
- **Governing law:** reflection has priority over the object reflected until dawn is honestly witnessed.
- **Upkeep:** reflected entrances, darkness, and memories surrendered by users.
- **Access:** cross a reflected doorway while the original remains closed.
- **Owner and continuity:** the active interior is an astral-pattern artifact ecology. Any imprisoned or ruling intelligence must declare its own continuity; the atlas fixes none.
- **Inhabitants:** reflection doubles, lost travelers, memory fauna, and whatever a future adventure establishes behind the central pane.
- **Exports:** covert access, reflected evidence, and glass capable of holding one route.
- **Failure state:** original and reflection exchange jurisdictions, leaving Material rooms trapped behind their own images.
- **Story uses:** infiltrate an inaccessible site; rescue a person whose Double returned first; determine whether a speaking reflection is soul, residue, copy, or predator.

## Patron Micro-Kingdoms

### Weighmoot

- **Navigational regime:** Cairnmarket of the Claim-Ribs.
- **Anchor:** a ring of mutually posted weights contributed by participating patrons.
- **Governing law:** no claim is enforceable until its mass, risk, and bearer are made comparable on the Moot floor.
- **Upkeep:** posted surety, adjudicated disputes, and continual trade.
- **Access:** sponsorship by a recognized weight-holder or deposit of independent mass.
- **Owner and continuity:** a changing assembly of heterogeneous anchor-owners. The Moot is institutional infrastructure, not one person.
- **Inhabitants:** patrons, proxies, brokers, collateral keepers, observers, and coerced dependents.
- **Exports:** valuation, inter-patron arbitration, anchor shares, and dangerous debt instruments.
- **Failure state:** posted weights return to owners and every unresolved claim seeks the nearest available bearer.
- **Story uses:** expose a false valuation; free a person treated as posted mass; settle a dispute without validating the predatory [[The Concord of Weights|Concord]] members using the Moot.

### Hearth Without Weather

- **Navigational regime:** Root-Glass March.
- **Anchor:** a communal hearth carried intact through several destroyed refuges.
- **Governing law:** no external weather or route pressure enters while every admitted resident receives fuel and a place to sleep.
- **Upkeep:** shared labor, fuel, witnessed admissions, and honest population limits.
- **Access:** invitation by a current resident and physical contribution to the hearth.
- **Owner and continuity:** a Driftkin refuge council with drift-rewritten-life; no patron owns the residents.
- **Inhabitants:** Driftkin families, Material refugees, Current Shepherds, and carefully managed Protons.
- **Exports:** shelter, acclimatization, route recovery, and training in personal ballast.
- **Failure state:** concealed overpopulation or unequal fuel allocation admits the excluded weather all at once.
- **Story uses:** negotiate admission during a mass flight; expose a council hiding capacity; preserve the hearth while relocating a refuge that has outgrown it.

## Anchor-Lord Palaces

### House of the Returning Cup

- **Navigational regime:** Cairnmarket routes.
- **Anchor:** a plain cup that returns to the table from which it was freely offered.
- **Governing law:** hospitality given without coercion creates a safe return route; stolen hospitality closes one.
- **Upkeep:** meals freely shared, guests allowed to depart, and restitution for abused welcome.
- **Access:** accept water from the Returning Host without surrendering a name.
- **Owner and continuity:** the Returning Host, an Anchor-Lord with astral-pattern continuity and full agency.
- **Inhabitants:** household servitors, guests, asylum seekers, and Current Shepherds.
- **Exports:** sanctuary, return passage, mediation, and cups carrying one bounded invitation.
- **Failure state:** if the Host coerces a guest or a guest seizes the cup, the House breaks into inaccessible dining rooms.
- **Story uses:** protect a criminal entitled to guest safety; recover the cup without violating sanctuary; test whether the Host's celebrated fairness survives scarcity.

### Palace Under Folded Wind

- **Navigational regime:** Breathwaste.
- **Anchor:** seven winds folded into a fan that never fully opens.
- **Governing law:** distance is paid through surrendered direction; every arrival closes another route until the wind is unfolded again.
- **Upkeep:** mapped journeys, released winds, and visitors carrying news from distinct destinations.
- **Access:** follow a wind that names where the traveler is leaving rather than where they are going.
- **Owner and continuity:** the Folded-Wind Prince, an Anchor-Lord with astral-pattern continuity and full agency.
- **Inhabitants:** route courtiers, petitioners, Tether Hounds, and travelers unable to remember a direction home.
- **Exports:** rapid transit, redirected weather-like currents, and access to otherwise disconnected anchors.
- **Failure state:** every stored direction opens simultaneously and tears the palace into moving corridors.
- **Story uses:** purchase a route with a politically dangerous map; free travelers whose directions were taken; stop the Prince from closing a refugee path to preserve a profitable one.

## Refuge Realms

### Procession of Nine Halls

- **Navigational regime:** mobile between Ossuary Verge and Root-Glass March.
- **Anchor:** nine barge-halls linked by ritual bridges and a shared route genealogy.
- **Governing law:** the halls must change order at every waking cycle; no hall may lead twice in succession.
- **Upkeep:** movement, bridge rites, communal memory, and true-silt repair.
- **Access:** join at the rear hall under a witnessed travel name.
- **Owner and continuity:** nine Driftkin hall communities with drift-rewritten-life; ownership is distributed and contested.
- **Inhabitants:** Driftkin families, adopted Material refugees, route scouts, and symbiotic Protons.
- **Exports:** escort, mobile shelter, current charts, and living ballast craft.
- **Failure state:** a missing or stationary hall makes the remaining geometry fold inward and duplicate residents by route role.
- **Story uses:** recover a stolen hall; adjudicate leadership without freezing the procession; escort it through a patron blockade.

### Chalkhaven Recursion

- **Navigational regime:** Knot-Sleeper Basin.
- **Anchor:** a refuge plan redrawn in chalk by every generation.
- **Governing law:** a place remains safe only while inhabitants can redraw it from lived memory rather than copy.
- **Upkeep:** teaching, daily walking, new chalk, and truthful correction of changed streets.
- **Access:** draw a route one has personally walked and leave one deliberate imperfection.
- **Owner and continuity:** a Driftkin refuge assembly; the recursion is an astral-pattern place, not a person.
- **Inhabitants:** Driftkin households, teachers, Material guests, and lost copies created by failed redrawings.
- **Exports:** resilient refuge design, memory mapping, and temporary recursive shelters.
- **Failure state:** rote copying produces a perfect empty Haven while living residents become errors outside its walls.
- **Story uses:** restore experiential memory after plague; decide what to do with a copied district; protect children who know routes adults have forgotten.

## Institutional Realms

### Vortharan Spire-Annex

- **Navigational regime:** Lantern Registry alignment.
- **Anchor:** the Black Spire's recurrence joined to a session compact of Material wards.
- **Governing law:** declared continuity, mass, and exit remain distinct while the market is open.
- **Upkeep:** regulated access, archive deposits, proof work, ward repair, and host-faction cooperation.
- **Access:** a proved Collegium threshold during a scheduled alignment.
- **Owner and continuity:** no sole owner. Vortharan host factions operate it; the responsive Spire mechanism is a non-conscious constructed-pattern of unresolved pre-Atherian origin.
- **Inhabitants:** traders, patrons, proxies, Driftkin, proof crews, and temporary visitors.
- **Exports:** patron brokerage, route certification, demiplane services, and cross-realm intelligence.
- **Failure state:** undeclared mass or broken host cooperation causes routes to overlap and assigns visitors to the wrong owners or exits.
- **Story uses:** conduct a patron negotiation; survive a market closure; expose a host faction using registration to seize competitors.

### Far Cabinet of Castara

- **Navigational regime:** Index-Shelves of Knots.
- **Anchor:** a University cabinet whose drawers correspond to sealed expedition rooms.
- **Governing law:** a specimen cannot leave its drawer until provenance, hazards, and return responsibility are recorded.
- **Upkeep:** trained custodians, redundant catalogs, anchor material, and periodic Material inspection.
- **Access:** University seal, drawer key, and a live custodian's countersign.
- **Owner and continuity:** the University of Castara, a human institution; the Cabinet is a constructed-pattern.
- **Inhabitants:** researchers, quarantined expeditions, specimens, ward systems, and occasional stowaways.
- **Exports:** controlled storage, quarantine, remote field stations, and verified samples.
- **Failure state:** catalog disagreement causes drawers to open into the wrong expedition sites or classify people as specimens.
- **Story uses:** rescue a team filed under deceased; stop a specimen transfer; uncover a custodian falsifying provenance for political reasons.

## Failed Realms

### House That Forgot Its Door

- **Navigational regime:** drifts between Lantern Registry and Breathwaste.
- **Anchor:** an intact house-name whose entry relation has been erased.
- **Governing law:** every interior room remembers being entered but no threshold remembers admitting anyone.
- **Upkeep:** accidental arrival and the inhabitants' attempts to reconstruct an entrance.
- **Access:** currently possible only through a False Lane, failed teleport, or interior invitation with no destination.
- **Owner and continuity:** original owner unknown; the remaining house is an astral-pattern environment without confirmed personhood.
- **Inhabitants:** stranded people, Near-Enough rooms, scavengers, and possible descendants of earlier arrivals.
- **Exports:** doorless architecture, lost household records, and clues to erased routes.
- **Failure state:** a false door may attach the whole House to an occupied Material building and overwrite its exits.
- **Story uses:** find a missing expedition; choose which claimed home receives the House; distinguish a true trapped community from destination mimics.

### Unfinished Shore

- **Navigational regime:** Siltward currents below Cairnmarket.
- **Anchor:** thousands of refugee destination-stones naming a coast never completed.
- **Governing law:** arrivals gain a body suited to the promised refuge, but the land ends wherever no shared description exists.
- **Upkeep:** remembered landscapes, new destination-stones, and communal agreement about what safety requires.
- **Access:** cast a stone naming a lost home into a Siltward current.
- **Owner and continuity:** the founding Driftkin builders dispersed or died. The remaining realm is an astral-pattern place; copied voices are not surviving founders.
- **Inhabitants:** stranded refugees, provisional bodies, memory fauna, and settlements at incompatible shorelines.
- **Exports:** adaptive shelter matter, recovered homeland memories, and water-like Drift storage.
- **Failure state:** competing descriptions split the shore into mutually exclusive refuges and eject anyone not represented.
- **Story uses:** complete a refuge without erasing cultural differences; recover a founder's true plan from copied voices; stop a patron from buying the destination-stones as undeveloped land.
