# Taxonomy of Egg White Patrons

This creator taxonomy classifies patrons by what actually persists behind the bargain. It replaces moralized species lists and visual categories such as radiant, deathless, fey, or infernal.

## Mortal and Institutional Owners

Living humans, guilds, states, universities, churches, and magical schools may own or operate anchors. Their human members retain ordinary soul continuity. The institution itself is not a soul.

Examples include [[Demiplane Atlas#Vortharan Spire-Annex|the Vortharan Spire-Annex]] and [[Demiplane Atlas#Far Cabinet of Castara|the Far Cabinet of Castara]].

## Human Persistence Projects

Creator language may call these liches, deathless, or undying leaseholders. Each case must declare one continuity:

- **retained-human-soul:** the same person is held from routing;
- **bound-human-soul:** the person is imprisoned in an anchor;
- **refusing human soul:** a judged soul manifests from Hell rather than merely living in Astral space;
- **residue intelligence:** a new being assembled after the human soul departed;
- **reconstruction or copy:** a pattern performs the dead person's memories without being that person.

Human self-binding remains objectively evil even when voluntary. Breaking a true binding resumes the soul-road.

The [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]] supplies the best-attested ancient family of `bound-human-soul` Patrons. Meresha, Khaseret and Tamekha each preserved one complete human soul through an embalmed body and a private Egg White estate. Their inhabitants require separate classification: Hesenu remained a captive human soul, while copied servants and estate offices were never transformed into people by resemblance alone. Nemeret's failed house shows the opposite case, in which the human soul departed and a dependent Greed Mask later occupied the surviving office.

## Drift-Rewritten Owners

[[Driftkin]] individuals, processions, and refuges may lease routes or shelter. They have no routable human souls but may possess full moral agency. Their communities are not automatically patrons and do not form one Astral nation.

## Anchor-Lords

[[Anchor-Lords]] are native Astral persons whose palace-demiplanes support patronage. Genie is a local umbrella, not an elemental ancestry. Every Lord has an individual governing law, upkeep, anchor, and moral character.

## Great Drift Owners

[[Great Drift Beings|Proto-Sovereigns, Sleeping Wrongs, and Ossuary Minds]] may provide power or access. The apparent speaker might be a true Astral person, a regime-level computation, or a proxy generated by a pattern. The individual dossier must settle agency before a bargain can be judged.

## Crafted Owners

Construct networks and adaptive systems can control mass, issue terms, and enforce routes without interior personhood. Their designers, operators, owners, and standing instructions carry responsibility. A soulbound engine is classified by its prisoner as well as its machinery.

## Extrarealm Owners

Angels, saints, Menhir courtiers, Idol masks, Depth beings, damned souls, Shell patterns, and Material creatures can become stranded or establish anchors in Astral space. They retain their original continuity and allegiance. Location does not turn them into a morally neutral patron species.

## Severed Exalts

[[Severed Exalts]] is a register for singular beings that escaped, lost, or transformed an earlier office and now maintain an Astral anchor. It is not a family. Each Exalt must state origin, continuity, former office, severance, present anchor, agency, and defeat condition.

## Patron Servitors

[[Patron Servitors]] are recurring means of acting at range. Tether Hounds are fauna, Proxy Bodies are non-independent manifestations, and Current Shepherds are accountable persons. Service never overwrites continuity.

## Classification Questions

For every named patron, answer:

1. What continuity owns or speaks through the anchor?
2. Is the visible speaker the owner, a proxy, a copy, or an employee?
3. What mass creates the place?
4. What law does the place enforce?
5. What pays upkeep?
6. What can be leased, and what cannot?
7. What happens on refusal, default, death, and anchor collapse?
8. Who bears moral responsibility for enforcement?
