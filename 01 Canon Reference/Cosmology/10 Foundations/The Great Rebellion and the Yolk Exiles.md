# The Great Rebellion and the Yolk Exiles

This note is the creator-facing authority for the oldest rebellion and the origin of the sovereign powers now called the Menhir and the Idols. Mortal scriptures, star myths, court genealogies, and Giant claims preserve fragments of these events, but none sees the whole.

## Rebellion at the Making of Humanity

When humanity was made in the [[The Image, Soul, and Creaturely Continuity|Image of the Lord]], the Adversary refused the place given to humanity and called several angelic orders into rebellion. Their revolt was not caused by human dreams, vice, nature, or an error in creation. It was a chosen rejection made by created spirits capable of understanding the good they opposed.

Two great bodies joined the Adversary without sharing his final purpose:

- the office-holders and cohorts later known through the **Menhir**, whose commissions concerned created natural and mythic law;
- the office-holders and cohorts later known through the **Idols**, whose commissions concerned right human relation, desire, authority, rest, provision, courage, gratitude, and delight in another's good.

The Adversary sought terminal refusal. These cohorts sought to possess their commissions as sovereign property. Their alliance was therefore real, culpable, and unstable from the beginning.

## The Second Desertion

After the rebellion failed, both bodies deserted the Adversary rather than follow him into completed refusal. This was not repentance. While they still possessed angelic moral comprehension, they attempted to evade judgement by forcing themselves into the two metaphysical yolks pressing upon the young Material Realm.

- The future Menhir entered the Faewild and fused each principal office-holder, its cohort, and a body of created natural law.
- The future Idols entered the Shadowlands and fused with prehuman ecologies of meaning, fear, appetite, imitation, and counterfactual identity.

Joining the rebellion and choosing this flight were moral acts for which the exiles were responsible. What emerged from fusion was no longer an ordinary angelic order.

## Two Outcomes of Yolk-Fusion

Both groups became `yolk-sovereignty`, but the yolks did not preserve them in the same way.

### The Menhir

Faewild fusion subordinated each sovereign personality to the natural commission it had attempted to own. A Menhir remembers, intends, bargains, favors, retaliates, and pursues its domain, but it no longer apprehends good and evil as binding categories. It understands terms, consequences, hierarchy, trespass, exchange, and preference. It does not understand mercy, justice, guilt, or love except as behaviors displayed by other beings.

This moral blindness does not make Menhir benevolent. It also does not make heat, winter, tide, predation, fertility, or decomposition evil. Their present conduct is capricious natural sovereignty rather than an alternate moral revelation. See [[Menhir Sovereignty and Bargain]].

### The Idols

Shadowlands fusion preserved moral comprehension while turning betrayed commissions into feeding economies. The Idols know that the good exists and deliberately counterfeit it. Their continued predation is chosen, scalable, and culpable. Their [[The Counter-Countenances of the Idols|Counter-Countenances]] testify against them from within their own dominions.

The fused guilty continuity persists as a Throne even if the executive personality organizing it changes. Cultural Masks may turn over frequently; true Regnant succession is exceptionally rare and consists of a claimant voluntarily assimilating into the same culpable sovereignty. [[Idol Thrones, Regnants, and Cultural Masks]] controls this distinction.

The difference is load-bearing. Menhir law is amoral and dangerous. Idol appetite is knowingly evil. Neither is divine authority.

## Yolk-Sovereignty

`yolk-sovereignty` describes a former created spirit whose continuity has become inseparable from a yolk-scale dominion. A Menhir's named sovereign is ordinarily the enduring organizing personality of the larger fusion. For an Idol, the creator-name ordinarily names the enduring guilty Throne, while a Regnant organizes its cohort remnants, realm, law, and recurring manifestations. Neither is a fallen-angel species, a human soul, or a biological lineage.

A yolk-sovereign:

- persists through a realm, its laws, and its recurring forms rather than one body;
- cannot fully enter the Material Realm without destroying the conditions that give it sovereignty;
- can project masks or bounded manifestations, shape native ecologies, alter living lineages, and establish temporary overlays;
- cannot be summoned, killed, redeemed, or commanded by repeatable mortal procedure;
- does not create the Image or manufacture routable human souls.

The Menhir later fought the real post-fusion conflict recorded in [[The Faewild Schism]]. The Idols instead developed the seven feeding ecologies controlled by [[Idol Manifestation and Feeding]].

## First-War Remnants

Not everything abandoned during the rebellion became a sovereign. Broken banners, discarded commission-bodies, and witnesses driven from both armies persist in the wild Shadowlands as [[First-War Remnants]]. Named fallen angels remain individual `created-spirit` persons governed by [[Fallen Angels and Broken Commissions]], not members of these residue families.

## What Mortals Get Wrong

- Human dreams did not create the Shadowlands, Faewild, Menhir, or Idols.
- The Menhir and Idols are not servants, children, or regional names of the Adversary.
- Deserting the Adversary was not repentance.
- Menhir exactness is not justice, affection, or moral approval.
- Idol complexity does not make their feeding morally neutral.
- Ancient Titanic descent from bounded Menhir manifestations, an Idol Throne acting through a dependent Mask, or a separately autonomous Archmask is genuinely possible and often real. Giant courts nevertheless compress remote ancestry, patronage, alteration, imitation, adoption, and fraud into claims that the present dynasty is the immediate, pure, and exclusive child of its named power. [[Giant Descent and Creaturely Continuity]] controls the distinction.
- Similar symbols do not establish common origin: Wrath's fire is not Hell, a patron is not an Idol, and a court beast is not an angel.
