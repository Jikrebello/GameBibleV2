# The Great Rebellion and the Yolk Exiles

This note is the creator-facing authority for the oldest rebellion and the origin of the sovereign powers now called the Menhir and the Idols. Mortal scriptures, star myths, court genealogies, and Giant claims preserve fragments of these events, but none sees the whole.

## Rebellion at the Making of Humanity

When humanity was made in the [[The Image, Soul, and Creaturely Continuity|Image of the Lord]], the Adversary refused the place given to humanity and called several angelic orders into rebellion. Their revolt was not caused by human dreams, vice, nature, or an error in creation. It was a chosen rejection made by created spirits who understood that they were rejecting a good.

Two great cohorts fought beside the Adversary without sharing his final purpose:

- the orders later known through the **Menhir**, whose commissions concerned created natural and mythic law;
- the orders later known through the **Idols**, whose commissions concerned right human relation, desire, authority, rest, provision, courage, gratitude, and delight in another's good.

The Adversary sought terminal refusal. These cohorts sought to possess their commissions as sovereign property. Their alliance was therefore real, culpable, and unstable from the beginning.

## The Second Desertion

After the rebellion failed, both cohorts deserted the Adversary rather than follow him into completed refusal. This was not repentance. They attempted to evade judgement by forcing their identities into the two metaphysical yolks that pressed upon the young Material Realm.

- The future Menhir entered the Faewild and fused with created natural and mythic law.
- The future Idols entered the Shadowlands and fused with its prehuman ecologies of meaning, fear, appetite, imitation, and counterfactual identity.

The act changed both cohorts irreversibly. They ceased to be ordinary angels while remaining accountable for the choices that made them what they are.

## Yolk-Sovereignty

The controlled continuity `yolk-sovereignty` describes a former created spirit whose identity has become inseparable from a yolk-scale dominion. It is not a fallen-angel species, a human soul, or a biological lineage.

A yolk-sovereign:

- persists through a realm, its laws, and its recurring forms rather than one body;
- cannot fully enter the Material Realm without destroying the conditions that give it sovereignty;
- can project masks, shape native ecologies, alter living lineages, and establish temporary overlays;
- retains moral responsibility for the original rebellion and its present dominion;
- cannot be summoned, killed, redeemed, or commanded by a repeatable mortal procedure.

Individual Menhir portfolios and Idol dossiers describe what their fusion permits. [[Idol Manifestation and Feeding]] controls the seven predatory sovereigns. Menhir origins require a later dedicated reconciliation, but their current portfolios and thirty-form Faewild ecology remain canon.

## The Suppressed Commissions

Yolk fusion did not make the original commission unreal. Each Idol continually suppresses the created good it was commissioned to serve. This surviving contradiction appears as a [[The Counter-Countenances of the Idols|Counter-Countenance]] inside the Idol's own dominion.

A Counter-Countenance is neither a second deity nor proof that the Idol is morally balanced. It is the testimony of a betrayed commission, incapable of being converted into a cult or a dependable source of power.

## First-War Remnants

Not every thing abandoned during the rebellion became a sovereign. Broken banners, discarded commission-bodies, and witnesses driven from both armies persist in the wild Shadowlands as [[First-War Remnants]]. Named fallen angels remain individual `created-spirit` persons governed by [[Fallen Angels and Broken Commissions]], not members of these residue families.

## What Mortals Get Wrong

- Human dreams did not create the Shadowlands or the Idols.
- The Menhir and Idols are not servants, children, or regional names of the Adversary.
- Deserting the Adversary did not make either cohort good.
- A Giant claim of descent from a Menhir or Idol normally compresses patronage, alteration, imitation, or propaganda into genealogy.
- Similar symbols do not establish common origin: Wrath's fire is not Hell, a patron is not an Idol, and a court beast is not an angel.
