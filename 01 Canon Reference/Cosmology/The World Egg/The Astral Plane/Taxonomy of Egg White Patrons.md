# 1) The Anchor-Owners

_“Stand on my ground, and you will not dissolve.”_

### What they are

Beings who possess or physically inhabit a major anchor: divine debris, a relic-lode, an oath massif, a fossilized law fragment, or a shard of something greater. Their identity is often inseparable from their anchor.

### Why they offer pacts

- **Footprint expansion:** their anchor is stable but local; a mortal extends their reach into yolk-space.
- **Mass acquisition:** the mortal can retrieve relics, names, contracts, and other mass-currency.
- **Route enforcement:** they want reliable access points—thin spaces, sutures, and gates.

### Personality pattern

Transactional, territorial, pragmatic. They think like landlords, harbor-masters, or fortress-captains.

### What their magic looks like (patron “portfolio”)

- anchoring wards, resistance to distortion, boundary enforcement,
- “place-making” (creating safe rooms, doors, demiplane pockets),
- gravity/weight/insistence effects.

### Typical pact terms

- you maintain a shrine-anchor in the Material,
- you bring back mass-currency on a schedule,
- you establish a “standing place” (a safe point) for them to route influence.

# 2) The Rent-Engines

_“I run a machine that pays reality to stay consistent.”_

### What they are

Entities that contain a self-sustaining upkeep system—often built from repeating rites, cyclical sacrifices, or closed-loop conversions of one resource into coherence. Some are living demiplanes; some are former archmages turned into infrastructure.

### Why they offer pacts

- **Feedstock needs:** their engine requires ongoing input (memory, heat, vows, attention, time).
- **Externalization:** it’s cheaper to harvest resources via agents in yolk-space than to raid directly.
- **Redundancy:** they want distributed “backup nodes” in case their engine falters.

### Personality pattern

Operational, procedural, sometimes eerily polite—like a civil service or a machine with etiquette.

### Magic portfolio

- ritual amplification, “battery” casting, debtwork tools,
- protective clauses (sanctuary bubbles, emergency resets),
- repayment mechanics (you suffer later so you can win now).

### Pact terms

- you tithe an agreed resource periodically,
- you keep a rite running (daily pattern, monthly alignment),
- you provide “audit reports” (truthful accounts that become part of the engine).

# 3) The Route-Barons

_“I control how you get there.”_

### What they are

Patrons whose power is navigation: they own current-lanes, control sutures between anchored regions, or possess unique methods of transit (tethers, convoy-lantern chains, map-true naming).

### Why they offer pacts

- **Network growth:** each pact-holder is another node that makes their route system more profitable and resilient.
- **Monopoly enforcement:** mortal agents sabotage rival routes, seed false maps, or secure waystations.
- **Exploration:** mortals scout new thin spaces and lane openings.

### Personality pattern

Mercantile, predatory, information-hoarding. Think “smuggler prince,” “cartographer king,” or “naval admiral.”

### Magic portfolio

- teleport-like “arrivals” with conditions,
- concealment, misdirection, pursuit-breakers,
- map-sight, lane-reading, “wrong turn” curses.

### Pact terms

- you must never reveal certain paths,
- you must plant their signs at thresholds,
- you must pay tolls in secrets or maps.

# 4) The Name-Collectors

_“Identity is currency. I mint it.”_

### What they are

Entities that specialize in Metaphysical Mass via **names, titles, and true labels**—harvesting them, refining them, binding them into lattices. They survive because naming is one of the most efficient anchors in high-entropy space.

### Why they offer pacts

- **Name extraction:** mortals can obtain true-names, birth-names, throne-titles, and secret epithets.
- **Catalog expansion:** they want their naming system to become the default in yolk-space.
- **Control by definition:** once something is correctly named under their system, it becomes legible—and therefore governable.

### Personality pattern

Scholastic, obsessive, immaculate, sometimes cruelly pedantic. They are rarely emotional; they are proprietary.

### Magic portfolio

- bindings, compulsions, “can’t cross this line” wards,
- true-seeing, disillusion, anti-glamour,
- identity rewriting (rename yourself, shed a title, steal a title).

### Pact terms

- you must deliver names and preserve records,
- you accept a patron-mark (a title that follows you),
- you must not lie in formal declarations.

# 5) The Wreck-Kings (Salvage Sovereigns)

_“Everything that dies becomes my material.”_

### What they are

Beings who rule debris fields, corpse-reefs, and ossuary belts. They are powerful because they have access to **divine rubble** and **fossil law fragments**—the best construction and enchantment substrate in the Egg White.

### Why they offer pacts

- **Supply chain:** they need a steady stream of salvage from yolk-space catastrophes and thin-space casualties.
- **Weaponization:** they want relics that can stabilize or destabilize rival holdings.
- **Prestige:** the most valuable mass often exists in yolk-space (saint relics, repeated rites, historic oath-sites).


### Personality pattern

Hard, opportunistic, feudal. They treat reality like a scrapyard kingdom.

### Magic portfolio

- artifact infusion, bone-and-shard crafting,
- fortification and siege metaphysics,
- summoning of “constructed” entities (made from debris + vow).

### Pact terms

- you must deliver materials and keep “claim flags” active,
- you may be required to start disasters or exploit them,
- you must never allow certain relics to be sanctified (sanctification makes them harder to salvage).

# 6) The Severed Exalts

_“I was made for an absolute. Now I survive by contract.”_

### What they are

Exiled beings originally aligned to outer extremes—angelic or infernal agents, or related high-grade servitors—who have been cut off long enough that their function is no longer fed by that pole. They become Egg White–adapted: less crusade/temptation, more survival and leverage.

### Why they offer pacts

- **Relevance:** they need agents in yolk-space to remain part of the world’s story.
- **Re-fueling:** they can’t draw directly from the outer pole, so they require substitute fuels (oaths, attention, fear, devotion, sacrifice).
- **Return projects:** some are building a path back; others are preventing anyone else from doing so.

### Personality pattern

Intense and purposeful, but strange—less moral certainty, more “functionality.” They can be terrifyingly calm.

### Magic portfolio

- “borrowed absolutes” (brief moments of impossible clarity or impossible corruption),
- smiting/banishing analogues as procedural effects (boundary edits),
- oaths, geasa, and taboo-driven power.

### Pact terms

- you carry a prohibition that preserves their function,
- you act as their witness, herald, or collector,
- you protect or sabotage specific gates in the shell.

# 7) The Sleeping Wrong (Failure-State Titans)

_“My dream is a machine. Your life is a lever.”_

### What they are

Not truly dead. Their remains are stuck in a repeating becoming-loop, paying their own rent by consuming tiny increments of nearby coherence. They are dangerous patrons because their power is vast but **nonlinear** and **side-effect heavy**.

### Why they offer pacts

- **New variables:** mortal agents introduce novelty into the loop, potentially allowing it to progress or break.
- **External anchors:** they need stable nodes in yolk-space to stabilize the “wake sequence.”
- **Coherence harvesting:** pacts draw resources the loop can metabolize.

### Personality pattern

Dreamlike, alien, not reliably communicative. They express intent through recurrence and pattern, not dialogue.

### Magic portfolio

- loop effects (redo, delay, echo), probability distortion,
- “almost happened” conjurations,
- memory editing as a byproduct of temporal fray.

### Pact terms

- repeated rites at specific intervals,
- taboo against breaking patterns,
- periodic “offerings of novelty” (a first experience, a name never spoken, a choice never made).

# 8) The Proto-Sovereigns (Native Great-Beasts)

_“I am an organism that learned to own a climate.”_

### What they are

Exceptionally large or old Egg White fauna that have become quasi-territorial over a regime (a current-lane, an anchor reef, a driftstorm corridor). They are “animal” in origin but “sovereign” in effect.

### Why they offer pacts

- **Territory enforcement:** agents mark routes, drive off hunters, spread breeding conditions.
- **Feedstock acquisition:** they require a specific resource that yolk-space supplies (rare memory patterns, repeated vows, living attention).
- **Reproduction:** some pacts exist to seed larval forms into thin spaces.

### Personality pattern

Instinctive, seasonal, domain-bound. Communication is omen-like: appetite, migration, threat display.

### Magic portfolio

- survival boons (resistance to drift, navigation, predator-sense),
- bodily adaptation, metamorphic toolsets,
- “regime control” (calling driftstorms, calming them, bending lanes).

### Pact terms

- you must act as a vector (carry spores/eggs/marks),
- you must hunt specific targets or protect specific sites,
- you must never shelter prey it has claimed.

# How patrons differ by “goal type” (quick sorting tool)

When you invent a new patron, pick one prime objective; everything else follows.

1. **Expansion:** establish more stable nodes and shrines in yolk-space.
2. **Acquisition:** gather mass-currencies (relics, names, contracts, memories).
3. **Network Control:** monopolize routes, gates, thin spaces, and crossings.
4. **Survival:** pay rent, keep the engine running, avoid repossession.
5. **Restoration:** reassemble a dead/sundered self or return to an outer pole.
6. **Containment:** prevent rivals, prevent awakenings, prevent gate openings.

# What “warlock power” actually is in your cosmology

A warlock is not receiving “raw divinity.” They are receiving one (or more) of:

- **Stability allotment:** temporary coherence so their magic can function beyond local rules.
- **Access rights:** permission to stand on the patron’s anchor (and draw from it).
- **Route privileges:** shortcuts and safe arrivals along controlled lanes.
- **Debt instruments:** the ability to “borrow” order now and pay later.
- **Name authority:** binding and enforcement through titles and labels.
- **Exported regime effects:** carrying a piece of Egg White physics into yolk-space.

This keeps warlocks distinct from yolk-native spellcasters: warlock magic feels like **licensed metaphysics**.

# A few distinct “patron personalities” you can reuse

- **The Landlord:** calm, contractual, punitive only when rent lapses.
- **The Navigator:** charming, secretive, values leverage and silence.
- **The Archivist:** precise, controlling, obsessed with names and definition.
- **The Engine:** polite, procedural, horrifyingly fair.
- **The Wreck-Baron:** blunt, feudal, treats relics as currency and people as labor.
- **The Severed Herald:** intense, duty-shaped, not moral so much as functional.
- **The Loop-Dreamer:** alien, repetitive, offers power as a side-effect of waking.
- **The Great Beast:** instinctive, ecological, demands service as a survival relation.
