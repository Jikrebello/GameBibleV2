### Lunara

*Second Circle — The Tide-Queen and the Hunt of Night*

------

#### Appearance and Forms

- **Primary Form (The Pale Current):** Lunara appears as a tall, luminous figure wrapped in robes that move like slow water. Her skin carries the soft, cold sheen of moonlit stone; her eyes hold shifting phases—waxing, waning, and dark—like a clock that never stops counting. Her hair falls in long streams of silver-black, flowing as if suspended underwater, stirred by unseen tides rather than wind.
- **Wolf Form (The Great She-Wolf):** Lunara becomes a gigantic wolf of silver fur and star-dark undercoat, her breath misting into curling ribbons that cling to the ground. In this form she is not “a mother of monsters,” but the **patron of pack-law**: scent, pursuit, hunger, and the ruthless unity of the hunt. Her gaze carries the stillness of a predator that has already chosen the moment of the kill.
- **Mist Form (The Fog-Tide):** Lunara may dissolve into a low, pale fog that slides through forests, down gullies, and across water. The fog is not “fear magic”; it is **tide without ocean**—a lunar current moving across land. In it, distance lies and direction drifts. Travelers emerge miles away, barefoot, salted, and unable to explain how they crossed stone as if it were shore.
- **Eclipse Form (The Black Phase):** When the moon vanishes, Lunara is not absent—she becomes **unlocatable**. The night feels wider than it should; stars seem incorrectly placed. Creatures that hunt by sound go silent, and the world behaves as if it has forgotten the comfort of cycles.

------

#### Implements and Signs of Power

Lunara’s tools do not “break reality.” They **pull** on it the way the moon pulls on seas.

- **The Silver Chalice (Chalice of Tides):** A silver vessel that holds an ever-shifting liquid—sometimes water, sometimes milk-white dew, sometimes ink-dark brine. To drink is to be made receptive to **lunar patterns**: migration routes, breeding seasons, hidden currents, the rhythm beneath a place.
  - It grants visions in the sense that it forces the body to understand what the mind cannot: the tides of hunger, the pull of home, the scent-map of the world. Those who drink too deeply may lose the “human timetable” and begin living by moon-law instead.
- **The Crescent Sickles (The Waning and Waxing):** Two crescent blades of pale metal that cut not the soul, but the **anchor** of a creature’s fixed form. Their strike does not send you into dreams; it makes you temporarily **unstable**, as if your body remembers older shapes and tries them on. Wounds from these sickles heal strangely—scar tissue forming patterns like wave-lines or fur-growth.

------

#### Nature and Character

Lunara is not a manipulative matriarch and not a dispenser of “forbidden knowledge.” She is the **cycle that hunts**—the cold, periodic force that commands tides, instincts, and the nocturnal half of the living world.

- **Keeper of Cycles:** Lunara governs the recurring truths that no civilization can legislate away: tides, seasons of mating and molting, migrations, the rise and fall of predator and prey. She does not “judge.” She **returns**.
- **Predatory Mercy:** Under Lunara, mercy exists in the way the pack kills quickly and the winter prunes the weak so the spring may come. Her kindness is never comfortable. It is the kindness of a clean ending and a path back into the cycle.
- **Instinct Made Sovereign:** Where Solzareth exposes by light, Lunara exposes by **pull**. In her presence, creatures feel their ancient instincts rise: to travel, to mate, to hunt, to hide, to howl. Mortals interpret this as madness because they are unaccustomed to being moved by something older than reason.
- **The Unknowable Law of Night:** The cosmic terror of Lunara is not nightmares—it is the realization that the night is not a “time without the sun.” It is a living regime with its own rules, and those rules remain indifferent to human dignity.

------

#### Worship and Influence

Lunara’s worship is not a centralized priesthood. It is a network of **night-rites, pack-vows, and tide-observances** practiced where moonlight touches water, forest, and stone.

- **Moon-Sites (not temples):** Her sacred places are **shore-caves, tidal pools, forest clearings that flood with mist**, and high cliffs where the moon seems close enough to reach. Offerings are laid where moonlight falls cleanly—on water, on pale stone, on silver bark.
- **Rites of the Full and the Dark:**
  - **Full Moon:** pack-binding, hunting vows, fertility rites, and oath-sworn journeys.
  - **New Moon:** silence vigils, concealment rites, the hiding of names, and “lost-road” ceremonies where travelers beg the night to mislead pursuers.
  - **Half Moons:** rites of balance—calling back those whose instincts are overtaking them.
- **Offerings:** silver, salt, rare herbs that bloom at night, the first catch from night fishing, the first blood drawn in a hunt—always framed as **exchange with the cycle**, not appeasement of a temper.
- **Moon-Readers (not high priestesses):** Her attendants are trackers, midwives, tide-watchers, and nocturnal guides who read the moon as a calendar of living things. They are consulted for migrations, births, hunts, and disappearances—not for moral decrees.

------

#### Symbols and Relics

- **Symbols:** the crescent moon over water, a wolf’s pawprint half-submerged, a silver chalice ringed with wave-lines, a spiral of tidal foam.
- **Relics:** moonstone amulets that steady a shifting body, salt-thread talismans that mark safe paths through fog-tides, and crescent blades etched with phase-marks. These objects do not grant “dark power”; they grant **stability** or **access**—the ability to endure the night’s pull without being torn apart by it.

------

#### Followers

- **Lycanthropes and Moon-Touched:** Those whose bodies respond strongly to lunar pull—skinwalkers, shapeshifters, and the moon-marked. They are not inherently “evil.” They are people living closer to the animal calendar than to human society.
- **Trackers, Midwives, and Tide-Walkers:** Hunters who respect pack-law, coastal folk who live by tides, and guides who navigate fog and forest by scent, sound, and moon-angle.
- **Exiles and Pilgrims:** Those who must vanish, change, or travel far—runaways, oathbreakers seeking re-binding, and wanderers who accept that the safest road is the one that cannot be mapped.

------

#### Common Phenomena

- **Fog-Tides:** Mist rolling inland like a tide, dragging scent and sound out of place. Roads bend. Distances lie. Travelers arrive where they did not intend, as if the land itself became shoreline.
- **Pack-Weather:** Wolves (and sometimes people) moving in unnatural coordination—silent, disciplined, purposeful—like a storm front made of bodies.
- **Moon-Shift Nights:** Under certain phases, the boundary between fixed forms softens. Some creatures molt into new shapes; some humans briefly become more animal than person. This is not punishment or hysteria—it is **Faewild potential pressing through the cycle**.

------

#### Common Bargains and Prices

Lunara grants the gifts of the night—at the cost of daylight certainty.

- **Boon:** heightened senses, safe passage through mist and dark, pack-bonding that cannot be broken by distance, controlled shifting, and the ability to “hear” currents in land and sea.
- **Price:** loss of fixedness. Your sleep, appetite, and desire begin to follow lunar rhythm. You become harder to recognize in the social sense—less predictable, more migratory, more governed by season than schedule. Some lose their name for a phase; others lose the comfort of staying still.

------

#### Summary

Lunara is the moon as predator and calendar: the tide that climbs land, the instinct that rises in blood, the pack-law that makes the hunt clean and inevitable. Her horror is not psychological manipulation—it is the vast, indifferent truth that the night has its own sovereignty, and the living world obeys it whether they wish to or not.
