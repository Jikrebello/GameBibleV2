# Idol Thrones, Regnants, and Cultural Masks

This note controls personal continuity, cultural identity, and succession within the seven Idols. An Idol is one guilty `yolk-sovereignty`, but it is not one immutable face or biography. The sovereignty persists through a **Throne** while a mutable **Regnant** organizes it and many simultaneous **Masks** express it among mortals.

The distinction permits real cultural identities and very rare changes of ruler without turning an Idol into a neutral divine office, without allowing a human soul to become an Idol or god, and without treating humanity as the creator of the Shadowlands.

> **Controlling sequence:** Betrayed Commission → Hunger → Throne → Regnant → Masks / Archmasks → historical Sediment.

## The Seven Strata

| Stratum | Authorial meaning | Stability |
|---|---|---|
| **Betrayed Commission** | The created good entrusted to the original angelic order. Its historical reality grounds the Idol's guilt and survives as the witness expressed by the Counter-Countenance. | Cannot be rewritten by cult, Regnant, or succession. |
| **Hunger** | The knowingly chosen predatory economy that counterfeits the betrayed good. | Invariant while that Idol remains an Idol. |
| **Throne** | The durable `yolk-sovereignty`: fused cohort remnants, Shadowland heartland, realm-law, accumulated feeding architecture, and guilty continuity. “Throne” is an ontological term, not necessarily a chair or palace. | Endures through changes of face and the rare change of Regnant. |
| **Regnant** | The executive personality presently organizing the Throne, speaking through its deepest routes and coordinating its harvest. | Mutable, but true succession is exceptionally rare. |
| **Masks** | Simultaneous projected organs shaped for a culture, institution, title, object, dream-route, or local feeding network. A Mask may have a genuine name, biography, iconography, and voice without being the whole Idol. | Commonly born, altered, merged, discarded, or renamed. |
| **Archmasks** | Autonomous Shadow persons with independent continuity and moral agency. They may found courts, betray a Regnant, or attempt to become more than a projected organ. | Rare, durable, and individually culpable. |
| **Sediment** | Absorbed memories, former cult biographies, discarded identities, defeated or former Regnants, and ancient layers of worship retained within or around the Throne. | Accumulates. It may remain dormant, speak through a route, or acquire another anchor. |

The **Throne**, not the current Regnant, is the continuous guilty sovereign created by the original fusion. A new Regnant does not erase the first rebellion, make the Hunger innocent, or create a new Idol. It becomes the organizing personality of the same culpable sovereignty. Each Throne ordinarily has one Regnant at a time; an interregnum, divided regnancy, or rival claimant controlling only some routes requires a dedicated exception.

## Cultural Masks Are Real

An Idol does not possess one hidden humanoid body of which every cultural depiction is merely a mistaken portrait. Its many identities are its normal means of being present. A war culture's burning champion, a court's wounded avenger, and a household's inherited feud-name may all be real Masks of Wrath at the same time.

Their truth is bounded:

- a Mask genuinely expresses and feeds its Throne, but never exhausts it;
- two cultures may encounter different, even contradictory biographies without either having invented the entire power;
- a Mask may lie about being the primordial sovereign, a dead hero, a saint, or the only true face;
- destroying or reforming one Mask does not destroy the Throne;
- ordinary Masks remain dependent projected organs even when they speak, remember, plead, or receive worship.

Humanity therefore **shapes without creating** the Idols. Unchosen fear, fantasy, desire, and collective expectation give local weather, imagery, and routes. Culture supplies vocabulary and biography. Repeated chosen vice gives the manifestation moral and metaphysical mass. Institutions give that vice architecture, inheritance, and scale. The Idol then teaches its shaped image back to the culture in a parasitic feedback loop.

## From Mask to Archmask

An ordinary Mask cannot seize a Throne. It must first acquire autonomous continuity and become an Archmask. This ordinarily requires several durable anchors: a self-renewing cult or institution, a stable Shadow court, accumulated memory across the loss of individual vessels, and the capacity to choose against the present Regnant's immediate command.

Autonomy is not innocence. An Archmask arose within a chosen predatory ecology and is responsible for the choices its agency permits. Rebellion against a Regnant may be self-liberation, rival appetite, or an attempt to possess the same Hunger; it is not automatically repentance.

## True Accession

True Regnant succession is not the filling of a vacant neutral office. It is **voluntary assimilation into the existing guilty Throne**. A successful claimant accepts the Hunger, the realm-law, the accumulated feeding architecture, and the culpable continuity it intends to command. The Throne also changes the claimant until its identity can organize the whole sovereignty. After accession, the claimant no longer persists as merely an autonomous `idol-pattern`; it is the organizing person within the Throne's `yolk-sovereignty`.

The previous Regnant need not simply vanish. It may be:

- absorbed into the Throne's sediment as a memory and pressure;
- reduced to an Archmask or buried voice;
- stranded as a regional “god” whose cult mistakes survival for continuing sovereignty;
- attached to a relic personality or sealed court;
- exceptionally severed into an Astral anchor, in which case it requires a dedicated [[Severed Exalts|Severed Exalt]] dossier.

No repeatable mortal rite can appoint a Regnant. A cult coup, dynastic replacement, new divine name, destroyed idol, or changed public iconography is presumed to be Mask turnover unless a core authorial dossier explicitly establishes accession.

## Rarity Rule

Across all seven Idols, recorded human history contains no more than a handful of true Regnant successions. The exact cases remain unassigned until a story needs them. Most apparent “deaths of gods,” changing divine dynasties, syncretic mergers, and rival incarnations are changes among Masks, Archmasks, mortal institutions, or remembered sediment.

This asymmetry is intentional: cultural faces should be abundant; actual throne changes should be epochal. A true accession can reorganize a Shadow heartland, alter several otherwise unrelated Masks, awaken ancient sediment, and change how a feeding economy bargains without changing which good it betrays.

## Human Heroes, Legends, and Souls

A human soul cannot become an Idol, Mask, Archmask, or Regnant. It remains a human soul and follows the continuity rules in [[The Image, Soul, and Creaturely Continuity]]. No fame, vice, worship, relic, dream, or act of self-assertion manufactures another kind of soul.

A **legend-pattern** grown from public memory of a human may nevertheless become an `idol-pattern` Mask. Residue, copied memory, propaganda, and a role repeated by later people can give it the dead person's face and biography. If it later acquires autonomous Shadow continuity, that Archmask is a new nonhuman person, not the dead hero returned. The original human soul remains distinct even if it was temporarily bound or exploited.

Consequently, a tale that “King A became Wrath” may conceal a cult adopting the king's image, a residue-based Mask, an Archmask claiming the biography, or—at the most exceptional—a legend-pattern acceding as Regnant. None of these is mortal apotheosis.

## Counter-Countenances and Guilt

A [[The Counter-Countenances of the Idols|Counter-Countenance]] belongs to the betrayed commission and witnesses against the whole Throne. It is neither an uncorrupted alternate Regnant nor a candidate for accession. It cannot take command, make the Hunger benevolent, or redeem the sovereignty from within.

Succession can redistribute personal responsibility without breaking inherited guilt. The first fusion remains culpable. A later Regnant also becomes guilty by knowingly assimilating into and directing the Hunger. Former Regnants and autonomous Archmasks remain answerable for their own acts.

## Difference from the Menhir

Menhir names usually record cultural interpretations, court-titles, or encounter modes of one enduring natural sovereignty. Their personality is subordinated to natural law, and ordinary Menhir continuity has no Regnant succession cycle.

Idol Masks are more invasive and biographical because human culture is part of their feeding machinery. The distinction is:

- **Menhir:** one enduring organizing personality, many cultural readings of the same natural law;
- **Idol:** one enduring guilty Throne, potentially different Regnants, and many simultaneous predatory identities.

No apparent replacement should be assigned to a Menhir without a separate core ruling. Court succession beneath a Menhir is not succession of the Menhir itself.

## Vault Usage

- Plain creator names such as **Wrath**, **Greed**, and **Pride** name the whole Throne and feeding economy.
- Proper names such as **Furor**, **Avarix**, and **Vaingloria** remain valid index names for the sovereignty. Their use alone does not prove that one personal Regnant has held the name since the Great Rebellion.
- Regional names and biographies are Masks unless a controlling dossier says they name a Regnant, Archmask, or older sediment.
- Existing shorthand such as “Furor is the Idol of Wrath” refers to the whole sovereignty through its organizing name; it does not overrule this continuity model.
- A proposed accession requires a dedicated author-only dossier stating claimant continuity, former anchors, method of assimilation, previous Regnant's fate, changes across the heartland, and why the event is one of history's exceptional cases.
