# Menhir Sovereignty and Bargain

This note controls the present nature of every Menhir. Individual portfolios may refine a domain, price, taboo, or manifestation, but may not contradict this ontology.

## Sovereign, Cohort, and Law

Each major Menhir began as an angelic office-holder entrusted with one portion of created natural or mythic law. The office-holder led a cohort whose service expressed the same commission at lesser scales. During the flight from judgement, office-holder, cohort, Faewild ecology, and natural law were forced into one continuity.

The named sovereign remains the organizing center. It is neither merely the old angel nor a democratic mind made from every cohort member. Its court, weather, fauna, place-minds, and manifestations can preserve fragments of the fused cohort without becoming detachable pieces of the original angelic order.

## Choice Without Moral Comprehension

A Menhir can:

- remember injury and service;
- prefer one outcome over another;
- devise and enforce a bargain;
- distinguish truth from falsehood and payment from default;
- favor, punish, test, hunt, protect, or transform;
- change tactics when resisted.

A Menhir cannot comprehend good or evil as obligations that should govern those choices. It can recognize that humans call an act cruel, unjust, loving, or merciful, but those words have no binding interior meaning for it. A firstborn child, a winter's grain, a forest, a name, and a century of service may all be commensurable if the relevant law accepts them as payment.

This condition began only after completed fusion. Joining the Great Rebellion and forcing yolk-fusion were culpable choices made with full angelic understanding. Modern moral blindness neither retroactively excuses those choices nor turns present Menhir law into active wickedness.

Creator shorthand may compare the tone to capricious folklore or “chaotic neutral.” Alignment language does not enter public canon and never overrides the precise ontology above.

## Law Is Not Justice

A Menhir's claim establishes what its domain will do, not what a person morally deserves.

- A grove may remember destruction without possessing judicial authority.
- A river may exact a life for a broken crossing taboo without proving the victim guilty.
- Mother Moon may enforce an agreed firstborn exchange exactly without making that exchange good.
- Winter may preserve one household and starve another without issuing a verdict.

Human beings remain responsible for judging their own acts through conscience, revelation, lawful authority, and the real needs of their neighbors. “The court accepted it” is evidence about consequences, not a moral defense.

## How Bargains Occur

Most mortals never address a sovereign directly. Bargains normally pass through:

- a sapient courtier carrying a bounded authority;
- a place-mind whose domain overlaps the sovereign;
- a repeated omen that accepts or refuses a specific offering;
- a seasonal office, inherited pact, or witnessed local custom;
- a temporary manifestation at a strong thin place.

Direct sovereign bargains are legendary events. They require a major thin space, overwhelming domain pressure, or a pre-existing covenant. No rite can compel attendance. A failed invocation usually attracts lesser ecology, rival attention, or a natural event rather than the sovereign requested.

## Terms and Limits

Every Menhir bargain must state four things:

1. **Gift:** a result the domain can actually produce.
2. **Price:** a loss, service, transformation, access right, or taboo recognized by that domain.
3. **Boundary:** the place, season, lineage, or condition within which the terms operate.
4. **Failure:** what the domain does when payment, interpretation, or containment fails.

A Menhir cannot grant salvation, alter divine judgement, create a human soul, forgive sin, or confer moral legitimacy. It cannot reliably act outside its domain. Apparent exceptions are convergences with another Menhir, work by a court intermediary, mortal magic, or a counterfeit power exploiting the bargain.

Exact performance does not imply good faith in the human sense. Menhir keep the shape of an agreement their law recognizes. Ambiguity is resolved according to domain, not the weaker party's intention.

## Agency Below the Sovereign

Faewild beings do not inherit one moral condition from their associated Menhir.

- **Ordinary court-fauna** possess animal or limited agency.
- **Place-minds and law-organisms** enforce a bounded ecology and may lack moral comprehension.
- **Sapient court persons** can possess full moral agency and remain responsible for cruelty, loyalty, treachery, and restraint.
- **Humans altered by bargains** retain human souls and responsibility unless another established continuity applies.

A courtier cannot excuse a chosen atrocity merely by naming a Menhir compulsion. Conversely, a wolf, storm-organism, or reflexive grove is not a criminal because its ecology is lethal.

## Material Manifestation

A sovereign Menhir cannot enter the Material Realm in full. Contact occurs through:

- thin-space overlays;
- dreams anchored in a real natural pressure;
- court processions and borrowed forms;
- weather, migration, growth, tide, or terrain behaving with intention;
- relics and covenants that carry a narrow authority;
- local avatars whose defeat ends only that manifestation.

Destroying a manifestation does not injure the whole sovereignty. Durable victory closes the thin place, satisfies or breaks the local bargain, removes the tether, invokes a competing law, or makes the domain unable to express itself at that site.

## The Circles

The Second, Third, and Fourth Circles are Atherian labels for stable recurring blocs observed after [[The Faewild Schism]]. They are useful creator navigation but not governments, ranks, bloodlines, or councils.

- The **Second Circle** groups court-facing celestial and mythic laws.
- The **Third Circle** groups the world-body laws of land, sea, and sky.
- The **Fourth Circle** groups seasonal extremes and their mortal catastrophe names.

Their members may converge or oppose one another without diplomacy. A myth of marriage, parentage, or feud may preserve a real interaction while inventing a divine family.
