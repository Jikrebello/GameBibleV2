# The Second Circle

The **Second Circle** is the Atherian name for eight court-facing Menhir whose laws repeatedly converge around celestial rhythm, pursuit, transformation, route, conflict, and hidden systems. It records a real ecological bloc left after [[The Faewild Schism]], but it is not a family, council, rank, or government.

## The Eight Sovereignties

- [[Father Sun]] governs exposure, heat, visibility, ripening, and endurance beneath light.
- [[Mother Moon]] governs cycles, tides, instinct, migration, and mutable form.
- [[Astaroth]] governs collision, resistance, structural conflict, and testing through force.
- [[Lyraxis]] governs adaptation, metamorphosis, novelty, and disruptive variation.
- [[Orionis]] governs pursuit, predation, tracking, selection, and mastery made visible.
- [[Hecatara]] governs routes, adjacency, crossroads, thresholds, tolls, and consequence.
- [[Typhonius]] governs recombination, hybridization, regeneration, and stable new forms.
- [[Arachnara]] governs distributed systems, silk, venom, swarm coordination, and hidden infrastructure.

Each began as an angelic office-holder fused with its cohort and domain. Each now possesses choice without moral comprehension under [[Menhir Sovereignty and Bargain]].

## Atherian Family Language

Imperial religion called Father Sun and Mother Moon the parents of the Star Children. It interpreted convergences as marriages, rivalries as sibling feuds, and recurring court roles as offspring. Those metaphors remain valuable evidence of encounter. They do not establish biological or divine descent.

[[Solar Sons]] and [[Midnight Daughters]] preserve recurring manifestation roles around sun and moon. They are not additional Menhir, species, or catalogue forms.

The Atherians also inserted “Thanatos” into this Circle. The encountered being was [[The Boatman]], a true angel of the soul-road with no Menhir parentage.

## Bargain Rule

Second-Circle bargains are normally mediated through court persons, place-minds, inherited pacts, or strong thin places. A petitioner must identify the gift, price, boundary, and failure state. Exact performance does not establish mercy or justice.
