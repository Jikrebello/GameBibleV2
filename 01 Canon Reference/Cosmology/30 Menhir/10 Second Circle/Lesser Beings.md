### **Ashenzari**

- **Theme:** knowledge, divination, and **curses/bindings**. 
- **Gameplay:** you get power from being “bound” (cursed/locked-in gear); the god pays you back with strong information tools (detection, mapping, ID support) and skill boosts. 
- **Best for:** players who like planning, scouting, and committing to gear.

### **Beogh**

- **Theme:** the god of **orcs**; modern Beogh has been significantly reworked (“new beogh”). 
- **Gameplay (high-level):** centers on building/leading an orcish warband and getting value from orc allies; exact mechanics depend on the rework state in your version. 
- **Best for:** players who enjoy ally-centric melee play and “army management.”

### **Cheibriados**

- **Theme:** deliberation, time, and **slow power**. 
- **Gameplay:** you accept being slower / rejecting haste, and get paid back with large stat advantages and time-themed tools.
- **Best for:** careful players; tanky or high-impact characters that can afford slow repositioning.

### **Dithmenos**

- **Theme:** shadows, tricks, stealth, and “clever manipulation.” 
- **Gameplay:** strong stealth/shadow kit; modern Dith also supports “shadow mimic” interactions (even with newer spell schools in current development). 
- **Best for:** stabbers, skirmishers, or anyone who wants positioning and tempo tools.

### **Elyvilon**

- **Theme:** healing, mercy, pacification. 
- **Gameplay:** you get powerful “panic button” healing/cleansing and can sometimes neutralize threats rather than killing them. 
- **Best for:** newer players, fragile species, and anyone who values survival tools over raw damage.

### **Fedhas Madash**

- **Theme:** plants and fungi. 
- **Gameplay:** plants become friendly; you gain plant-themed battlefield control (notably **Wall of Briars**, plus other growth/overgrowth tools). 
- **Big constraint:** you generally avoid harming Fedhas-protected flora.
- **Best for:** tactical players who like terrain control.

### **Gozag Ym Sagoz**

- **Theme:** gold, deals, commerce. 
- **Gameplay:** you leverage money as a resource (buying advantages, bribery-style solutions, “petitions”); tends to be extremely strong if you use abilities proactively. 
- **Best for:** players who like “economic builds” and converting gold into momentum.

### **Hepliaklqana**

- **Theme:** ancestral memory and a permanent ancestor ally. 
- **Gameplay:** you get a **customizable, reviving** ancestor companion and a suite of abilities that improve/coordinate with it; piety likes exploration. 
- **Best for:** most characters, especially those that benefit from a durable buddy and extra frontline presence.

### **Ignis**

- **Theme:** the **Dying Flame**—front-loaded power with an intended end. 
- **Gameplay:** you start strong, but **do not gain piety**; you only **spend down** Ignis’ piety on fire-focused abilities, and once it’s gone you typically switch gods. 
- **Best for:** early-game stabilization; players comfortable planning a midgame conversion.

### **Jiyva**

- **Theme:** slime, mutation, adaptation; one of the **chaotic gods**. 
- **Gameplay:** body-warping benefits and slime/ooze tools (e.g., **Oozemancy** is explicitly a Jiyva ability). 
- **Best for:** brawlers who like regeneration/adaptation, and players okay with chaos/side effects.

### **Kikubaaqudgha (Kiku)**

- **Theme:** necromancy and death magic.
- **Gameplay:** supports corpse play (where relevant), necromancy spell access, pain/undead-style toolkits; typically rewards killing and leaning into necromantic solutions.
- **Best for:** necromancers, hybrid melee+necro, and characters who want “dark scaling.”

### **Lugonu**

- **Theme:** the Abyss, distortion, corruption; a **chaotic god**. 
- **Gameplay:** strongly tied to Abyssal mechanics; has an explicit **banishment** tool (Lugonu invocation) and other space-warping effects. 
- **Best for:** players who want emergency escape/space control and don’t mind high variance.

### **Makhleb**

- **Theme:** destruction and demons; a **chaotic god**. 
- **Gameplay:** classic “kill things faster” religion: rewards slaughter with sustain (often through on-kill style benefits) and demon/destruction abilities.
- **Best for:** straightforward aggression, especially melee bruisers and hybrids.

### **Nemelex Xobeh**

- **Theme:** gambling, decks, “it’s all in the cards”; classified as **chaotic** in modern versions. 
- **Gameplay:** you build around consumable-ish, card/deck-like effects: explosive turns, emergency answers, and swingy power spikes.
- **Best for:** players who like controlled randomness and tactical “toolbox” effects.

### **Okawaru**

- **Theme:** honorable combat. 
- **Gameplay:** two signature buffs—**Heroism** (skill boost) and **Finesse** (attack speed boost)—plus steady combat gifts. 
- **Best for:** almost any weapon user; very “plug-and-play.”

### **Qazlal**

- **Theme:** storms, destruction, noise.
- **Gameplay:** tends to make you loud/obvious but hard to kill, with storm-based area damage and elemental mayhem.
- **Best for:** tanky characters and players who don’t rely on stealth.

### **Ru**

- **Theme:** sacrifice and apocalyptic power.
- **Gameplay:** you permanently sacrifice parts of your character (stats, resistances, skills, etc.) in exchange for very high-impact abilities; Ru is called out as interacting strongly with faster piety gain effects. 
- **Best for:** players who understand what they can afford to give up to buy winning buttons.

### **Sif Muna**

- **Theme:** magic, knowledge, spellcasting endurance.
- **Gameplay:** supports casters with tools that keep magic online (think “mana economy” + spell support); generally favors committed spellcasters.
- **Best for:** pure casters and magic-heavy hybrids.

### **The Shining One (TSO)**

- **Theme:** holy war against evil/undead/demons.
- **Gameplay:** powerful survivability and holy offense, often with weapon blessing and anti-evil tools; usually comes with strong constraints against unholy behavior.
- **Best for:** extended game plans, undead/demon-heavy branches, and straightforward “good crusader” play.

### **Trog**

- **Theme:** anger, violence, anti-magic. 
- **Gameplay:** hates spellcasting; pays you with berserk-oriented abilities and strong weapon support (including antimagic bias on gifts). 
- **Best for:** pure melee, especially berserkers and “no-spells” bruisers.

### **Uskayaw**

- **Theme:** rhythm, combat momentum, performance.
- **Gameplay:** rewards staying in the fight and building tempo; typically offers escalating crowd control / burst based on how the battle is going.
- **Best for:** melee characters who can keep hitting without needing to disengage often.

### **Vehumet**

- **Theme:** destructive magic.
- **Gameplay:** a blaster-caster god: helps you kill with conjurations/elemental destruction and rewards doing so, usually via spell support and/or range/throughput advantages.
- **Best for:** conjurers, elementalists, and any “kill them from over there” mage.

### **Wu Jian Council**

- **Theme:** martial arts, movement, positional attacks.
- **Gameplay:** turns movement into offense/defense with special martial moves; excels when you fight while repositioning (rather than standing still trading hits).
- **Best for:** melee skirmishers and anyone who likes high-skill positioning play.

### **Xom**

- **Theme:** pure chaos; a **chaotic god**. 
- **Gameplay:** Xom’s “piety” behavior is atypical and the god acts unpredictably; effects that increase piety gain can change how often Xom acts. 
- **Best for:** entertainment, challenge runs, and players who accept that the god may both save and kill them.

### **Yredelemnul**

- **Theme:** death knights and controlled necromancy via the **Black Torch**. 
- **Gameplay:** modern Yred revolves around lighting the torch per level, gaining piety while it burns, and reaping/raising allies from kills in your umbra; has several strong mid-to-late invocations.
- **Best for:** fighters who want undead support without running a full necromancer spellbook.

### **Zin**

- **Theme:** law, purity, order. 
- **Gameplay:** strong defensive/anti-status kit (notably mutation protection scaling up to immunity), plus powerful crowd-control style invocations like Recite and safety tools like Sanctuary. 
- **Best for:** players who hate mutations/hexes and want structured, reliable defensive power.
