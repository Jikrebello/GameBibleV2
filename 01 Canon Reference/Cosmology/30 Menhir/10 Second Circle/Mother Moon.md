# Mother Moon

**Mother Moon**, called **Lunara** or **Lunara Noctis**, is the Menhir sovereignty of cycles, tides, instinct, migration, bodily rhythm, and mutable form.

## Original Commission and Cohort

The office-holder and cohort that became Mother Moon administered tides, reproductive and sleeping cycles, migration, embodied change, and the recurring measures by which living things survive time. They were commissioned to serve change without dissolving creaturely identity.

They joined the Rebellion seeking possession of cycle itself. Fusion bound office-holder, cohort, lunar court ecology, and mutable law into Lunara. She remembers every repeated term and can choose which form a cycle takes, but cannot understand why a human child should outweigh a lineage's continued transformation.

## The Faewild Schism

Trium attempted to make tide, migration, and bodily change functions of its total world-order. Mother Moon resisted any cycle fixed to one integrated design. Her pressure made coasts move, forms molt, and routes change between one phase and the next.

## Domain and Compulsion

Mother Moon makes the fixed become periodic. She governs waxing and waning, instinctive return, migratory inheritance, pack rhythm, sleep, tide, and forms that alter without becoming arbitrary chaos.

She does not govern generic darkness, madness, forbidden magic, or the human soul. Her horror is exact recurrence without moral exception.

## Manifestations and Signs

- tides answer a moon no one can see;
- scars, fur, antlers, or older body-patterns emerge for one phase;
- paths and animal migrations change according to a hidden calendar;
- sleepers share posture, heartbeat, or destination;
- a pale figure appears differently to every witness but returns to the same outline each month;
- exchanged children, changelings, or missing travelers return at the appointed phase without aging normally.

## Courts and Ecologies

The **Pale Household** includes [[Midnight Daughters]], tide minds, moon-marked animals, migratory court persons, and mutable-form lineages. These beings do not share one moral status: court persons answer for chosen acts, while animals and place-law organisms follow narrower compulsions.

## Bargain Grammar

- **Permitted gifts:** one controlled transformation, safe passage through a cycle, tide access, migratory knowledge, hibernation, or inherited bodily adaptation.
- **Recognized prices:** a fixed habit, one phase of service, a name used only at one moon, a changed descendant, or surrender of something that must not remain permanent.
- **Taboo:** demanding that a cyclic gift become permanent, denying a promised return, or refusing a phase after taking its benefit.
- **Failure:** the recipient becomes unstable between forms, repeats an unwanted season, or is claimed whenever the relevant cycle returns.

Most dealings pass through [[Midnight Daughters]], the Pale Household, tide minds, moon-marked animals, or inherited pacts.

## Mother Moon's Bargain

[[Mother Moon's Bargain]] is a real Ättar covenant: hybrid authority and inherited rage in exchange for one firstborn child from each old-rite ruling line per generation. Lunara administers the exchange exactly and cannot understand why exact consent by an ancestor fails to justify taking a later child.

The agreement is therefore morally evil by the Lord's order and by the child's human dignity, even though Mother Moon does not choose it as an act of malice. Living human rulers remain responsible for continuing, resisting, or renouncing it.

## Mortal Names and Misreadings

Atherians made Lunara the mother of star gods. Ättar old-rite halls call her ancestress and patron. Folk seers more cautiously describe the Moon Court as a power that remembers terms longer than families remember why they agreed.

## Limits and Counterplay

Mother Moon cannot alter divine identity, create a soul, erase human responsibility, or grant unrestricted shapeshifting. Counterplay fixes a boundary in time, names the original form, invokes daylight or iron where appropriate, completes the promised return, or renounces an inherited covenant while accepting the loss of future benefits.

## Story Uses

- a village's protective transformation returns every generation and has begun selecting children its founders never named;
- a missing procession comes back after a century believing only one night passed;
- a tide bargain opens a harbor while moving another coastline into danger;
- an old-rite heir must choose between political legitimacy and ending a firstborn exchange.
