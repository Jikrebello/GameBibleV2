# The Making of Saints

The Eclessian and Thalmyric churches recognise sanctity through different institutions, while the Freeflame movement rejects formal canonisation. These practices shape feast days, relic cults, pilgrimage and the public memory of holy lives. They can identify a saint or mistake one; they cannot create sainthood. A living saint remains a human person in the Material Realm, and a [[Manifested Saints Overview|manifested saint]] is a redeemed human soul sent from Heaven after judgement. Relics, hymns and canonisation procedures cannot compel such a manifestation.

## Eclessia: Canonization (the Juridical Way)

Sainthood in the West is a **verdict**. A cause is opened; a postulator assembles the life, the miracles, the witnesses; the **Adversary's Counsel** — a standing office whose whole vocation is arguing that the candidate was *not* a saint — attacks everything; the five tests are applied by the relic courts; and the Lord Primarch pronounces. The process takes decades and produces paperwork by the wagonload, which is precisely the point: an Eclessian saint is *proven*.

The resulting cults are **personal, specific, and charismatic**. Western saints are individuals with faces, quirks, and portfolios — the saint *of* bakers, *of* barren women, *of* doubters — celebrated on name-days, painted realistically, begged for particular favors. And Western sanctity runs hot in the living: Eclessia is where devotees manifest **the Four Marks** — spontaneous wounds at wrists and ankles matching the Four Nails of the Binding — along with weeping images, ecstasies, and visionary raptures. The relic courts authenticate a few and expose many; the charismatic stream and the juridical dam define each other.

**Failure mode:** sanctity as fee schedule. When the verdict machine is captured — political canonizations, purchased causes, the routing-assurance logic applied to heaven's honors — the West produces exactly the corruption Ansgar burned for naming.

## Veridun: Glorification (the Recognized Way)

Sainthood in the East is an **acknowledgment**. No court, no counsel, no cause: the people begin venerating; the elders discern; a synod eventually confirms what the faithful already know, adding a name to the diptychs "as one records that the sun has risen." An Eastern saint is *recognized*.

The classic Thalmyric saint is not a charismatic but a **wonder-working elder** — the desert-monastery type: decades of hiddenness, clairvoyant counsel dispensed to pilgrims through a cell window, healings the elder attributes to the pilgrim's own faith, ferocious humility, and after death an incorrupt body and a lamp above the tomb that **weeps sweet oil instead of soot**. Visible sanctity-phenomena in the *living* are treated with deep suspicion: an elder who displayed the Four Marks would be told, gently, to wash his wrists and resume kneading bread. The East's great fear is **false kindling** — the counterfeit fire of pride, delusion, or an Idol wearing light (Saint Virella's twin veils are the founding doctrine) — and its discernment tradition exists to test every flame before anyone warms themselves at it.

**Failure mode:** sanctity as heraldry. When recognition is captured by the throne — a dynasty glorifying its founders, an empire canonizing its wars — the East produces exactly the Saint-Emperor dispute the West never tires of citing.

## The Freeflame: No Making At All

Ferro's congregations recognize **tested elders** in this life and leave the next to the Lord: no canonization, no invocation, no relic cults — *"the only register is the Book of Life, and we do not hold the pen."* The saints of the old rolls are honored as exemplars, quoted like scripture, and never prayed *to*. In practice, Freeflame communities keep unofficial memory-days for Ansgar and Menos with an intensity that looks remarkably like everything they abolished, and their opponents say so.

## Limits of Recognition

The Flame answers the righteousness and purpose of a person rather than the prestige of an institution. Honest Eclessian inquiry can expose fraud, and humble Thalmyric discernment can recognise holiness. Both fail when political advantage, wealth or pride governs the process. Neither church possesses this explanation in full, and each commonly treats the other's failures as evidence for its own method.

A true posthumous manifestation confirms the particular commission under which the redeemed person has appeared. It gives no general approval to every claim made by that person's earthly admirers. Counterfeit apparitions, persona echoes, Patron imitations and [[Borrowed Saints]] may appear even around genuine relics.
