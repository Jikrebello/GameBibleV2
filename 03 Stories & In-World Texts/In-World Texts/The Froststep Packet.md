# The Froststep Packet

The authenticated packet [[Claims-Factor Marco Bellandi]] opens before Bernard, the player, Brina, and Sister Hanne in [[Embers Without Excuse]].

**Read whole, on screen, in this order.** No character paraphrases these documents, and no dialogue summarizes them before the player has read them. The packet is not the emotional climax of the quest and must not be staged as one — it is three pieces of paper in a room where a relief run is being loaded.

Petra's letter is **fixed across every playthrough.** It does not vary with Bernard's direction, restitution state, approval, or the player's conduct. Her separate standing instruction — `accepted-no-reconciliation`, `redirected-to-road-care`, or `contact-closed` — is selected by the actual restitution record and is a *line item*, not a letter. The distinction is the design: her personhood is not a reward tier.

---

## I. From Petra Ilev

*Set down by the hand of Anezka Faber, carrier, at Froststep Lower Refuge, there being no other clerk on the road that week.*

> Bernard.
>
> Bellandi's man says you will read this in front of witnesses, so I have kept out what is mine.
>
> The bakehouse floor. I scrubbed those bricks two winters as a girl for hospice bread and I could have told you which of them lifted. That is the part I still find remarkable — not that you took it, but that you thought the floor was yours to know about and nobody else's.
>
> My hand works. I relearned buttons in a month. Laces I still cannot do and I have stopped intending to; there is a woman in Froststep village who makes me a strap and charges me too much for it and I let her, because she is old and I like the strap.
>
> I drive relief now. Second sled, usually, which the other drivers think is a joke I am making. It is not a joke. I know exactly what is on my sled and so does everyone who loads it, and if that is a thing you helped make of me then you do not get it, because I made it, in a bad year, on my own.
>
> Do not write to say you are sorry. You said that already and I believed you the first time. That was never the difficulty.
>
> Petra Ilev, her mark.

*Design note: her diction is tired rather than cruel; the concrete memory (the bricks) does the work that no accusation could; the strap is the flat Kedravan joke she is entitled to. She is not a symbol and does not speak in principles.*

## II. From Hospice-Mother Radena Vosk

*Her own hand. Unsealed, as always.*

> Bernard, my dear —
>
> The goose is alive. She is eleven and she still cannot bear the bell and she still runs at Deacon Ferrin, who deserves it, so you may stop asking.
>
> We relaid the bakehouse floor last spring, and the road-wall niche is mortared, and the stable box has a proper lock on it with the key on the pantry ring where anyone may take it. I had it all done at once. I am telling you because I know you have kept the map of this house in your head for twenty years, and I would like you to be able to put it down.
>
> You have been paying me back for a roof since you were nine years old, and I have watched you do it by trying to own every future roof you might ever need. That is not the same as being grateful and it has never once been necessary. We had enough blankets, Bernard. We have enough now.
>
> Come back if you can be honest here. Do not come back if it is somewhere to hide the account.
>
> — Radena

## III. From Watch-Captain Matej Solin

*Certified copy, Froststep inquiry, appended assessment.*

> The plan and the store count were reasonable for what we knew. No commander ordered the concealment and no commander could have found it in the time we had.
>
> He confessed after the injured sled came in, and he led us to all four caches without argument, and by then Ilev's hand was already black. Both of those are the record. I have never been able to put one of them in front of the other, and I have stopped trying.
>
> If sustained accountability is witnessed independently, an ordinary petition would be heard. I will not travel to say so.
