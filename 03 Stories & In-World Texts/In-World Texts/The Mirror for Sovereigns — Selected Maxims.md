# The Mirror for Sovereigns — Selected Maxims

*Scholar's apparatus: written c. 1340 AR by Nicanor Vetti, dismissed under-secretary of Sermora, in exile; dedicated — with irony no one has ever securely measured — to Lauro Medori the Magnificent. Banned by the Sanctum's Index and Veridun's Codex alike; condemned by Covenant jurists (whose marginalia, where recovered, are admiring); found in the private shelves of every ruler on the board. The dedication copy is in the Medori vault. Of course it is.*

---

> **On the two churches.** Quarrel with one church, and the other will bless your banners for nothing. Quarrel with both, and you will discover how much they agree.

> **On relics.** A relic in the treasury is worth two saints in heaven. But display it seldom: a wonder shown daily becomes furniture, and a people who are done wondering begin counting.

> **On mercenaries.** He who holds his state by hired swords holds a lease, and has not read it. The company that saves you in autumn prices you in spring. Arm your own people or resign yourself to being their tenant.

> **On debt.** The Medori forgive no sins; they collect them. A prince may safely owe the Bank any sum that would embarrass the Bank to lose. Owe less and you are a client; owe vastly more and you are a partner.

> **On fear and love.** It is answered of old whether it is better to be feared than loved. I say: it is best to be solvent. Fear rusts, love strays; a paid garrison in a full granary loves and fears you precisely enough, every day, at a known price.

> **On contracts.** Sign nothing an Ahl-e-Ahd witness smiled at. Their law is honest the way a well is deep.

> **On heresy.** Never persecute a heresy that empties your rival's collection plate. Doctrine is the one commodity that grows scarcer when hoarded and dearer when burned.

> **On the appearance of piety.** Men judge by the eye, and few stand close to the altar. A prince need not kindle. He need only glow.

> **On fortune.** Fortune is a river in spring flood: divinity when she drowns your enemy's fields, engineering the rest of the year. Build the dikes in summer and let the priests explain the flood.

> **On the last account.** All princes owe one fare that cannot be refinanced, delayed by counsel, or paid by an heir. The Boatman audits everyone. Govern as though the ledger were already open — not for virtue's sake, but because it is.

*(Sanctum censor's note in the Index entry, hand unknown: "The wickedness of this book is that it is never wrong about anything small.")*
