# The Aquilan Epistles — Selected Passages

*Scholar's apparatus: The Epistles of Saint Aquilan the Turned (born Sha'il bar Qoreth, d. c. 45 AR) survive in both great canons with minor variances the churches pretend are major. The Jirahar Keepers maintain a counter-collection, the* File Against the Hound*, annotating every verse. Selections below follow the Sanctum recension; disputed readings noted.*

---

## From the Epistle to the Sestarans

*Written to the harbor congregations, where every god had a stall and a price.*

> I walked your quay of a thousand altars, brothers, and I read the bills of lading. You have gods for the tide and gods for the ledger, gods insured against gods. And I say you were never idolaters — you were *customers*. The Fire is not for sale in your harbor, because it is not for sale anywhere. It was bought once, with nails.

> Though I bind with the tongues of scribes and of angels, and have not love, I am become a sounding seal, an empty contract. Though I hold all names in custody, and have not love, the vault holds only me. Love keeps no ledger of wrongs; love voids no oath by flood; love is the one covenant without a breach-clause. Contracts end, brothers. Prophecy ends. The Scrolls themselves shall be rolled shut. Love does not end.

*(The Covenant's jurists have studied this passage for eight centuries with professional discomfort. Suhrab the Lawbinder is said to have kept a copy.)*

## From the Epistle to the Tower-Born

*Written to the Jirahar congregations — his own people — and the most consequential document of the age.*

> Ask me not whether the Path was true. The Path was our tutor, and a tutor is not disproven when the child comes of age — he is *thanked*. The law was a lamp for the night; shall we curse the lamp because the dawn has come? I say the Path is fulfilled, not abolished; completed, not condemned.

> The towers builded him, and the towers refused him. **[Here the pogrom-readers stop. The verse continues:]** Yet the root bears the branch, and not the branch the root. If some boughs were broken, it was that you, wild sprig, might be grafted in — and He who broke can graft again, and will. Boast not against the towers, you nations: you stand on their root, and drink from their well, and if the Lord spared not the natural branches, take heed.

*(Sanctum note: the full verse is read on the Feast of Saint Aquilan. Keeper note, from the* File Against the Hound*: "Four centuries of mobs have quoted the first sentence. We have counted. Not one is recorded quoting the second.")*

## From the Virelos Testimony

*His own account of the road, dictated late in life and read at every mission-founding.*

> I do not ask you to trust my doctrine. Doctrine can be argued, and I was the best arguer of my generation; I had warrants in my satchel to prove it. I ask you to explain the road. There was a light above the light, as noon stands behind noon, and a voice that did not accuse me of cruelty, nor of zeal, nor of error in the law. It asked me only: *Hound, whose scent do you follow?* And I, who had an answer for every court in the empire, had none. Three days I sat blind at Virelos, and the congregation I had come to chain washed my eyes with the water I had come to forbid. What I know, brothers, is not that I was right ever after. It is that I was *found*. The seat they say I lack — let them keep their seats. I was commissioned on a road, by the Fire, in front of no witness but my own dead certainty. Whoever the Fire finds is found indeed.

*(This final passage is the most-quoted text in Freeflame worship. Both hierarchies have separately considered whether it can be quietly de-emphasized. It cannot.)*
