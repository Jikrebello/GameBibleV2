# The Hundred Grievances — Selections

*Scholar's apparatus: first sung by Frater Ansgar of Brenholt in the late spring of 1297 AR at the Blackwater pithead in Brenholt, over forty-one drowned miners, in place of the funeral rite and against the sale of routing assurances. The crowd answered each Grievance with Saint Gerlind's saying, "named, not bought." The singing followed Ansgar's Count, the tally he kept in his own parish of more than ten letters sold for every rite sung. A pit-tally girl chalked the text on the board where the shifts were counted; the Ironvale printing of c. 1299 follows her copy, and the bargemen's sung version of the Weiss differs from it in eleven places. The First Grievance answers Lord Primarch Alessior II's Bull of the Sufficient Seal (1283). Possession was a burning offense in four duchies until the Korrheim Accord; it is now merely a career-ending one in six, and singing it at a graveside is still a flogging offense in Aurelia. The tally board is a Freeflame relic — an irony their theology forbids them to enjoy and their congregations enjoy anyway. Selections follow the Ironvale printing; the response is omitted.*

---

> **The First Grievance.** No seal of wax weighs anything at the Weighing. You have sold the poor a bridge that is not yours.

> **The Ninth.** When our Lord from the Pillar said that it was finished, he did not add: *pending further payment*.

> **The Fourteenth.** You teach that the Vestibule hears truth and consent. Then it does not hear coin. Then what, fathers, did you sell? And if it does hear coin, tell the poor plainly that heaven has a door-charge, and see how long your roofs keep their lead.

> **The Twenty-Second.** The Sanctum's new dome is roofed with the pennies of widows who feared for their dead, and painted inside with their husbands' names in gold, so that the dead may look down and see what was paid for them. I have seen the fear sold to them at the graveside, printed, with a schedule of rates. The rate for a child is less. I ask the Lord Primarch: why is the rate for a child less? By what audit of souls? Or is it only that grief for a child sells faster?

> **The Thirty-First.** You keep an officer called the Adversary's Counsel, to argue against every sainthood. He is diligent, learned, and unpaid by results. I observe that no such counsel is retained against a *donation*.

> **The Fortieth.** Lord Primarch Alessior, of blessed memory, of the house of Malvora, of the ledgers of Medori: eleven times these Grievances name you, and I am told this is disrespectful to the dead. Very well. Let the dead take it up with the Weighing, where his seals — I am reliably informed, by the painters who saw him walking under his own dome — weigh nothing.

> **The Sixty-Sixth.** You have sold the Empty Seat by the hour. There are chapels where a man may pay to sit in the Companion's place at the rite and be seen sitting there. Ischar also thought the seat had a price. He at least returned the money.

> **The Eighty-Eighth.** I do not say the Flame is not with you. I say the Flame is not *yours*. The Fire keeps no landlord — and if this be heresy, then the road to Virelos was heresy, and the Hound's warrants were canon law.

> **The Hundredth.** Prove me wrong with Fire, fathers, and not with fees. I will stand where you ask, before whom you ask. If the Flame is in your keeping as you claim, you know where to send it. I am easily found. I am the one the widows can afford.

*(Eight years later the Synod of Aldersbruck took him at his word and put his psalter to the brazier. It did not burn. They voided the test and kept him in a bridge-tower to wait for orders, and the spring flood took the tower. The psalter is in the Sanctum's lead vault; the man is in the river; and the two facts together are the whole quarrel of the age in miniature.)*
