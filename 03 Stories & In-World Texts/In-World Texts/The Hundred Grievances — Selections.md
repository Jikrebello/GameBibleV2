# The Hundred Grievances — Selections

*Scholar's apparatus: posted by Frater Ansgar of Brenholt on the chapel door at Brenholt, autumn 1297 AR, against the sale of routing assurances. Possession was a burning offense in four duchies until the Korrheim Accord; it is now merely a career-ending one in six. The original door is a Freeflame relic — an irony their theology forbids them to enjoy and their congregations enjoy anyway. Selections follow the Ironvale printing.*

---

> **The First Grievance.** No seal of wax weighs anything at the Weighing. You have sold the poor a bridge that is not yours.

> **The Ninth.** When our Lord from the Pillar said that it was finished, he did not add: *pending further payment*.

> **The Fourteenth.** You teach that the Vestibule hears truth and consent. Then it does not hear coin. Then what, fathers, did you sell? And if it does hear coin, tell the poor plainly that heaven has a door-charge, and see how long your roofs keep their lead.

> **The Twenty-Second.** The Sanctum's new dome is roofed with the pennies of widows who feared for their dead. I have seen the fear sold to them at the graveside, printed, with a schedule of rates. The rate for a child is less. I ask the Lord Primarch: why is the rate for a child less? By what audit of souls? Or is it only that grief for a child sells faster?

> **The Thirty-First.** You keep an officer called the Adversary's Counsel, to argue against every sainthood. He is diligent, learned, and unpaid by results. I observe that no such counsel is retained against a *donation*.

> **The Fortieth.** Lord Primarch Alessior, of blessed memory, of the house of Malvora, of the ledgers of Medori: eleven times these Grievances name you, and I am told this is disrespectful to the dead. Very well. Let the dead take it up with the Weighing, where his seals — I am reliably informed — weigh nothing.

> **The Sixty-Sixth.** You have sold the Empty Seat by the hour. There are chapels where a man may pay to sit in the Companion's place at the rite and be seen sitting there. Ischar also thought the seat had a price. He at least returned the money.

> **The Eighty-Eighth.** I do not say the Flame is not with you. I say the Flame is not *yours*. The Fire keeps no landlord — and if this be heresy, then the road to Virelos was heresy, and the Hound's warrants were canon law.

> **The Hundredth.** Prove me wrong with Fire, fathers, and not with fees. I will stand where you ask, before whom you ask. If the Flame is in your keeping as you claim, you know where to send it. I am easily found. I am the one the widows can afford.

*(He stood at the Synod of Aldersbruck eight years later. They sent the fire they had. His psalter is in the Sanctum's lead vault, unburnt, and both facts are the whole quarrel of the age in miniature.)*
