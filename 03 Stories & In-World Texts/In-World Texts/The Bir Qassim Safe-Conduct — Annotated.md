# The Bir Qassim Safe-Conduct — Annotated

*Scholar's apparatus: the instrument under which the grandson of the Prophet and seventy-two of his household traveled to arbitration in 603 AR, and were killed at the wells of Bir Qassim when it was ruled void. The text below is the Witnessed-Seal recension taught in the law-colleges of Iqashar; the Sealed-Blood recension differs in no clause and in every annotation. Recited clause by clause at the Mourning of the Wells, with the response after each: * "And yet the well was dry."

---

**IN THE NAME OF THE ONE WHO WITNESSES ALL SEALS**, this instrument of passage and protection, between:

**The First Party:** the Caliphate, by the hand of its Marshal of the Northern Roads; **The Second Party:** Husan ibn Raiq, called the Prophet's Grandson, and his household to the number of seventy-two souls, hereafter *the Travelers*.

**Clause the First.** The Travelers shall proceed under the Caliphate's protection to the arbitration at Iqashar, there to present the instrument called by their party the Counter-Instrument, and shall suffer no harm, hindrance, insult, or delay from any sworn to the Caliphate.

**Clause the Second.** Protection shall extend from the Travelers' departure until the seventh sunset after the arbitration's close, inclusive of return.

**Clause the Third.** The Travelers shall bear arms of the person only, and shall present themselves unarmored at the arbitration.

**Clause the Fourth.** *The protected passage shall follow the North Well Road, by the stations of Qafra, Bir Nadim, and Bir Qassim, and no other way.*

**Clause the Fifth.** Breach by either party voids this instrument entire, saving nothing.

*Witnessed under three seals: the Marshal's, the College's, and the Guard's. The fourth seal-space is empty. Every recension agrees the fourth space is empty. No two agree why.*

---

## What Happened

The spring flood of 603 took the causeway at Bir Nadim. The escort itself led the Travelers off the North Well Road onto the drover's track — six miles, rejoining the road above Bir Qassim, where the wells stood dry after the same flood's silting. There the escort's commander sent to the breach-court riding with the column, and the court ruled within the hour, in the ruling every Covenant law-student memorizes first and forgets never:

> *"The route being named, the route is of the essence. The instrument does not distinguish whose feet strayed, nor why, nor that water moved them; Clause the Fifth saves nothing, and nothing is therefore saved. The instrument is void. What stands before us now stands under the open sky of the ordinary law."*

Under the ordinary law, Husan ibn Raiq was a rebel under arms. It was finished by sunset. Seventy-three graves; the escort dug them; the commander paid for the headstones from his own purse, which the mourning-liturgy notes annually with a contempt eight centuries have not softened.

## The Annotations

**The Witnessed-Seal schools** have produced three defenses (necessity-of-form, corruption-of-the-escort-only, tragedy-without-error) and no peace. The dominant modern position, after Suhrab's *Ledger of Ledgers* introduced act-of-water clauses to all passage contracts (six centuries too late, as the Sealed-Blood liturgy observes), is the quiet formula: *lawful and wrong* — a phrase that does more work in Covenant jurisprudence than any other two words.

**The Sealed-Blood annotation** is shorter. Beside Clause the Fourth, in every copy of their recension, one line: *"The law was the knife. The flood only carried it."*

**The Grenzburg-relevance note** (authorial): this document is the setting's masterclass in how the Ahl-e-Ahd horror works — nothing supernatural occurs in it. The devils are elsewhere. Here there is only text, weather, and men who chose the text.
