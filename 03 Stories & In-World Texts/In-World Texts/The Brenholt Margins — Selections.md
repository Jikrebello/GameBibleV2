# The Brenholt Margins — Selections

*Scholar's apparatus: the marginal notes of Frater Ansgar of Brenholt, chaplain of the Brenholt Sending House, written in his psalter between about 1284 and the spring of 1290 AR. They stand beside the names of the dead he sang without fee and the tally he kept of more than ten letters sold for every rite sung, which is called Ansgar's Count. He drowned in the Blackwater pit in the late spring of 1290 with forty-one miners. At their funeral the tally-keeper Irmel read the notes aloud in place of the rite, the crowd answering each with Saint Gerlind's saying, "named, not bought", and chalked them on the board where the shifts were counted. The board held a hundred; the psalter is said to hold more, and nobody outside the Sanctum has seen it since 1299. The Ironvale printing of c. 1299 follows her board, and the bargemen's sung version of the Weiss differs from it in eleven places. The First Grievance answers Lord Primarch Alessior II's Bull of the Sufficient Seal (1283). Possession was a burning offense in four duchies until the Korrheim Accord; it is now merely a career-ending one in six, and singing it at a graveside is still a flogging offense in Aurelia. The tally board is a Freeflame relic — an irony their theology forbids them to enjoy and their congregations enjoy anyway. Selections follow the Ironvale printing; the response is omitted.*

---

> **The First Grievance.** No seal of wax weighs anything at the Weighing. You have sold the poor a bridge that is not yours.

> **The Ninth.** When our Lord from the Pillar said that it was finished, he did not add: *pending further payment*.

> **The Fourteenth.** You teach that the Vestibule hears truth and consent. Then it does not hear coin. Then what, fathers, did you sell? And if it does hear coin, tell the poor plainly that heaven has a door-charge, and see how long your roofs keep their lead.

> **The Twenty-Second.** On feast days the Chancery seals the poor for nothing and calls it alms. The widow who is given your wax goes home and does not sing, because she has been told it is done. I have seen the same fear sold at the graveside to those who can pay, printed, with a schedule of rates. The rate for a child is less. I ask the Lord Primarch: why is the rate for a child less? By what audit of souls? Or is it only that grief for a child sells faster?

> **The Thirty-First.** You keep an officer called the Adversary's Counsel, to argue against every sainthood. He is diligent, learned, and unpaid by results. I observe that no such counsel is retained against a *donation*.

> **The Fortieth.** Lord Primarch Alessior, of blessed memory, of the house of Malvora, of the ledgers of Medori: eleven times these notes name you, and I am told this is disrespectful to the dead. Very well. Let the dead take it up with the Weighing, where his seals — I am reliably informed, by the clerks who have seen him at his desk since — weigh nothing.

> **The Fifty-Third.** My father sells them at the door and I sing them at the altar, and between us we keep one promise in ten. He does not know the count. I have not found the hour to tell him.

> **The Sixty-Sixth.** You have sold the Empty Seat by the hour. There are chapels where a man may pay to sit in the Companion's place at the rite and be seen sitting there. Ischar also thought the seat had a price. He at least returned the money.

> **The Eighty-Eighth.** I do not say the Flame is not with you. I say the Flame is not *yours*. The Fire keeps no landlord — and if this be heresy, then the road to Virelos was heresy, and the Hound's warrants were canon law.

> **The Hundredth.** Prove me wrong with Fire, fathers, and not with fees. I will stand where you ask, before whom you ask. If the Flame is in your keeping as you claim, you know where to send it. I am easily found. I am the one the widows can afford.

*(Nine years after he drowned, the Synod of Aldersbruck took a dead man at his word and put his psalter to the brazier. It did not burn. They voided the test, sealed the book in lead and charged the Weiss country a second fee for its funerals. The psalter is in the Sanctum's vault; the man is in the river; and the two facts together are the whole quarrel of the age in miniature.)*
