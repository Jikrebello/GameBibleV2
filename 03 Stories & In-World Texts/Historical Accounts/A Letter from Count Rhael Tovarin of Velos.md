## **“The Mask of Mercy: A Letter from Count Rhael Tovarin of Velos”**

_Confiscated during the Sanctum’s Inquisition, Year 46 of the Interregnum_  
_Stored in sealed archives beneath the Basilica of Aurelia_

> _“If this letter is read, let it not be by priests. Let it be read by princes—those who would one day be tested and not know, until the hour is upon them, what they truly are.”_

---

To the one who finds this,  
I am **Rhael Tovarin**, once Count of Velos, now styled **Emir-Regent of the Third Province**, servant of the **Emirate of Zanthir**, vassal to the **Caliphal Court** of the **Ahl-e-Ahd**.  
You may call me traitor. You would not be the first.

Let me tell you what I saw.

---

The empire was **already dying** before they came. The Atherian Senate met less and less. Taxes rose while the grain harvests fell. Eastern provinces whispered rebellion. The faith that once united us had splintered into **ritual and rot**. The Redeemer’s image was everywhere, but His will was absent.

We stood on the walls of Velos and watched **golems walk across the river**. I do not mean siege towers—I mean _walking fortresses_, shaped like beasts and bound by contracts older than the city itself. One struck the eastern gate once. Just once.

It fell in less than a minute.

The army we raised—the one the emperor ordered—**deserted within two days**. We were told reinforcements would come from the east. They never did. We sent ravens. They did not return. Only smoke on the horizon.

The legate from **Zanthir** arrived not with chains, but with a contract.

---

The terms were clear:

- Surrender the city.
- Maintain civil order.
- Convert in name, not in ritual.
- Swear loyalty to the **Caliph’s shadow**, and keep your lands.

In return:

- No bloodshed.
- No razing.
- No devils unleashed within the walls.

I had the seal in my hand, the signet ring of my house still warm on my finger. My advisors screamed. My sons wept. My wife prayed.

I signed.

---

And here is the part that will damn me in your eyes:  
**Velos did not burn.**

The scribes arrived and redrew our contracts. The Ahl-e-Ahd established a **puppet emirate**—and I became its regent. My people lived. Trade resumed. The devils stayed at the gates, bound and leashed. We lit no pyres. We dug no mass graves.

And in the shadows of the burned world, that was something.

---

Yes, we bowed. Yes, we translated our liturgies into the language of contracts and submission. But I kept the shrines open. I bribed the wardens. I **hid monks in the wine cellars**, taught the children of noble houses in secret. I even let the old songs be sung on feast days—as long as no scribe stood in attendance.

I wore their colors. I spoke their tongue. But in the **silence of my halls**, the Redeemer still burned.

Let the zealots of **Caldria** call me coward. They were not here when the empire left us to die. Let **Thalmyria** proclaim their purity—they rose from the ashes _we were forced to kneel in_. Let **Eclessia** point their trembling fingers—they cannot even unite their own altars.

We survived.

And perhaps one day, when the golems fall silent, and the chains of parchment are torn, we will rise again.

---

> _“Better a prince in shadow than a martyr no one remembers.”_  
> _—Inscribed on the underside of Count Rhael’s signet ring, recovered from his tomb_
