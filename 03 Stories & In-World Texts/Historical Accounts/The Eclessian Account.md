## **“In the Light Yet Scattered: A Devotional Chronicle of the Eclessian Struggle”**

By Brother Malvian of Aurelia, Chronicler of the Sanctum Divinitatis

> _“We are the children of the Redeemer's dawn, but we rise each day in shadow. The flame still burns, yet none among us has dared to grasp it without being burned.”_

---

### **I. From Outpost to Revelation: Eclessia's Origin in the East**

The lands now called **Eclessia** were once the **eastern frontier** of the great and terrible **Atherian Empire**—a realm of imposing aqueducts, silver coinage, and celestial pride. The legions carved roads through our forests. The governors raised forts atop our hills. Yet it was here, far from the imperial heart, that the **Redeemer** chose to walk among mortals.

He was born not to lords, nor priests, but among the **Jirahar caravans**, people scorned and misunderstood by both empire and tribes. And it was through **Isfir the Redeemer** that the heavens were split open and the **Holy Flame** cast into the world.

His message—of love, of sacrifice, of the _burning away of false idols_—spread quickly through the rural peoples of the east. We, the forgotten, became His first followers. When He was brought in chains to **Caleran** and crucified by those who wielded both imperial scepter and Jiraharin law, it was the east that wept. It was here, in **our soil**, that faith grew like fire in dry grass.

---

### **II. Scourged in Fire: The Pogroms and the Pain of Division**

After the Redeemer's **Ascension**, the empire flailed. His followers were hunted. **Martyrs lined our roads**. In time, the Atherian Empire bent, adopting His teachings—too late for many of us.

The **Jirahar**, who had not repented, were purged. Their **Tower-Towns** razed. Even now, the ruins of those spires dot our hills like broken teeth. Some say this was justice. Others whisper that we traded one imperial cruelty for another. But none can deny that it was **here**, in the east, that the **Faith of the Redeemer** became more than a doctrine—it became a **wound** we still carry.

---

### **III. Cracks in the Temple: The First Crusade and Eclessian Abandonment**

When the **Ahl-e-Ahd** struck the imperial west in a terrible wave of war-magic and infernal engines, the empire called for a **Crusade**—and it was our sons who were sent. **Caldria** in particular, with its armies of flaming steel and faith, marched westward to protect the capital.

And so we were left defenseless.

In that vacuum, our lands tore themselves apart. **Bandits roamed the sanctified roads**. The **Enaidfae** surged from the southern wilds, raiding and slaughtering. The cities of the east—**Aurelia**, **Verdissia**, **Brauval**, even holy **Marchburg**—became like candles in a storm, flickering, isolated, desperate.

The **Sanctum Divinitatis**, from its high marble seat in Aurelia, tried to call the lords together. But pride, old grudges, and divine ambition fractured what unity we had. What emerged was not a nation—but a dozen feuding realms, each with banners soaked in both scripture and blood.

---

### **IV. The Interregnum: A Crown No One Could Bear**

This period—our **Interregnum**—has never ended.

We are **Veridancia’s diplomacy**, **Caldria’s blades**, **Korrheim’s mercenaries**, **Virelia’s plough**, **Brauval’s ale**, **Ironvale’s ore**, and yet we are not one. The **Sylvan Marches** hold the southern forests against the fae, but do so alone. **Caldria**, mighty Caldria, holds the pass and keeps the fires of crusade alive—but even they cannot impose unity by steel alone.

There were moments—brief flickers—when a crown seemed near. Aurelian kings anointed by the Sanctum. Caldrian warlords bearing relics of the Redeemer. Even merchant leagues in **Brauvalis** advocated governance by coin and scripture. But each effort collapsed under weight of heresy, ambition, or holy doubt.

Worse still, the **Fäskogarfolk**, wild and terrible, grew bolder. From the southern forests they swept north, attacking villages, burning monasteries, defiling sacred groves. Caldria and the Sylvan Marches mounted crusades in reply, but even now, the **war on the frontier is endless**.

---

### **V. The Third Crusade and the Blood-Soaked Miracle**

When the **Ahl-e-Ahd** turned east, seeking to press beyond their northern holdings and into our fragmented heart, it was the **Duchy of Caldria** that stood alone.

The **Third Crusade**, mounted from **Caldros**, gathered not kings but knights, not armies but oaths. They were joined, controversially, by warlocks from the **Citadel of Vorthara**—an unholy alliance to some, a necessary sin to others.

The battle at Caldria’s gates—where **siege golems shattered on Aurelian steel**—is spoken of in hushed tones as a second miracle. The **Valley Ambush** that followed crippled the Ahl-e-Ahd's retreat. And when the **Thalmyric rebels** rose in the west, marching to reclaim **Caleran**, it was **Eclessian blood** that bought them time to strike.

And yet… when **Thalmyria** crowned its emperor, **they did not look east**.

---

### **VI. Thalmyria, Sestara, and the Shadow of Ahl-e-Ahd**

We, the children of the Redeemer’s own homeland, were offered no unifying banner from the west. The **Thalmyric Church** declared their emperor divinely chosen. The **Sanctum Divinitatis**, from Aurelia, denied this claim. Thus the **Great Schism** began.

And so we remain as we are:

- **Aurelia**, cloaked in relics and golden hymns, still believing unity will come through holiness.
- **Caldria**, its knights ever marching, its Duke always praying with sword in hand.
- **Korrheim**, its mercenaries fighting everyone’s wars but its own.
- **Veridancia**, ever gilded, whispering in courts, cloaking ambition in velvet.
- **Ironvale**, hammering away in smoke and coal, indifferent to scripture.
- **Virelia**, vast and hungry, watching the fractured lands like a wolf.
- **Brauvalis**, brewing truth into ale, letting the world come to it.
- **The Sylvan Marches**, standing sentinel where no one else dares.

---

### **VII. The Church and the Crown That Isn't**

The **Sanctum Divinitatis** has proclaimed it plainly:  
_“Whoever reunites Eclessia shall be crowned not merely king, but servant of the Lord—true heir to the Redeemer’s earthly dominion.”_

But who among us dares?

The crown weighs too much. It would mean war. And worse—it would mean risking **heresy**. For what if the wrong man takes it? What if the Redeemer’s legacy is distorted once more?

And so, we wait.  
In candlelight.  
In hymn and dust.  
In border-skirms and pilgrim-processions.  
In wine-stained treaties and war-torn groves.

---

### **VIII. A Land of Fragments, Yet Still Burning**

We are **not dead**.  
We are not lost.

We are **Eclessia**.

We are the morning liturgy, and the midnight assassin. We are the crusader's blade, and the relic-bearer's trembling hand. We are flawed. Faithful. Fractured. And we wait.

We wait for the Redeemer’s will to reveal itself.

And perhaps, one day, we shall not just **remember the empire that was**, but become the **kingdom that must be**.
