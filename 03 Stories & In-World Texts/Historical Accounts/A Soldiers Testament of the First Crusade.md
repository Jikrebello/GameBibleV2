## **“Ashes of the Western March: A Soldier’s Testament”**

By Commander Tiber Senhal of the IV Cohort, West Atherian Legion  
_Written in exile, Year 41 of the Interregnum_

> _“I was there when the sky cracked open and the empire learned fear. I did not break—I was broken. There is a difference.”_

---

I served in the **IV Cohort**, garrisoned in the city of **Albretor**, near the western edge of the empire. It was a sleepy place—more known for barley fields than blood. We were veterans, mostly; old men, half-retired, half-forgotten. We drilled. We patrolled. We enforced the law. We believed in our invincibility.

Then came the **night sky full of fire**, and the sound of **iron hooves without riders**.

---

We had heard rumors, of course—whispers of **siege golems** the size of towers, of **Janissaries** who knew no fear, of **contracts written in flame**. But we had faced rebels and raiders before. The empire was always victorious. It was said we had the **favor of the Redeemer**, and that our enemies would break like waves on Atherian stone.

We did not know we were the stone.  
We did not know how easily stone could be shattered.

---

The **Ahl-e-Ahd** did not march—they **erupted**. Their war host appeared like a storm—no scouts reported them, no messengers warned us. Their first assault came not by sword, but by **sigil and pact**. I watched a fortress dissolve into **sand and shrieking ash** as its very name was struck from the world.

Their Janissaries were like statues of war: **tall, perfect, silent**, never flinching, never retreating. Their siege engines moved with a kind of hunger, fed not by coal or draft beasts, but by **bound spirits and fire-tongued incantations**. And when the devils came—no horns, no jokes, no riddles, only the screaming—we understood we were not fighting men.

We were fighting **contracts incarnate**.

---

We tried to hold the line. At **Raventor**, we made a stand. Four thousand of us. Cavalry, skirmishers, even retired mages from the old colleges. They sent **one golem**. Just one.

It crushed the gates without effort. The wall held—until it **leapt** over them. I still remember the sound of its limbs cracking stone, the way its footsteps shook the earth like a judgment. I lost half my cohort in less than an hour.

And then they marched on.

---

The **Velian States** fell in weeks. Not conquered—**subjugated**. Their princes bent the knee so quickly I wonder if they had been waiting for it. The Ahl-e-Ahd didn’t just take territory—they **rewrote it**. Their scribes carved symbols into the stone of our cities, and the cities forgot their names.

We sent dispatches to **Caleran**, begging for reinforcements. The reply?  
_“You are the reinforcements.”_

---

When the **First Crusade** was called, it was done with urgency—not ceremony. There was no grand anointing, no procession of holy relics. Only the order:  
_“All legions west of the Thalassian Spine—march.”_

We burned our own crops so they wouldn’t feed the invaders. We left villages empty, cities hollow, so that we might concentrate what remained. The legions from the east—**good men, faithful men**—were pulled from their posts, their provinces abandoned. I remember meeting a captain from **Eclessia**—a boy, really. Seventeen. They’d sent him with a hundred barely-armed recruits.

He died at my side two days later, an arrow through the eye.

---

We did slow them. The crusade **broke their advance**. At the **Pass of Vantros**, we held for ten days. At **Dalan Bridge**, we collapsed the span while our own still ran across it. These were not victories. They were **sacrifices**.

And for every inch we reclaimed, **Eclessia burned** behind us.

I remember seeing the orders come in—**pull back from Aurelia**, **abandon Sestara**, **concentrate on the heartland**. We were saving the core of the empire at the **cost of its soul**.

---

By the time the crusade ended, we had held a line. Just barely. But the **eastern provinces were gone**. No more roads. No more grain. No more prayers answered.

I still hear the voice of a field priest at Raventor, before the golem came. He stood on the parapet and shouted:  
_“The Flame does not flicker!”_

But it did.  
It flickered.  
And in some places, it went out.

---

> _I am Tiber Senhal. I served. I obeyed. I survived. But I do not know if I won._
> 
> _If any still remember the IV Cohort, know this: we did not die cowards. We died forgotten._
