## **“The Ascent of the Flame-Bearer: Caedros and the Birth of Thalmyria”**

_By Historian-Monk Elenmar of the Flamebound Athenaeum, written in the 3rd Year of the Reign of Emperor Auren IV_

---

> _“The Redeemer sent no thunderbolt, no angelic host, no pillar of fire. Instead, He sent Caedros—son of the mountains, sword of the faithful, shadow of the old empire and spark of the new.”_

---

### **I. The Ashen Years**

In the aftermath of the **Second Crusade**, the Atherian world burned.

The **Velian Campaign**, intended to reclaim the empire’s northern provinces, had ended in **utter catastrophe**. The legions were ill-supplied, poorly commanded, and fractured by rival noble houses. The **Ahl-e-Ahd**, far from defeated, had anticipated the offensive. Their counterattack was swift and absolute.

As the shattered crusader remnants fled south, the desert war-host surged forward like a divine storm. With no standing army left to stop them, the **Ahl-e-Ahd marched on Caleran**.

The capital—**once eternal, once divine**—fell in less than a fortnight.

There are no songs for what happened next. Only silence. Only ruins.  
**The emperor and his household were executed publicly**, their bodies strung in the square of the Redeemer’s Basilica. The Holy Flame was extinguished.  
The great roads ran red with blood—so much so that, even now, they are called the **Iron Veins**.

---

### **II. A Flame in the Mountains**

The survivors—faithful, soldiers, priests, children—fled into the **Volcanic Mountains**, into the **Ashen Passes**, into **forgotten garrisons and fire-wracked caves**. It is said that in those days, even the Flame faltered, and the faithful began to believe the age of miracles had ended.

But among the exiles was a man of no great house, **Caedros of Naranth Keep**, a noble of humble lineage, barely known even in the southern provinces. A war captain. A mountain-born soldier. A man hardened by ash and silence.

He took no crown. He gave no decrees. He **fought**.

With what soldiers remained—veterans, volunteers, deserters—he began a campaign not of conquest, but of **defiance**. He struck at supply lines, harried Ahl-e-Ahd convoys, liberated slave caravans, and razed outposts. His band became a legion. His raids became campaigns. His name passed from whisper to prayer.

They called him **Ashwolf**, **Oathblade**, **General of the Forgotten**, and at last—**the Flamebearer**.

---

### **III. The Sword in the Fire**

According to the oldest Thalmyric chronicles, in the weeks following the successful routing of Ahl-e-Ahd forces at **Dornheim** during the Third Crusade, Caedros received a vision beneath the shadow of **Mount Rhaset**, the highest peak of the Ashen Range. There, deep within a sealed vault once belonging to an Atherian order of mystic paladins, he uncovered an ancient weapon, long forgotten by both Church and Empire.

It was called **Soltirael**, the **Sword of White Flame**.

Forged in the days of the Redeemer’s earliest followers but never wielded by Him—who fought no battles with steel—it was said to bear a flame that did not burn flesh, but **judged the soul** of the bearer. Only one bound by sacred oaths and tested by loss could awaken its fire.

When Caedros laid hands upon it, the blade is said to have ignited.

Some say it was a miracle. Others, a prophecy fulfilled. But all agreed: the general had been chosen.

---

### **IV. The Turning Tides**

With **Soltirael** in hand, Caedros led the faithful to a legendary ambush at the **Valley of Halberths**, where his tactics crippled an elite division of the Ahl-e-Ahd. This, combined with the earlier defense of **Caldros** and the coordination with the warlocks of **Vorthara**, marked the turning of the tide.

Soon after, the Thalmyric banners flew at **Virelos**, and then at the gates of **Caleran**.

---

### **V. The Crowning of Caedros**

When the army marched into Caleran, they did not find the Redeemer’s Flame. But they did find the altar where it had once burned. Caedros did not attempt to reignite it.

He drove **Soltirael** into the stone steps of the sanctuary and knelt beside it.

It was the **High Confessor of Virelos**, not a noble or general, who declared the truth first:

> _“We are not exiles. We are inheritors. Let the world know: the Empire is not dead—it is reforged.”_

In the **great courtyard of the Basilica**, amid ruins still smoking, **Caedros was crowned**. He refused a circlet of gold and instead wore a **crown of scorched bronze**, forged from the bells of burned monasteries.

Thus was born **Thalmyria**—named not for any ancient house or province, but for the **Thalassian Flame**, which never truly died.

---

### **VI. Legacy and the Living Myth**

In the years since, **Caedros** has become more than a name. He is now **Patron of the Reclaimed Flame**, **First Sword of the Redeemer**, and **Sanctified Flamebearer**, canonized by the **Thalmyric Redeemer Church** in the year following his death.

Statues show him not as a king, but as a general—scarred, unyielding, with **Soltirael in one hand and a contract of rebellion in the other**.

To this day, on the **Day of the Reclamation**, the emperor walks barefoot into the ruins of the old Basilica, and lays a wreath of red ash at the tomb of Caedros.

> _“He was not chosen by crown or council. He was chosen by fire.”_
