## **“Ashes of the Western March: A Soldier’s Testament”**

By Commander Tiber Senhal of the IV Cohort, West Atherian Legion  
_Written in exile, Year 41 of the Interregnum_

> _“I was there when the sky cracked open and the empire learned fear. I did not break—I was broken. There is a difference.”_

---

I served in the **IV Cohort**, garrisoned in the city of **Albretor**, near the western edge of the empire. It was a sleepy place—more known for barley fields than blood. We were veterans, mostly; old men, half-retired, half-forgotten. We drilled. We patrolled. We enforced the law. We believed in our invincibility.

Then came the **dust on the north road**, and the sound of **horsemen who did not hurry**.

---

We had heard rumors, of course—whispers of **breach-courts**, of jurists who rode with the army and read a town's charter at its own gate, of **contracts that could unmake an oath**. But we had faced rebels and raiders before. The empire was always victorious. It was said we had the **favor of the Redeemer**, and that our enemies would break like waves on Atherian stone.

We did not know we were the stone.  
We did not know how easily stone could be shattered.

---

The **Ahl-e-Ahd** did not storm us—they **served** us. Their host halted a bowshot from the gate, and a jurist in grey rode forward under an awning with his scribes and his seal-boxes. He asked to see our charter, and the witness who had sealed it. Our charter had been sworn under the Stars, before augurs. The college that witnessed it had been dissolved for nearly two hundred years, and nobody had asked that star anything since. He read one sentence. I watched a fortress dissolve without a stone falling, as its oath was struck from the world.

The men of the garrison had sworn on that charter. By evening half of them were saying they had sworn to nothing. Some walked home with the pay chest. The money-lenders besieged the counting-houses. Before dawn someone opened the postern, and nobody would say who. No horns, no jokes, no riddles, only the reading—and we understood we were not fighting men.

We were fighting **contracts incarnate**.

---

We tried to hold the line. At **Raventor**, we made a stand. Four thousand of us. Cavalry, skirmishers, even retired mages from the old colleges. Raventor would not hear the court, so they sent the **horsemen**.

Qatada's riders came over the ditch at dusk with the levies behind them. The wall held—until someone inside it remembered that he had sworn to nothing. I still remember the sound of the gate bar dropping, the way the riders' hooves shook the earth like a judgment. I lost half my cohort in less than an hour.

And then they rode on.

---

The **north** fell town by town, in a handful of years. Not conquered—**served**. Its magnates took terms so quickly I wonder if they had been waiting for it; they had not forgotten the requisition columns of the hungry years. When the northern field army rode out to kill a court's escort at the **Dry Ford**, Qatada was waiting, and by evening there was no northern field army. The Ahl-e-Ahd didn’t just take territory—they **rewrote it**. Their scribes carved symbols into the stone of our cities, and the cities forgot their names.

We sent dispatches to **Caleran**, begging for reinforcements. The reply?  
_“You are the reinforcements.”_

---

For twenty years after, we held what we could and lost the rest a town at a time. When the **First Crusade** was called, it was done with urgency—not ceremony. There was no grand anointing, no procession of holy relics. Only the order:  
_“All legions west of the Thalassian Spine—march.”_

We burned our own crops so they wouldn’t feed the invaders. We left villages empty, cities hollow, so that we might concentrate what remained. The legions from the east—**good men, faithful men**—were pulled from their posts, their provinces abandoned. I remember meeting a captain from the **eastern provinces**—a boy, really. Seventeen. They’d sent him with a hundred barely-armed recruits.

He died at my side two days later, an arrow through the eye.

---

We did slow them. The crusade **broke their advance**. At the **Pass of Vantros**, we held for ten days. At **Dalan Bridge**, we collapsed the span while our own still ran across it. These were not victories. They were **sacrifices**.

Behind us the hermit-bishops went from town to town, reading each charter aloud again before its people. Where the Fire came, it stood on the wax like a white candle-flame, and no court of theirs ever unmade those seals. The bearers who kindled at the sealings paid for it. I saw one led away blind after his ninth town.

And for every inch we held, **the east went hungry** behind us.

I remember seeing the orders come in—**strip the eastern garrisons**, **let the Sestaran cities hire their own ships**, **concentrate on the heartland**. We were saving the core of the empire at the **cost of its soul**.

---

By the time the crusade ended, we had held a line. Just barely. But the **northern provinces were gone**. No more roads. No more grain. No more prayers answered.

I still hear the voice of a field priest at Raventor, before the riders came. He stood on the parapet and shouted:  
_“The Flame does not flicker!”_

But it did.  
It flickered.  
And in some places, it went out.  
And in others, on the wax of a charter in a market square, it stood up white, and there we held.

---

> _I am Tiber Senhal. I served. I obeyed. I survived. But I do not know if I won._
> 
> _If any still remember the IV Cohort, know this: we did not die cowards. We died forgotten._
