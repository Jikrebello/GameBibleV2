# Gap Analysis & Refinement Targets

External review of the full vault (July 2026), measured against the stated standard: LOTR depth, Elder Scrolls texture, ASOIAF political-historical richness.

## Verdict in One Paragraph

The metaphysical spine of this setting is already at or above the standard you're aiming for. The World Egg cosmology, the axis of coherence-versus-refusal, astral rent, contract magic, the sainthood protocol, and "institutions as truth engines" together form an original, mechanically generative system that most published settings never achieve. The Setting Commandments and Historical Spine are genuinely excellent discipline documents. What separates the vault from Tolkien/TES/ASOIAF right now is not imagination — it is **specificity**: names, dates, distances, genealogies, and the ground-level texture of ordinary life. The cosmology is a cathedral; the map underneath it is still mostly blank parchment.

## REALIGNMENT — Status After the July 2026 Working Sessions

The three structural gaps are broken. **Chronology (#2): done** — AR dating, present day 1360, the full spine dated and sanity-checked. **Named history (#3): substantially done** — sixty-plus figures across every age, the Companions, Amaryah, the courts of 1360, in-world texts written. **Geography (#1): v1 done** — world gazetteer, labeled world map, Grenzburg regional map and geography; remaining are refinement layers (duchy borders, roads, caliphate internals, art-quality maps).

**Enforcement sweep: done.** Mojibake repaired vault-wide (28 files); naming drift resolved as canon via [[Names of the Menhir, Masks of the Idols]] (Solzareth=Father Sun, Furor/The Fury=Wrath, Avarix=Greed, Lunara=Mother Moon, Dreamlands=archaic Shadowlands, Star Pantheon=Menhir worship canonized); Mono-Myth reframed as in-world source with the hybrid ruling in its preface; von Bismarck → **von Ehrenfeld**, "Biohesda" → Grenzburg-Model, DeepSeek file archived; "jihad" → **the Unsealing** across active files (Jihad al-Kalima → the Milla-Strife); Timeline Overview banner-superseded; Vesari integrated into Scattering/Spine/Ancient Ages; Mael–Lakekeeper linked; Grenzmark/Grenzburg ruling written into the Duke's note; Historical Accounts rated in the new [[Reliability Register]]. **Still staged for a dedicated pass:** the deep language filtering of the Jirahar/Ahl-e-Ahd/Nsizwa culture docs (Sahifat, inkandu, Spirit-Keepers, etc. — wholesale terminology rework deserving its own session), and the 30-odd `needs-review` flags which should be burned down as each area gets its next content pass.

**Recommended focus order from here:** (A) the **canon enforcement sweep** — one disciplined pass propagating every decision into the legacy files and clearing hygiene, before the two-canon problem metastasizes; then (B) the **bestiary rebuild** to World Egg logic; then (C) **culture daily-life texture** (Eclessia and Folk first, feeding Grenzburg); then (D) the **numbers pass** (populations, garrisons, prices — Grenzburg first); then (E) **villain infrastructure** (named Sinner counter-churches, Idol cults, patron syndicates at world level).

## Tier 1 — Structural Gaps (these block everything else)

### 1. Geography has no names

The world map is unlabeled: no borders, capitals, rivers, seas, roads, or scale. Nearly every weakness elsewhere traces back here — wars without corridors, cultures without homelands you can point to, a Grenzburg that "sits on a frontier" of nothing in particular. Tolkien's depth is inseparable from the fact that every league of Middle-earth is named and walkable. The Geography Atlas already lists exactly what's needed; it just hasn't been done. The southern-hemisphere inversion (south = cold) is a distinctive hook and deserves to be visible on the map itself.

**Target:** one labeled political/physical map pass — even ugly — naming the ~8 major realms, ~15 key cities (Caleran, Veridun, Virelos, Fiorenzia, Vorthara, Al-Qahur, Grenzburg, Castara...), major rivers/passes/seas, and the Great Glass Desert.

### 2. No dating system, no numbers anywhere

There are no years, reign lengths, or intervals anywhere in the timeline ("~80 years after the schism" is nearly the only number in the vault). ASOIAF's texture depends on chronological precision: readers can compute that Robert's Rebellion was 15 years ago. Without dates you cannot check whether the Fall of Caleran, the four Crusades, the Thalmyric founding, and the schism actually fit in believable human lifespans — and right now they probably don't.

**Target:** pick an epoch (the obvious candidate: dating from the Redeemer's execution/resurrection, e.g. "AR"), then assign years to the ~20 spine events and present day. One afternoon of work, enormous downstream payoff.

### 3. History has almost no people in it

Outside Grenzburg's cast, the entire historical record is nearly nameless. The Great Prophet has no name. The Twelve Companions are a set, not twelve people. There are no king-lists, no dynasties, no named popes/patriarchs of Eclessia or Veridun, no famous saints beyond protocol descriptions, no great traitors, no doomed lovers. ASOIAF-grade history is gossip about specific dead people. Every "inherited wound" in the Historical Spine should have at least one name, one place, and one relic attached.

**Target:** a "Who's Who of the Dead" pass — 3–5 named figures per age, plus dynastic lines for Thalmyria (Aetherion), the major Eclessian crowns, and the Caliphate.

## Tier 2 — Canon Conflicts (decide, then enforce)

### 4. Two incompatible origin stories for the Menhir and Idols

The Mono-Myth Overview (older phase, filed as canon) says the Menhir and Idols are **fallen rebel angels** who fled into nature and the "Dreamlands" after the Great Rebellion. The current World Egg cosmology says the Menhir are **pre-mortal natural laws given sovereignty** ("would exist even without mortals") and the Idols are Shadowlands meaning-engines. These are different metaphysics. Options: (a) demote the Mono-Myth to an in-world text (it reads beautifully as one), (b) rewrite it to match the World Egg spine, or (c) canonize the rebellion as true and adjust the cosmology docs. Commandment 3 says the author's spine must know — currently it doesn't.

### 5. Naming drift between phases

An inventory of collisions that need a canonical answer: Father Sun vs **Solzareth** (The Second Circle.md) — same being? Mother Moon vs **Lunara Noctis / Lunara** (Ättar docs, Nythalum). Idols as **Furor/Avarix** (prehistory) vs **Wrath/Greed** (current). The Grobi origin cites "**The Fury**" — presumably Wrath. "Dreamlands" vs "Shadowlands." Also: the Age of the Redeemer names the "High Augur of **Astaroth**" — Astaroth is a Second Circle Menhir, which implies the Atherian Star Pantheon was Menhir worship. Is that intended? If so it's a great revelation; it should be stated in the authorial spine.

## Tier 3 — Depth & Texture Gaps

### 7. Language and naming discipline

Currently three regimes coexist: real-world names used verbatim (Bishop Konrad **von Bismarck**), real-world languages borrowed wholesale (actual Arabic for Ahl-e-Ahd/Jirahar: jihad, Sifra d'Dinai; actual Zulu for the Nsizwa: inkandu, Spirit-Keepers, war-regiment), and invented names (Caleran, Vorthara, Thalmyria — these are good). ASOIAF-style analog cultures are a legitimate choice, but even GRRM filters names (Jaime not James; ser not sir). Tolkien-standard requires at least consistent phonetic rules per culture. Decide the policy once, then apply it: pure analog, filtered analog, or constructed naming tables per culture. Von Bismarck in particular must go.

### 8. Cultures have theses but not yet daily life

Commandment 8 is only half-fulfilled. Every culture answers reality; almost none of them eat, sing, joke, marry, or bury in documented detail. The cultural thesis docs themselves list the needed texture (feast calendars, folk heresies, wedding customs, veteran villages). Priority order given Grenzburg: Eclessia first, Folk/Fäskogarfolk second, Thalmyria third. Also: the **Tarkhan** are an explicit stub, and the **Nsizwa** — one of the best-developed cultures — float unconnected to the map and the present-day board. The Ethnic Groups Overview is mostly phenotype description; it needs rebuilding as culture-first with the physical notes folded in.

### 9. Bestiary is thin and phase-inconsistent

Drakes are ~200 words each; the Bestiary MOC's own standard (ecology, tactics, signs, lair, loot, hooks) is unmet everywhere. Creature-cosmology integration is inconsistent: Grobi are cursed by "The Fury" (old Idol naming); the drake color-coding reads as legacy D&D rather than World Egg logic (where do drakes sit — Faewild fauna, Shell expressions, Hell-drake kin?). The Adversary doc establishes dragons as abyssal exile-intelligences, while Grenzburg's Act II features a huntable Emerald Drake — the relationship between "drakes" and "dragons" needs one paragraph of canon law.

### 10. The left side of the board is abstract

Saints, the Church, and rightward institutions are richly developed. Their opposition is mostly categorical: "Sinner cults are plural and adaptive" — but not one named counter-church, Idol cult, necromantic order, or patron syndicate exists at the named-NPC level outside Grenzburg's Pale-Skins (whose canon identity — vampire bloodline? Idol clients? — needs pinning). Villains need the same specificity as churches.

### 11. Numbers for the material world

No populations, army sizes, travel times, or price anchors exist. A game bible needs order-of-magnitude anchors at least for Grenzburg: city population, garrison size, grain stores in wagon-loads, days of travel to the nearest Eclessian crown. Commandment 1 (material realm first) currently has no arithmetic behind it.

### 12. In-world texts need reliability tags

The Historical Accounts folder is a strong start (multi-perspective accounts of the same wars is exactly the right instinct). The existing backlog item — mark each as reliable / disputed / propaganda / apocryphal, in frontmatter — remains undone, and the Mono-Myth question (see #4) is the biggest single entry in that ledger.
