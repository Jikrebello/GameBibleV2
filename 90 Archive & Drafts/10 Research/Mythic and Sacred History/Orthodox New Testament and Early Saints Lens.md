# Orthodox New Testament and Early Saints Lens

## Status and Purpose

This is an author-only exploratory lens. It does not add another apostle, rewrite the Redeemer's life, canonize a historical seed, or turn an Orthodox saint's Life into a one-to-one World Egg biography.

It extends [[Orthodox Old Testament Lens]] into the Redeemer and post-Redeemer ages through two related but unequal bodies of material:

1. the canonical New Testament, read through its Eastern Orthodox Christological and liturgical setting;
2. the early saints as received in Eastern Orthodox commemoration, while distinguishing early historical testimony from later hagiographic and folkloric growth.

The intended yield is a broader historical ecology around the setting's already-fixed sacred events:

- flawed disciples and minor co-workers rather than replacement apostles;
- households, widows, merchants, prisoners, deacons, translators, physicians, grave workers, patrons, clerks, and sympathetic officials;
- martyr companies and survivor networks;
- letters, court records, feast days, relic translations, charitable institutions, and disputed Lives;
- genuine repentance that does not erase victims or consequence;
- saints whose historical importance lies in founding a hospital, preserving names, correcting an institution, refusing an office, translating a hymn, or feeding strangers;
- later wonder-cycles that can be historically powerful without being contemporary biography;
- active saintly commission kept categorically distinct from relic response, residue, office, legend-persona, Patron imitation, and Idol counterfeit.

The governing premise is:

> The New Testament supplies the post-Redeemer theological and moral lens. Early Orthodox saints supply tested forms of historical reception and holy life. World Egg history, cultures, and metaphysics determine the actual stories.

## Relationship to the Old Testament Lens

The two lenses are not competitors.

- The Old Testament lens remains the chief sacred-historical grammar for Creation, Chains, Exodus, Scattering, judges, kings, exile, resistance, restoration, and the social formation of idolatry.
- The New Testament lens governs the interpretation of the Redeemer, Church, martyrdom, mission, repentance, resurrection hope, spiritual gifts, service, and post-Redeemer sanctity.
- The early saint tradition shows how those New Testament patterns enter actual institutions, cities, households, persecutions, arguments, famines, courts, monasteries, and later communal memory.

The Lord, the Redeemer, the Passion, the Resurrection, Pentecost, and the final restoration are not reusable heroic motifs. They are theological centers from which lesser human grammars may be learned.

## Orthodox Scriptural Ordering and Liturgical Cautions

### The Gospels are the center

The four Gospels are not merely classical biographies. They are ecclesial witness to the Redeemer. In Orthodox worship the Gospel Book is enthroned upon the altar, and the Gospel is proclaimed as a liturgical act. This gives the material a different weight from a saint's Life, court chronicle, or later legend.

**World Egg consequence:** no comparative seed may turn the Redeemer into one ascending hero among others. The Redeemer's identity and work remain unique. Human-scale planting belongs chiefly around the sacred center: witnesses, households, failures, burial care, hospitality, desertion, restoration, and mission.

### Acts is the bridge from sacred event to lived history

Acts supplies the strongest scriptural bridge for the Underground Centuries:

- a frightened and divided company becomes a missionary body;
- language difference is met without abolishing peoples;
- widows' complaints produce administrative reform;
- martyrs, prison doors, shipwrecks, trials, councils, households, workshops, roads, and letters all matter;
- Gentile inclusion is discerned through events, testimony, argument, Scripture, and conciliar judgement;
- named apostles remain central, but much of the work is carried by deacons, merchants, artisans, hosts, messengers, soldiers, prisoners, and local congregations.

Acts is read liturgically in the Paschal season. Its institutional stories should therefore remain part of sacred witness, not be reduced to a neutral handbook for revolutionary cells or church bureaucracy.

### The Epistles preserve ordinary networks

The letters are unusually valuable for garden planting because their greetings and local disputes reveal a Church made of particular households, trades, women and men, patrons, slaves, travelers, prisoners, teachers, rivals, scribes, and co-workers.

They also provide moral limits:

- gifts differ and no member is the whole body;
- apparent spiritual power requires discernment;
- wealth, speech, favoritism, faction, and hypocrisy can corrupt a congregation;
- leaders remain accountable;
- repentance produces material obligations rather than merely a new reputation;
- a church may be holy in vocation while its local members and authorities remain capable of grave error.

### Revelation is symbolic, liturgical, and not a codebook

Revelation belongs to the Orthodox New Testament but is not read in the ordinary Orthodox liturgical lectionary. Orthodox introductory teaching explicitly warns against assigning its images one exclusive identification with particular historical people and events.

**World Egg use:** Revelation can shape imperial-beast imagery, faithful witness under predatory sovereignty, the contrast between the devouring city and heavenly worship, and the exposure of counterfeit glory. It must not become a cryptographic timeline, a direct key to an Idol's Regnant sequence, or evidence that a current empire is the unique final enemy.

## Source-Status Ladder for Saints

Every use of a saint must record the kind of source being used. “The Life says” is not enough.

| Level | Source type | What it can safely establish for research | What it cannot establish by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | canonical New Testament | governing sacred witness and theological pattern | a reusable substitute Redeemer, apostle, Passion, or Resurrection |
| **B** | near-contemporary letter, apology, court record, or early martyr act | unusually early earthly-life testimony, community network, trial, teaching, or death memory | the truth of every later miracle or iconographic feature |
| **C** | patristic history or theological biography written within living or recoverable memory | how the Church understood a life and used it to teach or form an institution | modern critical certainty about every scene, speech, number, or marvel |
| **D** | synaxarion, menaion, calendar Life, feast, hymn, or relic commemoration | the saint's received place and spiritual meaning in Eastern Orthodox tradition | a flat contemporary chronicle of all narrated details |
| **E** | later miracle, military-intervention, translation, animal, dragon, sleep, or pilgrimage cycle | the history of devotion, local identity, ritual memory, and legend accretion | automatic proof that the earthly saint performed the event during life |
| **F** | modern devotional retelling or popular conflation | present reception and research leads | independent historical or doctrinal authority |

Several levels may be true and important at once. The purpose of the ladder is not to sneer at later tradition. It is to ask what kind of truth a source is carrying: early biography, theological memory, liturgical recognition, local appropriation, wonder testimony, or folklore.

### Pre-schism and post-schism caution

The figures below belong chiefly to the undivided early centuries and are part of a broad Christian inheritance. This dossier studies **their reception in Eastern Orthodox tradition**. It does not claim that later Roman Catholic and Eastern Orthodox devotion, canonization procedure, iconography, relic law, or popular patronage developed identically.

This is an Eastern Orthodox lens, not an Oriental Orthodox survey. Shared saints and overlapping ancient sources do not erase later ecclesial distinctions.

## Non-Transferable Sacred Centers

The following are not available for reskinning:

1. the Redeemer's divine-human identity;
2. Incarnation, Passion, Resurrection, Ascension, and final victory as repeatable hero stages;
3. the Theotokos as a generic mother-goddess grammar;
4. Pentecost as a spell that erases language, culture, study, or translation labor;
5. the Twelve as an expandable roster;
6. apostolic authority as a mantle attainable by any successful adventuring company;
7. martyrdom as a mechanism that forces salvation or sainthood;
8. relics as batteries containing divisible pieces of a soul;
9. liturgical recognition as a magical manufacturing procedure;
10. Revelation as an event-by-event future chronology.

## Highest-Yield New Testament Story Grammars

### 1. The flawed commissioned company

The disciples are called from ordinary labor, misunderstand their commission, compete for precedence, fail under pressure, scatter, and require restoration. One betrays; another denies; several disappear from the narrative's foreground while the work continues.

**World Egg use:** companies may enter history without understanding their destination. Their later offices and legends should not erase cowardice, correction, ordinary labor, or members who never become headline figures.

**Boundary:** the existing [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer|Companion roll]] is closed. Apply this grammar to later companies, not to new companions of the Redeemer.

### 2. Fidelity after apparent defeat

The myrrh-bearing women, Joseph of Arimathea, and Nicodemus act when public victory appears impossible. Burial, washing, witness, and presence become historically decisive forms of courage.

**World Egg use:** burial hands, body-recovery networks, witnesses who retain names, households that shelter the disgraced, and people who serve without expecting reversal.

### 3. The household as historical infrastructure

Lydia, Priscilla and Aquila, Cornelius, the Philippian jailer, Martha and Mary, and numerous epistolary hosts show that roads and missions depend upon houses, workshops, kitchens, money, hospitality, kin, servants, and local credibility.

**World Egg use:** a household can carry an age without becoming a secret order. Its descendants may inherit a door-sign, guest-right, burial roll, workbench, debt, or disputed memory rather than a bloodline destiny.

### 4. The complaint that creates an office

In Acts, a dispute over neglected widows is not treated as an embarrassing distraction from spiritual work. It produces named responsibility and a new pattern of service.

**World Egg use:** institutions should often arise from a concrete failure in food, burial, language access, prison care, or refugee distribution. A sacred institution can begin because overlooked people complained truthfully.

### 5. The minor co-worker network

Acts and the Epistles preserve messengers, hosts, scribes, married artisans, deacons, merchants, physicians, jailers, sailors, provincial officials, and people known only through a greeting.

**World Egg use:** create “Praise of the Co-workers” rolls for the Redeemer and Underground periods. One-line names can later grow into roads, feasts, lawsuits, martyr traditions, or local saints without adding a new universal apostle.

### 6. Prisoner roads and letters

Paul's captivity and the later journeys of Ignatius show how a prisoner can remain an actor through letters, visitors, guards, hearings, shipmates, and congregations along the route.

**World Egg use:** a prison road can braid otherwise separate communities. The surviving letters may disagree, be recopied, or be attached to the wrong prisoner without requiring a hidden master organization.

### 7. Council after conflict

The Jerusalem council does not begin with unanimity. Testimony, argument, Scripture, local experience, authority, and a written decision all matter.

**World Egg use:** councils can be dramatic historical events whose decrees solve one question while leaving implementation, resentment, translation, enforcement, and minority experience open.

**Boundary:** no missed letter or single friendship failure should become the sole cause of the Great Split.

### 8. Correction without humiliation

Priscilla and Aquila take aside the eloquent Apollos and teach him more accurately. Authority, learning, craft, marriage, and private correction coexist.

**World Egg use:** an artisan household may correct a famous preacher, jurist, or hero without becoming the public face of the movement.

### 9. Restitution-bearing repentance

Zacchaeus makes material restitution. Paul lives with the consequences of having persecuted the Church. Onesimus and Philemon raise difficult obligations within an unequal social order.

**World Egg use:** conversion does not erase stolen property, broken families, compromised offices, victims, or the law. Genuine forgiveness and continuing restitution can coexist.

### 10. Gifts that do not become sovereignty

The Pauline image of one body with many members refuses to make one gift, office, or charismatic figure into the whole Church.

**World Egg use:** healer, seer, exorcist, martyr, patron, bishop, translator, and ruler remain distinct vocations. A miracle cannot make its bearer doctrinally or politically infallible.

### 11. Healing as relation, skill, and sign

The Gospels and Acts include healings, but the early Church also preserves physicians, caregivers, and almoners. Human medicine and miracle are not enemies and must not be collapsed into one power system.

**World Egg use:** medical skill solves what skill can solve. A heavenly sign answers its bounded commission. Failure to receive a miracle is never evidence of guilt or insufficient humanity.

### 12. Exorcism without erasing the person

The New Testament distinguishes the human sufferer from the hostile power afflicting them.

**World Egg use:** possession, Idol feeding, residue, trauma, biological illness, disability, and deception require different diagnoses. Do not use demonic language to stigmatize mental illness, epilepsy, disability, or cultural difference.

### 13. The poor or foreign witness

The Good Samaritan, the widow's offering, the Ethiopian official, the centurion, women witnesses, and socially suspect converts repeatedly unsettle assumed centers of righteousness.

**World Egg use:** the decisive witness may come from outside formal power. No entire culture should be cast as “the Samaritan people,” and poverty must not be romanticized as a resource for someone else's sanctity.

### 14. Apocalypse as exposure of empire

Revelation joins political terror, economic seduction, martyr witness, worship, and counterfeit universal power.

**World Egg use:** it is an excellent grammar for how an Idol-facing empire tries to make commerce, law, image, and worship speak with one mouth. It is not a cipher for naming a unique historical Regnant.

It also does **not** permit removal of the received bodily trial when imprisonment, sexual threat, pregnancy, family coercion, exposure, beasts, burning, freezing, mutilation, beheading, exile, voluntary martyrdom, severe asceticism, or frightening miracle is load-bearing. Preserve that hardness at its correct documentary, Life, liturgical, iconographic, or folklore layer; never invent torture merely for atmosphere.

## What the Saint Tradition Adds that the New Testament Alone Does Not

### 1. A history of reception

Saint tradition shows how one human life produces several later histories:

1. an earthly person and soul;
2. documents written during or soon after the life;
3. a local burial and feast;
4. a liturgical title and iconographic form;
5. relic translations and institutional claims;
6. later miracle and folklore cycles;
7. political appropriation by cities, armies, guilds, rulers, migrants, or rival churches.

These layers may reinforce, correct, or conflict with one another. They never become one undifferentiated supernatural identity.

### 2. Holiness after persecution

Martyrdom is not the only saintly grammar. When persecution declines, holiness moves visibly into:

- hospitals and poorhouses;
- common-rule communities;
- desert counsel;
- preaching and exile;
- translation and hymnody;
- household management;
- famine and plague service;
- resistance to Christian rulers and bishops;
- the slow correction of Christian institutions.

This prevents the Conversion era from becoming a story in which sanctity ends when public Christianity begins.

### 3. Collective memory without a collective soul

The Forty Martyrs, Perpetua's companions, family groups, and monastic communities show how a company may receive one feast or title while every member remains a distinct person.

**World Egg ruling:** a collective saint-title is liturgical and historical, not a merged `human-soul`, hive person, mantle, or minor god. A true manifested saint remains an individually judged and redeemed person even when appearing in company.

### 4. The saint whose legend becomes larger than the biography

George, Nicholas, the Seven Sleepers, Thekla, and several healer traditions demonstrate that the later story may become more socially important than the recoverable earthly record.

World Egg can use this aggressively, provided the layers remain classified. A dragon-slaying cycle may preserve:

- a later manifested commission;
- a local hero absorbed into a saint's name;
- a performed allegory;
- a Menhir compact rewritten as Christian legend;
- an Idol Mask defeated by people invoking the saint;
- a Patron borrowing the icon;
- or pure folklore attached to a genuine martyr.

None of these possibilities is automatically the earthly biography of the saint.

### 5. The same devotional office carried by different people

The multiple Cosmas and Damian pairs are especially valuable for the World Egg. They provide a real traditional example of distinct saints bearing the same names and healer identity without becoming one soul.

This can inspire:

- two or three local saints later conflated by copyists;
- one feast covering several distinct healers;
- rival relics that are both genuine but belong to different people;
- a later office-persona that borrows all their signs;
- a counterfeit Mask exploiting the conflation;
- church investigators correctly separating souls while retaining a shared liturgical commemoration.

## World Egg Saint-Memory Classification

Every active saint claim must be classified before a story assigns agency.

| Reported phenomenon | Possible continuity | What would distinguish it |
|---|---|---|
| the saint personally speaks or acts under a bounded holy purpose | `human-soul` under heavenly commission | moral coherence, freedom from compulsion, bounded task, fruits consistent with the Lord, and no self-exalting ownership |
| a bone, garment, icon, lamp, or grave responds within narrow terms | object-anchor, residue, or separately commissioned saintly action | effect proves only its own behavior; the object contains no soul fragment |
| a scene, phrase, or gesture repeats without free moral response | `residue` | repetition, locality, inability to answer beyond the imprint |
| an office or shrine keeps selecting a recognizable public persona | `infrastructure-office` or legend-persona | continuity follows rite, custody, or institution rather than the dead person |
| a revered figure demands feeding, domination, flattery, or exclusive ownership | dependent Idol Mask, Archmask, or another hostile counterfeit | self-exaltation, predation, coercive recurrence, moral contradiction, Counter-Countenance evidence |
| a stable Egg White actor borrows saintly signs | Patron persona or other created actor | anchor bargain, limited jurisdiction, identity discontinuities, and no heavenly commission |
| witnesses or clergy manufacture testimony | fraud | documentary, bodily, financial, and custody contradictions |

One case may contain several columns: a genuine saint, a forged relic, a real local residue, and an Idol counterfeit can all gather around the same name. The human soul remains one person throughout.

## Historical Planting Priorities

### Redeemer generation: plant sideways

This period already has the densest sacred cast. Add no new universal apostle, second betrayer, substitute Theotokos, or secret thirteenth Companion.

Prefer:

- households that host or refuse travelers;
- guides, boat crews, guards, cooks, cloth workers, physicians, scribes, debtors, and grave workers;
- people healed who later live ordinary and morally mixed lives;
- people who misunderstand, depart, return, or serve briefly;
- local witnesses whose testimony becomes important only generations later;
- enemies or officials who perform one just act without thereby becoming converts or saints.

### Underground Centuries: strongest saint-history bed

This should become an ecology rather than a sequence of spectacular executions:

- prison correspondence;
- lists of captives and confiscated books;
- burial and body-recovery fraternities;
- food and medicine networks;
- catechumen companies;
- recanters, confessors, survivors, and families of martyrs;
- officials who enforce, mitigate, falsify, or quietly obstruct decrees;
- local feast origins and competing death dates;
- relic fraud arising beside genuine memory;
- communities arguing how to receive people who lapsed under torture.

### Legalization and Conversion: victory creates new temptations

Use Constantine, Helen, Basil, Macrina, Chrysostom, and Olympias as different questions rather than a single “Christian empire” template:

- what can a ruler lawfully protect without governing doctrine?
- what happens to former cult workers and confiscated property?
- how does a persecuted church behave when it receives buildings, money, courts, and coercive access?
- who resists a Christian ruler's injustice?
- can a genuine saintly ruler leave wicked policy behind?
- can household and charitable institutions outlast a dynasty?
- what happens when relic discovery becomes politically useful?

### Decadence and Long Frontier: sanctity beyond the capital

The strongest grammars are:

- displaced teachers and bilingual hymnographers;
- wandering physicians and unmercenary clinics;
- famine houses and burial crews serving across confessional lines;
- married artisan missionaries;
- reluctant elders who refuse central office;
- local women founding durable communities through household work;
- bishops, deaconesses, and patrons exiled by fellow Christians;
- translation disputes that preserve several valid cultural expressions without making every doctrine relative.

### Later ages: remember, divide, translate, and appropriate

Post-schism history should inherit the same early saint differently:

- different feast emphasis;
- rival biographies and miracle collections;
- relic partitions and translations;
- Eclessian juridical findings versus Thalmyric popular-liturgical recognition;
- separate iconographic types;
- military, mercantile, medical, household, or monastic appropriations;
- Freeflame rejection of invocation while retaining intense exemplary memory.

Neither church's later reception retroactively changes the saint's human soul or earthly biography.

## Strongest New Research Beds

The following are the highest-yield additions for later development. They are not yet seed cards.

### 1. The prison-letter road

A condemned regional leader's letters survive because ordinary congregations, guards, widows, merchants, and rival copyists carry them. Later churches disagree about which recension is authentic and whether the writer sought death too eagerly.

### 2. The widows who forced an office into existence

A language or ration dispute exposes that a holy movement has neglected part of its own poor. The complainants—not the eventual officeholders—are the historical founders of the reform.

### 3. The plague bishop and the unbelievers' graves

A disputed leader organizes care and burial for opponents as well as believers. Later chronicles preserve the service but disagree fiercely about his other policies and the treatment of recanters.

### 4. The household that became a city of mercy

A woman's estate, freed dependants, workshops, and kin network slowly become a hospital, guesthouse, school, or common-rule house. Later famous men are trained there, but the institution's founding memory remains contested.

### 5. The saint with three biographies

One genuine martyr acquires a court Life, a local beast-slaying cycle, and a military apparition tradition. Each layer leaves a real trace, but only one belongs to the earthly life and perhaps one to a later heavenly commission.

### 6. The several healers with one feast

Distinct physicians in different centuries are merged by names, icons, and copied miracles. The churches must decide whether to separate relics, retain a shared office, and expose a predatory counterfeit using the composite face.

### 7. The converted bandit and the unforgotten victims

A violent person becomes genuinely holy without regaining a clean public reputation. Victims' descendants, monastic disciples, secular courts, and later miracle stories preserve incompatible but partly true memories.

### 8. The deaconess in the losing faction

A wealthy woman builds shelters and supports exiles, then is prosecuted by Christians after her bishop loses an institutional struggle. Her sanctity cannot be reduced to loyalty to him, and his righteousness cannot be proven solely by her persecution.

### 9. The language that saved the famine house

A poet or translator refuses high office but uses vernacular hymns and public speech to shame wealthy storehouses into feeding a displaced city. Later elite translations remove the verses naming the donors who refused.

### 10. The sleepers' outdated coin

A dormant-return legend is attached to a company whose actual historical core may have been sealed records, hidden descendants, or rediscovered bodies. The old coin remains a trace without requiring literal sleep or explaining a Weird.

## Saint-Dossier Fields

Any developed World Egg saint or saint-claim should add these fields to the ordinary [[Historical Seed Card Template]]:

- **Earthly person:** name, culture, livelihood, relationships, sins, virtues, and ordinary competencies.
- **Earliest witness:** contemporary document, near-contemporary account, oral memory, later Life, or unknown.
- **Death and judgement boundary:** what is historically known; what is claimed; whether authorial judgement is fixed.
- **Recognition history:** local memory, feast, synod, court, opposition, and date range.
- **Liturgical identity:** title, hymns, iconographic signs, and theological function.
- **Relic chain:** body, partitions, translations, thefts, substitutions, custody gaps, and tests.
- **Wonder strata:** during life, near death, at grave, through relic, later apparition, and folklore.
- **Political uses:** dynasty, city, guild, army, hospital, monastery, faction, or reform movement.
- **Continuity classification:** `human-soul`, object-anchor, `residue`, `infrastructure-office`, legend-persona, Patron persona, dependent Mask, Archmask, or fraud.
- **Competing receptions:** Eclessian, Thalmyric, Freeflame, local secular, hostile, and family memory.
- **Bounded proof:** exactly what any authentic miracle or manifestation demonstrates.
- **Unresolved remainder:** what later tradition may continue debating without authorial answer.

## Transformation Formula

For post-Redeemer material, the whole-history formula becomes:

> **New Testament moral pattern + early-saint reception grammar + culture-specific historical pressure + World Egg metaphysical consequence + contested later memory = world-native post-Redeemer seed**

Not every seed requires a named saint analogue. Often the best use is institutional:

- a feast born from burial work;
- a hospital founded by household wealth;
- a letter route sustained by prisoners and widows;
- a translation school formed by displacement;
- a relic court created because several sincere traditions conflict;
- a local saint cult whose genuine human center must be separated from later Mask accretion.

Originality threshold:

1. change at least three major axes from any one source;
2. never preserve source culture, office, signature wonder, persecution, and ending together;
3. use at least one ordinary historical pressure independent of the supernatural claim;
4. retain a meaningful human story if every reported wonder is false;
5. if a wonder is true, classify its continuity and bound its proof;
6. never use hagiographic uncertainty as permission to mock the tradition or flatten it into fraud.

## Theological and Historical Constraints

1. **One Lord:** sainthood is creaturely participation by grace, never lesser divinity or apotheosis.
2. **Unique Redeemer:** no saint repeats, completes, replaces, or rivals the Redeemer's saving work.
3. **One human soul:** the earthly saint and true manifested saint are the same redeemed `human-soul`; legends and offices do not divide or duplicate it.
4. **Recognition, not manufacture:** Eclessian canonization and Thalmyric glorification may recognize or misidentify; neither creates heavenly fact.
5. **Martyrdom:** death for the faith is witness, not a coercive sacrament that forces judgement.
6. **Collective commemoration:** one feast or title may honor many distinct persons without creating a corporate soul.
7. **Relics:** bodily continuity and holiness matter, but a relic is not a soul fragment, summoning component, or endlessly rechargeable device.
8. **Manifestation:** a saint appears only under heavenly commission and cannot be bound, inherited, deployed, or compelled.
9. **Miracle scope:** a miracle validates its actual commission, never an entire biography, dynasty, institution, war, or relic market.
10. **Repentance:** forgiveness does not erase victims, restitution, legal consequence, or surviving predatory continuities.
11. **Institutional fallibility:** a holy Church and genuine saint can coexist with corrupt bishops, unjust councils, political defeat, mistaken popular memory, and forged documents.
12. **Idol counterfeit:** popular devotion may supply a Mask with imagery and feeding architecture; it does not create the Idol's original guilty Hunger.
13. **Menhir distinction:** a local natural correspondence later placed under a saint's name is not thereby the saint, an angel, or an Idol.
14. **Illness and disability:** possession, sin, disease, trauma, and disability are never interchangeable diagnoses.
15. **Pre-schism reception:** common early saints may be received differently after the Split without becoming different souls.
16. **No supersessionist people-mapping:** no World Egg culture stands in for Jews, Greeks, Romans, Samaritans, “pagans,” or an entire modern church.
17. **No Weird explanation:** sleeper, relic, apparition, or apocalyptic material may approach but never solve a registered Weird without explicit authorization.
