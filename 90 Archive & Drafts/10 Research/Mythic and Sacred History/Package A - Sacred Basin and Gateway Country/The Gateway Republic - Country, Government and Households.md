# The Gateway Republic — Country, Government and Households

## Qarzeth and its country

Qarzeth stood above a deep harbour with water basins cut into the lower rock and store districts climbing the defensible rise behind them. The capital grew wealthy by bringing cultivated inland country into contact with sea routes. Its shipyards consumed timber, fibre, pitch and metal; its inhabitants consumed grain, oil, fruit, fish and animals supplied by neighbouring communities.

The city's dominion contained several kinds of dependant. Belqara owed grain after conquest. Tammur supplied pilots under a negotiated treaty. Esharat paid road and market dues while retaining influential elders. Sarqen maintained repair beaches and shelter for ships. Overseas Oresh supplied revenue, soldiers and metals through relationships established by the Ordan commanders.

These communities acquired real benefits from the association and bore unequal costs. Convoys reduced piracy, common measures eased exchange and Qarzeth could avenge an attack upon a smaller harbour. The capital could also redirect food, conscript crews and begin wars which exposed its dependants first.

## Civic institutions

Two Harbour Judges served at the head of public government. They received foreign envoys, supervised financial decisions and placed measures before the assembly and Council of Houses. Their terms limited formal office. Wealth, family clients and religious patronage allowed some judges to remain influential for decades.

The Council of Houses represented established property and cult interests. Its membership changed as new families bought land, financed fleets or entered through marriage. The citizen assembly elected magistrates and approved major wars, taxes and settlements. Its recognition rules excluded much of the adult population. Citizens living far from Qarzeth also found participation costly.

Military command existed beside civic office. A commander received defined authority, troops and financial expectations. Overseas conquest allowed the Ordan household to build revenue and loyalty beyond the Council's immediate control. This tension helped the Republic recover from defeat and made its government dependent upon a family it could not easily dismiss.

## Households, women and inheritance

Households owned land, ships, workshops, debts and enslaved people. Marriage joined commercial networks and could settle a political rivalry for a generation. Elite women managed estates and credit, inherited property and funded sanctuaries. Sapha of Hadrath's marriage to Meleq Ordan brought cavalry, grain and a disputed border claim into the family's power.

Women outside elite houses worked in fields, markets, fishing, sailmaking, pottery, food preparation and household service. Widows often continued a spouse's workshop or carrying agreement. Their legal independence varied by town. Esharat recognised several maternal inheritance customs which Qarzeth judges treated as provincial exceptions.

Children linked households across time and served as hostages, heirs, apprentices and workers. This gave the capital's sacrificial collections their particular horror. Officials took people whose lives carried family affection, labour, expectation and inheritance. Opposition grew through those relationships.

## Cultivation and work

The Gateway country cultivated grain, olives or their local equivalent, vines, legumes, orchard fruit and fodder according to water and soil. Herding districts supplied animals, wool and transport. Irrigation existed on a smaller and more dispersed scale than Qahur's basin works. Wells and seasonal channels made local knowledge decisive.

Shipbuilding joined specialised work from several communities. Sarqen carpenters shaped hull timbers. Qarzeth smiths and rope houses supplied fittings and rigging. Pilots from Tammur understood reefs and currents. Enslaved labourers performed dangerous hauling, quarrying and warehouse work beside paid specialists.

Markets offered imported cloth, metalwork, perfumes, carved goods and foods. Returning sailors brought songs and jokes as readily as cargo. Families marked a safe return with shared meals; creditors marked it by presenting accounts before the celebration had ended.

## Military composition

Gateway armies combined citizens, subject levies, allies, paid companies and troops serving particular commanders. Citizen status therefore cannot be inferred from service. A Belqara farmer compelled to march and an Oreshi officer rewarded with land belonged to different political relationships.

Fleets required rowers, sailors, marines, pilots, shipwrights and shore crews. War losses extended beyond battle. Storm, contaminated water, disease and delayed pay could destroy a force. Replacing ships increased demands upon timber country and workshops.

The Republic recruited effectively because its commanders understood difference. Zamaret Ordan addressed contingents through their own officers and made separate promises to allied towns. Those arrangements also limited her power. A force assembled against Caleran could disintegrate when pay failed or a local objective had been achieved.

## Religion beyond the Provider

Gateway households honoured ancestors, shore guardians, springs, winds and patrons of craft and travel. Town cults preserved older coastal traditions alongside beliefs brought by proto-Ahdari and maritime settlers. Priests and singers arranged some of these powers into shared family stories. Similar images did not guarantee a single supernatural person.

The Bronze-Faced Provider became powerful because Qarzeth's public life gave its sanctuary land, officials and an audience. Several towns rejected human sacrifice while retaining other shared rites. Opponents could therefore resist the Provider without abandoning every Gateway tradition.

Shore Menhir relationships concerned weather, passage, catch and boundary. Their gifts carried particular prices. They neither required the child collections nor authorised the Republic. Patron visitors and fraudulent ancestors sometimes claimed responsibility for profitable voyages. Their claims require independent evidence.

## Political character

The Republic produced capable sailors, cultivators, financiers, diplomats, builders and commanders. It created a civic world in which public argument could restrain one household and enrich another. It also maintained slavery, unequal subjecthood and an institution that murdered children under civic protection.

Its destruction ended the capital and its government. The country's people, work and local institutions survived in altered form. Their history cannot be reduced to either Qarzeth's achievements or its crimes.
