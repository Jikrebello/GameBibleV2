# The Three Gateway Wars and the Rope Camp Revolt

## The first contest for Tammur, c. 650–625 BR

Tammur stood across a narrow reach from ports tied to the Atherian leagues. Its pilots knew the reef passages by changes in colour and the flight of shore birds. Qarzeth stationed a garrison there and required harbour service. A dispute over the seizure of three local vessels divided the town. The faction led by **Neris Tal**, an owner of pilot boats, expelled the garrison and appealed to Delkos. The rival **House of Suthar** asked Qarzeth to restore order.

Harbour Judge Qelmar Suthar expected a punitive voyage. Caleran senator Aster Vey persuaded the Compact to defend the Tammur faction, arguing that Qarzeth would otherwise command the inner passage. A local quarrel became a war as both powers occupied neighbouring anchorages and called upon allies.

Qarzeth began with more experienced squadrons. Atherian cities responded by building and commandeering ships. Their first crews suffered badly in storms and close fighting. A wreck season in 641 BR killed thousands on both sides and filled southern beaches with timber. Salvage crews became wealthy; households waited for people whose bodies never returned.

Caleran eventually sustained more ships because the Aurin basin and allied ports replaced losses. Its advantage depended upon grain, pitch, rope and money drawn from communities far from the fighting. Qarzeth raided supply depots and attempted to detach Atherian ports through separate settlements. A failed relief expedition in 626 left the capital unable to retain Tammur.

The peace of 625 transferred Tammur and two anchorages, limited Qarzeth's fleet and imposed payment. Caleran celebrated Aster's command with a captured prow in the civic square. Tammur's assembly gained release from its old garrison and acquired an Atherian levy. Neris Tal died before the settlement; his children disputed whether he had freed their town or exchanged one master for another.

## The Rope Camp revolt, c. 625–619 BR

The army returning to Qarzeth contained paid companies, citizen troops, allied contingents and levies from dependent towns. They gathered at the Rope Camp outside the walls. The Council offered part of the promised pay and planned to recover the indemnity through new collections from Belqara and other agricultural districts.

Damen, elected spokesman by several paid companies, demanded the full amount and safe discharge. Yasra of Belqara joined him after collectors seized seed grain and hostages. Their coalition wanted incompatible outcomes. Soldiers sought pay; cultivators wanted the collections ended; subject towns wanted autonomy; some captains wanted possession of Qarzeth.

Council agents seized a negotiating party. Damen's followers tortured and killed captured officials and returned the bodies to the gate. Government commanders blocked food entering the camp and crucified rebel prisoners along the road. Yasra's people attacked loyalist estates, burned stores and killed households accused of assisting the capital. The violence destroyed trust within the coalition as well as between the rebels and Qarzeth.

Meleq Ordan secured food and cavalry from King Harzun of Hadrath. He promised disputed border land and married Harzun's daughter Sapha. Meleq paid two local contingents, allowed them to go home and persuaded an allied commander to change sides. He then enclosed the remaining camp and starved it.

Damen died during the attempted breakout. Yasra surrendered after Meleq promised to spare Belqara. She was executed when she refused to name the households which had hidden rebel families. Meleq reduced the district's collection and repaired its stores. He also sold captured soldiers and their dependants to meet part of Qarzeth's debt.

The revolt saved the Republic from immediate dissolution and destroyed its faith in ordinary command. The Ordan household emerged with an army, foreign marriage and the argument that only extraordinary authority could protect civic government.

## The Oresh command, c. 619–600 BR

The Council granted Meleq authority over Oresh, an overseas district of coastal towns, metal routes and cultivated ground. The commission gave him freedom to make local agreements and raise revenue. Oresh already had rulers, assemblies and landholding families. Some accepted Qarzeth's protection against neighbours; others fought.

Meleq established veteran settlements, took captives and confirmed local estates whose owners supplied troops. He cultivated religious and marriage ties through Sapha's Hadrathi family. Qarzeth received revenue. Oresh gained protected shipping and lost control over important land and appointments.

Zamaret Ordan succeeded to the command after serving as interpreter, supply organiser and field officer. The Council preferred an experienced member of the household to a stranger unable to command its followers. Her appointment strengthened Qarzeth and demonstrated how far republican need had made one family hereditary in practice.

## Zamaret's invasion, c. 600–580 BR

Zamaret intended to break Caleran's alliance system. She promised lighter service to defecting towns and restoration of local exiles. Her fleet carried the army to a friendly landing. Pack trains moved grain through the first upland stages; agreements with allied communities provided later supplies.

The early campaign succeeded. Zamaret defeated one Atherian force near the coast and destroyed another at the Red Fords. Her troops trapped the second army away from dependable water and cut its retreat. Thousands died or surrendered. She released selected allied prisoners without ransom and sent them home with offers to their cities.

The victories created fear and admiration without producing the political collapse she needed. Some Atherian allies changed sides. Others remained because their local rivals had joined Zamaret or because their walls and fleets gave them reason to expect relief. Caleran continued to collect soldiers and food from the core alliance.

Celor Marath's delaying command refused a large battle. His troops shadowed the invasion, attacked foragers and burned grain ahead of it. The policy made Zamaret's supply harder and exposed farms which Caleran claimed to protect. At home, Celor's opponents accused him of cowardice; displaced allies accused him of preserving the army at their expense.

Dema Varin carried a separate war to Oresh. Local houses which had lost land under the Ordans supplied guides. Atherian forces captured ports, freed some political prisoners and enslaved inhabitants of resistant settlements. Loss of Oresh reduced Zamaret's replacements and money.

The final campaign crossed into the Gateway homeland. Hadrath, now divided between heirs who disputed Sapha's marriage settlement, joined Caleran in exchange for border districts. Zamaret returned to defend Qarzeth and accepted peace after defeat outside Belqara.

The settlement restricted the Republic's fleet and foreign policy. Zamaret later used the Harbour Judgeship to recover public money from houses which had profited during the war. The Council exiled her. She died at an eastern court around 566 BR after refusing Caleran's demand that she be surrendered.

## The final war, c. 525–520 BR

Five decades of recovery restored Qarzeth's trade and agriculture. Hadrathi kings meanwhile occupied disputed farms with Caleran's approval. When Qarzeth armed a force against the latest encroachment, Caleran treated the act as a breach of the settlement.

The senate divided over policy. Orsen Valeor demanded the destruction of the capital and made escaped children and temple testimony central to his cause. Mera Castren argued that disarmament, inspectors and hostages could contain the Republic. Both recognised strategic danger. Valeor's coalition included sincere opponents of sacrifice, creditors, land-seekers and commanders who expected office.

Initial negotiations obtained weapons and hostages. Caleran then ordered Qarzeth's inhabitants to abandon their city and settle inland. The demand united priests, opponents of sacrifice, poor residents and elite houses which had little else in common. They manufactured weapons and resisted.

The siege depended upon Belqara grain, Esharat carriers, Sarqen repair work and the blockade maintained by Atherian fleets. Shortage killed civilians before the breach. Gateway sorties burned siege engines and captured workers. Caleran commanders executed prisoners after one attack; Qarzeth officials killed suspected informers within the city.

The assault of 520 opened the holding chambers and destroyed the sacrificial establishment. It also produced murder, rape, enslavement and organised looting. The subsequent salting of Qarzeth required cargo space, labour and guards. It was a public policy carried out after the military victory.

## Military consequences

The wars taught Caleran how to build fleets, sustain distant armies and convert allied obligations into long commands. Contractors, commanders and veteran patrons acquired wealth. Captives entered Atherian estates. Allied ports retained grievances over levies imposed for victories celebrated at Caleran.

The Gateway Republic learned to combine forces from many political relationships and failed to reconcile those relationships after defeat. Zamaret's battlefield success could not replace an alliance system because a town's loyalty depended upon neighbours, supplies and confidence in the future.

Qarzeth's destruction ended one rival capital. It did not make the Gateway people disappear. The spared towns, their fields and their memories produced the kingdom which later entered the Atherian succession wars.
