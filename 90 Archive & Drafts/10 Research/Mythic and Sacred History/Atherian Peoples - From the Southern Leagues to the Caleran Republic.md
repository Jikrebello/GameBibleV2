# Atherian Peoples — From the Southern Leagues to the Caleran Republic

> [!note] Author-only development record
> The approved identities and belonging rules were promoted through [[Ancient Peoples before Atherian Unification - Canon Integration Review]]. This dossier preserves the development reasoning.

## AP-01 — The southern war-settlement leagues

The southern leagues formed among households that left the region of the Glass after the Prophet's death. Their members possessed the shared language, worship, measures and memories of the One Glass People. They did not leave as a single nation. A league commonly took its public identity from a captain, a destination, a remembered camp or the work around which its households had assembled.

The broad expressions translated as **outward households** and **southern leagues** describe this phase without pretending that every migrant used one endonym. Membership depended on acknowledged household ties and participation in the movement. Marriage, adoption and attachment to a captain could bring a person in. Captives and enslaved inhabitants could live inside a league without receiving equal standing. Some local allies entered through sworn agreements; others regarded the settlers as occupiers and retained their own communities.

Commanders organised defence, provisioners controlled shared stores, craftspeople maintained equipment and teachers preserved the sacred account of liberation. None inherited the Prophet's commission. Conflict arose when one office claimed resources needed by another. Settlement changed these offices because a store meant for a journey became a granary, an expeditionary captain became a landholder and a temporary fort claimed surrounding fields.

By about 1900 BR, the leagues had ceased to be one coherent social form. Some had become garrison households, river communities or harbour societies. Others had dissolved into older populations. Their Ark-derived ancestry remained important, but later claims that every Atherian descended through an honoured southern captain concealed adoption, enslavement and local survival.

**Identity ruling:** Ark-derived in principal descent and rapidly mixed in lived population; a migratory political form rather than an ethnicity with one self-name. At 350 BR it survived as ancestry, ritual memory and founder claims, not as a living league identity.

## AP-02 — Maritime Atherians

Between approximately 1900 and 1400 BR, ship-owning households, harbour residents and armed fleets created a recognisable maritime Atherian culture. A prominent household might possess a vessel, marriage ties to a timber district, relatives in a store town and dependants drawn from several shores. Captains moved between trade, raiding, escort and settlement. Their followers judged leadership by the division of food and proceeds as well as courage at sea.

The name **Atherian** is proposed to have acquired broad cultural use in this world. It identified related speech, remembered descent from the southern leagues and a growing body of maritime stories and rites. It did not confer citizenship in a common state. People normally gave a harbour, island or household when asked where they belonged. Foreign merchants used the wider name because it made a complex coast easier to describe.

Local Orphaned populations supplied pilots, cultivators, craftspeople and spouses. Captives rowed ships and worked yards. Freed people could found households whose children spoke Atherian and observed harbour customs while remembering another ancestry. A maritime household therefore transmitted belonging through upbringing, work, marriage and patronage as well as descent. Owners often represented this mixed society through genealogies of heroic captains.

Religious life preserved the Lord and the Prophet within increasingly local stories. Voyagers also bargained with shore powers, adopted inland rites and gave familiar names to manifestations encountered at sea. These practices contributed to a shared imaginative world without proving that every harbour dealt with the same supernatural being.

**Identity ruling:** an Ark-descended and extensively mixed cultural family. It was recognised by speech, household practice and maritime participation rather than one government. By 350 BR it remained visible in port customs and elite ancestry, although city and civic identities had become more precise.

## AP-03 — Atherian city peoples and Caleran republican belonging

The cities which arose between approximately 1400 and 800 BR converted residence into more formal kinds of belonging. Citizens received access to assemblies, landholding, public protection and recognised cults according to local rules. Other free residents could work, marry and worship in the city without possessing those rights. Subject villagers, foreigners, resident allies, freed people and enslaved inhabitants occupied further positions.

People called themselves by their city before they invoked a general Atherian identity. A Caleran, Ortheian, Pyrassan or Irenai citizen belonged to a distinct political community. Shared language and festival traditions made those cities intelligible to one another but did not join their treasuries or armies. The Celestial Compact formalised obligations among twelve cities while leaving many Atherian-speaking settlements outside it.

Caleran's republic developed a particularly influential civic vocabulary. **Caleran citizen** described a recognised legal membership. **Compact ally** described a relationship between political communities. **subject**, **protected town**, **military dependant** and **resident foreigner** carried different obligations. Later writers often called all these populations Atherian because they served an Atherian power. The distinction mattered to people who paid taxes or supplied soldiers without voting on the campaign.

Admission occurred through birth to recognised parents, adoption, marriage arrangements, manumission, military service or a public grant. The route varied by city and period. A wealthy foreign household could receive citizenship while a locally born labourer remained excluded. Families celebrated admission because it changed inheritance and public standing; neighbours could resent the privilege or challenge the genealogy used to obtain it.

By 350 BR, **Atherian** had three established meanings: participation in a broad language and culture, membership in an Atherian city, and political inclusion within the Caleran-led republic. The meanings overlapped but remained separable. The new imperial settlement inherited all three.

**Identity ruling:** a civic and cultural field rather than one biological people. Caleran republican status must always be distinguished from Atherian speech or ancestry.

## AP-04 — The Enathi

The Enathi developed along the Aurin waterways and cultivated lowlands from Orphaned communities surviving Arsuvan. Their self-name was regional and practical. It joined villages that shared related speech, river customs, marriage connections and dependence on boats and maintained channels. An Enathi usually identified a home landing or kin group as readily as the broader people.

Households held fields, animals, boats and obligations to shared bank work. Marriage commonly joined cultivators to carrying families. Adoption brought children and adults into a household, especially after flood, war or enslavement had broken another family. Captives could be sold or retained as servants; participation in river work did not itself make an enslaved person Enathi. Release, marriage and acknowledgement by local kin could give descendants fuller belonging.

The arrival of southern settlers created several political outcomes. Some Enathi communities allied with a fort against local rivals. Some lost land and freedom. Others supplied boats and food on terms that gave their leading families influence in the new settlement. A family might adopt Atherian speech for trade and later acquire Caleran citizenship while preserving Enathi marriages, river offerings and ancestor meals.

By 350 BR, Enathi identity remained meaningful in lowland districts and household ancestry. It did not denote an independent kingdom. Urban descendants could call themselves Atherian and Caleran without repudiating Enathi kin. Republican land claims sometimes rewarded the Atherian civic part of that identity while ignoring the local title from which the property had come.

**Identity ruling:** a continuing Orphaned river people incorporated unevenly into Atherian society. Cultural and civic Atherianisation did not convert their ancestry into Glass descent.

## AP-05 — The Serathi

The Serathi were the people of the Aurin hill towns and older settled districts between river and upland. Their common identity rested on related speech, intermarrying ancestor houses, craft traditions and teachers who travelled among competing cities. Thesra became an influential centre without becoming the capital of every Serathi community.

An ancestor house included recognised kin, dependants, workshops, tombs and the obligations attached to them. Marriage could move land, tools and ritual responsibility between towns. Apprenticeship brought outsiders into a household's work. Adoption supplied heirs or protected displaced children. Enslaved people remained under an owner's power even when their skills sustained the house's reputation.

Serathi belonging consequently had several degrees. A person could be born into an acknowledged house, married into one, adopted, attached as a dependant or held as property. Town recognition and participation in festivals gave public form to these relationships. Rival houses sometimes declared opponents foreign in order to seize workshops or burial land, even when the condemned family shared the same regional ancestry.

Atherian rulers valued Serathi pottery, metal fittings, learning and marriage connections. Some houses entered Caleran's public cult and citizenship while retaining their tombs and teaching lineages. Others were dispossessed or withdrew to towns beyond direct republican control. Serathi customs entered Atherian culture through people whose names were later omitted from founder genealogies.

**Identity ruling:** a continuing Orphaned hill-city people with strong household and urban forms. At 350 BR Serathi identity survived inside and beside the Caleran republic.

## AP-06 — The Vardeni

The Vardeni inhabited the Aurin uplands, including permanent valley farms, seasonal pastures, mines and the routes connecting them. Their self-name covered communities whose livelihoods differed but whose marriages, assemblies and exchange made them recognisable to one another. Olveth served as an important meeting place rather than a universal capital.

Belonging usually began in a valley household. Seasonal movement joined relatives who farmed with those who herded animals or carried ore. A leader required the support of households able to supply food, pack stock, metalworkers and fighters. Agreements about pasture and winter stores mattered as much as descent when a displaced family sought admission.

Lowland rulers recruited Vardeni soldiers and drivers. Wages and military grants allowed some families to acquire property below the hills. Other rulers tried to keep the relatives of recruits dependent on royal stores. Captives entered mines and estates under coercion. A Vardeni name could therefore mark an upland origin while the bearer served an Atherian commander or lived in a Caleran ward.

The spread of the Common Sky encountered older land, sun and hunting rites. Vardeni households adopted, translated or resisted different parts of the settlement. Public Atherian worship did not remove local relationships with place or the memory of Giant solar tyranny.

**Identity ruling:** a continuing Orphaned upland people. At 350 BR Vardeni communities ranged from relatively autonomous valleys to households deeply incorporated into Atherian military and commercial life.

## AP-07 — Arkenan and Saronikan communities

The names **Arkena** and **Saronika** are later geographic conveniences for an ancient eastern maritime region. Its inhabitants more commonly named Verion, Pyrassa, Irenai, Delkos, Kyrame, Naverion, Erenost or a smaller island and harbour. Their populations combined older Orphaned shore communities, maritime Atherian settlers, freed captives, enslaved workers and families drawn by craft and trade.

These communities shared Atherian speech to different degrees. Some older households used another language at home for generations. Civic status belonged to a particular port. An Irenai teacher, a Pyrassan store owner and an Erenost shipwright could recognise a common maritime culture while owing no political loyalty to one another.

Water and food shaped identity. Island households depended on cisterns, terraces and imported grain. Shipbuilding linked them to timber and metal districts. A city which maintained stores through a failed harvest acquired pride distinct from a harbour known for repair or teaching. Festivals, schools and rivalries turned material specialisation into local character.

Valeron's hegemony, Eryndor's expedition, the Delkosi Successor state and the Gateway Wars placed these cities under changing demands. A citizen might serve a local assembly, an upland king, a Delkosi fleet and a Caleran commander within one lifetime. Political service did not automatically alter ancestry or civic origin. Military settlements and marriages nevertheless produced new households whose children inherited several loyalties.

By 350 BR, these ports belonged to the Atherian cultural world and were entangled with Caleran power. **Arkenan** and **Saronikan** should be used as regional groupings in the encyclopaedia, followed by the ancient city actually concerned. Neither term describes a single ancient ethnicity or state.

**Identity ruling:** several early Braided and Atherianised maritime populations with durable civic identities. Regional labels clarify geography; city membership remains the principal ancient public identity.

## Atherian belonging at 350 BR

The following combinations were all possible at the imperial foundation:

- an Enathi-speaking river household with Caleran citizenship and Common Sky worship;
- a Serathi ancestor house whose members used Atherian in assemblies but retained a hill-city marriage circle;
- a Vardeni military family holding republican land while maintaining upland pasture ties;
- an islander culturally Atherian but citizen of a port outside the Celestial Compact;
- an Ortheian who spoke an Atherian language and had served a royal enemy of Caleran;
- a freed household whose Atherian identity came from residence, speech and civic admission rather than Glass ancestry;
- an enslaved Atherian-speaking person denied civic membership by an Atherian owner.

These cases should anchor the canon treatment. Atherian identity became powerful because it could extend beyond ancestry. Its expansion remained unequal because governments controlled which forms of belonging carried land, office and protection.
