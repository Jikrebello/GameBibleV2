# Vathra and the Tarkhan Orphaned World — The Walking Court and the Great Unyoking

## Purpose

The strongest existing answer to the Tarkhan Giant-age question is **Vathra, the Walking Court**. Vathra already occupies the correct geography and possesses the right political form: a mobile Giant crown whose court, herds, workshops, shrines and records travelled together. It exchanged cavalry, beasts, captives, brides and mercenaries with the Five Imperial Horizons while retaining a political identity of its own.

The Tarkhan did not descend from Vathra's royal blood. Their earliest ancestors were the human populations who lived within, beside and beyond its moving dominion. Some served as herders, drivers, scouts, hunters, smiths, foster-kin, tribute collectors and captive labourers. Others avoided the court, traded with it or fought it. Vathra's fall released these populations into a landscape whose roads, wells and grazing circuits had been organised for Giant appetites. Their survival produced the Orphaned societies from which the Tarkhan later developed.

This account preserves the decisive gap in Tarkhan history. Vathra knew mounted warfare, but the early Tarkhan did not inherit an intact horse empire. The court's specialised herds, breeding grounds and command system were destroyed during the Great Unbinding. Human survivors retained route knowledge, animal craft and stories of mounted rulers, then relied chiefly upon camels, small stock, carts, sledges and walking herds for many centuries. The First Catch after 905 AR therefore remains a genuine revolution.

## Comparative ground

Mobile societies form states through movement as readily as settled societies form them through walls. Pastoral economies follow water, winter shelter and grazing. Their rulers may draw revenue from tribute, raiding, protection, trade and relations with neighbouring agricultural states. The Eurasian evidence also warns against treating every mobile population as one tribe or every confederation as a permanent nation. Political unions expand, divide and recombine with changing leadership and access to external wealth.

Vathra draws from that grammar at Giant scale. Its court moves because its herds and subject districts cannot sustain it in one place. Mobility also serves domination. The court appears at a pasture, consumes tribute, takes hostages and moves before resistance can assemble. Its portable wealth includes animals, metalwork, textiles, weapons and people. Great burial mounds and occasional winter camps remain, but most of its architecture consists of roads, wells, trampled ground and abandoned corrals.

The Met's survey of Eurasian steppe cultures emphasises mobile herding, riding, portable animal art and elaborate funerary sacrifice, while UNESCO's Silk Roads materials stress exchange, ecological adaptation and the role of mobile peoples as intermediaries. Those patterns supply social and material structure. They do not establish that Vathra or the Tarkhan are copies of any one Turkic, Mongol, Scythian or other historical people.

## The Vathran royal line

### Recommended descent

The first Vathran sovereign should be a **Titan-Firstborn conceived through a bounded manifestation of Hecatara and a human woman**. The full Menhir sovereignty never enters a body or diminishes itself. The resulting being has `titan-continuity`, no human soul and an exceptional command of route and adjacency. Its early victories allow the court to carry workshops and herds across distances that ordinary caravans cannot cross in one season.

This ancestry suits Vathra better than a generic sky-father genealogy. Hecatara governs passage, thresholds and displaced distance. She does not grant empire, justice or ownership. Vathran kings later claim that every road belongs to them because their ancestor opened it. That claim is a political abuse of an actual inherited correspondence.

Later rulers are High and Dynastic Giants born from the Firstborn's descendants, intermarriage with neighbouring Giant houses and occasional unions with mortals. Their power becomes graduated through blood, while their continuity remains categorical. Giant-blooded humans possess complete human souls. A royal house may exaggerate a remote Giant ancestor without changing the nature of its human members.

Nevaura influences Vathran winter practice without becoming a second parent. Courts buy preservation, dormancy and rationing through local bargains. These agreements preserve seed, breeding animals or a winter camp by denying warmth and food elsewhere. Human officials make the selections and bear responsibility for the people excluded.

### Rival genealogies

Vathra's western houses claim descent from the First Road-Mother. Eastern cadets claim that the founding child was adopted after a victory and that their own ancestor supplied the royal beasts. Captive scribes produce genealogies joining both lines. These versions can coexist because fosterage, political marriage and seizure of a royal herd all create real forms of succession without changing metaphysical descent.

## A dominion made of moving rings

Vathran government travels in five broad rings.

1. **The axle household** contains the ruling Giant, close kin, body servants, hostages and the principal portable shrine.
2. **The great herds** contain draught beasts, cattle, sheep, camels and a limited stock of horses reserved for Giant retainers and selected human riders.
3. **The craft train** carries smiths, leatherworkers, wheelwrights, healers, cooks, singers and captive specialists.
4. **The dependent camps** supply labour, animals, children for fosterage and fighting men. Some move willingly because the court protects a profitable route; others are driven from one extraction season to the next.
5. **The outer peoples** include well towns, hunters, farmers and smaller herding communities who pay intermittent tribute or resist when the court is distant.

This arrangement allows a large polity without pretending that its entire population wanders in one caravan. Many subjects remain settled around wells, river remnants, salt pans and mountain approaches. The court returns to them according to season and political need.

## Work, wealth and cruelty

Vathra's wealth comes from animals, tolls and the ability to move scarce goods between ecological zones. Its caravans carry ore from the Copper Stairs, preserved food from Narash, timber from northern margins and luxury work from Giant courts. Vathran animal healers and breeders achieve genuine skill. Foster households join distant communities, and seasonal gatherings support marriage, music, wrestling, racing and trade.

The same system consumes people. Defeated households lose herds and children. Royal marriages treat women and men as guarantees between dynasties. Captive craftsmen travel under guard and may never see their home again. Failed tributaries are exposed during winter, sold to neighbouring crowns or driven before the court as living route-markers. Great rulers receive funerals in which servants, captives and valuable animals are killed beside the burial mound. These practices are political choices, not an inevitable expression of mobile life.

Human households resist through concealed animals, false herd counts, fosterage exchanges, flight and assassination. Some obtain office and profit from the system. The Tarkhan inheritance therefore includes ancestors who endured Vathra, ancestors who served it and ancestors who preyed upon their neighbours under its authority.

## The Great Unyoking, c. 2300–2260 BR

Vathra enters the Great Unbinding already divided. Several cadet houses want permanent winter territories. The ruling train attempts to preserve control by taking breeding animals, hostages and stored food from its dependent camps. At the same time, Hecatara routes fail or demand accumulated prices, and Nevauran preservation bargains begin withholding sustenance from the people whom officials marked as expendable.

Human drivers, herd women and captive smiths coordinate a revolt remembered as **the Great Unyoking**. They cut traces, release breeding stock, kill overseers and lead dependent camps away from the royal itinerary. Some Giant cadets arm human followers against the ruling house. Rival trains collide at wells which can no longer sustain them. Royal riders massacre several fleeing camps; rebels retaliate against Giant client households and collaborators.

The court's moving archive burns during the struggle. Its loss breaks tribute claims, foster genealogies and the schedule by which distant camps expected protection. A final royal procession attempts to use the ancestral road correspondence to escape the collapsing north. The route opens for the axle household and closes across the train behind it. Whether the first Titan remains bound beneath a road mound, escaped bodily into the Egg White or had already been divided among cadet houses remains deliberately open pending collision with the established ancient actor slots.

Vathra ceases to exist as a state. Its subject population does not disappear. Camps divide around surviving wells, herds and kin. Some communities become more settled because walls protect grain from raiders. Others move farther and more regularly because no court maintains the old winter stations. The earliest Tarkhan world grows from this uneven survival.

## From Vathran subjects to Tarkhan peoples

Four inheritances matter most.

- **Route knowledge:** survivors know grazing sequences, storm shelters and ruined roads. Later songs turn practical itineraries into the rides of ancestral heroes.
- **Foster kinship:** children raised across camps create relationships broader than immediate descent. This later supports confederation without requiring one common ancestor.
- **Portable authority:** oath-spears, witnessed gifts and seasonal assemblies survive because fixed royal buildings have failed.
- **Animal craft:** herding, veterinary practice, harness-making and cart repair continue after the specialised royal horse system collapses.

The western dry belt favours wells, camels and caravan exchange. The eastern cold country favours hunters, foot herders, sledges and carts. Local river remnants support settled communities. These groups remain related through movement and marriage, yet no single form of livelihood defines them all.

Early Ahdari and Jirahar contact occurs centuries later. Those encounters change trade and political language but do not create the Tarkhan. The post-905 First Catch then joins surviving animal knowledge to abundant remounts, new tack and confederated warfare. Later singers claim the Tarkhan merely recovered Vathra's horses. The material history shows a far more radical change.

## Surviving evidence and possible traditions

- road mounds containing Giant and human burials in sharply unequal arrangements;
- horse bones concentrated in royal sites, followed by a long regional scarcity;
- cart and harness designs persisting in camel and ox equipment;
- foster lineages shared by communities with otherwise incompatible genealogies;
- oath-spears whose metal came from different Giant horizons;
- a children's unyoking game in which the smallest player wins by releasing the team rather than seizing the cart;
- songs that remember moving tents as palaces and ordinary winter camps as conquered cities.

The most useful heroic cycle concerns a human driver who can guide the royal train but cannot command where it goes. The driver helps release the subject camps, then discovers that one beloved foster household chose to remain with a cadet Giant. Victory breaks the empire and also breaks the driver's family.

## Collision and continuity rulings

1. Vathra is the existing lesser Giant crown, not a new sixth Imperial Horizon.
2. Tarkhan are Orphaned humans, not descendants of Vathra's Giant dynasty as a people.
3. The Walking Court is unrelated to [[G-59 - The Walking Court and the Wall That Stayed]], a later Near-Glass human episode.
4. Vathra's limited horses do not anticipate the Grass-Tarkhan horse civilisation established by the First Catch.
5. Hecatara's manifestation creates one exceptional ancestor. It neither endorses Vathran rule nor becomes the ancestor of all Tarkhan.
6. Nevauran bargains affect particular winters and stores. They do not explain every famine or nomadic movement.
7. Any surviving Titan, bodily escaped Giant or copied ancestor must be reconciled with the five established ancient actor slots before promotion.

## Recommended canonical direction

Adopt Vathra as the principal Giant polity whose collapse shaped the Tarkhan ancestral world. Preserve several neighbouring populations and resistant communities so that Tarkhan ethnogenesis remains plural. Fix the Great Unyoking as a human-led destruction of Vathra's transport and tribute system during the Great Unbinding. Keep the founder's exact identity and final fate author-only until the ancient actor review is reopened.
