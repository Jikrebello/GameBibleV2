# Ark Humanity, Orphaned Remnants, and the Distant-Lands Gradient

> [!info] Atlas integration
> Current canonical destination: [[Peoples in the World Historical Atlas]]. This dossier retains its research history; former holds and promotion gates are superseded only where the [[World Historical Atlas - Integration and Coverage Ledger]] records an explicit outcome. Preliminary or rejected variants do not override their corrected descendants.

> [!note] Sequence superseded — 2026-08-31
> The Ark/Orphaned/Braided classification in this dossier remains controlling. Its older shorthand of a direct three-way post-Glass division is retained as research history but has been superseded by [[The Two Fissions - Canon Promotion Review]]: the southern proto-Atherian fission occurs first, and the Near-Glass continuum divides generations later.

## Research Question

Can the world's human geography be divided between:

1. peoples principally descended from the ordered Glass Ark; and
2. peoples principally descended from human communities that survived the Giant collapse outside the Prophet's host;

while allowing the familiar southern imperial world to become only one historical zone inside a much larger, harsher and frequently regressed world?

**Research ruling:** yes. The ancestry architecture and long Glass formation have been approved and reconciled into controlling canon. The proposal fits the Great Unbinding, supplies a strong basis for distant post-apocalyptic regions, and replaces the older assumption that nearly every known people was one branch of the Exodus.

This ruling is historical, institutional and genealogical—not a division between kinds of human soul. Every human possesses the complete Image-bearing `human-soul`. Ark descent does not confer superior blood, salvation or intelligence. Remnant descent does not imply rejection by the Lord. Civilizations, laws, religions and practices can nevertheless be judged as better or worse, more truthful or more corrupt, more technically capable or more materially impoverished.

# I. The Approved Human-Ancestry Architecture

## 1. The Glass-Ark Convergence

The principal Ark host should not enter the Glass as three proto-peoples. It enters as a mixed convergence drawn from many Giant horizons and becomes **one historical people during the long Glass sojourn**. The provisional author-only handles are **the Glass People**, **the People of the Camp**, or **the Prophet's People**; none is a promoted endonym.

Only after the Prophet's death and the southward failure does that people divide into three later civilizational families:

- **proto-Jirahar:** camp law, burial names, commandment, route memory and prophetic expectation;
- **proto-Atherians:** portable crafts, military organisation, record fragments and later appropriation of Giant knowledge;
- **proto-Ahl-e-Ahd:** oath, water distribution, guest law, camp contract and survival jurisprudence.

These are not three pure bloodlines and were not separate nations inside the Ark. They are three institutional crystallisations **out of one camp-formed people**. Later intermarriage, adoption, conquest, slavery, conversion and migration prevent any credible doctrine of biological Ark purity.

The Ark advantage is concrete:

- deliberately preserved seed, animals, tools and medicine;
- living artisans and specialists selected by circumstance rather than eugenics;
- burial, household and ration registers;
- shared memory of the Prophet and a minimum common law;
- bounded Glass routes and anchor camps;
- enough organised population to re-establish towns, markets and armies quickly.

The Glass refuge preserves and forms the host before the failed southern sanctuary fractures it. The Ark preserves a civilizational **starter package** and a real common people, not a permanently unified or collectively righteous nation. [[The Glass Sojourn]] now controls this interval; [[The Glass Sojourn - Refuge, Ethnogenesis, and the Last Witness]] retains its research basis.

## 2. The Orphaned-World Remnants

The Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and **Nsizwa** should instead descend principally from communities that never completed the Glass crossing. Their ancestors may include:

- subject villages missed by the gathering;
- revolt communities that rose after the columns passed;
- people who deliberately held a city, pass, storehouse or harbour;
- fugitives who escaped toward mountains, forests, islands, tundra or grasslands;
- slave-soldier bands and burial fraternities;
- inhabitants of abandoned client states;
- survivors protected temporarily by a Menhir bargain, Giant defector or failing machine;
- people born generations later inside the ruins of the orphaned Giant world.

They survive the direct consequences the Ark host escapes: collapsing irrigation, predatory war systems, Giant succession violence, escaped crafted creatures, Titan fragments, abandoned Idolic feeding architectures, hostile Patrons, severed roads and the loss of regional trade. Their later cultures are therefore products of **reassembly**, not simply migration from one camp.

This makes their oldest stories structurally different:

- liberation arrives as rumour, local revolt, vanished masters or inexplicable system failure;
- the Prophet may be remembered as a distant road-breaker, a storm, a treacherous deserter, several siblings or not at all;
- Giant machines become barrows, cursed wells, sky bones or ritual landscapes;
- technological knowledge survives as isolated procedure without explanatory theory;
- local heroes rebuild one food system, burial law or safe road rather than divide a common Exodus inheritance.

## 3. The Braided Peoples

The **Sestaran harbor populations** and **Vesari** should be treated as later Braided peoples rather than primary branches of the Ark or singular Orphaned remnants. Their formation requires different local mixtures of remnant populations, Ark-descended migrants, absorbed neighbours, captives, client communities and political confederations. “Sestaran” later becomes primarily civic and league language; Vesari identity becomes a river-world ethnogenesis rather than an untouched Exodus fragment.

The **Eclessians** are an even later and more visible Braided people. Existing canon already calls Eclessia a layered ethnogenesis: Atherian provincial populations join and rule among Folk substrates; Markish societies arise through frontier mixture; Vesari populations enter the Eclessian world through Virelia; later refugees, converts, conquerors and local populations add further layers. The creator-facing comparison to Celtic, Germanic and Italic mixtures is structural, not a one-to-one ancestry map.

Other cultures should not be assigned automatically. They may prove to be:

- principally Ark-descended;
- principally remnant-descended;
- the result of a later joining between an Ark colony and a remnant population;
- a confederation containing several remembered origins;
- a political identity that makes biological origin historically irrelevant.

At least several major peoples should be braided. Otherwise “Ark” and “remnant” will harden into a false racial binary that neither the metaphysics nor ordinary migration can support.

### Current author-only classification

| Class | Peoples presently assigned | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Ark-derived** | proto-Jirahar, proto-Ahl-e-Ahd, proto-Atherians | later fissions of the one people formed during the Glass sojourn |
| **Orphaned** | Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan, Nsizwa | principal descent from human communities surviving outside the Ark, without implying one common remnant origin |
| **Braided** | Sestaran harbor peoples, Vesari, later Eclessians | later ethnogenesis through the joining of Ark-derived, Orphaned and other local populations |
| **Open** | all unassigned peoples | requires its own historical audit rather than inference by geography or present culture |

# II. Collision with Current Canon

## Already aligned

[[The Great Unbinding]] already fixes that the Exodus is the largest deliberate convergence but not the only surviving humanity. It explicitly preserves successor cities, isolated enclaves, failing regions, islands and separate human histories. [[Age of Exodus]] repeats that outside communities are not rejected humanity.

The proposal therefore does not require changing the Unbinding's causal backbone, the Glass miracle or the Prophet's commission.

# III. The Civilizational Gradient

## Not one smooth circle

The desired tonal gradient works best as a broken field rather than a rule that every mile from the Redeemer produces equal decline.

Three historical distances interact:

1. **distance from the Ark package:** access to preserved crafts, records, livestock, seed and portable law;
2. **distance from surviving infrastructure:** whether irrigation, roads, mines, ports and specialist populations endured the Giant collapse;
3. **distance from later Atherian and Redeemer institutions:** contact with writing, hospitals, monasteries, sacramental teaching, trade, military organisation and imperial law.

This normally makes the Thalmyric–Eclessian–Covenant world feel recognisably medieval or late antique and many distant lands feel post-apocalyptic. It still allows exceptions:

- an isolated remnant city may preserve machinery beyond Atherian knowledge;
- a near-core valley may have fallen back to subsistence after plague and war;
- a distant monastery or converted kingdom may preserve doctrine better than a corrupt imperial capital;
- a technically poor people may possess excellent ecological, oral-legal or navigational knowledge;
- a highly advanced remote state may be an Idolic prison rather than a desirable civilisation.

## Equal Image, unequal civilisations

The setting does not need moral relativism. Practices can be judged plainly:

- human sacrifice is evil;
- chattel slavery and hereditary dehumanisation are evil;
- ritual sexual coercion and forced marriage are evil;
- exposure of unwanted children, captive mutilation and cannibal cult are evil;
- destroying such institutions can be a genuine good.

But those judgements attach to acts, laws, rulers and institutions—not to a biological category of people. The world can state that one civilisation possesses better law, truer worship, safer medicine, greater literacy or more humane institutions than another without claiming that its every conquest is just or its people possess more human worth.

The strongest formula is:

> **one human nature and one objective moral law; radically unequal inheritance, knowledge, institutions and obedience.**

## What technological regression actually looks like

“Stone Age” and “Iron Age” should be disaggregated into things people can see:

| Capacity | Post-collapse expression |
|---|---|
| metallurgy | salvaged Giant metal cannot be reproduced; bloomery iron exists only in two valleys; copper ritual objects outlast iron tools |
| food | seed diversity collapses; irrigation fails; people return to mobile foraging, pastoralism or swidden systems |
| building | cyclopean ruins remain, but lime, cranes, arches or standard brick are lost |
| writing | one priestly or mortuary register survives while ordinary administration becomes oral |
| transport | old roads exist without bridges, beasts, wheels or safe jurisdiction |
| medicine | practical plant and surgical knowledge survives without hospitals or written pharmacology |
| war | stone, bone or bronze weapons coexist with a few inherited superweapons nobody can repair |
| government | village councils and war bands replace territorial tax states; some become predatory sacral kingships |
| religion | fragments of liberation, Giant propaganda, Menhir bargains and Idol Masks are recombined into locally coherent but historically false systems |

Regression must have mechanisms. A people do not “forget iron” because they are primitive. They lose mines, charcoal territories, transport, specialist lineages, furnaces, stable demand or the political surplus that supported full-time smiths.

# IV. Eberron Distant-Land Extraction

## Xen'drik — the strongest primary grammar

Xen'drik is a continent of ancient Giant power destroyed through overwhelming intervention and enduring curses. Its old centres are ruins; routes and distances are unreliable; later civilisations repeatedly fail to reach the former scale; modern expeditions use Stormreach as a colonial and commercial threshold. Eberron creator Keith Baker stresses that the draconic assault destroyed Giant and lesser civilisations alike, while the Traveler's Curse disrupts travel and the Du'rashka Tul destroys societies that grow too large ([Xen'drik and the fall of the giants](https://keith-baker.com/ifaq-dragons-bad/), [the Du'rashka Tul](https://keith-baker.com/dragonmarks-31215-origins-authors-and-thrane/)).

### Useful World Egg extractions

- ruin ecologies larger than present peoples can understand;
- modern cultures descended from slaves, fugitives, client peoples and failed successor states;
- artefacts used correctly for the wrong reason;
- routes that fail because of specific old damage or jurisdiction;
- external salvager ports whose wealth depends upon dangerous inland extraction;
- advanced outsiders treating local inhabitants as labour, guides, obstacles, converts or evidence;
- remote communities with distinct relationships to the same ruin: worship, avoidance, habitation, dismantling or guarded technical use;
- the possibility that successful state formation awakens a dormant war system or Idol feeding architecture.

### Do not copy

Do not place one universal curse on every remnant people or make the continent incapable of civilisation by authorial decree. World Egg pressures should be plural and local: poisoned waters, Giant recurrence machines, hostile roads, monster ecologies, Menhir jurisdiction, Patron tribute, Idol cults, ruined soil and recurring human war. Otherwise every failed city has the same answer and local history becomes irrelevant.

## Argonnessen — hidden asymmetry of power

Argonnessen presents the oldest and most magically advanced civilisation in Eberron beside peripheral human cultures that know the dragons chiefly through myth, patronage and prohibition. The dragons usually ignore mortal suffering unless their own interests or the Draconic Prophecy require action; when they act, they may destroy whole civilisations rather than govern them ([Keith Baker on Argonnessen](https://keith-baker.com/dragons-bad/), [Seren knowledge and the forbidden interior](https://keith-baker.com/dragonmarks-523-lightning-round-3/)).

### Useful World Egg extractions

- a land can look technologically poor on its human surface while concealing powers vastly beyond the imperial core;
- peripheral peoples possess detailed stories about one patron but almost no reliable knowledge of greater-power politics;
- human kingdoms may serve as buffers, ritual clients, breeding grounds, observatories, sacrifices or pieces in nonhuman disputes;
- greater powers intervene for their own bounded reasons, not because they are secret guardians of humanity;
- a single sympathetic power can aid humans without making its entire kind benevolent;
- outsiders mistake a patronised frontier culture for the master of the hidden civilisation behind it.

### World Egg fit

Use this grammar for isolated Patron dominions, escaped Giant courts, ancient Idol enclaves and deep Menhir jurisdictions—not for a single council secretly managing the world. Menhir remain morally blind and locally continuous; Idols remain guilty Hungers; Patrons have individual anchor relations. Their differences matter more than a common “elder race” label.

## Sarlona — advanced captivity rather than regression

Sarlona is the necessary counterexample to a simple centre-to-periphery decline. Riedra is stable, infrastructurally sophisticated and apparently content because the Inspired manipulate dreams, communication, breeding and public memory. Its rulers first worsen conflict and then present themselves as the only possible peace. The monolith network supports common dreams, communication, power and transport, so destroying the tyranny without replacing its functions may cause panic, famine and civil war ([Riedra's shared dream and infrastructure](https://keith-baker.com/ifaq-inspired/), [the Dreaming Dark's saviour strategy](https://keith-baker.com/ifaq-kqg/)).

### Useful World Egg extractions

- a remote civilisation may be more orderly and technically capable than the Redeemer empires while being spiritually enslaved;
- an Idol's Masks can shape imagery, aspiration and public ritual without having created the humans who feed them;
- citizens can sincerely prefer the regime because it ended real war, feeds people and keeps monsters outside;
- resistance must answer what happens to food, roads, medicine and security after liberation;
- mountain, desert or ruined enclaves can preserve unwanted memory without automatically being morally pure;
- a ruler's beautiful or apparently holy body may be a vessel, office, Patron persona or Mask while the human person remains separately classified.

### Do not copy

Do not create one fantasy-Asian hive empire, dream parasite or monolith network by changing the names. Separate the usable functions: manufactured civil war, salvific dictatorship, coerced consensus, infrastructure dependence, controlled dreams, bred office-bearers and genuine frontier defence. Different distant states may use only one or two.

# V. Historical Reality Check

The real historical material supports objective judgement and severe cultural difference, but not a single hierarchy in which “Europe” was simply advanced and the Americas, Southeast Asia, India, Africa and Australia were one primitive block.

## Mexica human sacrifice

Human sacrifice in the Mexica world is historically and archaeologically attested. It was connected to state religion, captive warfare and cosmic obligation. Spanish and Indigenous allies destroyed the imperial cult that sustained it. The scale claimed in some conquest-era texts remains disputed, and the conquest also produced massacre, enslavement, forced labour and epidemic catastrophe. The usable principle is not that conquest sanctifies the conqueror. It is that **ending a genuinely evil institution remains good even when the people ending it also commit grave evil**. Mexico's Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia describes material evidence of large-scale captive sacrifice at Tecoaque ([INAH](https://inah.gob.mx/boletines/el-tzompantli-de-tecoaque-un-hito-dentro-del-discurso-del-museo-nacional-de-historia)).

## British abolition and naval suppression

Britain's 1807 abolition of its slave trade, later emancipation in its colonies and Royal Navy suppression campaigns were real, morally consequential and expensive. Parliamentary records describe nearly 1,400 captured slave ships and roughly 160,000 people freed by the West Africa Squadron. British treaties, patrols and seizures also attacked the East African and western Indian Ocean trade operating through Zanzibar, Arabia and the Persian Gulf; photographs and naval reports record captives released from dhows by ships such as HMS *Daphne* ([UK National Archives on the East African patrol](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/education/resources/slavery/source-2a/), [Royal Museums Greenwich research guide](https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/library/rmgl-157797)). The same record also requires remembering that Britain had been deeply involved in and enriched by Atlantic slavery; compensation went to slave owners, and some people “freed” from captured ships were channelled into coerced military service or apprenticeship ([UK Parliament overview](https://www.parliament.uk/slavetrade/), [Parliamentary research briefing](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/LLN-2019-0104/LLN-2019-0104.pdf), [West Africa Squadron figures](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld200607/ldhansrd/text/70607-0010.htm)).

The useful grammar is again double without being relativist: an empire can perform a costly objective good that does not absolve its prior crimes, every later conquest or the corrupt administration of the people it rescues.

## African slavery and predatory states

Slavery, captive-taking and the use of enslaved soldiers existed in multiple African societies before direct European rule. African rulers, merchants and war leaders could be responsible agents rather than passive scenery. That fact should not be flattened into “Africa did this for ages”: the continent contained radically different states, stateless societies, religions, economies and anti-slavery responses. External Atlantic, trans-Saharan and Indian Ocean demand also expanded the scale and profitability of raiding and helped produce particular militarised slaving states. A recent Cambridge study distinguishes the presence of slavery before European arrival from the later rise of West and East African slaving states under greatly increased overseas demand ([Cambridge study of slavery and African state formation](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/something-new-out-of-africa-states-made-slaves-slaves-made-states/6E27318C9103B59F54C44EB8748333EB)).

The setting can therefore include remnant kingdoms built upon slave soldiers, cattle raiding, captive children, ethnic expulsion or sacrificial kingship without euphemism. Each must have a specific polity, economy, enemy and historical mechanism. “African-coded” cannot itself function as the explanation.

## Caste and devadasi institutions

Caste hierarchy and severe hereditary exclusion predate British rule. British governance did not simply abolish caste; census and legal classification sometimes hardened or redirected existing categories. Cambridge studies note both precolonial caste enumeration and the powerful consequences of later colonial ranking systems ([precolonial and colonial enumeration](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/comparative-studies-in-society-and-history/article/cents-sense-census-human-inventories-in-late-precolonial-and-early-colonial-india/5BEC4697ECEA9D07EFCF1F3B5D9A992A), [caste in the colonial census](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/from-hierarchy-to-ethnicity/caste-in-the-census-of-india/454332F27D95CE922FD3AAA18784E14B)).

“Religious prostitution” is also too blunt as a description of all devadasi institutions across centuries and regions. Temple service, hereditary artistry, patronage, caste, concubinage and sexual exploitation existed in different combinations. Abolition involved Indian women and anti-caste reformers as well as colonial law; it could rescue girls from dedication while also impoverishing or silencing adult performers. Muvalur Ramamirtham, herself a former devadasi, is one important reform voice ([study of Ramamirtham's abolitionist writing](https://journal.kci.go.kr/snu-ioh/archive/articleView?artiId=ART002723149)).

The worldbuilding rule is to name the exact institution and coercion: dedication of girls, hereditary sexual access, temple debt, caste exclusion, elite concubinage or artistic patronage. Do not hide abuse, but do not collapse every temple woman into one category.

## Technological asymmetry

European states eventually acquired decisive advantages in oceanic logistics, finance, artillery, industrial production and the capacity to sustain overseas war. Those packages were historically specific and uneven. The precolonial Americas also contained large states, roads, cities, intensive agriculture and sophisticated metallurgy; the Inka road exceeded 20,000 miles ([Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian](https://americanindian.si.edu/explore/exhibitions/item?id=945)). Southeast Asia possessed long-standing metallurgy, shipbuilding, navigation and interregional trade before modern colonial rule ([Oxford on Southeast Asian metal networks](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61799/chapter-abstract/546319937), [early-modern trade networks](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-981-13-3131-2_4)).

Precolonial Australia lacked the urban states, metallurgy and military logistics of early modern Europe, but its societies were not static. Archaeology records deep technological innovation, environmental management, aquaculture, grinding and regionally changing economies ([Cambridge historiographical review](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/historical-journal/article/deep-past-of-precolonial-australia/F128C60AE14F4F07045FA65AEC152CB2)).

Therefore use **domain-specific superiority**. One society can possess steel, siegecraft and writing while knowing less about desert water, fire ecology, navigation or local medicine. The former may still conquer the latter because certain capabilities matter disproportionately in war. That is enough to create a harsh civilizational gradient without requiring historical falsehood or equal moral approval of every culture.

# VI. The Distant-Lands Garden

Do not compress every far region into one “primitive continent.” Plant several independent environments:

## 1. Orphan Ruin Continents

The main Xen'drik grammar: shattered Giant infrastructure, multiple remnant peoples, monster ecologies, salvage coasts and inland routes nobody fully controls.

## 2. Hidden Continuity Enclaves

A physically or metaphysically isolated Giant, Patron or remnant technical society retains extraordinary knowledge. Its neighbours may possess only fragments or tribute objects.

## 3. Idol Pacifications

Orderly remote states have roads, food and public peace because an Idol's throne-system has captured memory, office and desire. Their horror is not lack of civilisation but civilisation made into feeding architecture.

## 4. Menhir Jurisdictions

Human settlements survive inside bargains with morally blind powers of winter, sea, forest, fertility, sleep or stone. Their technology may remain low because settlement growth violates a real local ecology or bargain, not because the Menhir morally punishes progress.

## 5. Patron Protectorates

A Patron shelters one population in exchange for tribute, anchor maintenance, hostages, dreams, memories or military service. Rival Patrons move human towns as border pieces.

## 6. Salvager Ports and Mission Frontiers

Core-world merchants, churches, slavers, soldiers and scholars establish coastal footholds. They can bring genuine medicine, literacy, iron, abolition and sacramental teaching while also stealing land, bodies, relics and labour. Each expedition must be judged by what it actually does.

## 7. Recovered Native Kingdoms

Some remnant societies climb from village survival into iron kingdoms or archive states without imperial rescue. Their rise can produce law and security alongside conquest, caste or sacrifice. They prevent the distant world from being frozen in one post-apocalyptic stage.

## 8. Catastrophe Basins

Regions remain almost uninhabitable because of specific ancient systems: war-brood hatcheries, Glass scars, Titan fragments, predatory calendars, abandoned weather works or devouring routes. Human life survives only through seasonal entry, taboo and mobile expertise.

# VII. Implication for the Next Historical Pass

The next pass should be renamed:

> **Ark Scattering and Orphaned Reassembly Pass**

It must begin with a people-by-people ancestry audit rather than six founder tales.

## Phase 1 — Ancestry and continuity map

For every existing human culture, record:

- principal Ark, remnant or braided ancestry;
- which Giant horizon or secondary state its ancestors survived;
- preserved and lost technologies;
- initial knowledge of the Prophet;
- first verified contact with Ark descendants;
- first verified contact with Redeemer teaching;
- present relationship to Giant ruins, Menhir, Idols and Patrons;
- whether current origin stories remember, appropriate or deny another population.

## Phase 2 — Civilizational-pressure map

Score regions separately for:

- food surplus;
- metallurgy;
- writing and archives;
- roads and shipping;
- medicine;
- state capacity;
- supernatural predation;
- monster pressure;
- trade contact;
- Redeemer institutional presence.

No combined “civilisation number” is required. The uneven profile creates flavour.

## Phase 3 — Historical saplings

Only then select stories. The first batch should include:

1. one Ark household splitting into a later institutional people;
2. one remnant city surviving the Giant fall and then losing its infrastructure across generations;
3. one remnant people preserving a true technique inside a false cosmology;
4. one first contact between Ark-descended and remnant-descended humans;
5. one genuine abolition or conversion that becomes entangled with conquest;
6. one remote power using humans as pieces in a nonhuman conflict.

This gives the Scattering human breadth without making every people a child of the same caravan or every distant land a renamed Xen'drik.
