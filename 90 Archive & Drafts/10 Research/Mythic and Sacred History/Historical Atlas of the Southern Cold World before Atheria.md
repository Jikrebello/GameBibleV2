# Historical Atlas of the Southern Cold World before Atheria

## Scope

The southern cold world extends from the outer coasts and islands through the western river hills, middle forests and marshes to the eastern rivers and the Gale-Crown approaches. Beyond the inhabited ranges lie the polar wastes and the ruins of [[Nhaldur - The Penal Crownland and the First Rewriting|Nhaldur]], a Giant penal crownland whose collapse helped scatter people and violence across the region. Farther south means colder. Winter weather, short growing seasons, navigable rivers, sheltering valleys and the movement of Grobi packs gave each frontier its practical form.

No state governed this whole country in antiquity. Its inhabitants built farms, halls, ports, earthworks, gords, mines, market landings and roads of timber laid across wet ground. Most of those monuments were repaired repeatedly rather than cut once in enduring stone. Fire, rot, flooded soil, Grobi war and later Atherian rebuilding left an incomplete material record which imperial writers mistook for an absence of civilisation.

Three overlapping Folk constellations emerged from older Orphaned communities. The **Belathi** name became common among western river and coastal peoples; the **Hartheni** among several middle-forest districts; and the **Odrani** along eastern rivers whose upland neighbours included the **Kreseni**. These were neither pure races nor three united nations. People continued to identify themselves by a hill, river, island, assembly or kindred, and households moved between every broad group through marriage, fosterage, captivity, adoption, trade and flight.

Giant ancestry was one strand within this mixture. Nhaldur received genuine lesser Giants and giant-blooded humans from the cold southern crowns as political prisoners, condemned soldiers and hostage dynasts. When the Crownland broke, some of these prisoners led warbands or married and took concubines among local humans. Most descendants in the later Folk and Ättar lines were human and possessed complete human souls. They could inherit unusual height, strength, hardiness and an increased aptitude for particular bodily or elemental arts. A rare child born a true Giant retained Giant creaturely continuity and must be classified separately. Repeated intermarriage spread Giant traits through the human populations without making every southerner Giant-blooded or reducing either people to a breeding programme. The same history helped preserve the pale complexions common to the far-cold populations: most of the local Giant and human contributors already came from southern cold-country lineages.

The Ättar developed from closely related far-cold populations whose great halls, boats and seasonal musters concentrated authority. The Grobi developed from the separate living continuity begun by Wrath's First Rewriting. Neither people was a branch of the other, and neither history can be reduced to the wars between them.

## Nhaldur and the chain country, before c. 2300 BR

Nhaldur was a lesser Giant crownland built around six principal ring-fortresses and a much larger chain of mine prisons, labour camps, geothermal estates, road towers, hostage houses and harbour compounds. It stood below the [[Lesser Crowns of the Giant Age|Five Imperial Horizons]]. The **Council of Keys** administered it for major imperial courts and numerous lesser Giant crowns, especially the southern cold-country states which could move prisoners to Nhaldur without crossing the whole world.

A rotating Crown Gaoler controlled the central stores and the warded road, while five contributing Giant houses maintained their own compounds, workshops and armed clients. Those houses were themselves exile dynasties. Their members had received Nhaldur as a sentence, a hostage obligation or an alternative to execution, and most could not lawfully return to the courts they served. They were gaolers and prisoners at the same time, with greater rooms, weapons and authority than the people locked beneath them.

The system treated distance, cold and dependence on heat as instruments of confinement. Qianglong courts sent disobedient officers and people who possessed forbidden medical or military knowledge. Ishkatar sent debt rebels, skilled deportees and rival estate claimants. Xolotlan rulers removed failed priests, captive soldiers and calendar dissenters. Nythalum sent engineers accused of berth theft or sabotage. Ansharan courts sent rebellious client households and human servants whose testimony was dangerous. Kharadi road houses supplied much of the administration and expected Nhaldur's iron, stormglass, prisoners and transport knowledge in return. Lesser southern crowns supplied most of the ordinary Giant prisoners: defeated cadet houses, giant-blooded claimants, mutinous guards and politically dangerous kin whose execution might start a feud.

Older southern communities already fished the coasts, cultivated sheltered ground and moved herds between valleys. The Crownland seized some of them as labour districts and forced others into supply agreements. Free client households also profited by carrying grain, fuel, captives and letters. After several generations, a single fortress neighbourhood might contain an exiled Giant prince, a human mine gang from Qianglong country, Ishkatari water engineers, local fishers and children who had never seen the places named in their parents' sentences.

Nhaldur survived because prisoners possessed indispensable skills. **Molkar** supplied iron and hard stone. **Suldren** controlled geothermal heat and winter glasshouses. **Avarn** guarded the harbour and evacuation fleet. **Veyrun** contained tuned horns and speaking galleries. A caller spoke into one prepared chamber, and relay crews repeated the message at successive towers so that exact words could cross ravines even when a blizzard drowned an ordinary shout. **Ossar Ring** held the Council of Keys and the central hostage archive. **Kharvos** combined military barracks, bodily workshops and the largest grain vault. The six rings governed scores of smaller confinement sites which could not feed or defend themselves without the main road. Kharvos's attempt to make a cold-adapted servile army became the setting of the First Rewriting.

## The War of Open Keys and the First Rewriting, c. 2292–2264 BR

The Great Unbinding severed Nhaldur's dependable contact with its northern patrons. Grain ships stopped arriving regularly. Rival wardens concealed news, opened cells to recruit followers and executed hostages whose foreign houses could no longer retaliate. Human crews withheld fuel, altered ration tallies and occupied the galleries through which heat and orders moved. The struggle became known as the **War of Open Keys** because keys passed through theft, murder, negotiation and public surrender faster than any Crown Gaoler could record lawful possession.

The human revolt had several centres. **Vaska Ren**, a heatworks engineer at Suldren, organised a fuel stoppage while preserving the pipes serving nursery and infirmary rooms. **Ilyen Marr**, a registrar at Kharvos, copied the captive rolls and falsified transfer orders. **Sorna Vale**, an Avarn pilot, carried families away on store barges after the sea-going wardens reserved the sound vessels for Giant houses. Their cooperation was practical rather than ideological. They disagreed over whether captive Giant rebels could be trusted, whether food should be shared with surrendered guards and which fortresses had to be abandoned.

In c. 2284 BR, Crown Gaoler **Urdazar Vorzhal** concentrated 2,014 human captives inside Kharvos. The register listed 611 mine prisoners and their dependants, 487 soldiers punished for mutiny or desertion, 532 heatworkers and children seized from Suldren, and 384 hostages from older southern communities. **Nashara Nemek**, master of the bodily workshops, intended to use deprivation, forced combat, Giant blood preparations and resonant commands to produce soldiers who could endure cold and obey through hunger. The captives were starved while fighting outside the ring cut the water galleries and prevented their planned transfer.

Wrath had already entered the project. A Red Reckoner attached to the Xolotlan prison compounds supplied Nashara with altered casualty tables and a sequence of punishments presented as imperial war science. The Mask directed her to sort bodies by appetite, feed victory with defeated flesh and reserve the strongest for a final opening of the grain vault. Nashara accepted and refined the regimen because it promised her an army. Urdazar treated it as one more coercive method and ignored its source. Wrath's purpose was larger than their civil war: the Giant armies were collapsing across the world, and Furor intended to fill the open country with self-reproducing soldiers which could grow by consuming whatever they conquered.

The sealed barracks, forced fights and killing at the ration doors completed the prepared architecture. Wrath then acted directly. The 2,014 human souls separated from the bodies and entered the proper judgement road. Furor tore down and rebuilt the vacated flesh, making hunger govern growth, injury provoke regeneration and domination organise instinct. The first Grobi were living, reproducing and intelligent, but Wrath omitted the moral conscience by which a human person can recognise mercy or justice as goods. Their descendants could plan, remember, speak, suffer, form packs and choose among strategies; their creaturely nature drove those choices towards domination, fighting and consumption. They were neither human souls nor dependent Wrath Masks.

The first Grobi broke out of the lower barracks, killed wardens, prisoners and rescuers, and consumed the opened stores. Pieces of human language and trained bodily response persisted unevenly, but they did not make any new being the human person whose body had been taken. Ilyen's copied register and the later **Names Beneath the Ninth Door** commemorate the victims. Grobi accounts remember an awakening in hunger and enclosure, usually without accepting the human register as their own genealogy.

Nhaldur ended fortress by fortress. Suldren's revolt preserved part of the heat network before fighting flooded its deepest galleries. Veyrun's human crews opened the eastern road and carried resonant work words into the mountains. Molkar burned for nine winters after rebels fired the lifting shafts. Avarn's last wardens sailed with selected prisoners and abandoned hundreds on the quay. Ossar Ring split when rival Giants loaded opposite arcs of its wall with incompatible wards. Kharvos became a Grobi refuge around the breached granaries. No common crown survived after c. 2264 BR.

## Dispersal, recovery and the first Grobi hearths, c. 2264–2050 BR

Freedom and catastrophe travelled together. Refugees carried tools, seeds, fish hooks, mining knowledge, songs and prison languages into older southern settlements. They also carried feuds, armed followings and claims to supplies which could not feed everyone. Some communities welcomed escaped workers as relatives or useful specialists. Others enslaved strangers, killed former client families or barred people whose Giant association was feared. Several bodily surviving Giants established short-lived enclaves before human coalitions or Grobi bands destroyed them.

The first Grobi did not move as one army. Packs fought over Kharvos's stores and separated along the mine road, coast and eastern passes. Their earliest young made clear that the Rewriting had established a true lineage rather than a one-generation curse. Packs which reserved food for breeding produced more hunters and fighters. Those which concentrated everything in the largest stages created brief ogre-led hosts and then collapsed when the stores failed.

The **Narrow-Bowl Hearths** formed around a rule that breeding chambers and young received a fixed share before the war-feast. The rule protected the future strength of the pack, not a moral claim held by the weak. The **Ash-Marrow Hearths** treated growth through victory as proof of Wrath's favour and fed the largest fighters first. Both preyed upon human settlements. They differed over whether a host became stronger by preserving many young or by feeding a few enormous killers.

Human settlement also spread. The remains of Veyrun supported eastern stone and sound craft. Suldren supplied warm-water gardens until mineral blockage ruined most of the channels. Coast families repaired Avarn's smaller hulls. Farther west, communities which had never belonged to Nhaldur rebuilt farms, river landings and cattle circuits of their own. The reconstructed **Fäskogarfolk** continuum belongs to these connected populations and languages, not to a lost state centred on the prisons.

## Proto-Atherian contact and regional peoples, c. 2050–1650 BR

Ark-descended war-settlers began reaching some northern approaches, rivers and coasts around 2050 BR. They encountered occupied country. A proto-Atherian fort could offer reliable measures, metal fittings and defence against a rival while also demanding timber, grain, hostages and land. Folk communities destroyed several forts and absorbed their surviving families. Others entered alliance, sold captives, accepted mixed marriages or became dependent upon foreign weapons and markets.

Among the western rivers, Belathi-speaking kindreds built defended heights and market enclosures. The Caerethi around **Brael Tor** linked cattle country to a navigable river; Dovren estuary households supplied pilots and salt; Morlach island kindreds maintained fosterage and sea roads. Their learned houses preserved law, praise, genealogy and difficult relationships with local Menhir courts.

The middle forests contained Hartheni farm districts, Ossen marsh communities and Rauven coalitions nearer the Atherian approaches. Longhouses combined storage, animals and productive work. Public assemblies set compensation, organised watches and raised temporary war leaders. A chief with a successful retinue could enlarge a coalition quickly, but followers expected food, gifts and results.

Along the eastern rivers, Odrani villages cleared fields, kept bees, fished, made iron and built timber-and-earth gords at portages and market landings. Kreseni upland communities controlled passes and mining valleys. Vezhari cold-shore settlements joined river traders to far-cold boats. Their speech and material customs overlapped with one another and with Vardeni communities beyond the north-eastern approaches. This reflected old southern contact and continued intermarriage, not one people divided solely by a later Atherian border.

Veyrun's command language survived among eastern work crews. Its words first opened vents, steadied bridges and sent spoken messages along tuned chambers and horn relays when wind made ordinary speech impossible. Odrani and Kreseni teachers turned that inheritance into **war-voice**, a learned school of resonance later called the [[The Deep Voice - Masters Wars and the Quiet Measure|Deep Voice]]. Access followed apprenticeship rather than ancestry.

In the far-cold coast and protected valleys, winter stores concentrated several households around larger halls. Boat ownership, iron work and permanent Grobi watches gave successful chiefs more dependable authority than most inland speakers possessed. Lion, Ox, Bear and Wolf appeared first as broad standards and regional affiliations. They had not yet become hereditary supernatural kindreds.

## The War of the Empty Halls, c. 1608–1596 BR

Every Grobi winter brought a northern war season. Packs which survived the previous cold bred and fed through spring, summer and autumn; goblins became hobgoblins, successful hobgoblins became orcs, and the richest warbands could sustain ogres. When winter closed the southern hunting grounds, those enlarged bands converged upon human herds, villages and granaries. Most such hosts broke apart after one or two raids. The Nine-Drum Host became famous because it united nine of them and attempted permanent conquest.

Several abundant summers enlarged Grobi herds and warbands along the Kharvos road. A sudden sequence of early freezes then ruined forage and trapped stores farther south. **Gharuk Eight-Bowls**, an orc-stage commander between seven and nine feet tall and stronger than at least five human men, assembled the **Nine-Drum Host** to seize northern halls, livestock and a permanent warm road. His quartermaster **Varka Red-Ladle** lashed crude ration sledges together and bullied smaller bands into the column. Wrath speakers attached themselves to the undertaking and interpreted every victory as a command to continue.

The host entered the north-eastern passes with fighters, crude smithies, breeding hearths, animals and human captives kept as marching meat. Grobi technology remained below that of the halls they attacked. Most weapons were heavy, ugly and poorly finished, made from wood, bone, scavenged metal or roughly welded iron. Orc strength made such weapons lethal. Adult goblins, comparable in size and strength to human children of ten or twelve, used traps, poison, ambush and repeated hit-and-run attacks instead of meeting grown warriors in a fair contest.

The host intended to occupy the warm halls after consuming their inhabitants and livestock. That made it harder to turn aside and more vulnerable to the loss of its food train. The Narrow-Bowl leader **Urzha** did not join the human cause. Gharuk had seized two of her camps, placed their young on Varka's sledges and begun eating them to keep ogres large. Urzha betrayed his route to the hall chiefs so that human fighters would break his ration road while her surviving pack escaped east. The exchange was a temporary alignment between enemies, bought with maps and safe conduct rather than friendship.

The far-cold halls responded badly at first. Messengers reached assemblies already divided by feud. Some chiefs defended their own store country and refused to release boats or food to rivals. Gharuk took three halls intact and emptied others. Grobi killed and ate the inhabitants during the assault, then stripped livestock, grain, leather, tallow and timber from the settlement. They kept only those prisoners whom they meant to consume later or force through an immediate task before slaughter. The dead froze beside feast benches, giving the war its enduring name.

Four surviving coalitions gathered under their existing Lion, Ox, Bear and Wolf standards. Their chiefs—**Savar of Red Shoal**, **Torvek of Hornwall**, **Brennar of Stoneden** and **Eldran of Grey Pass**—asked Mother Moon's Pale Household to put the beasts on their banners into their bodies and the beasts' fury into every spear sworn beneath them. The courtiers divided those words into two exact gifts. Each named chief received one monstrous hybrid shape, anchored to his office and passed through the paternal line by which that office was inherited. Everyone who swore beneath the four standards received the lesser bodily surge later called rage, and marriage spread that inheritance through the wider Ättar population.

The price followed the same grammar of blood and replacement. Each ruling line owed the first living child of every future pact-holder. The Court would take the child during sleep beneath open moonlight, leave a changeling to occupy the empty place and return the original when the substitute reached adolescence. The returned child would possess the same human soul and apparent age, but not an uninterrupted memory of the missing years. **Hedra Store-Keeper**, who had kept the common granary open through the assembly, understood that the chiefs were purchasing immediate power with children who had neither spoken nor consented. She opposed the bargain and nevertheless supplied the battle which followed.

The transformed chiefs broke the forward Grobi companies at separate choke points. Human skirmishers cut Varka's sled road, while boats moved grain and wounded people between surviving halls. When the Nine-Drum Host divided over its remaining food, Gharuk could no longer feed the large stages upon which his authority depended. Wounded and beginning to shrink near the third captured hall, he was dragged down and eaten alive by his own orc guard. Each killer tried to prove mastery by taking part of the former chief's flesh. Varka led part of the host back through the eastern pass; Urzha's pack withdrew under the purchased safe conduct.

The victors did not honour every restraint they claimed afterwards. Fighters killed wounded Grobi and young at two abandoned camps and enslaved human captives whom they accused of serving the host, although the Grobi had kept those people as food. The bargain saved the hall ranges from conquest and bound political legitimacy to the repeated loss of innocent children. Its four coalitions became pact-kindreds, not four states.

## Forts, gords, halls and winter roads, c. 1550–800 BR

The centuries after the war produced stronger regional institutions. Pact halls built concealed stores, beacon ridges and roads which could carry a winter levy. Grobi packs established lairs at Kharvos, along heated ruins and beside predictable herd routes. Folk river leagues and forest assemblies developed their own defence agreements without entering the lunar covenant.

Western Belathi centres grew rich through cattle, salt, ore and river tolls. Brael Tor expanded from a defended height into a settlement of workshops, yards, markets and surrounding farms. Morlach crews linked islands and the Tidewood approaches, while Dovren ports received Atherian and other foreign merchants. Fosterage connected ruling houses and could either interrupt a feud or place a child inside the next war.

The Hartheni middle country developed oath assemblies, compensation schedules and retinues whose service crossed ethnic boundaries. Captured weapons were deliberately broken and sunk in **Ossen Fen** after the War of the Three Crossings. The deposit commemorated a real victory and the post-battle killing of surrendered fighters. Later Atherian writers turned one community's rite into a claim about all forest peoples.

Odrani gords collected tolls and sheltered craft specialists. Kreseni pass holders sold iron, stone and convoy service. During the [[The Deep Voice - Masters Wars and the Quiet Measure#The Seven Gords|War of Seven Gords]], far callers sent spoken orders along known wind paths, gate strokes released pressure into tested barriers and earth wakes brought down slopes whose fractures had been mapped beforehand. None of these workings summoned lightning. The art reached its heroic height and acquired both prestige and patrons. The **Dramovar Descent** of 1018 BR later buried part of an allied army and a valley settlement when a combined working entered unfamiliar rock. Survivor Sarvek the Listener withdrew to the highest Gale-Crown slopes and founded the **Quiet Measure**, a small order of mountain druids who prayed to [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/20 Third Circle/Zephyraxis|Zephyraxis]] and treated silence, wind and close knowledge of the mountain as religious disciplines. Most Deep-Voiced schools remained martial or practical and regarded Sarvek's order as an austere fringe.

Maritime and river exchange survived the wider Breaking of the Bronze Roads unevenly. Long-distance bronze became difficult to replace, courts lost imported grain and armed carriers kept cargo when payment failed. Local iron, food production, boats and shorter routes allowed some southern centres to grow. Others sold dependants, raided neighbours or abandoned specialised workshops.

Grobi packs also changed. The **Eighth Share** spread from Narrow-Bowl practice among hosts which wanted enough surviving young to replace winter losses. Kharvos hosted a winter exchange under the **White Pot truce**, where armed intermediaries bartered hides, stolen goods, iron scrap, salt, livestock, prisoners intended as food and route information. A standing pot marked a temporary calculation: anyone who attacked early would face every other pack still waiting to trade. When the pots overturned, no obligation survived. Human neighbours who entered the market understood that a profitable Grobi seller could join the next winter assault.

## Atherian centuries, c. 800–520 BR

Atherian city-states renewed contact through merchants, military colonies, client leaders and recruiters. Northern Folk districts supplied timber, iron, cattle and soldiers. Atherian goods included wine, worked metal, glass, grain and coin. The traffic enlarged local inequality. Chiefs who controlled a landing or mine could arm followers and lend food to poorer households, then demand land or years of service when debts failed.

No single policy governed the southern approaches. Some Hartheni and Rauven companies served Atherian armies and returned with pay, foreign spouses and captives. Some Belathi towns admitted merchant wards while forbidding foreign surveyors from marking agricultural land. Odrani princes hired Atherian engineers and kept Deep-Voiced retainers whose schools disagreed about service abroad. Ättar crews traded, raided imperial shipping and accepted auxiliary contracts. Grobi bands sold route intelligence or prisoners as meat at one frontier and attacked its granaries the following winter.

The Tidewood coast followed its own local history. Its capture of Aulon's dry fleet around 1260 BR, the Tarred Hand workshops and the Three Beaches relationships belonged to particular communities. They did not found the western Folk constellation or the Ättar pact-kindreds.

## Casvilar's campaigns and the southern balance, c. 390–350 BR

Teren Casvilar's campaigns from about 390 to 379 BR struck the northern Folk approaches rather than the whole cold world. Morveth's lesser river coalition supplied boats and guides. Drenoc formed a resistance around Kellovar. Velcara organised stores, helped families escape and survived captivity. Kellovar fell in 383 BR. Casvilar mutilated surrendered fighters at Darneth in 380 and returned north with wealth and captives, leaving posts, clients and obligations whose reach remained uneven.

The campaigns revealed a persistent material limit. An Atherian army could take a height, landing or market if it maintained river supply and secured local guides. Holding dispersed farms, wet forests and winter refuges yielded less revenue and demanded more grain than its commanders expected. Casvilar's cold-coast voyage in 385 obtained hostages and gifts but no lasting occupation. Tidewood's much older dry fleet remained unrelated.

In 350 BR the southern world remained plural:

- Casvilar's former garrisons and Morveth's clients controlled selected northern crossings, while Kellovar and Darneth households lived under the costs of defeat;
- Belathi river towns, island kindreds and Atlantic ports ranged from trading partners to independent confederacies;
- Hartheni assemblies and war leaders negotiated, resisted or entered Atherian service without a common king;
- Odrani gords and Kreseni pass communities maintained rival princes, Deep-Voiced schools and trade towards the eastern sea;
- Ättar pact halls formed jarldoms and temporary chief-kings but no government of all four kindreds;
- Grobi breeding hearths, predatory markets and winter hosts competed for food and mastery within the same Wrath-shaped nature.

Thalion therefore inherited routes into the south, allied communities and memories of conquest rather than a completed province. Any later empire seeking deeper rule still had to move food, secure local labour, survive winter and decide which existing authorities it would recognise or destroy.

## Work, family and pleasure

Most southern lives were organised around food and repair. Households cleared fields, kept cattle, sheep and pigs, tended ovens and beehives, fished rivers and cold seas, cut peat and timber, made charcoal, mined ore, wove cloth and preserved meat. A great hall or gord could not survive on warriors alone. Its cooks, herders, smiths, boat-builders, healers, children, dependants, free tenants and enslaved workers made refuge and campaigning possible.

Marriage moved grazing rights, harbour shares and obligations between communities. Fosterage could educate an heir, create trust or hold a child as an honourable hostage. Captive marriage and sexual access under slavery remained coercive even when descendants later belonged fully to the receiving people. Adoption, manumission and sworn household entry gave some people routes out of exclusion while leaving others dependent for life.

The Folk constellations and the Ättar halls shared an annual **All-Thing** tradition. Once each year, chiefs and free men travelled to a recognised open-air assembly to settle disputes, hear compensation claims, witness marriages and inheritances, declare outlawry and decide whether neighbouring districts would join the next levy. Local assemblies continued through the rest of the year. The All-Thing mattered because a chief who wanted several kindreds to enforce the same judgement had to speak before men who did not live in his hall. Attendance was a male political right; women, slaves, dependants and strangers affected by its judgements normally had to speak through a recognised free man, though powerful household heads and learned women sometimes forced a hearing through rank or reputation.

Gatherings provided enjoyment as well as judgement. Belathi cattle fairs held races, harp contests and satirical praise. Hartheni assemblies included wrestling, board games and competitions in carved spoons whose usefulness mattered more than ornament. Odrani river feasts paired boat races with improvised strongman stories. Ättar halls judged winter boasts against work completed in spring. Grobi drums, food contests and weapon boasts served dominance: a joke was successful when it humiliated a rival who could not retaliate.

## Religion and extraordinary arts

Folk relationships with local powers varied by place. A Belathi learned house might keep a sovereignty rite with one Menhir court while a neighbouring island offered to a different sea power. Hartheni oath places, ancestor songs and victory deposits did not form one church. Odrani household spirits, Kreseni pass rites and Vezhari cult centres likewise remained distinct. Real Menhir persons, Idol Masks, Egg White Patrons, residues and ordinary natural events could receive similar human names without sharing an identity.

The Deep Voice operated through resonance between breath, understood words, bodily stance, Material substance and the Air-and-Earth structures of the Elemental Shell. Particular schools used prepared chambers or local bargains, but no Menhir owned the whole art. It could move matter, weather and living attention within learned limits. It could not restore a human soul, guarantee victory or command a sovereign Menhir.

Ättar Moon-blood followed a different history. Mother Moon's bargain created four paternal lines of hybrid chiefs and spread the lesser rage inheritance through the people sworn beneath them around 1600 BR. It did not create the Ättar, and war-voice did not supply its power. Grobi Wrath nature was different again. Grobi were intelligent predators made without human moral conscience. They could choose a route, leader, victim, bargain or moment of attack, but their choices remained ordered towards domination, violence and consumption. Wrath fed that nature without issuing every command through a species-wide mind.
