# Package B — Final Reconciliation and Canon Readiness Review

## Decision

Package B passed its joint canon review on 11 September 2026. Its five principal research accounts established one chronology from the early Jirahar road houses to the political balance of 350 BR. The Upland Conqueror research, Qahur's Single Radiance, the Alexander romances and the Delkosi inheritance were incorporated into that chronology rather than left as competing proposals. [[01 Canon Reference/History/10 Chronology and Control/Package B - Qiryath and Eastern Empires Canon Integration Review.md|The canon integration review]] records the controlling destinations and boundaries.

The proposed history gives each civilisation an independent course. Qiryath develops through road households, local protectors, sanctuary government, destruction, exile and return. Arqesh grows from canal cities into a deporting river empire and falls through dynastic war, subject revolt and two foreign commands. Vashar becomes an empire because Arezan Tiravan turns a highland coalition into a durable government. Ortheia briefly dominates the Atherian city world through Valeron II and Eryndor III, then loses that hegemony in a succession war. Qahur survives intervention by all of them through its crowns, river communities and temple estates.

No part of this review changes canon. It records the version suitable for promotion if approved.

## The Qahuri and Arqeshite bridge

The Single Radiance ends in 814 BR, more than a century before Arqesh's fall. Its foreign-marriage crisis belongs to an early stage of House Ubar's western expansion.

Narethi, widow of Hesaret, seeks an outside prince because marriage to an Aseret commander would surrender the throne to the military faction controlling her household. Merod II sends his younger son Ashkur. The escort is attacked before it reaches the Sacred Basin, and Ashkur dies. Zaheret's party, former Radiance officers and local commanders all accuse one another. The killer remains unknown.

Merod already wants Qahuri grain routes and eastern canal towns. He turns Ashkur's murder into a public grievance and enlarges the intervention between 811 and 802 BR. The marriage crisis therefore changes the language, urgency and support for an existing policy. It neither creates Arqeshite imperialism nor causes Neshkar II's later attack on Qiryath.

## The fall of Arqesh

Arqesh falls through five connected pressures:

1. Kassur III and Mardun Ubar divide House Ubar, the palace army and provincial loyalties.
2. Nemeru withholds boats, Qahuri rulers attack isolated garrisons and open-country leaders strike the remount system.
3. Arezan Tiravan unites Vashari houses and provincial defectors around the highland roads.
4. Eryndor defeats the western field armies, captures Terys and supplies the siege force, fleet and treasury needed to reach the capital.
5. Nemeru crews and forced workers open channels and routes which the royal defences assumed were closed.

Arezan seizes the eastern gates and water stations. Eryndor's soldiers cross the opened channel. Fighting and plunder continue after the surrender, and both victorious commands kill inhabitants and enslave captives. The alliance destroys House Ubar's government without creating a shared state.

Kassur escapes and is murdered in 688 BR by a commander seeking reward. Eryndor executes the killers and buries Kassur as a king. The ceremony helps Eryndor claim the Ubar inheritance while concealing that his army had helped destroy it.

## Eryndor, Parena and the inheritance

Eryndor marries Parena Tiravan after his alliance with Arezan. Parena is Mithara's adult daughter and Arezan's niece. Mithara remains the senior organiser of the Tiravan coalition and estates; Parena develops a separate position inside Eryndor's mixed court. She receives Vashari officers, Arqeshite families and Qahuri envoys whose interests cross the two commands.

Eryndor dies in 684 BR while Parena is pregnant. His adult half-brother and the posthumous child become nominal heirs. The army's commands, treasury and provinces pass into four practical centres:

| Successor | Principal inheritance | Historical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Arezan Tiravan** | Vashar, eastern roads and most of the Ubar basin | creates the durable Crown of Roads and Dawns; issues the return decree |
| **Demeran Kavos** | Ortheia and the western army | destroys the nominal heirs; loses much of Valeron's city hegemony as Caleran recovers |
| **Serevan of Delkos** | allied fleet, islands and eastern harbours | creates a mixed maritime court whose officer culture reaches House Nerethan |
| **Neresh of Qemaret** | southern garrisons and selected Qahuri crown towns | establishes temporary military settlements later expelled or absorbed by local houses |

Demeran imprisons and kills Parena and her child. Her surviving attendants enter Vasharan and Delkosi households and preserve incompatible accounts of the succession. No ruler inherits Eryndor's full network.

## Second Qiryath and Vasharan government

Arezan's Decree of Roads and Houses serves restoration and government at the same time. It permits selected displaced households to return, restores named sanctuaries and recognises property under Vasharan authority. It also weakens Ubar military estates, reopens roads and obliges restored communities to support the new empire. People displaced by Arezan's own conquest receive no general restoration.

Second Qiryath is consecrated around 660 BR. Returned households, river craftworkers and surviving villages supply its labour and food. Some Jirahar remain in the Ubar cities or western diaspora. The rebuilt sanctuary therefore becomes the centre of a dispersed people rather than the place to which every exile returns.

Vasharan government develops through road commands, subject cities, local assemblies and older canal officials. Arezan's conquest supplies a royal foundation; Daryav turns it into an administrative system. Later kings confront coastal revolts and Atherian leagues without losing the Vashari highland core.

## The Delkosi and Qahuri inheritances

Serevan's court at Delkos supplies the selected Ptolemaic political grammar. It combines an Atherian-speaking military household with local sailors, Arqeshite administration, Vashari officers and Qahuri royal forms. Its kingdom fragments, while its officer houses and naval culture survive.

Nerethan belongs to one of those later officer houses. He does not descend from Eryndor or necessarily from Serevan. His arrival at the Second Harbour in 499 BR carries a real Delkosi inheritance into his marriage with Hadrima. Their descendants rule Sarqen through that military tradition and the authority of the spared towns.

Qahur remains the independent Egyptian-shaped river civilisation. Neresh's garrisons and other successor settlements produce a Qahuri Hellenistic period without replacing the Twin-Reed crowns. Samyra later draws upon junior Qahuri ancestry, spared-town sovereignty and Delkosi court culture; none grants her dominion over the whole Sacred Basin.

## Collision check

- Eryndor is neither Teren Casvilar nor Aurelius Thalion. His hereditary kingdom, eastern conquest, early death and fragmented successors serve a different historical function.
- Arezan remains the founder of Vasharan government. He is neither Eryndor's subordinate nor the sole conqueror of Arqesh.
- Merod II's Qahuri intervention, Neshkar II's destruction of First Qiryath and Kassur III's last war belong to different generations.
- Parena is Mithara's daughter. The later Queen Mithara I is named for her great-aunt and belongs to another generation.
- Qahur, Arqesh and Vashar remain separate river and highland civilisations. Shared Pride imagery does not merge their cults or supernatural actors.
- Delkos supplies a political and military inheritance to House Nerethan. It does not restore Qarzeth or turn Sarqen into Qahur.
- The Gateway Wars, Wars of the Inheritance and Eastern Sea Wars remain separate conflicts with connected participants and institutions.
- The Low Door Companions, Bent-Key Fellowship, Crownless Company and older sea-kings remain independent traditions.
