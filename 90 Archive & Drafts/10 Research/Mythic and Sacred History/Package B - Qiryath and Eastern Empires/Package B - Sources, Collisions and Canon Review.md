# Package B — Sources, Collisions and Canon Review

## Purpose and status

This review controls the proposed history of Qiryath, Arqesh, Vashar and the eastern sea approaches from the death of the Great Prophet to Atherian unification. The five companion accounts form one development delivery. They are written with proper names and connected events so that the proposed world can be evaluated as history; none of those additions becomes canon until a separate promotion is authorised.

The package preserves the established sequence of First Qiryath's destruction in 742 BR, Arqesh's fall around 690 BR and Second Qiryath's consecration around 660 BR. It supplies the people, governments, material life and regional relationships that the older chronology left unnamed. It also gives the former “High-Road Empire” the provisional endonym **Vasharan Empire**, an Orphaned Vashari population and House Tiravan.

> [!important] Alexander-layer reconciliation
> The five companion histories now incorporate [[The Upland Conqueror - Atherian Placement and Canon Impact Review]]. Arezan remains the founder of the Vasharan imperial settlement; Eryndor III of Ortheia is the second principal external conqueror of Arqesh. Eryndor's death and contested inheritance supply the immediate setting for the Decree of Roads and Houses. [[Package B - Final Reconciliation and Canon Readiness Review]] controls the complete integrated proposal.

## Research base

### Ancient Israel, Judah and received Scripture

The history uses the Eastern Orthodox scriptural sequence as a moral and narrative source: patriarchal households, local judges, monarchy, divided allegiance, siege, deportation, return and the difficult rebuilding of communal life. The proposed Jirahar history does not reproduce one biblical genealogy or make Qiryath a second Jerusalem. The Great Prophet remains unique, and no later office inherits his commission.

Modern historical work distinguishes the development of Israel and Judah from the final literary form of their received histories. The [Cambridge Ancient History discussion of Israel and Judah](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-ancient-history/israel-and-judah-until-the-revolt-of-jehu-931841-bc/E2EEB5F6616D5739578F2DA958285080) and the [Cambridge history of Israelite and Judean religions](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-religions-in-the-ancient-world/israelite-and-judean-religions/2F2D491D1F54246BA5B130276A575D59) support a layered account of households, shrines, kingdoms and later interpretation. Our proposal likewise allows Jirahar institutions to emerge across centuries.

The siege of First Qiryath retains scriptural hardness: people die in fighting and famine; captives are enslaved and deported; collaborators survive; family members make incompatible choices. Return restores neither the dead nor the earlier city.

The expanded protector period uses three distinct scriptural strands. Tovan follows Gideon and Abimelech: reluctant deliverance, deceptive night victory, refusal of kingship in words, a sacred object that becomes a snare and sons who turn inherited prestige into massacre. Hador follows Samson: a bodily gift joined to a vow, appetite and sexual relationships, betrayal, mutilation, revenge and a final victory that kills captives and servants with the enemy rulers. Haviel, Qoram and Zimra follow the Elijah, Ahab, Jezebel and Naboth cycle in 3 Kingdoms: foreign royal marriage, cult policy, judicial murder for a vineyard, public signs, the killing of rival diviners and the prophet's flight under a death threat. These adaptations preserve the guilt of the deliverers and prophets as well as the rulers they oppose.

### Assyria, Babylonia and imperial deportation

Arqesh draws upon Neo-Assyrian scale, administrative violence and royal representation, together with Babylonian canal-city continuity. The [Metropolitan Museum's overview of Assyria](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/essays/assyria-1365-609-b-c) describes an empire sustained by military organisation, provincial rule and deportation. Its account of [Sennacherib and Jerusalem](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2014/assyria-to-iberia/blog/posts/sennacherib-and-jerusalem) illustrates how royal inscriptions, monumental images and local sources can preserve sharply different perspectives upon one campaign. The [British Museum's Assyrian galleries](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/galleries/assyria-lion-hunts) show how palace reliefs projected the king's controlled violence and mastery.

Deportation in the proposal is a material policy. Arqeshite officials move households for labour, security and production; family separation, forced marches, disease and death follow. Skilled captives also establish workshops and maintain canals. The Oriental Institute's study of [forced migration under Assyrian rule](https://oi.uchicago.edu/sites/default/files/uploads/shared/docs/Publications/nn232.pdf) informs this combination of violence, state purpose and later cultural change.

Arqesh is not a renamed Assyria. Its Ubar canal geography, royal houses, Qiryath campaign, decline and supernatural ecology belong to the World Egg. Its cities continue after the dynasty falls, drawing upon the broader archaeological fact that Mesopotamian urban and scribal life outlasted many conquering houses. The [Met's Mesopotamian chronology](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/04/wam.html) provides a comparative frame for that long continuity.

### Persian rule, return and the eastern wars

Vashar draws upon the Achaemenid combination of road administration, regional government, royal household politics, multilingual representation and accommodation after conquest. The [Metropolitan Museum's Achaemenid overview](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/the-achaemenid-persian-empire-550-330-b-c) and Encyclopaedia Iranica's account of [Achaemenid satrapies](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/achaemenid-satrapies/) support an empire governed through varied provinces rather than uniform replacement. The Persepolis archive demonstrates the ordinary administrative labour and movement concealed by royal monuments.

Arezan's Decree of Roads and Houses uses the historical grammar of restoration associated with Cyrus while preserving political calculation. The [British Museum's Cyrus Cylinder](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/W_1880-0617-1941) and the Met's account of [Cyrus and the Judean diaspora](https://www.metmuseum.org/es/perspectives/cyrus-and-the-judean-diaspora) show the need to compare royal presentation, local memory and imperial interest. Arezan allows return, restores selected sanctuaries and executes rivals. A beneficial decree does not make every act of his reign righteous.

The Right Order and the Lie draw cautiously upon Iranian religious language. Encyclopaedia Iranica's survey of [Achaemenid religion](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/achaemenid-religion/) describes evidence whose interpretation remains contested. World Egg theology treats the Lie as chosen corruption within creation, never as an equal uncreated principle. Daryam's fire is ordinary created fire tended by human keepers. The dependent Pride Mask later exploiting royal radiance remains a distinct supernatural person.

The Tasar revolt and Xarun's invasion use the political and logistical grammar of the Ionian Revolt and the Greco-Persian Wars. Herodotus remains a literary source with regional interests rather than a transparent transcript. The relevant narrative sequence appears in [Book 5](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0126%3Abook%3D5), [Book 6](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0126%3Abook%3D6), [Book 8](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0126%3Abook%3D8) and [Book 9](https://www.perseus.tufts.edu/hopper/text?doc=Perseus%3Atext%3A1999.01.0126%3Abook%3D9). Encyclopaedia Iranica's accounts of the [Ionian Revolt](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/ionian-revolt/) and [Greek–Persian relations](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/greece-i/) help restore Persian aims and the choices of subject cities. The World Egg conflict has different geography, rulers, institutions and outcomes; Vashar survives 350 BR.

### Traveller histories and guarded social knowledge

Earlier research on Romani and Traveller peoples informs the development of dispersed work, multilingual households, route memory and guarded speech. It supplies no ancient ancestry. Jirahar are an Ark-derived people with their own historical formation. Romani, Irish Traveller, Scottish Traveller, Romanichal and Yenish histories remain distinct real traditions.

This control matters because mobility is neither timeless wandering nor an inherited refusal of settled life. Ancient Jirahar include tower residents, farmers, craftspeople, scholars, carriers and dispersed road households. Persecution, deportation and changing work enlarge mobile institutions at particular times.

## Newcomer-readability clarification, 10 September 2026

The first prose review clarified the material organisation of Mareh bat Sivan's three camps. Extended households held most grain and animals, while each camp maintained a common reserve for guards, sick people and households whose food had failed. Grain borrowed from the reserve was normally repaid after harvest. Pack animals carried water, seed and people who could not walk. These details explain Mareh's authority, the captain's attempted seizure and the cost of preserving transport during shortage.

The stranded servant household is provisionally defined as a hired driver, his wife, their two children and his widowed mother. A broken axle and insufficient water make the decision to continue intelligible. The received tradition remains divided over whether Mareh opposed the departure or gave the deciding order. This is a minimal historical clarification for causal coherence, not a new independent story lead.

The same review corrected summary-table errors in this document. Mikalet is Yedran's wife and Shaqel's daughter; Haviel is a prophetic teacher; Elior is the chief guardian and Maraiah a councillor; Sameth is a musician; Yaelith keeps the surviving cistern; and Ishara is the smith who signals the opening of Khalbar's gate. The companion narratives already held these roles and remain controlling for the proposed biographies.

## Identity and metaphysical rulings

### Human peoples

- The Jirahar are Ark-derived humans whose ancestors emerge from the Near-Glass continuum. Every Jirahar person has a complete Image-bearing human soul.
- Arqeshite, Vashari and older eastern coastal populations are Orphaned human peoples. Their states, religions and magical practices do not alter their human continuity.
- Deportation, intermarriage, adoption, conversion and citizenship create mixed households and political identities without manufacturing or dividing souls.
- The names **Vashari** and **Vesari** designate unrelated peoples. Similar spelling creates no ancestry.

### Offices and supernatural actors

- No Jirahar judge, king, sanctuary keeper or road-house elder inherits the Great Prophet's commission.
- Tovan and Hador possess complete human souls and no Giant continuity. Tovan's requested signs and Hador's vow-strength belong to received cycles whose exact historicity remains open.
- The flame and rain at Haviel's public confrontation and the later response at the Six Women's Gate are bounded signs. The first exposes the court cult in that event; the second answers the rescue at that gate. Neither authenticates Haviel's killing order, Qiryath's rulers or a repeatable relic.
- Haviel is a living pre-Redeemer prophetic teacher, not a manifested saint. His signs arise from particular commissions and leave no active White-Flame object.
- The **Visible Measure** is an Arqeshite royal theology and ceremonial system. The **Crowned Countenance** is a dependent Pride Mask exploiting that system. King, office, image and Mask remain separate.
- The Qahuri **Single Radiance**, Arqeshite **Crowned Countenance** and Vasharan **Witness of Dawn** are separate dependent expressions within Pride's ecology. They do not merge into one historical person, transmit a human soul or constitute a true Idol succession.
- Zimra's court shrine uses a dependent expression of the Crowned Countenance. The Mask neither creates her political aims nor compels Qoram's judgement against Tobel.
- Vashari tended fires are ordinary created fire. The **Witness of Dawn** is a dependent Pride Mask exploiting royal radiance and claims of universal truth.
- Astaroth remains the existing Menhir. Military correspondences disclose particular relations of force and never confer divine approval.
- Local springs, passes, winds and animal-shaped visitors may involve existing Menhir relationships or Faewild court persons. Cultural names do not settle their identity.
- Eryndor possesses a complete human soul. Father Sun's limited response during his Qahuri journey does not make him a Menhir child or authorise conquest. The Crowned Countenance's attempt to inhabit his court imagery remains distinct from his person, royal office, corpse and later romances.
- No new Menhir, Idol Throne, Archmask, Patron, Giant continuity or true Idol succession is proposed.

## Human-coherence chains

### First Qiryath's rise

Bronze-road failure isolates tower settlements → households pool refuge, worship and storage at Qiryath → protectors gain sustained authority → Yedran turns emergency command into kingship → rival households fight over crown and council → his death ends the crown while leaving stronger civic institutions.

### Tovan and Hador

Seasonal raiding exposes the weakness of dispersed villages → Tovan leads a small night attack and recovers captives and grain → he refuses a hereditary title but accepts tribute and political marriages → the ring-standard becomes an illicit source of judgement → his son converts the family's prestige into fratricidal rule. Gedran coercion and Hador's vow-strength then produce a separate cycle → Hador treats private grievance as public deliverance → Gedran rulers exploit and mutilate him → he destroys their hall while killing dependants and captives with the ruling household → the weakened fortress survives until Othan's later coalition.

### Qoram, Zimra and Haviel

Empty military stores make Qoram dependent upon House Keresh → marriage brings grain, soldiers and the Crowned Countenance shrine into his court → Zimra orders false testimony and Qoram confirms the sentence which kills Tobel and transfers his terraces → Haviel publicly confronts the rulers and cult → genuine signs expose the cult, followed by Haviel's order to kill its diviners → Zimra threatens him and Qoram searches the road houses → Haviel returns during siege and a second sign admits abandoned families → later traditions divide responsibility for the miracles and killings.

### Arqeshite conquest

House Ubar needs hostages, tribute and secure western roads → Neshkar demands Qiryath's submission and royal image → Qiryath refuses and Arqesh prepares river and pack supply → siege closes the city in 742 BR → defenders and civilians die; survivors are enslaved or deported → dispersed households preserve practices which later shape Second Qiryath.

### Vasharan restoration

Arqeshite succession divides the empire → Arezan joins provincial, Vashari, Qahuri and Tarkhan interests while Eryndor invades from the western sea → their separate commands defeat the field armies and exploit internal revolt → Nemeru crews and forced workers open routes into Arqesh → both armies massacre, enslave and plunder while capturing the capital → Eryndor dies and his household fractures → Arezan secures the eastern provinces and permits selected returns → Vasharan rule restores roads while creating a new imperial hierarchy.

### Eastern sea wars

Vasharan succession intervention and ship levies anger Tasar → Liora invites Atherian aid → rebels and foreign ships take Avesh and kill a surrendered governor → Xarun reconquers Tasar and prepares punitive war → the great invasion fails when Ilyessan defeat breaks sea supply → Atherian leagues and Vasharan road commands both become stronger and more coercive.

### Balance of 350 BR

Client wars divide the coast → Veyr builds personal commands among ports → Caleran civil war destroys his network and draws Vasharan aid without its central army → Thalion wins the western succession struggle → Orvaz retains a viable eastern empire through limited commitment and negotiation → Atheria begins imperial government beside a surviving peer.

## Recommendation

The package is internally coherent and ready for canon review. Its eastern history now contains two connected achievements without assigning either civilisation a supporting role: Eryndor supplies the Atherian conquest and successor age, while Arezan turns a highland coalition into the Vasharan empire that survives as an eastern peer in 350 BR. Qiryath's return occurs inside their divided settlement and preserves Jirahar life beyond the fortunes of either king.

Recommended disposition for all five companion dossiers: **retain with incorporated reconciliation**. The controlling dates are 702–690 BR for the eastern conquest, 684–c. 650 BR for the Wars of the Inheritance and 514–491 BR for the Vasharan–Atherian sea wars. All material remains exploratory until the user authorises canon promotion.
