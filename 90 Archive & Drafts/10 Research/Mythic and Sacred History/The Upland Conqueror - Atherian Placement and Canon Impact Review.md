# Eryndor III of Ortheia — Atherian Placement and Canon Impact Review

The World Egg has room for an Alexander-shaped ruler separate from Teren Casvilar and Aurelius Thalion. **Eryndor III of Ortheia**, later called the **Far-Road King**, occupies that place around **706–684 BR**. He inherits his father Valeron II's military hegemony, compels the Atherian city leagues, joins Arezan Tiravan's war against Arqesh and carries the conquest into regions which neither ally can govern alone.

His death creates a true Successor age. Caleran recovers authority among the western cities; Arezan establishes the Vasharan Crown of Roads and Dawns; commanders and local houses create mixed maritime and river kingdoms; Qahuri crowns survive foreign garrisons and dynastic marriages. These states shape the political world later inherited by Casvilar and Thalion without turning either imperial founder into Alexander.

This remains an exploratory recommendation. The Package B reconciliation has incorporated Eryndor into its account of Arqesh's fall and the eastern successor age. No canon changes through this document.

## Controlling history

The following points remain fixed:

- The Celestial Compact forms around 800 BR among twelve substantial Atherian cities. Caleran becomes its principal meeting place and later the centre of a republic.
- Arqesh destroys First Qiryath in 742 BR. Its mortal House Ubar and canal empire remain distinct from Giant ancestry and supernatural Masks.
- Arqesh falls around 690 BR. Arezan Tiravan and the Vashari highland houses are essential to that defeat; the Decree of Roads and Houses follows in 684.
- Ancient Qahur is divided after 858 BR and experiences Arqeshite and High-Road intervention without losing its own cities, crowns and river society.
- The Gateway Republic, its wars with Caleran and Qarzeth's destruction belong principally to c. 650–520 BR.
- Teren Casvilar remains the Caesar-like republican conqueror assassinated in 370 BR.
- Aurelius Thalion remains Casvilar's heir and the Augustus-like founder who establishes durable imperial government around 350 BR.
- Vashar remains an independent imperial peer at 350 BR unless later work separately changes that settlement.

The placement must enrich those facts rather than replace them with one conqueror's biography.

Alternative II creates the cleanest historical sequence: heroic maritime societies, city-states, an upland conquest and Successor world, the Caleran overseas republic, Caesar-like civil war and Augustan imperial settlement.

## Ortheia and Valeron II

**Ortheia** lies in the colder mountain country between the older Atherian city world and the lands inherited by later Thalmyria. Its people belong to the broad Atherian cultural sphere through language, marriage, sanctuary participation and service while retaining their own royal houses, upland assemblies and military traditions. They are neither a biological subdivision of later Thalmyrics nor a foreign people who civilise the cities.

Eryndor's father, **Valeron II**, begins as a threatened king among competing valleys. Mines supply metal and pay; forests supply timber; pasture supports horses and pack animals. He brings young aristocrats from subordinate houses into his household, reorganises seasonal levies around a permanent infantry core and employs engineers and city-trained clerks. Marriage joins him to neighbouring crowns. Conquest, bribery and hostage education extend his influence into the Celestial Compact.

The Compact cities retain their councils and public rites beneath this hegemony. Some accept Valeron because he restrains local wars and prepares defence against Arqesh. Others regard his common campaign as tribute enforced by upland soldiers. Caleran supplies money, ships and grain while attempting to preserve its assemblies. Valeron is assassinated before beginning the eastern war, leaving Eryndor, rival relatives and commanders whose first loyalty was to the dead king.

This arrangement gives the conqueror something real to inherit. His army, treasury, league and proposed war exist before his accession. His achievement lies in securing and transforming them.

## Arqesh and the two-principal alliance

The reconciled Package B history gives the war **two principal commands with different bases**.

Arezan brings Vashari highland houses, rebellious Arqeshite governors, knowledge of the road stations, mounted forces and relationships with forced workers. His sister Mithara organises food, marriages and communication among allies who distrust one another. Their objective is to destroy Ubar supremacy and establish a highland-led order over the eastern roads.

Eryndor brings the Compact contingents, siege engineers, a professional infantry core, allied ships and the money to keep them abroad. His first public objective is retaliation for Arqeshite intervention and freedom for the coastal cities. Victory changes that purpose. Possession of the Ubar treasury and royal household encourages him to claim the whole river monarchy and the roads beyond it.

Neither man becomes the other's subordinate. Their treaty divides military responsibilities, captured revenue and appointments. Eryndor marries **Parena Tiravan**, Mithara's adult daughter and Arezan's niece. Mithara remains the senior organiser of the Tiravan estates and allied households. Parena establishes her own position between the two courts and acts for Vashari officers and Arqeshite petitioners within Eryndor's camp.

The fall of Arqesh then retains every cause already developed: Ubar succession conflict, rebellious provinces, Nemeru crews, Qahuri intervention, Tarkhan attacks and labourers opening Khalbar. The foreign army helps make the collapse decisive. It does not cause every rebellion or win every front.

## Campaign character and cost

Eryndor is personally recognisable. He remembers ordinary soldiers, shares dangerous crossings, rides and marches conspicuously, enjoys poetry and argument, drinks heavily and reacts badly to being treated as the beneficiary of his father's work. Affection for companions and fascination with distant societies coexist with an appetite for submission.

His proposed biography includes:

1. **The destroyed city at home.** An allied city revolts after the father's death. Its destruction secures the league and stains every later claim that the eastern expedition represented civic freedom.
2. **The first river victory.** A smaller army defeats a larger Arqeshite command through disciplined infantry, cavalry and the enemy's poor deployment. The royal household escapes or is captured separately.
3. **The harbour siege.** Engineers join an island or fortified headland to the shore, permanently changing the local coastline. Defenders kill envoys or refuse the conqueror's entry to a sanctuary; victory ends in massacre and enslavement.
4. **Qahur and the oracle.** Competing crowns receive the army differently. Grain, boats and guides make the next campaign possible. The conqueror accepts a Qahuri royal title and later presents an ambiguous sign as confirmation of exceptional birth.
5. **The decisive Arqeshite battle.** Arezan and the conqueror coordinate armies without merging their commands. The last Ubar king escapes, is later killed by his own followers and receives royal burial from the victor.
6. **The eastern fortress war.** Local rulers continue resistance after the imperial capital falls. New settlements, hostage marriages, executions and village destruction accompany hard campaigning.
7. **The refused road.** The army declines to continue beyond a major river or exposed pass. This collective refusal defines a human limit which no miracle removes.
8. **The lethal return.** A route chosen for speed, punishment or glory kills soldiers, enslaved carriers, servants, children and animals. The king survives the crossing and later memorials concentrate upon his endurance.

The ruler also requires two intimate crimes. An experienced officer of his father's generation is killed after the officer's son is accused of concealing a plot. A companion who once saved the king is killed by him during a drunken quarrel over the honour due to the father and the conquered peoples. Remorse changes later conduct but restores neither man.

## The mixed court

Arqesh cannot be ruled solely through the conqueror's upland household. He therefore retains canal officials, employs Jirahar and Arqeshite interpreters, accepts Vashari and Qahuri officers and separates military from financial authority in selected provinces. These arrangements are improvised and differ from city to city.

His veterans understand the captured treasury and appointments as rewards for conquest. Eastern houses understand court rank, dress, marriage and royal ceremony as the language through which a ruler becomes legitimate. The conqueror begins wearing Ubar and Vashari elements, admits eastern youths to military training and arranges marriages between senior followers and local houses. Some marriages create enduring families; others are repudiated after his death.

This produces cultural change without presenting the conqueror as the inventor of a superior civilisation. Atherian language and civic forms travel east. River administration, astronomy, luxury craft, cavalry practice and royal ceremony travel west. Enslaved people, displaced artisans, soldiers' households and merchants carry much of that exchange.

## Supernatural pressure

The conqueror remains a human man with a complete human soul. He is not the child of a Menhir, Giant or Idol and never becomes a god.

Three supernatural relationships are sufficient for the first pass:

### The campaign correspondences

Astaroth may answer particular military formations, bridges or siege works through the same narrow correspondences already known in Atherian warfare. The answers provide information about the passage of force. Priests convert repeated victories into claims that the Star Child selected the king to rule the world. The Menhir supplies no moral judgement.

### The Qahuri oracle

A genuine but limited phenomenon associated with Father Sun can occur during the oracle journey: an unexpected passage through the dry country, a repeated celestial alignment or a voice heard by several witnesses. Human priests interpret it through Qahuri royal theology. The king later encourages a claim of starry paternity. The sign proves only the guidance or disclosure actually given; it does not make him a son of the Menhir or authorise conquest.

### The captured royal image

The dependent Pride Mask called the **Crowned Countenance** survives the fall of House Ubar. It attempts to attach its royal imagery and feeding relationships to the conqueror who has taken the treasury and accepted eastern ceremony. Dreams, portraits and court repetition make him appear to be the latest body of one universal king. His own ambition gives the Mask useful material; it does not compel him.

After his death, several Successors claim the same images. The resulting masks, copied court personas and political offices remain separate from the dead man's soul. This offers a strong TES-like mantle dispute without creating a true Idol succession.

## Death and the Wars of the Inheritance

Eryndor dies before an adult child can inherit. The court contains his pregnant wife Parena, an adult royal half-brother able to bear the title but unable to control the army, Arezan and Mithara, senior generals, secretaries controlling royal correspondence and soldiers expecting pay and return.

The first settlement recognises two nominal heirs and distributes commands. It is a way of postponing war rather than solving succession. Armies kill rival officers in the capital. The royal women arrange alliances and order deaths while other commanders claim to protect them. Over the following generation, the nominal kings, widow and child are murdered. No one inherits the whole personal network.

The first settlement produces four principal independent histories:

- **Vashar and the Ubar basin:** Arezan converts his coalition, highland legitimacy and eastern command into the Crown of Roads and Dawns. His Decree of Roads and Houses secures support from restored communities while binding them to the new empire.
- **The upland homeland:** Demeran Kavos takes Ortheia and the western army. Caleran and several cities recover room to act as he destroys the nominal royal heirs and loses control of the old hegemony.
- **The eastern maritime kingdom:** Serevan of Delkos holds islands and harbours through a mixed court. Its ships, coinage and officer families later enter Caleran, Gateway and Nerethan history.
- **Qahur and the southern river approaches:** Neresh of Qemaret controls selected crowns for a generation through garrisons and military marriages. Local houses retain irrigation and temple authority and eventually displace or absorb his followers.
- **The farther east:** one commander establishes a large but thin kingdom beyond the present core geography. Its later fragmentation gives the World Egg room for distant Hellenistic-style cities without extending Atherian sovereignty over the entire world.

These are developed states, not a countdown to Caleran. Their wars create new cities, workshops, libraries, mixed households and trade as well as repeated siege, enslavement and dynastic murder.

## Why Caleran later becomes the Roman centre

The conqueror never creates a durable Atherian empire. His homeland is a monarchy in the colder uplands. Caleran participates through the Compact, supplies ships and money, and endures royal supervision without becoming the court's natural capital.

After his death, Caleran uses its assembly, stores and maritime relationships to organise resistance to the western regent. It restores Compact meetings, admits displaced officers and acquires ships from commanders unable to pay their crews. The city can present this recovery as liberation while retaining land and revenues seized during the conquest.

The Wars of the Inheritance give Caleran military experience and an eastern network. The Gateway Wars beginning around 650 then make it a sustained overseas republic. This sequence explains why a city previously dominated by an upland king later becomes the Roman-shaped power of the setting.

## Distinction from Casvilar and Thalion

| Figure | Political origin | Principal expansion | Relationship to government | Death and legacy |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Eryndor III of Ortheia** | hereditary mountain king inheriting Valeron II's army and league | helps destroy Arqesh and campaigns into the far east | personal monarchy improvised across older states | dies young without settlement; Successors create several kingdoms |
| **Teren Casvilar** | indebted aristocrat and magistrate of a mature republic | conquers inhabited southern Folk districts | concentrates republican offices and patronage | assassinated by intimates; leaves disputed civic and royal inheritances |
| **Aurelius Thalion** | Casvilar's designated heir within civil war | wins succession war and annexes Sarqen | turns military supremacy into durable imperial administration | dies after a long reign with an adopted adult successor |

The three careers form a useful progression. The first shows that personal world conquest cannot govern itself. The second shows a republic captured by one commander's obligations. The third shows the moral and material price of making that concentration durable.

## Effect upon Package B

Package B remains coherent after the following incorporated revisions:

1. **Arqesh's fall:** replace a single Arezan-led conquest with the two-principal alliance and the existing internal revolts.
2. **Arezan:** retain him as indispensable Vashari coalition leader and founder of the High-Road empire; make him the strongest regional Successor rather than the sole destroyer of Arqesh.
3. **Mithara and Parena:** retain Mithara as Arezan's sister and senior organiser; make her daughter Parena Eryndor's wife and a political actor in the mixed court.
4. **The last Ubar king:** Kassur III remains distinct from Neshkar II, whose sack of Qiryath occurred fifty-two years earlier.
5. **The Decree of 684:** place it within the first succession settlement. It becomes both restoration policy and Arezan's claim to legitimate authority after the conqueror's death.
6. **Second Qiryath:** preserve return, contested property and consecration around 660. Returned households now live through the Successor wars as well as the transfer from Arqesh to Vashar.
7. **Vasharan government:** retain the road commands and varied provinces. Add institutions and marriages inherited from the joint conquest.
8. **Eastern Sea Wars:** preserve the 514–491 conflict. Its navies and mixed coastal courts inherit technologies and claims from the Successor period without becoming the same war.
9. **Balance of 350:** preserve Orvaz II and independent Vashar. Thalion confronts a state with three centuries of post-conquest history.

The five Package B reader histories now incorporate these revisions. Their shared chronology and identities are controlled by [[Package B - Final Reconciliation and Canon Readiness Review]].

## Effect upon the exploratory corpus

- **Arezan and the High-Road material** receive revisions rather than duplicate figures.
- **The Champion and the Unrecognised Son** can belong to a Successor house, giving the Vashari heroic cycle a specific political world.
- **The Road Without Milestones** remains a separate imperial-survey tradition. Conquest roads may supply an earlier contrast but do not become the same event.
- **The Oar-King without a Country** remains an older harbour founder. Later singers may falsely enrol him among the conqueror's admirals.
- **The Bent-Key Fellowship** remains a city-state cycle and should not become the conqueror's staff.
- **The Crownless Company** remains a separate First-Kings company rather than the origin of the upland dynasty.
- **Idol succession leads** remain protected. The Crowned Countenance's transfer of attention is Mask behaviour within one guilty sovereignty, not a new Regnant.
- **Erased persons and protected Weirds** receive no explanation through lost Successor records.
- **Alexander-Romance material** is developed in [[The Alexander Romance - Sky, Sea, Speaking Trees and the Limits of Conquest]]. The recommended arrangement gives the famous adventures separate historical kernels inside existing campaigns: a siege bell, a Sky-Lion observation frame, a speaking Root-Mind grove, a local sealed pass and a failed journey to a restorative Egg White seep. Later cultures enlarge and recombine them. They never become one secret extra expedition, a divine commission or proof that every romance is factual.

The effect upon Qahur, Delkos, House Nerethan and Samyra is assessed separately in [[After Alexander - Ptolemaic Egypt, Samyra and the Successor Inheritance]]. Its recommended route leaves Qahur under its own ancient crowns while placing a maritime Successor court at Delkos. Nerethan's later officer-house inherits that court culture and joins it to Esharati and junior Qahuri ancestry at Sarqen.

## Recommended expanded canon pass

If the placement is approved, the Package B canon pass should expand into four stages:

1. **Research reconciliation:** completed across the five Package B histories and their final canon-readiness review.
2. **Historical selection:** Ortheia, Valeron II, Eryndor III, Olyssar, Terys, Kassur III, Parena Tiravan and four principal Successors have been selected for the proposed history.
3. **Canonical integration:** revise the existing canon destinations above and create substantial articles for the upland kingdom, conqueror, eastern campaign and Wars of the Inheritance. Add a separate article for the destroyed city only if its later history warrants one.
4. **Legendary integration:** establish which cultures tell which conqueror romances. Their existence may become canon while their supernatural truth remains disputed.

This is larger than the earlier Package B canon delivery because it changes the shared Atherian–eastern bridge. Integrating the change once across both shelves will be cleaner than promoting Package B first and repairing it afterwards.

## Proposed canon boundary

### Suitable for authorial fact after approval

- An Atherian-speaking upland royal house imposes hegemony upon part of the city world before the Gateway Wars.
- Its young king and Arezan Tiravan become the two principal external leaders in Arqesh's final defeat.
- The king accepts eastern royal forms, employs local officials and carries the campaign beyond Arqesh.
- He destroys resisting communities, kills intimates, loses people during the return and dies without an adult successor.
- The succession kills the nominal heirs and produces several states.
- Caleran, Vashar, selected Qahuri crowns and eastern maritime cities emerge from the settlement with different inheritances.

### Suitable as received history with literal details open

- The king's taming of a dangerous mount or court beast.
- The exact words of the Qahuri oracle and the claim of starry paternity.
- A meeting with the dead Ubar king in a dream.
- Flights, undersea descents, speaking trees, a gate against monsters and a search for the water of life.
- The king's final words and claims about who should inherit.

### Authorial controls

- The conqueror is wholly human and possesses no Giant continuity.
- His soul is distinct from every later office, portrait, Mask, copied persona and Patron claimant.
- The oracle grants no divine paternity or authority to conquer.
- The Crowned Countenance remains a dependent Pride Mask and undergoes no true Idol succession.
- Arezan, Mithara and eastern communities retain independent motives and agency.
- Atherian cultural exchange follows conquest through mixed households and existing societies; it is not civilisation bestowed upon empty lands.

## Matters left open

- The exact location and name of Ortheia's royal seat.
- The identity and conduct of the king's mother.
- The selection of Eryndor's closest companion and the officer killed by the king.
- The exact nature of the Qahuri sign and the human oracle's words.
- The cause of the conqueror's death.
- The number, borders and complete dynasties of the Successor states.
- The literal truth of every later supernatural romance.
- All protected Weirds, true Idol successions and events after 1360 AR.

## Recommendation

Adopt the reconciled Eryndor architecture for canon review. It supplies the missing Macedonian and Hellenistic layer between the Atherian city world and the Caleran overseas republic. Arqesh falls through armies and internal revolts whose participants plausibly divide its inheritance, Vashar acquires a dramatic foundation without becoming a renamed Persia, and Qiryath returns during a dangerous succession rather than through an uncomplicated change of empire.

The next action after approval is a joint Package B canon pass across the eastern and Atherian shelves.
