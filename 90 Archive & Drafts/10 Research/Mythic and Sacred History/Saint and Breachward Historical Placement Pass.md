# Saint and Breachward Historical Placement Pass

## Purpose and Placement Rule

This pass places six roots selected in [[Saints, Threshold Orders, and Heavenly Campaigns Review]] into the existing chronology. It does not promote them into canon or add a second historical matrix. Each root attaches to an existing bed and supplies one missing institution, local history, or disputed sacred memory.

The six are intentionally not one lineage. Practices travel through copied rules, prisoners, refugees, defectors, manuals, marriages, captured archives, military necessity, and independent rediscovery. No secret order connects the Underground prison road, the Long Frontier wardens, the High-Noon city cult, the common saint of the Split, and the present investigation.

# I. Underground Centuries

## SB-P01 — The Three-Lock Prison Road

- **Lane / scale / carrier:** human, institution, road, and saint-memory; a local prisoner-support network whose practices travel farther than its membership; three-knotted cords, medicine lists, ransom marks, burial names, prison letters, and visitor ledgers.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 145–205 AR during uneven Atherian persecution; one provincial prison corridor connecting an inland tribunal city, a river transfer station, and an execution or labor site. The final province remains open so it does not silently absorb every Underground tradition.
- **Humble origin:** a prison laundress, an apothecary's assistant, a cart repairer, two relatives of prisoners, a bribable guard, and a clerk begin moving food, medicines, letters, names, and bodies through three successive locks. Some are Christians, some are paid, some are related to prisoners, and at least one joins because the prison market threatens an ordinary livelihood.
- **Scriptural pattern:** Acts' prisons, households, letters, minor co-workers, and deliverances; Matthew 25's identification with prisoners; the road and burial antecedents already governing B3-06 and B3-07.
- **Primary comparative grammar:** the received Life of Anastasia as prisoner visitor, healer, ransom worker, clandestine correspondent, victim of domestic confinement, witness to a prisoner massacre, and martyr. Ignatian prisoner roads and early martyr-company correspondence supply secondary documentary structure.
- **Historical pressure:** judicial torture, sexual coercion, confiscation, ransom fraud, forced labor, execution, corpse denial, informers, prison disease, and families deciding whether to shelter people who recanted under pain.
- **Hard event:** officials order one prison cleared in a night. Many prisoners are executed before the Road reaches them. The network saves records, several children or dependants, some wounded prisoners, and a small number scheduled for transfer; it does not rescue everyone. One member betrays a lock under torture, and the received story later assigns the betrayal to a simpler villain.
- **World Egg torque:** repeated truthful identification and freely offered care establish a narrow correspondence across the three sites. At one later crisis, a door, cord, dream, or familiar voice may answer in a way eventually attributed to a saint. The historical rescue functions without that claim: bribes, keys, medicine, carts, lies told to persecutors, and human courage do most of the work.
- **Continuity / classification:** every worker, prisoner, betrayer, victim, and later claimant remains a distinct `human-soul`; the road procedure may acquire a limited `infrastructure-office`; prison residue, copied saint-persona, relic claim, and any true manifestation remain separately classified.
- **Institutional afterlife:** burial fraternities copy its name rules; hospital visitors copy its medicine baskets; later wardens study its lock marks; smugglers use the same signs for profit. None is automatically the original organization continued.
- **1360 AR trace:** three-knotted cords remain attached to prison and hospice petitions; two churches preserve incompatible lists of the dead; a secular ransom law requires a second witness to a transferred prisoner's name; an alleged founder is commemorated in one province and denounced as an accomplice in another.
- **Competing interpretations:** first prison order, criminal bribery ring, martyr household, women's clandestine ministry, municipal relief route, saint-founded confraternity, or several unrelated roads compressed into one Life.
- **Authorially known:** one real network crossed three custody sites; many prisoners died; at least one betrayal occurred under torture; the work preserved persons, names, evidence, and bodies without determining judgement or mechanically creating saints.
- **Open:** province, people, faiths, massacre, prisoners saved, betrayer, true saint status, manifestation, relics, relationship to the Empty-Purse Road and Burial Hands, and every ultimate fate.
- **Source-fidelity ruling:** **received source:** Acts and the received Orthodox Life of Anastasia. **Borrowed element:** clandestine prison visitation, medicine, correspondence, ransom, massacre, bodily danger, and later intercession. **Original hard edge:** confinement, beating, sexual threat, mass execution, starvation, attempted drowning, burning, and martyrdom. **Current state:** `intact at parity`; only the acts needed by this prison-road element transfer. **Historical layer:** earthly network, disputed later Life, and possible later manifestation remain separate.
- **Protected boundaries:** no martyrdom mechanism creates sainthood; no prison key opens Hell; no unburied person is damned; no rescue turns guilty prisoners innocent; no new apostle or universal Underground command is introduced.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — preserve the three-site custody road, incomplete rescue, massacre, betrayal under torture, mixed motives, and later saint-memory dispute while keeping every person, site, manifestation, and institutional descent provisional. Promotion remains locked.

# II. Long Frontier

## SB-P02 — The Rule of Five Closures

- **Lane / scale / carrier:** institution, land, archive, and local encounter; one mixed-border breachward tradition, not a universal order; closure manuals, survivor rolls, stone marks, water-court testimony, liturgical watches, and repair oaths.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 680–760 AR in a Velian–Zanthir–Thalmyric frontier corridor where church houses, Covenant courts, mixed settlements, caravan law, older sites, and imperial military interests overlap.
- **Humble origin:** masons, a well clerk, a Velian translator, two caravan scouts, a monastic watcher, a former raider, a physician, local householders, and soldiers improvise a response after officials misclassify a thin space and lose the first containment party.
- **Scriptural pattern:** Nehemiah's builders working under threat, Acts' multilingual council and local co-workers, Pauline distinction of gifts, and the obligation to test spirits without despising truthful witness.
- **Primary comparative grammar:** Riftwarden-style boundary expertise transformed into a regional craft tradition; Orthodox watchfulness and frontier monastic service supply its moral discipline.
- **Historical pressure:** caravan raids, old Giant and Atherian ruins, Patron routes, Menhir jurisdictions, displaced villages, cross-confessional suspicion, military demands for rapid passage, and authorities tempted to call every unfamiliar rite sorcery.
- **The five closures:** later manuals distinguish provisional actions rather than five universal spells: close passage; close feeding; close unlawful custody; close recurrence; and close the human practice reopening the wound. A site may require only one, several, or none. The fifth is often politically hardest because it requires ending a mine, prison, tax, cult, war-work, or profitable route.
- **Hard event:** the founding crisis includes two culpable failures. The first party seals a visible opening while leaving an inhabited cellar or refuge beyond it; people suffocate, burn, or are taken by the pressure. A later commander reopens the route to recover valuable equipment, killing rescuers and spreading the crisis into another settlement. The Rule develops from named failure, not triumphant mastery.
- **World Egg torque:** every closure begins with classification. Shell pressure, Patron anchor, Idol feeder, Menhir compact, Astral current, damaged soul-route, and Abyssal contradiction cannot be treated as one enemy. The Rule requires local witnesses and records what a successful closure leaves unpaid or displaced.
- **Continuity / classification:** members remain `human-soul` persons; tools and ritual offices may bear `infrastructure-office`; Menhir court-law, Patron contract, Idol pattern, Shell manifestation, true angel, saint, and road mechanism remain distinct.
- **Institutional politics:** Church authorities dislike Covenant testimony; Covenant judges distrust unregistered saint claims; soldiers resent civilian vetoes; merchants suppress dangerous route findings. Nevertheless, no group can consistently close sites alone.
- **1360 AR trace:** Long Watch houses preserve evacuation and witness sections; Lantern schools preserve classification diagrams; some Velian jurists preserve the fifth closure as a rule against profitable recurrence; Qahirid breach-courts cite related but differently ordered maxims. These are inheritances and convergences, not proof of one surviving order.
- **Competing interpretations:** first breachward order, mixed heretical school, Covenant theft of Church craft, Church appropriation of frontier law, emergency manual assembled from unrelated failures, or lost universal technology.
- **Authorially known:** a mixed group formulated a durable five-part decision grammar after lethal failure. It closed a bounded crisis and improved later practice. It did not make the Long Frontier safe, unite the faiths, or found every modern order.
- **Open:** corridor, sites, people, precise pressures, dead, surviving manuals, whether one member was later recognized as a saint, and which present institutions knowingly descend from the practice.
- **Source-fidelity ruling:** **received source:** Acts, Pauline gifts, Orthodox discernment, and Pathfinder Riftwarden structure. **Borrowed element:** specialized boundary defence, coalition expertise, corrupted or failed crusader frontier, and institutional afterlife. **Original hard edge:** incursions, suspected refugees, purges, failed wards, torture, and destroyed settlements. **Current state:** `intact at parity`; failure kills particular people and produces rules. **Historical layer:** authorial frontier practice surrounded by competing institutional chronicles.
- **Protected boundaries:** no wardstone analogue, universal anti-planar spell, forced ecumenism, saintly institution, or permanent heavenly garrison; no culture owns breach expertise by blood.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — preserve the five-part regional decision grammar, mixed expertise, named lethal failures, political fifth closure, and scattered later inheritances while rejecting a universal spell system or single surviving order. Promotion remains locked.

## SB-P03R — The School of the Open Cinder

- **Lane / scale / carrier:** Sinner institution, technical archive, route market, and survivor memory; one corrupt school among several possible breach-trafficking traditions; burned diagrams, branded test subjects, military contracts, confiscated instruments, and successor arguments.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 760–840 AR in the later Long Frontier, operating through several fortified towns, abandoned estates, caravan refuges, and Egg White access markets rather than possessing one public capital.
- **Humble origin:** a warlock engineer, a refugee guide, an indebted translator, a physician, several smugglers, and one or more dismissed boundary workers independently bring partial techniques to a siege, quarantine, or state closure. They open a short-lived escape route and save real people. Patron-backed buyers then turn the coalition's improvised craft into a market. No founder needs to belong to the Rule of Five Closures tradition.
- **Scriptural pattern:** Simon Magus attempting to purchase sacred authority; Acts 16 and 19 on profitable supernatural practice, possession, public disorder, and the destruction or surrender of costly techniques; the warning that technical efficacy does not establish righteousness.
- **Primary comparative grammar:** Blackfire Adept breach exploitation as the dark institutional mirror of Riftwarden defence.
- **Historical pressure:** refugees denied lawful routes, state monopolies on passage, military siegecraft, Patron tolls, warlock labor, ransom, censorship, and the desire to reach closed places without accepting their jurisdiction.
- **Hard event:** the school purchases or receives prisoners, debtors, captured wardens, and unwanted refugees as test subjects. It tortures wardens for route knowledge, sends people through unstable openings, and records survivorship as technical success. Some are fused with anchor-work, lost in transit, returned mutilated, or delivered into slavery. Teachers justify this with the lives saved in the founding escape.
- **World Egg torque:** there is no one Black Ember power. Cells exploit different mechanisms: Patron tethers, Shell leakage, Idol-fed desire for escape, Hell contracts, stolen Menhir correspondences, or deliberately aggravated thin spaces. Their shared doctrine is that every boundary is property held by someone too weak or hypocritical to deserve it. Some later cells steal fragments of the Five Closures; others predate those thefts, reject the Rule, or never encounter it.
- **Internal conflict:** some members want only paid transit; some serve Sinner theology; some are military contractors; some preserve genuine rescue practice; some defect and become vital witnesses for breachward orders. Purges against defectors kill families as well as practitioners.
- **Continuity / classification:** teachers and victims remain `human-soul`; instruments and procedures may carry `infrastructure-office`; a Patron anchor, devil mantle, bound spirit, Idol Mask, and Abyssal wound remain separate even when one laboratory uses several.
- **1360 AR trace:** censored diagrams survive in military engineering schools; certain blackened doorway marks are still used by smugglers; a Lantern archive holds testimony extracted under torture and cannot use it without confronting provenance; descendants of survivors demand names and reparations from institutions that secretly bought the research.
- **Competing interpretations:** necessary rescue school corrupted by rulers, pure Sinner cult, neutral technology persecuted by the Church, Patron front, military intelligence project, or name imposed on unrelated breach crimes.
- **Authorially known:** a real founding escape saved people; the later school knowingly tortured and experimented on unwilling persons; public authorities and private buyers enabled it; not every student or successor shared one allegiance; and the school is neither the direct child nor metaphysical opposite of SB-P02.
- **Open:** founders, rescue, sites, victims, sponsors, cells, Patron involvement, manuals, defectors, closure, and which later schools falsely claim or deny descent.
- **Source-fidelity ruling:** **received source:** Acts 8, Acts 16, Acts 19, and Blackfire Adept structural material. **Borrowed element:** purchased sacred technique, profitable supernatural practice, hostile boundary expertise, captive wardens, and corrupted rescue. **Original hard edge:** enslavement, possession, torture, experimentation, massacre risk, and destructive public reaction. **Current state:** `intact at parity`. **Historical layer:** authorial criminal institution, hostile and apologetic chronicles, later professional myth.
- **Protected boundaries:** no magical profession is inherently damned; no victim becomes a nonhuman species through injury; no efficient technique is automatically evil; no persecution becomes righteous merely because this school was guilty.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` — retain the founding rescue, guilty experimentation, rival cells, sponsors, survivors, and technical afterlife while severing required descent from the Rule of Five Closures and any universal Black-Ember lineage. Promotion remains locked.

# III. Great Split and Its Aftermath

## SB-P04 — The Saint of the Two Ferries

- **Lane / scale / carrier:** saint-memory, road, relic, institution, and possible heavenly commission; one pre-Split local saint later claimed by two separated cities or jurisdictions; ferry rules, hospice ledgers, two icons, disputed bones, and hostile miracle collections.
- **Era / region / culture:** earthly life before 987 AR; the principal dispute c. 996–1035 AR across one Eclessian-facing and one Thalmyric-facing river city or paired ferry district. It intersects the Last Common Road without requiring company membership.
- **Humble origin:** the earthly person begins as a ferryman, hospice keeper, widow managing stores, minor deacon, bridge worker, interpreter, or former prisoner who repeatedly protects travelers and enemy dead at a disputed crossing.
- **Scriptural pattern:** Acts 15 and the Pauline refusal to divide holy allegiance into party ownership; Good Samaritan and prisoner-road hospitality; divided kingdom and rival sanctuary traditions as warnings rather than equivalences.
- **Primary comparative grammar:** shared pre-schism saints, contested relic translation, common patronage, and rival military-apparition traditions.
- **Historical pressure:** the Lamp Controversy, rival jurisdictions, relic passports, confiscated hospices, mixed marriages, shrine seizure, beatings, imprisonment, mob killing, and the need of both churches to make an older saint speak exclusively for later claims.
- **Hard event:** after one ferry shrine is seized, a mob kills a host or custodian and throws bones or a relic into the road or river. Retaliation imprisons clergy and expels households on the opposite bank. Rival apparitions then bless seizure and revenge. At least one is counterfeit; neither community can establish every claim through spectacle.
- **World Egg torque:** if the genuine saint manifests, the commission concerns particular prisoners, travelers, the violated dead, or a breach at the crossing. The saint may protect people on both banks and command true angels against one supernatural intrusion while refusing to declare either church the exclusive owner. This action would not resolve the Kindling Question or negate either hierarchy's real case.
- **Continuity / classification:** one saint remains one redeemed `human-soul`; same-named dead, relics, local residue, two icon offices, Patron personas, Envy Masks, Borrowed Saints, and true manifestations remain separate.
- **Institutional afterlife:** each church writes a complete partisan Life; ferry households preserve a shorter work-rule; one city calls the saint a defender, the other a liberator; later polemicists erase the saint's care for enemy prisoners.
- **1360 AR trace:** two icons contain one matching correction under different overpainting; both banks maintain related guest duties under opposed names; rival relics may both be authentic but require proof that they belong to the same person; one annual crossing is conducted without either hierarchy's banners.
- **Competing interpretations:** common saint, two same-named saints, divided saint, Patron double, Envy forgery, political compromise legend, or ferrymen's attempt to protect trade.
- **Authorially known:** one historical pre-Split holy person served the crossing; real violence and appropriation followed the Split; later counterfeits exploited the name. Whether and when the redeemed saint truly manifested remains open pending an individual dossier.
- **Open:** person, cities, river, earthly life, judgement, relics, counterfeit forms, genuine manifestations, relationship to the Last Common Road and Lamp, and present cults.
- **Source-fidelity ruling:** **received source:** New Testament anti-factional teaching and Orthodox shared-saint reception. **Borrowed element:** one saint received by divided communities, relic conflict, local intercession, and partisan military memory. **Original hard edge:** schism violence, imprisonment, desecration, expulsion, and killing committed by people claiming holy loyalty. **Current state:** `intact at parity`. **Historical layer:** earthly biography, rival Lives, relic claims, apparitions, and folklore remain independently classified.
- **Protected boundaries:** no missed message or saint causes or solves the Split; no saint becomes two souls or a neutral third church; no relic proves universal primacy; no event explains the Bells of the Split.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — preserve the one pre-Split earthly person, divided reception, real post-Split violence, multiple counterfeits, related ferry duties, and possible bounded commission without resolving doctrine or fixing the manifestation. Promotion remains locked.

# IV. High Noon

## SB-P05R — The Saint Above the Harbor Wall

- **Lane / scale / carrier:** city, saint-memory, warfare, relic politics, and civic law; one preferred Sermoran or other Sestaran harbor placement during High Noon; wall stations, dock rolls, a refused relic casket, veterans' songs, enemy testimony, and a feast of repair.
- **Era / region / culture:** earthly life several generations earlier; principal siege and apparition tradition c. 1120–1180 AR in one Sestaran harbor city. Sermora is the preferred nursery location because sea-lane politics and later appropriation offer strong pressure, but final selection remains open.
- **Humble origin:** the earthly person is a foreign-born shipwright, chain tender, dock laborer, interpreter, minor harbor officer, or prison visitor who helps open the harbor chain to a refugee flotilla during an earlier blockade, fire, persecution, or killing storm. The city council had ordered the entrance closed.
- **Scriptural pattern:** prophetic confrontation within civic office; protection of the vulnerable under siege; the warning that a city cannot purchase righteousness through sacrifice, wealth, or sacred reputation.
- **Primary comparative grammar:** Demetrios of Thessaloniki as martyr, civic patron, defender on the walls, locally retained relic, and figure later appropriated by several political cultures.
- **Historical pressure:** Sestaran rivalry, naval blockade, food shortage, mercenary contracts, prisoner exchange, harbor chains, displaced foreigners, competing citizenship law, and a council that later wants the rescuer it condemned to function as proof of civic sovereignty.
- **Hard event:** the city arrests and executes the rescuer for disobedience, treason, contagion risk, smuggling, or the deaths caused when desperate vessels force entry behind the opened chain. Some refugees survive and some defenders or harbor workers die; neither side's later memory is clean. Generations afterward, attackers kill civilians and defenders fight lethally on the same walls during a new siege. After a terrifying apparition or received vision helps break a supernatural assault or opens an evacuation, city soldiers murder prisoners, foreign residents, or people blamed for the breach. The received saint's rebuke becomes as important as the defence, though official songs suppress both the execution and the rebuke.
- **World Egg torque:** if the executed rescuer was truly redeemed and later commissioned, the manifestation may terrify aggressors, kill an active supernatural commander, break a Patron or Idol siege instrument, and hold one wall or harbor passage. The saint defends persons within the city that killed them without declaring the old sentence righteous. The commission does not sink an innocent fleet, choose the city's rulers, or bless reprisals.
- **Continuity / classification:** saint, mortal defenders, prisoners, witnesses, relic, city office, legend-persona, Patron protector, Idol counterfeit, and any true angels remain separate. City affection does not create or feed the saint.
- **Political afterlife:** councils suppress the opening of the chain and claim the saint guaranteed closed-wall independence; harbor families remember a refugee rescuer; admirals claim offensive wars continue the later defence; merchants sell wall fragments; minority families preserve the rebuke and names of murdered prisoners; enemy chronicles admit a terrifying defender while denying the city's innocence.
- **1360 AR trace:** one wall circuit cannot be demolished without public unrest; during the feast the harbor chain must be lowered for one refugee, prisoner-exchange, or foreign relief vessel before it is raised for the civic procession; older songs preserve the chain tender as traitor; two military orders dispute whether the rebuke applies to them.
- **Competing interpretations:** genuine city saint, Patron defender under a saint's face, exaggerated veteran memory, natural storm, Sestaran propaganda, or several sieges merged into one cycle.
- **Authorially known:** a real person opened the chain, a real refugee entry and civic execution followed, and a later siege involved civilian suffering, lethal defence, and retaliatory atrocity. The executed rescuer's genuine sainthood and later commission remain plausible but unconfirmed until the earthly person, judgement, source layers, and manifestation are separately reviewed.
- **Open:** city, rescuer, refugees, reasons for closure, casualties, trial and execution, judgement, later siege, aggressors, supernatural instrument, manifestation, prisoners, rebuke, feast, and political reception.
- **Source-fidelity ruling:** **received source:** Orthodox Life and cult of Demetrios. **Borrowed element:** bodily execution, city defence, terrifying wall apparition, and political appropriation. **Original hard edge:** persecution, killing, martyrdom, siege terror, combat, threatened massacre, and contested enemy identities. **Current state:** `intact at parity`, transformed through a foreign refugee rescuer executed by the defended city rather than another civic governor-martyr. **Historical layer:** earthly rescue and execution, received Life, siege chronicle, apparition, and later civic folklore remain distinct.
- **Protected boundaries:** do not copy Thessaloniki, Demetrios, Slavic enemies, feast, icon, or exact martyrdom; do not make a population inherently hostile; do not make city survival proof of every civic policy.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` — retain the High-Noon city-patron bed but ground it in the city's execution of a refugee rescuer who opened the harbor chain, thereby changing the inciting act, relationship to civic power, and later moral wound while preserving siege severity. Promotion remains locked.

# V. Reformation and Present

## SB-P06 — The Closed House Hearing

- **Lane / scale / carrier:** present institutional investigation; one former independent prison-house associated with the Inquisitors of Saint Malveris and reviewed by Lantern personnel, secular officers, survivors, and church courts; sealed registers, torture rooms, confiscated relics, victim petitions, and disputed apparition testimony.
- **Era / region / culture:** abuses accumulate during the Reform Wars and their aftermath; the hearing begins c. 1357–1360 AR in one Eclessian jurisdiction. The house is a single chapter or predecessor institution, not the whole Inquisition or Church.
- **Humble origin:** laundresses, guards, junior clerks, released prisoners, a physician, gravediggers, and relatives preserve evidence that senior officers ordered coerced confessions, secret detention, property seizure, and dangerous testing of suspected relics or breaches.
- **Scriptural pattern:** Revelation's calls for particular churches to repent; Zacchaeus-like restitution; Susanna and false witness; New Testament correction that protects a body by naming its wrongdoing rather than pretending it never belonged.
- **Primary comparative grammar:** founder saint returning to correct an order; failed righteous institution; contested apparition investigation.
- **Historical pressure:** post-Reform oversight, curtailed independent prisons, rival confessions, political canonization, forged warrants, cult investigations, fear of real Idol or Patron infiltration, and authorities who argue that exposing crimes will strengthen enemies of the faith.
- **Hard event:** detainees were tortured, some died in custody or after release, families lost property, and at least one suspect was executed on coerced or fabricated evidence. A dangerous object or true hostile power may also have been present; its reality does not excuse the treatment of prisoners or make every confession true.
- **Present pressure:** several witnesses report that Saint Malveris—or a figure using the title—appeared inside the sealed house, named victims, condemned the order's practices, and refused the senior inquisitor's demand for an exclusive sign. The claimant may be a genuine manifested saint, Patron persona, Borrowed Saint, residue, conspiracy, or mixed event. The Forward Rule protects the investigation and outcome.
- **World Egg torque:** every claimant has partial access to real evidence. A genuine saint would defend persons, truth, repentance, and restitution rather than institutional reputation. An Envy or Pride counterfeit might accurately expose crimes while demanding destruction of the Church, worship, revenge against descendants, or transfer of the confiscated objects. Accuracy alone cannot classify the apparition.
- **Continuity / classification:** historical Saint Malveris, if genuine, remains one `human-soul`; each apparition, relic, record, victim, office-holder, object, Mask, Patron persona, residue, and infrastructure-office must be tested separately.
- **Institutional stakes:** the Inquisitors risk dissolution, reform, secular prosecution, or schism; Lantern investigators risk being used as political executioners; survivors disagree over disclosure, compensation, punishment, and continued handling of genuinely dangerous objects.
- **1360 AR trace:** the sealed house, living survivors, missing registers, impounded objects, unauthorized former chapters, and a hearing whose testimony can change present institutions. No outcome is prewritten.
- **Competing interpretations:** founder's correction, reformist forgery, Idol revenge, Patron seizure attempt, Freeflame propaganda, institutional cover-up, or several manifestations by different powers using the same face.
- **Authorially known:** the house committed real abuses and concealed evidence. The supernatural classification of the alleged correction, present verdict, institutional survival, restitution, and every participant's fate remain open.
- **Open:** city, chapter, victims, perpetrators, objects, Saint Malveris's actual biography and judgement, apparitions, investigators, legal venue, reparations, and consequences.
- **Source-fidelity ruling:** **received source:** New Testament church correction, Susanna, Orthodox saintly correction grammar, and crusader-order failure. **Borrowed element:** a holy patron confronting an institution that claims ownership of the patron's memory. **Original hard edge:** torture, false accusation, execution, confiscation, institutional loyalty, and costly repentance. **Current state:** `intact at parity`. **Historical layer:** documented abuse, disputed apparition testimony, church inquiry, public propaganda, and possible genuine commission remain separate.
- **Protected boundaries:** no apparition is authenticated in advance; no true saint can fall; no abuse proves the entire Church false; no dangerous prisoner or object excuses torture; no present outcome is fixed outside play or later authorial choice.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — preserve the documented prison-house abuses and current correction claim while leaving apparition classification, verdict, restitution, institutional survival, and all present outcomes under the Forward Rule. Promotion remains locked.

# VI. Cross-Card Boundaries

## No master lineage

- The Three-Lock Prison Road may influence later prison visitation and naming practices without founding the Rule of Five Closures.
- Open Cinder cells may steal selected Rule material without making the School its descendant, mirror, or single enemy.
- The Saint of the Two Ferries and Saint Above the Harbor Wall may share devotional motifs without being the same person or two Masks of one saint.
- The Closed House may own texts descended from any older tradition, but possession does not establish institutional continuity or rightful inheritance.
- A recurring lock, lamp, wall, ferry, cinder, or fivefold sign may be ordinary convergence, copied practice, propaganda, residue, office, or counterfeit. Repetition never proves one hidden organization.

## Heavenly scarcity

Of the six placements, only SB-P04 and SB-P05 presently permit a possible historical manifested saint, and neither manifestation is fixed. SB-P01 can generate a later saint claim; SB-P02 can contain a member later recognized as a saint; SB-P03 contains no presumed saint; SB-P06 presents a current disputed claimant under the Forward Rule.

This keeps true posthumous intervention rare while allowing saintly memory, patronage, institutions, counterfeits, relic politics, and petitions to remain historically pervasive.

## Violence and consequence

The six placements preserve:

- executed prisoners and a betrayal under torture;
- civilians killed by a culpably failed seal and a profit-driven reopening;
- prisoners and refugees tortured or used as breach experiments;
- mob killing, imprisonment, shrine seizure, and relic desecration during the Split;
- siege death followed by retaliatory murder;
- institutional torture, false execution, confiscation, and living survivors in the present.

None of these acts exists merely to make a saint impressive. Victims leave names, claims, relatives, law, property disputes, testimony, graves, institutional obligations, and resistance to celebratory retelling.

# VII. Review Gates

1. **Source-layer gate:** earthly history, received Life, liturgical commemoration, relic claim, apparition, icon, legend and propaganda remain distinguishable.
2. **Continuity gate:** every human soul remains distinct from saint, angel, road office, relic, residue, Patron persona, Mask, Sinner technique and institution.
3. **Commission gate:** a true saint or angel acts only under a bounded commission; no order summons or inherits that authority.
4. **Institution gate:** practices can transmit without a secret organization, bloodline, universal founder, or uninterrupted charter.
5. **Severity gate:** torture, execution, sexual coercion, starvation, experimentation, massacre, siege and retaliation remain direct where load-bearing without invented excess.
6. **Repentance gate:** correction requires confession, changed conduct and material repair where possible; it cannot resurrect victims or compel reconciliation.
7. **Church gate:** real corruption does not turn either church into an Idol; genuine sanctity does not make an institution incapable of atrocity.
8. **Interfaith gate:** mixed-border cooperation is not covert conversion, syncretic metaphysical equivalence, or proof that revelation is merely local custom.
9. **Weird gate:** no seed explains the Bells of the Split, Quiet Ninth, Third Nail, Averted Legion, or another registered Weird.
10. **Forward gate:** SB-P06 fixes only past abuses and present investigative pressure; apparition identity, judgement, reform, survival, and every participant's outcome remain open.
11. **Company gate:** none is a new signature adventuring company or cause of an age.
12. **Promotion gate:** every card has now received one disposition, but no placement enters [[The Historical Seed Garden]] without a separate promotion decision and the outstanding local details named in the register.
