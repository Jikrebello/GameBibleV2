# Vathra in the Upper Air — Sky Giants, Flying Cities and the Grounding of the Tarkhan

## Purpose and conclusion

This study revises the proposed form of [[Vathra and the Tarkhan Orphaned World - The Walking Court and the Great Unyoking|Vathra, the Walking Court]]. Vathra works more powerfully as a **network of aerial Giant courts supported by a much larger civilisation on the ground**. Its royal households live in drifting palaces and seasonally moving cities. Human subjects maintain wells, herds, quarries, landing grounds, anchor towers, storehouses and caravan roads beneath them. The court appears to wander because its centres of government literally travel over this ground economy.

The strongest comparison is Pathfinder's **Shory Empire**, whose aeromancers founded several flying cities and whose crashed city of Kho remains a dangerous, partly functioning ruin.[^1] Dungeons & Dragons adds the surviving cloud castle, the ranked Giant court and the catastrophic flying empire.[^2] Eberron's Xen'drik supplies a related history of several Giant civilisations destroyed amid slave revolt, war and overwhelming magical intervention.[^3] Older narrative traditions provide different images: Aeolus's enclosed floating island and captured winds, Puṣpaka as a flying palace and sign of sovereignty, Solomon's wind-borne carpet, and the lofty people of ʿĀd destroyed by a furious wind.[^4]

The recommended Vathran model takes one important feature from each source without reproducing any complete setting or myth:

- several aerial capitals fail in different ways;
- rank is expressed through altitude, wealth and control of movement;
- city-scale flight depends upon labour and infrastructure distributed across the ground;
- woven flying platforms are carried through prepared wind routes rather than possessing unlimited independent flight;
- civil war, human revolt and the wider Great Unbinding break different parts of the system at different times;
- some cities crash, some are grounded deliberately, and a very small number continue to drift without reliable control;
- the Tarkhan descend from the human people who kept the ground system alive, not from the Giant royal houses.

This arrangement also strengthens the established camel history. Camels already carry water, food, fodder, textiles and replacement parts between Vathra's widely separated anchor estates. When the cities fall, the aerial system and its restricted horse stocks disappear. The camel roads, wells, herding knowledge and portable households survive. The later First Catch remains a genuine horse revolution after 905 AR.

Everything below is author-only research. It proposes a replacement for parts of the earlier Walking Court model, but changes no canon.

## Controlling World Egg boundaries

Several established rulings determine what Vathra can become.

### Giants remain genealogical beings

World Egg does not contain separate cloud-giant and storm-giant species. The names can describe dynastic cultures, political ranks, magical disciplines or hostile human classifications. They cannot replace the existing descent model. A Titan-Firstborn possesses `titan-continuity`; later High and Dynastic Giants possess `giant-continuity`; giant-blooded humans possess complete human souls. Blood may weaken by degree while continuity remains categorical.

This allows Vathran houses to cultivate very different bodies and powers. An upper house may breed for storm-sight, great stature and resistance to thin air. Another may value illusion, wealth and cloud concealment. Both remain branches within Vathra's actual Giant genealogies.

### Zephyraxis supplies sky pressure, not royal legitimacy

[[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/20 Third Circle/Zephyraxis|Zephyraxis]] governs atmosphere, altitude, storm architecture, migration and the movement of weather. Its bargains recognise ballast, exposure, carriage and destination. Vathra can therefore exploit particular Zephyraxis correspondences to lift structures, divert winds or preserve breathable corridors. None of these effects makes the Giant rulers rightful owners of the sky.

[[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Hecatara|Hecatara]] remains relevant to routes, gates and displaced distance. Her correspondences join seasonal sky roads to specific anchor sites. [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth|Astaroth]] may appear in the structural knowledge that keeps a lifted mass from tearing itself apart. These are three distinct sovereignties and sets of consequences. Vathran engineers can combine their effects through separate works and bargains; they cannot restore the ancient permanent Trium or manufacture a new Menhir.

### The sky cities remain Material

The Vathran cities travel through the Material sky. They are not Elemental Shell settlements, Egg White demiplanes or lunar vessels. A city that later attempts bodily escape into the Egg White would undergo a separate event with separate evidence. No such escape is required for the aerial civilisation to work.

### Human causation remains central

The Great Unbinding supplies exceptional pressure, but Vathra falls through identifiable decisions. Giant rulers start wars, sacrifice dependent districts and fight over dwindling routes. Human workers cut moorings, sabotage lift works, seize wells and abandon stations. Heavenly severance may end one unlawful recurrence or bind one Titan-Firstborn under its commission. It does not perform the whole revolt or make every city fall at once.

## Comparative fantasy research

### The Shory Empire: several cities and several endings

Pathfinder's Shory are the closest match for the civilisation being sought. Paizo's published timeline dates Kho, the first legendary Shory flying city, to 2323 years before Absalom's foundation.[^1] Later material treats Kho as a crashed city whose magical engines continue to sputter and attract scavengers. Shory ruins also preserve recoverable technological wonders for neighbouring people.[^5]

The value of the Shory comparison lies in its plurality. A flying civilisation need not possess one capital, one engine and one final disaster. Individual cities can depend upon different rulers, summoned powers and maintenance traditions. Disease, internal war, monsters and technical failure can destroy them separately. The resulting ruins have distinct histories. A wreck can continue to alter its surroundings because some parts of the old system still work.

Vathra should borrow this distributed failure. One Vathran city falls in battle. Another is brought down by its own ground crews. A third remains in the air after losing the ability to choose its route. A fourth sacrifices inhabited districts to escape a storm. Their shared civilisation disappears even though no single moment destroys all Vathran works.

The Shory should not determine Vathra's people, visual culture or magical mechanism. Vathra belongs to World Egg's Giant Age, northern dry country and Menhir metaphysics. It also possesses a much sharper master–subject divide because its aerial splendour rests upon human labour below.

### D&D Giant courts: altitude as inherited rank

Dungeons & Dragons places storm and cloud giants at the top of the ordning, a religious and social hierarchy among Giant kinds. Storm giants are associated with prophecy and isolation; cloud giants with wealth, status and deception. Cloud-giant status is measured through accumulated wealth, and the highest families may occupy surviving ancient castles that still drift across the sky. The art of building those castles vanished with Ostoria.[^2]

World Egg can use the social grammar while refusing the species list. Vathra's highest dynasties claim the **storm rank** because they occupy the thinnest air, read distant weather and control access to the principal lift works. Wealthy cadet families claim the **cloud rank**, maintaining lower palaces, tribute fleets and concealed approaches. These are Vathran ranks with disputed genealogical rules. A rich cloud house may attempt to rise; a declining storm house may execute rivals rather than admit its loss of control.

The surviving cloud castle supplies an excellent present-day image. One or two Vathran structures may still drift in 1360 AR. Their continued flight proves that part of the old apparatus persists. It does not prove that their inhabitants survived, that they can steer, or that entering them is safe.

### Netheril: the human cost beneath magical abundance

Forgotten Realms' Netheril provides a different model: an empire of flying cities at the height of magical excess, divided between a privileged high society and an underclass below. Current D&D material deliberately pairs the flying empire with political division, folly, catastrophic collapse and areas where gravity or magic remains unstable.[^6]

The useful translation is infrastructural. A city capable of flight still needs food, water, labour, repair and a means of managing waste. If the ruling city levitates while dependent communities remain on the ground, the spectacle makes inequality physically visible. The city takes tribute upward and sends refuse, broken objects, condemned workers and violence downward.

Vathra should not receive a Netherese central engine or an analogue of Karsus's apotheosis. Its lift comes from distributed Vathran works engaging existing Menhir pressures. This makes failure more interesting. Breaking one anchor changes a route. Killing one choir interrupts breathable air. Burning one textile house grounds the couriers. The civilisation can unravel without a single magical switch.

### Xen'drik: Giant plurality, slave revolt and wounded geography

Eberron's Xen'drik combines several Giant civilisations, enslaved peoples, a revolt and magical intervention vast enough to damage geography and movement. Keith Baker's account emphasises that the dragons did not conquer the giants conventionally. They shattered population centres and imposed curses that warped conduct, travel, time and space.[^3]

World Egg already possesses its own form of this sequence: Giant civil war, human revolt, the Prophet's liberation and heavenly severance during the Great Unbinding. The Eberron comparison supports the simultaneous presence of these causes. It also supports a landscape in which later societies inherit distorted roads and dangerous ruins from imperial destruction.

The differences must remain clear. The Phoenix's commission is bounded. It does not exterminate Vathra's human subjects or curse an entire continent. Vathran route failures arise from broken bargains, damaged works and accumulated prices, not an imported Traveller's Curse. Tarkhan survival is a human history rather than the residue of a magical punishment.

### Dragonlance and Skyreach: cities as weapons

Dragonlance's flying citadels turn lifted fortresses into strategic weapons. Their value lies in movement, surprise, aerial command and the terror of bringing an army over walls that were designed to face the ground. D&D's Skyreach Castle similarly presents a cloud giant's flying fortress as a mobile military and political asset.[^7]

This suggests a Vathran distinction between a flying city and a flying war-platform. Some Vathran works carry royal households, gardens and workshops. Others are deliberately stripped fortresses: barracks, landing courts, prisons, stone-dropping galleries and weather weapons. Their crews can raid wells, seize hostages and move before a human coalition gathers. A damaged war-platform may also become the most dangerous kind of uncontrolled ruin because its weapons remain active after its command lineage dies.

## Mythic and literary grammars

### Aeolus and the contained winds

In *Odyssey* 10, Aeolus inhabits a floating island enclosed by an unbreakable wall. He confines the courses of the winds in a hide and gives them to Odysseus. The crew, suspecting hidden wealth, opens the bag and releases the storm that drives the ship away from home.[^4]

The Vathran use is specific. Wind can be gathered, assigned and carried, yet the assignment remains vulnerable to mistrust and mishandling. A Vathran wind-house may store a seasonal current in enormous layered skins or woven chambers. Its servants know which knots govern release. A court that opens a reserve in the wrong place can ruin a harvest hundreds of miles away or drive a city past its landing ground.

The enclosed household of Aeolus also supports the intimate politics of an aerial court. A floating palace can be both a marvel and a prison, with dynastic marriage, guarded children and servants who cannot leave without falling.

### Puṣpaka and the palace as sovereignty

The Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* presents Puṣpaka as a splendid flying palace made for Kubera, seized by Rāvaṇa and later used by Rāma. Danielle Feller's study shows how the vehicle functions as transport, coveted treasure and a visible sign of divine or quasi-divine authority. Later textual development also personifies it and associates it with bird form.[^8]

Vathra can use the relationship between palace, owner and political claim. Possessing a city capable of answering thought or voice makes a ruler appear cosmically authorised. The appearance may be misleading. A city can respond to an office, a hereditary mark, a living court person, a coerced operator or a repeated command pattern. Later worshippers may mistake that response for proof that the ruler became divine.

The source also warns against reducing a flying palace to transport. It is architecture, court and royal emblem at once. Vathra's largest cities should carry audience halls, gardens, animal courts, burial chambers and shrines as well as engines and docks.

This material comes from a living Hindu textual tradition and must remain specifically attributed to the Vālmīki *Rāmāyaṇa* and its later receptions. World Egg should borrow the political function of the flying palace, not transplant its sacred identities or treat the text as evidence of ancient aircraft.

### Solomon and the carpet carried by powers

The familiar self-propelled magic carpet developed through a long narrative history. A recent study traces the carpet in Prince Aḥmad and Perī Bānū back through Muslim stories of Solomon. In several earlier forms the vehicle is an ordinary or specially made platform carried by winds or by spirits under Solomon's command; its independent magical property develops later.[^9]

That distinction suits Vathra. A **Vathran sky-carpet is not a universally enchanted rug**. It is a carefully woven airframe whose knots, conductive threads and stiffened edges allow it to be taken up by a prepared pressure route. Large carpets carry freight, animals or soldiers. Small ones serve messengers, surveyors and wealthy travellers. Away from a Vathran route, most become heavy textiles. A damaged one may rise without steering or drag its passengers towards the destination for which it was last tied.

Human weaving houses become indispensable to the empire. They spin hair, plant fibre, metal wire and treated membrane into flight cloth. Giants control the sky rights while human women and men possess much of the practical knowledge. During the Great Unyoking, the weavers can cut command knots, falsify destination borders and turn royal carpets into funeral shrouds, tents or camel cloth.

### ʿĀd, Iram and destruction by wind

The Qurʾān remembers ʿĀd in connection with Iram and lofty pillars, and elsewhere describes the people of ʿĀd as destroyed by a furious wind. Later Arabic reception develops Iram in several directions: cursed city, lost paradise, invisible city and object of historical speculation.[^10]

The Vathran borrowing should remain narrow and attributed. Lofty rulers rely upon monumental height and believe their strength secures them against consequence; the wind that supported their claim becomes the medium of their ruin. A Vathran descent song may remember towers falling like uprooted trunks. The World Egg account does not reproduce the prophet Hūd, identify Vathra with ʿĀd or claim a Qurʾānic judgement for the fictional civilisation.

The later invisible-city tradition also offers a useful afterlife for one lost Vathran court. People may see its lights moving above a dust storm or hear chains in a clear sky. Whether this is the original city, a recurrent route image, a copied court procession or ordinary folklore remains open.

## How an aerial civilisation functions

The greatest danger in using flying cities is allowing spectacle to replace society. Vathra requires farms, water, fuel, craft knowledge, reproduction, sanitation and movement at several scales. The proposed system answers those needs through three inhabited levels.

### Crown-cities

A small number of **crown-cities** remain aloft for years at a time. They contain Giant dynasties, hostage households, elite human servants, high workshops, shrines, gardens, cisterns and weather apparatus. Their permanent population is limited by food and water. They descend to prepared moorings for major repairs, dynastic ceremonies and the transfer of heavy tribute.

The largest crown-city may carry thousands of people, most of them human. It still depends upon a ground population many times larger. Its grandeur therefore rests on deliberate political concealment: official art shows the Giant palace among clouds and removes the lines, towers, camel trains and labour camps that keep it alive.

### Rising estates and war-platforms

**Rising estates** spend part of the year on the ground. A royal household lifts its residence, storehouses and chosen workshops for migration, war or summer relief, then settles them upon another prepared base. These estates preserve the original “Walking Court” rhythm while making its movement extraordinary.

**War-platforms** are smaller and harsher. They carry soldiers, stone shot, captive guides and landing crews. Some transport a limited number of elite horses for rapid deployment after landing. Others release storms, fire or debris against ground settlements. They can dominate fixed defences, though wind, weight and supply constrain their range.

### Tether towns and road camps

Most Vathran subjects live in **tether towns**, wells, farms, mines, pasture camps and caravan stations. Anchor towers secure descending cities. Cisterns hold water for transfer. Fields and herds feed populations above. Quarries produce stone that can replace damaged ballast and fabric. Roads connect sites that the sky routes cannot reach safely.

These communities are politically varied. Some profit from royal visits and control the trade generated by landings. Others endure requisition, conscription and child-taking. A distant well may see the crown only once in a generation while paying annual tribute to local Giant or human officers.

The three levels permit real mobility without placing an implausible entire empire in the air. They also explain why control of ground infrastructure remains a decisive weapon.

## The proposed Vathran sky hierarchy

Vathran rank should combine ancestry, altitude and service rather than import the D&D ordning intact.

### The storm houses

The oldest and most powerful dynasties occupy crown-cities at the highest safe altitude. They cultivate weather sight, long hearing, lightning craft and resistance to cold or thin air. Their rulers claim that distance from the ground demonstrates purity of descent. Storm houses arbitrate sky roads, approve city liftings and demand dynastic hostages from lower courts.

Their isolation has practical costs. They know ground life through reports and tribute. Failed harvests become numbers until a city descends hungry. Prophecy becomes politically dangerous because rival interpreters can accuse a ruler of steering against the ordained weather.

### The cloud houses

Cloud houses govern tribute, diplomacy, concealment and most commerce. Their cities travel lower and visit human stations more often. They display wealth in coloured awnings, suspended gardens, bells, glass and captive animals. Mist screens hide their approach. Court entertainments prize riddles, impersonation and costly deception.

These families often possess more usable wealth than the storm houses while lacking the oldest descent claims. Competition between the two becomes one engine of the final civil wars. Cloud rulers accuse their superiors of wasting tribute on height and prophecy. Storm rulers accuse wealthy cadets of buying rank from human subjects.

### The tether houses

Younger Giant branches and giant-blooded human dynasties govern landing fields, wells and pasture districts. Court ideology places them below the aerial houses because their power touches the ground. In practice they control food, repair and communication. Some tether houses become brutal extractors. Others acquire enduring relationships with human communities and later support revolt against their own kin.

This layer requires precise continuity records. A High Giant, a Dynastic Giant and a giant-blooded human may belong to one political house while remaining different kinds of person. The law may pretend otherwise; authorial classification cannot.

### Human sky households

Human pilots, carpet weavers, wind-keepers, animal handlers, healers, cooks, gardeners, water carriers, scribes and builders live aboard the cities. Some serve by contract or inherited privilege. Many are captives or hereditary dependants. A few households gain influence because no Giant can replace their work quickly.

Their position produces both collaboration and resistance. An honoured pilot may profit from raids that enslave other humans. A captive engineer may save a city full of children while planning its later grounding. The Great Unyoking grows through these compromised relationships rather than a morally simple separation between wicked rulers and untouched victims.

## Vathran flight and its limits

The proposed mechanism has four parts. None works alone at city scale.

### 1. Lift bodies

Vathran builders create enormous hollow foundations from light stone, giant bone, wood, metal lattice and sealed chambers. Their form distributes stress through the city. Astaroth-aligned structural knowledge reveals where the mass will break, though it supplies no judgement about whom the structure serves.

### 2. Pressure mantles

Large woven mantles catch and circulate Zephyraxis pressure. They resemble layered sails, tents and carpets more than wings. Their knots define load, direction and permissible exposure. Each city has to shed, take on or redistribute ballast as conditions change. A city that refuses the price may lift too high, drift, or dump weight through its lower gates.

### 3. Route anchors

Hecatara correspondences join a city to a chain of destinations. Towers, mounds and marked clearings provide points of arrival and departure. Shortened distance accumulates elsewhere. Repeated journeys can lengthen a nearby ground road, misplace a seasonal stream or make one approach return travellers to the place they left.

### 4. Human operation

Choirs call pressure changes, pilots read storm layers, tether crews manage descent, weavers repair mantles, and caravans place food and water at the next station. Giant blood permits rulers to survive conditions that kill ordinary crews. It does not remove the need for crews.

These limits keep Vathra mystical without making it arbitrary. A city can be magnificent, terrifying and fallible. Every movement has preparation, workers and a cost displaced somewhere else.

## Carpets, cloaks and lesser flight

The Vathran flying carpet should be common enough to define the civilisation and rare enough to matter.

### Forms

- **Courier cloths** carry one or two people between a city and its tether field.
- **Tribute carpets** carry sealed chests, food baskets, water skins and animals.
- **War carpets** move archers, kidnappers or boarding parties across a short prepared route.
- **Processional carpets** display a ruler above a crowd while remaining tied to concealed guide lines.
- **Funeral cloths** carry a Giant body or divided Titanic fragment to an aerial tomb.

### Operation

The textile contains no free and limitless power. It must be caught by a current opened for a destination, accompanied by a trained caller or launched beneath an active city. Some high royal pieces carry an individual court person or other established intelligence; those cases require separate classification. Most carpets are objects.

This makes theft dangerous. A stolen carpet may retain the destination of its former owner. It can deliver a fugitive to the royal prison, rise until its riders cannot breathe, or stop when it leaves the prepared wind. A human rebel who understands the knots is more dangerous than a thief who merely possesses the object.

### Afterlife

Very few complete carpets survive the Unbinding. Cut fragments become tent panels, burial covers, camel saddles and marriage hangings. Some rise briefly during particular weather. Later Tarkhan stories call every fine ancestral textile a piece of the sky, but only material testing and repeatable behaviour can distinguish an old pressure mantle from ordinary cloth or a newer fraud.

## Camels beneath the cities

The aerial revision strengthens rather than removes the older Vathran herd economy. A city consumes supplies at a rate no carpet fleet can meet cheaply. The empire therefore moves most bulk goods by ground caravan.

Dromedaries became historically important because their tolerance of heat, aridity and water loss made long desert transport practical. They carried people and goods across the Arabian incense routes and supported pastoral households through transport, milk, meat and fibre.[^11] Modern genetic work also shows how later camel populations spread along caravan networks.[^12] These examples offer economic grammar rather than a date to be copied into World Egg.

Vathran camels perform five essential tasks:

1. carry water and grain to landing fields;
2. move ore, timber and woven mantles between specialist districts;
3. follow a drifting city when its next descent cannot be predicted exactly;
4. evacuate ground households after a failed landing or raid;
5. maintain trade after the royal sky route passes elsewhere.

Horses occupy a narrower elite role. Vathra keeps valuable riding and chariot lines at selected upland estates and aboard a few military platforms. They need protected fodder, water, handlers and breeding records. Civil war destroys or scatters these concentrated herds. Some animals die in crashes; others are eaten during sieges, seized by neighbouring crowns or fail to breed in the harsher open country.

Camels survive because their population and knowledge are dispersed among human households across the ground network. The fall of the cities removes their royal customers, but does not remove the wells, routes or daily uses that sustained camel keeping. Early Tarkhan civilisation therefore becomes more visibly camel-based after the Great Unyoking. The First Catch many centuries later introduces abundant war-capable horses, Atherian tack and remount organisation into societies that already possess sophisticated animal medicine and mobile government.

## The Great Unyoking as an aerial civil war

The existing Great Unyoking should remain a human-led revolt inside Vathra's wider dynastic collapse. The aerial model gives the name three literal meanings: animals are released from royal harness, dependent towns sever their obligations, and ground crews cut the physical yokes holding cities to their landing system.

### 1. The closing of the sky roads

During the wider Closing-Court crisis, familiar wind routes become costly and unreliable. Storm houses demand more ballast, stored food and human operators. Cloud houses conceal shortages while competing for lower routes. Several cities arrive late or miss their anchor stations entirely. Ground communities that prepared tribute for one court are stripped again when a rival arrives.

### 2. The war of altitude

The royal succession fails. Competing Giant houses claim the highest city and authority over the route calendar. One side cuts rain from an opponent's tether country. Another attacks a pressure mantle while the city carries noncombatants. War-platforms bombard anchor towers and drop burning wreckage onto towns. Cloud houses use false beacons to draw rival cities into bad air.

The fighting kills Giants and humans together. High and Dynastic Giants ordinarily die bodily when their cities fall. A Titan-Firstborn can be scattered or bound but requires heavenly severance for permanent ending. No account of a fallen founder should blur that distinction.

### 3. Revolt on the ground

Human drivers, herd women, weavers, pilots and captive smiths coordinate across tether stations. They release camels and breeding animals, hide water, cut command knots and kill overseers. Some pilots bring their cities down onto survivable ground. Others abandon royal passengers in the upper air. Several rebels also loot subject towns, murder collaborators and settle old feuds under cover of liberation.

One royal reprisal should remain especially hard. A city ordered to descend refuses the weight of its dependent ward. Its rulers cut loose houses, prisoners, animals and workers while families are still inside them. The discarded district kills people on the ground as well as those thrown from the city. This episode resembles the pressure behind the separate [[First Unknown Histories - Six Recoverable Lore Capsules#UH-01 — The Caravan That Cast Off Its Wards|Caravan That Cast Off Its Wards]], but it is not the same court, place, escape or evidence chain.

### 4. Heavenly severance

The commissioned Phoenix intervenes only where Vathran rulers attempt an unlawful recurrence, Titanic reassembly or war-brood continuation. Its fire may expose a false immortal, break one mechanism that repeatedly restores a Giant commander or bind a distributed Titan away from its fragments. It does not steer rebel armies, choose a Tarkhan ruler or destroy all flight.

### 5. The last descents

Once the route calendar and anchor network fail, each city faces its own decision. Some rulers land and bargain. Some keep fighting. Some throw away people and stores to remain aloft. Some attempt a distant route and never return. Vathra ends as a government because no house can reconnect court, food, movement and obedience across the whole northern system.

## A plural fate for the sky cities

The exact number and names of Vathra's crown-cities should wait for Package C's geography and dynastic work. The history benefits from at least six distinct outcomes.

| Fate | Historical event | Lasting consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **The battlefield fall** | Rival cities fight for one high route. A damaged mantle tears and a war-city strikes a tether town. | A vast sloping ruin, fused stone and a human burial landscape beneath Giant wreckage. |
| **The chosen grounding** | Human pilots and one Giant defector bring a city down across prepared fields before its rulers can flee. | Survivors strip it for cisterns, metal and shelter; later towns grow around parts of its streets. |
| **The cast-off ward** | A royal court saves its core by discarding an inhabited lower district. | Two separated ruins preserve opposed memories of the same night. |
| **The route loop** | A city enters a Hecatara path after its destination anchor has been destroyed. | It returns at long intervals to the same empty stretch of sky, weathered further on each appearance. |
| **The blind drifter** | Its lift remains active while steering and breathable management fail. | The city still wanders above the distant north. Its lights, bells or falling debris appear without a predictable route. |
| **The vanished crown** | One court attempts a forbidden escape or distant crossing during the final war. | No confirmed wreck exists. Later Patrons, copied ancestors and ruins all claim its identity. |

Only the first three need become ordinary fixed history in the initial canon pass. The route-loop and blind drifter are strong 1360 hooks whose exact paths should remain open. The vanished crown should remain protected until the ancient escaped-Giant actor slots are reviewed.

## The grounded Tarkhan inheritance

The fall of Vathra does not reduce its human survivors to blank primitives. It forces a change in the scale and organisation of their civilisation.

### What survives

- camel herds, small stock and the water knowledge of caravan households;
- wind reading, seasonal astronomy and the memory of aerial routes;
- textile engineering, now used in tents, sails, saddles, stretchers and signal cloths;
- foster kinship connecting distant anchor towns;
- portable assemblies, oath-spears and witnessed gifts;
- medical knowledge developed for altitude sickness, exposure, falls and animal injury;
- humour and games that invert the old hierarchy, including the child's unyoking game already proposed;
- an aversion in some communities to permanent roofs, which recall the threat of falling masonry and royal confinement.

### What disappears

- city-scale lift construction;
- coordinated route anchors across the northern sky;
- concentrated royal horse studs and their command bureaucracy;
- Giant-controlled rain allocation;
- long-distance carpet fleets;
- the ruling archive linking tribute, hostages and foster claims.

### What changes meaning

The sky ceases to be the visible location of government. Human communities begin to treat open sky as freedom from the descending court, while retaining fear of lights and shadows above. Later Tarkhan faith gathers several atmospheric experiences into **Kokh, the Blue Eternal**. Kokh expresses human religious interpretation and is not Zephyraxis under another name.

Vathran objects also change meaning. A royal carpet becomes a tent over a freed household. A severed tether becomes a marriage rope. A piece of fallen city becomes a well curb. These inheritances give Tarkhan culture an ancient connection to the sky without making the people secretly Giant or restoring Vathra as their lost golden age.

## Proposed chronology

| Date band | Vathran development | Human experience |
|---|---|---|
| before c. 2450 BR | early Giant courts learn to lift small estates along prepared pressure routes | human quarry, weaving, herding and water communities are drawn into the anchor system |
| c. 2450–2350 BR | several crown-cities and war-platforms form a confederated aerial dominion | tether towns prosper unevenly while tribute, captivity and child-taking expand |
| c. 2350–2300 BR | storm and cloud houses compete over altitude, succession and route ownership | requisitions rise; wells and storehouses are militarised; covert human networks spread |
| c. 2300–2260 BR | Great Unbinding, dynastic civil war and Great Unyoking destroy the common system | human crews revolt; cities crash or descend; massacres, reprisals and mass displacement follow |
| c. 2260–2165 BR | surviving Giant courts disappear, die or withdraw into isolated ruins | Orphaned communities salvage cities, restore wells and organise around surviving herds |
| c. 2165–1900 BR | no aerial Vathran state remains | western camel circuits and eastern hunting, herding, cart and sled societies develop separately |
| after c. 1900 BR | Vathra survives through ruins, feared skies and contradictory royal memories | related populations gradually become identifiable Tarkhan societies |
| 905–920 AR | no revival of Vathra accompanies the First Catch | eastern Tarkhan capture Atherian remounts and create a genuinely new horse economy |

The dates before c. 2300 BR remain broad. Package C should determine how long mature Vathra needs to shape wells, trade and subject identities without turning it into another Imperial Horizon.

## Collision review

1. **Vathra remains a lesser Giant crown.** Its spectacle and reach do not create a sixth Imperial Horizon.
2. **Vathra is not Nythalum.** Nythalum seeks physical lunar Ascension. Vathra rules through the Material atmosphere and ground routes.
3. **Vathra is not a native Air-Shell civilisation.** The Elemental Shell remains sterile and nonpersonal without constructed refuges.
4. **Vathran ranks are not species.** “Storm house” and “cloud house” describe political and magical traditions inside existing Giant genealogies.
5. **The Tarkhan remain Orphaned humans.** Their ancestors include subjects, neighbours, collaborators and rebels; they do not inherit Giant continuity as a people.
6. **The First Catch remains after 905 AR.** Vathra's restricted horses disappear with the royal system. Camels and mixed ground transport dominate the ancient Tarkhan world.
7. **The Great Unyoking is not the whole Unbinding.** It is one regional human revolt entangled with Giant civil war and wider heavenly action.
8. **The cast-off ward is not UH-01.** The events share a cruel decision grammar but have different courts, locations, mechanisms, evidence and consequences.
9. **A blind drifter proves no surviving dynasty.** An empty city, copied operator, trapped court person or automatic route can maintain limited motion.
10. **No crash explains a protected Weird.** The Glass, Bird-Marked Sites and other protected mysteries retain their existing boundaries.

## Recommended Package C direction

The next Package C development should adopt the following broad historical facts for review:

1. Vathra consists of several aerial crown-cities, seasonal rising estates, war-platforms and a much larger ground network.
2. Storm and cloud are Vathran dynastic ranks and magical cultures, not Giant species.
3. City flight combines physical construction, Zephyraxis pressure, Hecatara routes, separate structural knowledge and indispensable human labour.
4. Vathran carpets are woven platforms carried through prepared wind routes. Most lose flight when the network fails.
5. Human subjects already possess a mature camel economy because they provision anchor sites across the northern dry belt.
6. The Great Unyoking attacks animals, political obligations and physical city moorings at once.
7. Giant civil war produces deliberate bombardment, false beacons, cut rain, discarded wards and mass-casualty crashes.
8. Several cities receive distinct fates. At least one is grounded by human action, at least one crashes, and at least one remains a possible uncontrolled drifter.
9. Ground survivors preserve craft, wells, camels, route knowledge and portable government while losing city flight and royal horse systems.
10. The later Tarkhan remember the sky as both ancestral terror and freedom from rulers above.

The following details should remain open until the complete Package C account can test them against geography and neighbouring histories:

- the number and names of crown-cities;
- the exact Vathran royal genealogy;
- whether the proposed founder is the Hecatara-linked Titan-Firstborn;
- the city that carried the final sovereign;
- whether any Vathran court escaped bodily into the Egg White;
- the route and present location of an uncontrolled city;
- which grounded ruins become later Tarkhan settlements;
- which object, if any, still supports repeatable carpet flight in 1360 AR.

## Research assessment

The aerial model fits the established setting better than the earlier image of a wholly terrestrial royal caravan. It preserves everything useful in the Walking Court—seasonal government, herds, moving workshops, hostages, tribute, fosterage and the Great Unyoking—while giving those elements a more distinctive cause. The old “rings” become the levels beneath and around the cities rather than one enormous train.

It also gives Package C a strong historical centre. The Tarkhan begin among people who knew the sky as an occupied political territory. Their later camel roads are the durable half of a broken air-and-ground economy. Their horse revolution comes much later and therefore feels like a second mastery of speed, created by humans on the ground rather than inherited from Giant rulers.
