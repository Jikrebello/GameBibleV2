# Package E — First Historical Draft to 350 BR

> [!warning] Author-only first pass
> This is **not canon** and changes no public chronology, map, people or article. It joins the selected Package E decisions into a readable historical account stopping at **350 BR**. The underlying [[Package E - Comparative Synthesis and Canon Readiness Questions|Elmaric and Vorthara decision register]] and [[Atlantis Azlant Absalom and the Drowned-City Problem|drowned-city decision register]] control where this draft compresses detail. Existing Packages A–D and the current historical atlas control dates and neighbouring peoples. **Selected direction** is distinguished below from **connective interpretation** and **open promotion gates**. Later merchant republics, the mature Atherian Empire and the present Sestaran Concord are afterlives, not institutions smuggled into this period.

[[Package E - Local Place Dossiers to 350 BR]] breaks the major island-sites, archipelago areas and Vortharan neighbourhoods into shorter author-only review units. Its working place labels do not add approved names or a map.

## The historical shape

Package E concerns **several related but politically separate island peoples**, the mixed harbours around them and the refuge city at the Black Spire. There was no ancient Elmaric nation, no universal “sea peoples” invasion and no single maritime republic from the Giant age onward. The islands became a world through surviving households, short crossings, food dependence, kinship, captivity, ritual meeting places and wars in which neighbouring powers sought particular ports rather than whole archipelagos. Vorthara followed a separate highland trajectory: a human refuge inside and around an avoided Spire grew into a market for shelter, labour and dangerous expertise. These histories meet through travellers, captives, grain, clients and later Atherian power, not through a common founding state.

## Before there was an Elmaric world

The late Giant age did not leave an empty sea for later settlers. Human island communities already knew their cisterns, grazing ground, reefs, landings and burial places. Some lived under Giant demands or within a Giant-linked maritime economy; others survived beyond the reliable reach of any imperial household. The Five Imperial Horizons were not an exhaustive map of power, and a Giant claim over a harbour did not make every fishing island an administered province. These local populations are the principal **Orphaned** roots of the later Elmaric family, not children of a lost pan-island empire.

One exceptional colonial project belongs to this older age. Nythalum founded an inner-sea harbour among its wider Moon Harbour relationships. Giant-blooded colonists and imported human stock conquered existing human inhabitants, occupied the best water and harbour sites and subordinated older institutions. Native-descended communities nevertheless persisted in dependent villages, marginal islands, service households and contested sacred places. As the colony accumulated wealth, its dynastic houses called their rising polity a *sister* or successor to Nythalum. That was a political claim: it did not make the colony as ancient as its founder or give it Nythalum's lunar and crystal-memory programme.

Its alternative power was **living marine infrastructure**. Earlier human reef husbandry, appropriated by colonial rulers, met Giant fleshcraft. Coral, shell, sponge, kelp and altered animals grew harbour walls, freshwater membranes, buoyancy systems, signals and living vessels. The dynastic houses divided control over these systems while depending upon human cultivation and repair. Their governing ideology praised bloodcraft and treated the organisms as equipment even when they could suffer. The city was therefore technologically strange in a different way from Nythalum: its apparent mastery depended upon organisms, ecology and labour it neither fully understood nor valued.

The Black Spire was another pre-Atherian anomaly, but its original function remains unknown. Its Giant-selective emanation made close approach dangerous to Giants and strongly expressed Giant-blooded people while ordinary human fugitives could inhabit accessible spaces. Escaped slaves and other outsiders sheltered inside or against it. Their paths, plots, water places and warning posts spread onto neighbouring ridges. **This is the beginning of continuous habitation, not the founding of the later factional city** and not proof that the Spire was built as a prison or sanctuary.

## Collapse without an empty aftermath

The Great Unbinding damaged the Nythalum-founded harbour amid earthquakes, supply failure and rebellion. The senior dynasty tried to absorb rival control lineages and, knowing that the living foundations were wounded, ordered a forced deep migration to save its palace and chosen dependants. Contrary commands, contracting biological structures and destabilised ground dragged much of the city down. The revolt of human labour communities and native-descended inhabitants did not originate the disaster; those groups fought against a ruling decision that would abandon them. This is the selected historical cause, distinct from later accounts of divine judgement.

Survival was unequal. Some people reached high ground or fled by ship. Selected households used living chambers to transform their **existing bodies** for sea life; workers and rebels seized some of those facilities, while many candidates died or emerged unable to live as before. The later claim that *the whole city became dolphins* compresses a rationed escape and erases those left to drown. A changed human body does not create a new soul, and the transformed survivors do not explain every later Deep Envoy community. Near the ruin, survivors from several backgrounds occupied fishing places, salvage wharves and refuge quarters. They retained some knowledge of tides and safe approaches, not sovereign command of the old organism-city. The site remained inhabited around its damage without restoring the colonial state.

Elsewhere the Unbinding left islands to rebuild on their own terms. Local survivors recovered water stores, terraces, fisheries and short craft. Because one island lacked timber, another grain and another a sheltered landing, households resumed seasonal crossings before they possessed a common name or foreign patron. During the later Ark scattering, Folk kindreds moving north reached southern coasts and nearby islands. Proto-Glass and proto-Atherian voyagers also landed in some harbours and **stopped**, rather than continuing toward the settlements from which later Atherian cities grew. They joined, fought or displaced people already there. Their descendants became part of particular island societies, not a permanent third block labelled “Atherian” centuries before an empire existed.

The same mobility included coercion. Captains captured men, women and children from neighbouring islands and coasts for labour, ransom, sale and household incorporation. Captured women were disproportionately retained as concubines, coerced wives and reproductive dependants. In many places a recognised portion passed through harbour or lineage redistribution; a minority of island authorities developed harsher programmes assigning unions according to imagined inherited qualities. A child acknowledged by a citizen household could eventually gain kinship and civic standing while their mother remained unfree. This produced real mixed ancestry and transferred languages, skills and rites, but **mixture does not redeem the violence that produced it**. Later origin songs calling captive women honoured foreign mothers preserve only one side of that history.

## Islands joined by need, not government

Over the Hearth and City-State ages, related languages and practices became recognisable as an **Elmaric** family. The Elmarai Isles formed one important heartland, not a name for every island or a capital of them all. A scattered specialised archipelago, an independent sanctuary-market island and low shoal-and-lagoon communities supplied the deepest network. Fosterage, marriage, adoption, pilotage, seasonal crewing and reciprocal refuge linked households across political borders. Local people still belonged first to their own harbour, island, lineage or interior district. The larger island and near-mainland harbour islands were contested outer zones, inhabited by older and mixed populations and later claimed in pieces by outside states. No clean circle separated “native heartland” from “colonial frontier.”

Food made the sea political. Fishing, orchards, vines, grazing and specialised crops could support much life, but the denser island harbours could not rely upon their own grain in every season. Opposite-shore fields and selected outer islands supplied grain through **both treaty purchase and coercive holdings**. Existing cultivators, Elmaric settler families and enslaved workers could labour in the same grain district under different claims. Imported timber, pitch and masts were a second strategic dependence. A grain ship might feed several islands; losing one channel could make a small pilot community more important than a king's hall. Convoys, granaries, cargo records, harbour tolls and fortified stores grew from these necessities, not from an abstract love of commerce.

The sanctuary island offered a way to transact among enemies. Its resident custodians witnessed oaths, protected a precinct, measured grain, recorded convoy agreements, handled ransom and sometimes arbitrated cargo losses. Several Elmaric rites shared the sacred place without becoming one pantheon; Thalassor's followers were present but did not own every island cult. The sanctuary could not tax the heartland or command a national fleet. It also did not turn every captive into a free person. A fugitive who reached its precinct was protected from immediate seizure, yet normally faced return, exchange or ransom; valuable or well-sponsored people had a better chance of a formal hearing. Raiders could enter conditionally for settlement and exchange while powerful captains negotiated wider latitude. **Sanctuary law reduced some violence and administered other parts of the captive economy.**

There was no single Elmaric polity. The great island later ancestral to Sermora sustained local dynasties, inland communities and rival ports; the neighbouring archipelago held additional pilots and island households. The western Saronikan-side ports more often retained civic councils and ship-owning associations. These are ancient tendencies, not immutable constitutional destinies: the later Sermoran republic descends from a more dynastic zone, while the later Saronikan kingdom overlays older civic traditions. Near-mainland communities had farms, relatives and ritual duties across narrow channels before a state called them dependencies. Foreign garrisons and settler quarters later complicated these relationships without creating them from nothing.

One future Sermoran predecessor remembered as **the Harbour Founded Twice** makes this layered urban history concrete. Settler-descended houses dated it from a damaged convoy's landing, stockade and seizure of grain and shore. Older shore houses and their allies dated it from returned exiles, escaped captives, local fighters, defectors and pilots defeating the punitive fleet. Both remembered real contributions and suppressed others. This harbour was **one** community later absorbed into a larger capital, not the foundation of all Elmaric civilisation or the completed modern republic. By 350 BR Elmaric peoples remained the most populous cultural family in the Sermoran great-island zone even where settler shipowners held disproportionate quays and stores. On the Saronikan side, slower pre-imperial Glass-descended displacement had already altered some harbours, but the later imperial conquest and final cultural absorption must not be backdated.

## The moving harbour and the cost of refuge

While these island worlds matured, survivors remained beside the original drowned city. Around **1000–900 BR**, an exceptional **Morlach** sea-kindred ritual specialist followed a genuine but ambiguous tide-borne vision to them. He was a follower of [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/20 Third Circle/Thalassor.md|Thalassor]], not a priest representing every Folk community or a prophet given moral certainty. Survivor knowledge, local Deep Envoy negotiation and the injured animal's needs shaped what he could do. He eased some ancient control grafts and made a bounded Thalassorian bargain around an immense **natural reefback** that the old colonists had enlarged and altered. The city fed and defended its bearer; the reefback carried an inhabited shell, living structures and a variable flotilla through a central island circuit, with rarer long treks. It was a mobile harbour, **not** a geological island or the drowned capital raised intact.

The bargain required motion rather than permanent territorial rule and left a **living human return** owed to the sea. The founder became its first payment; his fate below remains permanently unresolved. His successors converted an exceptional bargain into hereditary authority and recurrent claims upon other lives, especially children who could not consent. A failed fishing and raiding season later caused hunger; residents and crews cast the sacred ruling household into the sea after it demanded further returns from less powerful families. A covenant council replaced the dynasty's monopoly but **did not abolish the tribute**. It protected recognised citizens by pushing the cost onto captives, enslaved foreigners and condemned people. Thalassor did not declare outsiders worth less. The city made that hierarchy itself.

Captains eventually dominated the council because routes, defence and mobile neighbourhoods depended upon vessels. In the mature polity, a recognised share in a ship or essential installation gave civic protection and a voice; residence or years of work did not. The captains' assembly might grant a share for exceptional service, but the existing owners decided whose service counted. Warrants distinguished authorised reprisals and paid sea war from unlicensed piracy within the city's own law; raided coasts could reject those distinctions. Fishing, salvage, trade, escort, ransom, smuggling, labour capture and slavery sustained different households. Surface and deep peoples met there, but “fishmen” or “Deep Ones” was an outsider's flattening of distinct communities, including some transformed descendants of the predecessor. Child-taking belonged to particular covenants, courts and crimes, not to every Deep Envoy.

To residents the moving city could be refuge and marvel; to neighbouring coasts it could be a predatory slaver. Its colour, mobility and living architecture later invited a single romantic story, but it passed through **sacred dynasty, covenant council and captains' oligarchy** with different distributions of power and blame. The original drowned ruin stayed within the central island world. The later wreck called the **Black Reef** would be a second, separate site.

## War reaches the outer islands

The Age of Stars brought heavier pressure from Atherian-speaking cities, Caleran and other mainland powers. The Elmaric-connected waters had **two interlinked theatres**. Gateway-facing routes drew captains and harbours into Caleran's wars with the Gateway Republic and its successor country. Delkosi–Vashari-facing channels involved eastern crowns and the older Tasarian, Ilyessan, Aveshi and Arkenan–Saronikan port worlds. An island house could sell grain or ships to one belligerent while kin at another harbour supported its enemy. No Elmaric assembly chose a side for all.

During the **Gateway Wars, c. 650–520 BR**, some Elmaric crews carried Caleran pilots, provisions and troops; others served the Gateway or raided under their own shares. Professional independent crews were the raiding baseline, though dynasties and merchants financed others. Families on opposite sides argued over captured kin, debt and cargo long after campaigns ended. After Qarzeth fell in **520 BR**, Caleran offered useful captains amnesty or contracts in waters it could influence and hunted less protected former opponents as pirates. Its strongest direct footholds developed along the Gateway-facing outer approaches. Eastern connections were more often treaties, paid crews, allied harbours or isolated positions. None amounted to conquest of the Elmaric heartland.

By the later Age of Stars, rival leagues and dynastic blocs pooled ships for particular convoy seasons, famine responses, disputed passages and wars. They did not absorb every island. The sanctuary's grain court remained useful precisely because the islands were not one state. The central great-island zone later ancestral to Sermora remained predominantly Elmaric and politically separate; the Saronikan-side communities already showed related but distinct dialects, route preferences and arrangements with Atherian cities. These were **regional branches, not yet Sermorans and Saronikans in their modern political senses**.

## A town inside and around the Black Spire

Vorthara meanwhile grew through continuity rather than one charter. More fugitives and strange specialists entered the old refuge; accessible Spire interiors became substantially inhabited, while dense streets formed against its lower outside, at its foot and along the ascent. Nearby ridge hamlets provided plots, herds and cultivated medicinal or magical plants. The town still needed imported grain. Its unusual Spire conditions attracted some investigators, but social refuge, accumulated clients and the ability to practise constrained crafts were the stronger reasons to come. The Spire did not simply strengthen every spell.

Open routes served strangers, traders, carriers and food sellers. Specialist neighbourhoods shared some main passages while controlling side rooms and workshops; dangerous practitioners and gangs held deeper **occupied** thresholds. “Deeper” does not imply that the Spire's unknown whole interior had been entered. By **350 BR** the inhabited interior and exterior worked as one substantial settlement in daily use, yet refuge households, gangs, magicians and markets still governed their own premises. Common water and freight encouraged limited cooperation without a ruler, council, comprehensive law or the later Spire Truce.

Several waves of **Qahuri** mortuary workers brought preservation, funerary contracts, estate craft and contested death-working into this environment. Qahur's own chronology offers pressures for migration; it does **not** prove a named caravan arrived on a particular year. By 350 BR the **Pale Hand** led a recognisable death-working neighbourhood. Its crews prepared bodies and used mostly **soulless** animated porters for hazardous labour. Skilled outsiders could join or be hired. The faction publicly rejected human soul-binding while a concealed circle or protected clients pursued it. An ordinary working corpse, a bound human soul and a vampire's retained human soul are different conditions. A distinct but adjoining cult quarter grew from old refuge rites and the later service market. Menhir-associated courts, Idol houses, Egg White patron rooms, infernal claimants and supposed angelic houses could advertise openly without sharing one theology or a citywide licence. Loyal angels cannot be hired or made to grant spells; not every claimed patron is real as described.

Vorthara's deepest internal conflict followed its two attractions. People fleeing Giant slavery and later slaveholding societies came for shelter, while buyers and sellers found a town without a general prohibition and with real demand for captive labour. The opposed interests used **overlapping streets, lodgings and passages**, not necessarily one free district facing one slave district. An old owner's outside claim had no recognised force inside, but that did not guarantee a fugitive protection against a new local seizure. Refuge households and captive traders rescued, bought out, kidnapped or retaliated against one another. Other factions and gangs maintained a limited understanding to intervene **when fighting threatened trade and usable routes**; they did not agree to emancipate captives or govern the town. Whether recurring clashes culminated in one especially large pre-350 BR crisis remains an open authorial choice.

## The last years before 350 BR

After Casvilar's death in **370 BR**, Atherian succession struggles drew ships, stores and clients across the sea. **Ardel Veyr's maritime following hired the independently governed wandering city** as an allied floating harbour, provisioning partner and source of contracted ships and raiders. Its captains sought both payment and their own maritime independence; the city did not become part of Ardel's state. Their ordinary central-island circuit made long-distance work costly, but an exceptional trek carried them east of that circuit to the **eastern approaches of the central Thalassian anchorages**, where Ardel's main force was campaigning. In **355 BR**, **Istran Melvo** staged a withdrawal and left an apparent Atherian command ship as a contested prize. Hidden troops and incendiary crews entered the city's protected formation with it; the first operational Atherian **sea-fire** burned the pearl-white living works black. Melvo's returning fleet then broke Ardel's nearby main force in the **same linked operation**. Ardel escaped east and was executed in **354 BR**. The burned urban mass tore from the maimed reefback and grounded as the **Black Reef** along the eastward route from the anchorages. Most city residents died or entered Atherian captivity. Smaller surface and deep diasporas survived; the animal's later fate and many “Black Bearer” sightings remain uncertain. Sea-fire stayed a rare Atherian state secret at the stopping point.

> Existing [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]] and [[Istran Melvo]] describe Melvo's **355 BR defeat of Ardel Veyr's main force among the central Thalassian anchorages**. The author has selected **one linked battle**: the mobile city's independent flotilla supported Ardel near those anchorages; Melvo's prize-ship attack destroyed the city while his returning fleet defeated Ardel's nearby force. “Eastern trek” means east of the city's **ordinary island circuit**, not a second battle in a different eastern theatre. Ardel's escape and 354 BR execution remain unchanged. The exact anchorage and Black Reef chart position await geographic review, and canonical articles have not yet been edited.

The Black Reef's fall did not end Elmaric piracy, turn the sanctuary into an Atherian province or settle the independent heartland. Caleran and its successor commanders still needed local pilots, coastal provisions and bargains with rulers they could not simply command. Around **350 BR**, as Thalion concentrated imperial power, Atheria described Vorthara publicly as a small lawful market while watching the surrounding routes. A temporary gathering of Vortharan households, pass keepers and influential practitioners accepted a limited external arrangement; inner neighbourhoods remained self-governing and no general extradition right arose. **Exclusive imperial hire, provider-specific service schedules, protected commissions and an imperial tribunal are later expansion-era additions**, not facts to insert into this 350 BR settlement.

## The sea and Spire at the boundary

At **350 BR**, the central Elmaric heartland is populous, related and divided. Its sanctuary has recognised religious and commercial functions but no mandate to rule the islands. Dynastic houses are especially prominent in the future Sermoran zone; councils and ship-owning leagues remain influential farther west in the future Saronikan zone. Grain and timber expose both to mainland pressure. Caleran holds some outer footholds, strongest toward the Gateway, while eastern channels remain contested among several powers. The drowned predecessor still has occupied margins in the central islands. The wandering successor has been destroyed under the selected 355 BR account; its people, seized expertise and blackened reef have different afterlives. Vorthara is one inhabited Spire-and-ridge town with no common government, open trade routes, specialist quarters and a severe unresolved conflict between refuge and captive commerce.

None of these populations is an embryonic modern state awaiting its inevitable name. The later Sermoran republic, Saronikan kingdom, Atherian provincial system and Sestaran Concord will be made through conquest, settlement, religious change, municipal law and further conflict **after this draft stops**.

[[Package E - Sestaran City Origins and Imperial Handoff 350 BR to 0 AR]] records how this stopping point leaves room for pre-existing Fiorenzia and Vardessa, later imperial Aversia and Orsena, distinct Sermoran and Vortharan trajectories, and detached Castara. It assigns no new post-350 BR event or foundation date.

## Review gates before canon promotion

1. **355 BR canon integration:** the author has selected one linked central-anchorage battle in which Melvo destroyed the independent city allied to Ardel and defeated Ardel's nearby main force. At promotion, expand and cross-link the canonical Ardel/Melvo account without replacing Ardel's defeat or adding a second 355 BR campaign. Set exact anchorage and Black Reef geography before mapping them.
2. **Geography and names:** review the [[Package E - Local Place Dossiers to 350 BR|local dossiers]] before promotion. The predecessor ruin, sanctuary island, reefback's central circuit, Black Reef, individual Elmarai islands and finer Elmaric branches have relational places but no approved exact map placement or new proper names. Keep the original drowned site and Black Reef distinct; treat Vorthara's working areas as pre-350 BR neighbourhoods, not present-day ward borders.
3. **Precision:** Qahuri arrivals at Vorthara, the formation of the Pale Hand, the sacred dynasty's fall, the shift to captains' rule and the first large refuge–slave-trade crisis have selected sequences but no exact years. Do not manufacture them from the age boundaries.
4. **Moral and metaphysical controls:** retain coerced incorporation and captive markets without romanticising them; distinguish ordinary soulless corpse labour from soul-binding; distinguish Thalassor's Menhir bargain from approval of child tribute; distinguish humans, Giants, transformed humans and diverse Deep Envoys.
5. **Public knowledge:** decide which predecessor facts, founder motives, hidden Vortharan practices and battle details a beginner-facing encyclopedia could report confidently, and which belong only to authorial history or disputed tradition.
6. **Scope:** later Atherian contracts, imperial assimilation, merchant republics, Redeemer-era religion, modern wards and the present Concord are comparative trajectories only. The 350 BR boundary is firm.
