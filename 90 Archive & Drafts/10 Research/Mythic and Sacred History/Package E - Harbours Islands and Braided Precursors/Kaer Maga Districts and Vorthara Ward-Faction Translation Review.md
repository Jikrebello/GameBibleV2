# Kaer Maga Districts and Vorthara Ward–Faction Translation Review

> [!warning] Author-only comparison
> Kaer Maga is a design reference, not a source of World Egg events. The eleven Kaer Maga districts below are **not** eleven proposed Vortharan districts. Existing Vorthara canon controls. Decisions 38–70 in [[Package E - Comparative Synthesis and Canon Readiness Questions]] concern the refuge at about 350 BR and a later imperial-contract trajectory; they do not establish the present-day boundaries or continuous existence of every ward. The Pale Hand is now selected as an early organised exception.

## Source and confidence key

- **Direct Paizo evidence:** James L. Sutter's [2010 *City of Strangers* preview](https://paizo.com/blog/city-of-strangers) identifies the Bottoms, the abolitionist Freemen, Ankar-Te's necromantic market and the Ardoc golem-makers. The [Paizo book description](https://paizo.com/products/btpy8fda) confirms that the classic 1E book gazetteers all eleven districts and their factions. The later first-edition [*Return of the Runelords* Player's Guide map](https://downloads.paizo.com/ReturnOfTheRunelords_PlayersGuide.pdf) confirms their names and layout, but not every social detail.
- **Working district glosses:** The remaining one-line functions are condensed from the classic *City of Strangers* district treatment as indexed or repeated in public summaries. They are useful for design comparison but should be checked against the book itself before precise Pathfinder attribution. They are not independent historical evidence.
- **World Egg canon:** [[The Citadel of Vorthara Overview]], [[Magical Schools and Factions of Vorthara]], [[Wardens of the Crown Road]], [[The Crucible]], [[Collegium of Doors]], [[The Ironbound Guild]], [[The Crafters’ Union and The Elixirium]], [[The Fleshweavers and The Red Covenant]], [[The Pale Hand]], [[The Nightveil Coven]] and [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Sestaran Concord/30 Society and Daily Life/Life Paths/Lives of Vorthara.md|Lives of Vorthara]]. The **analogue** column below points to these existing institutions, not to newly established wards.

## What makes Kaer Maga's districts work

The useful feature is not just eleven memorable neighbourhoods. Each has a different **physical setting, livelihood, jurisdiction and source of conflict**. The marketplace needs caravan passage; the visitor quarter feeds that market; industrial districts need water and freight; elite places buy separation; the poor occupy damaged or hazardous fabric; abolitionists and slave-dealers coexist because neither controls the whole city. The ancient ring gives Kaer Maga its own architecture. Vorthara instead has a climb, an old Spire of unknown purpose, ridge settlements, external roads, water stations and the four later Shell anchors.

The Kaer Maga Undercity and Halflight Path are **not a twelfth district**. They illustrate subterranean inherited space and a freight choke point. Vorthara may have sealed Spire-adjacent workrooms and dangerous ways, and canon already has the Crown Road ascent; this does not establish a matching undercity, prison past or original Spire function.

## Vorthara's stronger spatial grammar

These are **candidate functions to test**, not names, fixed boundaries or approved present-day districts. Several can overlap in one ward, and one faction may have sites in several zones.

1. **Ascent and first-arrival belt.** Gates, water stations, cargo inspection, carriers, cheap beds, import grain, visitor brokerage. Wardens protect the shared road/water role; nearby wards keep their own law. This makes the dependence on ordinary food and labour legible from the city's entrance.
2. **Working terraces and reagent gardens.** Ridge food plots and herds help but cannot feed the whole refuge; native and imported magical plants provide valuable reagents. At 350 BR growers are independent households and specialist clients. A present-day link to the Elixirium is plausible but **not yet canon**; the Elixirium's established identity is medicine and reagents, not ownership of all cultivation.
3. **Heavy-work and draw approaches.** The Crucible, Ironbound, Crafters and Elixirium create overlapping freight, heat, waste, repair and water demands. The four canon Shell anchors and their failure interactions constrain any layout; no single faction owns all four.
4. **Spire-foot containment and restricted access.** Collegium of Doors proximity, sealed rooms, watch arrangements and qualified access could make a visible high-control edge. This is not proof that the Collegium founded Vorthara or understands the Spire's first purpose. The Conclave appears only during alignments under temporary session compacts.
5. **Body and death-service edge.** Pale Hand corpse contracts/mortuaries are distinguishable from Fleshweaver/Red Covenant surgery, warbeasts and hidden procurement. Their sites may trade, feud or share customers, but a single "necromancy ward" would blur different legal and metaphysical questions.
6. **Refuge and coercion frontier.** A protective household cluster opposite a slaving or coercive-pact cluster is the sharpest Kaer Maga-style conflict already authorised in the **350 BR** decisions. For the present, whether either cluster survives, changes sides or acquires named faction backing remains open. Boundary-crossing rescue, kidnapping, redemption purchases, retaliation and disputed apprenticeships can provide causal stories without a citywide moral consensus.
7. **Visitor, arena and public-service streets.** Food, lodging, healers, performers, contract-scribes, guides and arena business link outsiders to insiders. These must also show non-magical livelihoods. The chapels under interdict in *Lives of Vorthara* stand outside the walls near Lamplighters' Gate; do not casually relocate them into a generic temple strip.
8. **Protected households and precarious housing.** Wealthy patron compounds, schools' housing and makeshift poor shelters should have different water security and escape routes. Avoid assuming every poor resident is an apprentice or every elite household belongs to a magical school.

## Factions are not districts

The canon says a faction may control **workshops, housing, clients, guards, roads and obligations inside a ward**. That does not mean one faction equals one ward. A useful map would overlay four different systems:

| Layer | Present canon | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Neighbourhood control | Schools, guilds, families, gangs, shrines, arenas and compacts | Boundaries can cut across occupational clusters; rival powers can share a street. |
| Shared ascent | Crown Road Wardens | They keep gates, water stations and bells, not general criminal jurisdiction. |
| Industrial dependency | Four permanent Shell anchors and connected goods | Sabotage can cascade between rival wards; the Spire Truce protects infrastructure, not daily peace. |
| Temporary market | Astral Conclave at alignments | Its session law cannot become year-round city government. |

## Three high-value borrowings, with collision checks

1. **The Bottoms versus Downmarket:** a concrete protection-versus-captivity frontier fits decisions 48–50 and Vorthara's non-extradition norm. It should have identifiable local actors, costs and enforcement; a refuge slogan alone is insufficient.
2. **Cavalcade/Bis against the ascent:** Vorthara's own industrial identity is stronger than Kaer Maga's copied trades because freight, Shell anchors, repairs, medicine and cultivation need each other. Mapping load routes and waste routes could make ordinary life and faction conflict credible.
3. **Hospice/Warren/Highside contrast:** the visitor sees inns and public services, but workers and poor households keep the city functioning while patrons buy privacy. This helps Vorthara read as a lived city rather than a list of magical colleges.

## Non-transferable elements

No ancient stone ring, confirmed prison origin, Karzoug/Thassilon, Caulborn, exact eleven-district template, Freemen/Ardocs/Pathfinder cults, blanket undead servants, generic soul batteries, central council, municipal police, automatic spell-strengthening radiation or solved Black Spire mystery. The Black Spire's Giant-selective emanation is bounded and not a universal source of Vortharan magic or plants. At 350 BR, the Pale Hand is the **selected early named exception**; do not backdate the other present school names, Shell system, Spire Truce, Concord relationship or formal Astral Conclave.

## Current author focus

[[Vorthara before 350 BR - Refuge Cults and the Dead]] now develops the Qahuri-rooted, openly recruiting Pale Hand, its public ban and concealed soul-binding, and the **distinct, adjoining, mostly open** microcult quarter which grew through layered refuge custom and a later service market. Rival deterrence rather than cult-wide law keeps smaller houses precariously present. Atheria's official "small lawful market" description understates the town, while perimeter surveillance and the **initial nonintervention bargain** leave inner ward affairs outside imperial jurisdiction. Exclusive hire is a **later addition during expansion**, not a term already in force at the 350 BR boundary. A temporary gathering accepts its framework, but only named providers sign for their own **listed services**; imperial enforcement targets their outward contracts, and independent non-signatories remain legally free. Pay, access and imperial protection for **authorised magical work** induce signatories to join; an imperial commission overrides local bans within Atherian jurisdiction, though it does not cover private acts. A separate imperial tribunal hears disputes about the commission's scope with local testimony. Decisions 69–70 select a city **substantially inhabited both within accessible Spire spaces and around its lower exterior, foot and ascent**, with connected ridge hamlets and a gradient from open trader routes to partly shared specialist passages and guarded dangerous thresholds. Finer refuge/coercion boundaries, visitor streets and later ward mapping remain to be placed. Neither a later tribunal nor a copied Kaer Maga district count settles their form.
