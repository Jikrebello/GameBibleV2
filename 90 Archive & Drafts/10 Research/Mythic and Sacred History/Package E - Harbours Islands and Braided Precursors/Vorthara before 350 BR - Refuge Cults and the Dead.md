# Vorthara before 350 BR — Refuge, Cults and the Dead

> [!warning] Status and boundary
> This is an **author-only working historical model**, not a canon article. The facts under “Established controls” are already fixed; the staged developments below are proposals for review. The account stops at the imperial boundary around 350 BR. The present-day faction and district trajectories are tests of plausibility, **not** proof of continuous institutions or permission to backdate the Spire Truce, four Shell anchors or Astral Conclave.

## Established controls

[[Package E - Comparative Synthesis and Canon Readiness Questions|Decisions 38–71]] establish a continually inhabited refuge landscape around the pre-Atherian Black Spire: earliest hidden habitation within or against it, then scattered ridge shelters; Giant avoidance associated with a selective emanation; a sizeable but anarchic refuge town and pass market by 350 BR, **substantially inhabited both inside accessible Spire spaces and around its lower exterior, foot and ascent**, with access grading from open market routes to guarded specialist thresholds; powerful but mostly informal magical practitioners; no universal extradition, citywide government or general ban on internal coercion; local food and imported grain, with native and introduced magical plants cultivated for reagents. Several Qahuri waves supply the necromantic roots of a death-working quarter that later receives smaller traditions from other peoples. **The Pale Hand is already its leading organised faction by 350 BR**, publicly forbidding soul-binding while hiding its practice among some insiders or protected clients. A distinct cult quarter adjoins the death-working streets at a contested edge; it grew in layers from old refuge rites into a crowded, **mostly openly advertised** microcult district held in an unstable balance by rival deterrence. The town has no common magical government. Its historic refuge households and active slave trade create a severe, recurring conflict, contained only when other interests fear damage to business. As Thalion's empire emerges, a **temporary** gathering negotiates limited external terms; the inner settlement keeps practical autonomy. The imperial public line calls it a small, lawful market, while surveillance falls on the surrounding region. **Exclusive imperial hire for signed providers and named services is added later during expansion**, not to the first agreement at the 350 BR boundary.

[[The Citadel of Vorthara Overview]] and [[Magical Schools and Factions of Vorthara]] control the present: factional wards, narrow Crown Road Warden duties, Spire Truce, four permanent Shell anchors, and a session-bounded Astral Conclave. [[The Pale Hand]] controls present corpse-work and its metaphysical limits; the author now gives it a **pre-350 BR institutional ancestor bearing that faction identity**, without backdating its every current technique or ward arrangement. [[Faiths of the Seven Cities]] places Redeemer chapels **outside** Vorthara's walls in the present and notes the unresolved interdict. The other present institutions are not automatically ancient.

The periods follow [[Age of Chains]], [[Age of Scattering]], [[Age of Hearths and First Kings]], [[Age of City-States]] and [[Age of Stars]]. Dates are broad synchronisms, not invented foundation years.

## Revised origin: Qahuri mortuary diaspora

The author identifies **ancient Qahur**, the Egyptian-coded human civilisation of the Sacred Basin on the northern side of the wider inner-sea world, as the natural source of Vorthara's necromantic tradition. This supersedes the earlier choice between an independently invented Vortharan burial craft and a freight-first local invention. Qahur's [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns|mortuary estates]] had professional embalmers, funerary suppliers, burial fraternities, physicians, dependent workers, images, residue and contested claims upon the dead. From c. 1540 BR, the minority [[The White Measure and the Second Houses|White Measure]] developed much more dangerous corpse-anchored soul-binding and Egg White patronage from Ishkatari remnants. **Qahur is not Ishkatar**, and White Measure practitioners do not represent every Qahuri funeral household.

The author has chosen **several waves** of Qahuri movement: early small mortuary households, then larger and more diverse arrivals around later Qahuri crises. Embalmers, mortuary workers, estate fugitives, merchants' dependants, dissidents and sometimes ambitious House-Builders can travel through coastal and inland exchange networks. Qemaret's fall in 1076 BR and the Single Radiance closures and aftermath in 842–814 BR supply plausible pressures, though the exact Vortharan migration routes, numbers and dates are still open. Vorthara offers clients and distance from Qahuri kings, temples, patrons and family claims. No single colony, mass exodus or founder is established.

The **Vortharan development** is a local branching of that imported craft. Preparation, preservation, funerary instruments, estate-office patterns and corpse proxies arrive with Qahuri practitioners. In Vorthara, rival households apply different parts of that expertise to the dangerous pass economy, eventually creating conspicuous soulless working dead. The elite White Measure project of keeping a particular human soul from judgement remains a separate, rarer and more predatory undertaking. The two can meet in one street, but a zombie porter is not automatically a House-Builder or its captive soul.

Qahuri households remain the quarter's **origin and technical core, not its sole population**. Individual Folk druids and Orphaned specialists bring smaller, non-identical ways of tending graves, bargaining over bodily decay, reading residue or guarding against reanimation. A few ancient Ansharan vampires may hide among its clients or patrons. Over time, a Qahuri-led association becomes the **Pale Hand**, open to capable outsiders who need room to practise and want paid work. Different members and neighbours collaborate in different combinations for particular jobs rather than adhering to one rite. Vorthara attracts some because authorities or neighbours elsewhere punish their work; others were tolerated at home and arrive for profit, protection, rival expertise, escape from a particular patron or freedom from oversight. This avoids turning every foreign death-worker into an innocent refugee or every home culture into an anti-magic mob.

## How separate settlements became one Vorthara — spatial sequence

> [!note] Author-review synthesis, not a new locked decision
> The **inside-and-around form** is selected in Decision 69. The sequence below connects the first hidden refuge, later ridge hamlets and the substantial 350 BR town. Individual routes, interior and exterior neighbourhood positions and the date at which residents began calling the whole built-up area one settlement remain open. It does not identify the Black Spire's makers or original purpose.

1. **A sheltered point, not a founded citadel.** During the Giant age the first fugitive households occupy accessible rooms **inside or immediately against** the Black Spire. They use its concealment and the danger it presents to Giant pursuers. Living space is limited by what can be entered, heated, supplied with water and escaped from; no evidence requires the whole Spire to be habitable. Gardens, herds and warning posts must extend beyond the structure.
2. **A constellation of shelters.** As escapees and other outsiders arrive, kin and allies establish ridge huts, hidden plots, water stops and small hamlets along safe approaches. They share the refuge's reputation but defend separate thresholds. Habitation also spreads through **additional accessible Spire spaces** where households can make them safe and supplied. The Spire is a growing refuge as well as a reference point, but not a seat of government over the ridge communities.
3. **A working interior and exterior foot.** After the Giant collapse, ordinary pass traffic, building, food imports and specialist exchange favour both **inhabitable Spire spaces** and the accessible ground **against its lower exterior, at its foot and along the ascent**. Carriers, repairers, cooks, lodgings, stores and water sellers need one another and the road; interior households need supplies carried through defended thresholds. Construction fills gaps between older shelters where access is worth more than seclusion. Ridge families remain part of the network, but neither the interior nor the outer streets absorb everyone.
4. **Adjacent specialisms, not a planned grid.** Repeated clients and shared equipment draw Qahuri-rooted mortuary households into a death-working cluster near freight and funerary access. Older scattered ritual gathering spots attract witnesses, lodgings and later microcults into an **adjoining** cult quarter; their shared edge serves clients but remains disputed. The refuge–slave-trade conflict is **not** confined to one pair of adjoining districts: fugitives, sellers, buyers and protectors use many of the same routes and thresholds. Growers remain near workable terraces, water and light. These are **causal relationships, not fixed compass directions or a ward map**.
5. **One settlement in use, many jurisdictions in practice.** By 350 BR, **substantial interior neighbourhoods** and dense exterior frontage connect through used thresholds to the foot, ascent, market, specialist clusters and nearby ridge neighbourhoods. Together they are commonly treated as Vorthara. A visitor can cross from inner to outer habitation and from one local jurisdiction to another within the same inhabited fabric. Water, grain, freight and defence create temporary cooperation, but **physical coalescence does not create a council, common citizenship, citywide law or the later Spire Truce**. More distant ridge hamlets remain tied to the town without necessarily becoming continuous streets.

The selected **inside-and-around form** gives Vorthara two substantial linked built environments: **inhabited accessible Spire interior** and **neighbourhoods against its lower exterior, foot and ascent**, with an outer halo of ridge settlements. This fits canon's “Black Spire and surrounding ridges,” the locked first refuge **within or against** it, and the later importance of gates, water and freight. It does **not** imply that the entire Spire is accessible, its hidden reaches are known, or every ward has the same interior–exterior footprint. Exact density, entrances, vertical routes and ward boundaries require later choice before promotion.

### A graded network of passages

By 350 BR, the routes most useful to **strangers, traders, food carriers and ordinary clients** remain openly usable, with private houses and businesses opening onto them. Farther from this public market, specialist neighbourhoods share a few main passages but control side ways, workshops and chambers. More secretive or dangerous practitioners and gangs guard deeper occupied thresholds, bargain over entry, charge for it or bar outsiders. This is a **gradient of customary access and effective force**, not three legally defined rings: a dangerous house can sit near a busy lane, and an open passage can run through inhabited Spire space rather than only outside it.

Open does not mean centrally owned or safe; guarded does not mean an entire deeper level belongs to one faction. The routes emerge because trade and supply need continuity while occult work, captive labour, refuge and factional rivalry encourage enclosure. Their exact entrances, elevations and rights of passage remain for later mapping. No part of this selection reveals the Spire's full depth, its makers or its first purpose, and it does not backdate modern ward institutions or the Spire Truce.

### The refuge and captive-market fault line

Vorthara's oldest inhabited story begins with people escaping Giant masters. Its later reputation continues to attract fugitives from slaveholding rulers, households and markets. At the same time, a substantial pass town with no citywide ban on enslavement offers buyers, sellers and coercive brokers a place to trade captives and labour. **Both populations have reasons to be here.** A fugitive's old owner has no recognised right to reclaim them within Vorthara, but a different local trader may still try to seize or bind them. Whether someone is safe depends on a protector, allies and the next threshold, not on a universal civic promise.

This contradiction does not need a single “free quarter” facing a single “slave quarter.” Escape routes, food lines, lodgings, markets and guarded passages bring the sides into repeated contact across the inside-and-around town. Refuge households can rescue, buy freedom or retaliate; sellers can hide captives, hire guards or enlist a local patron. An attempted recovery or rescue can spread into a feud that endangers unrelated traders and residents. By 350 BR this is plausibly the city's **largest internal flashpoint**, but no particular battle, massacre or date is yet established.

Other factions, gangs and trading interests maintain a **limited understanding that they will intervene when this fighting obstructs business**. They protect market access, customers, freight and usable routes, not the rights of every captive. They may pressure combatants, block a passage or join a temporary settlement, then resume their ordinary rivalries. Because only the participants can enforce it, this understanding neither resolves the moral conflict nor constitutes city government, universal law or the later Spire Truce. Its exact participants, tools and any decisive crisis remain for author review.

## Two distinct but adjacent strange places

### 1. A neighbourhood where the dead work

This should be unmistakable by 350 BR, without being a copy of Kaer Maga's Ankar-Te. Its craft lineage is **Qahuri**, but its built environment and everyday work are Vortharan. The Pale Hand leads through guarded workshops, body and client contracts, equipment, training, work crews and paid access—not a writ of authority over every street. Its practical pitch is: if you have death-working skill and nowhere else to practise it, there is work here. The slopes and food imports give animated porters a local reason to proliferate: a body that can pull a cart through night, cold, fumes or a hazardous cut changes a carrier's calculation. Living people still prepare the bodies, tend them, sign and falsify contracts, guide the line, remove failures, sell food to the workers and fear a loose cart. The place therefore has immigrant households, apprentices, local clients and a labour dispute, not merely rows of monsters.

Its central moral fault line is **who owned the body and who consented**. A witnessed postmortem labour pledge, a purchased cadaver, a stolen corpse and a soul-held person cannot be treated as the same transaction. [[The Pale Hand]] specifies that most animated corpses have **no routed person inside**; memory-like gestures can be residue or physical habit. Intelligent undeath needs a stated trapped/refusing soul, occupant, mantle or other mechanism. A necromancer who claims that every walking body is a grateful ancestor is either mistaken or lying. This distinction can be argued openly in a ward of rival mortuary households without giving Vorthara a unified law.

The older refuge's descendants might object to any bound labour because it resembles slavery. Other residents may argue that an empty body is a tool that spares the living the worst work. Both arguments have stakes, but neither licenses corpse theft, coerced consent or soul imprisonment. Qahuri-origin households need not agree with one another: an embalmer protecting a family's dead, a carrier hiring empty bodies and a House-Builder seeking a soul-cage pursue very different ends. The early Pale Hand can broker or hire across those differences while internal members dispute lines they will not cross. Soul-binding is already a hidden practice by 350 BR, but the present black medium and precise later research methods still require separate development or dating.

By 350 BR the Hand's **declared line** is clear: it does not bind human souls. This makes it credible to bereaved families, ordinary paid members and clients who distinguish the moving body from the departed person. A concealed inner circle, favoured patrons or both cross that line under the protection created by the Hand's workshops and secrecy. The organisation's public ban is neither a metaphysical proof of innocence nor enforceable law outside its own relationships. Discovery threatens recruitment, contracts and internal solidarity; the exact hidden rite, leadership knowledge and first victims remain open. The present black medium is not automatically ancient.

Smaller arrivals complicate the street. A Folk druid might bargain with [[Morvalis]] over bodily closure or the prevention of residue animation; that is a Menhir relationship, **not** a method for summoning or owning a human soul, and could put the druid at odds with corpse-animators. Other Orphaned specialists may bring burial, preservation or residue disciplines that require individual definition before canon. A vampire may be old enough to remember Ansharan displacement, but vampirism is a retained-human-soul blood lien, **not** proof of skill in necromancy. Individual vampires might seek secrecy, blood or specialised treatment in the quarter; none becomes an ordinary zombie, a White Measure mummy or a victim of the Spire's Giant-selective effect merely by being a vampire. See [[Vampire Origins]] and decisions 43 and 55.

### 2. A quarter of rival cult courts and patron rooms

This precinct grew **in layers**: old refuge households met and kept different rites near familiar paths; later traffic brought lodging, paid witnesses, interpreters, brokers and clients; microcults established rooms where each could recruit and deal with humans. Its **spatial** unity is commercial, defensive and habitual, not a revealed pantheon or a special emanation of the Black Spire. Its **religious** life remains incompatible. At 350 BR it is visibly heterogeneous and constantly renewed: small cults form, split, vanish, sell their lease, leave heirs arguing over a once-real encounter or prove to have followed an impostor. Most openly display the name, sign or declared power under which they receive visitors, **including Idol and infernal houses**. A few hide altogether; many disclose their allegiance while concealing what happens behind the door.

- **Menhir-associated courts** bargain through local places, courtiers, omens or imported custom. Their laws can be exact without being just. No practitioner can compel a sovereign to appear. See [[Menhir Sovereignty and Bargain]].
- **Idol cult houses** are human networks for predatory powers. Some may conceal their allegiance behind charitable, ecstatic, funerary or prosperity services; genuine adherents, coerced people, dupes and opponents may live close together. Idols cannot create souls or enter in sovereign fullness. See [[The Idols Overview]].
- **Egg White patron rooms** are small, particular deals with anchored owners, proxies or route houses; a patron's presence in Astral space does not make it morally neutral or a member of one species. Each bargain must identify the actual owner and continuity. See [[Taxonomy of Egg White Patrons]]. These are **not** the later Astral Conclave or an ancient Spire-Annex.
- **Infernal and “demon” cults** may follow damned humans, fallen angels, devil offices, Depth beings, an Abyssal wound or a being from somewhere else miscalled a demon. The vernacular label alone settles nothing; [[Hell, Depth, and the Abyss]] and [[The Adversary]] distinguish these origins and their risks.
- **Angelic cults** are **human institutions**, not property of loyal angels. A sincere group may petition the Lord or honour a remembered commission; another may mistake a Mask or patron proxy for a heavenly visitor. [[True Angels Overview|Loyal angels]] reject worship and cannot be summoned, hired or made to grant a repeatable spell. Genuine angelic help remains possible only under a bounded commission.

Smaller Menhir courtiers and place-minds, Idol Masks and Archmasks, human-led rites with no living supernatural partner, and obscure Egg White intermediaries fill the gaps between these broad categories. Some participants gain powers; others gain training, money, status, protection, dreams, warnings, social control or nothing at all. **A cult's existence or public sign does not prove its patron exists as described, answers petitions or grants divine magic.** Outside powers benefit from Material agents, but that is not the same kind of relationship in each case: a loyal angel does not recruit worshippers like an Idol Mask, and a Menhir courtier does not sell Astral rent like a patron.

Open advertisement does not make the quarter peaceful. Each house defends its own door with followers, neighbours, clients, pledges or hired force. A stronger house can seize a lease or convert a vulnerable following, but an attempt to rule all the lanes gives threatened rivals a common short-term interest. Temporary coalitions, reciprocal warnings and retaliation preserve **unequal plurality** without a cult parliament or stable truce. The Pale Hand may be hired at the shared edge, but its leadership in death-work confers no cult-wide jurisdiction.

Possible World Egg-specific street texture, **not yet events or map facts**: a Menhir courtyard whose bargain applies only during one wind; an Idol house selling relief from a hunger it quietly cultivates; a patron clerk who must prove whether the speaker is owner, servant or copied proxy; a funeral procession crossing the same street as a soulless porter bearing grain; a refuge household warning newcomers which threshold is safe only until nightfall. The oddness comes from distinct powers and consequences sharing crowded geography, not from random spectacle.

These practices might overlap physically with healers, apothecaries, conjurers, fugitives and travellers of other devotions, including worshippers of the Lord. There are **no manifested saints before the Redeemer's work**, and the present-day Redeemer interdict should **not** be backdated. Its eventual history may be affected by these cults, but is not explained by their mere presence.

### Their contested edge

By 350 BR the cult courts and Pale Hand's death-working quarter meet without dissolving into one district. The overlap is most useful for funerary clients, Qahuri Egg White patron rooms, lodging, witnesses and brokers. A cult house might purchase preservation or corpse removal from the Hand; a Hand apprentice might seek an external patron; a family might insist that neither side touch a deceased relative. Menhir and Idol houses maintain separate spaces and claims beyond that busy edge. No single street patrol, shared theology or city magistrate settles conflicts. The exact lane, market, steps and boundaries remain for mapping, but each quarter should be recognisable to a visitor.

### Imperial market cover and the outside bargain

The emerging Atherian state publicly treats Vorthara as a **small, above-board trading market**. This is a convenient official description, not a reliable census or an assertion that the cult quarter has gone underground. It places scrutiny on the surrounding roads, pass approaches and movement beyond the refuge, rather than accepting responsibility for every cult dispute or forbidden practice within it. Some officials may know far more than the public formulation admits.

At the 350 BR boundary, Atheria secures a **nonintervention bargain** over Vorthara's internal arrangements. A temporary assembly can negotiate these external terms without creating a permanent city government. **Only later, during imperial expansion**, is an exclusive right to hire covered Vortharan specialist services added. A later temporary gathering accepts the framework, while **named factions, households and individual providers sign** service commitments. Each contract names the services reserved for Atheria; other work by that signatory remains open. Atheria is the sole external hirer of **those scheduled services**, not of every Vortharan's work: non-signatories remain legally free to serve other clients. Imperial officers look for breaches by signatories on roads and passes, not inside wards. Signatories negotiate pay and useful access, and receive imperial protection for practising the craft and magic **authorised by official work**. Within Atherian jurisdiction that commission **overrides local prohibitions**: local officials cannot arrest or punish a practitioner merely for the commissioned practice. It is not blanket immunity for private acts or work beyond the commission. A **separate imperial tribunal** hears disputes over the commission's scope and admits local witnesses and complainants; it is not the commissioning officer's personal verdict or a court over Vorthara's wards. The exact later date, individual schedules, tribunal procedure and sanctions remain open. Do not backdate exclusivity or a mature imperial inspection network to the 350 BR boundary, or treat the market cover as an imperial power to compel independent providers, extradite residents or govern wards.

## Collision controls and next decision

The Black Spire's **original builders and function remain unknown**. Its Giant-selective effect is not a generic magical amplifier, necromancy fuel, Idol aura or automatic source of imported plants. The 350 BR city has no ruler, council, citizenship, common police or comprehensive system of modern schools; the early Pale Hand is a **specific factional exception**, not a general government. Menhir, Idol and Egg White bargains are not interchangeable. A visibly undead quarter does not require trapped souls in ordinary zombies; predatory soul-binding remains rare, consequential and morally evil. Kaer Maga's [classic *City of Strangers* preview](https://paizo.com/blog/city-of-strangers) confirms the inspiration's necromantic market and contrasting abolitionist district, but Vorthara's causal system must remain its own.

**Next author focus:** decide whether the refuge–slave-trade conflict produced one especially large pre-350 BR crisis or remained a series of smaller clashes, then develop the working ascent. Later imperial contract sanctions and present-day ward boundaries can wait.
