# Package E — Research Programme and Source Register

> [!warning] Research boundary
> This remains an author-only comparative corpus, not canon. The later [[Package E - First Historical Draft to 350 BR]] now draws upon its research and the subsequent authorial decisions; that draft requires separate review before any promotion.

## Research question

How did island environments and port networks shape food security, political scale, mixed populations, coerced labour, religious exchange, violence and later civic identities from the Bronze Age through the Hellenistic–Roman transition? What institutional structures can inform Package E without turning the old Arkenan and Saronikan coasts into renamed Greece, Phoenicia, Varisia or the early-modern Caribbean?

The programme separates two chronological uses:

1. **Ancient comparison:** evidence potentially useful for World Egg history through the 350 BR boundary.
2. **Maritime afterlives:** medieval and early-modern merchant republics, corsair systems and Caribbean buccaneer bases. These provide institutional comparisons only; they do not establish ancient World Egg events.

## Profile method

Every full profile addresses the same questions, proportionately to the evidence:

- environment, fresh water, food and resources;
- settlement form and relationship to mainland shores;
- political institutions, citizenship, household and status;
- labour, slavery and dependent populations;
- shipping, war, mercenary service, raiding and piracy;
- cult, myth and later folklore, with dates kept distinct;
- imperial or colonial relationships and historical afterlife;
- portable historical structures;
- unsuitable or misleading borrowings;
- possible Package E applications;
- collisions with established World Egg canon.

The dossiers mark four epistemic categories:

- **Source fact** — directly supported by archaeology, texts or institutional scholarship.
- **Scholarly dispute** — evidence or interpretation remains contested.
- **Inference** — a cautious reconstruction joining several facts.
- **World Egg translation** — a design use, not a claim about history and not canon.

Repeated labels are not attached to every sentence; profile sections make the category explicit. Translation blocks never retroactively convert analogy into fact.

## Translation controls

- “Greek”, “Phoenician”, “Punic”, “Roman”, “Illyrian” and modern national labels are not timeless ethnic substances. Local communities, settlers, imperial administrators and later historians must be distinguished.
- Pottery, architecture or cult objects demonstrate contact and practice; alone they do not prove migration, language or common descent.
- “Colonisation” may include settlements, negotiated enclaves, conquest, land division and hybrid communities. It is not treated as one uniform event.
- Island communities are neither automatically isolated nor automatically cosmopolitan. Connectivity changed by season, technology, war, harbour access and political protection.
- Literary myths are evidence for the period and purposes of their recoverable tellings, not transparent Bronze Age reports.
- Later folklore may supply narrative motifs only when its later date is explicit.
- Piracy, privateering, corsairing, naval service, raiding and smuggling remain analytically distinct.
- Captivity, enslavement, sexual violence, forced rowing, sale and ransom are structural parts of maritime violence, not atmospheric colour.

## Source quality and limits

### Tier 1 — controlling scholarship and primary evidence

Peer-reviewed books and articles; excavation reports; archaeological survey projects; critical editions and ancient texts read with source criticism. These control disputed claims and regional interpretation.

### Tier 2 — strong institutional synthesis

UNESCO site dossiers; national archaeological services; major museums; university projects. These are especially useful for site sequence, material culture and preservation, but nomination files can simplify local complexity.

### Tier 3 — orientation and source discovery

PathfinderWiki, Wikipedia and general encyclopaedias. They identify cases and bibliography but do not carry substantive claims alone. PathfinderWiki is used only to locate classic 1E Paizo material.

### Evidentiary limits

Ancient citizenship, family life and slavery are unevenly documented outside literate city-states. Several island clusters have strong excavation records but poor political texts. Modern boundaries distort ancient seaways. Submerged coastlines, harbour silting, uneven publication and twentieth-century excavation priorities all bias the surviving record. The dossiers therefore avoid false symmetry: some profiles can recover institutions; others mainly recover settlement, material exchange and imperial pressure.
