# Ancient World Synchronisation Ledger

## Function

This ledger keeps the regional histories on one calendar while allowing them to develop at different speeds. It records relationships that another package must respect, identifies provisional geography and separates approved canon from material awaiting review.

## Material relationships

### Food and water

The Sacred Basin exports grain, dates, linen and preserved fish in years of dependable water. The Gateway country supplies salt, imported timber, metals, ship-borne goods and access to markets beyond the inner sea. Qahuri rulers repeatedly discover that control of the river does not give them command of the coast. Gateway councils repeatedly discover that ships cannot replace failed harvests.

### Transport

River boats move bulky food and stone within the basin. Pack trains cross the drier approaches between the lower river and Esharat. Coastal shipping links Sarqen and Qarzeth to the island corridors and Atherian ports. Campaign seasons depend upon water storage, fodder, prevailing winds and the repair of hulls. No army crosses the desert or inner sea merely because a ruler orders it.

### Labour and slavery

Both societies use coerced labour, while their institutions differ. Qahuri crowns command seasonal canal work, temple dependants, debt-bound households, prisoners and enslaved captives. Gateway households own enslaved workers, dependent towns owe levies, and armies include citizens, allies, subject troops and paid companies. These categories must remain visible when the histories are promoted.

### Religion

Qahuri communities maintain flood bargains, mortuary cults and later royal celestial rites. Gateway communities possess shore cults, household ancestors and the bronze-faced provider's sacrificial establishment. The provider is a dependent Gluttony Mask. Similar sun discs, boats and ancestral titles create human identifications without merging Menhir, Mask or the Boatman.

The White Measure is a minority Qahuri development within this religious world. A successful House-Builder remains a complete human soul bound to a corpse-anchored Egg White demiplane and may act as a Patron. Failed tombs, estate offices, residue and Greed Masks require separate identification. Gateway use of a funerary store or Patron service does not make the coast part of the movement.

## Package B material relationships

### Road, river and sea

The **Dawn Road** joins Kavresh, Daryam, Durqesh, Arqesh and Avesh. Jirahar road houses maintain older branches across Reqa and towards Qahur. The Ubar carries grain and heavy goods between Arqesh, Nemeru and the lower canal country. Tasar and Ilyessa connect those land routes to the eastern inner sea. No one government controls all three systems.

### Food and military supply

Ubar grain, Vashari animals, highland metal and coastal ships sustain the Vasharan empire. Qiryath and the tower country provide interpreters, pack services and smaller stores. The great invasion of 497–491 BR fails after naval defeat separates the land army from regular sea supply; the army remains dangerous while hunger, disease and lost animals force withdrawal.

### Marriage, hostages and resettlement

Arqeshite, Vashari, Qahuri, Jirahar and coastal houses exchange spouses, political wards and hostages. The same relationship may secure trade and expose a child to execution when alliances change. Deportation creates mixed neighbourhoods through coercion. Later marriages among their descendants cannot turn the original removal into voluntary migration.

### Slavery and labour

Arqesh and Vashar use enslaved captives, debt-bound households, conscripted villagers and paid specialists in different combinations. Canal repair, road building, estates, mines, households and armies all depend upon labour whose freedom varies. Coalition victory over House Ubar ends one dynasty while producing new captives and estates.

### Religion and political image

Qiryath's worship, Arqeshite royal display and Daryam's teaching of Right Order remain separate traditions. The Crowned Countenance and Witness of Dawn are dependent Pride Masks exploiting different royal systems. Tended fires and royal images do not contain human souls. Astaroth's military correspondences among Atherians neither merge him with those Masks nor approve conquest.

## Map notes for later revision

- Show the Sacred Basin as a cultivated river corridor north of the colder Atherian heartlands, with a northern dry margin and eastern approaches towards the river empires.
- Preserve Qasr Ilyun on the Glass-facing frontier of later Al-Qahur.
- Place the ancient Gateway country at the Qabir–Zanthir approach where cultivated land, desert roads and Thalassian shipping meet.
- Mark Qarzeth and Sarqen as separate sites. Qarzeth occupies a defensible principal harbour with nearby cultivated land; Sarqen lies at a smaller sheltered landing that can enlarge after Qarzeth's destruction.
- Place Esharat inland from Sarqen on a cart and pack route to cultivated country and the basin approaches.
- Preserve the ruined capital's salted district as local terrain. It does not sterilise the whole gateway country.

These are cartographic suggestions. They do not revise the current map.

## Protected and unresolved matters

- Ishkatar's lost river and the Sacred Basin's living river remain separate.
- The fate of individual Titan-Firstborn and Giant rulers remains governed by existing continuity rules.
- Vashari, Vashar, House Tiravan, Ortheia, Eryndor and the principal Successor names are canon at the degree recorded in their articles.
- The Gluttony Mask's condition after the destruction of its Qarzeth feeding architecture remains unknown.
- No Qahuri flood response proves a king righteous or a dynasty divinely chosen.
- No Gateway or Nerethan ancestry carries inherited guilt for child sacrifice.
- Post-1360 outcomes, protected Weirds and true Idol succession remain untouched.
