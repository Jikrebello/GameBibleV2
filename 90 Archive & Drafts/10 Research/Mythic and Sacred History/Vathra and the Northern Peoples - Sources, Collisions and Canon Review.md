# Vathra and the Northern Peoples — Sources, Collisions and Canon Review

## Review purpose

This document controls the Package C development proposed in:

- [[Historical Atlas of Vathra and the Northern Ground before Atheria]]
- [[Vathra - Crown-Cities, Houses and the Great Unyoking]]
- [[The Grounded Tarkhan - Wells, Grasslands and First Kings]]
- [[The Ahdari before the Covenant - Camps, Markets and Well Countries]]

The four accounts give the northern world a connected history from Vathra's last century to the balance of 350 BR. They remain author-only. This review distinguishes established canon, incorporated research, new proposals and protected questions so that an eventual promotion can proceed without importing superseded ideas.

## Controlling canon

The following decisions govern the package.

1. The Great Unbinding culminates around 2300–2260 BR. The Great Prophet and the Glass refuge belong to a different part of that history.
2. The One Glass People remain in the desert until the Prophet's death around 2165 BR. Southern separation follows; the northern fission develops gradually between about 1950 and 1750 BR.
3. Tarkhan are principally Orphaned humans whose ancestors survived outside the Glass.
4. Ahdari ancestry develops from the Near-Glass camps as they join and compete with older desert populations.
5. Vathra is a lesser Giant crown within a many-state Giant world. It is not a sixth Imperial Horizon.
6. Human souls, Titan continuity, Giant continuity, court persons, Masks, Patron roles, offices and constructed patterns remain distinct.
7. The First Catch of 905–920 AR is the decisive Tarkhan horse revolution. Package C cannot place an equivalent mounted civilisation in antiquity.
8. Arqesh destroys First Qiryath in 742 BR and falls during the wars ending around 690 BR. Arezan Tiravan's coalition includes northern leaders pursuing their own aims.
9. Vasharan power follows Arqesh and reaches selected Qahuri and northern routes without governing every dry-country society.
10. Lethren Ostar dies at Rethmar in 381 BR after Kharuz and Tavisha defeat his expedition.
11. The Nine Wells of Tergai are ancestral Tarkhan works seized by Covenant forces in 558 AR.
12. Mujalib receives the Covenant revelation in 541 AR. No ancient Ahdari practice may be described as Al-Ahdiya.

## Comparative sources and their use

### Pathfinder and Dungeons & Dragons

The Shory Empire provides several flying cities with different ends and dangerous surviving ruins. Dungeons & Dragons supplies cloud castles, ranked Giant courts and mobile fortresses. The World Egg translation uses distributed aerial power and plural collapse. It does not import Shory aeromancers, D&D Giant species or a universal floating-city engine.

### Eberron and Netheril

Xen'drik offers a history in which Giant wars, enslaved peoples and catastrophic magic leave a wounded landscape. Netheril demonstrates how magical abundance can depend on labour and extraction hidden beneath elite spectacle. Vathra uses these social and infrastructural functions. The Phoenix does not curse a people or land, and no mortal attempts a copied apotheosis.

### Flying palaces, carpets and winds

Aeolus, Puṣpaka, Solomonic wind carriage and the later flying-carpet tradition contribute captured currents, mobile sovereignty and textiles carried by a power beyond the object itself. The Vathran carpet therefore requires a prepared wind route, trained caller or active city. The comparison supplies no Solomon analogue and makes no claim about historical Arabian technology.

### ʿĀd and Iram

The received destruction of lofty ʿĀd by furious wind and the later transformation of Iram into a lost-city tradition supply bodily catastrophe and uncertain legendary afterlife. Vathra receives its own politics, metaphysics and chronology. No Vathran city substitutes for the Qurʾānic people or its judgement.

### Ancient Arabian open-country societies

Safaitic inscriptions, archaeology and later poetry support seasonal movement, sacrifice, grief, raids, literacy, lineage and prayer. Nabataean and client-king histories connect mobile households to towns and empires. Package C uses these relationships for Ahdari and western Tarkhan societies while keeping their distinct ancestry and theology.

The hard material remains explicit: murder during raids, enslavement, captive concubinage, forced marriage, feud, animal sacrifice and betrayal of sanctuary. Human sacrifice appears in the local Kor Ashal episode, supported structurally by the external ancient report concerning Duma. It never becomes a universal desert custom.

### Hunnic, Turkic and Mongol polities

These histories supply rapid coalition, dynastic confederation, route government, conquered specialists, remount logistics and fragmentation among successors. They belong chiefly to Qarayel and the Horse Centuries. Ancient client courts may show temporary multi-house following and foreign subsidy, but they receive no remount empire, decimal army or continent-wide relay.

### Apache horse adoption

Recent evidence for earlier and socially networked horse integration offers a strong model for revising the First Catch. It has little direct role in the pre-350 history. Its presence in the research corpus prevents later canon from describing the Tarkhan horse revolution as one day's theft followed by instant mastery.

## Plain causal chains

### Vathra's fall

Failing routes and disputed succession → Ozhur and Varakesh increase extraction and attack rival infrastructure → human operators coordinate sabotage while Giant courts fight → anchors fail, cities crash or descend and subject camps escape → rulers, workers and uninvolved families die; food distribution collapses → Vathra ceases to govern and human communities inherit useful ruins, famine and political freedom.

### Tarkhan formation

Vathran ground populations survive in separate ecological zones → wells, herds, fosterage and craft require repeated cooperation → shared assemblies, speech and stories connect western and eastern communities → outsiders and northern singers adopt a broad Tarkhan name → local identities remain while a durable people forms → later horse adoption can transform this people without creating it.

### Ahdari formation

Near-Glass unity weakens after the Prophet and southern departure → some families invest in defended water and enforceable local protection → older well-country and Tarkhan populations join through marriage, service, adoption, captivity and manumission → shared speech and obligations spread between camel roads and markets → Ahdari peoplehood develops → later Covenant revelation enters an old civilisation rather than an empty desert.

### Rethmar

Ostar seeks water and passage for northern expansion → local leaders refuse and remove supplies → Kharuz separates the columns while Tavisha controls water → Ostar dies and the expedition collapses → captives, ransom and fever divide the victors → Rethmar remains independent and Atherian commanders inherit a practical warning about northern logistics.

## Collision review

### Vathra and the Five Imperial Horizons

Vathra remains one powerful lesser crown. Its flying cities do not change the register of Five Imperial Horizons. It traded and married with several horizons without becoming one of their complete civilisational systems.

### The Great Unyoking and the wider Unbinding

The Great Unyoking is a regional human revolt entangled with Vathran civil war. It neither causes the entire Unbinding nor replaces the Prophet's liberation. Heavenly severance remains separately commissioned.

### Vathra and Nythalum

Vathran cities fly within the Material atmosphere. Nythalum pursues physical lunar departure. The two technologies, destinations and political histories remain separate.

### Vathra and the Egg White

Ordinary Vathran flight uses Material sky, pressure and route correspondences. Yazuran's attempted escape remains open. No crown-city automatically becomes a Patron realm.

### Dazhur and the Caravan That Cast Off Its Wards

Dazhur discards an inhabited physical district during aerial battle. UH-01 concerns an Egg White escape, copied ancestors, decoy processions and a later Hunt. The participants, place, mechanism, evidence and consequence differ.

### The Walking Court and the Wall That Stayed

G-59 is a later human episode during the northern fission. Vathra's epithet “Walking Court” describes an older Giant kingdom. The stable title of G-59 and explicit link labels must preserve their non-identity.

### Narash and Tergai

The Hundred Wells of Narash are a broad western Giant-age caravan network. The Nine Wells of Tergai are a smaller water chain with ancestral Tarkhan tenure. They may share houses and trade without sharing a site or ownership history.

### Ancient horses and the First Catch

Vathran horses and Bronze-age chariot lines are scarce, concentrated and lost. The First Catch brings abundant remount stock, new tack, breeding and mounted networks after 905 AR. No ancient protagonist fights as a later Grass-Tarkhan horse archer.

### Tarkhan “riders” in Arqeshite accounts

Existing canon occasionally calls the northern participants in Arqesh's fall “Tarkhan riders.” Package C recommends changing the noun during canon integration to **leaders**, **scouts** or **fighters** unless a particular person belongs to the surviving limited riding houses. This is a wording reconciliation, not a change to the coalition.

### Ahdari and Ahl-e-Ahd

Ahdari is an ancient people. Ahl-e-Ahd is the later imperial-confessional civilisation containing several peoples. Reader-facing ancient history ends in 350 BR and should not foreshadow the Covenant by describing every institution as an unfinished form of later law.

### Rethmar and the Nine Wells

Rethmar is a winter gathering and market in the western well country. It is not one of the Nine Wells of Tergai. Ostar's defeat in 381 BR and the Covenant seizure of Tergai in 558 AR remain separate conflicts.

### Kor Ashal and Qarzeth

Kor Ashal is one local oasis sacrifice in the ancient well country. Qarzeth's child collections belong to the Gateway Republic's state cult and later wars with Caleran. The episodes share a horror without sharing a cult, Mask, institution or political result.

### Kharuz, Tavisha and later Hordes

Kharuz and Tavisha act centuries before the First Catch. They found no Horde and cannot be assigned to White, Iron, Moon or Ash identities. Their collaboration joins particular ancient communities for one defence.

- **Vathra** as the established name of the aerial Giant confederation.

## Material life and logistics check

The proposed campaigns depend on supplies rather than abstract mobility.

- Vathran cities consume water and food brought to anchor fields by camel train.
- Chariot houses require sheltered pasture, grain, wheelwrights, metal fittings and grooms.
- Ancient Tarkhan eastern movement uses walking herds, carts and sledges; western movement uses camels and donkeys.
- Arqeshite power reaches the region through garrisons at dependable water, hostages, grain payment and client houses.
- Rethmar defeats Ostar by removing water and separating columns; no supernatural storm is required.
- A mounted Tarkhan remount system remains absent until after 905 AR.

## Human-coherence check

Each major development has visible agency and consequence.

- Ozhur and Varakesh respond to route failure by increasing extraction and making war.
- Human workers revolt because they possess the skills and positions needed to interrupt the system.
- The fall of Vathra removes oppressive government and the distribution network upon which many subjects had depended.
- Shared ecological work draws post-Vathran populations into Tarkhan formation.
- Fixed water and inherited Glass institutions draw Near-Glass communities and older residents into Ahdari formation.
- Saqara's massacre moves trade to Mazar because merchants choose a less prestigious route with more dependable protection.
- Client courts rise through foreign food and arms and collapse when payment and consent fail.
- Rethmar survives because its defenders control water, animals and information.

## Canon-readiness assessment

The package is ready for authorial review. It satisfies the historical dependencies required for canon promotion:

- a connected chronology from Vathra to 350 BR;
- named recurring people, cities, houses and events;
