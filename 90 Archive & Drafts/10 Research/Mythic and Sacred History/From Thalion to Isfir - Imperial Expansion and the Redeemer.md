# From Thalion to Isfir

Thalion inherited a capital, a navy, the annexed port of Sarqen and an uneven belt of former republican conquests. Othran II inherited a state capable of sending grain from one province to another, assessing land, moving officers across the inner sea and enforcing a capital sentence against a teacher from an eastern tower town. The proposed history below follows the people and institutions that made that change possible. The revised succession is recorded in [[Age of Glory - Authorial History and Integration Review]]. The new regional settlements and Gospel developments are for review.

## The emperors and what they inherit

| **Aurelius Thalion, 350–318 BR** | Annexes Sarqen in 350. Settles veterans, maintains military pay and adopts Meren after Caelor's death. | Retains Sarqen's harbour workers and service contracts. On the southern approaches, starts surveying disputed land behind former republican garrisons. Some families recover access to common ground. Dovren crews sell carriage and repair work under terms they can enforce at their own ports. Cassara's store reports tell Valeran which works can be supplied. His quarry assessment makes distant towns pay in labour, and the families expelled in 274 lose housing as well as wages and harvest time. |

Samor's settlement rewards the towns that continued to supply him and makes others surrender land for veterans. |

Southern auxiliaries bargain to serve near home and keep their local officers. Where imperial pay becomes regular, some households choose service and settle near forts. Others lose grazing to those settlements. |
| **Aren I, 229–203 BR** | Develops bridges, cisterns and town assessments. Returns the Crown of Olveth in 219 BR. Adopts Orent, Tessa's nephew. | Extends governed southern country along adjoining river and hill routes whose towns can maintain crossings together. Tessa's correspondence carries complaints about shortages to court. The Olveth settlement makes the army return the shelter object while leaving its road garrison in place, which produces two different local memories of Aren's rule. |
| **Orent I, 203–174 BR** | Secures the Arcelon corridor c. 190–182 BR through war and coerced construction. | Uses shipping to supply the inner-sea end of the campaign. Arcelon opens a way towards Raveth. The corridor's workers leave a record of the springs and unstable ground that the official survey omits. |
| **Orent II, 174–151 BR** | Funds municipal works and crop relief. Mariel patronises public disputations. | **Elian I, 151–126 BR** | Secures a negotiated accession and widens provincial citizenship. | Offers access to office to municipal households that can meet public obligations. Qahuri grain and shipping contracts become more attractive to a court that has seen the costs of dependence on one river province. |
| **Cassen I, 101–76 BR** | Inherits Elian's estate administration through adoption. Imperial Star worship remains established. | Governs an eastern province where travel, petitions and sanctuary authority are already connected to imperial law. ## How the provinces join up

**South.** The early gains run from the old Rauven-facing approaches at Kellovar and Darneth to adjoining agricultural districts and selected northern Belathi river towns. Aren's works allow more of that territory to be assessed and held through winter. Later generations can extend along tributaries and nearby roads into the country that will contribute to Eclessia. The proposed limit is where carts, boats, markets and garrisons cease to support one another through the cold months. Brael Tor, Ossen Fen and the Tidewood harbours each need their own agreements or refusals. The existing Tidewood history gives Brackenmouth a purchasing station, Rookstrand repair contracts and Keldscar pilotage without a permanent garrison. Those arrangements can keep a fleet in service while the coastal communities remain politically independent. [[Historical Atlas of the Southern Cold World]] · [[History of the Tidewood Coast]] · [[The Imperial Army and the Frontiers]]

**Sea and Gateway.** Sarqen is directly governed from 350 BR. Across the Elmaric sea, the fleet needs protected stores, pilots who know the channels and yards willing to repair ships out of season. In the proposed sequence, eastern and central harbours obtain different terms as their councils and rivals bargain for escorts, access and market privileges. Seravon's pilot communities give Caleran a reason to negotiate separately with its ports. Vorthara's working precincts can bargain over supplies and access on their own terms. Ortheian and Delkosi authorities bargain for passage and duties at the eastern end. Date each foundation or charter for the future Sestaran cities after checking the local record. The network is strongest where a damaged ship can reach another willing port without returning to Caleran. [[The House of Nerethan]] · [[Historical Atlas of the Elmaric Sea before 350 BR]] · [[The Making of the Sestaran Cities]]

**East.** Arcelon is the first fixed gain in this sequence. Raveth's towns can choose a separate settlement over river tariffs and garrison protection while Vashar retains its highlands and substantial eastern territory. For Qiryath, a later charter negotiated under Cassen or Velen is the working proposal. Sanctuary authorities want dependable pilgrim access and protection of courts, sacrifice and teaching. Atheria wants assessed dues and control of capital cases involving public order. House Tiravan can accept a limited settlement while preserving trade and influence with the eastern tower communities. Avesh, Arqesh and the main Ubar reaches need their own geographic check before final terms. By 33 BR Isfir's birth town is already imperial. By 0 AR the Qiryath elders can bring a charge, and the imperial authorities have the power to impose death. [[The Two Roads of Arcelon]] · [[The Keeper of Borrowed Grain]] · [[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]] · [[Historical Spine of the Jirahar]]

**Qahur and the northern margin.** At 350 BR, Tenaret, Qemaret and Sakhret have their own courts and temple and canal jurisdictions. A late intervention can begin when rival crowns block grain and passage, leaving a lower-river coalition willing to invite Doran's protection at a port it can supply. Inland rule requires separate terms with canal officers, temple estates and town authorities that can keep the next sowing and levy viable. The proposed sequence is port and grain guarantees under Doran, intervention and direct assessment under Othran I, and established provincial administration by Isfir's birth. The order of the three crowns' submission, the cause of their dispute, and whether all of Sakhret's cultivated reach is held by 33 BR are open. Beyond the basin, defend particular wells, roads and mouths through negotiated access, tariffs and garrisons. The well communities still control the water and local knowledge that make overland movement possible. Dio's account of Octavian putting Egypt under a governor helps frame the change in jurisdiction. Qahur's three courts and canal obligations supply its own route to that outcome. [[Qahur and the Gateway before Atheria]] · [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]] · [[The Ahdari before the Covenant]] · [[The Tarkhan before the Horse Hordes]] · [Cassius Dio, Roman History 51.17](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Cassius_Dio/51%2A.html)

The draft reserves the decisive Atherian–Vasharan wars for 0–540 AR. For a Roman comparison, Tacitus's account of Armenia and Parthia supplies a precedent for rival powers contesting nearby routes and client rulers through local allegiances, negotiation and pressure. It gives a question to ask of House Tiravan and the border cities. Their own needs must explain the outcome. [Tacitus, Annals 2.1–4](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Tacitus/Annals/2A%2A.html)

## Isfir in the world Othran II inherits

The canon places Isfir's birth in an eastern Jirahar tower town in 33 BR, the opening of his public ministry around 3 BR, and his death and Resurrection at Second Qiryath in 0 AR. Isfir is the Lord entering human life, and the Resurrection restores the soul-road. [[The Rise of the Redeemer]] and [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]] record the trial, burial, Resurrection and later custody of the Ember.

1. **Birth and ordinary life, from 33 BR.** Begin with Amaryah, the carpenter's household, the Scrolls, craft, kin and pilgrimage. Luke situates Jesus's birth and John's ministry under named rulers, and makes the mother and ordinary witnesses central. Develop the announcement to Amaryah and her willing response. Decide separately how much of the Nativity to carry across, including the conception, the two-town geography and the flight from a ruler. The birth tower's name remains open. [Luke 1–3](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/luke/1) · [Matthew 1–2](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/1)
2. **Public ministry, c. 3 BR–0 AR.** Give Isfir a public beginning through a herald's call to repentance and washing, followed by his own proclamation of the Lord's kingdom. Use parables, shared meals, healing, exorcism, forgiveness and the call to follow. Let his Jirahar hearers argue about how he fulfils the Scrolls. The dispute is intelligible only if they already know and care about the law. Include women who travel, serve and bear witness within the existing cast and community. [Mark 1](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/mark/1) · [Matthew 5](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/matthew/5) · [Luke 8](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/luke/8)
3. **The Passion at Second Qiryath, 0 AR.** The pilgrimage brings the sanctuary authorities, the provincial governor and Isfir into the same city. Ezrahel's faction brings a religious charge and asks the governor to sentence Isfir as a rival king. The governor authorises the execution. Burial precedes the Resting Day, and Solana is among the women who return to the empty tomb on the third morning. [[The Binding at Qiryath - Trial Resurrection and the Eternal Ember]]
4. **Ascension, Holy Flame and first assemblies.** The Companions remain in Qiryath after the Ascension. The Gift commissions the gathered community. A coal from its hearth passes through Solana and Amaryah's household, and a daughter flame later reaches Caleran's martyrs' precinct. Acts gives a model for the spread of teaching and disputes over admission. The established thirteen called and the Empty Seat remain the basis of this draft. [Acts 1](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/acts/1) · [Acts 2](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/acts/2) · [Acts 15](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/acts/15)

## What the Resurrection changes first

For the Companions, the shared meal takes on a meaning they could understand fully only after Isfir's death. Their proclamation begins with what they claim to have seen. Their teaching has practical consequences when a host admits someone their household would once have turned away, a congregation provides for people with no local kin, or a witness refuses to honour the Star cult in exchange for release. These are proposed points of development for the first assemblies. [Acts 2](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/acts/2) · [Acts 4](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/acts/4) · [Acts 15](https://bible.usccb.org/bible/acts/15)

In Qiryath, the sanctuary keeps offering sacrifice and the sign of acceptance ceases. Its priests still have duties to perform and pilgrims still arrive. They and the people who depend on the sanctuary need to decide how to respond to something they can observe and explain differently. The Tower Revolt is decades away. Its causes include taxation, official violence and divisions over the sanctuary. [[Qiryath Reqa]] · [[Jirahar Memory of Isfir]] · [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]]

The imperial court keeps its army, courts and public Star worship. Its records can continue to describe a completed sentence while the witnesses carry a different account along the same routes that move petitions and grain. How Othran II learns of their claims and when imperial officials begin treating the assemblies as a legal problem belong to the first post-0 pass. Roman accounts offer several different scales for this response, from Tacitus's account of punishment at Rome to Pliny's later consultation with Trajan over cases in his province.
