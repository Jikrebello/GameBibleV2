# From Thalion to Isfir

Thalion inherited a capital, a navy, the annexed port of Sarqen and an uneven belt of former republican conquests. Odrun II inherited a state capable of sending grain from one province to another, assessing land, moving officers across the inner sea and enforcing a capital sentence against a teacher from an eastern tower town. The proposed history below follows the people and institutions that made that change possible. The revised succession and the decided Causality Pass 03 history are recorded in [[Age of Glory - Authorial History and Integration Review]]; the regional settlements in the south and the Elmaric sea remain for review.

## How the provinces join up

**South.** The early gains run from the old Rauven-facing approaches at Kellovar and Darneth to adjoining agricultural districts and selected northern Belathi river towns. Aren's works allow more of that territory to be assessed and held through winter. Later generations can extend along tributaries and nearby roads into the country that will contribute to Eclessia. The proposed limit is where carts, boats, markets and garrisons cease to support one another through the cold months. Brael Tor, Ossen Fen and the Tidewood harbours each need their own agreements or refusals. The existing Tidewood history gives Brackenmouth a purchasing station, Rookstrand repair contracts and Keldscar pilotage without a permanent garrison. Those arrangements can keep a fleet in service while the coastal communities remain politically independent. [[Historical Atlas of the Southern Cold World]] · [[History of the Tidewood Coast]] · [[The Imperial Army and the Frontiers]]

**Sea and Gateway.** Sarqen is directly governed from 350 BR. Across the Elmaric sea, the fleet needs protected stores, pilots who know the channels and yards willing to repair ships out of season. In the proposed sequence, eastern and central harbours obtain different terms as their councils and rivals bargain for escorts, access and market privileges. Seravon's pilot communities give Caleran a reason to negotiate separately with its ports. Vorthara's working precincts can bargain over supplies and access on their own terms. Ortheian and Delkosi authorities bargain for passage and duties at the eastern end. The foundation and charter dates of the future Sestaran cities remain open. The network is strongest where a damaged ship can reach another willing port without returning to Caleran. [[The House of Nerethan]] · [[Historical Atlas of the Elmaric Sea before 350 BR]] · [[The Making of the Sestaran Cities]]

**East.** Arcelon is the first fixed gain in this sequence, built to answer Vashar's Dawn Road. Raveth's towns, tributaries under the Avesh command's canal duty, choose a separate settlement over river tariffs and garrison protection c. 160 BR while Vashar retains its highlands and substantial eastern territory. Qiryath passes to Atheria under the late Halreths: during Vashar's War of the Two Dawns the sanctuary Keeper dies, the sign answers an interim Keeper while a Vasharan purchaser holds the confirmation, the Atherian governor's augur asks the sign to decide, the purchaser refuses and no sign is given at the interim Keeper's offering, and the governor rules on the sanctuary's record. A Qoreth scribe drafts the charter: protected sacrifice, courts and burial jurisdiction; no image in the sanctuary; a daily offering for the emperor; the governor's capital jurisdiction and seat at the pilgrimage; the dues as surety; a copy in each archive. Vashar accepts it early in Velen's reign for the Dawn Portion and calls it a lease. The precise lines at Avesh, Arqesh and the main Ubar reaches remain open. By 33 BR Isfir's birth town is already imperial and protected by the charter. By 0 AR the Qiryath elders can bring a charge, and the imperial authorities have the power to impose death. [[The Two Roads of Arcelon]] · [[The Keeper of Borrowed Grain]] · [[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]] · [[Vashar and the Crown of Roads and Dawns]] · [[Historical Spine of the Jirahar]]

**Qahur and the northern margin.** At 350 BR, Tenaret, Qemaret and Sakhret have their own courts and temple and canal jurisdictions, and Vasharan agents collect tribute and sponsor claimants. Vasharan tribute lapses during the War of the Two Dawns, so the same crisis loosens Vashar's hold on both the Basin and Qiryath. At 0 AR Qahur is three crowns inside the Atherian sphere: Qemaret's lower river is bound by grain and harbour contracts, with an Atherian station at its harbour, and Tenaret and Sakhret are crowned clients. Any direct rule belongs after 0 AR. Sarqen remains the only royal country placed under a governor after its queen's fall. Beyond the basin, Atheria defends particular wells, roads and mouths through negotiated access, tariffs and garrisons. The well communities still control the water and local knowledge that make overland movement possible. [[Qahur and the Gateway before Atheria]] · [[Ancient Qahur and the Twin-Reed Crowns]] · [[The Ahdari before the Covenant]] · [[The Tarkhan before the Horse Hordes]]

The decisive Atherian–Vasharan wars belong to 0–540 AR. House Tiravan and the border cities contest routes and client rulers through local allegiances, negotiation and pressure, and their own needs explain the outcome; the Dawn Portion's end in 70 AR is one of their grievances.

## Isfir in the world Odrun II inherits

The canon places Isfir's birth in an eastern Jirahar tower town in 33 BR, the opening of his public ministry around 3 BR, and his death and Resurrection at Second Qiryath in 0 AR. Isfir is the Lord entering human life, and the Resurrection restores the soul-road. [[The Rise of the Redeemer]], [[The Binding at Qiryath - Trial Resurrection and the Eternal Ember]] and [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]] record the trial, burial, Resurrection and later custody of the Ember. The governor, the augur, the referral, the threat of complaint to Caleran and the elder's petition for the body all run through the Qiryath charter.

## What the Resurrection changes first

For the Companions, the shared meal takes on a meaning they could understand fully only after Isfir's death. Their proclamation begins with what they claim to have seen. Their teaching has practical consequences when a host admits someone their household would once have turned away, a congregation provides for people with no local kin, or a witness refuses to honour the Star cult in exchange for release. These are proposed points of development for the first assemblies.

In Qiryath, the sanctuary keeps offering sacrifice and the sign of acceptance ceases. Its priests still have duties to perform and pilgrims still arrive. They and the people who depend on the sanctuary need to decide how to respond to something they can observe and explain differently. The Tower Revolt is decades away. Its causes include taxation, official violence, the charter's surety clause and divisions over the sanctuary. [[Qiryath Reqa]] · [[Jirahar Memory of Isfir]] · [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]]

The imperial court keeps its army, courts and public Star worship. Its records can continue to describe a completed sentence while the witnesses carry a different account along the same routes that move petitions and grain. How Odrun II learns of their claims and when imperial officials begin treating the assemblies as a legal problem belong to the post-0 history. [[The Age of the Redeemer]] · [[The Ember Underground]]
