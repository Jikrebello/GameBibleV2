## Review Question

The six capsules in [[First Unknown Histories - Six Recoverable Lore Capsules]] were designed from existing conflict ecologies and source grammars. Density is desirable, but repeated pressures must not silently become one event or create duplicate stories distinguished only by titles. This review therefore asks five separate questions of each capsule:

1. Is it the same event as an earlier root?
2. Does it reuse any person, polity, document, relic, route, ruin, institution, or descendant population?
3. Does it perform a distinct historical function?
4. Does its discovery chain rely on distinct evidence?
5. Can it remain separate without removing the source's hard edge or its World Egg mechanism?

The governing ruling is **separate identity throughout**. Recurring Giant flight, infernal imprisonment, coerced refuge, return litigation, and partisan saint claims are historical families, not proof that every example belongs to one hidden institution.

# Capsule Reviews

## UH-01 — Separate Decoy-Caravan Crime

The Cut-Loose Wards already owns a clear function: dependent districts petition over a Patron court's choice to preserve elite estates, with contradictory ballast lists and severed foundations as the durable carrier. UH-01 originally repeated too much of that anatomy. Its distinctive strength was elsewhere: copied ancestors, incompatible evacuation missions, stolen banners, and a High Hunt redirected into innocent refugees.

The revised capsule therefore has no surviving collective petition, ballast register, matching foundation, inherited tenant-law institution, or shared descendant claimant. Its evidence arises from three mutually exclusive service itineraries, two false procession carcasses, Hunt scoring marks, palace seals attached to the wrong childhoods, and a present classification hearing involving a wrecked decoy. The abandoned wards remain real because removing that crime would sanitize the event; they no longer perform the same documentary role as the Cut-Loose Wards.

**Final ruling:** separate event inside the Hunt of the Escaped Courts. `revise`.

## UH-02 — Visible Source, Sufficient Transformation

UH-02 intentionally preserves the causal sequence that makes Genesis 34 morally severe: sexual violation, an attempted property-and-marriage settlement, deceptive disarmament, collective killing, plunder, captive women and children, and kin guilt that cannot be made clean by defending family honor. Removing one of those acts merely to disguise the source would violate severity parity.

The World Egg history nevertheless changes the decisive historical axes. The aggressors are famine refugees negotiating guest admission rather than a settled patriarchal household seeking intermarriage; the disarmament mechanism is a noncovenantal communal weapon-lock; the violated woman explicitly refuses the proposed settlement; captives and attackers form one mixed successor community; the long consequence is maternal challenge law and separate testimony; and the truth is recovered through a killing layer, lock tokens, granary seal, legal anomalies, and displaced laments. It shares no person, line, town, company, or artifact with another seed.

**Final ruling:** retain as an independent Scattering atrocity with honestly visible scriptural grammar. `retain`.

## UH-03 — One Mortal Aperture into a Larger War

The War of the Last Map is an umbrella ecology older and larger than recorded humanity. UH-03 is now bounded to one c. 830–790 BR summoning programme and the prisoners it supplied. Its map-lord is one damned human person retaining `human-soul`. The devil map mantle is a separate `infrastructure-office`. The branded captives remain human persons. Their detachable road-names are command tokens and never copies of their souls or identities. The Abyssal contradiction remains an `abyssal-wound`, not a people or moral faction.

The prisoners' revolt contains one directed wound because they corrupt the office's station sequence and destroy the mortal replacement route. It does not heal the Abyss, defend Hell, defeat either dominion, or disclose the war's beginning. No other planted history shares its summoning cellar, forty-pace road, collars, prisoners, mandate, or trapped garrisons.

**Final ruling:** retain as the first recoverable local episode of the War of the Last Map. `retain`.

## UH-04 — Failed Platform, Not Living Brass Metropolis

EL-R01 remains available for a living, cosmopolitan Fire-littoral metropolis with durable government, monopolized forge-charge, industrial classes, foreign trade, and an ongoing population. UH-04 now concerns only several hundred refugees on one fortified furnace platform which fails within its founding generation. It has one unnamed Anchor-Lord, one Material heat sink, one hereditary labor regime, and no surviving government or stable present route.

The emergency collars can reproduce a sub-minute aperture onto a dead workshop. That procedural test does not reopen a polity. The Patron escapes, but no current owner, anchor, client population, or descendant settlement is presumed. People left in sealed vaults may have died or escaped elsewhere; they cannot quietly become the population of EL-R01. The slavery, burning, lethal gate fight, and genuine initial rescue remain intact.

**Final ruling:** separate failed refuge using the same broad Fire-littoral grammar. `revise`.

## UH-05 — Municipal Reprisal, Not Refugee-Treasury Tradition

The Unreturned Ledger concerns displaced clerks recording property and the people who carried or maintained it, with refugee credit and possible Patron-realm history behind its surviving trace. The Deed in the Bread Jar concerns one household parcel, subsistence, custody, and preserved documentary continuity. UH-05 now excludes both structures.

Its town never leaves the Material world. Its central document is a municipal water-court maintenance register, destroyed locally after a mass execution. Its evidence consists of a hospital-wall grave, reconstruction weapons, scraped municipal tax tablets, repair chronology, a merchant's paired debts, and a monastery's redacted copy. No ledger, clerk, treasury, route, room, service-credit formula, bread jar, starter, household deed, or parcel crosses between the histories. Similar disputes arise because return after occupation repeatedly produces conflicting claims, not because one archive secretly travels through every case.

**Final ruling:** separate local crime within the Reconquest's wider property problem. `revise`.

## UH-06 — Inland Military Saint, Not the Two Ferries

The Saint of the Two Ferries remains a pre-Split holy person associated with a paired river district, ferry and hospice labor, two cities, divided local reception, and a still-open manifestation question. UH-06 now uses none of those elements. Its saint is a different early military physician. Its site is a landlocked mountain fortress. Its claimants are rival army cults rather than paired civic ferry communities. Its evidence consists of campaign standards, a supply-postern contract, a prison list, grave theft, a refuse-gate windlass, porter songs, and an arsenal hearing.

The saint's manifestation is authorially fixed only for this exploratory capsule and acts at the enemy gate under one custody commission. The associated `infrastructure-office` belongs to the gate mechanism and cannot summon or contain the saint. The Patron apparition and Envy Mask remain separate counterfeit actors. This preserves the broader Orthodox grammar in which a saint answers at the morally necessary place rather than the politically requested battlefield without duplicating the Two Ferries' saint, geography, relic problem, or institution.

**Final ruling:** separate fortress episode within the War for the Common Saints. `revise`.
