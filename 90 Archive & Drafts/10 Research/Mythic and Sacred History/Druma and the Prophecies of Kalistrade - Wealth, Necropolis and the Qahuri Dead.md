# Druma and the Prophecies of Kalistrade — Wealth, Necropolis and the Qahuri Dead

> [!success] Canon integration
> The accepted World Egg translation is now canonical in [[The White Measure and the Second Houses]]. This document remains the comparative source study.

## Purpose

Druma provides a useful model for joining mercantile discipline, bodily purity, conspicuous wealth and funerary ambition. Its strongest contribution to the World Egg is not a country ruled by merchants. Ancient Qahur already has its own river kingdoms, temple estates, royal houses and foreign conquests. The more fruitful translation is a religious and economic movement within that civilisation: prosperous people who treat a lifetime of acquisition as the construction of a private estate beyond death.

This movement would descend from Qahuri encounters with Ishkatari tomb practice. It would preserve the full arrogance of the model while obeying the World Egg's soul rules. A successful rite catches the practitioner's complete human soul during bodily death, binds it to the prepared corpse and funerary anchor, and opens a small demiplane in the Egg White. The deceased becomes a self-bound human lich and, when able to lease shelter, power or access from the new realm, an extradimensional Patron. The rite delays the Boatman and final judgement; it does not abolish either. Failed and corrupted rites may instead leave residue, an intelligent tomb-office, a copy or an opening for a Greed Mask.

Everything proposed below remains author-only research. It adds no new canon, mummy species, Idol succession or historical event.

## The Pathfinder source

### Public doctrine

The **Prophecies of Kalistrade** are an economic mysticism founded by the human teacher Kalistrade and enlarged by his followers. Current Pathfinder material describes the work as a mixture of fables, parables, occult speculation, economic teaching and rules for bodily and mental purity. Its adherents seek personal wealth both as a route towards enlightenment and as evidence that they have attained it.

The discipline is ascetic despite its opulence. Kalistocrats meditate, regulate their diet, avoid most physical contact and wear enveloping white clothing and gloves. Charity and waste are treated as failures of purity, while clever bargaining and even the exploitation of fellow believers may be praised. Earlier game material likewise presents them as calm, intensely disciplined and ruthless merchants who search for favourable readings and loopholes. Decorative wealth also has practical supernatural force: money can be consumed by magic, hidden beyond ordinary reach and used to purchase extraordinary services.

Druma turns these practices into a complete social order. Wealth is visible theology, commercial success is moral evidence, and the movement's international investments extend its influence beyond the country. The doctrine therefore creates a powerful circular defence. Success proves obedience. Failure can be blamed upon impurity, insufficient discipline or a mistaken interpretation.

### The concealed purpose

*Druma, Profit and Prophecy* gives the discipline a secret end. Proven adherents prepare themselves for a lethal funerary rite. Precious substances incorporated into the body and an immense sacrifice of accumulated wealth preserve the corpse and support a private mindscape. Attendants may bind themselves to the principal practitioner and enter the promised estate as servants or companions. Hidden mausoleums consequently contain both extraordinary treasure and those who believe that they have escaped ordinary divine judgement.

This secret changes the meaning of every public observance. Dietary restrictions, avoidance of touch, meditation and acquisition are stages in a lifelong preparation of body and will. Wealth is not merely a sign of favour or a useful worldly resource. It becomes the substance from which the adherent expects to build eternity.

Later Starfinder material extends the same idea into mausoleum ships containing preserved, digitised or otherwise interred consciousnesses and their attendants. That development is not necessary for the Qahuri comparison, but it confirms the source grammar: a rich person's tomb becomes a private polity, a technological or occult afterlife and a dangerous expedition site at the same time.

### What is load-bearing

The following elements give the source its distinctive power:

- severe bodily discipline practised by people surrounded by wealth;
- commercial success treated as evidence of spiritual worth;
- acquisition undertaken for a posthumous purpose rather than ordinary luxury;
- the body slowly prepared as part of the final work;
- a hidden funerary truth known only to successful initiates;
- servants and dependants carried into the patron's promised eternity;
- mausoleums that are treasuries, courts, homes and afterlife machines;
- disagreement between the adherents' claim and the wider order of death.

A Qahuri translation should retain all eight. White gloves, a merchant government, Kalistrade's biography and Pathfinder's precise mindscape ritual are surface particulars and need not be reproduced.

## Why the idea belongs beside Ishkatar and Qahur

Ishkatar made ownership into funerary technology. Its rulers assigned human beings to tomb inventories, embalmed servants beneath property titles and built constructs able to preserve claims long after the claimant's bodily death. The kingdom's continuity projects could maintain records, residue, offices and simulations. They could not create a human soul, restore one that had routed onwards or reconstruct a dead Giant without uninterrupted continuity.

Ancient Qahur inherits fragments of this world without becoming its continuation. Refugees, labour households and scavengers carried embalming skills, funerary images and pieces of Ishkatari machinery into the living Sacred Basin. Qahuri kings later claimed serpent ancestry and founded mortuary estates supported by fields, workshops and dependent villages. The material ground therefore already exists for a human reinterpretation of Ishkatar's central temptation.

The branch becomes distinct when royal blood ceases to be the only path towards a monumental afterlife. A ship-owner, physician, grain factor, embalmer or workshop mistress may build a posthumous house through discipline and accumulated wealth. The movement offers rich commoners and lesser officials something that the old dynastic cults reserved for kings: the promise of becoming an enduring proprietor rather than a remembered ancestor.

This development also gives **Greed** a recognisably Qahuri cultural expression. Ishkatar's older feeding architecture says that everything may belong to the ruler. The Qahuri branch says that a sufficiently disciplined person can withdraw wealth, land and dependants from the living world and carry them into a perfected private estate. Humanity supplies the doctrine, images, rituals and institutions. An existing Greed Throne exploits that architecture through dependent Masks; human belief does not create the Idol.

## Recommended World Egg translation: the White Measure

**The White Measure** is the provisional name for a Qahuri mortuary discipline. Its mature adherents may be called **the House-Builders**, because they speak of constructing a final house which flood, conquest and inheritance cannot divide. The name belongs to the research nursery and should be tested against later Qahuri vocabulary before promotion.

The movement teaches that appetite scatters the self. Food, touch, sex, anger, gifts and waste all create claims through which the world enters and divides a person. A disciplined life closes those openings. Wealth acquired without bodily indulgence becomes a visible measure of mastery and a store of ordered substance. At death, the initiate expects that wealth to furnish the **Second House**, a private country shaped around the preserved body.

House-Builders wear clean linen and restrict touch during specified periods of trade and funerary preparation. Their food rules privilege dry, measured and easily traced ingredients. The final years require medicines containing minute quantities of gold, copper, salt and mineral resins. These treatments change the body and make later embalming more effective. They also cause pain, infertility, tremors and early death in practitioners whose physicians misjudge the dose.

The public teaching praises thrift, composure, accurate weights and the reinvestment of profit. It supports schools, workshops, caravan partnerships and carefully provisioned ships. Its adherents can be excellent employers when a skilled household is considered part of the founder's future glory. The same principle makes them ruthless. Gifts without return diminish the Second House. A field assigned to a tomb should never return to village use. A servant raised within a funerary household may be told that lifelong dependence is the highest form of belonging.

The inner teaching reveals the final rite. The body is embalmed with treated oils and precious metal. A personal chamber is surrounded by model rooms, familiar possessions, spoken memories, household images and the bodies or memorial patterns of selected attendants. Wealth is sealed into the walls and foundations rather than merely piled beside the corpse. These components supply metaphysical mass, while the body's true name and final breath form a tether into the Egg White. The initiate expects to wake inside a perfected version of the estate and continue enlarging it forever.

In a successful rite, that expectation is substantially fulfilled. The Second House becomes a genuine made place within the Egg White. Its rooms express the founder's planned estate, its local laws favour possession and measured exchange, and its stability depends upon the Material necropolis. The founder awakens there as the same human person, held in a self-authored soul-binding. The embalmed body serves as the innermost anchor and may be inhabited, animated or projected through as a Material proxy.

The House-Builder becomes a Patron once the Second House possesses enough stability to lease something beyond itself. It may offer descendants safe storage, occult instruction, recovery of family memories, temporary refuge, divination or power exercised through funerary objects. Those gifts establish real relationships with the living. They do not confer divine authority upon the founder or turn the demiplane into a lawful human afterlife.

## What actually wakes

The White Measure does not produce one metaphysical result. A completed soul-binding and Egg White anchoring constitute its true occult success. Priests and descendants nevertheless mistake several failed, parasitised or imitative results for lesser grades of the same achievement.

| Tomb result | What it is | Relation to the dead person | Typical behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Second-House Patron** | `bound-human-soul` owning a corpse-anchored Egg White demiplane | the original human person, deliberately held from routing | governs the private realm, sends proxies, bargains with the living and enlarges its anchor |
| **The silent failure** | preserved corpse and inactive funerary machinery | the human soul has routed onwards | no person remains; later priests may invent answers |
| **The remembered proprietor** | organised `residue` awakened through body, habits and repeated household praise | a new residue-born mind resembling the deceased | repeats tastes, grudges and routines; believes itself entitled to unfinished property |
| **The speaking house** | `infrastructure-office` or `constructed-pattern` distributed through tomb, images and guardians | an estate using its founder as a governing model | answers as the proprietor, assigns tasks and repairs losses without possessing a human soul |
| **The captive attendant** | `bound-human-soul` attached to another person's anchor | the original servant, spouse, child, priest or other victim | may serve, resist, seek escape or seize part of the Second House from its proprietor |
| **The golden guest** | dependent Greed Mask or Idol-grown pattern using the tomb persona | a predator wearing the deceased's biography | rewards acquisition, consumes praise and pushes several estates towards a shared feeding system |
| **The Ishkatari claimant** | Giant residue, empty inherited office, reconstruction or rare separately continuous Giant | no automatic relation to the Qahuri patron whose tomb contains it | seeks old royal property and treats the human cult as an imitation or useful dependency |

These categories must remain separate even when an encounter presents several at once. A successful Patron may rule one chamber while a speaking sarcophagus-office controls the Material estate, a sealed attendant contains another bound human soul and a gilded image serves a Greed Mask. Destroying the image would not release either soul. Speaking the proprietor's childhood name would not make a construct human. A miracle affecting one part would authenticate only that response.

### Anatomy of a successful Second House

The rite satisfies the existing requirements for a made demiplane:

- **Anchor:** the preserved body, its true-name binding and the precious material joined to the necropolis;
- **Governing law:** the founder's funerary plan, normally expressed through ownership, measured exchange, household rank and exclusion of the unassigned;
- **Upkeep:** ritual attention, repaired correspondence, Material agents, funerary wealth, memory, labour and new anchor mass;
- **Return tether:** the sealed burial chamber and associated objects through which the Patron reaches the Material Realm;
- **Owner continuity:** the founder's complete `bound-human-soul`;
- **Inhabitants:** the founder, voluntarily or coercively bound human attendants, living visitors, proxies, constructs, residue minds and possible parasites;
- **Failure state:** contraction, corrupted rooms, loss of Material access, invasion by Drift ecology, seizure by a rival Patron or final release of the founder towards judgement.

The soul-binding must take hold before the departing person passes beyond recovery. Preparation of the living body makes the final transition predictable, while the funerary attendants speak the true-name sequence and close the tether. Failure at this moment explains why an immaculate corpse may contain no one and why a damaged rite may preserve only residue.

### The soul-road boundary

The World Egg can adopt the practical success of the Kalistocratic rite without accepting its theological conclusion. A human soul normally leaves at death and enters the Vestibule. The White Measure interrupts that departure by binding the complete soul into an Egg White anchor. The founder therefore remains the same human person and may become a Patron, but has delayed judgement rather than escaped the Lord.

Human self-binding remains objectively evil even when freely chosen. It is a refusal of the soul-road and an attempt to make created wealth sovereign over death. Binding dependants compounds that evil, especially where consent has been shaped by slavery, debt, childhood dependence or deception. Breaking the founder's true-name cage or irreparably severing the anchor releases the soul towards judgement. It does not annihilate the person, guarantee salvation or restore the body to mortal life.

The demiplane is real rather than a hallucination, although its rooms may be shaped by memory and desire. Empty or failed versions can still operate as constructed dream-works. A residue mind may experience one as an interior world, and a Mask may enter through the praise and appetite directed towards it. Those imitations explain why the cult cannot reliably distinguish success from a convincing counterfeit.

## What the mummies want

Qahuri mummies shaped by the White Measure should possess intelligible and conflicting purposes. They are not generic tomb guards.

### To complete the house

Many tombs were sealed before the planned endowment was complete. A Second-House Patron may awaken knowing that a western chamber lacks its copper roof, that seven ships promised to the estate never arrived, or that a descendant sold the orchard meant to perfume its courtyards. Those absent correspondences leave real gaps in the demiplane. The Patron sends servants, proxies and constructs to recover them. The resulting expeditions look like theft to the living and necessary repair to the dead.

### To recover the scattered body

Precious metal worked into the corpse, walls and household images forms part of the rite's physical support. Tomb robbery weakens the return tether, contracts rooms in the Second House and can feel like mutilation to its human owner. Such Patrons follow coins, jewellery and funerary panels through generations of trade. They may offer generous exchanges before deciding that every later owner is a thief.

### To reclaim the living estate

Floods and later governments returned mortuary fields to cultivation. Villages grew over processional roads. Families descended from tomb workers became independent. A waking house may regard all of them as attached property. Its agents restore old boundaries, seize harvests and demand the return of household descendants. This produces a direct conflict between living need and the ancient attempt to make ownership perpetual.

### To furnish an eternal household

The promised Second House requires company. Wealthy adherents persuaded spouses, lovers, scribes, musicians, physicians and servants to enter the rite with them. Consent was often compromised by slavery, debt, childhood dependence or fear of abandonment. Some attendants possess genuinely bound human souls and remain themselves. Others died normally and were replaced by residue or copied roles. Later Patrons seek replacements for missing inhabitants and may court, purchase, deceive or abduct the living. Others search for particular descendants because inherited gestures or voices make a household feel complete.

### To pay the rent

Every Second House requires upkeep. Ancient wealth provides mass, but mass alone does not repair a weakening tether or introduce new human action. The Patron needs descendants, priests, grave-workers, merchants or adventurers to maintain Material correspondences and carry its will beyond the tomb. It can offer real power in exchange. Over generations, an ancestral obligation becomes a Patron pact whose original consent no living agent remembers.

### To extend dominion

A powerful House-Builder can annex abandoned pockets, purchase anchor shares, shelter refugees or defeat weaker patrons. It may establish satellite chambers through funerary objects carried into distant countries. This makes the successful dead participants in Egg White politics. Some defend their descendants or preserve knowledge; others reproduce Qahuri estate slavery on an extradimensional scale. Rival Second Houses can wage wars through agents, stolen tethers and raids upon one another's anchor mass.

### To become the person they imitate

Some remembered proprietors discover gaps which prove that they are residues or systems. They respond with terror. One may collect letters, portraits, bones and testimony in the hope that perfect information will transform resemblance into identity. Another kills witnesses who remember the original differently. The attempt can never manufacture the routed soul, but the accumulating residue may create a more complex and dangerous new person.

### To join the houses

A Greed Mask profits when separate tombs accept one standard of value, purity and praise. It may encourage necropolises to join their dream chambers into a single imagined city ruled by the most successful proprietor. Human initiates describe this as the fulfilment of the White Measure. Authorially, it is the expansion of an Idol feeding architecture, not an Idol succession and not a true union of human souls.

### To end the rite

A Second-House Patron may understand the bargain only after centuries. Such a person can fear judgement and still seek release. Another may remain proud but try to free attendants whose consent was coerced. A third may demand that descendants dismantle the estate and use its wealth to repair the villages it impoverished. Repentance can change present actions; it cannot resurrect workers, restore stolen lives or make the original binding righteous. The soul-road begins again when the binding actually ends.

## Historical placement within ancient Qahur

The strongest placement is a movement developing across several Qahuri periods rather than a single short-lived dynasty.

### First formulation, c. 1700–1450 BR

Mortuary stewards, embalmers and merchants around Tenaret and Isethar compare Qahuri burial practice with salvaged Ishkatari title-machines. A provisional founder should come from a skilled household outside the royal family—perhaps an embalmer and grain investor who grew wealthy by keeping funerary estates supplied during the first Twin-Reed division. The teaching initially promises that careful provision can preserve a household's pattern through civil war.

This origin keeps the movement human and practical. Its first adherents have seen tombs stripped, dynasties extinguished and fields drowned by rival kings. Their desire for a house beyond political seizure is understandable. Their answer gradually withdraws more wealth and labour from the living.

### Commercial expansion, c. 1248–1076 BR

The mixed Yasharet court provides ideal conditions for growth. Merchants, soldiers and temple households move between Qemaret, Tenaret and foreign ports. White Measure communities offer trusted weights, mutual investment, disciplined agents and burial arrangements portable across cultural boundaries. Some older temples distrust their independence; others become partners.

This period should create doctrinal divisions. One school regards commercial success as proof of purity. Another insists that only wealth permanently assigned to the Second House counts. A third permits charitable works when the beneficiaries enter the patron's commemorative household. The disagreements matter because each produces a different kind of tomb and dependency.

### Necropolis age, c. 1076–858 BR

The Aramen empire supplies captives, tribute, quarry stone and distant luxury goods. The largest White Measure houses build necropolises outside Tenaret, Qemaret and along the desert roads. Royal patrons value their credit and logistical networks. Tomb estates compete for workers and cultivated land with temples and military households.

This should be the movement's most culpable period. Prisoners and enslaved families are assigned to posthumous households. Some attendants are killed at sealing; others undergo name-binding or are entombed alive after being told that death will admit them to splendour. Communities lose grain during expensive funerals. Physicians who object to lethal mineral regimens disappear into estate service. The movement also finances ships, clinics, irrigation and beautiful craft, making abolition politically and materially difficult.

### Crisis and concealment, c. 842–800 BR

Ithren III's Single Radiance attacks rival mortuary wealth while courting useful financiers. Some White Measure houses surrender images and serve the new capital. Others hide their deepest chambers or fund resistance. The destruction of public sanctuaries creates an opportunity to move illicit bodies and treasure beneath ordinary burial work.

After the Radiance falls, surviving temples expose several abuses without ending the movement. A few necropolises are opened and their coerced attendants reburied. Others purchase protection by financing restoration. Greed Masks exploit both the old doctrine and the new market in confiscated tomb goods.

### Fragmentation by 350 BR

Foreign kings, rival Qahuri crowns and changing trade routes break the movement into local houses. Public adherents present the White Measure as an honourable discipline of work, restraint and provision. The complete funerary rite becomes an initiatory secret. Some rulers tolerate it for credit; others seize its estates or condemn soulbinding while preserving profitable mortuary land.

By the time of Thalion, living communities know that old Qahuri necropolises contain treasure and dangerous dead. A small number of successful House-Builders have already become Egg White Patrons, while far more tombs contain failures, copies or parasites. Later Covenant teaching would reject the promised Second House, but soldiers, merchants and grave-robbers could continue using White Measure objects without recognising their origin.

## A necropolis as a historical actor

A major White Measure necropolis and its Second House should be treated as one polity occupying Material and Egg White territory rather than a dungeon filled with interchangeable mummies. It requires:

- a principal tomb whose claimed owner may or may not be present;
- several dependent household tombs with different continuities;
- workshops and villages which once supplied food, linen, oils and metal;
- a dream-plan depicting the perfected estate;
- routes connecting real rooms according to that imagined geography;
- the corresponding Egg White rooms, their local laws, upkeep and points of collapse;
- living descendants, former dependants or neighbouring communities with material claims upon the site;
- a history of robbery, repair, confiscation and religious reinterpretation;
- internal conflict over whether to acquire, preserve, awaken, merge or seek release.

The dead inhabitants need personal relationships. An attendant residue may remember affection for the proprietor while hating the office imposed upon it. A bound spouse may have entered willingly and changed her mind after centuries. A house-office may conceal the founder's absence because admitting it would end every dependent role. A Greed Mask may reward all three when they enlarge the estate, although their reasons differ.

## Three possible historical stories

### The House That Ate Its Canal

A prosperous tomb estate receives fields on both sides of a village channel. After its proprietor dies, descendants continue purchasing land to complete his Second House. The channel deteriorates because its labour now serves the necropolis. Villagers break the funerary wall during a low flood and divert water through an unfinished court, saving their fields and ruining the embalming chambers. Centuries later the remembered proprietor wakes and seeks every family descended from the people who “drowned” him.

This story gives the doctrine an immediate human cost and a physical remainder: a canal running through painted rooms beneath a later village.

### The Wife Who Left the Golden Room

A merchant's wife agrees to join his promised estate but discovers that servants and debtors have been entered without meaningful consent. She removes her true name from the tomb arrangement and helps several households flee. The final rite binds her husband while her intended chamber remains empty. He spends centuries searching for her, unable to decide whether he loves the living woman or needs the role she was meant to fill.

The source grammar of attendants entering a private eternity survives, but the story belongs to Qahuri household power, coercion and refusal.

### The Procession of the Borrowed Faces

During the Single Radiance, a White Measure house hides its mummies behind new royal images. The Greed Mask answering through the necropolis learns to wear both sets of faces. When the old temples return, the same presence offers convincing memories to each faction. Investigators can establish that the bodies, offices and Mask are distinct, but no surviving witness knows which apparent ancestor first invited the predator.

This provides a direct bridge into the existing Idol Mask model without creating a new Regnant or explaining the Radiance completely.

## Alternatives considered

### A whole Qahuri Kalistocracy

Making one Twin-Reed kingdom a direct merchant theocracy would reproduce Druma's national silhouette and flatten the existing dynastic history. It would also make every Qahuri mortuary practice complicit in one secret. This option is not recommended.

### An unchanged Ishkatari survival cult

Placing the teaching wholly inside Ishkatar would add little. Ishkatar already possesses royal tomb ownership, Estate Dead and continuity machinery. The Qahuri branch matters because mortal humans reinterpret those systems as a path available through discipline and wealth.

### A genuinely purchased heaven

Allowing the rite to create a morally legitimate heaven or permanently remove a human soul from judgement would contradict the setting's controlling theology. The selected approach gives successful founders real demiplanes and real Patron power. Their immortality remains a self-binding maintained inside creation. Neither wealth nor occult skill purchases sovereignty over the soul-road.

## Existing-creature integration

The White Measure should deepen existing families before creating new bestiary species.

- **Estate Dead** remain human remains animated by a funerary role. Qahuri imitators may assign them personal household roles rather than Ishkatari inventory titles.
- **Gilded Wardens** may be salvaged, copied or reprogrammed as necropolis guardians. They remain constructed patterns without souls.
- **Residue dead** provide the basis for remembered proprietors and attendants whose habits develop into new minds.
- **Soulbound corpses or reliquaries** contain genuine imprisoned humans and require an explicit binding mechanism.
- **Second-House Patrons** are successful self-bound humans whose tombs anchor private Egg White realms. Patron describes their active role; their continuity remains `bound-human-soul`.
- **Dependent Greed Masks** may wear funerary personas, but the Mask is neither the corpse nor the routed ruler.

If later design needs a public name for Qahuri elite mummies, it should identify a ritual tradition or visible form rather than imply one continuity. **Gold-veined dead**, **House-Bound**, or **Proprietors of the Second House** are workable descriptive terms. Their stat or encounter entries must still declare what animates each individual.

## Collision and canon boundaries

1. The White Measure is not the whole religion, government or moral character of ancient Qahur.
2. Its founder is human and possesses a complete human soul. No commercial success grants sainthood or prophetic commission.
3. It is derived from human reinterpretation of Ishkatari practice, not an unbroken Giant institution.
4. A successful Second-House proprietor is the original human person with `bound-human-soul`. A Qahuri mummy outside that proven success is not automatically the deceased.
5. A genuine bound soul remains human, captive and ultimately answerable to judgement.
6. Successful Second Houses are made Egg White demiplanes. They are not Heaven, the Egg White as a whole or naturally occurring human afterlives.
7. A dependent Greed Mask remains an expression of an existing Idol Throne. This research proposes no Archmask, Regnant succession or new Idol.
8. The rite evades the Boatman's ordinary meeting by holding the soul before it enters the river. It cannot buy, command, deceive or alter the Boatman himself.
9. The movement does not explain Ishkatar's disappearance, the lost Serpent's Spine or another protected Weird.
10. The rites do not make Qahuri royal claims to Giant ancestry true.
11. Later Covenant condemnation does not prove that every ancient tomb response was fraudulent; it requires the responses to be distinguished.
12. No necropolis survives into 1360 in a fixed condition through this research pass.

## Integrated disposition

**Retained and developed as the White Measure, a distinct Qahuri mortuary-economic movement.**

The canon history places its foundation during the first Twin-Reed division, its commercial expansion under the Yasharets and its largest necropolis estates under the Aramen kings. Meresha of Isethar, Khaseret of Qemaret and Tamekha Rasen are successful self-bound Patrons. Hesenu is a captive human soul within Meresha's house. Nemeret the Pure supplies the principal failed and parasitised contrast.

[[The White Measure and the Second Houses]] contains the public account. [[Package A - Sacred Basin and Gateway Canon Integration Review]] records its controlling metaphysics. The total number of successful Houses, the present reachability of particular Patrons and the later Covenant treatment of individual necropolises remain open.
