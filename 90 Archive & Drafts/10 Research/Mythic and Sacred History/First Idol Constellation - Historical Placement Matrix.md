## Purpose and Status

This pass gives four reviewed nurseries from [[Idol Heartlands, Masks, and Hunger Wars Review]] provisional historical soil. It selects one episode in each chronological block while leaving six reviewed Idol leads unplaced.

[[Greater-Power Garden Balance and Trace Promotion Review]] later promotes only **The Victory Recut Four Times** from ID-P01 and **The Grave Prepared for No Relic** from ID-P03. ID-P02R and ID-P04R remain withheld. The light traces do not promote any complete history, Archmask, Mask identity, dynasty, cult, relic, route or succession claim.

The four are not stages of one Great Game, appearances of one Archmask, evidence of a seven-Throne council or a hidden cause of human history. Each has different people, institutions, Masks, evidence and consequences. Recurring Hunger grammar explains resemblance without establishing shared identity.

The controlling rule remains:

> Culture supplies an Idol's local face and route. Human beings feed it when they knowingly make a betrayed good serve predation and build that choice into something others must repeat.

No episode establishes true Regnant succession. Every apparent “new god,” “changed god” or “victorious incarnation” remains Mask turnover, Archmask politics, cult biography or propaganda.

# II. Four Historical Placements

## ID-P01 — The War of Stolen Victories

### Historical placement and ordinary aperture

- **Source nursery:** ID-R01.
- **Era:** late Chains, provisionally c. 2385–2335 BR, before the Great Unbinding's closing crisis.
- **Region:** two neighboring secondary river crowns in the provisional Reed Crowns of Sarrakai, inside the Ishkatari imperial horizon. Neither is Ishkatar's final capital or universal dynasty.
- **Population:** Dynastic Giants, giant-blooded human clients with complete souls, enslaved canal workers, levy soldiers, tenant farmers, boat crews, captive households and burial fraternities.
- **Ordinary aperture:** irrigation wardens and grain-boat crews begin the war by contesting a real diverted channel. They do not know their local rulers will turn the dispute into a dynastic extermination claim.
- **Scale:** one river war and its forty- to fifty-year social aftermath, not the fall of Ishkatar or a cause of the Great Unbinding.

### Received sources and transformation

- **Received sources:** Warhammer Great Game grammar; Greek division of honours and spoils after victory; Giant-age Wisdom 13–15 cult machinery already established in the programme.
- **Borrowed element:** rival powers taking different fruits of one victory; monuments, captives, property and betrayal memory becoming separate prizes.
- **Retained hard edge:** massacre after surrender, execution, enslavement, family separation, confiscation, destroyed villages, corpse denial and renewed revenge killing.
- **Changed axes:** a river-and-irrigation war replaces a cosmic battlefield; separate human and Giant institutions carry every transfer; four dependent Masks act in sequence rather than four gods holding council; archaeology and household custom replace mythic omniscience; the outcome helps weaken two minor crowns but does not explain an age.

### Actors and continuity

| Actor | Classification | Function and limit |
|---|---|---|
| Wrath, Pride, Greed and Envy | four distinct guilty `yolk-sovereignty` Thrones | receive separate routes; none enters Material reality or commands the other three |
| the River Reckoner | dependent Wrath Mask anchored in casualty lists and surrender exclusions | expands the enemy class; not an Archmask or war god |
| the Tall Crown in the Reeds | dependent Pride Mask anchored in triumph, portrait and ruler-acclaim | erases allies from victory; not the ruler's soul |
| the Keeper of the Drowned Deeds | dependent Greed Mask anchored in prisoner sale and canal title | converts captured people and land into recoverable assets |
| the Unavenged Sister | dependent Envy Mask anchored in the excluded ally's grievance cult | teaches dispossessed survivors that another subject population stole their rightful victory |
| Dynastic Giants | `giant-continuity` | command armies and choose crimes; their blood and office do not make them Idols |
| human clients, soldiers, captives and civilians | complete `human-soul` | remain separately culpable, coerced or victimized according to their choices |
| monuments, deeds, lists and bodies | objects, records and mortal remains | carry evidence and anchors; contain no divided person |

The four local handles are provisional descriptions, not creator-facing names or proof of personal continuity across later cultures.

### Feeding architectures and visible mechanism

1. **Wrath:** commanders declare the entire losing crown, including surrendered boat crews and farming households, answerable for the original canal deaths. The casualty register becomes a Red March route. Weapons heat when anyone from the expanded target class approaches.
2. **Pride:** the victor's dynasty recuts the levee monuments so one Giant king appears to have won alone. Collaborating human boat clans and a defecting Giant cousin are removed. Processional streets physically narrow for people bearing erased household marks.
3. **Greed:** officials classify prisoners, irrigation shares, graves and village labor as transferable spoil. Sale tablets and canal deeds open a Hoarded-Vault overlay in the impound yards. Freed captives remain legally attached to the land unless the underlying title is repaired.
4. **Envy:** excluded allies and displaced descendants are taught that the surviving tenant population possesses the prosperity, children and honor that should have been theirs. The resulting cult does not invent the dispossession; it redirects a true wrong toward replacement and renewed killing.

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** one crown diverts water during scarcity and several workers die resisting the seizure. → **Decision:** rival rulers make the local crime a hereditary war and refuse bounded settlement. → **Mechanism:** Wrath expands the target class; Pride monopolizes the victory; Greed turns prisoners and water rights into property; Envy converts excluded allies' real grievance into hatred of later tenants. → **Effect:** the military war ends while four separate institutions keep transferring its victims and meanings. → **Cost:** surrendered people are murdered, families are sold apart, villages are depopulated, graves are denied, canals fail and retaliatory raids continue for generations. → **Consequence:** both crowns weaken, refugee and burial networks spread beyond their borders, and later chronicles compress four predatory afterlives into one “war of the river gods.”

### Evidence and 1360 interpretations

Independent carriers include:

1. **Landscape:** a diverted palaeochannel, burned field layers and mass graves on both banks establish the material war.
2. **Architecture:** levee reliefs preserve four recutting phases; the oldest shows several allied contingents later removed.
3. **Objects:** prisoner sale-tags fit attachment holes beneath later “free tenant” canal titles.
4. **Custom:** descendant boat households omit one triumphal verse but read the names of the surrendered dead before irrigation work.
5. **Documents:** hostile deed traditions agree on the original water diversion while assigning incompatible hereditary guilt.
6. **Bounded test:** restoring one erased household mark to the processional route widens only the relevant Pride overlay; it does not affect the Greed impound route or Wrath target list.

In 1360, Ishkatari antiquarians may treat the episode as proof of a lawful dynastic settlement; Giant-cult polemic may call it a war among literal gods; river-descended communities may preserve only the sale and burial memory. Multiple evidence classes can establish the war and four successive captures without identifying every Mask or victim.

### Known, open and bounded

- **Authorially fixed for the nursery:** a real water crime began the conflict; rulers expanded it; four separate Hunger architectures captured its aftermath; massacre, slavery, dispossession and feud occurred.
- **Open:** crowns, dynasties, precise river, full casualty scale, local Mask names, duration, surviving communities and present route activity.
- **Identity boundary:** not the entire [[Great-Power Mythic Ecology and Planting Matrix#GP-I-02 — The Sevenfold Feast War|Sevenfold Feast War]], the origin of the Red Tally or Under-Ledger, Ishkatar's fall, Avarix's immediate family history or a true Idol succession.
- **Placement disposition:** `retain`. The later balance review promotes only **The Victory Recut Four Times** as a light trace; the complete placement remains locked.

## ID-P02R — The Champion of Four Doors

### Historical placement and ordinary aperture

- **Source nursery:** ID-R04R.
- **Era:** late City-States, provisionally c. 1010–940 BR, before the Age of Stars synthesis.
- **Region:** one fortified inner-gulf city controlling a grain quay, hill gate, debt court and victory road. It is later absorbed into Atherian civic history but does not found Caleran or the empire.
- **Population:** free citizens, resident foreigners, enslaved dock laborers, hill farmers, mercenaries, debtor households, captive rivals and temple dependants.
- **Ordinary aperture:** gate wardens and ration clerks face a real siege after the ruling league's failed war. A hidden court faction offers an old Envy Archmask as the one defender capable of opening every necessary route.
- **Scale:** one city and its dependent fields across one siege, counter-campaign and collapse; not a signature company or pan-Atherian divine war.

### Received source and transformation

- **Received source:** multi-patron champion grammar from Warhammer, sharpened by the World Egg Throne–Mask model.
- **Borrowed element:** one powerful intermediary accepts incompatible gifts from rival predatory powers, wins through them and becomes trapped by their contradictory prices.
- **Retained hard edge:** sacrifice, enslavement, mutilation, betrayal, execution of surrendered enemies, abandonment of dependants and violent collapse when obligations cannot be reconciled.
- **Changed axes:** the champion is one nonhuman Archmask rather than an elevated mortal; four city institutions replace divine gifts carried in one body; the cult league knowingly accepts the terms; disabling one route removes one power; no species or sovereignty merger occurs.

### Actors and continuity

| Actor | Classification | Function and limit |
|---|---|---|
| the Four-Door Claimant | autonomous Envy-rooted `idol-pattern` Archmask | one continuous person using four routes; not a human, Regnant or composite Idol |
| Envy | native Throne | supplies the Claimant's durable comparison court and its desire to occupy another city's victory |
| Wrath | foreign Throne | leases a surrender-excluding battle route through a dependent Mask and gate rite |
| Greed | foreign Throne | leases claim over prisoners, debtors and captured grain through the debt court |
| Pride | foreign Throne | lends public acclaim and sole authorship through victory-road procession |
| cult league and magistrates | humans with complete `human-soul` | knowingly accept, administer and benefit from the terms |
| coerced households, slaves and captives | humans with complete `human-soul` | their forced acts are boundary maintenance, not worship or consent |
| four doors, offices and tools | objects and institutional anchors | each carries one route and can fail separately |

The Claimant's Envy continuity predates the siege. The three foreign grants are leases and projected grafts, not new constituent persons inside it. No Regnant offers a detachable piece of itself.

### Four doors and exact prices

| Door | Gift | Price and victim class | Observable operation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hill gate / Wrath** | routed movement toward the besieging force and unnatural endurance during sorties | surrender never ends enemy status; healers, relatives and prisoners enter the target class | gate opens toward a named enemy camp regardless of distance while the execution list remains active |
| **debt door / Greed** | emergency grain and access to confiscated stores | debtors, resident foreigners and captured households become inheritable civic assets | keys work only for the registered claimant; releasing a debtor without repairing title closes the store again |
| **victory door / Pride** | command coherence and public recognition of one defender | every rival commander, allied contribution and civilian rescue must be erased | the processional road widens for the Claimant's image and narrows around suppressed names |
| **black-pool door / Envy** | the ability to imitate an enemy advantage or stolen civic role | the original holder must be diminished, expelled or killed so the copy can count as rightful | the copied capability fails when the erased original is truthfully restored |

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** a city loses an aggressive war and faces siege, grain shortage and internal revolt. → **Decision:** magistrates enthrone an existing Envy Archmask as emergency protector and accept three foreign Hunger leases. → **Mechanism:** four institutions reproduce four different chosen distortions and open four separable routes. → **Effect:** the Claimant breaks the siege, takes rival forts and becomes the sole public defender. → **Cost:** prisoners and healers are executed, debtor families are enslaved, allied commanders vanish from the record and copied civic techniques require the expulsion or murder of their original holders. → **Consequence:** fulfilling one door's price breaks another's supply; the cult league turns on itself, the city loses fields and population, and later Atherian accounts remember either a four-faced god or four unrelated tyrants.

### Evidence and 1360 interpretations

Independent carriers include:

1. **Architecture:** four gate systems use unrelated construction phases but converge on one later court chamber.
2. **Bodies:** execution pits contain surrender tokens and healer tools; debtor family burials disappear from the same decades.
3. **Documents:** ration tablets, erased officer rolls and property transfers each preserve one price without naming the other three.
4. **Art:** the Claimant has one consistent under-form beneath four changing civic faces.
5. **Custom:** later route hearings keep an empty chair for the “unrestored original,” though the office no longer remembers why.
6. **Bounded test:** repairing one debt title opens only the Greed store route; it grants no battle endurance, acclaim or copied skill.

Later Atherian philosophers may classify the four faces as aspects of one civic guardian. Church writers may call them four demons. Idol cults may claim the Claimant almost became a Regnant. The evidence supports one Archmask and four routes but not accession, survival or the full identity of its original comparison victim.

### Known, open and bounded

- **Authorially fixed for the nursery:** the claimant was one Envy Archmask; three foreign leases remained separate; human magistrates knowingly accepted the prices; the victory caused irreversible human harm and political collapse.
- **Open:** city, enemies, original Envy victim, claimant name, exact gifts, cult league, defeat, surviving anchor and present activity.
- **Identity boundary:** not the [[Great-Power Mythic Ecology and Planting Matrix#GP-II-03 — The Crown-Market of Shadows|Crown-Market]], [[The Shoreless King]], Seven Who Took the Wrong Road, an Atherian founder god, True Dragon, Sinner, Giant, human apotheosis or Regnant claimant.
- **Placement disposition:** `revise`. Fix Envy as the native continuity, make the three foreign powers leased routes and prevent the city from founding later Atheria. Garden promotion remains locked.

## ID-P03 — The Campaign of the Borrowed Martyr

### Historical placement and ordinary aperture

- **Source nursery:** ID-R06R.
- **Era:** one execution during the late Underground Centuries, provisionally c. 272–286 AR; competing cult campaigns after Legalization, c. 298–338 AR.
- **Region:** one western Atherian quarry-town and a road sanctuary several days away. Neither is Caleran, Velos, Qiryath or a Companion's known burial place.
- **Population:** prisoners, quarry families, guards, provincial Christians, old-cult households, road keepers, clergy, pilgrims, officials and the executed person's surviving kin.
- **Ordinary aperture:** a prison laundry and burial rota allows one otherwise obscure prisoner or worker to carry food, messages and the names of dead detainees between quarry cells and families.
- **Scale:** one executed person, two local cult systems and the affected families; no new apostle, universal Saint or explanation of Legalization.

### Received sources and transformation

- **Received sources:** Orthodox martyr reception, with distinct historical, received-Life, relic, icon and folklore layers; TES mantle and cultural-biography grammar.
- **Borrowed element:** a bodily witness whose death becomes the object of competing Lives, relic claims and supernatural counterfeits.
- **Retained hard edge:** imprisonment, torture, informing, an ambiguous recantation, execution, denial and theft of the corpse, coercion of relatives, grave desecration and retaliatory killing.
- **Changed axes:** the executed person is deliberately minor and provincial; two different Hungers counterfeit different virtues; surviving family testimony resists both cults; a tested local mechanism can expose each Mask without settling sainthood; no received Orthodox saint is renamed.

### Actors and continuity

| Actor | Classification | Function and limit |
|---|---|---|
| the executed person | one complete judged `human-soul` | historical person; neither Mask contains, inherits or divides this soul |
| body and remains | mortal remains | can be stolen, divided, misidentified or used as an anchor; never equal the departed soul |
| the Crowned Witness | dependent Pride Mask | makes suffering prove the quarry-town hierarchy's unique sanctity and right to command |
| the Weeping Double | dependent Envy Mask | gives the road sanctuary the martyr and miracles it believes the town stole |
| Lives, icons and relic claims | texts, images, objects and possible remains | contain evidence and may anchor counterfeits; no class authenticates itself |
| surviving kin, clergy, officials and pilgrims | humans with complete `human-soul` | hold different knowledge, choices, guilt and vulnerability |
| any genuine Saintly response | one redeemed human soul under a later bounded commission, if separately established | remains open; no institution can summon or compel it |

### Historical mechanism

The person is arrested after a prison informer identifies the burial-and-message route. Torture produces a written denial or ambiguous formula which later copyists disagree about. The person subsequently refuses a second public demand and is executed. Officials deny the corpse to the family. Guards sell or transfer a body, while another burial fraternity claims to have recovered it first.

After Legalization, quarry-town clergy build a triumphant Life around unbroken confession and public authority. Pride's dependent Mask attaches to processions, an elevated icon and official recognition. A road community excluded from the civic cult preserves the torture and family grief but gradually replaces the person's actual relationships with its own deprivation story. Envy's dependent Mask attaches to a weeping image, comparison pilgrimage and claims that the town stole the “real” martyr.

Both Masks perform effects. Neither effect proves the executed person's judgement, the body's location or the truth of the full Life.

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** an ordinary prison worker or detainee helps feed prisoners and return names and bodies to families. → **Decision:** officials torture and execute the person; later clerics and road custodians suppress inconvenient testimony to monopolize the death. → **Mechanism:** Pride anchors in official acclaim and a perfected Life, while Envy anchors in comparison, exclusion and a rival image. → **Effect:** two dependent Masks answer different petitions, punish doubt and mobilize rival pilgrims. → **Cost:** the person is tortured and killed, the body is stolen, relatives are threatened, a recanter is murdered, graves are opened and clashes kill pilgrims and custodians. → **Consequence:** the two communities preserve enough hostile evidence to expose both counterfeits while remaining unable to settle final judgement or relic ownership.

### Evidence and bounded tests

Independent carriers include:

1. **Bodies and burial:** a family plot contains a prepared but unused grave; a quarry pit contains the murdered recanter; claimed relics do not match every documented injury.
2. **Documents:** hostile Lives share the same prison rota, torture date and kin names beneath incompatible confession endings.
3. **Architecture:** the town icon platform and road sanctuary use different anchor geometries and were built after the execution.
4. **Family testimony:** a private phrase and burial custom survive outside both cult hierarchies.
5. **Economic record:** guard payments and pilgrim tolls show who profited from corpse transfer and rivalry.
6. **Bounded tests:** the Crowned Witness ceases responding when the official acclaim and precedence claim are removed; the Weeping Double ceases when comparison with the town is removed. Neither test summons the human person.

In 1360, one church may treat the figure as a local confessor, another as a doubtful martyr and relic courts as unresolved. Weeping Courts or Diadem investigators may recognize feeding architecture without knowing the human truth. Establishing two counterfeits still does not establish that no genuine Saintly intercession ever occurred.

### Known, open and bounded

- **Authorially fixed for the nursery:** one human was tortured and executed; the body was denied and contested; Pride and Envy later maintained separate dependent Masks; human authorities and cult operators committed later violence.
- **Open:** name, sex, exact charge, truth and wording of recantation, final judgement, body location, surviving kin, genuine miracles, institutions and present cult remnants.
- **Identity boundary:** not [[Borrowed Saints]] as a single recurring person, Bruckmoor, GS-01's Red Passage, UH-06's enemy gate, the Forty Flames, a lost Companion, the whole Siege of the Martyrs' Names or proof for either church.
- **Placement disposition:** `retain`. The earth-first history, separate cult mechanisms and saintly commission lock are sufficient. The later balance review promotes only **The Grave Prepared for No Relic** as a light trace; the complete placement remains locked.

## ID-P04R — The Archmask That Ate Its Biography

### Historical placement and ordinary aperture

- **Source nursery:** ID-R10R.
- **Era:** Two Crowns and post-Split consolidation, provisionally c. 1068–1102 AR.
- **Region:** one inland successor court along the Virelian–Vesari road belt, with one fortified city and its directly administered rural wards. It is not Strazgorod and does not explain the Great Split.
- **Population:** mixed Atherian provincial descendants, Folk and Vesari households, clergy of rival obediences, soldiers, road settlers, archivists, portrait workers, grave custodians and families tied to the former civic cult.
- **Ordinary aperture:** painters, masons and women preserving maternal kin names discover that each official restoration requires the removal of an older face, family and public duty from the city's protector image.
- **Scale:** one city and its dependent wards across one ruler's consolidation and purge; not a civilization-wide *damnatio memoriae* campaign.

### Received sources and transformation

- **Received sources:** TES mantle-control and identity-accretion grammar; Roman and later *damnatio memoriae* as destruction of public biography; World Egg Pride and Envy ecology.
- **Borrowed element:** a supernatural ruler changes the conditions of its identity by destroying memories, images, offices and witnesses that a rival can use as anchors.
- **Retained hard edge:** execution of priests, archivists and witnesses; killing of children or dependants selected as lineage evidence; grave destruction; forced renaming; deportation; burned settlements and retaliatory murder.
- **Changed axes:** an autonomous Pride Archmask replaces the human or divine ruler; one Envy dependent Mask uses the older biography; human government executes the purge for its own political reasons; physical infrastructure preserves the truth; the purge narrows rather than strengthens the Archmask's capacities.

### Actors and continuity

| Actor | Classification | Function and limit |
|---|---|---|
| the Unpictured Protector | autonomous Pride-rooted `idol-pattern` Archmask | one local court ruler; not Pride's Regnant, a Saint, emperor or human soul |
| the Elder Face | dependent Envy Mask grown around the Protector's discarded civic biography and displaced families' comparison | rival anchor and counterfeit continuity; not the Protector's former soul or a former Regnant |
| Pride and Envy | separate guilty Thrones | supply distinct Hungers and routes without direct bodily entry |
| successor prince and officials | humans with complete `human-soul` | order and execute the purge for political unity, land and office |
| old-cult families and later victims | humans with complete `human-soul` | preserve memories without becoming the Masks they describe |
| portraits, graves, offices, roads and forbidden names | anchors, objects and social carriers | affect manifestation but contain no human person |

The Protector acquired autonomous continuity before this period through still-open local anchors; that earlier history remains deliberately unassigned. Historical placement does not establish how it became an Archmask or whether it survives.

### Historical mechanism

The Protector's older biography binds it to several civic roles: defense of one gate, recognition of certain maternal houses, opening of a famine store and appearance in a victory hall. Displaced families use those duties to demand restitution after a succession war. An Envy Mask grows around their increasingly perfected “Elder Face,” promising that restoring the old protector will give them every good the current court possesses.

The autonomous Protector refuses both correction and replacement. It persuades a human prince that unity requires one uncontested civic patron and one genealogy. Officials destroy portraits, grave markers, household registers and village shrines; execute custodians and witnesses; remove children from named houses; rename wards; and burn settlements that conceal the old images. The Archmask's Shadow court loses the disputed old routes and adopts a harsher new face anchored only in palace acclaim and military protection.

The purge works locally. It does not change the past, the Pride Throne or Heaven's knowledge. It also deprives the Protector of the famine-store and maternal-recognition functions tied to the destroyed biography.

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** displaced houses use an Archmask's older civic obligations to challenge a successor prince and the Protector's current court. → **Decision:** the Protector and prince choose historical annihilation rather than restitution or limited office. → **Mechanism:** human officials destroy the people, images, graves, offices and place-patterns that sustain the older biography; an Envy Mask fights through the same memories. → **Effect:** the Elder Face loses most routes, and the Protector manifests through a narrower military-acclaim identity. → **Cost:** witnesses, clergy, children and dependants are killed; families are deported; rural wards burn; graves and archives are destroyed; famine relief fails after the corresponding old office disappears. → **Consequence:** later histories mistake the record gap for an ancient mystery, while roads, paint, bodies and forbidden kin names preserve evidence of deliberate removal.

### Evidence and 1360 interpretations

Independent carriers include:

1. **Landscape and architecture:** roads, irrigation and tithe paths terminate at demolished ward foundations absent from official maps.
2. **Art:** protector portraits share a concealed older under-face and erased maternal emblems beneath later military paint.
3. **Bodies:** execution and deportation pits contain grave custodians, children wearing prohibited kin tokens and soldiers killed during reprisals.
4. **Custom:** several maternal lines use substitute kin names and refuse to paint faces above door lintels.
5. **Documents:** pigment purchases, troop rations and empty tax sequences establish coordinated destruction without preserving the banned biography.
6. **Bounded test:** speaking one forbidden kin-name before the surviving old gate triggers a narrow recognition response; it neither summons the Protector nor proves the Elder Face's identity.

In 1360, Virelian chroniclers may call the purge necessary resistance to pagan sedition; Vesari songs may identify every victim as an ancestral saint; rival churches may blame one another; Idol investigators may mistake the Elder Face for a defeated Regnant. The central purge and two distinct Mask architectures are recoverable, but the earlier Archmask history and final fate remain open.

### Known, open and bounded

- **Authorially fixed for the nursery:** one Pride Archmask and human prince knowingly purge its older biography; one Envy dependent Mask exploits the displaced memory; people are murdered, deported and dispossessed; the local court loses functions tied to destroyed anchors.
- **Open:** city, prince, Archmask name, earlier biography, affected houses, exact purge scope within the bounded court, Elder Face's later fate, present route and survival.
- **Identity boundary:** not ER-01's human institutional erasure, ER-02's supernatural name wound, LH-04's distant soul-binding purge, Caleran's archive crime, the Crown That Replaced Its Wearer, a former Regnant or the cause of Virelian–Vesari identity.
- **Placement disposition:** `revise`. Bound the purge to one city and dependent wards, fix the Elder Face as a dependent Envy Mask rather than former Regnant, and leave the Archmask's acquisition and survival open. Garden promotion remains locked.
