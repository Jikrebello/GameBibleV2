# Great-Power Mythic Ecology and Planting Matrix

## Purpose

The first historical garden chiefly asks what ordinary humans did while history became legend. This companion pass asks what the setting's created powers were doing beyond the human foreground: contending for routes, feeding systems, offices, anchors, witnesses, boundaries, and the interpretation of sacred memory.

These conflicts are not a second creation story or a secret master chronology. Most do not cause the human age beside which they are planted. They create distant thunder, strange alliances, inherited enemies, relic ecologies, impossible refugees, contradictory cult histories, and powers with old reasons to recognize—or hate—one another in 1360 AR.

All twelve beds are author-only, exploratory, and `nursery-unreviewed`. Their titles, dates, participants, outcomes, and cultural recensions remain provisional.

## Governing Theological Rule

There is no war among equal gods.

- The Lord is uncreated, sovereign, and never one combatant inside a cosmic balance.
- Loyal angels and manifested saints do not pursue private kingdoms, prestige, territory, worship, or vendetta. They act under bounded commission and withdraw when that commission ends.
- Idols are guilty `yolk-sovereignty` powers whose Hungers, Regnants, Masks, and Archmasks can scheme, feud, counterfeit, consume, betray, and temporarily ally.
- Menhir are continuous `yolk-sovereignty` persons expressing morally blind natural law. Their conflicts are collisions of jurisdiction, recurrence, and ecological pressure, not moral crusades.
- Patrons are anchor-owners of many continuities. They bargain, foreclose, ally, betray, and wage war according to individual agency and interests.
- Sinners are living humans aligned with refusal. Damned rulers remain judged `human-soul` persons; devil mantles remain `infrastructure-office`; Abyssal beings remain `abyssal-wound` continuities.
- A saint remains one redeemed `human-soul`; an angel remains a `created-spirit`; a Giant Continuant remains `giant-continuity`; a Mask remains `idol-pattern`; Astral persons remain `astral-pattern`. Alliance, defeat, residence, worship, or shared imagery changes none of these by itself.

## What Each Power Is Allowed to Do in Story

| Power | Characteristic action | What it cannot become |
|---|---|---|
| Idol Throne, Regnant, Mask, or Archmask | cultivate feeding architecture, counterfeit identity, capture an office, raid another Hunger's network, bargain through proxies, attempt accession | a morally neutral god, a redeemable sovereignty, or a human soul raised to divinity |
| Menhir or court-being | press natural jurisdiction, hunt a violation, answer pact, phase-lock with another law, abandon an ecologically useless alliance | a moral judge, church, ordinary political dynasty, or interchangeable Idol |
| Patron or Severed Exalt | own and lease anchors, redirect routes, impose local law, sell protection, seize collateral, shelter dependants, defect between markets | a species, proof of divinity, or rightful owner of a human soul |
| Living Sinner or damned ruler | recruit, imitate sacrament, command an office, construct dominion, manipulate grief and refusal | a second church equal to the Redeemer's body or an infernal species replacing human continuity |
| Devil mantle | enforce a stated mandate through a bearer, instrument, authority chain, and removal condition | a universal devil ancestry or self-authorizing moral person without an established bearer |
| Abyssal wound-being | express and propagate an active contradiction until the wound is closed | a morally innocent nation, stable empire, or ordinary monster population |
| Loyal angel or manifested saint | bear a warning, protect a threshold, expose a lie, command a commissioned host, preserve retreat, oppose a named supernatural work | an autonomous celestial faction, summonable army, relic commodity, dynastic patron, or partisan proof on demand |

## Source Grammars

### Eastern Orthodox Scripture and received tradition

- **Job 1–2:** accusation and trial occur under the Lord's sovereignty; the accuser is neither an equal god nor an independent author of reality.
- **Tobit 3–12:** an angelic mission, a hostile spirit, a household journey, healing, burial, and hidden identity intersect without making every illness demonic.
- **4 Kingdoms/2 Kings 6:** an unseen host may surround an ordinary military crisis; revelation of that host does not make human war automatically holy.
- **Daniel 10:** a messenger may be delayed by conflict among created powers expressed through imperial and territorial imagery.
- **2 Maccabees 3:** terrifying heavenly intervention protects one bounded sacred charge rather than validating every custodian or later political claim.
- **Wisdom 13–15:** images, grief, rulers, craft, institutions, and habit create feeding architecture into which existing Idol power can extend.
- **Revelation 12 and 19–20:** heavenly war, dragon imagery, imperial violence, martyr witness, and final divine victory supply grammar without becoming a code for a current Regnant or timetable.
- **Acts 8, 13, 16, and 19:** rival wonder, possession, commercial cult interest, apostolic correction, and civic violence must be diagnosed rather than collapsed into one demon species.
- **Received Orthodox Lives:** martyrs, dragon cycles, military apparitions, relic translations, frightening miracles, and saintly intervention retain documentary, Life, liturgical, iconographic, and folklore layers.

### Comparative myth and folklore

- **Hesiod's *Theogony* and the familiar Gigantomachy composite:** wars of divine generations, mutilation, binding, succession, and landscape-breaking combat.
- **The Poetic and Prose Eddas:** Aesir–Vanir exchange, hostage settlement, divine feuds, Loki's betrayals, and Ragnarök as the destruction of an order rather than a harmless pageant.
- **The *Mahabharata*, *Ramayana*, and specifically selected Purāṇic deva–asura cycles:** vast coalitions, boons with dangerous wording, ascetic power, kinship across battle lines, and victories that generate later obligations. No generic “Hindu war” is transferable.
- **The Irish *Cath Maige Tuired*:** successive supernatural peoples, maiming, contested kingship, mixed ancestry, craft, and a battle whose political aftermath matters.
- **Mesopotamian *Enūma Eliš*:** generational cosmic conflict and a defeated body used as political-cosmological order. Its theology and creation act are non-transferable.
- **European Wild Hunt families:** terrifying processions, seasonal pursuit, doomed riders, and the danger of being swept into a power's passage. Every developed recension still requires regional attribution.
- **Saint-and-dragon and saint-against-demon folklore:** a holy person's victory may be preserved as local landscape memory without proving the earthly biography, species, or universal method.

### Modern structural grammars

- **D&D's Blood War:** an apparently endless conflict between incompatible hostile orders can generate mercenaries, border economies, defections, propaganda, and mortal spillover without making either side good. World Egg use changes the combatants, metaphysics, purpose, and victory condition completely.
- **Warhammer's Great Game:** appetite powers can spend as much effort fighting one another as attacking the good, and temporary coalitions remain treacherous. World Egg use belongs to Idol Masks and feeding networks, not to seven coequal gods created by belief.
- **TES identity and mantle grammar:** wars can be fought over who may inhabit a role, which history becomes metaphysically active, and whether a defeated persona survives through office. Continuity is never inferred from performance alone.

## Five Recurring Conflict Ecologies

### 1. The Great Game of the Seven Hungers

Idol Masks raid, counterfeit, consume, or reclassify one another's feeding systems. Wrath may turn Envy's grievance into war; Pride may claim the victory; Greed may acquire the dead; Gluttony may consume the memorial; Lust may turn alliance into possession; Sloth may make every remedy impossible. No Hunger becomes another, and temporary advantage is not true Regnant succession.

### 2. Menhir Conjunctions and High Hunts

Menhir pressures collide wherever natural jurisdictions phase-lock badly: pursuit crossing migration, summer refusing to close, sea entering root, moon concealment opposing solar exposure. Mortals remember councils, marriages, wars, hunts, and divine children. Authorial truth remains a conflict among continuous natural sovereignties and their courts.

### 3. The War of the Last Map

Mapped-Hell dominions require stable boundaries, offices, prisons, armies, and routes. Abyssal wounds erase distinction, causality, and place. Damned rulers and devil-office systems therefore fight to keep Hell mappable while repeatedly exploiting Abyssal breaches as weapons against rivals. The war continually recreates what it claims to contain.

This is the nearest World Egg analogue to the Blood War, but its axes are different:

- it is tyrannical refusal defending usable dominion against anti-creation, not law versus chaos;
- Abyssal wounds are not a demon nation and possess no common government;
- the Adversary can exploit both without administratively commanding every participant;
- soul-road forces oppose unlawful interception, not on behalf of Hell's map-lords;
- closing the historical contradiction behind a wound matters more than killing its current body.

### 4. Anchor and Rent Wars

Patrons wage war through route denial, mass theft, proxy bodies, debt assumption, counterfeit arrival, demiplane foreclosure, and the capture of upkeep systems. A bloodless exchange at a market can kill a refuge years later when its anchor fails. These wars are political and ecological rather than automatically infernal.

### 5. Last-Door Battles

At mass death, damaged thresholds, and intercepted soul routes, several powers may converge: Undertow predators, Idol lures, damned manifestations, unlawful binders, Patrons seeking retained labor, threshold offices, angels, and manifested saints. These are episodic commissioned battles, not a permanent celestial frontier. A heavenly victory secures actual passage; it does not canonize the state, army, or church standing nearby.

# Chronological Planting Matrix

## Block I — Creation, First War, Giant Antiquity, and Chains

### GP-I-01 — The War of Broken Standards

- **Era and scale:** after the Great Rebellion and forced Yolk Exiles; recurrent fighting in unclaimed Shadow routes, later touching Giant-age ruins.
- **Principal actors:** Broken Standards, Commission Husks, Unhoused Witnesses, early Idol Masks, Menhir court detachments, and rare named fallen spirits only where separately established.
- **Source grammar:** Revelation 12; Hesiodic divine-generation war; *Cath Maige Tuired*; TES office and identity afterlife.
- **Authorial substrate:** abandoned allegiance-patterns continue sorting the battlefield after their armies have gone. Idol Masks hunt commission remnants that expose their Throne's betrayed good. Menhir courts recover pathways or natural functions once joined to their sovereignty. Unhoused Witnesses sell true fragments while ensuring that no claimant reconstructs the whole account.
- **Hard edge:** manifestations are torn apart, bound into obsolete commands, forced to attack former allies, or consumed as anchor material. These bodies are not surviving angels, but their suffering and agency must still be classified individually.
- **Human aperture:** later Giant courts mine a broken front for weapons and mistake Commission Husks for captive gods. Human slaves learn that lowering a Standard can end a compulsory battle which defeating its followers only restarts.
- **1360 AR trace:** banners that divide any expedition into hostile camps; a Husk that recognizes an unnamed saint as the bearer of a missing office; witness tokens recording the First War from no human viewpoint.
- **Open facts:** battlefield, participants, which commission is exposed, and whether any named fallen angel was present.
- **Protected boundaries:** do not provide a complete Great Rebellion chronology, identify every Menhir or Idol origin, or turn remnants into soul fragments.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

### GP-I-02 — The Sevenfold Feast War

- **Era and scale:** deep Giant imperial history through the Closing Courts; a succession of Shadowland proxy wars around Giant sacrifice, conquest, praise, appetite, dynastic marriage, hoarding, and enforced idleness.
- **Principal actors:** dependent Masks and occasional Archmasks of all seven Idol Thrones; Giant cult offices; counterfeit ancestral patrons; no promoted Regnant succession.
- **Source grammar:** Wisdom 13–15; Hesiodic and Purāṇic divine-war composites; the Great Game's structural rivalry; TES Mask and office accretion.
- **Authorial substrate:** no Idol wishes another to monopolize a civilization's vice architecture. Each Hunger repeatedly captures the consequence of another's work: Wrath supplies prisoners, Pride assigns rank, Greed prices bodies, Gluttony consumes them, Lust converts kinship into access, Envy rewrites inheritance, and Sloth prevents repair.
- **Hard edge:** captive sacrifice, dynastic rape, famine feasts, mutilation, kin murder, and abandoned populations remain the real substance of the network. The Masks do not create those human choices, but cultivate and contest them.
- **Human aperture:** Giant priests believe they are negotiating one pantheon. In reality, the same altar changes Idol routes several times while retaining its public genealogy.
- **1360 AR trace:** one cult complex with seven incompatible underlayers; weapons that answer a different vice than their inscriptions; a feast liturgy whose missing verse changes which Mask receives the offering.
- **Open facts:** whether one autonomous Archmask survived, which Giant horizon hosted the decisive feast, and whether any apparent consumed god was a Mask, office, or counterfeit ancestor.
- **Protected boundaries:** no true succession, no single Idol cause of Giant civilization, and no culture-wide inherited guilt.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

### GP-I-03 — The Hunt of the Escaped Courts

- **Era and scale:** collapse of the Giant world and the first centuries after the Unbinding; Egg White routes used by fleeing Giant courts and their dependants.
- **Principal actors:** genuine Giant Continuants, counterfeit ancestor-patterns, early anchor-owning Patrons, High Hunt court-beings, route predators, Ossuary Minds in formation, and Idol proxies following stolen feeding architecture.
- **Source grammar:** Exodus 14; European Wild Hunt families; Aesir–Vanir hostage and settlement grammar; the Titan and Giant flight material already fixed for the World Egg.
- **Authorial substrate:** some Giant courts flee bodily, some send copies, some move only their offices, and some lie about escaping. Hunters pursue different quarry for incompatible reasons: Menhir law seeks a violated natural claim; Idols seek lost anchors and worship systems; Patrons seek mass and tenants; abandoned dependants seek the ruler who took their refuge with it.
- **Hard edge:** refuges are foreclosed mid-crossing, dependants are cut loose as ballast, proxy children and copied ancestors are sacrificed to confuse pursuit, and whole caravan-demiplanes fray when rulers withdraw upkeep.
- **Human aperture:** a few human households enter the Egg White as servants, hostages, agents, or abandoned tenants without becoming Giant continuities or losing their souls.
- **1360 AR trace:** wandering palace caravans, carcass thrones, an ancestor who remembers the wrong childhood, and a hunt-horn that opens only toward the court it once pursued.
- **Open facts:** the one genuine Giant escape selected for later dossier, the hunter's exact Menhir jurisdiction, and the fate of every human tenant.
- **Protected boundaries:** Nythalum's imperial Ascension remains physically lunar; no Hunt explains the Glass or collects every escaped Giant.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

## Block II — Exodus, Scattering, First Kings, City-States, and Stars

### GP-II-01 — The War of Unfinished Seasons

- **Era and scale:** Scattering through early Hearths; several regional Faewild conjunctions later compressed into one divine war.
- **Principal actors:** Veradia, Solrath, Morvalis, Nevaura, affected place-minds and courts, with Father Sun, Mother Moon, and other Menhir appearing only where an actual pressure intersection warrants it.
- **Source grammar:** Job 38–41; Hesiodic seasonal conflict; Eddic winter and world-ending imagery; locally attributed seasonal procession folklore.
- **Authorial substrate:** the collapse of Giant recurrence systems leaves artificial seasons, famine engines, plague gardens, and death offices running out of sequence. Menhir pressures move to complete, intensify, preserve, or proliferate what those systems began. Their conflict is real but morally blind.
- **Hard edge:** plague blooms consume settlements, killing heat follows refugees, premature decay ruins seed, and lethal frost preserves bodies while starving survivors. None of the four chooses victims by guilt.
- **Human aperture:** early communities survive by discovering which local duty ends one artificial recurrence. Later kings rewrite this work as an alliance with the morally good season against an evil one.
- **1360 AR trace:** a valley where four funerary calendars overlap; weather weapons that still call a wrong season; a Menhir feast at which no prayer may call the defeated pressure wicked.
- **Open facts:** number of conjunctions, regional recensions, and whether any human hero crossed more than one.
- **Protected boundaries:** natural catastrophe is not divine moral judgement; no Menhir replaces the Lord's providence or becomes a saint.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

### GP-II-02 — The War of the Last Map

- **Era and scale:** older than human records but first coherently described by late City-State and Star-age summoners; ongoing across mapped Hell and its Abyssal margins.
- **Principal actors:** damned map-lords, Crowned Refusers, devil-office systems, native Depth fauna, Abyssal wound-beings, opportunistic Idol proxies, Patron brokers, and soul-road forces only where passage is attacked.
- **Source grammar:** Daniel 10; Revelation 12 and 20; familiar Gigantomachy; D&D Blood War as structural inspiration only.
- **Authorial substrate:** Hell's dominions require stable refusal, named prisoners, enforceable roads, and repeatable offices. Abyssal wounds dissolve the distinctions those tyrannies need. Map-lords contain wounds to preserve dominion, then direct them into rival territory; every such weapon opens another contradiction.
- **Hard edge:** damned bearers are forced into devil mantles, prisoners are mutilated into boundary markers, dominions disappear from causal memory, and summoned offices carry the war into mortal prisons and battlefields. No soul is annihilated, but injury and captivity remain real.
- **Human aperture:** summoners believe they are choosing the disciplined side against monsters or the liberating side against jailers. Both offers conceal the human cost that sustains the front.
- **1360 AR trace:** infernal maps whose borders are made from named refusals; veterans manifesting offices from wars they never fought in life; a sealed wound that both a devil marshal and a saintly threshold commission refuse to let the other control.
- **Open facts:** no single beginning, capital, commander, or final front is fixed; individual dominions require dossiers.
- **Protected boundaries:** neither side is good; Abyssal wounds are not a people; the Adversary does not become a dark emperor; heavenly forces do not defend Hell's tyranny.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

### GP-II-03 — The Crown-Market of Shadows

- **Era and scale:** First Kings through the Age of Stars; recurring Shadow market appearing near contested coronations and extinct dynasties.
- **Principal actors:** Pride, Envy, Greed, Wrath, and Lust Masks; royal office-personas; dead ruler residue; Giant ancestor claimants; occasional Patrons leasing proof or ancestry.
- **Source grammar:** 1 Kingdoms/1 Samuel 8–31; Wisdom 14; Greek and Norse contested kingship; TES mantling and role pressure.
- **Authorial substrate:** Masks do not merely sponsor candidates. They trade elements of kingship—victory, pedigree, beauty, vengeance, treasury, fertility, acclamation, and the right to erase a predecessor—then fight over which element defines the crown.
- **Hard edge:** claimants murder kin, seize spouses, destroy archives, sell dependants, and execute defeated households to satisfy the purchased definition. The market exploits those acts but cannot manufacture a human soul or make coercion legitimate.
- **Human aperture:** a ruler may refuse one offer and still accept another. A court can expose the market while remaining guilty of the murders by which it sought entry.
- **1360 AR trace:** crowns with incompatible Mask signatures; coronation clauses that name no human beneficiary; an extinct royal persona still bidding for a body it cannot lawfully possess.
- **Open facts:** whether the market is one recurring demiplane, several copied rites, or a family of Mask courts.
- **Protected boundaries:** no crown creates kingship, no Mask becomes the human ruler, and no transaction proves Regnant succession.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

## Block III — Atherian Glory, Redeemer, Conversion, and Long Frontier

### GP-III-01 — The Siege of the Martyrs' Names

- **Era and scale:** Underground Centuries into legalization; a distributed struggle around prison rolls, graves, feast names, icons, dreams, and early shrines.
- **Principal actors:** living Sinner networks, damned Hollow Prophets, Envy and Pride counterfeits, relic brokers, Patron personas, true saints under rare commission, angels of witness, and ordinary church custodians.
- **Source grammar:** Acts 7–8, 13, 16, and 19; Revelation 12; received martyr acts and relic-translation traditions; saint-and-demon folklore.
- **Authorial substrate:** persecutors destroy bodies and names; later hostile powers compete to occupy the empty identity. One counterfeit offers spectacular revenge, another healing for payment, another political legitimacy, and another a perfect Passion that erases recantation and fear. A genuine manifested saint acts only when commissioned and never needs the counterfeit biography to defend personal honor.
- **Hard edge:** torture, informing, recantation, execution, corpse desecration, grave theft, and retaliatory cult violence remain historical. Counterfeits exploit the dead and survivors rather than inventing all suffering.
- **Human aperture:** clerks, widows, gravediggers, recanters, bishops, thieves, and relatives must decide which evidence to preserve when every supernatural claimant can perform something impressive.
- **1360 AR trace:** rival feast rolls, several active objects under one name, a saint who appears in none of the expected forms, and one counterfeit still demanding punishment of descendants.
- **Open facts:** selected martyr, locality, genuine manifestation, and which later Life preserves the earthly person most accurately.
- **Protected boundaries:** devotion does not create a saint; destroying a counterfeit does not injure the redeemed soul; no martyrdom mechanically produces holiness.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

### GP-III-02 — The War of the Closed Doors

- **Era and scale:** legalization, compulsory conversion, Decadence, and early Covenant transition; conflict across abandoned or repurposed sanctuaries.
- **Principal actors:** displaced Menhir court-beings, dependent Idol Masks, expelled temple offices, Patron claimants buying relic mass, Sinner restorers, church investigators, and occasional saintly or angelic commissions protecting persons rather than property blocs.
- **Source grammar:** 4 Kingdoms/2 Kings 23; Bel and the Dragon; Letter of Jeremiah; Acts 19; *Cath Maige Tuired*'s contested sovereignty and maiming grammar.
- **Authorial substrate:** closing a temple can end fraud, interrupt an Idol feeder, violate a real Menhir compact, displace innocent workers, release a bound office, and expose valuable anchor material simultaneously. Powers compete over what the closed door used to hold and who inherits its obligations.
- **Hard edge:** custodians are killed, sanctuaries smashed, converts and holdouts coerced, relics looted, retaliatory mobs burn churches and older shrines, and displaced court-beings take bodies when old pacts are broken carelessly.
- **Human aperture:** the Children of the Closed Temple and related investigators encounter a war already in progress whose sides do not align neatly with old faith and new faith.
- **1360 AR trace:** doors that admit only former enemies acting together; a church crypt built around a still-valid river compact; Idol Masks using the memory of murdered custodians as proof of their own innocence.
- **Open facts:** exact sanctuary, which compact was lawful, whether a reputed saint ever appeared, and final custody of the dangerous objects.
- **Protected boundaries:** real Menhir response does not vindicate idolatry; real Idol presence does not excuse collective punishment; miracles prove only their commission.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

### GP-III-03 — The Long Rent War

- **Era and scale:** 612–884 AR Long Frontier; Egg White routes paralleling frontier roads, wells, translation houses, and caravan jurisdictions.
- **Principal actors:** Anchor-Lords, Giant Continuants, Driftkin refuges, crafted route systems, Ossuary Minds, stranded Menhir courtiers and Idol Masks, human institutions, and the Creditor's agents where already permitted by canon.
- **Source grammar:** Tobit's journey; Daniel 10's delayed messenger; Aesir–Vanir exchange and hostage settlement; dangerous hospitality; modern planar-trade-war structure.
- **Authorial substrate:** no single empire controls the frontier's Astral correspondences. Patrons fight over safe arrival, translation, water-memory, storage, and return tethers by buying one another's debts, stealing Current Shepherds, moving anchor reefs, and making rival refuge upkeep impossible.
- **Hard edge:** default strands travelers, collapses homes, repossesses leased bodies or memories, separates families across routes, and forces refugees into service. Clear contracts can still distribute lethal risk to people who never signed them.
- **Human aperture:** physicians, interpreters, jurists, keel-hands, and caravan guards become valuable because Material witness can settle distinctions Astral proxies deliberately blur.
- **1360 AR trace:** a border inn with doors owned by rival patrons; Driftkin communities remembering a human court's betrayal; an inherited route debt whose supposed original creditor is a copied Giant ancestor.
- **Open facts:** the central route, parties, relation to the Creditor, and whether one fair Patron survives into the present.
- **Protected boundaries:** Patron is a role, not a race; the war does not explain the Long Frontier as a whole; no contract owns judgement or a human soul.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

## Block IV — Fall, Split, Plague, Reformation, and Present

### GP-IV-01 — The War for the Common Saints

- **Era and scale:** Two Crowns and Great Split through High Noon; rival appropriations of saints whose earthly lives predate the division.
- **Principal actors:** Envy Masks, Pride offices, Patron counterfeits, Sinner polemicists, relic institutions, true saints under bounded commission, and witness angels.
- **Source grammar:** Acts 15; 1 Corinthians 1–3; Revelation's counterfeit worship; Orthodox shared-saint reception and military-apparition traditions.
- **Authorial substrate:** hostile powers attempt to make a common saint prove exclusive ownership. One counterfeit blesses seizure, another stages retaliatory wonders, and a Patron leases a familiar apparition to both sides. The real saint may refuse both requested battles and instead protect prisoners, a grave, or travelers whom each communion calls traitors.
- **Hard edge:** imprisonment, beatings, mob killing, shrine seizure, relic or corpse desecration, and murdered hosts remain the historical fuel. Counterfeits intensify culpable choices without making the churches or populations their puppets.
- **Human aperture:** Last Common Road houses and relic courts preserve evidence that the most politically disappointing apparition may be the genuine one.
- **1360 AR trace:** mutually hostile icons sharing one unforgeable correction; rival relics both authentic but belonging to different people with the same name; a Patron manifestation whose contract language survives under a saint's title.
- **Open facts:** saint, churches' later rulings, which appearances were genuine, and whether reconciliation of the records is possible.
- **Protected boundaries:** one saint remains one soul; neither church is an Idol; a saint cannot be summoned or made partisan by custody.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

### GP-IV-02 — The Ossuary Crown Dispute

- **Era and scale:** Grey Sweat and Black Wind; one Egg White ossuary belt receiving wrecked plague refuges, copied memories, abandoned route offices, and disputed remains.
- **Principal actors:** a maturing Ossuary Mind, funeral and archive Patrons, Gluttony and Envy proxies, damned name-strippers, Driftkin salvagers, human institutions, and a threshold saint or angel only if bound souls or unlawful interception establish a commission.
- **Source grammar:** Tobit 1–2; 2 Maccabees' burial and martyr memory; Ezekiel 37 as restoration imagery that must not become necromantic method; TES composite-identity grammar.
- **Authorial substrate:** the Ossuary Mind is a new Astral person assembled from wreckage, not the collective dead. Several powers nevertheless try to crown it as the voice, owner, or resurrection of the plague dead. It may accept the title for protection while knowing the claim is false.
- **Hard edge:** decomposing bodies, abandoned kin, mass graves, stolen remains, executed carriers, coerced memory extraction, and trapped souls where separately evidenced remain real. The Mind's accurate voices can retraumatize survivors and falsely condemn descendants.
- **Human aperture:** burial crews and descendants must distinguish sacred remains, ordinary remains, residue, copied testimony, bound souls, and the new Mind's own rights.
- **1360 AR trace:** a salvage crown speaking in thousands of voices; a plague register that changes when returned to Material ground; competing churches and Patrons each holding genuine but incomplete custody claims.
- **Open facts:** whether full agency has emerged, whether any soul is trapped, which remains must be returned, and whether destroying the crown would murder a new person.
- **Protected boundaries:** no collective human soul, no automatic resurrection, no explanation of the Quiet Ninth, and no saint manufactured from plague memory.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

### GP-IV-03 — The Proof That Attracted Three Heavens

- **Era and scale:** present day, beginning around one existing Held Proof, relic court, or bounded apparition; no post-1360 outcome fixed.
- **Principal actors:** one Idol Mask, one declared Patron or proxy, one Sinner network or devil-office bearer, mortal investigators, and a possible saintly or angelic commission that is not presumed in advance. “Three heavens” is a frightened folk phrase, not cosmology.
- **Source grammar:** Susanna; Bel and the Dragon; Daniel 10; Acts 16 and 19; competing relic and apparition dossiers.
- **Authorial substrate:** three hostile claimants offer mutually exclusive explanations for one functioning object. Each explanation contains one truth about provenance, custody, or behavior and one lie converting that truth into ownership. Human investigators must determine what the proof actually proves before any larger power can be opposed lawfully.
- **Hard edge:** no new atrocity is predetermined. Existing victims, contracts, stolen names, grave violations, or cult feeding remain only what the selected Held Proof already establishes.
- **Human aperture:** this is a present campaign pressure. The decisive work is testimony, custody, refusal, repair, and possibly combat, not discovering a hidden council controlling every age.
- **1360 AR trace:** the starting object and three incompatible petitions; all consequences remain forward-facing.
- **Open facts:** proof, city, claimants, manifestation status, companions, choices, casualties, and outcome.
- **Protected boundaries:** the Forward Rule; no ninth Held Proof; no secret master plot; no guaranteed saintly manifestation; no claimant gains final authority over judgement.
- **Planting status:** `nursery-unreviewed`.

# Promotion Gates

1. **No divine parity:** no conflict makes the Lord one side in a contest or permits an alternative creator.
2. **Commission gate:** saints and loyal angels receive tasks; they do not maintain independent geopolitical agendas.
3. **Continuity gate:** every actor retains its established continuity through alliance, office, manifestation, defeat, or Astral residence.
4. **Human responsibility gate:** supernatural cultivation never makes murder, rape, slavery, betrayal, or persecution cease to be the perpetrator's act.
5. **Menhir gate:** natural law conflict is morally blind, never proof that catastrophe victims deserved it.
6. **Idol gate:** Mask victory is not true succession; no new Regnant enters canon through these beds.
7. **Patron gate:** fair, predatory, and alien Patrons all remain possible; Patron never becomes a moral species.
8. **Infernal gate:** Hell has no universal government, Abyssal wounds are not a nation, and no fiend annihilates a soul.
9. **Saint gate:** church custody, belief, relic possession, and cultural need cannot summon or manufacture a saint.
10. **Weird gate:** no bed explains the Glass, Bird-Marked Sites, Year of Standing Stars, Thirty Sisters, Bells of the Split, Quiet Ninth, Qarayel's grave, or another registered Weird.
11. **Historical gate:** a great-power conflict may pressure an age but cannot secretly cause the entire human chronology.
12. **Forward gate:** GP-IV-03 establishes only a starting contest around an existing proof; every future act and outcome remains open.
