# Comparative Underdarks, Hollow Worlds, and Chthonic Realms Atlas

> [!info] Atlas integration
> Current canonical destination: [[The Country Beneath the Rains - Peoples and History]]. This dossier retains its research history; former holds and promotion gates are superseded only where the [[World Historical Atlas - Integration and Coverage Ledger]] records an explicit outcome. Preliminary or rejected variants do not override their corrected descendants.

> [!warning] Research, not a new cosmology
> This atlas compares subterranean setting grammars. It does not establish a World Egg Underdark, alter [[Underworld, Underways, and Corpse-Roads]], place a new people, connect the Last Houses to the known world, or make physical depth a route to Hell.

## Executive Finding

The word **underdark** hides several different ideas which should not be collapsed into one place:

1. **A physical cave ecology:** mines, aquifers, lava tubes, fungal forests and organisms adapted to darkness.
2. **A buried historical world:** older cities, roads, tombs and machines beneath later settlements.
3. **A second geopolitical surface:** large caverns with states, trade routes, wars, slaves and frontier zones.
4. **An engineered interior world:** constructed vaults or refuges with artificial climates and inherited maintenance systems.
5. **A topological otherworld:** a place reached by descending but not located at an ordinary depth.
6. **A chthonic jurisdiction:** a land of the dead, imprisoned powers or cosmic judgement.
7. **A hidden-master claim:** occult or fraudulent stories about an advanced civilization secretly governing the surface.

The strongest provisional World Egg model is therefore a **broken subterranean archipelago**, not one globally connected cave continent. Ordinary local caverns lie above buried human and Giant strata. A small number of immense but isolated deep countries and constructed vaults exist beneath particular regions. Rare breaches can open into Menhir jurisdictions, Patron correspondences, Shadowlands echoes, Shell platforms or infernal thin spaces, but those destinations remain metaphysically distinct.

This model preserves the current ruling that no ordinary tunnel crosses the whole world while allowing the setting to use the best parts of Forgotten Realms, Eberron, Pathfinder, Dwarf Fortress, Lovecraft and chthonic mythology without forcing them into one uniform ecology.

## I. Controlling World Egg Boundaries

| What people may call “the world below” | Authorial reality | Non-negotiable boundary |
|---|---|---|
| caves, mines, buried cities and deep basins | Material geography | air, food, water, heat, distance and collapse remain real |
| Jirahar Underways | engineered local passages and host chains | not ancient world tunnels and not a universal network |
| Giant roads and vaults | physical works, occasionally supported by nonhuman law or machines | age and scale do not make them afterlives |
| Menhir under-courts | place jurisdictions or Faewild correspondences approached through particular thresholds | culturally strange nature is not automatically evil or morally approving |
| Feast-shadows and corpse-roads | local Material–Shadowlands reinforcement organised by appetite and burial violation | not soul-roads and not one subterranean empire |
| Patron interiors | anchored Egg White realms with owners, rent and route law | “beneath” may be a route metaphor rather than physical depth |
| Shell littorals | constructed frames imposed on sterile elemental pressure | the Shell has no native nations or naturally personal elemental population |
| Hell and the mapped Depth | post-judgement infernal ecology | no amount of ordinary mining reaches it |
| the Abyss | causal wound and anti-place | not the lowest cave level |
| the human soul-road | divine routing after death | never a navigable geological system |

An underground city can sit beside a thin place to one of these realities. It cannot prove that the two are identical.

# II. Tabletop and Game Setting Atlas

## 1. Greyhawk and the D-Series — the expeditionary prototype

The 1978 sequence *Descent into the Depths of the Earth*, *Shrine of the Kuo-Toa* and *Vault of the Drow* gave the later Underdark its basic campaign grammar. Adventurers follow the patrons behind the Giant raids down a dangerous route, pass through trading and encounter nodes, learn that the enemy possesses a wider political world, and finally reach a great hostile city. The deep world first functions as a **pursuit road whose scale keeps widening**, not as a gazetteer waiting to be visited.

- **Spatial grammar:** a branching expedition route punctuated by caverns, crossings, markets and sanctuaries.
- **Historical grammar:** surface war is only the visible end of a deeper alliance.
- **Hard edge:** enslavement, sacrifice, punitive cult power and predatory states are ordinary political facts rather than isolated monster encounters.
- **Best use:** every stage of descent should reveal a larger patron, older grievance or less comprehensible jurisdiction.
- **Do not copy:** the exact Giant–drow–Lolth conspiracy or its sequence.

This is the strongest model for a **first campaign descent**. It is weaker as a complete ecology because its original purpose was movement and escalation.

Source: [Great Library of Greyhawk, “Descent into the Depths of the Earth”](https://www.greyhawkonline.com/greyhawkwiki/Descent_into_the_Depths_of_the_Earth).

### *Night Below* — the surface mystery becomes a deep war

Carl Sargent’s 1995 generic AD&D campaign begins with disappearances and local crimes in an ordinary surface district. Captives—especially useful spellcasters and laborers—are moved through several layers of intermediaries toward an aboleth project in the Sunless Sea and the city of Great Shaboath. Its great strength is **causal distance**: the farmers, kidnappers, cave factions and final masterminds inhabit different scales of the same crime, and the heroes have time to care about the surface before understanding what threatens it.

- **Transferable grammar:** a human-scale missing-person investigation opens into trafficking routes, dependent factions, an underground sea and a planned transformation of the surface.
- **Hard edge:** kidnapping, slavery, mental domination and the reduction of gifted captives to raw material are the engine of the plot.
- **World Egg use:** the first deep campaign should begin with a wound visible above ground, not with an encyclopaedia lecture about the deep.
- **Do not copy:** aboleths, Great Shaboath, the Sunless Sea or its domination towers.

Source summary: [*Night Below: An Underdark Campaign*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_Below).

## 2. Forgotten Realms — the underground as a second geopolitical world

Forgotten Realms develops the Underdark into a continent-scale secondary geography. Its Upperdark, Middledark and Lowerdark are not merely difficulty levels. They represent decreasing surface dependence, changing political scale and growing metaphysical alienation. Cavern cities, the Darklake, fungi, slave routes, abandoned civilizations and faerzress create a world in which travel, ecology and magic all behave differently.

Menzoberranzan supplies the most famous political model: a giant cavern city regulated by noble Houses, religious authority, intrigue, markets and mass slavery. It is important that this is one state, not the whole Underdark and not every drow person. Other regions support duergar kingdoms, svirfneblin communities, kuo-toan cults, illithid dominions, myconid groves, aboleth remnants and human-descended civilizations such as Deep Imaskar.

Undermountain demonstrates a different Realms mode: the **authored megadungeon** beneath a living surface metropolis. Its levels contain distinct factions and histories, while the Yawning Portal turns descent into a public economy of spectators, adventurers, salvage and death. It need not connect naturally to every cavern beneath Faerûn to feel inexhaustible.

- **Spatial grammar:** tiered depth; regional roads; lakes and fungus ecologies; magical interference with easy navigation.
- **Political grammar:** independent cities and species-polities compete without one universal emperor.
- **Campaign grammar:** escape, hazardous travel, diplomacy with compromised powers, faction war, madness and return to the surface with knowledge nobody wants.
- **Hard edge:** hereditary slavery, torture, sacrifice, House extermination, predatory breeding and the treatment of thinking captives as livestock.
- **Best use:** an underground region should contain several mutually hostile civilizations and neutral exchanges, not one “evil cave race.”
- **Do not copy:** drow Houses, Lolth, faerzress, the Darklake map or named peoples as a package.

Useful official summaries: [*Out of the Abyss*](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/oota), [D&D Beyond on Menzoberranzan](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1028-welcome-to-menzoberranzan-the-drow-city-of), [D&D Beyond on drow beyond that city](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/323-a-light-in-the-darkness-playing-a-drow-character), and [*Dungeon of the Mad Mage*](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/wdotmm). A 2026 Unearthed Arcana article returns to the D-series and experiments with “Unlight,” but it is playtest material rather than settled lore: [designer discussion](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2226-designer-insights-from-unearthed-arcana-underdark).

## 3. Eberron — Khyber as prison, wound and worlds within the world

Eberron’s most distinctive move is refusing to make Khyber a normal layer of rock. A descent can enter a demiplane whose entrance occurs in several distant places, whose landscape expresses an imprisoned power and whose internal laws need not match Material distance. The daelkyr are not all sitting in one deep capital: each can be bound within its own nightmare realm while servants, cults and altered creatures move through openings.

The dwarven Realm Below adds a second, more political grammar. The Mror Holds rediscover ancestral territory overrun by daelkyr forces and must decide whether the symbionts and biological tools of their enemies are corruptions, weapons, inheritances or all three. The underworld becomes a **war of historical reclamation in which useful enemy technology changes the reclaimers**.

- **Spatial grammar:** doors into separate interior laws rather than one continuous map.
- **Historical grammar:** the prison is also a resource field and the lost homeland of later claimants.
- **Hard edge:** forced biological alteration, fleshcraft, inherited contamination, madness and factions which knowingly use the enemy’s living tools.
- **Best use:** each great deep realm can have an owner, law, ecology and failure condition of its own.
- **Do not copy:** Khyber, daelkyr, dragon shards or symbionts as names and mechanics.

This fits the World Egg particularly well if the local physical cavern and the exceptional otherworld threshold are kept separate. A Giant prison, Menhir jurisdiction or Patron interior may have many entrances without making the rock between those entrances hollow.

Sources: Keith Baker’s [*Exploring Eberron* FAQ](https://keith-baker.com/faq-exe/), [“Reaching for the Stars”](https://keith-baker.com/dm-stars/), and the [*Exploring Eberron* contents page](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/exeb).

## 4. Dragonlance — the sealed refuge becomes a nation and a moral bottleneck

Dragonlance usually makes the subterranean world more local and historical. Thorbardin is not a generic monster continent. It is a sealed dwarven homeland with clans, castes, engineering, internal war, memory and a gate which starving outsiders may beg to enter. The Dwarfgate conflict makes refuge morally expensive: the mountain population protects limited stores while kin and refugees outside face famine, and civil war follows.

The War of the Lance repeats the pressure when surface refugees seek entry. *Dragons of Dwarven Depths* turns descent into negotiation over food, legitimacy, the Hammer of Kharas and who may claim sanctuary. Other locations, including Chorane under Taladas, show that the setting can also support a remote multi-people civilization beneath a polar land without calling it the same underworld.

- **Spatial grammar:** one monumental enclosed country with limited gates, underground water and engineered habitation.
- **Political grammar:** clans and castes are more important than monster taxonomy.
- **Hard edge:** closing the gate means real starvation; kin kill kin over access; refuge capacity becomes a weapon.
- **Best use:** an underground kingdom should control a scarce entrance or refuge whose hospitality can save a people and condemn another.
- **Do not copy:** Thorbardin’s clans, Hammer, Skullcap or exact Dwarfgate history.

Sources: Dragonlance Nexus on [*Dragons of Dwarven Depths*](https://dragonlancenexus.com/products/dragons-of-the-dwarven-depths/), [dwarven races and castes](https://dragonlancenexus.com/dwarven-racescastes/), and [Taladas and Chorane](https://dragonlancenexus.com/taladas/guide-to-taladas/the-continent-of-taladas/).

## 5. Mystara — preserved inner worlds and the cruelty of survival law

Mystara offers two different models.

The **Hollow World** is a literal inner surface lit by an internal sun. Immortals move threatened peoples and species there and impose a Spell of Preservation which protects cultures partly by making change difficult. It is both sanctuary and museum: extinction is prevented, but living people inherit divine curatorship they did not choose.

The **Shadow Elf caverns** are a post-catastrophe exile civilization. They possess multiple cities, strange gravity, fungus agriculture, rivers and a revealed law attributed to Rafiel. That law orders social life and, in the classic material, requires infants born with certain deformities to be abandoned. This is not decorative darkness; the underground refuge creates a disciplined community whose survival theology also authorises killing, exclusion and later vengeance against the surface.

- **Spatial grammar:** a true inner world can be distinct from enormous but conventional cavern nations.
- **Historical grammar:** gods or Immortals preserve peoples while freezing them into curated versions of themselves.
- **Hard edge:** eugenic abandonment, isolation, theocratic control and surface reconquest justified through ancestral injury.
- **Best use:** preservation can be a genuine mercy and an assault on freedom at the same time.
- **Do not copy:** the internal red sun, Spell of Preservation, Rafiel, Fourteen Verses or shadow elves.

Sources: [Mystara Hollow World overview](https://wiki.mystara.net/hollow_world) and [Shadow Elf cavern overview](https://classic.mystara.net/shadowelf.html).

## 6. Dark Sun — the undercity as compressed civilizational ruin

Athas does not need a planet-spanning Underdark. Under-Tyr works because the current city stands over older urban layers, ruins, cisterns and passages associated with a world that had more water and different technology. Criminals, rebels, slaves, preservers and state agents all use the same buried fabric for different reasons.

- **Spatial grammar:** streets, palaces and utilities from several lost cities crushed into one undercity.
- **Historical grammar:** ecological collapse is readable vertically in masonry and sediment.
- **Hard edge:** slavery and political terror drive people underground; unsafe ruins are the refuge available to those excluded from lawful space.
- **Best use:** every modern capital can possess a smaller undercity whose layers contradict the official history above.
- **Do not copy:** Tyr, sorcerer-kings, defiling or the specific Green Age.

The important lesson is that a powerful underworld can fit beneath one city and never connect to another realm.

## 7. Planescape — the undercity as topological refuse

Undersigil turns “below” into an institutional category. Sewers, crypts, abandoned districts and discarded structures sit under a city whose geometry already ignores ordinary space. A passage may lead beneath a different ward or into a portal relation rather than a predictable coordinate.

- **Spatial grammar:** civic waste, lost law and impossible topology.
- **Historical grammar:** regimes throw inconvenient people, gods, buildings and records downward without actually erasing them.
- **Hard edge:** the poor, criminalised and monstrous live among the physical consequences of institutions which deny they exist.
- **Best use:** a city’s underworld can be its suppressed archive and displaced population rather than a natural ecosystem.
- **Do not copy:** Sigil, the Lady, factions or portal keys.

## 8. Fourth Edition’s Nentir model — the road made by divine injury

The 4e *Underdark* gives the deep world a memorable causal scar. Torog’s violent passage makes the King’s Highway, and echoes of subterranean geography appear in the Feydark and Shadowdark. Travel follows a wounded god’s ancient route; blood, prison and pain remain materially present in the road.

- **Spatial grammar:** one transregional route cuts across otherwise separate deep domains and adjacent realities.
- **Historical grammar:** geography is a wound left by a greater power’s movement.
- **Hard edge:** torture, imprisonment and divine suffering are infrastructure.
- **Best use:** a route can be useful precisely because its maker committed or endured an atrocity.
- **Do not copy:** Torog, godsblood, King’s Highway, Feydark or Shadowdark.

For the World Egg, this grammar could belong to a bounded Titan path, Giant evacuation cut, Menhir pursuit or infernal breach. It cannot be a road which casually makes all planes identical.

## 9. Ravenloft — the useful absence

Ravenloft does not need a shared Underdark because each imprisoned domain can generate its own mine, crypt, buried city or lightless border. Its stronger lesson is negative: **not every setting benefits from connecting its caves**. Isolation can preserve tone, prevent easy travel and make the local ruler’s guilt shape the entire subterranean region.

## 10. Pathfinder’s Darklands — three different genres stacked vertically

Legacy Pathfinder divides the Darklands into three regions:

- **Nar-Voth:** upper wilderness, isolated settlements and ruins close enough to the surface for repeated contact.
- **Sekamina:** the familiar continent-spanning political underworld of cities, empires, trade and war.
- **Orv:** the deep “Darklands for the Darklands,” where immense Vaults contain black deserts, ancient machines, dinosaur jungles and other apparently constructed environments.

Orv is the most transferable idea. Its Vaults are not merely bigger caves; each can be a separate lost-world campaign with an absent maker, failed purpose and surviving maintenance ecology. The vertical sequence also changes genre: frontier exploration above, political fantasy in the middle and pulp/cosmic archaeology below.

The older material includes demon-worshipping drow Houses, duergar slavers, serpentfolk remnants, ghoul cities, intellect devourers, gugs, urdefhans, aboleth-related peoples and many other factions. Current Pathfinder’s Remaster direction should be treated separately from that legacy OGL-era register: drow no longer occupy the same current setting role, and later material uses cavern elves and other peoples without simply filing one replacement into the old drow empire.

- **Spatial grammar:** three depths which support different campaign genres.
- **Historical grammar:** the deepest places are constructed terraria whose makers have vanished.
- **Hard edge:** slavery, flesh theft, predatory empires, sacrificial cults, ancient racial projects and entire populations used as experiments.
- **Best use:** many independent vaults are better than one blended mega-vault.
- **Do not copy:** the three names, Vault Builders, named ancestries, Zirnakaynin or current/legacy retcons.

Sources: Paizo’s [“Down Orvway”](https://paizo.com/blog/down-orvway), [“Denizens of the Darklands”](https://paizo.com/blog/denizens-of-the-darklands), [*Darklands Revisited*](https://store.paizo.com/pathfinder-campaign-setting-darklands-revisited-pfrpg-pdf/), the [*Sky King’s Tomb* Player’s Guide](https://downloads.paizo.com/SkyKingsTombPlayersGuide.pdf), and [reporting on Paizo’s post-OGL drow decision](https://www.wargamer.com/pathfinder/drow-retcon).

## 11. Dwarf Fortress — depth as an economic temptation curve

*Dwarf Fortress* creates three cavern layers during world generation, each with its own terrain, water, organisms and access problems. Beneath them lie magma, rare materials and the concealed underworld. The player does not descend because the plot says so. The fortress descends because water, soil, metal, magma and adamantine are valuable.

That creates a powerful causal chain:

> the settlement needs or desires a resource → miners breach a less-known layer → the opening changes migration, defence and ecology → a forgotten beast or deeper population gains a route upward → the fortress must live with the decision.

Forgotten beasts are particularly useful because they are procedurally generated historical actors. One can poison a reservoir, destroy a civilization, become trapped for centuries or be remembered in art. The deepest “Hidden Fun Stuff” makes greed literal: the most desirable material is also the seal on catastrophe.

- **Spatial grammar:** several generated ecologies separated by rock and breaches.
- **Historical grammar:** player decisions create the age’s monsters, ruins and legends.
- **Hard edge:** mining accidents, starvation, flooding, magma, mutilation, madness and fortress-wide death follow comprehensible material decisions.
- **Best use:** every depth needs both a concrete resource and a concrete new risk.
- **Do not copy:** adamantine spires, demons, forgotten beasts or exact cavern generation.

Sources, with spoilers: [Dwarf Fortress Wiki on caverns](https://www.dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Cavern), [the underworld](https://new.dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Underworld), and [demons](https://www.dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/Demon).

# III. Lovecraftian Descent Grammars

Lovecraft’s subterranean fiction is most useful when treated as **archaeological horror**, not as a catalogue of monsters. The descent changes what the investigators think history, humanity and civilization are.

## The Nameless City

A desert explorer descends through architecture built for nonhuman bodies. Murals and preserved remains reveal a civilization older than humanity and a deeper luminous country beneath its ruined capital.

- **Transferable grammar:** architecture itself establishes the builders’ anatomy; each lower gallery rewrites the age and ownership of the ruin.
- **Hard edge:** the explorer knowingly continues after evidence of danger and becomes trapped by the returning dead or their surviving continuity.
- **World Egg use:** a Giant or prehuman city should be physically inconvenient for humans, not a human palace with large doors.

Primary text: [“The Nameless City”](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/nc.aspx).

## At the Mountains of Madness

An expedition reconstructs an alien civilization through bodies, murals, geology and engineered organisms. The apparent monsters possess art and history; their manufactured servants have also rebelled. Horror comes from discovering that neither antiquity nor civilization guarantees innocence.

- **Transferable grammar:** the ruin contains its own visual historiography, but the investigators must compare propaganda with biological and geological evidence.
- **Hard edge:** vivisection, slave creation, massacre and retaliatory extermination remain part of the recovered history.
- **World Egg use:** Giant wall histories should be valuable, self-serving evidence rather than omniscient exposition.

Primary text: [“At the Mountains of Madness”](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/mm.aspx).

## The Shadow Out of Time

Impossible memories lead to a buried archive whose records span species and epochs. A sealed lower threat proves that the great archivists were neither the first nor the final masters.

- **Transferable grammar:** testimony initially dismissed as madness becomes meaningful only when an independent ruin reproduces private details.
- **Hard edge:** bodily displacement, stolen lives and an archive assembled through involuntary possession.
- **World Egg use:** copied memories, archive-patterns and human souls must remain distinct even when records are accurate.

Primary text: [“The Shadow Out of Time”](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/sot.aspx).

## The Mound

The subterranean K’n-yan possess cities, science, slavery, biological alteration and methods of projecting or reanimating bodies. Still deeper Yoth preserves the remains of an older nonhuman order. The story repeatedly breaks the equation of technological sophistication with moral progress.

- **Transferable grammar:** the advanced hidden state uses surface humans as specimens, labor and entertainment; its own people cannot agree whether the surface is contamination, resource or escape.
- **Hard edge:** slavery, torture, vivisection, corpse use, sexual exploitation and punitive transformation are integral.
- **World Egg use:** a hidden civilization can be materially brilliant, spiritually corrupt and internally divided without being an inherently evil species.
- **Caution:** the work was written for Zealia Bishop and carries Lovecraft’s racial, colonial and eugenic obsessions. Those hierarchies are not transferable. The culpable institutions and bodily crimes are.

Primary text: [“The Mound”](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/mo.aspx).

## Additional short grammars

- **“The Rats in the Walls”:** the respectable house is built over a family’s cannibal economy; descent turns genealogy into criminal evidence.
- **“The Festival”:** an ordinary civic feast opens through cellars and catacombs onto a far older ritual geography.
- **The Dream-Quest cycle:** ghoul passages, the Vale of Pnath and Dreamlands routes make descent topological rather than geological.

The Lovecraftian rule worth keeping is:

> Every major layer should answer one historical question and create a worse one.

# IV. Mythic and Religious Source Families

These traditions do not describe one universal “ancient Underdark.” Their underworlds perform different religious, social and narrative work. Borrow the function only after naming the tradition.

## 1. Orthodox Christian Hades and Hell — a boundary, not a cave template

The Scriptural and Orthodox language of descent, Hades, the pit and the Harrowing of Hades concerns death, captivity, judgement and the Redeemer’s victory. It does not authorise a geological map in which miners eventually enter the soul-road or Hell.

The transferable grammar is **bounded sacred descent and liberation under unique commission**. It belongs to the Redeemer and to later saints only where their commission genuinely reaches a captive or threshold. It must not become an adventurers’ repeatable mine route.

## 2. Mesopotamian Irkalla and Inanna’s descent — gates strip status

In Sumerian and Akkadian traditions, the land of the dead is a ruled jurisdiction with gates, laws and a queen. Inanna passes seven gates and surrenders regalia at each before she is killed; restoration requires intervention and a substitute.

- **Transferable grammar:** entry is a legal process which removes rank, weapons, identity claims and exemptions one by one.
- **Hard edge:** death is real, return has a price and another person may be taken.
- **World Egg use:** an underground court can strip offices and protections without touching the human soul itself.

Source overview: [World History Encyclopedia, “The Descent of Inanna”](https://www.worldhistory.org/article/215/the-descent-of-inanna/).

## 3. Greek Hades and Tartarus — the dead realm is not the prisoners’ pit

Greek sources distinguish the realm of the dead from Tartarus, the much deeper cosmic prison where defeated Titans are bound behind bronze gates under warders. Later geography varies, but the functional distinction remains useful.

- **Transferable grammar:** one “below” receives the dead; another contains defeated created powers; rivers, gates, oaths and warders organise passage.
- **Hard edge:** divine kin are mutilated, overthrown and imprisoned; escape threatens cosmic government.
- **World Egg use:** Titan fragment-prisons can exist beneath the Material world without becoming Hell or the soul-road.

Source collection: [Theoi, “Hades — The Underworld”](https://www.theoi.com/Kosmos/Haides.html).

## 4. K’iche’ Maya Xibalba — the hostile court of tests

The *Popol Wuj* belongs specifically to K’iche’ Maya tradition. Its Hero Twins reach Xibalba through a dangerous route, confront deceptive death-lords, endure test houses and ballgames, suffer decapitation, orchestrate death and restoration, and finally defeat the court through performance and counter-deception.

- **Transferable grammar:** the underworld ruler weaponises hospitality, riddles, seating, sport and etiquette; intelligence matters as much as violence.
- **Hard edge:** earlier challengers are killed and displayed; bodies are dismembered; the victorious Twins deliberately deceive and kill their enemies.
- **World Egg use:** a deep court can make every social act into a lethal jurisdictional test.
- **Non-transferable surface:** K’iche’ names, maize theology, ballgame sequence, living ritual identity and lineage claims.

Sources: Smithsonian National Museum of the American Indian, [“Creation Story of the Maya”](https://maya.nmai.si.edu/the-maya/creation-story-maya), and the American Historical Association’s [*Popol Vuh* teaching resource](https://www.historians.org/resource/popol-vuh/).

## 5. Hindu Purāṇic Pātāla — a splendid underworld which is not hell

Purāṇic descriptions of Pātāla vary, but commonly present seven subterranean regions inhabited by nāgas, asuras and other nonhuman powers. These realms may contain brilliant cities, treasure, pleasure, rulers and artificial or jewel-light. They are distinct from Naraka’s punitive destinations.

- **Transferable grammar:** “below” may be wealthy, urban and beautiful rather than degraded; splendour and moral danger can coexist.
- **Hard edge:** rival divine and nonhuman lineages wage wars, take captives and use powers of illusion; luxury is not innocence.
- **World Egg use:** a deep nonhuman state need not be Hell-coded or technologically primitive.
- **Non-transferable surface:** nāga identities, Purāṇic cosmography and sacred names require their own attribution rather than becoming generic snake people.

Sources: [2026 comparative study of Pātāla traditions](https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1444/17/6/743) and [*Bhāgavata Purāṇa* 5.24 translation](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-bhagavata-purana/d/doc1127141.html).

## 6. Zoroastrian Yima’s Vara — the underground as catastrophe refuge

In the *Vendidad*, Ahura Mazda warns Yima of a deadly winter. Yima constructs the vara and preserves selected people, animals, plants and seeds. Scholarly readings often treat the enclosure as subterranean or otherwise sealed.

- **Transferable grammar:** survival requires selection, breeding stock, stored food, light, law and controlled closure.
- **Hard edge:** preservation necessarily leaves multitudes outside and gives the selector terrible power.
- **World Egg collision:** this is close to the Glass refuge and should deepen its logistics or inspire a different failed vault, not create a second Prophet or duplicate ark.

Sources: [Encyclopaedia Iranica on paradise and Yima’s enclosure](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/paradise/) and [the *Vendidad*](https://www.iranicaonline.org/articles/vendidad/).

## 7. Japanese Yomi — seeing the dead body breaks reunion

In the *Kojiki* account, Izanagi follows Izanami into Yomi, violates the condition governing their meeting, sees her decayed state and flees pursuit. A great boulder finally seals the pass between worlds.

- **Transferable grammar:** grief motivates descent; violated hospitality or taboo makes return impossible; a physical-looking gate is closed by an act which re-establishes cosmic separation.
- **Hard edge:** decomposition, shame, pursuit and the final exchange of death and birth make the separation irreversible.
- **World Egg use:** a tomb threshold can stage a false or symbolic reenactment without becoming the true human afterlife.

Source: Kokugakuin University’s [Encyclopedia of Shinto entry on Yomi](https://d-museum.kokugakuin.ac.jp/eos/detail/?id=8639).

## 8. Mexica Mictlan — death as a long hazardous passage

Mexica accounts place Mictlan within a larger plurality of afterlife destinations; death circumstances matter. Common colonial-era and later reconstructions describe nine levels or stages and a journey through water, mountains, cutting winds and other dangers.

- **Transferable grammar:** the dead road is not one courtroom but a prolonged loss, transformation and passage through hostile conditions.
- **Hard edge:** identity is worn down; the route takes years; the dead are not simply comfortable ancestors waiting beneath a hill.
- **World Egg boundary:** use this for a culture’s received funeral imagination, a Material mortuary pilgrimage or an exploitative counterfeit route—not to overwrite the established soul-road.
- **Non-transferable surface:** Mexica deities, death classifications, ritual objects and stage names.

Sources: [British Museum object note on Mictlan](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/E_Am1849-0629-2) and a current scholarly qualification of the standard layered model, [“Beyond Mictlan”](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0956536126100911).

## 9. Irish sí and mound thresholds — underground is an address, not a geology

Irish traditions concerning the **sí** join particular mounds, ancient monuments and landscapes to otherworld inhabitants. English “fairy” can flatten substantial differences among place, people and later folklore. The mound is often an aperture or overlapping address, not proof that the Otherworld fills the planet’s crust.

- **Transferable grammar:** a small local hill opens into a court with different time, hospitality and ownership law.
- **Hard edge:** abduction, substitution, wasting, retaliation and permanent time-loss remain possible consequences of crossing.
- **World Egg use:** this strongly supports Menhir courts reached through particular sites while ordinary caves remain ordinary.

Source example: [Cambridge Archaeological Journal on Irish fairy archaeology and the sí](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/cambridge-archaeological-journal/article/bewitched-by-an-elf-dart-fairy-archaeology-folk-magic-and-traditional-medicine-in-ireland/7EF2D9BD63A34CAA405A42E120C4D421).

# V. Modern Occult and Pseudohistorical Traditions

## Agartha — a modern occult hidden centre, not ancient Asian folklore

The Agartha complex is primarily a nineteenth- and twentieth-century Western esoteric construction. Louis Jacolliot introduced **Asgartha** in 1873 as an ancient Indian solar capital but did not yet give the familiar subterranean kingdom. Alexandre Saint-Yves d’Alveydre’s *Mission de l’Inde*—privately developed in the 1880s and circulated posthumously in 1910—made Agarttha a hidden initiatic centre beneath Asia. Ferdinand Ossendowski and René Guénon subsequently reshaped the tradition around the King of the World, spiritual hierarchy and concealed knowledge.

It is frequently conflated with Shambhala. That should be resisted. Shambhala belongs to the living Kālacakra Buddhist tradition as a hidden kingdom with its own sacred and eschatological functions; the hollow-earth capital and worldwide tunnel network belong to modern Agartha occultism.

- **Transferable grammar:** a concealed capital claims to preserve primordial law while surface governments decay; distributed entrances are controlled by initiates; apparent political events may be interpreted as instructions from an unseen centre.
- **Hard edge:** the hidden hierarchy treats ordinary peoples as unready children, legitimises authoritarian “wise” government and can become racial or conspiratorial propaganda.
- **World Egg use:** a Patron city, Giant continuant or human secret order may falsely claim this role. Its existence and wisdom must be independently tested.
- **Do not transfer:** Aryan master-race claims, fabricated Asian authority, occult antisemitism or the presentation of Agartha as authentic Buddhist or Hindu tradition.

Sources: Theosophical Society in America, [“The Kingdom of Agarttha”](https://www.theosophical.org/publications/quest-magazine/the-kingdom-of-agarttha-a-journey-into-the-hollow-earth); [CICAP on the development of the myth](https://www.cicap.org/articolo/luis-jacolliot-esploratore-di-misteri); and Guénon’s own discussion of [Western Agarttha sources](https://www.index-rene-guenon.org/index.php?Lang=FR&Scroll_bar=0&TableMat=0&affichage=aff_chap&largeur=45&mode=livre&page=7&police=Times+New+Roman&sigle=RM&taille=3).

## Akakor and the Ugha Mongulala — the fraud is the usable story

Karl Brugger’s *Chronicle of Akakor* presented an alleged fifteen-thousand-year oral history given by “Tatunca Nara,” who claimed to lead a hidden Amazonian people called the Ugha Mongulala. The story included ancient teachers, repeated catastrophes, underground refuge cities and German soldiers. Brugger never saw the supposed kingdom.

“Tatunca Nara” was identified as Günther Hans Hauck, a German who invented the Indigenous identity and led travellers into the Amazon. Several people connected to his expeditions disappeared or died; German investigators treated him as responsible for multiple deaths, though prosecution and extradition did not resolve the case. There is no sound archaeological or ethnographic basis for Akakor or the Ugha Mongulala as a real Indigenous people.

- **Transferable grammar:** one charismatic guide monopolises access to an unknown interior; all evidence passes through his recitation; missing records and dead expeditions are reinterpreted as proof of secrecy; outsiders create a market which rewards escalating claims.
- **Hard edge:** invented identity, financial exploitation, cultural impersonation, disappearance and death are part of the real modern story.
- **Best World Egg use:** a fraudulent survivor fabricates an entire underground people and kills or abandons seekers to protect the lie. The fraud may conceal an unrelated genuine ruin, but the invented chronicle must not become accidentally true in every detail.
- **Do not transfer:** the name Ugha Mongulala, the false Indigenous identity or its invented history as though it were Amazonian mythology.

Sources: NDR/ARD’s 2026 investigation, [“Tatunca Nara: Ein deutscher Hochstapler als Häuptling in Brasilien”](https://www.ndr.de/geschichte/chronologie/ARD-Crime-Time-Tatunca-Nara-Auf-Spuren-eines-Moerders%2Ctatuncanara100.html), and Brugger’s framing preserved in [*The Chronicle of Akakor*](https://www.sciences-faits-histoires.com/medias/files/akakor1.pdf).

# VI. Real Subterranean Ecology and Engineering

Fantasy caverns become convincing when their politics grow from physical limits.

## 1. Darkness is not the main problem; energy is

Fungi do not create calories from nothing. Most natural cave food webs depend on organic matter washed, dropped or carried in from the surface. Bat guano, seasonal floods, roots, corpses and migrating animals can support local abundance, but make the cave dependent on exterior cycles.

Movile Cave in Romania demonstrates a rarer alternative: a long-isolated ecosystem whose primary production comes from chemosynthetic microbes using sulphur and methane chemistry. That environment is productive but chemically hostile and locally specific, not a licence for endless pleasant mushroom plains.

World Egg underground populations therefore need at least one explicit energy base:

- surface imports and political control of sinkholes;
- root zones and seasonal animal migration;
- cultivated detritus, fungus and insects;
- fish or other water-borne food from an external watershed;
- geothermal or chemosynthetic production with toxic by-products;
- ancient machines, Menhir law or magic whose maintenance and cost are defined.

Sources: [Movile Cave microbial study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10672346/) and [review of its biodiversity and food web](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-2818/13/3/128).

## 2. Water is road, food, border and weapon

Karst systems move water quickly through sinkholes, conduits and springs. This can produce extraordinary wells and transport routes, but also allows waste, poison and disease to cross political borders without warning. A city which controls an upstream shaft may hold several lower communities hostage.

Source: [USGS overview of karst aquifers](https://www.usgs.gov/mission-areas/water-resources/science/karst-aquifers?page=1).

## 3. Isolation creates extreme localism

True cave-adapted organisms can be endemic to one cave or watershed and unable to survive on the surface. A believable deep world should not put the same monsters, crops and diseases in every cavern. Two basins separated by a few miles of unbroken rock may possess more biological difference than two surface kingdoms separated by an ocean.

Source: [US National Park Service on cave ecosystems](https://home.nps.gov/maca/learn/nature/caves.htm).

## 4. Atmosphere is political

Smoke, carbon dioxide, sulphur gases, radon, humidity and oxygen depletion gather according to chamber shape and ventilation. Cooking, forging, mass burial and warfare alter breathable space. Ventilation shafts are therefore strategic infrastructure. Sealing one can murder a district without breaching its walls.

## 5. Scale needs support

Natural caverns large enough for weather and nations are exceptional. The World Egg can justify some through Giant engineering, ancient collapse, Menhir jurisdiction, non-Euclidean correspondence or a constructed vault, but the cause should leave evidence and costs. Most inhabited systems should remain chains of chambers rather than an empty hollow planet.

# VIII. Recommended World Egg Design Direction

## The underworld should be plural

The research supports five provisional physical categories. These are design handles, not canonical names:

1. **Near deeps:** local mines, aquifers, tombs, undercities and Jirahar-scale engineered passages.
2. **Buried strata:** Giant estates, Orphaned cities, Atherian foundations, mass graves and obsolete infrastructure layered beneath later states.
3. **Deep countries:** immense isolated basins with their own watersheds, ecologies and populations; most have no present connection to one another.
4. **Made vaults:** Giant, human or other constructed environments with artificial light, maintenance law and an identifiable original purpose.
5. **False depth:** thresholds which look like deeper caves but actually cross into a Menhir court, Patron correspondence, Feast-shadow, Shell platform, Hell breach or Abyssal injury.

A sixth category—**broken deepways**—should remain a retcon question. A handful of severed ancient corridors could join some basins while leaving the canon prohibition on a universal network substantially true. This should be decided only after a regional map proves that the routes add more story than they remove.

## The best combined tone

The most World Egg-native blend is:

- Forgotten Realms for **political multiplicity**;
- Pathfinder’s Orv for **independent vault-worlds**;
- Eberron for **topological exceptions with owners**;
- Dragonlance for **refuge, famine and closed-gate politics**;
- Dwarf Fortress for **resource causality and player-made catastrophe**;
- Lovecraft for **evidence-led deep history**;
- real cave science for **energy, water and atmospheric limits**;
- mythic underworlds for **threshold law**, without confusing them with geography;
- Agartha and Akakor for **false claims about hidden masters**, not factual cosmology.

## Likely historical populations

No decision is made here, but the World Egg already has several native ways to populate depth without copying drow, duergar or generic “subterranean races”:

- Orphaned human remnants that survived the Giant collapse underground;
- Giant client populations abandoned in shelter or industrial vaults;
- human communities descended from prisoners, miners, mortuary workers or maintenance castes;
- Giant-blooded humans with complete human souls;
- genuine Giants or continuants in a few exceptional sealed holdings;
- residue-born persons whose original human souls have already routed;
- created animals, altered predators and autonomous constructed persons, each separately classified;
- Patron proxies, dependent Idol Masks and Menhir envoys entering through specific jurisdictions rather than constituting whole biological species.

Every culture can contain saints, sinners, victims, conquerors, craftsmen and factions. Brutal institutions should be stated plainly—slavery, sacrifice, predation, caste killing, forced breeding, child abandonment and cannibalism where actually planted—without making moral evil an inherited racial substance.

## What should remain unavailable

- no tunnel from the Last Houses to the known world merely to solve first contact;
- no globally safe underground trade route;
- no indigenous Shell civilization;
- no mine shaft which reaches Hell by depth alone;
- no subterranean society which preserves or manufactures human souls;
- no single ancient empire which built every vault;
- no claim that all surface myth remembers the same hidden kingdom;
- no Akakor or Agartha claim treated as authentic Indigenous or ancient Asian testimony;
- no fungus economy without an energy source;
- no protected Weird explained by a deep civilization.

# IX. Candidate Questions for the Later Translation Pass

1. Which region receives the first playable deep country: the known imperial core, a distant Orphaned land, or a wholly separate expansion region?
2. Does the current “no worldwide underdark” ruling remain absolute, or do two or three broken Giant deepways survive?
3. Which layer is old enough to contain Titan fragments, and what prevents their accidental reassembly?
4. What feeds the largest settlement, where does its water come from and who controls its air?
5. Which inhabitants predate the Great Unbinding, which fled it and which arrived later?
6. What did the deepest vault originally do: refuge, prison, mine, ark, observatory, breeding work, archive or failed escape platform?
7. Which apparent “deep god” is a Menhir court, Idol Mask, Patron, Giant continuant, Sinner office, archive-pattern or fraud?
8. What resource makes surface powers descend despite the cost?
9. What historical fact can be proven only by going below?
10. What false Agartha- or Akakor-like guide tradition already sends expeditions to their deaths?
11. Which deep society is materially more advanced than the surface, and what moral crime supports that achievement?
12. Which deep society is materially poor but preserves a true historical witness the surface destroyed?

## Research Disposition

- **Retain for translation:** broken subterranean archipelago; independent vault-worlds; depth as economic temptation; sealed-refuge politics; evidence-led historical descent; chthonic threshold law; false hidden-master traditions.
- **Revise before use:** continent-spanning travel, universally evil cave ancestries, natural elemental nations, literal mythic afterlives and benevolent immortal curators.
- **Defer:** exact layers, map, peoples, names, first campaign region and any connection among deep countries.
- **Reject:** one universal underground empire, physical access to the human soul-road, Agartha as ancient Asian testimony, Ugha Mongulala as an Indigenous people and any underground route which defeats the Last Houses’ no-contact ruling.

## First Translation Chassis

[[The Country Beneath the Rains - First Subterranean Region Chassis]] selects an unmapped Emerald Expanse watershed for the first full regional test. It applies the broken-archipelago model through surface sink country, Green Mouths, Giant and human Broken Galleries, an inhabited Rain Country, toxic Furnace Roots, separate Made Vaults and rare False Depths. Its populations are principally Orphaned humans with complete souls, divided among independent water cities, boat houses, an industrial crown, a sealed refuge and mixed surface–deep households. Air, water, food, light, technology and historical violence receive explicit mechanisms. No map, culture, first contact or seed is promoted.

Nothing in this atlas has entered the Historical Seed Garden.
