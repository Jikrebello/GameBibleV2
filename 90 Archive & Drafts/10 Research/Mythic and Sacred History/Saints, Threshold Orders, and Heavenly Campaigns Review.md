# Saints, Threshold Orders, and Heavenly Campaigns Review

## Purpose and Status

This is the first family review arising from [[Comparative Realms, Courts, and Sacred Warfare Atlas]]. It takes the twelve `ST-R` research leads and determines which are worth retaining, which require revision, and how they can enter history without confusing living institutions, redeemed saints, true angels, impersonal soul-road offices, relic effects, and hostile counterfeits.

Nothing in this dossier is canon, named history, or a promoted seed. The review establishes a usable nursery and the rules by which later historical placement can occur.

The central result is that sacred frontier work must have **five distinct participants**:

1. living human orders that can train, fail, corrupt, reform, own property, and continue after their founders die;
2. living saints and ordinary faithful whose holiness does not make them supernatural species;
3. manifested saints, who are redeemed human souls acting under rare and bounded heavenly commission;
4. true angels, who are created spirits and may fight, guard, witness, or serve under the same commission;
5. impersonal route mechanisms, including the existing [[Soul-Hunters and Threshold Wardens]], which have no biography, cult, repentance, or personal discretion.

Hostile claimants imitate every category. Much of the story lies in determining what has actually appeared.

# I. Controlling Distinctions

## The name correction

`Threshold Warden` already names an `infrastructure-office` on the soul-road. A Riftwarden-like mortal organization must not use the same generic title.

The provisional family term for living institutions is therefore **breachward order**. Individual cultures may later call their members verge-keepers, sealed-road brethren, lantern surveyors, last-wall companies, watchers of the seam, or another locally grounded title. None of those people is a soul-road mechanism merely by holding an office.

## What a saintly military intervention may actually do

The setting must not sanitize saintly warfare into glowing moral support. A true saintly commission may:

- kill an active supernatural aggressor or human murderer when the commission authorizes armed defence;
- command true angels in a siege, withdrawal, breach-closing, prison rescue, or soul-road battle;
- terrify an attacking army, expose infiltrators, break supernatural formation, or hold a gate through which civilians escape;
- destroy an engine, false image, binding instrument, breachwork, or hostile commission-body;
- refuse to protect an army that tortures prisoners, murders civilians, or treats the saint as national property;
- appear somewhere politically disappointing, leaving soldiers to lose a battle while prisoners, refugees, the dying, or an enemy household are preserved.

The intervention can be bloody and decisive. It cannot make murder, rape, torture, pillage, forced conversion, vengeance, collective punishment, or lying righteous. It proves only the actual commission.

## What mortal orders must do without miracles

A breachward order exists because most crises receive no visible saint or angel. Its ordinary work has five lanes:

1. **recognition:** identify which layer presses through and distinguish breach from haunting, disease, fraud, Menhir law, Idol feeding, Patron anchor failure, Shell leakage, or damaged soul-route;
2. **containment:** establish barriers, watches, quarantine, access law, and force sufficient to stop immediate spread;
3. **evacuation:** move people, names, bodies, animals, records, provisions, and dangerous objects without turning rescue into confiscation;
4. **repair:** close the actual condition, pay or lawfully challenge valid obligations, restore Material structure, and release unlawful bindings;
5. **witness:** preserve what happened, including the order's own failure, so later authorities cannot turn the dead into propaganda.

Failure in any lane has bodily consequences. Misclassification can burn a district, trap souls, starve a refuge, provoke a Menhir court, strengthen an Idol feeder, or abandon people behind a seal.

# II. Source Review

## Received Orthodox patterns

The useful Orthodox material is not a single warrior-saint template.

- **Demetrios of Thessaloniki:** civic office, refusal to persecute, imprisonment, killing by soldiers, secret burial, refusal of relic removal, and later terrifying defence of a particular city. The earthly Life, relic history, military apparition, national appropriation, and iconographic reception remain distinct layers. Source: [OCA, Saint Demetrios](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2020/10/26/103059-holy-glorious-demetrios-the-myrrh-gusher-of-thessaloniki).
- **Mercurius of Caesarea:** a soldier aided by an angel, a victory involving real killing, repudiation of imperial honor, torture, beheading, and a received posthumous military intervention against an oppressor. Source: [OCA, Great Martyr Mercurius](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/1989/11/24/103383-great-martyr-mercurius-of-caesarea-in-cappadocia).
- **Nicholas of Myra:** intervention against immediate execution, confrontation with a bribed governor, aid to captives and sailors, famine relief, dream warning, and an immense later cult. Source: [OCA, Saint Nicholas](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2026/12/06/103484-saint-nicholas-the-wonderworker-archbishop-of-myra-in-lycia).
- **Anastasia the Deliverer:** prisoner visitation, medicine, ransom, domestic confinement, a massacre of prisoners, sexual threat, starvation, attempted drowning, burning, burial, relic translation, and later healing intercession. The complete received Life is severe and institutionally fertile. Source: [OCA, Great Martyr Anastasia](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2006/12/22/103609-great-martyr-anastasia-the-deliverer-from-poisons-her-teacher-ma).
- **Cyrus, John, Cosmas, Damian, and the unmercenary traditions:** medical skill, free treatment, imprisonment, torture, execution, multiple same-named saints, relic translation, and later healing. Source: [OCA, Cyrus and John](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2026/01/31/100380-holy-wonderworkers-and-unmercenaries-cyrus-and-john-and-those-wi), [OCA, Cosmas and Damian](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2012/11/01/103133-holy-wonderworkers-and-unmercenaries-cosmas-and-damian-of-mesopo).

The World Egg may borrow their historical and devotional grammars. It may not copy names, ethnicities, feasts, exact tortures, signature wonders, or complete plots.

## Pathfinder structural patterns

Riftwardens contribute the structure of learned planar-border specialists. *Wrath of the Righteous* contributes ward frontiers, corrupt guardians, refugees suspected of infiltration, crusader factions, failed campaigns, celestial support, overzealous purges, and institutions whose righteous purpose does not make every act righteous. Blackfire Adepts contribute the hostile mirror: specialists who open or exploit the boundaries others defend.

Sources: [Pathfinder Society: Riftwardens](https://store.paizo.com/pathfinder-society-scenario-5-07-port-godless/), [*Wrath of the Righteous* Player's Guide](https://downloads.paizo.com/WrathOfTheRighteous_PlayersGuide.pdf), [Worldwound aftermath](https://paizo.com/blog/march-2021-organized-play-scenario-previews).

Only structure transfers. The World Egg does not receive wardstones, the Worldwound, demons as a species, Iomedae, herald classes, Blackfire proper nouns, or an imported crusade chronology.

# IV. Reviewed Nursery Cards

## ST-R01R — The Breachward Orders

- **Scale:** institutional family rather than one international order.
- **Humble origin:** gravediggers, hospice staff, masons, ferrymen, midwives, former smugglers, confessors, scouts, translators, and survivors first develop practices because bishops and armies arrive too late.
- **Borrowed element:** Riftwarden boundary defence combined with Acts-like minor co-worker networks and Orthodox watchfulness.
- **Hard edge:** early failures kill people. One company seals the wrong house and leaves families to burn; another opens a quarantine corridor that allows a predator into a hospice; a third abandons workers beyond a closing threshold to save a city. Later rules are written in the names of the dead.
- **World Egg torque:** no universal ward seals all layers. Each team must diagnose the actual pressure and involve relevant local knowledge. A rite useful against an Astral route may strengthen an Idol architecture or insult a Menhir jurisdiction.
- **Institutional pressure:** rulers want the orders to certify mines, conquests, prisons, relics, and roads as safe. Churches want doctrinal obedience. Merchants want predictable passage. Frontier communities want survival without annexation.
- **1360 AR trace:** rival manuals preserve mutually incompatible emergency rules because they arose from different layers; old failure rolls are read before dangerous closures; forged breachward warrants are a profitable trade.
- **Open:** number, names, origins, confessional distribution, relation to existing military orders, and which historical disasters produced the principal rules.
- **Restriction:** a breachward member is not a manifested saint, true angel, psychopomp, or infrastructure-office.

## ST-R02R — The Black-Ember Schools

- **Scale:** rival techniques, cells, teachers, and markets rather than one dark church.
- **Humble origin:** failed surveyors, expelled warlocks, ransom brokers, desperate refugees, military engineers, and sincere researchers who decided a controlled breach was preferable to political helplessness.
- **Borrowed element:** Blackfire Adept counter-specialist and Sinner counter-pastoral structure.
- **Hard edge:** schools torture captured wardens for route knowledge, expose prisoners and refugees to openings, sell escape passages that close on the poor, traffic in names, and leave settlements to absorb experimental failure. Some students begin with rescue and become executioners by degrees.
- **World Egg torque:** different schools exploit different continuities—Patron rent, Shell pressure, Idol appetite, Hell contracts, bound spirits, or Abyssal wounds. They do not share one power source or cosmology.
- **Political complication:** governments secretly retain the same specialists they publicly burn. Breachward orders sometimes buy their expertise during catastrophes, creating compromised rescues and later purges.
- **1360 AR trace:** censored manuals, branded survivors, laboratories beneath official seal houses, former adepts inside legitimate orders, and towns whose geometry records an experiment everyone denies.
- **Open:** every school name, lineage, sponsor, doctrine, relationship to the Adversary, and which can repent or be dismantled.
- **Restriction:** no population, magical profession, or research tradition is inherently Sinner-aligned.

## ST-R03 — The Saint on the Walls

- **Scale:** one city, one received saint, several centuries of appropriation.
- **Humble origin:** the earthly person begins as a civic officer, soldier, laborer, teacher, prisoner, or servant whose local duty becomes inseparable from the city's memory.
- **Borrowed element:** Demetrios's martyrdom, city patronage, relic refusal, military apparition, and later national appropriation.
- **Hard edge:** the founding history includes imprisonment, execution, attempted corpse denial, siege, starvation, killing on the walls, and civilians caught between defenders and attackers. A later manifestation may kill or terrify active aggressors. It does not erase atrocities committed by the defenders.
- **World Egg torque:** the saint appears because the Lord commissions defence of people and a particular passage, not because the city owns the saint. In a later siege the saint rebukes or obstructs the city's soldiers for murdering prisoners, refugees, or a resident minority.
- **Political use:** rulers make the saint civic heraldry; enemies claim the apparition proves the city itself demonic; rival districts dispute relic custody and the meaning of survival.
- **1360 AR trace:** a wall circuit incorporated into the feast; a relic that will not cross one gate; soldiers' songs that omit the saint's rebuke; enemy descendants who preserve a different apparition account.
- **Open:** city, saint, historical siege, authentic manifestations, casualties, relic, and cult layers.
- **Restriction:** defence of a city never proves all its wars, rulers, borders, or later claims righteous.

## ST-R04 — The Human Captain of the Host

- **Scale:** one possible greater saint and one age-altering cross-layer campaign.
- **Humble origin:** the earthly person may have been a low-ranking soldier, translator, physician, prisoner, widow, ferryman, monastic cook, or frightened survivor rather than a famous general.
- **Borrowed element:** Mercurius standing in relation to an angel; Orthodox military intercession; World Egg greater-saint authority; crusader-front grammar.
- **Hard edge:** the commissioned campaign is open warfare. Hostile bodies are broken, breach engines destroyed, captives injured during rescue, mortal allies killed, and settlements lost where evacuation fails. The saint may order angels to abandon a tactically useful position to protect people commanders considered expendable.
- **World Egg torque:** the saint commands true angels only inside the stated commission. The angels obey without becoming the saint's personal following. When the charge ends, the host withdraws even if mortal rulers demand conquest of the enemy's remaining territory.
- **Scandal:** mortal clergy distrust the saint's earthly culture, sex, former allegiance, disputed Life, or lack of recognized military rank. Others build a permanent militant cult out of a temporary command.
- **1360 AR trace:** contradictory campaign maps, one object retaining a narrow response, veterans' descendants, a hostile archive admitting the saint saved them, and an order claiming succession to authority it never possessed.
- **Open:** all biography, campaign, opposing power, angelic choirs, object, and historical placement.
- **Restriction:** only one such candidate should be developed in the next pass; it cannot become a standing celestial army or a second Phoenix event.

## ST-R05 — The Saint Who Guards the Wrong Gate

- **Scale:** recurring story grammar capable of several local recensions.
- **Humble origin:** the saint's earthly fame may rest on care for a road, prison, hospice, enemy dead, translators, children, or ordinary gates rather than battlefield command.
- **Borrowed element:** bounded intercession and the Gospel reversal of prestigious expectation.
- **Hard edge:** the army asking for aid may lose its battle. Soldiers die, a ruler is captured, or a fortress falls while the saint protects an evacuation road, enemy hospital, prison, burial party, or damaged soul-route. The loss is not proof that every defender was wicked; it is proof only that the saint was not commissioned to guarantee victory.
- **World Egg torque:** the saint's apparent refusal exposes the false assumption that patronage means ownership. A counterfeit may meanwhile appear at headquarters and offer the desired victory through atrocity.
- **1360 AR trace:** two feast traditions—one celebrating an alleged military betrayal and one preserving the rescued people; an unheroic gate; a commander's curse in the chronicles; a refugee hymn later adopted by both sides.
- **Open:** whether one or several saints acquire this folklore, which intervention was genuine, and whether the battlefield apparition was counterfeit.
- **Restriction:** do not make political disappointment automatic proof of sanctity; evidence and fruits still matter.

## ST-R06 — The Hospital Without Fees

- **Scale:** hospital, healing order, charitable estate, and disputed saintly reception.
- **Humble origin:** physicians, herbalists, laundresses, grave workers, cooks, translators, donors, former patients, and attendants build the institution before any miracle is claimed.
- **Borrowed element:** unmercenary physicians and prisoner-healers.
- **Hard edge:** patients die under triage; untreated poor people are turned away after later administrators impose fees; prisoners arrive tortured; infectious bodies endanger staff; corrupt custodians sell medicine and relic access. A miracle does not restore everyone or absolve preventable deaths.
- **World Egg torque:** a manifested healer may stabilize one ward, expose adulterated medicine, or appear among abandoned patients while remaining silent in the wealthy chapel. The wonder imposes practical work rather than replacing it.
- **Institutional conflict:** genuine costs exist, so the moral problem is not solved by declaring all payment evil. The dispute concerns refusal of care, profiteering, hidden reserves, coerced labor, false relic treatment, and who bears unavoidable scarcity.
- **1360 AR trace:** two account books, a free ward hidden behind the saint's prestigious foundation, competing relics of same-named healers, and a feast at which physicians publicly name patients lost through institutional fault.
- **Open:** founders, medicine, miracle, plague or war context, church recognition, and current ownership.
- **Restriction:** a saint's healing never proves a diagnosis, medical school, drug, hospital board, or relic market universally correct.

## ST-R07 — The Sword Stopped at the Block

- **Scale:** public justice cycle, legal reform, and later abuse of precedent.
- **Humble origin:** the endangered people are prisoners without prestige; the crucial witnesses may be a guard, clerk, servant, child, condemned accomplice, or person previously ignored by the court.
- **Borrowed element:** Nicholas stopping an execution and exposing a corrupted governor; Susanna's separated testimony as a related juridical grammar.
- **Hard edge:** the execution is real and imminent. Earlier prisoners may already have been killed. The accused can be tortured, sexually coerced, dispossessed, or condemned through bribed testimony. When the saint or living holy figure seizes the weapon, guards must choose whether to obey an unlawful sentence.
- **World Egg torque:** a genuine manifestation exposes one specific murder and may compel release, testimony, or preservation of evidence. Later rulers falsely argue that every execution not visibly interrupted has heavenly approval.
- **1360 AR trace:** a ceremonial blunt sword, a sealed governor's confession, a legal right to demand separated testimony, families of people whom the same procedure failed, and counterfeit intervention sold to wealthy defendants.
- **Open:** whether the initial figure acts during earthly life or posthumously, exact crime, judgement, punishment, and institutional reform.
- **Restriction:** miraculous interruption neither abolishes courts nor makes ordeal a reliable legal test.

## ST-R08R — The Prison-Road Commission

- **Scale:** one custody network or breached soul-route, not a conquest of Hell.
- **Humble origin:** prisoners, ransom collectors, medical visitors, cooks, correspondents, guards, smugglers, and burial workers create the earthly road later associated with the saint.
- **Borrowed element:** Nicholas and Anastasia as captive helpers; prison-martyr networks; threshold-saint service.
- **Hard edge:** captives are beaten, starved, poisoned, sexually threatened, tortured, executed, or murdered before rescue arrives. Some guards and prisoners commit grave crimes. Their guilt does not permit torture, soulbinding, disappearance of names, or denial of judgement.
- **World Egg torque:** a manifested saint may enter an infernal custody work or Material prison, preserve names, bar a soul predator, and open one viable route. Mortals must still unlock cells, carry the wounded, defeat lawful-seeming instruments, and decide how living perpetrators face justice.
- **Moral complication:** release from unlawful custody is not acquittal, resurrection, salvation by jailbreak, or escape from the Lord's judgement. The saint can defend the personhood of someone whose earthly crimes remain severe.
- **1360 AR trace:** coded prison letters, ransom ledgers, a route prayer, one gate that opens only when every prisoner's name is spoken, and rival cults claiming the saint as either abolitionist or patron of state prisons.
- **Open:** prison, captives, saint, true manifestation, relation to Hell, and outcome.
- **Restriction:** no saint annexes an infernal dominion, empties Hell, commands the Boatman, or releases judged souls into the Material Realm.

## ST-R09 — The Saint Who Refused Translation

- **Scale:** relic custody dispute with local, imperial, economic, and devotional layers.
- **Humble origin:** the grave survives through anonymous caretakers, threatened minorities, sextons, women maintaining lamps, or workers who conceal the body from persecutors.
- **Borrowed element:** Demetrios's refusal of relic removal and wider Orthodox translation traditions.
- **Hard edge:** the earthly history may include execution and denied burial. Later translation attempts involve grave opening, bribery, theft, soldiers, riots, and competing claims of safety. Violence is not mandatory unless the selected historical recension requires it, but institutional coercion must not disappear.
- **World Egg torque:** an object, grave, or manifestation can prevent one transfer without declaring every local custodian innocent. The saint does not inhabit the relic as a divisible soul and cannot be immobilized by its capture.
- **Political complication:** a threatened city may genuinely need the relic removed; an emperor may seek legitimacy; poor custodians may depend on pilgrimage; descendants may demand return; another church may possess a genuine object belonging to the same saint.
- **1360 AR trace:** an unfinished reliquary, a procession that returns to its starting place, fragments falsely sold as the refused relic, and a legal category distinguishing refusal from proof of title.
- **Open:** saint, remains, attempted translations, genuine responses, custodians, and remedy.
- **Restriction:** no relic response proves ethnicity, border, dynasty, church exclusivity, or the saint's consent to every local practice.

## ST-R10 — The Founder Who Corrected the Order

- **Scale:** one historical order and the crisis that reveals divergence from its founding charge.
- **Humble origin:** the founder begins among prisoners, patients, refugees, translators, gravediggers, maimed veterans, widows, or low-status frontier workers rather than receiving an institution fully formed.
- **Borrowed element:** saints correcting communities, failed crusader institutions, and Revelation's letters of judgement to churches.
- **Hard edge:** the later order has tortured suspects, seized property, imposed forced vows, experimented at breaches, abandoned poor patients, or cultivated ethnic hatred. People are dead or permanently injured. The correction cannot restore them or compel survivors to forgive.
- **World Egg torque:** the saint manifests to witnesses the leadership cannot easily erase, names specific crimes, protects records or victims, and withdraws. The saint does not personally rewrite charters, appoint a grandmaster, or make every reformer trustworthy.
- **Counterfeit pressure:** leaders call the apparition an Idol or Patron counterfeit; a real counterfeit then appears offering institutional survival through one cosmetic confession.
- **1360 AR trace:** two versions of the rule, erased victim names returning in liturgy, a broken chapter seal, a disputed deposition, and unauthorised companies claiming the founder actually blessed their violence.
- **Open:** order, founder, offence, manifestation, reform, schism, reparations, and present inheritance.
- **Restriction:** do not attach this automatically to the Long Watch, Blessed Watch, Lantern, Iron Martyrs, or another existing order without its dedicated review.

## ST-R11R — The Penitent Commander and the Unforgotten Roll

- **Scale:** one earthly biography and its contested reception, distinct from B3-10's penitent governor.
- **Humble origin:** the person rises from ordinary military, bandit, captive, slave-soldier, household, or frontier service rather than beginning as sovereign.
- **Borrowed element:** Manasseh's repentance without erased consequence, Pauline persecutor grammar, converted-bandit traditions, and severe military Lives.
- **Hard edge:** before repentance the commander ordered or personally committed killings, torture, hostage-taking, sacrilege, enslavement, or betrayal. The victims are named. No supernatural compulsion absorbs the commander's guilt.
- **World Egg torque:** repentance produces confession, surrender of office, release of captives, restitution, dangerous testimony, acceptance of punishment, and service without promised rehabilitation. If the person is later genuinely manifested, the saint restores victim names when a war cult celebrates only victory.
- **Conflict:** victims and descendants may reject reconciliation, dispute glorification, or accept restitution without venerating the penitent. A genuine saintly manifestation does not make their grief sinful.
- **1360 AR trace:** a victim roll omitted from the official Life, confiscated estates returned imperfectly, a feast divided between penitence and triumph, and soldiers who invoke only the pre-repentance commander.
- **Open:** crimes, victims, repentance, temporal mercy, sanctity, judgement, institutional recognition, and historical era.
- **Restriction:** never duplicate the Penitent Governor's purification-policy crimes or use later sainthood to excuse earthly culpability.

## ST-R12 — The Common Patron of Divided Cities

- **Scale:** two communions or successor cities contesting one pre-division saint.
- **Humble origin:** the earthly saint belongs to a shared older world and may have served travelers, laborers, prisoners, sailors, children, or a local disaster before becoming confessional heraldry.
- **Borrowed element:** shared Orthodox saints, contested relic reception, Acts 15, and the World Egg Great Split.
- **Hard edge:** shrine seizure, beatings, imprisonment, corpse or relic desecration, mob killing, punitive law, and expulsion lie beneath the dispute. Neither church's genuine case grants collective innocence.
- **World Egg torque:** Envy Masks, Patron personas, Sinner polemicists, residue, and forged apparitions offer partisan miracles. The true saint may protect people in both cities, refuse military summons, command angels against a common breach, and condemn different sins without declaring the institutional division imaginary.
- **1360 AR trace:** two icons preserving one unforgeable correction, rival relics that may both be genuine but belong to same-named people, a road feast observed on different dates, and hostile archives that each omit one protected household.
- **Open:** saint, communities, relics, manifestations, counterfeit claimants, and possibility of documentary reconciliation.
- **Restriction:** the saint cannot become a neutral third church, resolve the Kindling Question, explain the Bells of the Split, or be divided into two souls.

## Existing institutions that may inherit parts of the ecology

- The **Order of the Blessed Watch** already protects pilgrims, plague cemeteries, hospitals, and archives. It is a natural present-day bearer of evacuation and witness practices, but should not become the universal breachward order.
- The **Order of the Lantern** already supplies investigators, linguists, teachers, and field theologians. It fits recognition and classification but lacks automatic authority to contain or fight every breach.
- The **Inquisitors of Saint Malveris** provide the sharpest institutional-corruption interface because independent prisons and relic investigations already require oversight.
