# First Menhir Constellation — Historical Placement Matrix

## Purpose and Status

This pass gives six reviewed nurseries from [[Menhir Courts, Jurisdictions, and High Hunts Review]] provisional historical soil. It places one episode in each of six chronological and geographic apertures without promoting the episodes into canon or [[The Historical Seed Garden]].

The six are not chapters of one Faewild war, members of one court system, expressions of one hidden fairy nation or successive appearances of the same rulers. Similar thresholds, bargains and seasonal consequences recur because the same Menhir laws can become legible in unrelated places. Repetition never establishes shared identity.

The controlling rule remains:

> A Menhir court is a local ecology made legible through custom. Menhir law can make a consequence exact without making it just.

## Controlling Continuity Rulings

- Each named Menhir remains one intelligent, morally blind `yolk-sovereignty`. No local king, rider, mine voice, mountain or tool is a detachable piece or incarnation of that sovereignty.
- Sapient court persons and mature place-minds use `court-law` continuity and possess moral agency appropriate to their established capacities. Menhir pressure does not excuse their chosen abduction, enslavement, murder, betrayal or sacrifice.
- Reflexive Stone-Voices, route responses and other nonpersonal law reactions are not morally culpable actors merely because humans hear language or intention in them.
- Human participants, including fosterlings, captives, transformed travelers and remote Orphaned peoples, possess complete Image-bearing `human-soul` continuity. No crossing, service, bodily alteration or court title divides or replaces the soul.
- Mythic fauna retain their established biological or catalogued continuities. Hunt Hounds are animal agents; Hunt Riders capable of moral choice remain responsible for what they choose.
- A role, title, mask, repeated appearance, relic, tool, route and cultural name never establish personal continuity without separate evidence.
- No bargain grants moral title, judgement over souls, divine command, sainthood, forgiveness or rightful kingship.

# II. Six Historical Placements

## MN-P01 — The Court That Lost the Surface

### Historical placement and human aperture

- **Source nursery:** MN-R02.
- **Era:** late Chains and Great Unbinding, provisionally c. 2320–2260 BR.
- **Region:** one secondary Giant extraction estate in country later divided between Brauvalis and the Green March. This does not make either later culture the unchanged continuation of the estate.
- **Human aperture:** enslaved quarry hands, cellar workers, water carriers, cattle keepers and local households whose survivors later enter Orphaned reassembly live between Giant administration and a displaced court's remaining routes.
- **Scale:** one conquered surface settlement and its surviving threshold ecology, not a subterranean kingdom or general account of Giant relations with the Faewild.

### Received source and transformation

- **Received source:** Irish supernatural-polity and *síd* settlement grammar, especially traditions in which defeated powers lose open possession of land while retaining dangerous local addresses and negotiated relations.
- **Borrowed element:** dispossession without disappearance; continued access through hills, wells and thresholds; fosterage and toll relations after political defeat.
- **Retained hard edge:** seizure of inhabited land, killing during conquest, disappearance at thresholds, coerced service, cattle loss and deaths during attempted rescue.
- **Changed axes:** Giant extraction replaces the received conquest; an enslaved human population stands between both claimants; the surviving power is route control rather than a hidden national territory; later coexistence is archaeological rather than a completed settlement; neither side possesses divine genealogy or moral title.

### Court anatomy and mechanism

| Layer | Classification | Function in the episode |
|---|---|---|
| Hecatara | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | governs adjacency, thresholds and displaced passage |
| Sylvorn | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | preserves territorial memory, rooted succession and the site's resistance to being made interchangeable |
| displaced rulers and toll collectors | sapient `court-law` persons | retain and police surviving routes; choose captives and reprisals |
| old well and cellar responses | place-minds or reflexive `court-law` responses, classified individually | open when season, water level and remembered use align |
| cattle and route fauna | `biological-pattern` | follow surviving adjacency without recognizing human deeds |
| enslaved and Orphaned humans | complete `human-soul` | bear the bodily and intergenerational cost |
| toll stones and deeds | objects | record different kinds of access; neither contains a person |

The Giant estate does not conquer a physical underground country. Its overseers quarry the court's surface mound, line the well, build storehouses over old entrances and use forced labor to break the visible settlement. The Material structures fall. The adjacency sustained by repeated water, root and threshold conditions remains. At particular seasons, an ordinary cellar or well becomes next to the displaced court even though excavation finds only earth and stone.

### Bargain terms

- **Gift:** access through one old well-road, recovery of lost cattle, warning when a threshold is about to open and limited fosterage knowledge carried between households.
- **Price:** a declared toll, seasonal maintenance of the aperture and bounded service by a consenting named person.
- **Boundary:** particular wells, cellars and root lines when water, season and remembered use coincide.
- **Failure:** an unpaid crossing displaces the traveler into the court route; a falsely offered substitute is retained; a collapsed entrance redirects adjacency into another occupied cellar; rescuers who break the route strand captives and may bring the collapsing threshold down on both communities.

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** Giant clients need stone, water control and storage from an inhabited court-site. → **Decision:** they destroy the surface dwellings and use enslaved humans to seal the entrances. → **Mechanism:** the Material buildings are conquered but the Hecatara–Sylvorn adjacency survives through wells, roots and repeated threshold use. → **Effect:** the displaced court regains leverage through tolls, disappearances and coerced fosterage. → **Cost:** humans are crushed in excavation, taken into service, killed as alleged collaborators and lost in failed rescues; court inhabitants are killed or displaced as well. → **Consequence:** later surface communities possess the land while living under route claims neither their deeds nor the court's memories can morally settle.

### Surviving evidence and 1360 interpretations

Independent carriers include:

1. **Architecture:** sealed cellar doors which open only during one water-and-root conjunction, despite ending in ordinary masonry at every other time.
2. **Custom and genealogy:** two incompatible fosterage genealogies naming the same households as rescuers, collaborators or stolen kin.
3. **Objects:** court toll stones found in Giant drainage works far from any surviving entrance.
4. **Bodies and landscape:** excavation deaths beneath the estate wall and later rescue burials on both sides of the former surface boundary.
5. **Documents:** a materially accurate surface deed which records buildings and fields but cannot describe the surviving route below them.

In 1360, Green March custodians may call the doors ancestral obligations; Brauvalian antiquarians may call them Giant drainage rituals; local courts may treat later inhabitants as tenants; Church investigators may accept a real bounded ecology while rejecting the court's title claim. No single carrier proves which present court person, if any, descends continuously from a displaced ruler.

### Known, open and bounded

- **Authorially fixed for the nursery:** Giant clients destroyed and occupied the surface site; the court retained bounded route power; humans were coerced and killed; later possession does not settle moral title.
- **Open:** estate, court rulers, exact captives, surviving fosterlings, present apertures, possibility of coexistence and relation to later local families.
- **Identity boundary:** not [[The Court Beneath the Roots]], a Pale-Skin polity, [[The Country Beneath the Rains - First Subterranean Region Chassis|the Country Beneath the Rains]] or a hidden Folk nation.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`. Historical placement is accepted; Garden promotion remains locked.

## MN-P02R — The High Hunt of the Withheld Season

### Historical placement and human aperture

- **Source nursery:** MN-R10R.
- **Era:** late Scattering into early Hearths, provisionally c. 1850–1725 BR.
- **Region:** the cold watershed between the later Tidewood and Gale-Crown regions, north of the later Ättar halls.
- **Culture:** related ancestral populations later remembered within Folk and Ättar traditions, but already divided among independent halls and winter routes. The Hunt neither creates nor explains their later divergence.
- **Human aperture:** corpse washers, seed keepers, stable hands, winter route wardens and households whose survival depends upon stores held one season too long.

### Received source and transformation

- **Received source:** Norwegian Oskoreia or Åsgårdsreia traditions as the named regional Wild-Hunt recension: a noisy and dangerous midwinter host threatening farms and travelers. The source attribution follows [Store norske leksikon](https://snl.no/%C3%A5sg%C3%A5rdsrei), which also distinguishes the Norwegian ride from broader Wild Hunt syntheses.
- **Borrowed element:** a terrifying winter procession, farm intrusion, seasonal exposure and protective threshold customs.
- **Retained hard edge:** abduction, seizure of household necessities, bodily danger to people and animals and death for those caught in or resisting the ride.
- **Excluded source identities:** the riders are not unsettled human souls, Asgardian gods or the historical source's named leader. No soul is trapped between Heaven and Hell.
- **World Egg additions:** witnessed declaration, route and quarry-sign come from established High Hunt law; reproductive bodies, corpses, seed and stored food are physical seasonal claims, never unborn souls or future persons.

### Hunt anatomy and mechanism

| Layer | Classification | Function in the episode |
|---|---|---|
| Nevaura | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | preservation, winter scarcity, ration and dormancy |
| Morvalis | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | harvest completion, bodily death, decay and material release |
| local Hecatara-aligned routes | place-minds and bounded route offices | carry the Hunt without making Hecatara a permanent party to the phase-lock |
| declaring riders | sapient `court-law` persons or bounded Hunt offices, classified individually | interpret the declaration and choose whether to substitute easier victims |
| Hunt Hounds | biological agents | follow the quarry-sign and do not judge guilt |
| human quarry and opponents | complete `human-soul` | remain persons regardless of pregnancy, death customs or court classification |
| corpses, eggs and seed | embodied material continuities | are never souls, future persons or court citizens merely because the declaration counts them |

A catastrophic winter leads several halls and a preservation court to hold corpses unburied, seed ungerminated and food outside the ordinary release term. The practice initially saves lives. Court rulers then preserve their authority by refusing every later thaw and substituting neighboring stores for their own.

The Nevaura–Morvalis contradiction enters a temporary phase-lock. A witnessed declaration specifies what must leave the withheld condition. Quarry-sign appears on bodies and stores carried across the implicated routes. Riders and Hounds can follow the sign only while moving along those routes and only until the declared release is satisfied or the phase-lock breaks. This is one Hunt, not a permanent host.

### Bargain terms

- **Gift:** winter preservation of seed, food, vulnerable bodies and one hidden passage between halls.
- **Price:** release of the named stores, burial of the dead and reopening of the ordinary seasonal route once the danger ends.
- **Boundary:** one winter watershed, its connected barns, corpse houses and old passes during the declared phase-lock.
- **Failure:** preservation becomes non-release; seed remains alive but cannot germinate; corpses cannot enter decay; stores cease to nourish; the Hunt carries marked material and bodies away until the withheld term is discharged.

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** a real famine winter makes extraordinary preservation necessary. → **Decision:** hall and court rulers continue withholding bodies and stores after the bounded danger, protecting their own authority and favored households. → **Mechanism:** Nevaura's preservation and Morvalis's required completion phase-lock across existing routes; a declaration and quarry-sign form one temporary High Hunt. → **Effect:** Riders invade farms, tear bodies from mourners, seize seed and food, take living people and hunt pregnant animals whose embodied continuities fall within the declaration. → **Cost:** people starve, captives disappear, route closers are killed and breaking the preservation releases spoiled food, disease and armed enemies preserved through the same winter. → **Consequence:** later communities maintain release customs which save them from recurrence while disagreeing whether their ancestors ended a curse, paid a lawful debt or surrendered neighbors to save themselves.

### Surviving evidence and 1360 interpretations

Independent carriers include:

1. **Custom:** midwinter lintel marks placed on barns, corpse houses and seed stores to declare what has already been released rather than to repel all supernatural beings.
2. **Textile and body practice:** one quarry-sign repeated on burial cloth and on the harness of animals excluded from winter breeding.
3. **Records:** seasonal missing-person rolls which distinguish people taken before and after the declaration was corrected.
4. **Material test:** sealed seed from the implicated stores attracts Hunt Hounds only when carried across one surviving route during the corresponding winter phase.
5. **Hostile memory:** a Rider's face-board commemorates a court person who refused an unlawful human substitution and was killed by the other riders.

Folk accounts praise the hall that opened its stores; Ättar-related songs accuse that hall of diverting the Hunt; court witnesses insist the declaration was exact; Church commentators distinguish the real natural consequence from every later moral defence. None establishes that the old rulers acted justly.

### Known, open and bounded

- **Authorially fixed for the nursery:** one temporary Hunt occurred; preservation had once been necessary; rulers culpably prolonged it; embodied people, animals, corpses, seed and food were seized; no human soul was quarry as a soul.
- **Open:** halls, exact winter, declaration, riders, number and identity of victims, refused substitution, final release and which later custom is closest to the operative rule.
- **Identity boundary:** not [[Great-Power Mythic Ecology and Planting Matrix#GP-I-03 — The Hunt of the Escaped Courts|the Hunt of the Escaped Courts]], not the whole War of Unfinished Seasons, not Mother Moon's bargain and not a permanent Wild Hunt.
- **Review disposition:** `revise`. The regional Oskoreia attribution, Nevaura–Morvalis phase-lock and exclusion of dead-soul identity are incorporated. Garden promotion remains locked.

## MN-P03R — The Raid That Returned One Tool

### Historical placement and human aperture

- **Source nursery:** MN-R03R.
- **Era:** Age of City-States, provisionally c. 1120–980 BR.
- **Region:** a pre-imperial island corridor between the later Arkenan and Saronikan maritime worlds.
- **Culture:** one early Atherian sea-king's mixed warband, local island pilots, captives and an independent court stronghold. This is not a signature company or founding expedition for all Atherian navigation.
- **Human aperture:** hungry oarsmen, coerced pilots, ship carpenters, prisoners promised freedom and island households endangered by a blockade.

### Received source and transformation

- **Received source:** Welsh *Preiddeu Annwfn* and its guarded Otherworld-fortress grammar.
- **Borrowed element:** armed approach to a populated otherworld stronghold, a real guarded spoil, catastrophic losses and a diminished return.
- **Retained hard edge:** culpable theft, killing of defenders, drowning, capture, transformation and the fact that most raiders do not return.
- **Changed axes:** the prize is a bounded sounding-weight rather than a cauldron; the motive is a contested reef corridor during blockade; the expedition has no source number or roster; the stronghold's people are neither damned nor gods; the later consequence is maritime law rather than heroic sovereignty.

### Court anatomy and mechanism

| Layer | Classification | Function in the episode |
|---|---|---|
| Hecatara | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | creates the adjacency by which one reef passage can be approached from the correct side |
| Thalassor | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | governs current, coast and the custody of what the route carries |
| stronghold rulers, defenders and artisans | sapient `court-law` persons | guard the tool, negotiate, fight and remain responsible for treatment of prisoners |
| reef and gate minds | place-minds or bounded route organisms | open or close according to current, approach and sounding sequence |
| sounding-weight | nonpersonal court tool | reveals and stabilizes one safe adjacency; it neither commands a Menhir nor grants generic sea control |
| sea-king, pilots and raiders | complete `human-soul` | remain culpable people even when coerced, transformed or celebrated |

The sounding-weight records more than depth. When lowered at the correct current and from the correct approach, it identifies which stretch of Material water is temporarily adjacent to the safe court passage through the reef. The stronghold uses it to regulate one route and protect its population. Removing it does not kill the court, but it makes the safe approach unstable and exposes defenders, captives and neighboring islanders to wreck.

### Bargain terms

- **Gift:** one safe passage through a lethal reef corridor and warning when the adjacency is false.
- **Price:** declared custody, a bounded harbor toll and return of the weight after one passage.
- **Boundary:** one reef system, sounding sequence and current phase.
- **Failure:** using the tool from the wrong side exchanges arrival and departure; refusing return closes the retreat route; copying its marks produces ordinary weights which confidently indicate the wrong passage.

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** blockade and failed harvest threaten an island alliance dependent upon the reef corridor. → **Decision:** its sea-king demands permanent custody of the court's sounding-weight and launches a raid when refused. → **Mechanism:** coerced pilots breach the stronghold during a brief Hecatara–Thalassor alignment and the raiders steal the functioning tool. → **Effect:** one ship returns through the safe adjacency while the court closes every other route. → **Cost:** defenders are killed in their own stronghold; most raiders drown, are captured or return bodily altered; abandoned ships and prisoners remain on the far side; later safe voyages are purchased with the stronghold's continuing loss. → **Consequence:** the rescued harbor calls the theft civilization's victory, while surviving custody marks and prisoner descendants preserve a materially different history.

### Surviving evidence and 1360 interpretations

Independent carriers include:

1. **Object:** one disputed sounding-weight with nonhuman wear, court custody marks and a bounded response to the original current sequence.
2. **Maritime archaeology:** wrecks lying along routes which no ordinary ship could have followed from their documented departure points.
3. **Textual transmission:** victory songs whose returning roster becomes smaller in the oldest copies while later recensions invent glorious survivors.
4. **Human descent:** families on the court side preserving human burial and food practices associated with prisoners the songs declare drowned.
5. **Defender evidence:** repair marks within the stronghold identify people killed during the breach rather than demons defeated in an empty treasure house.

Thalmyric antiquarians may call the tool the beginning of imperial navigation; islanders may call it stolen emergency equipment; court claimants may demand restitution; skeptics may accept the wreck pattern while denying present authenticity. The tool proves only its own bounded response.

### Known, open and bounded

- **Authorially fixed for the nursery:** a populated stronghold was raided; the useful sounding-weight was stolen; defenders died; most raiders did not return; the raid's benefit does not make it righteous.
- **Open:** sea-king, port, stronghold, negotiations, participants, exact casualties, prisoners, present tool and whether any restitution could safely occur.
- **Identity boundary:** not Seven Who Took the Wrong Road, [[The Historical Seed Garden#Scattering — The Harbor Founded Twice|the Harbor Founded Twice]], a mobile Patron city or the Revolving Fortress.
- **Review disposition:** `revise`. The guarded prize, function, region and loss mechanism are fixed for placement while every person and exact site remains provisional. Garden promotion remains locked.

## MN-P04R — The Elfland Tithe

### Historical placement and human aperture

- **Source nursery:** MN-R05.
- **Era:** late Underground into the beginning of Legalization, provisionally c. 230–310 AR.
- **Region:** one western Kedravan fishing coast and winter harbor before Saltborn ethnogenesis.
- **Culture:** Atherian-Kedravan provincial households, older Orphaned coast families, prisoners, itinerant believers and two independent court households. The event does not make later Saltborn culture secretly court-descended.
- **Human aperture:** fishers, prison boatmen, fostered children, route-watchers and relatives attempting to recover people taken into service.

### Received source and transformation

- **Received source:** Scottish Elfland and tithe-ballad grammar, especially abduction, transformation, threatened payment and dangerous rescue.
- **Borrowed element:** one supernatural household owes recurring embodied service and substitutes an abducted mortal; a courageous rescue can recover one person without ending the larger obligation.
- **Retained hard edge:** abduction, coerced labor, family separation, bodily transformation, time loss, threatened substitution and death during rescue.
- **Changed axes:** the creditor is a rival court rather than Hell; the debt maintains one dangerous winter crossing; prisoners and fishing households replace the received lovers and queen; Conversion propaganda distorts rather than resolves the event; no court owns or transfers souls.

### Two-court anatomy and mechanism

| Layer | Classification | Function in the episode |
|---|---|---|
| Hecatara | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | governs the seasonal route and the displaced distance of the maintained crossing |
| Thalassor | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | governs current, coast, custody and the physical work required to keep the road traversable |
| shore-threshold rulers | sapient `court-law` persons | owe the quota and culpably select substitutes from humans and dependants |
| offshore winter-road court | independent sapient court household and route ecology | receives the agreed workers; it is not Hell, a Patron realm or the Court of Salt and Teeth |
| rower and watch offices | bounded `court-law` roles | can be performed by embodied persons but do not absorb or replace them |
| humans in service | complete `human-soul` | remain human persons through crossing, time loss and bodily alteration |
| service tokens | nonpersonal tally objects | count completed embodied watches but cannot identify, hold or judge a soul |

An earlier rescue bargain keeps one offshore winter road open when ordinary coastal water becomes impassable. The creditor court maintains the route through embodied rowing, warning watches and the carrying of ballast between two seasonal adjacencies. The shore court owes a stated number of completed watches. Its rulers gradually hide their own dependants and take fosterlings, fishers and prisoners whom the agreement's crude equivalence accepts as replacements.

Late Underground officials discover that people can be removed without a corpse, trial record or ordinary transport. Some hand religious prisoners and inconvenient debtors to the court. After Legalization, victorious polemicists reverse the record: every missing believer becomes an authenticated martyr and every nonbeliever becomes either a willing pagan servant or an invention. Both claims erase victims.

### Bargain terms

- **Gift:** one maintained winter crossing, warning of inward-turning currents and limited rescue of vessels caught between coastal routes.
- **Price:** a fixed number of embodied rowing and route-watch terms performed at the creditor court.
- **Boundary:** one western coast, one offshore road and its winter opening.
- **Failure:** an incomplete quota closes the crossing from the Material side; a rescued servant leaves an unfilled term; a false tally opens the route inward and wrecks vessels against a coast they cannot reach from ordinary water.

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** an older emergency compact preserves a winter sea route. → **Decision:** shore-court rulers protect their own household by substituting abducted humans, while provincial officials exploit the route to disappear prisoners. → **Mechanism:** the creditor's service tokens count embodied watches without judging consent or identity. → **Effect:** fishers, fosterlings, believers and debtors vanish into rowing and watch service; time and bodily adaptation make return increasingly difficult. → **Cost:** families are separated, captives die at work or during rescue, and recovering one person condemns another when the quota is left intact. → **Consequence:** later martyr catalogues, court ballads and provincial denials preserve mutually useful fragments while the winter road continues to demand the original work.

### Surviving evidence and 1360 interpretations

Independent carriers include:

1. **Custom:** certain western boats ship one deliberately empty rowing place and refuse to name a substitute while crossing the winter current.
2. **Objects:** service tokens show completed watches but bear marks from court dependants, provincial prisoners and free fishers whom later records separate morally.
3. **Song:** a rescue ballad ends with one captive restored; an older refrain records a second household losing someone on the following tide.
4. **Architecture:** a ruined provincial holding room opens toward the offshore road under the original winter conditions, proving official access without identifying every victim.
5. **Bodies and craft:** returned tools and one human burial show long-term cold-water adaptation inconsistent with the brief disappearances recorded on shore.

Old-Church local tradition may preserve genuine murdered believers without proving every captive a martyr. Court descendants may describe a valid debt while concealing substitution. Saltborn singers may inherit the ballad without inheriting either court or bloodline. Infernal investigators may falsely label the creditor Hell because the source grammar survived in polemic.

### Known, open and bounded

- **Authorially fixed for the nursery:** two independent courts made and maintained the compact; the shore rulers substituted humans; provincial officials exploited it; later Conversion memory falsified victim categories; no soul was transferred or owned.
- **Open:** courts, original rescue, quota, captives, rescuer, returned persons, exact officials, surviving route and whether the obligation can be ended without another death.
- **Identity boundary:** not [[Mother Moon's Bargain]], [[The Ashen Guest Court]], [[The Court of Salt and Teeth]], an infernal tithe, a Patron ferry or a Saltborn founding event.
- **Review disposition:** `revise`. The rival-court creditor, crossing function and Conversion distortion are incorporated. Garden promotion remains locked.

## MN-P05 — The Compact of the Knockers

### Historical placement and human aperture

- **Source nursery:** MN-R08.
- **Era:** High Noon, provisionally c. 1110–1170 AR.
- **Region:** one high mine in the Aversian Iron Foothills under a Shield-House extraction administration predating the present Soran regime.
- **Culture:** Aversian miners, charcoal families, prisoners, engineers, guards, migrant workers and local court interpreters.
- **Human aperture:** shift crews who learn to distinguish useful warnings from court speech, official signals and ordinary rock noise.

### Received source and transformation

- **Received source:** Cornish knocker traditions.
- **Borrowed element:** underground knocking as warning or misdirection, conditional aid, a demanded portion and a relationship grounded in mining labor.
- **Retained hard edge:** mine danger, misleading sound, conditional supernatural assistance and the bodily cost of violating a working custom.
- **World Egg hardening:** state coercion makes the compact more dangerous through forced labor, ignored warnings, executed strikers and deliberate prisoner exposure; these are Aversian historical acts rather than generic Cornish traits.
- **Changed axes:** no reusable knocker species is imported; sapient courtiers, reflexive Stone-Voices, geology, fauna and human interpreters remain separate; the compact concerns one seam and one mine rather than all mineral wealth.

### Mine anatomy and mechanism

| Layer | Classification | Function in the episode |
|---|---|---|
| Sylvorn | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | governs territorial memory, mineral succession and the fact that the seam is not interchangeable with another place |
| Astaroth | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | exposes load, resistance, fracture and structural weakness under force |
| mine courtiers | sapient `court-law` persons | translate, warn, bargain or deliberately mislead and remain culpable for chosen acts |
| Stone-Voice responses | nonpersonal or minimally attentive `court-law` responses, classified by occurrence | answer changes in load, buried water, gas and void without making moral choices |
| mine fauna | `biological-pattern` | react to air, vibration and court pressure |
| miners, prisoners and officials | complete `human-soul` | make political choices and bear bodily responsibility |
| portion niches and warning tools | objects | record performance; they contain no court person by default |

The compact protects one load-bearing seam. Workers leave a measured ore portion, keep an answering rhythm before cutting and never remove the reserved rib. In return, sapient mine courtiers sometimes guide crews, while Stone-Voice responses reliably change when fracture, gas, water or unsafe void alters the mine's load.

Shield-House officials turn a local safety custom into production law. They claim every knock authorizes continued cutting, withhold ore portions when quotas fail and declare dead workers court-defaulters whose families owe replacement labor. They force strikers and prisoners into the worst workings. Some officials counterfeit the answering rhythm; at least one court interpreter knowingly directs disliked workers toward danger. Reflexive Stone-Voices remain nonculpable even when their accurate warning is misrepresented.

### Bargain terms

- **Gift:** bounded warning of fracture, gas, water or unsafe void; occasional guidance; limited safe extraction around one seam.
- **Price:** one declared ore portion, maintenance of the answering rhythm and preservation of one load-bearing rib.
- **Boundary:** one geological formation and its connected workings.
- **Failure:** removing the rib redistributes load through inhabited galleries; false answers conceal the direction of failure; withholding the portion closes court guidance but does not itself cause every natural accident.

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** miners discover a reliable local relation between warning knocks, court guidance and structural danger. → **Decision:** the mining administration converts the compact into an instrument of quota enforcement and suppresses workers who interpret it independently. → **Mechanism:** officials counterfeit answers, remove the reserved seam and send coerced crews into the galleries carrying the transferred load. → **Effect:** part of the mine collapses and gas enters refuge chambers which officials had ordered sealed against strikers. → **Cost:** workers are crushed, mutilated, buried alive or executed before the collapse; prisoners die in exposure tests; families inherit fabricated labor debts; court inhabitants also lose one route and habitation. → **Consequence:** a medically and technically useful warning custom survives inside a political history which both officials and court claimants try to rewrite.

### Surviving evidence and 1360 interpretations

Independent carriers include:

1. **Architecture and geology:** the untouched portion of the reserved rib, the illegally cut section and the direction in which load then traveled can be inspected.
2. **Work custom:** Aversian crews still perform a three-part warning rhythm whose middle answer is never treated as permission to work.
3. **Bodies:** a sealed refuge gallery contains workers and prisoners killed by different mechanisms than the official collapse account claims.
4. **Objects:** portion niches and shift tools record when the compact was honored even where administrative ledgers say the dead defaulted.
5. **Documents:** execution orders describe independent interpreters as saboteurs and later recast their safety strike as court worship.

Modern engineers may accept the load evidence but deny sapient courtiers; worker societies may make every knock benevolent; Shield descendants may blame supernatural betrayal; court rulers may claim the mine and all ore as compensation. None follows from the bounded compact.

## MN-P06 — The Mountain the Court Abandoned

### Historical placement and human aperture

- **Source nursery:** MN-R11.
- **Era:** locally dated to an event materially correlating with approximately c. 1080–1160 AR. The distant culture does not use AR dating.
- **Region:** an unnamed far-southern mountain chain across the known world's outer sea, beyond verified contact in 1360.
- **Culture:** several unnamed Orphaned highland communities with distinct valley and summit traditions. They are not a lost Ark colony, proto-Folk, Ättar offshoot or evidence of empty land.
- **Human aperture:** porters, shrine keepers, shepherds, climbers, weather readers and families ordered to shelter the fleeing court.

### Received source and transformation

- **Received source:** H. P. Lovecraft's “The Other Gods.”
- **Borrowed element:** forbidden ascent, familiar local powers fleeing a deeper scale, an inaccessible height and catastrophic failure of human and lesser-power claims to mastery.
- **Retained hard edge:** lethal ascent, exposure, bodily damage, disappearance, helplessness before greater scale and the cruelty of powers abandoning those beneath them.
- **Changed axes:** the deeper pressures are intelligent existing Menhir rather than blind outer gods; the summit court contains morally accountable local persons; the highland population is not racially coded or described as degenerate; no rival creator, cosmic pantheon or revelation of ultimate metaphysics is introduced.

### Court anatomy and mechanism

| Layer | Classification | Function in the episode |
|---|---|---|
| Zephyraxis | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | governs altitude, wind, breathable passage and exposure through moving air |
| Father Sun | continuous `yolk-sovereignty` | governs exposure, fixed light and the removal of sheltering concealment |
| summit rulers and household | sapient `court-law` persons | falsely claim mastery, later flee, seize shelter and sacrifice guides |
| mountain and weather responses | place-minds and bounded law organisms | lose the protective arrangement which made the rulers appear sovereign |
| abandoned fauna | `biological-pattern` | die, migrate or descend according to bodily capacity |
| highland humans | complete `human-soul` | remain moral persons and historical communities rather than witnesses created for the court's downfall |
| regalia and royal weather tokens | offices or objects | cease functioning when the local arrangement ends; they contain no Menhir sovereignty |

For generations, the summit court times winds, cloud cover and one breathable route well enough to appear sovereign over the mountain. Its titles are observationally real but its mastery is not. A rare Zephyraxis–Father Sun conjunction removes cloud concealment, strips accumulated breathable corridors from the upper paths and exposes every structure to fixed light and migrating pressure.

The deeper powers do not chase the court as moral enemies. The court flees because its arrangement can no longer control the mountain. Its rulers close ascent paths behind themselves, abandon dependants and fauna, demand shelter in human shrines and kill guides whose knowledge might allow rivals or abandoned subjects to follow. Human climbers who attempt the summit during the conjunction suffocate, burn under exposure, fall or return with permanent bodily injury.

### Bargain terms

- **Gift:** one breathable ascent corridor, timed cloud shelter, storm knowledge and limited refuge at altitude.
- **Price:** surrender of summit ownership claims, carriage of displaced air or weather to a named lower destination and flexibility of structures built along the route.
- **Boundary:** one high mountain system during the conjunction of exposure and atmospheric migration.
- **Failure:** claiming sovereign ownership makes the corridor close; displaced weather descends upon an unnamed valley; rigid shelters fail; people who follow a route after its air has migrated encounter an apparently open path which cannot sustain them.

### Plain causal chain

**Cause:** a rare altitude-and-exposure conjunction ends the arrangement through which a local court managed summit access. → **Decision:** court rulers conceal their loss of control and flee only when their own survival is threatened. → **Mechanism:** breathable corridors migrate, fixed light removes cloud shelter and the court closes paths to prevent pursuit. → **Effect:** rulers and favored retainers descend into human territory while dependants, fauna and pilgrims remain above. → **Cost:** climbers die or return damaged; guides are sacrificed; human shrines and food stores are seized; abandoned court subjects die, turn against their rulers or seek shelter among people they once treated as tenants. → **Consequence:** the former gods become dangerous refugees, while human communities inherit both their knowledge and the unresolved claims of those abandoned on the mountain.

### Surviving evidence and 1360 condition

Independent local carriers include:

1. **Landscape and bodies:** dead ascent paths, preserved climbers and court dependants lying above the elevation at which ordinary explanations predict survival.
2. **Architecture:** lower human shrines enlarged from within after the court's descent and bearing repairs made by both human and nonhuman hands.
3. **Objects:** evacuated court regalia whose weather functions stop at the exact line where the deeper conjunction became dominant.
4. **Custom:** valley households maintain a shelter obligation while explicitly denying the guest any title to the house.
5. **Meteorological test:** summit weather ignores royal tokens but responds in a bounded way to load, exposure and safe destination terms matching Zephyraxis's established bargain grammar.

These traces exist in 1360 but are not available to Thalmyric, Eclessian, Ahdari, Jirahar, Sestaran, Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan or Nsizwa scholarship. Future first contact remains governed by the Forward Rule. Local traditions disagree whether the court was defeated, punished, betrayed by its servants or exposed as never sovereign.

### Known, open and bounded

- **Authorially fixed for the nursery:** the highland peoples pre-existed the event; the court falsely claimed mastery; an existing Menhir conjunction ended its control; court rulers abandoned dependants, seized shelter and sacrificed guides; the known world has no verified contact by 1360.
- **Open:** continent, range, cultures, rulers, surviving refugees, summit population, exact correlation date, present courts and any future contact.
- **Identity boundary:** not MN-R12R's Great Coherence, Trium's seat, a Faewild Olympus, a Patron capital, a blind god or evidence that worship created the court.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`. The remote placement preserves Menhir scale mismatch and local human history without entering the known-world chronology. Garden promotion remains locked.
