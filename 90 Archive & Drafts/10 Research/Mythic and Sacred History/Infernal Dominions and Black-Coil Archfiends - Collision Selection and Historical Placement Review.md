# Infernal Dominions and Black-Coil Archfiends — Collision, Selection, and Historical Placement Review

## Purpose and Result

This companion to [[Infernal Dominions and Black-Coil Archfiends Review]] tests all eighteen `AF-R` actor leads against existing Saints, Sinners, Idols, Patrons, True Dragons, devil offices, great-power beds and human history. It then selects a deliberately small first constellation and gives six reviewed dominion roots provisional historical apertures.

The result is:

- exactly **four historically selected actors**: AF-R01, AF-R07, AF-R09 and AF-R18;
- exactly **fourteen additional ecology-active actors** reviewed in [[Black-Coil Archfiends - Source Signature and Full Ecology Review]];
- exactly **six selected dominion or front roots**: HL-R01, HL-R05, HL-R06, HL-R07, HL-R09R and HL-R12;
- exactly **six separate historical placements**, spanning Late Chains through the Fall of Atheria;
- three historically selected damned `human-soul` persons and one autonomous Pride `idol-pattern` Archmask;
- one unique fallen spirit, one True Dragon, one non-dragon sovereign wound and the remaining Archmask and damned-ruler leads active elsewhere in the ecology without being backfilled into the first six events.

Selection does not confer canon status. The handles, dates and regional descriptions below remain nursery material. Nothing enters the canonical chronology or [[The Historical Seed Garden]] through this review.

# I. Selection Rules

## Earthly guilt before infernal grandeur

Every selected damned ruler begins as a human being with an ordinary social position, genuine choices, victims and an earthly death. Damnation does not promote a person automatically. Later control of a Crown, sentence, gate, route or laboratory office requires a separate seizure, bargain or appointment. The office, manifested eel-body, dominion and legend remain distinct from the damned `human-soul`.

## Actor and polity are not interchangeable

An actor can outlive, lose, occupy or contest a dominion. A city can replace its ruler. A prison can predate its conqueror. A legal war can involve two rulers without belonging to either. The six placements therefore do not create six permanent pairings or one infernal dynasty.

## No first-pass Blood War cast

The selected actors do not form a council, family, adventuring company, conspiracy or two-sided cosmic army. Only AF-R01 and AF-R07 receive a fixed personal rivalry, and that rivalry begins through one bounded posthumous legal conflict. AF-R09 and AF-R18 have no authenticated meeting with either ruler or with one another.

## Hell and Wrath remain visibly different

Every selected Hell placement uses black water, mud, pressure, flooded roads, burial earth, enclosure and loss of clean boundaries. None contains native fire or brimstone. AF-R18 may possess later stories showing flame, but those stories are false iconography unless a separately proven Wrath compact is present. No Wrath compact is selected in this pass.

# V. Placement Cards

## IP-01 — The Roster Under Black Water

### Historical account

During the Closing-Court crisis, one lesser Giant administration discovered that an infernal custody work could preserve repeatable skills after its earthly experts died. The court did not manufacture souls or genuine continuities. It maintained a black-water route that intercepted names, testimony and postmortem manifestations long enough for devil offices to compel engineers, physicians, interpreters and burial clerks to continue working.

The prison's Material anchor depended upon living captives reciting work rosters beside a pressure shaft. Refusal brought torture, mutilation, execution and the removal of the victim's burial name. Dead specialists were not converted into machinery; intercepted human persons remained human souls, while copied habits and office scripts were separately extracted and installed. Giant officials knowingly concealed this distinction because “the dead still serve” was more useful than admitting how many copies failed and how many persons remained imprisoned.

As the Great Unbinding broke the court, human burial fraternities and service workers attacked the roster house. They opened bodily cells, destroyed several name indexes and carried some intercepted persons to a viable route. They could not save everyone. When the stair flooded, officials chained prisoners below to keep the remaining locks stable. The surviving rebels sealed the stair from the Material side rather than risk reopening the whole prison.

No selected archfiend rules this custody work. Its builder, current Hell-side owner and relationship to other dominions remain open. The episode establishes that infernal states exploited the Giant collapse without causing it.

### Human-coherence chain

**Cause:** a Giant court wants expert labor to survive death → **decision:** officials contract with a custody work and conceal the difference between souls, copies and offices → **mechanism:** living rosters and a pressure shaft let devil offices intercept and compel named specialists → **effect:** the court preserves some services while captives are tortured and the dead are unlawfully held → **cost:** prisoners die in the revolt and others remain behind the sealed stair → **consequence:** later burial customs preserve names specifically to prevent administrative interception.

### Discovery interface

- **Evidence classes:** Giant pressure-marked tablets distinguishing “speaker,” “copy” and “held witness”; human burial lists with the same workers marked both dead and still on duty; a sealed stair whose route response occurs only when an omitted name is spoken; office fragments that preserve tasks but no human memories.
- **Bounded test:** reading one complete burial name at the upper landing makes only that person's old duty-chain release. It does not open the stair or identify the current owner.
- **1360 AR access point:** one far-flung mortuary fraternity still adds “not for the lower roster” after a burial name without knowing the clause's origin.
- **False reconstruction:** the rebels invented resurrection machinery and then destroyed it out of religious fear.
- **Corroboration threshold:** one Giant administrative tablet plus either a responding stair or an independently transmitted burial list establishes the prison mechanism.

### Boundaries

- **Authorially fixed:** persons, copied habits and offices were distinct; officials knew the difference; prisoners were tortured and some remained trapped.
- **Open:** Giant polity, exact site, ruler, number saved, prison builder, present owner and whether any lawful route still reaches the captives.
- **Collision ruling:** separate from Nythalum's lunar Continuants, Orqad's Titan fragments, UH-03's living boundary markers and every Glass Weird.
- **Source fidelity:** Exodus forced-labor and infernal-prison grammar retain compelled work, killing and denied burial without claiming a created power owned the souls.
- **Placement disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## IP-02 — The Audit of Seven Courts

### Earthly biography and infernal rise

The future Auditor begins as the literate child of a conquered dock household in one late City-State league. Employed first to count rations and indemnity grain, he discovers that superiors reward a ledger which makes suffering appear to be somebody else's shortfall. He rises through confiscation offices by assigning every missing ration to a named household, every escaped slave to a guard's family and every military failure to a subordinate district.

He does more than serve a cruel system. He invents transferable quotas, authorizes torture as an auditing tool, sells condemned families to meet revenue targets and arranges the deaths of capable deputies whose records could expose him. During a siege he closes a grain gate after privately exempting his own household. Hundreds outside starve or are killed. Later civic histories praise his “equal measure” while quietly removing the exemptions.

After death his judged human soul does not become an archdevil automatically. In mapped Hell he accepts a record office, betrays its bearer and then captures one local Crown through the same quota mechanism. He standardizes routes among seven otherwise independent courts so that each use transfers a fee, prisoner or mandate to his kingdom. Rivals can avoid those roads; rebellion conducted through them still enriches him.

His Black-Coil body is the kingdom's manifested seal-body, not his soul spread across Hell. The human face inside its mouth appears when the Crown pronounces an audit. Destroying the body or Crown would break present command and toll collection without erasing the damned person.

### Human-coherence chain

**Cause:** a conquered league needs grain and indemnity records → **decision:** a minor clerk converts political violence into transferable quotas and concealed exemptions → **mechanism:** punishments always descend while credit ascends → **effect:** he becomes indispensable, kills witnesses and condemns districts → **cost:** families are sold, prisoners tortured and a closed gate leaves hundreds dead → **consequence:** after judgement he repeats the architecture through a captured infernal Crown and seven standardized routes.

### Discovery interface

- **Evidence classes:** civic grain tablets showing erased exemptions; family sale records carrying the same seventh-receipt formula; seven incompatible infernal seals that all remit to one hidden column; a route toll reproducible only after a user invokes a transferred mandate.
- **Bounded test:** invalidating the seventh receipt prevents one road from remitting its next toll. It does not free all prisoners, void unrelated contracts or damage the Auditor's soul.
- **1360 AR access point:** two commercial schools still debate whether “the seventh receipt pays the absent judge,” unaware that the judge is a map-lord.
- **False reconstruction:** the Auditor was an impartial reformer whose subordinates corrupted a just system after his death.
- **Corroboration threshold:** an earthly exemption tablet and an infernal remittance seal together establish personal continuity of method; neither proves the whole biography alone.

### Boundaries

- **Authorially fixed:** the clerk personally designed the quotas, ordered torture, killed rivals, closed the gate and later seized a local Crown office.
- **Open:** mortal name, city, exact date, earthly death, judgement testimony, previous Crown bearer, current boundaries and number of courts still paying.
- **Collision ruling:** not [[The Creditor]], Sarqinyan, Suhrab the Lawbinder, Covenant revelation, a universal Asmodeus or author of all contract magic.
- **Source fidelity:** the Asmodean quota pyramid retains cultivated ambition, betrayal, torture, sacrifice of subordinates and a system which profits from apparent opposition.
- **Placement disposition:** `revise`; correction incorporated and promotion locked.

## IP-03 — The Charter of the Mire Keys

### Earthly biography and client city

The future Lady begins as the daughter of a provincial prison boatman and later becomes a clerk who knows which locks flood, which guards take money and which prisoners have families able to pay. During the Underground Centuries she sells genuine escape information to persecuted believers, political prisoners and ordinary criminals. She then reports selected clients, retaining their payment while earning promotion for recapture.

Once placed over sentences, she manufactures offences whose enforcement generates labor and informants. Refuge without a stamped sponsor becomes vagrancy; surviving sexual assault becomes illicit absence from custody; feeding a fugitive becomes theft of state grain. Guards receive access to prisoners as reward. She authorizes judicial murder, prison rape, disappearance of children into labor households and prosecution of survivors who cannot prove how they escaped. She sometimes releases an innocent person, but usually to create a witness against somebody more profitable.

After death and judgement she enters an existing infernal client city first as an expert bearer of one sentence office. The city is older than she is and its inhabitants remain distinct damned human souls with their own households and political memories. Through bargains, murder and captured keys she acquires several gate offices and eventually its local crown. Her charter makes every pardon open one cell by closing another, producing a constituency which benefits from somebody else's captivity.

Some residents love the city, keep friendships and protect neighbors. They also tolerate outsiders being enslaved and dissidents being sent into office works. Their remaining human affections make the polity more intelligible, not morally neutral.

### Human-coherence chain

**Cause:** persecution and ordinary crime make prison information valuable → **decision:** a clerk sells escape and recapture together, then writes new offences to expand the market → **mechanism:** keys, sponsors, sentences and witness privileges turn prisoners into political currency → **effect:** she rises while the institution manufactures guilt → **cost:** rape, judicial murder, forced labor, disappeared families and betrayed fugitives → **consequence:** after judgement she conquers offices in a client city whose pardons require replacement captives.

### Discovery interface

- **Evidence classes:** prison boat ledgers recording the same person as escaped and recaptured under her authorization; victim petitions naming reward access; sentence keys whose wards reproduce the client city's plan; three dream-derived street maps which agree on markets and disagree on hidden prisons.
- **Bounded test:** a correctly identified counterfeit pardon opens its promised cell but closes the named collateral cell. Refusing the exchange leaves both unchanged.
- **1360 AR access point:** one provincial guest-law still forbids a host from “closing the second door” when securing a prisoner's release.
- **False reconstruction:** she was a secret rescuer forced to betray a few fugitives to preserve a larger escape road.
- **Corroboration threshold:** a prison ledger and one survivor petition establish the double sale; a responding sentence key is required to connect the mortal official to the infernal ruler.

### Boundaries

- **Authorially fixed:** she knowingly sold escape and recapture, wrote predatory offences, authorized bodily abuse and later seized several infernal offices.
- **Open:** mortal and infernal names, province, precise victims, death, first office, client city's location, current council and whether her crown presently controls every key it claims.
- **Collision ruling:** not the Iron Listener, a founder of every infernal prison, a daughter of the Auditor, the Closed House claimant or an explanation for the Underground persecutions.
- **Source fidelity:** Glasya's prison-and-loophole grammar retains judicial murder, sexual violence, retaliation and criminal law engineered to produce captives.
- **Placement disposition:** `revise`; correction incorporated and promotion locked.

## IP-04 — The War of Two Exceptions

### Historical account

During compulsory Conversion, one provincial administration transfers prisoners, temple servants, confiscated property and condemned objects faster than its courts can classify them. Two groups of summoners obtain apparently useful infernal precedents. One doctrine says an unassigned prisoner falls under the last valid superior mandate; the other says an unlawfully held prisoner becomes evidence against the gaoler and therefore enters the custody of the sentence court hearing that offence.

The first exception remits authority toward the Auditor's Quota Kingdom. The second feeds the Lady's gate and sentence offices. Their Ash Advocates steal witnesses, alter definitions and engineer contradictory transfers. Neither ruler created the persecutions, and neither seeks justice. Each wants the newly opened flow of prisoners and confiscated standing.

The legal contest produces physical violence. Prisoners are moved in the night and drown in locked wagons during a flooded crossing. Temple custodians are tortured for mutually exclusive inventories. A converted magistrate is executed under a sentence that changes beneficiary after death. Families who pay one court discover that the rival exception has reclassified their payment as proof of conspiracy. Infernal office-bodies enter two Material gaols before human officials sever the seals.

The conflict ends locally when surviving clerks publish the names of all transferred prisoners without classifying ownership and destroy the paired witness seals. This interrupts the Material aperture but does not defeat either ruler or end their wider rivalry.

### Human-coherence chain

**Cause:** mass confiscation and prisoner transfer create ambiguous custody → **decision:** rival officials use two infernal exceptions to clear their backlogs and punish enemies → **mechanism:** each exception redirects mandates, witnesses and collateral to a different dominion → **effect:** the Auditor and Lady contest the same prisoners through Ash Advocate offices → **cost:** torture, drowning, execution, family dispossession and office attacks inside gaols → **consequence:** two later legal schools inherit incompatible precedents whose hidden beneficiaries survive.

### Discovery interface

- **Evidence classes:** two precedent families citing the same prisoners under opposite classifications; transport remains matching a locked-wagon drowning; paired seals with different office chains; a public name list containing persons whom both courts later claimed.
- **Bounded test:** applying both exceptions to one empty sentence causes the mandates to cancel at the witness layer. Applying them to a person risks a new transfer and is forbidden by the research protocol.
- **1360 AR access point:** one relic court and one secular prison court still cite opposite halves of the same case when deciding who may hold a dangerous object.
- **False reconstruction:** principled Christian and old-cult lawyers fought over whether conversion invalidated prior sentences.
- **Corroboration threshold:** documentary comparison proves the common cases; one paired-seal response establishes the two infernal beneficiaries.

### Boundaries

- **Authorially fixed:** both rulers knowingly exploited a human persecution; human magistrates chose to use the instruments; the published name list interrupted the local mechanism.
- **Open:** province, magistrates, summoners, which prisoners escaped, exact supernatural appearances and whether either court regards the conflict as formally concluded.
- **Collision ruling:** a local infernal parasite upon the [[Great-Power Mythic Ecology and Planting Matrix#GP-III-02 — The War of the Closed Doors|War of the Closed Doors]] and [[Great-Power Mythic Ecology and Planting Matrix#GP-III-01 — The Siege of the Martyrs' Names|Siege of the Martyrs' Names]], not either umbrella conflict and not the origin of compulsory Conversion.
- **Source fidelity:** infernal-law grammar retains correct technical arguments used toward evil ends and makes every abstract transfer alter actual captivity, food, torture or death.
- **Placement disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## IP-05 — The House of Divided Instruments

### Earthly biography and arsenal

The future Surgeon begins as an apprentice wound-dresser in a mixed Long-Frontier district where route accidents leave victims with missing intervals of memory, displaced injuries and names that no longer match official testimony. His early work is genuinely useful. He learns that a victim's conscious account can stabilize the sequence long enough for ordinary surgery and legal recognition.

He then decides that unwilling prisoners provide more repeatable evidence than consenting patients. A military patron gives him condemned people, captured scouts and displaced households whose complaints carry little standing. He keeps subjects conscious through mutilating procedures, withholds warnings from one settlement to measure recurrence, and executes assistants who try to release the casualty lists. The state calls the work defensive because some instruments really do predict route failure.

He dies during a deliberately repeated contradiction after refusing to stop the trial. After judgement, his damned soul bargains for stolen laboratory and route offices. He does not create an Abyssal wound. His Hell-side arsenal preserves three existing bounded contradictions through testimony clamps, place-beacons and divided manifestation anchors, then sells demonstrations to local rulers.

The arsenal's greatest weapon is not a sword but a range marker. Placed in a Material road or settlement under the right historical conditions, it tells one preserved contradiction where to recur. Every test damages the map on which the arsenal depends. The Surgeon treats that cost as evidence that the experiment matters.

### Human-coherence chain

**Cause:** real route injuries require testimony-guided treatment → **decision:** a gifted apprentice replaces consent with captive repeatability → **mechanism:** conscious testimony, clamps and beacons stabilize and redirect existing contradictions → **effect:** useful medicine becomes a state experiment and later an infernal arsenal → **cost:** vivisection, mutilation, destroyed settlements, murdered assistants and identity damage → **consequence:** several 1360 instruments remain medically valuable while one may still function as a wound range marker.

### Discovery interface

- **Evidence classes:** clinical notes whose patient numbers match prison transfers; three casualty lists altered by different sponsors; testimony clamps that preserve sequence without healing injury; a range marker whose placement produces the same short route failure under controlled empty-site conditions.
- **Bounded test:** place one marker between two unmanned survey posts after reconstructing its original route geometry. The measured distance repeats one historical discontinuity; no person may stand within the test circuit.
- **1360 AR access point:** a frontier hospital owns a divided instrument used safely for diagnosis, while a military collector seeks its missing paired marker.
- **False reconstruction:** the Surgeon was framed after discovering a natural cure which threatened military contractors.
- **Corroboration threshold:** a prison register and casualty list prove human experimentation; the controlled range test establishes that at least one infernal technique survived.

### Boundaries

- **Authorially fixed:** his first treatment was useful; he knowingly abandoned consent, used prisoners, killed assistants and repeated a lethal event; three existing contradictions entered the arsenal.
- **Open:** name, district, patrons, victims, exact death, office suppliers, present dominion, status of each contradiction and whether the paired marker survives.
- **Collision ruling:** not GS-09's Sealed-Door Reader, UH-03's boundary prison, Nythalum identity copying, the Unwritten Worm or a fourth established True Dragon.
- **Fire boundary:** no native or invented hellfire exists. Reports of blue or white flame are later illustrations unless separately proven to depict Wrath or ordinary fire.
- **Source fidelity:** Mephistophelean experimentation retains catastrophic ambition, disposable servants and mass testing while the setting-specific weapon is causal redirection rather than infernal flame.
- **Placement disposition:** `revise`; correction incorporated and promotion locked.

## IP-06 — The Last Door of the Borrowed Temple

### Historical account

Centuries before the Fall, one Atherian star sanctuary was closed, stripped and reused as a provincial prison and cistern refuge. The closure broke a Material route used by an autonomous Pride Archmask. The Archmask survived through other Shadow anchors and built a grievance identity: the Exile, a supposedly dispossessed heavenly queen whose return would restore imperial greatness.

When Atherian authority collapses around 905 AR, soldiers, prisoners, servants and refugees crowd into the old sanctuary. A commander reopens sealed chambers looking for weapons. The Exile offers constructed dragon-like war-broods and a promise that the province can become the empire's true successor. In exchange it demands a borrowed altar, public acclamation and condemned bodies for stabilizing the route. The commander consents. Prisoners are executed, corpses and stolen living tissue are used in war-brood frames, and refugees who refuse the acclamation are denied water.

The reopened route also gives one infernal custody work access to the deaths accumulating around the refuge. Its offices begin taking prisoners under false evacuation orders. A redeemed human saint acting under the already reviewed Liberator-of-Captives grammar, accompanied by loyal angels, receives one bounded commission: preserve the prisoners' names and hold the last viable soul threshold while living people break cells, carry the wounded and close the Material route.

The saint does not conquer the sanctuary, command the refugees' government or destroy Pride. Human prisoners and servants flood the altar chamber, break the acclaim engine and drag unfinished war-broods into the cistern. Many are killed. The local route closes, the custody assault fails and the Exile loses this opening again. The Archmask survives elsewhere; no present return is fixed.

### Human-coherence chain

**Cause:** imperial collapse crowds refugees into a repurposed sanctuary whose old route was never fully understood → **decision:** a commander reopens the chambers and accepts the Exile's weapons → **mechanism:** acclaim, a borrowed altar, executions and constructed bodies rebuild the Material anchor while a custody work raids the resulting death route → **effect:** dragon-like war-broods begin forming and prisoners are taken under false evacuation → **cost:** execution, corpse violation, denial of water, combat deaths and people abandoned in locked cells → **consequence:** mortal resistance closes the route while one bounded heavenly commission preserves captives at the last threshold.

### Discovery interface

- **Evidence classes:** unfinished constructed war-broods with human and nonhuman material but no souls of their own; prison water tallies recording denial for refusal to acclaim; an altar whose erased title predates the prison; mutually hostile refugee and garrison accounts agreeing that named captives vanished from locked cells; a door duty preserved by descendants of the rescuers.
- **Bounded test:** the door's old name-response occurs only when a threatened captive is identified and somebody physically accepts responsibility to carry that person. It cannot summon the saint or reopen the Archmask route.
- **1360 AR access point:** a military antiquarian possesses one inert war-brood crown and believes it proves the Exile was a benevolent dragon goddess betrayed by frightened clergy.
- **False reconstruction:** church agents massacred a lawful star cult, destroyed living dragons and invented an infernal attack to conceal the crime.
- **Corroboration threshold:** a water tally and war-brood remains establish the commander's bargain; hostile accounts plus the bounded door response establish a real captive-preservation event without naming the saint.
