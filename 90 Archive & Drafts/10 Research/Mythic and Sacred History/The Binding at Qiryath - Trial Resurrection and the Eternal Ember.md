# The Binding at Qiryath

In 0 AR, Isfir goes to **Second Qiryath** for the pilgrimage that remembers the Great Unbinding. He teaches in the sanctuary courts, shares his last meal with the Companions, and is arrested after dark. Lord-Scribe Ezrahel's faction brings him before the Atherian governor. The governor condemns him outside the sanctuary, and imperial soldiers bind and nail him to a timber on the road outside the city. He is buried before the Resting Day and rises bodily on the third day. The first congregation gathers in the city that has just seen him executed. This development was accepted on 13 November 2026. [[The Rise of the Redeemer]] and [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]] give the canon accounts.

Caleran becomes sacred through the people who carried the testimony there, the martyrs who died in its forum, and the lamp brought from Amaryah's hearth after legalization. The Eternal Ember carries the memory of Qiryath to Caleran through a history of shelter, tending and transmission.

## The city before the arrest

By 0 AR, Qiryath lives under a provincial charter. The sanctuary retains its sacrifice, priests, communal courts and burial jurisdiction. Its judges can rule on discipline and access to the sanctuary. Atheria reserves capital cases concerning imperial rule and the safety of its garrison and roads. Qiryath's archive holds the terms under which local officers can hand a case over. The provincial chancery holds the corresponding instrument that gives the governor power to receive it. The charter grows out of the political accommodation outlined in [[From Thalion to Isfir - Imperial Expansion and the Redeemer]]. Its date remains open. This is the charter of the living city. The later Empty Charter belongs to the Vestian clearance.

The governor comes to Qiryath to hold hearings during the pilgrimage, when merchants, sanctuary officers and claimants from other tower towns are already in the city. His soldiers guard the approaches and the road court. High Augur Severan is there with the governor's party, responsible for the imperial rites and reporting offences against the public cult. He has influence over how a sentence is presented. The governor holds the seal and commands the soldiers. Odrun II is emperor at Caleran and hears about the affair through provincial reports after the sentence. ## Pilgrimage, supper and arrest

Isfir arrives with pilgrims who know him as a teacher and healer. Some welcome him as the promised Mesakha. In a public court he overturns tables where agents are taking unauthorized fees on animals brought for sacrifice. He teaches from the Scrolls where the people affected can hear him. Other Jirahar teachers challenge his claims in public. Ezrahel fears that the gathering will bring imperial soldiers into the sanctuary and that one disturbance could cost other towers their protections. He chooses to remove Isfir before the pilgrimage disperses.

Isfir spends his final evening in a household guest room with the Companions. He gives thanks over the bread and cup, speaks of his life given for others, and charges them to remember him when they eat together. Later, at a place outside the crowded courts where he regularly goes to pray, he faces his approaching death. Ischar has sold the route to Ezrahel's officers and identifies Isfir for the arrest party. The officers take him there at night. A Companion starts to resist and Isfir stops him. This draft follows the Synoptic pattern of a final meal within the festival observance. The exact ritual date can be set when the pilgrimage calendar is developed. ## Two hearings and one sentence

Ezrahel questions Isfir during the night. At first light, the senior elders he has gathered hear the charge that Isfir has falsely claimed the authority of the Mesakha and spoken as if he had authority over the sanctuary. Witnesses disagree over his words about the tower. An elder objects that the testimony cannot establish a threat. Ezrahel's majority sends the case to the governor anyway. Their written charge puts public order and rival kingship first. They argue that a claimant acclaimed as king during a pilgrimage challenges Odrun's authority.

The governor questions Isfir at the provincial hearing court. He has reports of a crowd and a dispute at the sanctuary, and no report of a raised company or an attack on the garrison. Isfir acknowledges a kingship whose authority comes from the Lord. He says his followers would have fought for him if his kingdom depended on that kind of power. Severan presses the governor to make the punishment public. Ezrahel's faction threatens to send a complaint to Caleran if he lets an acclaimed claimant go. The governor chooses to keep the elders' cooperation and demonstrate control before the pilgrims leave. He authorizes death for a challenge to imperial rule. That order is his decision. Ezrahel's faction knowingly sought it, and the governor's soldiers carry it out. ## The Binding and burial

The soldiers take Isfir outside the city to the public execution ground on the river road, where departing pilgrims can see the sentence. They bind and nail him to an upright with a crosspiece. A notice names the offence as a claim to kingship. Amaryah, Calira, Solana and other women stay near enough to see him die. Isfir entrusts Amaryah and Calira to one another before he dies. A soldier confirms his death to the governor's officer.

The elder who objected at the hearing petitions for the body. He has a nearby family burial chamber cut into the rock above the road and enough standing to take responsibility for the burial. The governor releases the body under the charter's burial provisions, with the elder named on the receipt. Burial workers wash and wrap Isfir and lay him in the chamber before dusk. Solana and another woman see the body laid inside and the stone shut. At dusk the Resting Day begins. They remain in the city and wait to return with spices. The proposal uses local burial work and a known tomb. The riverbank, hearing court and burial slope can be mapped together later. ## The third day

On the morning after the Resting Day, Solana and the other women go back to the chamber. The stone has been moved. Inside they find the linen and an empty place where they saw the body laid. A heavenly messenger tells them he has risen. Solana lingers after the others turn back towards the city. She takes a man standing by the path for someone who works among the graves. When he speaks her name she recognizes Isfir. Later he stands among the Companions and lets them see the wounds. He eats with them. The women know which tomb to take the others to, and their report makes the Companions confront what they had thought was finished.

The Resurrection restores the human soul-road. The authorial event is bodily and real. The proposed dawn sequence follows the Gospel chronology of death and burial on the first day, rest on the second and discovery on the third. The existing name **Third-Night Fire** becomes the liturgical name for the vigil and the report of white light before daybreak. It supplies no exact hour for the Resurrection. The women witness the empty tomb and the risen Isfir. The sanctuary rites continue, but their former sign of acceptance ceases. Priests and households interpret the silence differently. The governor has a record of death and release of the body. There is no imperial finding on the Resurrection. ## The first gathering and Solana's jar

After appearances and instruction, Isfir ascends. The Companions remain in Qiryath until the Holy Flame comes upon the gathered community at a later feast. Pilgrims hear the proclamation in the languages they brought with them. Teaching, prayer, bread and the care of people without provision give the congregation a life after the crowd disperses. A host's lamp burns at the gatherings. At the time Solana leaves, she banks a coal from the gathering house's hearth, which they have used to keep those lamps lit. Amaryah and Calira are leaving with other people under threat. They travel by the road and river routes whose hosts know how to receive pilgrims.

The Gift of the Holy Flame is the divine event that commissions the people. The coal Solana carries is a material inheritance of their first common worship. She takes it in a covered clay firepot with a vent and feeds it fuel at stops. Amaryah lights her hearth-lamp at **Veridun Minor**. The house becomes a shrine as it shelters visitors and gives travelling believers food and a place to remember the meal. Calira dies defending it in **29 AR** against a local raid before the Great Purge's main wave. After Amaryah's Falling-Asleep around 55 AR and Solana's death in 63 AR, neighbouring households take turns tending the lamp, finding fuel and training new keepers. When a watch ends, the incoming keeper lights a fresh wick from the old one before removing it. The later shrine's custodians inherit the duty from those households. ## What Caleran receives

**An apostolic history of its own.** Jareth is executed in the Celestial Forum in 41 AR and Tyros and Aquilan die in Caleran during the persecution. Their deaths make the capital sacred to people who buried the martyrs, hid their writings and kept their households alive. After Cassian's legalization in 292 AR, the city congregation can claim a public place for worship and remembrance. In the proposed sequence, during Cassian's reign, keepers from Veridun Minor carry a daughter flame into the church at the former forum. They also bring the vessel venerated as Solana's jar, which accounts for its later shrine in the capital. The hearth-lamp remains at the refuge at the time of this transfer. The Caleran sanctuary flame takes the name **Eternal Ember** as its public cult grows. The precise dedication year and the later history of the refuge lamp remain open. [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]] · [[The Conversion of the Empire]] · [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]]

**A pillar inherited from the empire.** Keep the **Pillar of Concordance** in Caleran as an existing monument of imperial law. Officers once read public judgements at it. Jareth's execution belongs to the same forum, giving the converted precinct a reason to keep the pillar where it stands. The church remembers there the sentences imposed on the first witnesses and the authority that once pronounced them. Isfir's execution timber stays at Qiryath in this history. Its fate after the execution is unrecorded. A Caleran tradition that venerates the local pillar as a witness to persecution now has a concrete subject in the city. [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]] · [[Caleran Anchor Location Register]]

**The governor's case chest (approved in Causality Pass 04.8, 14 November 2026).** Sentences for rival kingship were reported to the emperor with the instruments as proof. The governor's account to Odrun II therefore travelled in a case chest holding the soldiers' nails, the notice of the charge and the receipt for the body. The chest lay in the judgement archive of the Celestial Forum until c. 41, when the Purge commission produced the nails at Jareth's trial and had them leaded into the Pillar; the notice stayed in the chest and is lost. Cassian's grant of the precinct c. 300–310 carried Pillar and nails to the Church. **No document on this route states a count, and none may be written to.** The Third Nail stays sealed. [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth#4. The Four Nails (provenance only)|Signs and Proof §4]]

**A tended relic that survives enemy custody.** The cathedral lamp's continuity is a succession of kindling and human care. The flame consumes fuel. The White Flame can be encountered through the relic under its own conditions. The lamp gives no holder authority over the divine gift. Idris still impounds it in **905 AR** and assigns guards and keepers. Their ledger records oil, wicks and the cost of custody. The flame is still burning when Caleran is retaken in **957 AR**, and Veridun pays the invoice. The occupiers helped preserve the holy place of the people whose city they held. The original Qiryath fire and burial site play no role in the property claim. **A place to die and a reason to return.** Caedros I still goes to the Pillar after the recovery and dies as witnesses report the White Flame descending. ## What Qiryath keeps

The first believers know the burial place. The destruction of **70 AR** breaks access to that part of the burial slope and disperses the households that could guide visitors. Later accounts preserve the approach in general terms, and the exact chamber cannot be authenticated in 1360 AR. The Empty Charter allows Jirahar custodians to tend the ruin and receive pilgrims under Eclessian oversight. Redeemer pilgrims come to the city where they believe he rose. Jirahar pilgrims come to the sanctuary and the graves of their own dead. The two groups depend on some of the same paths and custodians. The burial chamber and the Silent Gate belong to different parts of the site's history. Warmth beneath the gate stair remains the existing disputed sign. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]] · [[Qiryath Reqa]]

By the Great Split, Veridun's claim runs through Amaryah's household, the continuity of the Ember and the recovery of the capital. Eclessia still claims Jareth's Commission of the Pen and the custody of his writings. Qiryath lies in what becomes Eclessian territory, and its Jirahar custodians hold their own obligations under the Empty Charter. No ruler acquires authority over the whole faith through the location of the old sanctuary. The same arrangement leaves room for people who protect a path to the dead to disagree about what happened there. [[The Great Split — Two Crowns, One Fire]] · [[Qiryath Reqa]]
