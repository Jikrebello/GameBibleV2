# Patron Realm Historical Placement Pass

## Purpose and Status

This pass gives six reviewed Patron-realm nurseries from [[Patron Realms and Egg White City Politics Review]] preferred historical soil. It does **not** make their names, dates, owners, routes, events, or present conditions canon. Each placement now has exactly one `retain`, `revise`, `defer`, or `reject` disposition; promotion remains a later and separate decision.

The six roots are deliberately independent. They are not stages of one Astral empire, colonies of one hidden Patron, chapters of one route league, or evidence that all human history is secretly governed from the Egg White. A later city may quote an older compact without inheriting its government. Refugees may carry a legal custom without carrying an anchor. Similar rents may arise because the Egg White imposes similar pressures, not because one conspiracy designed them.

## Controlling Rulings

- A Patron is an owner or controller of metaphysical mass, shelter, routes, knowledge, or edits. Patron is a role, not a species.
- Realm, anchor, owner, proxy, government, inhabitants, copies, offices, and human souls remain separately classified.
- A realm can outlive its founder without becoming the founder's soul. A ruler can escape a collapsing realm without carrying its inhabitants. A population can overthrow a government without acquiring the anchor.
- Egg White residence, bodily crossing, realm ownership, or inherited Giant power never creates, divides, copies, redeems, or destroys the human Image.
- Rent may be lawful by local compact and still be predatory. Shelter may be sincere and still ruin those it saves. Neutrality constrains conduct but does not create moral innocence.
- No placement explains a Weird, the Glass, the Quiet Ninth, the Thirty Sisters, the Bells of the Split, Qarayel's grave, or Nythalum's lunar survival.
- Nythalum remains a physical lunar Ascension. None of these realms is a hidden Nythalum stage or colony.
- No Late-Chains placement identifies Anshara's final sovereign, supreme escape, or lost capital.
- The present placement obeys the Forward Rule: it establishes pressure, evidence, claimants, and possible consequences, never the party's choice or the final outcome.

# II. Six Historical Roots

## PR-P01 — The Court Cast Loose

### Historical placement

- **Era:** the Closing-Court crisis and Great Unbinding, provisionally c. 2350–2270 BR.
- **Material origin:** one secondary Giant court within the many-state world of the Five Imperial Horizons. It may be a mixed cadet court connected to the Black Orchard ecology or a lesser polity elsewhere, but it is not the final Ansharan sovereign and need not be Ansharan at all.
- **Scale:** a bodily Giant flight carrying a mobile court, captives, client households, constructed servants, archives, food stores, and sections of inhabited palace-city into the Egg White.
- **Carrier:** abandoned district petitions, caravan-road wounds, hereditary tenant claims, cut-loose town traditions, and later Patron genealogies.

### Transformation formula

- **Humble origin:** the first stable route is found by enslaved porters and repair crews sent ahead of the royal convoy. Court histories later attribute it to the ruler.
- **Scriptural lens:** Babel's architecture of self-preservation and proud ascent joined to the Exodus judgement upon an oppressive court. Flight from judgement is not liberation merely because the fugitive is endangered.
- **Comparative grammar:** wandering palace, divine-dynastic flight, city as royal household, and doomed rear guard.
- **Historical pressure:** prophetic warning, human revolt, Giant civil wars, heavenly severance, failing recurrence systems, and closing Material exits.
- **World Egg torque:** the ruler crosses bodily and keeps uninterrupted `giant-continuity`. Becoming a Patron means owning and leasing the caravan anchor; it does not change the Giant's species or give it a soul.
- **Contested residue:** some successor towns call the event the Saving of the Court; the towns cut away from it call it the First Abandonment.

### Political anatomy

- **Owner:** one genuine High or Dynastic Giant who escapes bodily; exact generation, lineage, court, and moral disposition remain open.
- **Anchor:** palace foundations, a moving road-engine, household title objects, accumulated captive labor, and pieces of Material ground carried through a prepared aperture.
- **Visible government:** royal household officers, military clients, accountants of weight, and human-souled descendants or servants who can perform acts of witness unavailable to repetitive court patterns.
- **Inhabitants:** the Giant court; giant-blooded humans with complete `human-soul`; ordinary human captives and dependants; constructed palace offices; copies generated by failed continuity machinery; and later Driftkin tenants.
- **Continuity / classification:** the owner retains `giant-continuity`; every ordinary or giant-blooded human retains a complete `human-soul`; constructed offices, copies, roads, and Driftkin are separately classified and never presumed to contain the court's persons.
- **Rent constitution:** labor, route repair, surrendered heirlooms, remembered geography, disciplined repetition, offspring service, and periodic acquisition of fresh Material mass.
- **External politics:** the caravan bargains with other escaping courts, fragment keepers, route predators, and nascent Anchor-Lords. It sells passage to weaker fugitives, sometimes after destroying their independent tethers.

### Hard event and collapse

The caravan cannot carry every district through a narrowing passage. Its rulers order entire wards cut loose as ballast: storehouses, slave quarters, infirmaries, prisons, workshops, and at least one client palace. Families are divided at sealed gates. Some discarded inhabitants fray or die; some are eaten by route predators; some survive because servants fasten local wells, graves, and work rhythms into crude secondary anchors. Soldiers kill people trying to cross into the retained wards. Court annals call the choice arithmetically necessary while concealing which elite estates were never considered expendable.

The severed districts do not all share one fate. One may become a hostile toll-town, another a petition-state preserving the old law against its former king, and another a ruin whose automatic household offices still demand service from anyone entering it. Their variety is the historical point.

### Present-day traces and hooks

- descendants or former tenants still present rent claims at every known appearance of the caravan;
- a cut-loose road joins the current lanes only when travelers carry an object once classed as palace property;
- one human lineage leases bounded Giant power while publicly claiming divine ancestry;
- competing fragments of the abandonment order place different districts on the protected list;
- the present court may be thriving, diminished, foreclosed, divided, or already ruled by a proxy while its Giant owner remains alive.

### Known, open, and forbidden

- **Authorially known for the nursery:** the flight is bodily; the owner retains `giant-continuity`; inhabitants were carried without equal consent; districts were deliberately abandoned; no soul was copied into the court archive.
- **Open:** empire, ruler, route, number of districts, degree of repentance, current survival, and whether temporal mercy was ever granted.
- **Forbidden:** identifying Anshara's final sovereign or capital; giving Anshara a direct prophetic plague; making the flight Nythalum's Ascension; treating copied ancestors as the escaped Giant; making escape proof of divine favor.

### Source-fidelity ruling

- **Received source:** PT-R06's Dreamlands palace-caravan grammar; Giant dynastic flight; Genesis 11 and Exodus as the governing scriptural lens.
- **Borrowed element:** a ruler moving a court away from catastrophe while settlement, status, and route become inseparable.
- **Original hard edge:** slavery, unequal escape, royal pride, pursuit, bodily death, and the abandonment of people who made elite survival possible.
- **Current state:** `intact`.
- **Restoration:** none. The severed districts, killings at the gates, family division, and court culpability are structural rather than atmospheric.
- **Historical layer:** disputed court chronicle over an authorially real bodily flight and abandonment.

### Review disposition

`retain` — preserve one guilty bodily Giant escape, the mobile court, unequal passage, deliberately severed districts, independent successor communities, and continuing tenant claims while leaving empire, owner, route, survival, and relationship to Anshara open. Promotion remains locked.

## PR-P02R — The City of the Second Hearth

### Historical placement

- **Era:** late Scattering into the first centuries of Hearths, provisionally c. 2210–1780 BR.
- **Material origin:** a small mixed refugee household that leaves the principal Exodus descendants without founding any later culture wholesale.
- **Scale:** a constructed refuge that survives its founder, becomes a real civic polity, and is later lost or evacuated rather than remaining a second present-day recursive refuge.
- **Carrier:** incompatible street maps, hearth law, family accusations, evacuation songs, citizenship fragments, and a few dangerous anchor shards.

### Transformation formula

- **Humble origin:** a cook, route-rememberer, mason, bereaved grandparent, or former domestic servant reconstructs one safe courtyard so children can sleep without hearing the wasteland.
- **Scriptural lens:** Tobit's exiled household piety, Lamentations and Baruch's dangerous longing for a lost city, and Ezra's problem of restoration by registers. Love of home is real; memory is not a warrant to exclude the living.
- **Comparative grammar:** the desired city, remembered childhood, refuge outliving its founder, and restoration that discovers it cannot restore the past.
- **Historical pressure:** the Prophet's death, fragmentation of the Exodus, marriages between survivor communities, route loss, hunger, and disagreement over which customs remain binding.
- **World Egg torque:** memory supplies temporary construction scaffolding only when joined to material anchors, labor, repeated routes, and present correction. By the third generation the city must remain stable through public law, maintenance, and current inhabitants rather than perpetual reenactment of the founder's childhood. Memory cannot create a soul, city, or person ex nihilo.
- **Contested residue:** some traditions say the city was drawn twice—once from recollection and once from the lives of people whom the recollection omitted—and then broken a third time when officials tried to restore the first drawing.

### Political anatomy

- **Founder:** provisionally a living human with a complete `human-soul`; a Driftkin founder remains a review alternative, not a second hidden identity.
- **Owner / anchor title:** the founder initially holds the constructed anchor in trust with the building households. After the founder's death or departure, title is disputed among the civic institution, Material heirs, anchor custodians, and residents whose labor supplied its mass; the founder's soul is not the property title.
- **Anchor:** cooking stones, a door lintel, burial objects, carried soil, a measured plan, and the labor of builders whose names the founder did not initially remember. Repeated household paths help during construction but do not remain the city's permanent governing law.
- **Government:** first a household assembly; later a civic council that replaces affection and remembered kinship with admission, service, residence, appeal, and public hearth obligations.
- **Inhabitants:** refugee builders, invited kin, unrelated newcomers, children born in the Egg White, Driftkin residents, constructed street offices, and copies formed when ceremonial recollection is mistaken for biography.
- **Continuity / classification:** every human founder, builder, child, refugee, and descendant has a complete `human-soul`; Driftkin, copied relatives, constructed offices, civic residue, and anchor shards retain their own separately established continuities.
- **Rent constitution:** construction-era narration followed by maintenance, Material correspondence, repair journeys, public hearth service, new civic festivals, and emergency surrender of memory. Later attempts to make personal recollection the permanent rent are a constitutional usurpation rather than normal civic law.
- **External politics:** neighboring Patrons offer mass in exchange for control of a gate, a census, or the founder's most painful memories. Material descendants claim return rights without agreeing whether the city is inheritance or betrayal.

### Hard event and succession crisis

The founder repeatedly gives the city memories that properly belong to living relatives left in the Material world. Faces fade from the founder before death; copied relatives appear inside the refuge and insist that they are the abandoned people. The first constitutional settlement nevertheless rejects personal recollection as a permanent title and recognizes residents through witnessed dwelling and shared hearth duty.

Generations later, a rent famine allows restorationist administrators to seize power. They privilege households present in the original memory, deny food and route access to later arrivals, and erase an unremembered workers' quarter from the official plan. Residents trapped in that quarter die when maintenance is withdrawn. Attempts to reconstruct the founder produce contradictory copied officials and incompatible street commands. The city fractures. Most inhabitants evacuate through routes opened by servants, children, and residents excluded from the first plan; others disappear with sealed districts. The city does not remain an intact modern counterpart to [[Demiplane Atlas#Chalkhaven Recursion|Chalkhaven]] or the [[Demiplane Atlas#Unfinished Shore|Unfinished Shore]].

After the founder dies or routes onward, no retained human soul rules the city. The lost anchor, municipal institution, accumulated memory, copies, refugees, and surviving laws are distinct continuities. Whether one recoverable anchor fragment still holds inhabitants remains open.

### Present-day traces and hooks

- two authoritative maps disagree over whether a sealed district ever evacuated;
- Material families claim the founder deserted them, while refugee descendants say those families are trying to inherit a country they did not build;
- one recovered anchor shard issues obsolete household punishments without proving that a copied official or citizen survives inside it;
- later refuge-builders quote the Second Hearth rule that present dwelling outranks remembered affection, but neither Chalkhaven nor the Unfinished Shore is its institutional continuation;
- several Scattering peoples preserve evacuation songs and hearth clauses, but none owns the city's complete ancestry.

### Known, open, and forbidden

- **Authorially known for the nursery:** craft and community built the refuge; memory alone did not; the city replaced founder-memory with civic law; a later restorationist failure killed residents and broke the city; founder, city, refugees, copies, shards, and soul remain distinct.
- **Open:** founder's culture and sex, exact relation to the Exodus, whether the founder abandoned or lost the waking household, routes of evacuation, sealed districts, surviving anchor fragments, and whether any copy achieved independent personhood.
- **Forbidden:** making it the source of all Scattering cultures; making memory a human-soul duplication engine; identifying the Year of Standing Stars; treating nostalgia as moral truth.

### Source-fidelity ruling

- **Received source:** PT-R02R; Lovecraft's “Celephaïs”; Tobit, Lamentations, Baruch, and Ezra restoration grammar.
- **Borrowed element:** a remembered city becoming more desirable than ordinary life and then becoming a polity beyond its dreamer's control.
- **Original hard edge:** estrangement, fatal preference for the otherworld, loss of ordinary obligations, and the exclusion produced by idealized memory.
- **Current state:** `intact after review revision`.
- **Restoration:** the city now becomes a genuine post-founder polity and later breaks when restorationists reimpose personal memory as public law. The founder's neglect, memory loss, exclusion deaths, civic resistance, and evacuation remain causal.
- **Historical layer:** civic archive and family accusation around an authorially real constructed refuge.

### Distinction from existing realms

- **Chalkhaven** is a canonical Driftkin recursion sustained by teaching, walking and truthful redrawing; the Second Hearth is a lost human-founded historical polity whose mature constitution rejected memory as permanent governing law.
- **Unfinished Shore** is a canonical refugee destination assembled from communal descriptions of an uncompleted coast; the Second Hearth begins from one household memory, becomes a city through public citizenship, and dies when elites try to reverse that development.
- Any later resemblance comes through refugees, legal sayings, salvaged shards, or independent rediscovery. Neither canonical realm is secretly the Second Hearth renamed.

### Review disposition

`revise` — preserve the founder's estrangement, constructed refuge, copied-relative crisis, civic struggle, exclusion deaths, and Scattering afterlife, but make the city a lost early polity whose mature law and later collapse distinguish it from Chalkhaven and the Unfinished Shore. Promotion remains locked.

## PR-P03 — The Port of Borrowed Standing

### Historical placement

- **Era:** the Long Frontier, provisionally c. 680–850 AR, during translation-house, water-court, caravan, and border coexistence.
- **Material interface:** a route accessible through several Long Frontier cities but sovereign to none of them.
- **Scale:** treaty port, route republic, and neutral market whose law protects exchange more reliably than people.
- **Carrier:** compact clauses, multilingual witness seals, extradition precedents, port slang, and a fragmentary citizenship roll.

### Transformation formula

- **Humble origin:** interpreters, ferrymen, Material witnesses, caravan factors, and stranded servants establish a safe quay after stronger Patrons repeatedly redirect arrivals into private custody.
- **Scriptural lens:** the command to protect the stranger and maintain honest judgement, tested against the repeated biblical failure to give foreigners, debtors, widows, and captives equal standing. Law must be judged by those it leaves outside the gate.
- **Comparative grammar:** neutral portal city, dangerous hospitality, merchant republic, loophole compact, and rival supernatural owners forced into procedural peace.
- **Historical pressure:** Long Frontier trade, mixed households, pilgrimage, translation, ransom, coastal raiding, slavery, theological rivalry, and governments that need deniable access to forbidden routes.
- **World Egg torque:** human Material testimony can witness across continuities that many Patron proxies cannot. This makes human witnesses valuable officials, corrupt brokers, assassination targets, and living points of jurisdiction.
- **Contested residue:** later lawyers remember the port as proof that enemies can share law; survivors remember how often the compact defined them as cargo.

### Political anatomy

- **Owners:** several Patrons contribute mutually vulnerable route knots and anchor shares. None owns the complete port.
- **Government:** magistrates chosen from human residents, Driftkin houses, proxy embassies, dock guilds, and anchor contributors under a compact of limited standing.
- **Inhabitants:** citizens by contribution or service, licensed residents, temporary refugees, pilgrims, foreign factors, translators, servants, enslaved cargo, criminals, spies, and unregistered arrivals.
- **Continuity / classification:** every human resident, witness, captive, criminal, and unregistered arrival has a complete `human-soul`; Patrons, proxies, Driftkin, offices, compact mechanisms, cargo records, and districts are separately classified.
- **Rent constitution:** docking mass, witness labor, translation, repair, passage levy, confiscated contraband, and unequal neighborhood upkeep.
- **Route policy:** seizure requires recognized standing inside the port, but owners manipulate categories of citizen, tenant, dependant, cargo, envoy, copy, and fugitive.
- **External politics:** Material rulers use the port for ransom, back-channel diplomacy, relic traffic, intelligence, and transactions their public law forbids. Patron embassies finance rival street factions.

### Hard event and failure

One compact crisis begins when a raiding factor registers kidnapped people as salvaged cargo. Magistrates know the classification is fraudulent but fear that voiding it will cause an anchor contributor to withdraw. A rescue attempt becomes a dock battle; captives, guards, bystanders, and witnesses are killed. Dependants seized just beyond the declared gate receive no remedy. Riots target foreign households rather than the owners who manufactured the crisis.

The port survives that episode but later loses one anchor share. Whole districts slide toward the nearest remaining owner, carrying residents into new tenancy without individual consent. The republic may persist as a smaller polity, divide into several ports, or close; the placement does not yet choose.

### Present-day traces and hooks

- Long Frontier contract schools quote mutually inconsistent clauses from the compact;
- Vortharan jurists may have borrowed witness procedures without inheriting the port or founding it;
- a surviving port fragment, current, or embassy claims that descendants of displaced citizens still owe rent;
- one register records people as cargo in one language and citizens in another;
- reopening a route could revive both the market and old extradition claims.

### Known, open, and forbidden

- **Authorially known for the nursery:** the port was genuinely plural; humans were unusually useful witnesses; neutrality failed captives; a partial anchor withdrawal displaced inhabitants.
- **Open:** location, names, participating Material cultures, founders, relationship to Vorthara, survival, and which communities later remember it.
- **Forbidden:** making the Long Frontier a utopia or uninterrupted oppression; assigning one culture collective guilt; making the port the secret founder of every later Conclave; allowing contract to own a human soul.

### Source-fidelity ruling

- **Received source:** PT-R01; Fargrave's portal-hub and Stricture grammar; Dreamlands dangerous ports; biblical stranger-law and judicial corruption.
- **Borrowed element:** a neutral supernatural trade city whose procedural peace enables commerce while rivals exploit legal categories.
- **Original hard edge:** kidnapping, slavery, proxy murder, corrupt classification, unequal protection, and bodily danger outside the gate.
- **Current state:** `intact`.
- **Restoration:** none. Captivity, deaths, displacement, and institutional complicity remain explicit.
- **Historical layer:** competing commercial and survivor archives over an authorially real compact crisis.

### Review disposition

`retain` — preserve the plural compact, indispensable mortal witnesses, legal classification of captives as cargo, dock killings, partial anchor withdrawal, and displaced districts while leaving every owner, access city, later fragment, and relationship to Vorthara provisional. Promotion remains locked.

## PR-P04 — The Treasury of the Unreturned

### Historical placement

- **Era:** Fall of Caleran and early Reconquest, provisionally c. 905–970 AR.
- **Material interface:** refugee routes from Caleran, coastal estates, eastern ports, and defeated provincial courts.
- **Scale:** an exiled Patron court that becomes a treasury, shelter, remittance house, and political machine for return.
- **Carrier:** return registries, mortgage tablets, portable palace rooms, remittance customs, and rival property titles.

### Transformation formula

- **Humble origin:** displaced clerks, servants, porters, widows, children, and minor officials pool portable goods around an already exiled Anchor-Lord because no Material ruler can guarantee the next night.
- **Scriptural lens:** Lamentations and Baruch's grief for a fallen city joined to Maccabean resistance, Ezra's return registers, and the prophetic judgement of wealthy cities that turn people into merchandise. Exile can preserve a people and still create a predatory elite.
- **Comparative grammar:** Alakā's displaced wealth, refugee court, portable capital, reconquest treasury, and absentee return claimant.
- **Historical pressure:** the 905 AR collapse, confiscation, occupation settlement, flight, mountain rebellion, competing reconquest titles, and the difference between restoring rule and restoring every prior property relation.
- **World Egg torque:** portable wealth and verified titles provide anchor mass. The realm grows stronger when claimants repeat that every treasure, servant, and room is only temporarily displaced and must someday return.
- **Contested residue:** restoration histories call it the Treasury of Return; descendants who never saw Caleran call it the City That Mortgaged Home.

### Political anatomy

- **Owner:** an Anchor-Lord or institutional Patron displaced from a Material tether during the Fall. It is not an Atherian emperor's soul, a universal genius of Atheria, or necessarily aligned with the Church.
- **Government:** an exiled household, creditor council, refugee guilds, military claimants, and registry judges.
- **Inhabitants:** wealthy claimants, landless refugees, servants, craftspeople, soldiers, debtors, children born after exile, captured occupation records, constructs, and hired Driftkin.
- **Continuity / classification:** every human claimant, servant, debtor, child, soldier, refugee, collaborator, and occupation-born resident has a complete `human-soul`; the Patron, constructs, registers, offices, portable rooms, and Driftkin remain distinct continuities.
- **Rent constitution:** portable treasure, document verification, remittances, labor quotas, inherited return claims, and the repeated designation of residents and objects as assets awaiting restoration.
- **External politics:** it finances raids, ransoms, archives, and rival return armies; buys information from occupation families; and competes with Material refugee governments that resent its power but need its credit.

### Hard event and succession crisis

In the first years the shelter saves thousands. As upkeep rises, its government converts admission into debt and debt into inherited service. Poor refugees repair palaces built from the goods of richer exiles. Dependants are pledged as collateral. A failed Material expedition leaves wounded soldiers and collaborating local families outside a closing gate so that treasury officers and title chests can enter. Later rulers erase this choice from the official Return Roll.

When parts of the homeland are retaken, the court presents old registers as claims to houses now occupied by survivors, occupation-born families, garrison descendants, monasteries, and freed laborers. Some residents leave to enforce return; others refuse to abandon the only city they know. The owner discovers that permanent exile supplies more reliable rent than successful restoration.

### Present-day traces and hooks

- rival registers still shape lawsuits arising from the Fall and Long Reconquest;
- one portable palace room appears at auctions whenever a major restoration claimant dies;
- descendants of servants argue that generations of upkeep bought citizenship, while creditor houses call them inherited staff;
- a remittance road may still fund political factions in Material successor states;
- the realm may have returned, divided, fossilized into ceremony, or become an absent court's first abandoned city.

### Known, open, and forbidden

- **Authorially known for the nursery:** the refuge saved lives; its economy became coercive; some dependants were treated as assets; old title did not erase later inhabitants' real claims; the owner profited from prolonged exile.
- **Open:** owner's species and name, exact refugees, relationship to Caleran, current survival, role in the Reconquest, and whether any restitution occurred.
- **Forbidden:** making it the secret government of the Reconquest or cause of the Split; erasing occupation-era families; making one register infallible; treating wealth as proof of providential favor.

### Source-fidelity ruling

- **Received source:** PT-R10R; Alakā's displaced-wealth grammar without conflation with Laṅkā; Lamentations, Baruch, Ezra, Maccabean resistance, and prophetic merchant-city critique.
- **Borrowed element:** a splendid city of displaced wealth whose identity depends upon loss, guarded abundance, and imagined return.
- **Original hard edge:** dispossession, kin and succession conflict, exile, coercive wealth, unequal refuge, and violence committed in the name of restoration.
- **Current state:** `intact`.
- **Restoration:** none. Debt service, collateralized dependants, abandoned wounded, and predatory return claims remain consequential.
- **Historical layer:** hostile return registers, household chronicles, and refugee testimony around a real shelter economy.

### Review disposition

`retain` — preserve the refuge that genuinely saves people and then converts survival into inherited service, restoration finance, and contested property claims. It may influence particular reconquest actors but neither directs the Reconquest nor causes the Great Split. Promotion remains locked pending the dedicated Alakā control and local history review.

## PR-P05 — The Palace of the Short Mercy

### Historical placement

- **Era:** the Grey Sweat, 1232–1247 AR, with legal consequences extending into 1360 AR.
- **Material interface:** one afflicted port, river crossing, monastery hospice, or plague road in Eclessia; final locality remains open.
- **Scale:** a sincere emergency refuge whose different passage of time saves bodies while destroying ordinary continuity of life.
- **Carrier:** sealed gifts, unchanged returnees, inheritance cases, asylum customs, and contradictory plague registers.

### Transformation formula

- **Humble origin:** a physician, ferryman, laundress, parish clerk, or grieving household discovers that an old hospitality route still opens when a guest carries a particular gift and asks for only three nights.
- **Scriptural lens:** Tobit's road, burial piety, household separation and return; Ecclesiastes' seasons and the inability to master time; Noahic refuge stripped of any claim that all outside were condemned.
- **Comparative grammar:** Ryūgū and Urashima's sincere otherworld hospitality, altered time, sealed gift, and devastating return, transformed into mass refuge and institutional aftermath.
- **Historical pressure:** recurring plague waves, corpse backlog, broken registers, quarantine violence, inheritance conflict, and desperate flight from towns that cannot bury their dead.
- **World Egg torque:** the palace's anchor folds sequence. Guests experience days while months or years pass outside. It preserves bodies from exposure but does not heal plague, stop contagion, judge worthiness, or cause the Quiet Ninth.
- **Contested residue:** survivors call it the Three-Night Mercy; heirs who aged without them call it the Theft of Years.

### Political anatomy

- **Owner:** one Anchor-Lord whose hospitality is genuine by the palace's law. Its species, history, and understanding of Material grief remain open.
- **Government:** household stewards, guest-law offices, Material interpreters, and an emergency admission committee formed when arrivals multiply.
- **Inhabitants:** the owner and court; old guests who never returned; plague refugees; servants; children conceived or born during refuge; constructs; and people whose legal identity expires outside while their bodies scarcely age.
- **Continuity / classification:** every human guest, refugee, servant, child, returnee, heir, and excluded petitioner has a complete `human-soul`; temporal displacement alters neither judgement nor species, while the Anchor-Lord, constructs, court offices, gifts, and palace remain separately classified.
- **Rent constitution:** appropriate gifts, surrendered sequences of memory, performed stories, household labor, and the metaphysical difference between promised time and experienced time.
- **Route policy:** entry requires invitation and gift; exit remains possible, but the external date cannot be guaranteed. Desperate officials conceal this fact to keep crowds from breaking the gate.
- **External politics:** cities petition for quotas, elites buy places, smugglers traffic invitations, families beg for relatives' return, and authorities debate whether refuge violates quarantine.

### Hard event and aftermath

The palace saves many people who would otherwise die. Admission is unequal. Guards and local officials beat or kill people pressing the gate; wealthy households obtain invitations while plague workers are denied. Some admitted parents return days later to find children dead of age or disease. Some spouses have remarried; holdings have passed to strangers; vows, offices, and debts are disputed. A sealed farewell gift opens into years of withheld grief rather than a weapon or cure.

The owner did not intend these losses, but its stewards understood the asymmetry before all refugees were told. Sincere hospitality does not cancel negligent disclosure, favoritism, or the deaths at admission. Nor does outside resentment make every saved guest culpable.

### Present-day traces and hooks

- a few returnees remain physically younger than grandchildren or legal heirs;
- courts dispute whether a missing person died, deserted, or remained continuously alive in another sequence;
- plague-era property still funds institutions whose title depends upon declaring the refugees dead;
- copies of the original gift circulate, most fraudulent and some capable of opening dangerous near-routes;
- the owner may receive new asylum petitions during war, creating the same consent problem under present conditions.

### Known, open, and forbidden

- **Authorially known for the nursery:** the refuge was real; altered time was real; it saved bodies; stewards concealed or inadequately disclosed the price; deaths occurred at the gate and outside during absence.
- **Open:** owner, locality, numbers, exact time ratios, surviving returnees, whether the route remains open, and responsibility for admission policy.
- **Forbidden:** explaining the Grey Sweat or Quiet Ninth; making refuge a cure, sacrament, divine judgement, or universally available solution; treating temporal loss as a harmless fairy prank.

### Source-fidelity ruling

- **Received source:** PT-R11; specifically attributed Ryūgū/Urashima traditions; Tobit and Ecclesiastes as scriptural lenses.
- **Borrowed element:** otherworld hospitality, a short stay, a sealed gift, and catastrophic temporal separation upon return.
- **Original hard edge:** irreversible loss of home, kin, social identity, and ordinary time despite beautiful and sincere hospitality.
- **Current state:** `intact`.
- **Restoration:** none. The refuge saves lives while causing permanent bereavement, dispossession, and culpable concealment.
- **Historical layer:** plague register, household testimony, legal precedent, and folklore accretion over a real temporal refuge.

### Review disposition

`retain` — preserve the plague asylum, sincere but culpably undisclosed hospitality, unequal admission, deaths at the gate, temporal bereavement, and later inheritance crisis while refusing any causal relationship to the plague or Quiet Ninth. Promotion remains locked pending the dedicated Japanese source and locality review.

## PR-P06 — The Petition of the Nameless Castellan

### Historical placement

- **Era:** active pressure in 1360 AR, with an unnamed earlier siege as its disputed foundation.
- **Material interface:** Vortharan Conclave, [[Weighmoot]], one breachward order, and several families claiming descent from gatekeepers; none automatically owns the fortress.
- **Scale:** a succession and recognition crisis in a fortress-state built from name custody.
- **Carrier:** erased veterans, contested name-restoration rites, kidnapped gatekeeper descendants, sealed registers, and a petition for external recognition.

### Transformation formula

- **Humble origin:** a clerk or descendant asks a simple legal question—who may speak for a fortress when its surviving defenders erased the names that proved they were citizens?
- **Scriptural lens:** Ezra and Nehemiah's restoration registers, Daniel's imposed court names, and the Gospel warning that public recognition and the true person are not identical. Registers protect communities and can also exclude the people whose sacrifice preserved them.
- **Comparative grammar:** name-fortress, besieged archive, self-erasing defenders, disputed succession, and present cult or relic audit.
- **Historical pressure:** renewed planar traffic, Conclave recognition politics, old siege debts, missing title evidence, demand for safe routes, and institutions tempted to treat vulnerable people as keys.
- **World Egg torque:** a true name grants a bounded handle over particular routes, manifestations, offices, or proxies. It is never ownership of a human soul or the total content of a person.
- **Contested residue:** the fortress says its citizens died when they erased themselves; nameless districts insist that surviving without archives is not death.

### Political anatomy

- **Owner:** unknown and disputed. Candidates include the prior Patron, an archive institution, a successor purchaser, the fortress anchor itself under bounded office law, or no presently competent owner.
- **Speaker:** a proposed castellan whose authority may derive from office, proxy appointment, copied register, conquest, inheritance, or fraud.
- **Government:** archive council, military offices, gatekeeper households, self-erased veteran districts, creditors, and a claimant regency.
- **Inhabitants:** named citizens, unregistered residents, nameless defenders, prisoners, dependants, archivists, constructs, seized gatekeepers, and descendants whose inherited aliases can trigger old wards.
- **Continuity / classification:** every human citizen, nameless defender, prisoner, gatekeeper, dependant, and descendant has a complete `human-soul`; names, aliases, offices, wards, constructs, proxies, archives, and the owner remain distinct bounded continuities or handles.
- **Rent constitution:** transcription, witness, surrendered aliases, memory substrate, dangerous verification, and service by families whose names were once posted as collateral.
- **External politics:** the Conclave wants a recognized signatory; the Weighmoot wants continuity evidence; rivals want stored routes; Material orders want prisoners or relics extradited; citizens want protection before title is settled.

### Present crisis

The petitioner seeks recognition for a castellan and threatens that delay will cause the wards to fail. Evidence suggests the old defenders erased their biographies and civic names during a siege so attackers could not torture them into opening successive gates. They survived, but the fortress ceased protecting people the infrastructure could not identify. Children disappeared from censuses, veterans lost homes, and debt collectors later seized unregistered families.

Now gatekeeper descendants are being kidnapped and tortured for aliases. Restoring every erased name might reopen captured routes, reactivate obsolete commands, attach offices to copies, or expose Material relatives. Destroying the archive might free collateral names while collapsing the wards that sustain entire districts. Recognizing the claimant could avert immediate failure or hand every stored handle to a fraud.

### Present-day choices without predetermined outcomes

- prove or disprove the castellan's bounded authority without deciding who is morally worthy to rule;
- restore selected citizens while protecting them from creditors and old commands;
- bargain with the Conclave, Weighmoot, inhabitants, or rival Patrons for temporary recognition;
- rescue gatekeeper descendants without treating their bodies or memories as tools;
- preserve, divide, evacuate, reform, sell, or destroy parts of the archive, each with known and unknown costs.

### Known, open, and forbidden

- **Authorially known for the nursery:** the old siege and self-erasure were real; torture was used against gatekeepers; civic exclusion harmed surviving defenders and families; names are bounded handles rather than souls.
- **Open under the Forward Rule:** claimant's truth, current owner, correct legal remedy, archive condition, who attacks, whether wards fail, and every outcome of player intervention.
- **Forbidden:** a predetermined claimant victory; a hidden master plot resolving all Held Proofs; total control of a person through one name; promotion of a new universal metaphysical rule through the case.

### Source-fidelity ruling

- **Received source:** PT-R04; TES Bastion Nymic's name-fortress and gatekeeper grammar; Ezra-Nehemiah register and Danielic renaming lenses.
- **Borrowed element:** a fortress protected through true-name custody, attacked through captive gatekeepers, and destabilized by the destruction of identity evidence.
- **Original hard edge:** torture, captivity, self-erasure, loss of family and civic identity, and victory that can destroy the people it preserves.
- **Current state:** `intact`.
- **Restoration:** none. Captive use, bodily coercion, institutional abandonment, and irreversible identity loss remain central.
- **Historical layer:** present legal petition over an authorially real siege, with claimant propaganda and damaged archives.

### Review disposition

`retain` — preserve the old siege, tortured gatekeepers, self-erased defenders, civic exclusion, disputed castellan, unstable archive, and bounded name law while leaving the claimant, owner, remedy, archive fate, and every campaign consequence open under the Forward Rule. Promotion remains locked.
