# Southern Peoples, Polar Giants and Grobi — Comparative Research for Package D

## Purpose and principal recommendation

This study asks how Package D can develop the [[The Folk Overview|Folk]], the [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Ättar/Ättar Overview.md|Ättar]] and the [[Grobi Overview|Grobi]] from the Great Unbinding to 350 BR. It compares several historical and fictional bodies of material without treating any of them as a complete model for a World Egg people.

The strongest arrangement is a **southern family of related peoples with four historical constellations**:

1. **Western and Atlantic Folk**, drawing upon continental Gaulish, Brittonic and Goidelic histories while preserving distinctions among them.
2. **Middle Forest Folk**, drawing upon continental Germanic farming, assembly, retinue and frontier societies.
3. **Eastern River and Upland Folk**, drawing upon several early Slavic developments, including forest-river settlement, migration, fortified centres and Baltic-coast cult polities.
4. **Ättar**, a related far-cold maritime people whose history passes through farm-and-hall, heroic, sea-king and great keel-taking phases rather than beginning as a finished Viking society.

These constellations should be related through old southern languages, repeated migration and shared experience of Giant rule. They should never be presented as four pure biological stocks. Households, captives, fosterlings, spouses and refugees repeatedly move between them. The word **Folk** remains an outside umbrella and a useful modern collective term. Most people within the ancient history identify themselves by river, hill, kindred, hall, island or coalition.

The southern polar Giant state should be developed as a **penal crownland formed from several Giant houses**, not as a sixth Imperial Horizon. Six principal ring-fortresses govern a larger chain of mine prisons, labour camps, road towers, hostage houses and harbour compounds. The Imperial Horizons and numerous lesser Giant crowns use it, with southern cold-country states supplying much of its ordinary prison population. Its Key Houses are privileged hereditary prisoners who serve as gaolers over people confined beneath them. The Great Unbinding turns the crownland into a civil war among gaolers, condemned dynasts and subject districts. Human uprisings help destroy it. Some lesser Giants and giant-blooded human prisoners become warband leaders and enter older southern populations through marriage, concubinage and conquest. This spreads inherited strength, hardiness and magical potential through later Folk and Ättar populations without making either people a Giant lineage or the product of one prison system.

Wrath should plan and direct its intervention in that collapse. A dependent Red Reckoner enters Kharvos as a Xolotlan casualty clerk, alters Nashara's experimental regimen and prepares a trapped human population through hunger, forced combat, consumption and the final opening of the grain vault. The Giant authorities intend to create obedient soldiers for their civil war; Wrath intends a self-reproducing army able to fill the military vacuum left by falling Giant states. Giant craft cannot create Grobi continuity. Wrath performs the categorical transformation: the victims' human souls separate and route towards judgement, while Furor rewrites the vacated bodies around growth through food, regeneration and predatory dominance.

The Grobi should keep their five food-driven stages, winter migrations, intelligence, suffering and relationship to Wrath. Author review rejects full moral agency for the lineage. Grobi are cunning social predators whose choices concern leader, route, victim, bargain and tactic; domination, fighting and consumption remain their creaturely ends. Warhammer contributes several useful structures: size and status reinforce one another; a successful war-host attracts specialised roles and feeds further growth; mass excitement can have dangerous supernatural effects; and a slave-warrior project can turn upon its makers. Fungal spores, genetically implanted engineering, a species-wide mind and the internet caricature that Ork belief makes any machine work would conflict with the setting and should not be imported.

## Method and evidentiary limits

The historical comparisons cover long periods and broad modern labels. “Celtic”, “Germanic”, “Slavic” and “Norse” are not four uniform ancient nations. They describe combinations of language, archaeological classification, outsider naming and later scholarly practice. The evidence is uneven. Roman writers described enemies and neighbours through Roman political and moral concerns. Early Irish and Welsh heroic literature was written down centuries after the societies it depicts. Byzantine authors supplied some of the earliest written descriptions of Slavs. *Beowulf* survives in an English manuscript while setting its action in a remembered Scandinavian world.

Material culture helps correct literary simplification, but pots, weapons and burial customs do not identify ethnicity by themselves. Recent ancient-DNA work has added important evidence about early medieval population movement. It cannot tell us what every person called themselves or believed. The 2025 study of Slavic-period populations, for example, supports large-scale movement from eastern Europe between the sixth and eighth centuries while also finding sharply different local patterns of mixture and assimilation.[^slavic-dna]

The game settings require a second distinction. Bethesda and ZeniMax present Elder Scrolls history through fallible in-world accounts as well as direct play. Games Workshop's Fantasy, Age of Sigmar and Warhammer 40,000 settings share creative themes without sharing one continuity. Pathfinder's Kaer Maga is a particular city with its own mysteries. The World Egg should borrow functions and tensions while leaving names, complete plots and metaphysics behind.

## I. The Celtic comparison: several worlds under one modern name

### Continental societies: Gaul and the central European Iron Age

The archaeological names **Hallstatt** and **La Tène** describe changing material cultures across parts of central and western Europe. Hallstatt communities included farming territories, fortified centres, long-distance exchange and exceptionally rich burials. La Tène art and objects travelled across a much wider area through trade, migration, patronage and war. Neither label identifies one state or proves that every bearer belonged to one self-conscious Celtic nation. The British Museum's collection definitions are useful summaries of the material categories, while Peter Wells's comparison of late Iron Age “Celts” and “Germans” shows how poorly Caesar's clean Rhine division matches many archaeological continuities.[^hallstatt][^latene][^wells]

Late Iron Age Gaul contained farms, villages, sanctuaries, mines, river traffic, coin-using economies and **oppida**, large fortified settlements which could serve political, manufacturing and exchange functions. Some regions supported complex urbanising societies before Roman conquest. Other districts remained organised around smaller centres and dispersed rural households. Roman villas did not simply replace a countryside without settled agriculture; archaeological work in Gaul shows continuity, appropriation and regional change beneath the victorious literary account.[^central-gaul][^gaul-farms]

Political life joined aristocratic households, clients, assemblies, priestly or learned specialists and temporary coalitions. Wealth came from land, herds, metal, tolls, gifts, captives and service. Caesar's account of Druids is indispensable and interested: he was explaining conquered societies to Roman readers and justifying command. It supports the presence of learned religious specialists in parts of Gaul, including training, judgement and sacrifice, but does not establish one identical Druidic church across all Celtic-speaking lands.[^caesar-druids]

The hard edge belongs in the World Egg translation. Raiding and captivity supplied wealth and labour. Bodies and weapons were displayed or deposited. Ancient sources report human sacrifice, while archaeology demonstrates ritualised bodily violence and head-taking in particular places without validating every hostile Roman claim.[^ritual-violence][^iron-age-captivity] A Folk culture drawing from Gaul therefore needs cultivated country, workshops, wealth and political argument alongside feud, captive-taking and terrifying sacred acts.

### Brittonic worlds: Britain, Wales and Cornwall

Brittonic-speaking communities did not form a single political people. Landscapes contained farmsteads, hillforts, lowland settlements, mines and maritime connections. In Wales, later-prehistoric archaeology shows continuing settlement and craft rather than a timeless population of forest mystics. Roman conquest altered roads, forts, towns, extraction and elite power unevenly; military occupation was intense in some districts, while households elsewhere adapted to new markets and obligations.[^museum-wales][^cadw-prehistory][^cadw-roman]

The Welsh and Cornish comparison becomes most useful after Rome. Brittonic languages and regional kingdoms survive imperial withdrawal, warfare and the growth of Anglo-Saxon polities. Later Welsh prose preserves tales collected as the *Mabinogion*: transformations, dangerous otherworld journeys, kin conflict, mutilation, sovereignty and disastrous honour all appear within literary works whose surviving manuscripts are medieval.[^mabinogion] These are excellent grammars for Folk–Menhir stories, provided the tale's medieval form is not projected backwards as a transcript of Iron Age religion.

For World Egg purposes, the Brittonic strand favours defensible uplands, mining districts, cattle and mixed farming, bardic memory, small kingdoms and border identities capable of surviving several imperial languages. It also supports humour, riddling, grotesque bodily change and rulers whose insult or broken hospitality damages the land around them.

### Goidelic and northern worlds: Ireland and Scotland

Early Irish society is unusually valuable because law tracts, saints' Lives, annals, genealogies and heroic literature preserve different kinds of evidence. These sources describe ranked households, honour-price, fosterage, cattle wealth, clients, learned professions and several forms of marriage and dependency. Their surviving texts belong to Christian manuscript culture and often combine inherited custom with the concerns of the writers who recorded them. Research into rank and marriage, fosterage and the *Lex Innocentium* shows a society in which relationship created enforceable duties while status profoundly altered the value and protection available to a person.[^irish-rank][^fosterage][^lex-innocentium]

The *Táin Bó Cúailnge* supplies a heroic cattle-war on an enormous scale. Cú Chulainn's individual defence, battle-frenzy, foster relationships, taunts and killings drive the action; Medb's desire for equal wealth begins a war that destroys lives far beyond the disputed bull. The text's written form participates in medieval Irish literary culture and dialogue with classical epic.[^tain] Package D can use its combination of personal prowess, cattle economy, foster kinship and catastrophic prestige competition without creating a World Egg Cú Chulainn by substitution.

Ancient and early medieval Scotland contained Picts, Britons, Gaels and later Norse settlers among other communities. Dunadd illustrates an early medieval royal centre connected to craft, ceremony and maritime movement, while National Museums Scotland's research emphasises regional transformations rather than a single unchanging “Scottish Celt”.[^dunadd][^early-scotland] A Scottish-influenced Folk strand can therefore contain island lordships, western sea roads, Pictish-like symbol traditions, Gaelic-speaking newcomers and Brittonic neighbours. Their later mixture belongs inside the history.

### What the Celtic comparison gives the Folk

The western and Atlantic Folk should contain at least three overlapping cultural provinces rather than one pseudo-Celtic nation:

- a **continental river-and-hill world** of farms, oppida-like strongholds, coin, cattle, war coalitions and learned houses;
- a **Brittonic upland-and-estuary world** of smaller kingdoms, mining, bardic houses, border warfare and enduring local speech;
- a **Goidelic and island world** of fosterage, cattle wealth, sea roads, ranked hospitality, heroic praise and damaging kin obligation.

Enaidfae seers may inherit some Druidic and other learned functions, but their Menhir-facing office must grow from World Egg history. Druid becomes a comparative term, never the automatic name for every Folk priest. Sacred groves, votive deposits, severed heads and human sacrifice may occur in particular old-rite societies where the relevant history establishes them. They are not universal cultural properties.

## II. Continental Germanic societies: farms, followings and frontier coalitions

### A name made broad by outsiders

Roman authors used *Germani* for numerous communities east of the Rhine and for selected peoples elsewhere. Language, archaeology and political allegiance do not produce identical maps. Coalitions appeared, divided and adopted new names. Migration-period identities could gather survivors, clients and captives around successful ruling houses. The Cambridge histories describe varied Germanic peoples and societies rather than a single tribal constitution.[^germanic-peoples][^germanic-society]

This comparison is especially helpful for the Middle Forest Folk. Ancient self-identification should ordinarily begin with a local people or coalition. A victorious league may lend its name to unrelated followers. Defeated households may keep their speech while entering another king's following. The later umbrella remains historically real without becoming pure descent.

### Household, assembly and retinue

Much of the Germanic-speaking countryside was agricultural. Longhouses joined people, animals, storage and productive work. Fields, pasture, woodland, iron production and exchange sustained chiefs and warriors. Political scale varied from local assemblies to temporary war leaders and more durable kings. A ruler's following depended on gifts, food, captured wealth, marriage and repeated success.

Tacitus describes assemblies, chiefs, armed young men, retinues, compensation and hospitality. His *Germania* is Roman moral ethnography written after generations of contact; it contrasts Roman vice with selected virtues and dangers attributed to northern peoples. It remains useful when read as an interested literary witness rather than a neutral handbook.[^tacitus]

**Weregild** and related compensation systems can deepen existing Folk feud-law. They put a price upon killing or injury in order to interrupt retaliation, but rank, freedom, kin support and political power affect whose injury receives payment. Compensation can end a feud, expose inequality or fail because one household refuses the value placed upon its dead. Assemblies likewise judge, bargain, intimidate and execute. They do not automatically produce equality.

Warrior followings create a different relationship from kinship. Young fighters may attach themselves to a successful leader for weapons, meals, gifts and reputation. Such a band can protect a frontier, overthrow a rival, extort its hosts or abandon a failed patron. The Middle Forest Folk should draw upon this interaction among household land, public assembly and personal warband rather than reduce “Germanic culture” to honour.

### Rome beyond the Rhine

Rome conquered Gaul and much of Britain while failing to retain a comparable province across most of Germania. The difference does not demonstrate innate Germanic superiority. Permanent occupation beyond the Rhine promised fewer established tax centres and supply networks, demanded long movement through forest, river and wet ground, and faced political societies able to disperse or form new coalitions. Roman decisions about cost and strategic priority mattered. Roman armies continued to campaign and win beyond the river after the Varus disaster; Kalkriese nevertheless became the material and memorial centre of a defeat which constrained imperial ambition.[^kalkriese][^roman-frontier]

This is a strong model for uneven Atherian expansion. Atherian commanders can conquer some Folk towns, recruit local rivals and hold river corridors. Occupying every dispersed farm and winter refuge brings poor revenue and constant losses. Forest coalitions need not win every battle to survive the empire. Their own political fragmentation can obstruct both conquest and sustained resistance.

### Sacred violence and defeated armies

Wetland deposits in Denmark include captured weapons deliberately damaged and offered after victories. The finds show organised collection and ritual destruction, not discarded battlefield rubbish.[^weapon-sacrifice] Alken Enge preserves the remains of defeated fighters subjected to post-mortem treatment and deposition, although the precise sequence and meaning remain debated.[^alken]

The Middle Forest Folk can inherit victory bogs, broken weapons, enemy standards sunk beyond recovery and assemblies held beside dangerous water. Some rites may include killing. Their neighbours can fear these customs without becoming reliable interpreters of them. The most memorable site should emerge from one war and one community rather than a universal Germanic appetite for sacrifice.

## III. Slavic comparisons: river settlement, migration and new political centres

### The problem of projecting modern nations backwards

“Russian Slavs”, “Baltic Slavs” and “eastern European Slavs” refer to several different histories. Early Slavic languages formed one branch of the Balto-Slavic language family; Baltic languages such as Lithuanian and Latvian belong to a related but distinct branch.[^balto-slavic] **Baltic Slavs** should therefore mean Slavic-speaking populations along the Baltic coast and lower Elbe, including Polabian and Pomeranian societies. It should not turn Baltic peoples into Slavs.

East, West and South Slavic identities developed through migration, neighbouring empires, Christian missions, trade and new states. Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Czech, Serbian, Croatian, Bulgarian and other later national identities cannot be treated as ancient tribes waiting to receive their modern names.

### Movement and settlement

Sixth-century Byzantine writers describe *Sclavenes* and *Antes* north of the Danube and during raids or settlements in imperial territory. Their accounts reflect frontier war and imperial categories. Archaeology identifies broad similarities in handmade pottery, houses and cremation practice, although these assemblages cannot by themselves tell us that every settlement possessed the same identity.

The 2025 ancient-DNA study materially changes the older debate. Across its sampled regions, it finds substantial population movement from eastern Europe in the sixth to eighth centuries, accompanied by local variation and assimilation. The result argues against explaining the entire spread through elite language change alone; it also rejects a single uniform replacement model for every district.[^slavic-dna]

Early settlements often occupied river valleys and mixed agricultural zones. Timber buildings, sunken-featured structures, ovens, fields, livestock, fishing and woodland products supported households. Fortified **gords** and larger political centres became more prominent under the pressure of tribute, trade and war. Some regions developed strong princely dynasties; others remained organised through local leaders and assemblies for longer.

This is useful for the Eastern River Folk. Their early history can begin among mobile refugee clusters and small river settlements, then produce timber-earth fortifications, market landings and confederacies. Their lack of stone capitals is a material and historical choice, not evidence that they never developed government or specialised craft.

### Baltic-coast Slavs and religious centres

Polabian and Baltic-coast Slavic societies developed among Frankish, Saxon, Danish and other neighbours. Warfare, tribute, conversion, revolt and maritime trade shaped their political worlds. Particular centres maintained temples and priestly establishments of regional importance. Christian accounts of those cults were written by ideological enemies and require care, but they support a useful World Egg possibility: one Eastern Folk federation may give a local Menhir or Idol-associated cult unusual public and military power without defining every eastern clan's religion.[^polabian][^polabian-christianity]

Evidence for pre-Christian Slavic religion is fragmentary and regionally uneven. Later folklore and reconstructed pantheons cannot fill every gap. The so-called *Book of Veles* is a modern forgery and should supply nothing to Package D. Byliny, the East Slavic heroic songs, survive in late recordings and transmit medieval themes through long oral and literary development; they are valuable for frontier champions, princely feasts, merchants, monsters and ambiguous strongmen when labelled accordingly.[^slavic-religion][^byliny]

### Rivers between the Baltic and eastern empires

The later Rus' world shows how Scandinavian voyagers, Slavic communities, Finnic peoples, steppe powers and Byzantine markets could interact along river routes. The Cambridge accounts emphasise trade in furs, silver and slaves, contested princely origins and the gradual creation of new political centres.[^rus][^viking-expansion] This history can link the Ättar and Eastern River Folk without making one the origin of the other. Ättar crews may establish seasonal stations, enter local service, marry and seize tribute. Eastern communities may absorb the newcomers, retain distinct rulers or send their own merchants to the cold sea.

### What the Slavic comparison gives the Folk

The Eastern River and Upland Folk should be a cluster of related societies whose history values:

- river landings, ovens, timber villages and mixed farming;
- forest clearance, beekeeping, fishing and iron-working;
- household migration and the absorption of older local populations;
- timber-and-earth gords, assemblies and changing princely followings;
- conflict with steppe, Atherian, Ättar and Grobi neighbours;
- a few powerful local cult centres alongside many household and place traditions;
- epic strongmen and princely feasts remembered through later songs whose historical layers remain visible.

The mountain communities already present in proto-Thalmyric history may share language and ancestry with one Eastern Folk branch. That relationship requires a deliberate Package D decision. The safe preliminary ruling is **old southern kinship with later separation**, not one people split solely by an Atherian border.

## IV. The Ättar comparison: from heroic halls to sea-kings

### A long northern sequence

The word **Viking** describes an activity and, in some sources, a person engaged in raiding; modern usage often expands it to whole Scandinavian societies. Package D should avoid placing a fully formed Viking Age at the beginning of Ättar history. A more convincing development draws separately upon the Nordic Bronze Age, Roman and Germanic Iron Ages, Migration and Vendel periods, then the Viking Age.

The earliest Ättar ancestors can be farmers, fishers, hunters, herders, metalworkers and boat-builders living within the wider southern family. Coastal chiefs gain prestige through imported metal, feasting and seasonal travel. Great halls become centres of storage, craft and armed followings. Improved sails, keels, navigation, portable wealth and foreign weakness later make long-distance trade, settlement and raiding profitable. Recent scholarship treats the early Viking Age as a process with earlier roots rather than a switch thrown at Lindisfarne.[^early-viking]

### *Beowulf* and the heroic hall

*Beowulf* is an Old English poem surviving in a manuscript of about the year 1000. Its action belongs to a legendary Scandinavian past. The poem therefore reveals an English Christian poet's sustained engagement with remembered northern heroic society, not a transcript of sixth-century ritual.[^beowulf-bl]

Its hall is a political achievement. A king gathers followers, distributes treasure, receives guests and makes protection visible through food and celebration. Grendel's attacks destroy that public order by making the hall unusable. Beowulf wins honour through dangerous service; the poem then follows him into kingship and old age. The final dragon fight brings victory and death together, leaving a people exposed when their protector is gone. Feuds and failed peace marriages continue outside the monster episodes.[^beowulf-feud]

The Ättar already possess the right foundations for this grammar. A hall is granary, assembly, refuge and feast-house. Package D should add the generations which made those halls necessary. The first monster-defending champion need not found a dynasty. The old ruler's last victory should leave a real succession problem instead of proving that heroic strength was sufficient government.

### Farms, ships, work and hierarchy

Viking-age economies depended upon farms, fishing, animal husbandry, textile work, shipbuilding and winter storage. Women could manage households, property and production, although law and political authority remained unequal and varied by region. Magnates maintained halls and followers through land, surplus, gifts and access to voyages.[^viking-social][^viking-women][^viking-magnates]

Trade and raiding belonged to connected maritime systems. Ships carried merchants, settlers, envoys, mercenaries, pilgrims, captives and armies. Silver, cloth, weapons, timber, fish, walrus products and enslaved people travelled through those routes.[^viking-trade][^viking-economy] A single crew could trade at one harbour, extort another and take captives from a third. The Ättar's existing “take the keel” custom should retain that moral and economic ambiguity.

Thralls formed a legally and socially subordinated population. People entered slavery through capture, birth, debt or purchase and could sometimes be freed. The system exposed women and children to sale, forced labour and sexual exploitation. Its importance should remain visible in the Ättar house, field, workshop and ship rather than appearing only as the wicked act of exceptional raiders.[^viking-thralls][^nordic-violence]

### Sacrifice and religious change

The Old Norse *blót* joined feasting, offering and relationships with gods or other powers. Animal sacrifice is well supported. Written accounts and selected archaeological contexts also support human sacrifice, while individual claims vary in reliability.[^viking-blot][^viking-human-sacrifice] Package D can retain sacrificial rulers, captives or household victims in particular old-rite histories. Mother Moon's firstborn bargain already places the seizure of children at the centre of Ättar authority and should remain its most distinctive horror.

Conversion should unfold through rulers, households, trade relationships, missionaries and conflict. A baptised hall may keep its language, law, shipcraft and feast while renouncing sacrifice. Old-rite relatives can regard the decision as betrayal because it ends the pact power and exposes shared defences. Converts may still own slaves or pursue violent expansion. Baptism changes worship and sacramental belonging without erasing every inherited sin or institution.

### Berserkers

Old Norse sources use *berserkr* and related animal-warrior imagery for feared champions. Saga accounts, poetry, names and iconography support a real cultural role or ideal, but they do not yield one agreed medical explanation. The popular claim that berserkers reliably used hallucinogenic mushrooms is speculative and should not control the World Egg version.[^berserker-museum][^berserker-study]

The current Ättar model is stronger than a drug explanation. Berserkers are trained hall champions able to trigger a dangerous Moon-blood rage; toxic iron-skin paint lets them withstand extraordinary injury and may kill them when the rage ends. Research suggests three improvements:

- make the office socially bounded through initiation, compensation obligations and the chief's responsibility for harm done by his champion;
- preserve animal-warrior performance, boasting, biting or striking shields and cultivated terror without making every account literally accurate;
- distinguish the controlled champion from the outlaw who kills kin or neighbours during rage. A hall that celebrates the first must answer for the second.

### Changeling exchange, fairy time and animal form

The expanded Moon bargain draws on three distinct bodies of folklore without copying a single tale. Irish fairy tradition repeatedly describes a human infant being taken and a contrary substitute left in its place. Marion Dowd's archaeological and folklore study connects these stories to identifiable ringforts and other places understood as entrances to the *sí*, while Edwin Sidney Hartland's comparative collection records attempts to expose a changeling and compel the return of the stolen child.[^fairy-archaeology][^science-fairy-tales]

Fairyland stories also treat time as a different order. A mortal may experience a brief visit while years pass outside, or return after long captivity believing little time has elapsed.[^fairy-time] This supports an Ättar exchange in which the changeling grows through a human childhood before returning to the Pale Household, while the original child returns at the same apparent age with discontinuous memory and lunar knowledge. The pattern preserves a real loss: the Court can balance bodies and ages according to its own cycle without restoring the child's uninterrupted childhood.

Medieval and later northern transformation stories often locate animal change in a skin, belt or other worn boundary. Recent scholarship on Old Norse-Icelandic and neighbouring material treats the skin as a central site of movement between human and animal form.[^shapeshifting-skin] The World Egg version keeps a bodily hybrid rather than a removable pelt. The comparison suggests why the greater gift belongs to four named chiefs and their paternal lines: they ask that the animals on their standards enter **their bodies**, so the Pale Household anchors one form to each petitioner and to the descent rule of his office. They separately ask that the animals' fury enter every spear beneath them, so the lesser rage passes through the gathered oath population and later intermarriage. The difference follows the exact grammar of the petition rather than an unexplained dilution of the same power.

### The annual All-Thing

The author review also selects a recurring open-air assembly shared by the Folk constellations and Ättar halls. Þingvellir's historical account distinguishes local district assemblies from the general Alþing established in 930 and records an assembly landscape in which men could attend proceedings while chiefs and designated office-holders occupied the law council.[^thingvellir-settlement][^thingvellir-site][^thingvellir-council] Package D adapts the institutional grammar rather than Iceland's dates or exact constitution. Each connected river, forest, gord or hall country holds its own annual All-Thing. Chiefs and free men settle cross-community disputes, witness succession and agree levies; local assemblies continue during the rest of the year. The gathering is neither a universal southern parliament nor an egalitarian democracy.

## V. Elder Scrolls comparisons

### Reachfolk: clan covenants rather than a single wild religion

The Reachfolk are culturally and politically diverse. Elder Scrolls Online's *Great Spirits of the Reach* states directly that clans maintain many different faiths: one may venerate an animal or spring, another may sacrifice to ancestral ghosts, while several great spirits cross clan boundaries.[^reach-spirits] Their history is shaped by harsh uplands, occupation, clan independence, witch traditions and relationships with Daedric powers. Outside accounts regularly flatten them into savage Daedra worshippers.

The strongest translation concerns some mountain and forest Folk, especially along the Gale-Crown and eastern uplands. Each clan can maintain its own bargains with a Menhir court, Patron or Idol Mask. Neighbouring clans may use similar names for genuinely different beings. A broad old-rite identity can develop without one priesthood or pantheon. Adoption, fosterage and oath can make a person part of a clan even where descent remains politically important.

The Reach comparison should not make the Folk secretly correct nature worshippers. Their powers are real, exacting and morally unreliable. Human sacrifice, predation and coercion remain possible. A clan also possesses agriculture, craft, humour, law and political memory beyond its supernatural bargain.

### Atmorans and later Nords

Atmora functions in Nord memory as an ancestral northern homeland. Migrations occur over time, and later Skyrim Nords form through settlement, conquest, internal conflict and relationships with peoples already present. Dragon worship and the Dragon Cult belong to this deep past. In the received history, priestly rule in Skyrim becomes markedly oppressive and provokes the Dragon War; the resulting society is not simply Atmora preserved under another sky.

This distinction is valuable for the Ättar. The old southern family should not possess every later Ättar institution at the moment of migration. Settlement in the coldest coasts, Grobi war, Mother Moon's bargain and maritime wealth turn related ancestors into a new people. Later skalds can describe their founding generation as already divided into the four pact-kindreds, while archaeology and rival songs show the division emerged afterwards.

Modern Skyrim also demonstrates how a settled northern culture can retain ancient ruins and heroic claims while becoming agrarian, urban, politically divided and involved in an imperial world. The Ättar should reach the same social completeness. They are never only emigrants from a mythic north or enemies appearing over the horizon.

### The ancient Tongues, Vivec and Red Mountain

Elder Scrolls tradition preserves two related stages in the history of the Voice. They should be distinguished. Vivec's *Sermon Nine* describes an earlier northern war against the Chimer and Dwemer through deliberately extravagant sacred history. Five northern chieftains possess individual forms of world-altering speech. Hoaga breathes through earth and fallen bodies; Chemua roars against the sky; Bhag Two-Tongued joins argument, bodily form and battle; Barfok sings events towards a victory she has already named; and Ysmir's voice hurls settlements into the sea. Nerevar and Vivec overcome these powers through equally mythic counteractions.[^sermon-nine]

The sermon is partisan religious literature rather than a neutral campaign record. Its importance lies in the range it gives the ancient Tongues. They are named masters with distinct arts, reputations and limits. Their speech can alter weather, earth, bodies, buildings, morale and magical action. The scale is sometimes enormous. The account does not reduce the tradition to a short catalogue of uniform shouts.

The later disaster at Red Mountain belongs to Jurgen Windcaller's story. Dragons use the Thu'um as an intrinsic language of power, while most human practitioners require prolonged training. Jurgen was a war leader and master of the Voice. After a Nord army was annihilated at Red Mountain, he interpreted the defeat as divine judgement upon the arrogant use of the Voice and founded the contemplative Way of the Voice. *Skyrim* presents this conclusion as Jurgen's received religious interpretation. The defeat therefore creates a reform movement inside an existing magical tradition; it does not establish an authorial rule that military failure proves divine condemnation.[^jurgen] The opponents in these accounts are Chimer and Dwemer. Calling them Dunmer projects a later transformation backwards.

Bethesda's account of the dragon language also makes language, meaning, sound and material inscription central to the Thu'um.[^dragon-language] Together, these sources offer Package D a useful two-stage grammar: an heroic age of competing voice-masters, followed by a catastrophic defeat, an argument over culpability and the division of the art into restrained and martial schools.

### World Egg translation: the war-voice of the Eastern Uplands

The **Eastern River and Upland Folk** should receive this tradition. Its working name is the **war-voice**; individual masters may be called the **Deep-Voiced** until Package D supplies period names. The art gives the eastern constellation a magical history equal in stature to the Ättar's Moon-blood, skinwalking and Iron-Skin champions. It also grows naturally from the setting's resonant account of magic.

War-voice is a regional school of resonance rather than a new source of magic. A trained speaker brings breath, bodily posture, understood words and the resonant properties of a place into deliberate alignment. The human voice serves as the instrument. Most workings address the relationship between Material substance and the Air-and-Earth structures of the Elemental Shell, placing the art close to unaccompanied [[01 Canon Reference/Magic & Systems/40 Rune and Shell Magic/Harmonic Shellcraft.md|Harmonic Shellcraft]]. Local schools may add a Menhir bargain, inherited Giant technique or sacred discipline, but those relationships affect particular lineages and workings rather than the human soul or the general source of the art.

Its earliest vocabulary plausibly develops in the southern Giant crownland. Prison galleries, command vaults, storm horns and stone chambers were built to carry orders through blizzard, mountain and warded air. Human labourers learned which syllables opened vents, steadied bridges, shifted loads or punished a disobedient work gang. During the Unbinding, rebels turned that knowledge against gates, towers and overseers. Communities escaping into the eastern mountains preserved the words through breath exercises, work songs and chambers cut to reveal whether a pupil could hold a tone without losing its meaning.

Package D should allow the ancient masters to be genuinely formidable. Different schools and exceptional speakers may:

- throw warriors from their feet, split shield walls or break a timber gate;
- carry intelligible speech through storm, forest or mountain passes;
- loosen earth, call an avalanche, steady a failing wall or bring one down;
- turn rain to bitter sleet, clear fog from a valley or draw dangerous wind through a pass;
- interrupt another resonant working by finding and voicing its contrary interval;
- bind a frightened company to one cadence long enough to retreat or charge together;
- produce, at the heroic extreme, a physical roar capable of overturning ships or shattering part of a settlement.

These are separate attainments rather than abilities possessed by every practitioner. The most spectacular ancient feats may survive through a mixture of established history, enemy testimony and heroic song. World Egg history can preserve one or two verified works on this scale while leaving other claims disputed. The Voice never restores a dead human soul, makes a predicted victory inevitable or grants authority over a Menhir. A singer who moves a corpse moves matter or a residue; the human person has already entered the soul-road.

Training is long and physical. Pupils learn language, breath, stance, listening and the character of stone, air or water before they attempt force. Great workings may require a prepared chamber, favourable terrain or several trained voices. Failure tears the throat, bursts vessels, cracks teeth, deafens companions and sends force through the wrong wall or hillside. Some masters permanently lose speech. A powerful Voice used among houses, herds or frightened civilians can kill the people it was meant to defend.

This danger gives the tradition a historical life beyond combat. One eastern war can produce famous masters whose feats save settlements and destroy others. A later defeat or civil catastrophe can send one survivor into the high mountains. Sarvek's Quiet Measure should become a small order of Druids praying to Zephyraxis at an exposed summit, closely translating the Jurgen Windcaller silhouette without turning his conclusion into the governing philosophy of every Voice school. Martial houses continue to serve princes; work schools preserve bridge and mine calls; villages retain avalanche or warning tones. Access depends upon training and reception into a school. Descent may influence language and social opportunity, but the art is not a racial faculty.

This placement also gives each southern family a distinct supernatural emphasis. Western and Atlantic Folk retain learned houses, sovereignty rites and dangerous relations with Menhir courts. Middle Forest Folk can develop oath-places, ancestral war songs and their own later magical practices. Eastern River and Upland Folk possess the great war-voice schools. Ättar power develops through Mother Moon's bargains, pact-kindreds, skinwalking and the Iron-Skin rage. Contact among them can still produce borrowing, rival claimants and mixed schools.

The war-voice remains **exploratory**, but its cultural home and magical fit are now selected for Package D development. The account should name its first schools, at least three masters with different voices, the conflict in which the art reaches its heroic height, and the later disaster that produces a fringe mountain religion without erasing continued military use.

## VI. Kaer Maga and the southern penal crownland

Paizo describes Kaer Maga as an ancient ring fortress which predates Thassilon and later served as a prison for Runelord Karzoug. Its hollow walls and interior districts become home to refugees, criminals, gangs, unusual religious groups and monsters; dangers remain in the structures below.[^kaer-maga] Its value lies in **historical repurposing**. A structure built for one purpose becomes a prison, then a city whose inhabitants make social worlds inside and around the architecture of confinement.

The World Egg should take that grammar into a much larger and colder history.

### Proposed state: the southern penal crownland

The provisional polity should consist of several ring-fortresses, mining valleys, geothermal estates, harbour prisons and warded roads near the southern polar country. A council or appointed Giant house governs it on behalf of several northern kingdoms. Its population includes:

- Giant rebels, defeated princes and God-Kin too dangerous or politically inconvenient to keep near their home courts;
- human slaves sentenced for revolt, sacrilege, desertion or the debts of their households;
- captives and raiders from several northern Giant states;
- skilled deportees required to keep mines, heatworks, ships, storehouses and ward-machines operating;
- older southern human communities subjected to the penal economy without originating in it;
- free client households who profit from supply, transport and punishment.

The Giant rulers call the cold, distance and Grobi-less early wilderness security features. Their prisoners turn the same conditions into bargaining power because no estate can survive without food, heating and repair. Districts develop mixed languages and local loyalties. A human engineer from one horizon may live beside a captive fisher from another and a family born in the crownland for three generations.

### Collapse during the Great Unbinding

The Unbinding breaks command from the north. Rival Giant houses open cells to recruit armies, execute hostages, seize heatworks and release war-broods against one another. Human work crews withhold fuel and food, occupy tunnels and destroy causeways. Some condemned Giants promise freedom and then restore slavery after victory. Others keep agreements for reasons of affection, prudence or repentance.

The crownland falls through several connected disasters rather than one explosion. Ring walls split; heated galleries flood or freeze; mines burn; ships carry selected households away while leaving dependants; escaped prisoners raid surrounding settlements. One or more fortresses become the setting of the First Rewriting. Other districts survive as human refuges or are buried beneath ice. The ruins therefore support several later origin claims without proving that every Folk or Ättar ancestor came from the prisons.

This model gives Package D a Giant history comparable in depth to Arsuvan and Vathra while keeping the **Five Imperial Horizons** intact. The polar state is a lesser crownland and dumping ground used by several horizons. Its rulers remain bound by established Giant descent and continuity rules.

## VII. Warhammer Greenskins and the Grobi question

### Separate source traditions

Warhammer Fantasy, Age of Sigmar and Warhammer 40,000 contain different Greenskin histories. Games Workshop's overview treats common themes—violent cultures, several body types, large war gatherings and attraction to powerful leaders—while identifying setting-specific forms.[^greenskins]

Warhammer 40,000 Orks belong to a wider fungal ecosystem. Spores support Orks, smaller Gretchin and Snotlings and various squigs. Orks grow larger through survival and success. Some are Oddboyz with inborn technical, medical or psychic dispositions. A large gathering produces Waaagh! energy which Weirdboyz channel at considerable risk.[^greenskins][^weirdboyz][^squigs]

Warhammer Fantasy Greenskins include several Orc and Goblin cultures and enormous temporary Waaaghs. The Black Orc tradition is especially relevant: Chaos Dwarf sorcerers sought a stronger, more disciplined slave-warrior and produced rebels capable of nearly destroying their masters. The exact lore has shifted across editions, so it should be treated as a setting tradition rather than a current scientific account.[^black-orcs]

### What should remain unchanged

The World Egg Grobi already possess a more distinctive biological structure than either source provides. Package D should retain:

| Existing element | Reason to retain |
|---|---|
| Wrath performed the First Rewriting | preserves the Idol ecology and prevents Giant craft from manufacturing a new soul category |
| the first victims' human souls separated and routed | protects the complete Image-bearing human soul and established judgement road |
| modern Grobi have `wrath-rewritten-life` | distinguishes living Grobi from humans, damned souls, Masks and Giant continuities |
| five food-driven stages | makes supply, famine and victory alter the military ecology in a uniquely World Egg way |
| stage-glands and bodily death | keeps individual continuity legible and prevents casual Ork-like recurrence from spores |
| intelligence, memory and suffering | permits language, cunning, pack attachment, hierarchy and tactical choice without making Grobi moral neighbours |
| seasonal migration and independent bands | keeps geography and food more important than a universal mastermind |
| separation from the Pale-Skins | prevents one subterranean or monstrous origin from swallowing the bestiary |

### What can be adapted

**Size and authority.** Warhammer Orks commonly follow larger and more successful leaders. Grobi physiology makes this causal relationship concrete. A leader who secures food grows; growth improves the leader's ability to win and carry prestige; followers arrive because the growing host promises food. The loop can fail abruptly when supply is cut.

**War-host accumulation.** A Grobi host should become more than a large warband. Rough smiths, animal handlers, red-knucklers, scavengers, cooks, scouts, drum-keepers, breeding hearths and captive interpreters kept until slaughter gather around it. These are learnt roles within a materially poor culture, not genetically installed technologies.

**Wrath resonance.** A large gathering, repeated victory and public anticipation of violence can strengthen a dependent Wrath effect. Drums synchronise movement; appetite fetishes become more responsive; shamans or rage-speakers suffer dangerous visions. The effect belongs to an Idol feeding architecture and local Mask relationship, not a species-wide psychic field. It can mislead, injure or consume its users.

**Slave revolt grammar.** The polar Giant gaolers try to select or alter formidable captives. Their project supplies bodies, discipline and conditions of mass violence. A Red Reckoner steers the regimen towards Wrath's larger design, and Furor directly produces the first Grobi as a self-reproducing army for the power vacuum after Giant collapse. The new beings turn upon Giant and human alike. This keeps the Black Orc reversal—an engineered servile force becoming its maker's catastrophe—while assigning intention and the true metaphysical act to Wrath.

**Environmental aftermath.** A large Grobi movement leaves changes beyond corpses: exhausted grazing, scavenger blooms, abandoned tools, altered predator routes, contaminated stage-gland dumps and smaller bands which settle after the host breaks. These traces can create future Grobi societies rather than a fungal biome.

**Exultation and dominance.** Warhammer Orks are memorable because violence does not eliminate exuberance, rivalry, crude craft pride or jokes. Grobi should possess competitive cookery, cruel sports, drum contests, weapon boasting, mockery and pack attachment to animals or tools. Every pleasure remains organised by appetite, hierarchy or humiliation rather than moral fellowship.

### What should be rejected or held

| Source element | Ruling | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| reproductive spores and a complete fungal ecosystem | reject | contradicts the existing true-breeding lineage, bodily stages and individual death |
| genetically implanted engineering or medical knowledge | reject | turns culture into biology and weakens the setting's material causality |
| “belief makes any machine work” | reject | an exaggerated popular reading even within 40k; too broad for World Egg metaphysics |
| one species-wide psychic mind | reject | collides with individual agency and would make every band part of a master plot |
| Grobi as dependent Wrath Masks | reject | living lineage and Mask are separate continuities |
| every Grobi is naturally predatory | retain with precision | domination, fighting and consumption are creaturely ends; individuals still choose leaders, routes, victims and tactics and do not share one mind |
| one universal Grobi language and nation | hold | trade speech may spread, but history should begin with bands, hearths and regional languages |
| endless comic incompetence | reject | undermines a threat able to shape several thousand years of southern history |

### The missing Grobi civilisation

The greatest current gap is not another creature stage. It is the absence of Grobi pack history. Package D should develop:

- reproduction, brood protection and the relationship between birth-stage and acquired stage;
- food seizure, shares, theft, ration discipline and the preservation or consumption of young;
- band names, dominance, desertion, temporary truce and the treatment of human captives as provisions;
- languages, drum codes and the ways a large host communicates across dialects;
- low craft traditions, especially hide, bone, scavenged metal, rough repair and stage-gland cutting;
- religion, including rival Wrath cults, counterfeit commands and dealings with other powers for advantage;
- stories Grobi tell about the First Rewriting, Giant gaolers and human neighbours;
- winter lairs, seasonal camps, ruined ring-fort occupations and predatory barter truces;
- different political ambitions among goblin, hobgoblin, orc, ogre and troll-stage people.

These questions should be answered through connected histories rather than a species questionnaire. One host may preserve its brood during a famine because more surviving young mean more future fighters. Another may eat the brood and briefly create several enormous war leaders before collapsing. The comparison reveals how food, cunning and inherited Wrath shape different predatory institutions.

## VIII. Proposed World Egg ethnographic architecture

### Shared ancestry without a single ancestral nation

The principal Folk and Ättar ancestry should remain **Orphaned**. Their oldest populations include subject villages that survived in place, fugitives from southern Giant estates, human rebels, coastal fishing communities and people displaced from the polar crownland. Later northern captives and immigrants add ancestry and skills. This produces a broad family resemblance without one lost patriarch or one exodus column.

“Fäskogarfolk” can remain the archaic scholarly name for the related ancestral population. Its use should narrow: it describes a reconstructed language-and-custom continuum, not a state or a pure people. Package D should establish period self-names for particular river leagues, hill peoples, eastern gords and far-cold halls.

### Three Folk constellations

| Constellation | Principal landscape | Political development | Social emphasis | Mythic and supernatural emphasis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Western and Atlantic | western coasts, islands, estuaries, hills and rich river corridors | kindreds, hillforts, oppida-like towns, cattle coalitions, island lordships | fosterage, learned houses, cattle wealth, clientage, bardic standing | Menhir courts, dangerous hospitality, sovereignty stories, head and weapon deposits |
| Middle Forest | central woodland, marsh, river crossings and northern frontier approaches | farm districts, assemblies, retinues, coalitions and treaty strongholds | compensation, war leaders, longhouses, wetland offerings, foreign auxiliary service | oath places, victory bogs, forest courts and ancestral war songs |
| Eastern River and Upland | eastern forests, river routes, mountain valleys and Baltic-like cold shores | villages, gords, assemblies, princely followings and selected temple centres | migration, ovens, beekeeping, mixed farming, river trade and tribute | household spirits, local cult centres, epic strongmen and the great war-voice schools |

The constellations overlap. A western hill town may use compensation law; a middle-forest community may maintain learned seers; an eastern river prince may foster children among Ättar. The table supplies historical centres of gravity rather than cultural cages.

### Ättar development

The Ättar emerge along the coldest coasts and protected valleys from populations related especially closely to the Middle Forest and western maritime worlds. Their difference accumulates through five changes:

1. winter stores and great halls concentrate authority;
2. boats connect distant resources and foreign service;
3. Grobi pressure rewards coordinated seasonal musters;
4. the War of the Empty Halls and Mother Moon's bargain establish four hereditary pact-kindreds;
5. later sail, iron and political opportunities create the sea-kings and great keel-taking traditions.

This sequence preserves the current c. 1600 BR bargain. Great halls and compensation assemblies precede it. The pact transforms an existing people and does not create them.

### Monument and civilisation

The Folk and Ättar should not be described as peoples who failed to develop civilisation or landmarks. Their monuments use different materials and scales:

- timber halls rebuilt on the same footprints for generations;
- earth-and-timber ramparts, cliff forts and ringworks;
- shipyards, harbour booms and winter boathouses;
- mound fields and cemeteries;
- bogs filled with deliberately broken arms and regalia;
- grove roads, assembly stones and carved passes;
- mining scars, charcoal landscapes and river diversions;
- abandoned Giant ring-fortresses occupied, feared or quarried by later people.

Cold, wet soil destroys much timber. Grobi warfare burns settlements and scatters populations. Atherian writers privilege stone cities, roads and tax registers. These three facts explain why later imperial readers underestimate the south. The peoples themselves still build states, markets, fleets, law and durable sacred landscapes.

## IX. Preliminary Package D chronology

This sequence changes no canon. It identifies the historical work needed before the authorial development delivery.

## X. Recommended Package D research and development structure

The present comparative dossier should lead to four connected authorial histories rather than one enormous cultural summary.

### 1. The southern Giant crownland and the First Rewriting

This dossier should name the crownland, its principal ring-fortresses, governing Giant houses, subject districts and human revolt leaders. It should give each fortress a material function and a distinct fate. The First Rewriting requires an authorial causal account which preserves the existing soul ruling while leaving later Folk and Grobi traditions partial.

### 2. The three Folk constellations

This history should provide period names and connected development for western/Atlantic, middle-forest and eastern river/upland peoples. It should locate existing Green March, Heartwood, Gale-Crown and Tidewood societies inside the larger past without making those modern regional names ancient universals.

### 3. The Ättar before the great keel-taking

This history should run from related southern ancestors through the first halls, Empty Halls war, Moon bargain, heroic kings and early maritime networks to 350 BR. Its extraordinary magical traditions remain Moon-blood, the four pact-kindreds, skinwalking and the Iron-Skin champions. Individual Ättar may encounter or learn fragments of eastern war-voice, but the tradition does not originate among them.

### 4. The war-voice masters of the Eastern Uplands

Package D should develop the Deep-Voiced as part of Eastern River and Upland Folk history. The account requires an early origin in Giant resonant works, several masters with distinct arts, a heroic military period, collateral destruction and a later school of restraint. It should establish which feats occurred, which survive as partisan song and how non-military village practices descended from the same training. The art remains learnable, physically costly and culturally guarded.

### 5. Grobi societies and the winter frontier

This history should develop Grobi breeding life, food custom, crude craft, dominance, regional packs, religion and carried memories. It should connect particular Grobi hosts to particular human wars. The War of the Empty Halls must involve named Grobi actors with intelligible predatory aims rather than a faceless wave.

## XI. Collision and sensitivity findings

### Existing canon that should control the development

- Folk and Ättar remain Orphaned peoples outside the Glass Ark.
- The War of the Empty Halls occurs around 1600 BR and establishes the four Ättar pact-kindreds through Mother Moon's bargain.
- Grobi continuity, food-driven stages and soul ruling remain fixed. Full moral agency is reopened by the 12 September author review and replaced in the Package D proposal by intelligent predatory nature without human conscience.
- G-61 concerns one hall's tragic division of duty and cannot become the universal founding event.
- Tidewood's completed history remains local and should not absorb the whole Atlantic Folk strand.
- Teren Casvilar's campaigns remain the fixed Atherian contact point around 390–379 BR.
- The south is colder. Grobi winter movement runs northwards and westwards from the deep southern wastes.

### Matters requiring correction or care

- Older “northern” wording attached to the Hall Facing the Grobi must be corrected wherever it survives.
- Any story in which captured human children become the biological ancestors of a Grobi clan must distinguish human captivity from Grobi reproduction. Humans do not become modern Grobi through ordinary adoption or descent.
- The Hollow-Kings and Pale-Skins remain independent of Grobi origins.
- Giant experimentation can prepare bodies or conditions but cannot replace Wrath as author of the First Rewriting. Wrath nevertheless acts deliberately and directly, using a dependent Red Reckoner to prepare Kharvos as the beginning of a self-reproducing army.
- A “Celtic” Folk branch must not turn Irish, Welsh, Cornish, Scottish and continental Gaulish histories into one costume.
- A “Slavic” Folk branch must distinguish Baltic-coast Slavs from Baltic peoples and avoid importing modern Russian identity into antiquity.
- Reachmen and Druids are comparative sources for local religious organisation. Neither name should appear as a World Egg institution.
- The war-voice belongs provisionally to the Eastern River and Upland Folk. It operates through the setting's existing resonance principles and remains close to unaccompanied Air-and-Earth Harmonic Shellcraft. Package D must still reconcile its named workings with Menhir jurisdiction and the exact limits of established Shellcraft before canon promotion.

## Conclusion

The research supports the user's fourfold human architecture: three varied Folk constellations and one Ättar people, all related through the complicated survival of the southern Giant world. Their civilisations should be visible in halls, forts, ports, gords, mines, ships, assemblies, songs and sacred landscapes. Geography and Grobi war encourage distributed power and perishable construction. They do not prevent cities, achievement or historical change.

The polar penal crownland gives these peoples a common pressure without giving them one origin. It also gives the Grobi's established metaphysics a historical stage of sufficient scale. Wrath's First Rewriting can become one of the Unbinding's worst southern events: a human population loses its embodied future, the human souls route towards judgement, and a new creaturely lineage awakens inside a collapsing prison world.

The Grobi themselves need the largest development. Warhammer provides energy, social escalation and the revolt of a weapon against its makers. World Egg's existing five-stage biology and soul ruling remain more valuable than fungal reproduction or a collective psychic mind. Their pack institutions should explain how intelligent apex predators breed, feed, organise and make war without turning them into misunderstood humans. Every winter remains a monster attack at regional scale; the history distinguishes the packs, leaders and material causes inside that recurring scourge.
