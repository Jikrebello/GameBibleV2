# Jirahar, Arqeshite, Vashari and Eastern Peoples before Atheria

> [!note] Author-only development record
> The approved identities and belonging rules were promoted through [[Ancient Peoples before Atherian Unification - Canon Integration Review]]. The account retains its 350 BR boundary.

## PB-01 — The ancient Jirahar, road houses and towers

The ancient Jirahar emerged from the Near-Glass continuum after the southern leagues had departed. Their ancestors belonged principally to the One Glass People. Marriage, adoption and conversion also brought Orphaned neighbours into the developing community. Other well keepers, farmers and former Giant subjects remained separate peoples even when they travelled with Jirahar households.

No universal endonym from the earliest road camps is fixed. Households named themselves through kin, work, a remembered founder or a route. Bronze-Pan people repaired vessels and measures. Night-Rein houses handled animals and difficult travel. Funeral washers took responsibility for bodies and distant kin. The familiar name **Jirahar** is appropriate once these houses recognised a shared sacred and social inheritance across several routes. Before then, authorial prose should describe the particular houses rather than imagine a finished nation.

A road house was larger than one travelling family. It included married couples, children, widowed kin, converts, dependants, servants, apprentices and sometimes enslaved people. Its members might separate for years while recognising the same marriages, debts, burials and promises of protection. Birth provided one route into the house. Marriage, adoption, apprenticeship and public conversion supplied others. An enslaved person living within the company did not gain equal membership merely by travelling with it.

Tower settlements developed from defended stores, water and recognised judgement. Permanent residents farmed, baked, brewed, taught, made tools and housed travellers. They could spend their whole lives near one tower while belonging to a people renowned for movement. Road and tower were therefore complementary forms of Jirahar life rather than nomadic and settled ethnic divisions.

The Orakha supplied common sacred recitation and language for judgement. Everyday speech varied. Multilingual houses used Arqeshite in river trade, Vashari in horse work and Atherian at eastern ports. Clothing, food and occupation followed means and region more closely than ancestry. Governments that classified every road speaker as Jirahar regularly confused carriers belonging to other peoples with members of the Path.

By 350 BR, Jirahar identity rested on acknowledged descent or conversion, household responsibility, participation in communal worship and recognition by other Jirahar. No single city or ruler controlled those tests everywhere.

**Identity ruling:** an Ark-derived and later mixed people whose unity survived through distributed households, religion and mutual recognition. Road and tower were ways of life within the people.

## PB-02 — The Qiryathi

**Qiryathi** described attachment to Qiryath, the great tower city of Reqa. It could refer to a resident, a recognised citizen, a sanctuary household or a person descended from the destroyed first city. These meanings became politically charged after 742 BR.

First Qiryath grew from permanent neighbourhoods, gardens, workshops, guest houses and villages around a refuge tower. Its population was principally Jirahar but included foreign merchants, servants, converts and enslaved people. A road-house member visiting for pilgrimage did not become Qiryathi. A locally born gardener might be Qiryathi despite never having travelled with a caravan.

The city's councils represented neighbourhoods, sanctuary houses and road communities. Protectors acquired exceptional authority during war. Claims to the title Guardian of Qiryath could therefore indicate military command without establishing hereditary kingship. Later royal songs and road-house accounts used different language because they disagreed about the proper source of rule.

Arqesh destroyed First Qiryath in 742 BR. Soldiers killed civilians and defenders, denied burial to leaders, divided families and deported households by skill and status. Survivors carried Qiryathi identity into river cities and road settlements. People who had never lived in the city helped preserve its rites, while local villagers maintained its water and fields under imperial occupation.

Second Qiryath joined returners, local survivors, converts, imperial settlers and people born in exile. Disputes over land and marriage made descent from the first city valuable. The Yaelith–Neram household helped secure recognition for publicly received converts and mixed families when restoration factions attempted to expel them.

By 350 BR, Qiryath was a sacred and market city under Vasharan supremacy. **Qiryathi** remained a civic and memorial identity inside the wider Jirahar people. It did not make every Jirahar subject to the city.

**Identity ruling:** a Jirahar civic population shaped by destruction and return. Qiryathi and Jirahar overlap without being synonyms.

## PB-03 — Jirahar in river exile and return

The deportations after 742 BR created Jirahar communities in Arqesh, Nemeru and other river cities. They did not experience exile uniformly. Royal workshops took skilled people. Estates received labourers. Court households held hostages and servants. Families were separated because officials assigned individuals according to the labour demanded by different institutions.

Exiles maintained belonging through neighbourhood worship, burial fraternities, marriage and communication with road houses. Sameth bar Yorin's family demonstrates the resulting ambiguity. His punishment for refusing the royal image made him a figure of resistance, while his daughter used court service to ransom relatives. Later judgement of her conduct depended on whether the teller valued visible refusal or survival achieved inside the conqueror's household.

Children born in the river cities commonly spoke Arqeshite. Some entered scribal schools, military service or canal administration. A successful family might own property and employ poorer deportees while remaining Jirahar. Others converted or married into local households and ceased to seek recognition from distant kin. The community's boundaries changed through human decisions, not through an unbroken biological register.

Return after 690 BR did not empty the river settlements. Those who travelled to Qiryath found inhabitants who had survived locally and families settled during occupation. Restored claims could dispossess these people. Arezan's decree recognised selected old property while denying a general remedy to households displaced by the coalition conquest.

By 350 BR, river Jirahar possessed several generations of local history. Some called Qiryath their sacred centre while considering Nemeru or Arqesh home. **Returner**, **exile house** and **river Jirahar** described historical experience and communal position, not separate ancestries.

**Identity ruling:** a diasporic branch of Jirahar life produced through coercive deportation and subsequent local development. Arqeshite language or office did not automatically dissolve Jirahar belonging.

## PB-04 — The peoples of the Ubar canals

The Ubar riverlands contained cities and villages long before House Ubar created its empire. Arqesh, Nemeru, Khalbar, Durqesh and smaller canal settlements shared irrigation, brick construction, river trade and related religious practices. Their inhabitants normally identified themselves through city, neighbourhood, temple and water district.

The collective expression **Ubar peoples** is a geographic and cultural term. It does not indicate descent from House Ubar. The dynasty took its name from the same river world and later presented royal rule as the fulfilment of its natural unity. Canal cities retained old rivalries and local languages or dialects beneath that claim.

Belonging depended on recognised household and civic position. Courtyard families included kin, apprentices, clients, servants and enslaved workers. Water obligations linked rural and urban residents to gates maintained beyond their own neighbourhood. A merchant might possess rights in several cities; a deportee could work the same channel without receiving local property or burial standing.

Nemeru's boat and dye households, Khalbar's transport workers and Durqesh's highland connections gave each city a distinct public character. These specialisations supported cooperation and political competition. Opening a canal for an enemy could save a neighbourhood or drown another. Imperial histories that celebrated coordinated water concealed the local bargaining and violence required to produce it.

By 350 BR, the Ubar peoples lived under Vasharan successor rule in most districts. Their city institutions, water knowledge and household cultures survived. They could be Vasharan subjects without being Vashari and Arqeshite in broad culture without accepting the dead Ubar dynasty.

**Identity ruling:** several related Orphaned river populations. The Ubar is their country and connecting system, not a royal bloodline.

## PB-05 — The Arqeshites

**Arqeshite** began as a civic designation associated with Arqesh and acquired wider meanings under House Ubar. Imperial administration, common measures, schools, military settlement and royal ceremonies spread the capital's language and practices across the riverlands. Subjects and foreigners consequently used *Arqeshite* for people whose relationships to the empire differed greatly.

An Arqesh citizen belonged to the capital's neighbourhood and civic institutions. An imperial officer might come from Nemeru, Qiryath or Vashar and adopt Arqeshite court practice. A deported family might become Arqeshite in speech and urban habit while remaining excluded from full property or temple rights. A provincial village paying Ubar taxes was subject to Arqesh without becoming culturally Arqeshite.

House Ubar rewarded soldiers with land, transferred skilled households and kept children as political security. These practices enlarged the imperial population and created mixed neighbourhoods. They also made Arqeshite identity attractive to people seeking office or protection. The Crowned Countenance exploited royal images and compulsory praise; its manifestations did not contain the souls of Arqeshite kings or subjects.

The coalition conquest of 690 BR killed inhabitants, plundered districts and displaced households. House Ubar fell, but Arqeshite culture survived through canal administration, writing, schools, music, dress and domestic practice. Some former officers entered Vasharan service. Others preserved loyalty to the old kings or developed civic histories blaming them for the disaster.

By 350 BR, *Arqeshite* most usefully described the urban and river civilisation shaped by Arqesh, while political allegiance belonged to successor governments. It should not be used as a synonym for every person formerly conquered by House Ubar.

**Identity ruling:** an Orphaned civic-imperial culture which outlived its dynasty. Arqeshite identity could be acquired through upbringing, language and urban participation as well as descent.

## PB-06 — The Vashari and their principal regions

The Vashari were related Orphaned peoples of the high country east of the Ubar. Their villages, fortified estates and sanctuary towns existed before House Tiravan. Shared languages, marriage customs, seasonal movement and teachings about Right Order gave them a broad cultural relationship without permanent political unity.

The name **Vashari** is treated as an established collective self-name by the mature hearth-domain period. Local identity remained stronger in many settings. **Kavresh** joined cultivated valleys and the household from which the Tiravans arose. **Ruzhan** named high pastures, horse-breeding houses and mobile aristocratic connections. **Daryam** joined mining approaches, sanctuary teaching and households responsible for tended fires. None represented the whole people.

A hearth domain united several villages and pasture households with a fortified family or sanctuary. Belonging came through land, kin, marriage, tenant obligation and participation in assemblies. Fosterage and political hostage-taking placed children in other domains. Captives worked estates and mines. A person serving a great house could be Vashari without belonging to its lineage.

Arqeshite pressure divided Vashari houses between collaboration and revolt. Some gained trade and office. Others lost animals, land or hostages. Arezan's coalition drew on these different grievances. Victory gave selected highland houses estates in the river country and placed Vashari officers over non-Vashari populations.

By 350 BR, Vashari identity remained rooted in the highlands and had also spread through military and administrative settlement. Regional differences survived the imperial crown. A Daryam teacher could condemn a Tiravan king's falsehood without ceasing to be Vashari.

**Identity ruling:** a related family of Orphaned highland populations. Kavreshi, Ruzhani and Daryami are regional identities within this wider people, not hereditary castes.

## PB-07 — Vasharan imperial subjects

The Crown of Roads and Dawns transformed **Vasharan** into a political identity. Its subjects included Vashari highlanders, Arqeshite river populations, Jirahar communities, coastal cities and districts with their own rulers. Royal service created common practices among officers without producing one Vasharan ethnicity.

A Vasharan subject owed taxes, road service or obedience through a particular arrangement. Qiryath retained internal courts. Arqeshite water officials maintained canals. Highland domains supplied animals and troops. Coastal cities negotiated ships and harbour payments. The empire punished rebellion and could alter these privileges by force.

Royal roads encouraged multilingual towns around stations. Vashari officers married canal families. Jirahar interpreters entered relay service. Arqeshite scribes trained highland administrators. Their children could possess several cultural affiliations. Court rank and use of a Vasharan administrative language demonstrated service, not ancestry or religious purity.

The Witness of Dawn encouraged the royal claim that truthful order required obedience. Some subjects accepted the crown as protector of roads while resisting its cult. Others used imperial language to settle local rivalries. The dependent Pride Mask exploiting these ceremonies remained separate from every human population.

By 350 BR, Vasharan identity was real as subjecthood, military service and imperial public culture. Vashari remained an ancestry and highland culture. Arqeshites remained river people. Jirahar remained a dispersed sacred community. A single person could hold two or more of these identities without contradiction.

**Identity ruling:** a multi-people political order and acquired imperial culture. It must never replace the identities of its subjects in population tables.

## PB-08 — The Ortheians

The Ortheians inhabited an upland kingdom within the broader Atherian-speaking world. Their towns, royal houses, assemblies and permanent infantry gave them a political history distinct from Caleran and the maritime leagues. **Ortheian** combined regional culture, speech and allegiance to the upland crown.

Ortheian speech belonged to the Atherian family. This made diplomacy and military coordination possible while encouraging later Caleran authors to treat Ortheia as an errant Atherian province. During Valeron's hegemony, the reverse claim appeared: royal writers presented the upland king as leader of all Atherian-speaking cities. Neither claim converted common language into common citizenship.

Households held land through local custom and royal relationships. Assemblies represented important houses and military communities. Royal marriages and hostage education joined Ortheia to coastal cities. Valeron and Eryndor enlarged a permanent army through compulsory contributions, allied contingents and service promised to particular commanders.

Eryndor's eastern conquest settled Ortheian soldiers and dependants around harbours and river crossings. Their children could retain Ortheian military custom while acquiring Arqeshite or Delkosi speech and kin. Demeran's seizure of the homeland and murder of Eryndor's heirs divided royal loyalty without ending the regional people.

By 350 BR, Ortheia remained a recognisable Atherian-speaking upland society whose relationship with Caleran had passed through hegemony, war and alliance. Its people were culturally related to Atherians and politically distinct.

**Identity ruling:** an Atherian-speaking regional people and kingdom. Shared language never retroactively grants Caleran citizenship or one Glass genealogy.

## PB-09 — The Delkosi

**Delkosi** first described people of Delkos and acquired a broader political meaning after Serevan established a Successor court there. The port already belonged to the eastern Atherian maritime world. Eryndor's death brought officers, sailors, attendants and displaced royal dependants from several countries.

The new court combined local island households, Ortheian military traditions, Arqeshite specialists, Vashari marriage connections and crews drawn from allied ports. It preserved a common fleet and royal titles for long enough to produce an inherited court culture. This was a Braided successor formation, although Delkos did not absorb every harbour under its command into one people.

Belonging could mean citizenship or residence in Delkos, service within the successor fleet, or participation in the court's mixed military culture. Officer families carried Delkosi titles abroad. One later branch produced Nerethan, whose arrival at Sarqen joined this inheritance to the Gateway country. That relationship did not make every Delkosi a Gateway ancestor.

The kingdom lost territory through later wars. Its coin standards, naval ranks, foods and household ceremonies survived among ports which no longer obeyed its ruler. Descendants disagreed over whether *Delkosi* properly belonged only to the island or to the wider service community.

By 350 BR, Delkosi remained a recognised maritime and successor identity. It arose from a city and mixed court rather than descent from Eryndor or Serevan.

**Identity ruling:** a Braided civic and military culture with an island core. Dynastic service, not royal blood, explains its spread.

## PB-10 — Tasarian, Ilyessan and eastern coastal peoples

Tasar, Avesh and Ilyessa connected the Ubar routes and Vashari roads to the eastern inner sea. Their populations included older Orphaned shore communities, Arqeshite and Vashari settlers, Jirahar households, Delkosi connections and Atherian-speaking merchants. No established evidence supports one ancient ethnic name embracing all three.

**Tasarian** and **Ilyessan** were civic identities. Citizenship depended on local households and institutions. Resident merchants, subject crews, freed people and enslaved rowers did not possess equal standing. A person could speak Atherian at the quay, use Vashari at a royal station and belong to a Jirahar house without becoming a citizen of either port.

Ship finance gave wealthy households influence. Citizens and subject towns supplied crews. Paid sailors and enslaved rowers served in the same fleet under sharply different conditions. The Eastern Sea Wars altered allegiance without homogenising the coast. Tasar expelled a Vasharan governor and invited Atherian help; its supposed liberators then demanded tribute and hostages. Ilyessa's naval victory protected the league while strengthening commanders able to impose further service.

The cities' paired liberation festivals reveal their layered identities. One public rite could celebrate release from eastern rule. Another song remembered people abandoned by the western allies. Families with interests in both systems did not experience the war as a clean national struggle.

By 350 BR, these cities remained mixed civic populations contested between Atherian and Vasharan influence. Their inhabitants belonged first to particular cities and households. **Eastern coastal peoples** is an encyclopaedic grouping, not their common endonym.

**Identity ruling:** several Braided and multi-lingual civic populations. Political alignment, language and ancestry must be recorded separately.
