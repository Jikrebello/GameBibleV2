# The Two Fissions — Collision and Disposition Review

> [!warning] Review scope
> This review tests [[The Two Fissions and the Atherian Long Formation]], [[Scattering Ethnogenesis and Inheritance Matrix]], and all twelve hooks in [[The Two Fissions - Twelve Nursery Hooks]]. It incorporates corrections and records dispositions. It does not amend chronology, revise a Canon Reference note, add a Garden bed, name a founder, or promote any event.

## Review Questions

The review asks of the historical model and every hook:

1. Does the date fit the existing Exodus, Scattering, Hearths, City-State, and Star chronology?
2. Does geography actually cause the movement rather than merely decorate it?
3. Are later Jirahar, Ahdari, Atherian, Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan, Sestaran, Thalmyric, and Eclessian identities kept out of periods before they exist?
4. Does the event collide with an existing company, Praise entry, Great-Power bed, unknown history, saint, institution, relic, or protected Weird?
5. Are culpable actors, bodily consequences, and irreversible costs stated without euphemism?
6. Does the scriptural grammar govern moral interpretation without granting Atheria an in-world holy-war warrant?
7. Is the 1360 AR trace useful even if the associated founder, miracle, or later institutional claim is false?

An incorporated `revise` is a passing result. It means the required correction now appears in the controlling research dossier.

# Group I — Vacant Commission

## TF-01 — Ordinary Seat, Extraordinary Claim

The existing setting already uses empty chairs as signs of Pride, persistent titles, missing company members, and infernal refusal. TF-01 survives because its seat does nothing. It does not lower the room, receive obedience without human enforcement, preserve an office, speak, answer, manifest a person, or authenticate a claimant. The violence occurs because a commander and guards use an ordinary object to make a political claim.

The seat's later multiplication is useful. Jirahar, Ahdari, and Thalmyric empty places can preserve related memory without any one being the object. The practice remains evidentially weak but culturally durable.

**Final ruling:** `retain`.

## TF-02 — Five as Memory, Not Constitution

The hook's grain requisition, dead dependants, condemned commander, and southern refusal are coherent. The danger was the numeral. If exactly five offices existed authorially, later writers would treat them as the hidden origin of five peoples, five ministries, five sacred staffs, or an ideal constitution which the setting has somehow forgotten.

The revision makes “Five Staffs” a later mnemonic found in several incompatible traditions. The underlying history fixes differentiated offices and a lethal requisition but not their number, insignia, simultaneous holders, or constitutional relation. Five-part warrants in 1360 show how the memory was organized, not what the original camp necessarily looked like.

**Final ruling:** `revise`, correction incorporated.

# Group II — Southern Seizure and Recovery

## TF-03 — One Gate, Not a Universal Conquest Key

TF-03 remains distinct from the Peace Feast at Nine Hearths. That capsule preserves a Genesis 34 sequence of sexual assault, compensation, ritual disarmament, massacre, enslavement, and later founding-marriage distortion. TF-03 concerns an insider bargain, night entry, garrison killing, uncontrolled conquest violence, betrayal of local collaborators, and a land register that makes seizure look original.

The gate does not reveal a universal weakness in Orphaned fortifications or found every Atherian city. The repaired masonry, burial pits, and incompatible allotments are sufficient carriers.

**Final ruling:** `retain`.

## TF-04 — Separate from the Hostage-and-Names Praise

The existing Praise entry already owns a captive who teaches two courts the names of enemy children, a paired naming feast, and the possibility that named children later die in feud. TF-04 would duplicate it if it also centered teaching, names, or reconciliation ritual.

The revised hook instead concerns an earlier fort-clan hostage cohort, the judicial murder of one returned hostage, indiscriminate retaliation, and a rule requiring both kin systems to witness punishment or transfer. No paired feast, teaching hostage, named children, court pair, object, or locality is shared. The coercion and child danger remain intact.

**Final ruling:** `revise`, correction incorporated.

## TF-05 — Braiding Through Occupation and Revolt

The Harbor Founded Twice gives the Sestaran category a needed hard beginning without turning it into one Sestaran origin. A seasonal guest quarter becomes armed occupation through grain control; resisters are killed, sold, or expelled; local pilots later destroy the parent league's punitive fleet. The mixed survivor city inherits both crimes and both claims to foundation.

The two calendars and quay alignments remain useful even if the later civic reconciliation is propaganda. The hook must remain attached to one unnamed harbor and cannot be used as the template behind every Sestaran city.

**Final ruling:** `retain`.

## TF-06 — Material Furnaces Only

The setting already contains the Seven Furnace Cities of Orqad, the failed Refuge at the Living Furnace, and a Living Brass Refuge lead. TF-06 has a different function: ordinary mining, charcoal, labor, standard measures, arms markets, food dependency, and regional network failure.

The revision excludes Titan fragments, living body-heat, Patron anchors, Elemental Shell platforms, apertures, inherited furnace offices, and shared guilds or descendants. Its historical horror remains human: hereditary mine service, purchased captives, punishment of independent smiths, burned fuel country, and starvation after routes fail.

**Final ruling:** `revise`, correction incorporated.

# Group III — Northern Fission

## TF-07 — The Old Well Is Not Every Later Well

Water law recurs because the setting's northern geography makes it recur. That does not permit one well to become the secret source of the Nine Wells conflict, the Four Who Brought Water, the Hundred-Wells Clerk, or all Covenant law.

TF-07 is now limited to one c. 1920–1840 BR seasonal well shared by Tarkhan, Near-Glass, and older settled communities. Its issue is the ranking of emergency drinking, maintained storage, and seasonal herd access after deliberate closure and retaliatory killing. Its three later legal recensions demonstrate influence or analogous reasoning, not a single unbroken code.

**Final ruling:** `revise`, correction incorporated.

## TF-08 — A Local Kin-Fight Inside a Larger Division

The Court That Walked and the Wall That Stayed does not found First Qiryath, every Jirahar caravan court, or every Ahdari protector house. It supplies one case in which emergency fortification becomes confiscation and shared kin turn weapons on one another.

The paired legal formulas can survive even if the site is never identified. Neither formula proves its side innocent: travelling judges fail to secure captives and stores, while wall-builders convert temporary defense into inherited exclusion.

**Final ruling:** `retain`.

## TF-09 — Personal Law Without Foundress Myth

The Bride of the Dry Road works because the woman is neither a passive reward nor a universal mother. She enters a constrained political marriage, refuses a demand to betray protected fugitives, loses her brother to the alliance's violence, invokes the agreement's exit terms, and takes responsibility for dependants afterward.

The later custody rule may descend from the case, attach itself to it, or preserve an analogous judgment. None of those possibilities makes her children the biological origin of Jirahar, Ahdari, Tarkhan, or a complete border population.

**Final ruling:** `retain`.

# Group IV — Ättar Divergence and Atherian Long Formation

## TF-10 — Before the Empty Halls

TF-10 fits between Orphaned southern reassembly and the War of the Empty Halls. It shows one coastal hall preferring permanent Grobi watch over a genuine northern kin obligation. The decision saves southern households while leaving northern relatives to death and dispossession.

This is one institutional divergence, not the Folk–Ättar ethnogenesis by itself. It neither anticipates the four pact-kindreds as already supernatural nor explains Mother Moon's bargain.

**Final ruling:** `retain`.

## TF-11 — One Harbor Tyrant-Founder, Not Another Signature Company

Davidic ascent is already distributed among the Crownless Company, Low Door Companions, Six at the Cinder Inn, and other seeds. TF-11 remains useful only if its maritime and individual function stays narrow.

The revision denies the ruler's followers permanent company identity, fixes only one already inhabited harbor, and preserves older archaeology beneath the conquest layer. The oar ceremony is a later civic object, not an original relic proving kingship. The ruler's mutiny, protection of followers, raids, captive sales, dispossession, and murder of a critic remain inseparable.

**Final ruling:** `revise`, correction incorporated.

## TF-12 — Compact First, Fabricated Precedence Later

The Celestial Compact must remain a real compact of twelve real cities. TF-12 originally risked implying that Caleran invented the cities' common membership and thereby replaced their political agency with an archive trick.

The event now occurs after the Compact exists. During coercive consolidation, Caleran acquires archives after internal war, maps different founders onto a standard sequence of offices, and recuts history to make Caleran's precedence ancient. Resistant custodians are imprisoned or executed and sanctuaries confiscated. The fraud changes memory and constitutional prestige; it does not erase people from causality, explain Orthea, or create the Year of Standing Stars.

**Final ruling:** `revise`, correction incorporated.

## Source-Fidelity and Hardness Check

No correction removes a causal wound:

- TF-01 retains civil killing over succession.
- TF-02 retains requisition deaths and protected culpability.
- TF-03 retains massacre, enslavement, betrayal, and land seizure.
- TF-04 retains child hostage-taking, execution, and indiscriminate retaliation.
- TF-05 retains occupation, killing, captive sale, and retaliatory destruction.
- TF-06 retains hereditary labor, slavery, war profiteering, and starvation.
- TF-07 retains deliberate water closure, road deaths, and reprisal killing.
- TF-08 retains captive loss, confiscation, destitution, and kin-slaying.
- TF-09 retains constrained marriage, captive exchange, demanded betrayal, and murder.
- TF-10 retains abandonment, death, and dispossession under a genuinely difficult choice.
- TF-11 retains piracy, slave trading, dispossession, murder, and abuse of rule.
- TF-12 retains coercive falsification, imprisonment, execution, and sanctuary seizure.

Joshua and Judges remain author-facing moral and narrative grammars. Nothing in either text, the Prophet's final command, Ark descent, or later victory grants Atheria a divine title to conquered land.
