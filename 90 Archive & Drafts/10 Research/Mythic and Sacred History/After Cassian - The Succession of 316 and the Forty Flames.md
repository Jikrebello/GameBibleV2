# After Cassian — Development Record

The approved history is recorded in [[The Reign of Sevrin]] and [[The Forty Flames]]. This authorial record preserves the reasoning and historical sources behind those articles. The executions in Cassian's household and the Wager remain unresolved. [[Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR|Cassian and the Four Sees]]

## Sevrin, 316–348 AR

Cassian gives Sevrin charge of the southern reserve and its winter depots while his eldest son is still heir. Sevrin comes to power through an appointment that gave him years of dealings with pay officers, garrison towns and the Folk communities that supply scouts and livestock. When Cassian executes the eldest son and the empress, Sevrin retains that command. The published orders give him the right to muster and pay the reserve. The court has no publicly witnessed designation making him sole heir.

In **316**, the capital guard acclaims Sevrin after the treasury issues the next pay warrant under his seal. The eastern commanders withhold recognition long enough to demand confirmation of their appointments, pay claims and revenue for the road garrisons. Their alternative is **Adran**, Cassian's nephew and an officer with clients among the eastern commands. Adran will accept confirmation as eastern commander under Sevrin if Caleran honours its debts, protects his household and hears petitions over property seized in the last court purge. Sevrin agrees. He enters Caleran, is recognised as emperor and leaves Adran in command under revocable warrant. The capital controls imperial appointments and the main treasury. The eastern road command keeps a dependable share of its own revenues. The accord averts another march on Caleran.

Astrion is born c. **317 AR**, a year after the succession, to Cassian's nephew. That makes him Cassian's great-nephew and leaves room for his established age of thirty-four at his death in 351. He is raised by Flame tutors in a family whose position depends on Sevrin's settlement. His interest in Star learning can begin with archives and staff retained in the eastern administration. His accession depends first on a command, soldiers and a treasury agreement. His subsequent religious policy can then divide some of the people who accepted his accession. [[Named Figures of the Known World]] · [[The Conversion of the Empire]]

Sevrin continues to fund recognised churches. Caleran brings his court petitions, Aurelium records the regional settlements, Halmenth's burial and relief stewards keep enforcing access under the 297 ruling, and Sermora can work through overseas hosts even when an imperial convoy is withdrawn. None has to declare Cassian a saint to have a grant confirmed. Sevrin's refusal to release the evidence on his father's executions gives families a reason to distrust imperial thanksgiving rites while their bishops continue using the property courts.

Constantine's successors included sons and a nephew. Eutropius records the collapse of that arrangement into killing and competing claims. Ammianus describes Julian's acclamation during a dispute over transferring troops. Those precedents give Sevrin and Astrion an immediate problem of rival commands and soldiers' households. The Atherian settlement of 316 rests on one emperor and a negotiated provincial command. ## The Forty Flames, winter 290 AR

The earlier conversion overview preserved a three-day rising ending in a massacre. The older Ironvale Life preserved a second interval of three days in an unlit furnace and used a legacy c. 470 AE date. The approved date, winter **290 AR**, puts the affair inside the closing prosecutions of 288–291, when the Caleran court already targeted gatherings, officers and their books. It gives workers a reason to fear political prosecution and gives the court a reason to keep the Ashforges supplying its army. [[The Forty Flames]] · [[The Church after Qiryath - From Exile to Legalization]] · [[Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)]]

1. **The rising lasts three days.** Inspectors in the Ashforges find enslaved workers singing Redeemer hymns and arrest several people. An order to transfer prisoners threatens to separate them from their families. Workers in adjoining crews break open the holding yard, seize food and open an outbound gate. Some fight the guards. Others lead children and injured workers out along the fuel track. The unrest draws people who share the danger of sale and punishment across more than one religion. Troops retake the yards on the third day and kill people during the assault and reprisals.
2. **The furnace confinement lasts another three days.** After the yards fall, the authorities identify forty prisoners as members of the forbidden assembly. They offer release from the furnace sentence to anyone who renounces the Redeemer and names the hosts who kept the assembly. The prisoners refuse. Guards bar the doors of a disused, unlit furnace and leave the flue open to the winter air. Over three nights cold, lack of water and wounds kill them. The official record counts the forty admitted and describes their deaths as exposure. It leaves out the demands made before the doors were shut.
3. **The flame appears when the doors open.** A brief, genuine, unconsuming flame is visible on the dead when a guard and forge workers open the chamber. The furnace stays cold. The prisoners have already died. A worker's account carries the sign into the local Life. Later claims that the bodies stayed incorrupt or that the Ashwake Veil warms during prayer retain their own uncertainty.
4. **Forty die. Thirty-nine are handed over.** Before the joint burial, a porter lets one family remove its dead in private for fear that the authorities will use the funeral to identify surviving kin. The common burial tally records thirty-nine bodies. An account that one of the Forty escaped and founded a forge-convent grows from that discrepancy and from memories of real fugitives. The private grave and its occupant's name remain unknown to later custodians.

## What legalization changes for Ironvale

The workers' households had buried and remembered the dead in private. After 292 they can petition for the burial ground as a place held for their common use. The furnace was part of a working estate before the rising. The petition establishes access to the graves, and a later lease from the forge estate lets the congregation use the disused furnace precinct for worship. Households contribute fuel, guard the site and negotiate safe entry around shift changes. The annual **Ashwake** grows from reading the names and lighting lamps at dawn. The site becomes the Sanctuary of the Forty over time.

Public recognition in Cassian's reign is local. Aurelium receives copies of the prison count and burial tally and circulates the Life. Other sees adopt the commemoration in their own time. The later **Council of Second Flame** remains in the tradition as a confirmation of the observance. Its AR date and institutional setting remain open. The legacy **491 AE** label needs checking against the chronology of Eclessian independence. The identification of individual remains and the provenance of the Emberbrands remain subject to evidence. Menos's earlier Ironvale risings and death in 45 AR, the Sarqen rising c. 189–192, and the Saintmaker's own life each have their own causes and witnesses.
