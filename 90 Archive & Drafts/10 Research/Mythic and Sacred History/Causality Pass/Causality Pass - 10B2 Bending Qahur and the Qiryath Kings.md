# Causality Pass — 10B2 Bending Qahur and the Qiryath Kings

> [!note] Record of applied bends, 14 November 2026
> Written under [[Causality Pass - Direction]] on the creator's instruction to bend the sequences that the [[Real-History Concordance]] listed as still close to their analogues: "Qahur as Egypt" and "the Qiryath judges and kings cycles". The bends are already written into the reader articles this writer owns (listed under [[#Files changed]]). Everything that other files must change is in the [[#Integration patch]]. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## Summary

- **Qahur.** The Hyksos expulsion becomes a foreclosure: a dead merchant's Second House takes Qemaret through its living factor, Aramen, and the empire he founds spends two centuries paying the dead. Amarna becomes the living crown's war on its dead landlords, which the dead win from inside the palace when the sun-king's heir takes the White Measure rite. Soldiers, not a restoring queen, end it, and the capital stays inhabited as a dead king's rent town. Zannanza's murder becomes a prince lost inside that sealed palace.
- **Qiryath.** Gideon becomes a named-petitioner bargain with the shelf's water that passes down a sister's line. Samson becomes a drover whose own fire kills his betrothed and who ends as gate captain of the fortress he fought. Jephthah's vow becomes a broken harvest truce whose surety is killed by that gate captain. Saul to David, Bathsheba and Uriah, Nathan and Amnon become **the stair**: the sign's reliability makes guardians into kings, a fevered guardian's dropped basket makes a wood-carrier guardian, and a widow's public question about whom the stones answer unmakes the claim. Naboth, Carmel and the Kishon become **the image on the stair**, a custodian killed for his witness-staff, diviners thrown down a cistern they had drained, and a prophet unhoused by his own judges. That morning becomes the precedent for Neshkar II's demand of 746. Psalm 137 becomes a three-fingered pipe.
- **Kept outcomes.** Qahur remains an Orphaned river civilisation of dynasties, temple estates and the White Measure; the Aramen empire and the Dead Landlords end of c. 858 stand; the 903 stoppage stands; Arqeshite pressure, Eryndor, Neresh and Vasharan supremacy follow as before; the House of Zaheret and Tazirun Zaheret (c. 548) stand. Qiryath's judges become protector-kings; First Qiryath falls in 742; the Withholding (743–654), exile and return are untouched.
- **Kept victims.** Every account of victims and atrocities in the old sequences is still on the page; where the incident was bent, the dead have a native cause. A table below maps each.

## Qahur

### Old beat sequence

1. A mixed foreign-led lower-river dynasty (Yasharet, c. 1248–1076) with foreign chariot crews; southern temples call it foreign.
2. Its last kings demand southern grain; Sakhret rebels; Aramen, raised as a hostage at its court, opens the capital through friendships and a gate; massacre; the foreign crown ends. Later "restoration poetry". (Hyksos, Kamose and Ahmose.)
3. Aramen's heirs build the widest empire. (New Kingdom.)
4. Tomb-workers' strike, 903. (Deir el-Medina; already native through the Copper Orchard.)
5. Dead landlords bankrupt the crown, c. 858. (Already native.)
6. Ithren III builds a new capital, closes the temples and imposes an exclusive sun cult (842); he dies (821); his heir holds the capital seven years; Queen Zaheret reopens the temples and disperses the court (814); the capital is abandoned. (Akhenaten, Amarna, Tutankhamun, Horemheb.)
7. A foreign prince sent to the Basin is murdered on the road (814), and his father uses the death to widen a war. (Zannanza; Narethi's marriage request already cut by 01.)

### New sequence and its causes

1. **Credit (1126–c. 1084).** Khaseret's House of the Returning Cargo goes on lending after her death. The Yasharet crown pledges its grain fleet, store courts and river-gate tolls. Its court hires Gateway pilots and dry-road caravan masters (the chariot crews go).
2. **The river (c. 1085–1084).** Two low floods, the Years of the Bare Quays, leave the pledged grain unharvested. *Human cause: weather.*
3. **Default (c. 1083).** Iddar Yasharet's priests preach that a dead woman can own nothing under the sun, and he seals the stores against her stewards. *Human choice, and the seed of the Radiance's doctrine two centuries later.*
4. **The factor (from c. 1081).** Aramen of Sakhret, raised as a hostage at Qemaret, is the House's factor on the cataract road. The crown has not paid the Sakhret garrison for three seasons; Aramen pays and feeds it from the House's warehouses, and pays Qemaret's river-gate guards the same way. *Supernatural cause: a Patron's claim, carried out by her Material agent (Package A's Patron rules).*
5. **The foreclosure (c. 1076).** The gate guards open a river gate to the men who have fed them. The soldiers treat the store courts as collateral: royal household, preaching priests, rival-creditor merchants and sheltering civilians are killed; house searches and reprisals last months. The House's cellars admit clients with clear accounts and leave inherited defaulters to die outside. Iddar is walled alive into the harbour vault as "returned cargo". Talzara's granddaughter escapes. Aramen stops the killing when the harvest organisation fails.
6. **The crown the dead built (1076–858).** Aramen's poets invent the southern restoration; Qemaret calls him the Factor. The "foreign" households stay. The first share of every royal harvest goes to the House for a generation, and the conquests (mines, caravan stages, Gateway ports) pay the dead as well as the living. The Dead Landlords end of c. 858 completes the founding.
7. **The Radiance (c. 842–821).** In a Basin owned by its dead, Ithren III makes a faith of taking land back: the sun owns what it shines on; the dead own nothing. Tomb estates, mortuary temple fields and Second-House endowments go to the garrison households who had been "paid in graves". Aseret is built on confiscated tomb land and forbids burial; its dead are ferried to pits at dusk. Closures, smashed images, forced processions, killed custodians and executed children stay; so do the opened tombs, exposed attendants, released captives and Nemeret's double-dealing tomb (now telling the confiscators where the richest vaults lie). Tamekha withholds her dreams from the middle river. *Supernatural: the Radiance's Pride Mask answers through dreams; Patrons wounded by stripped metal.*
8. **The heir (c. 821–815).** Deshren, Ithren's son, has a wasting sickness and a faith that promises nothing after noon. The Copper Orchard's masters bring Tamekha's dreams back; he returns her fields and takes the regimen. Aseret's treasuries, full of tomb metal, become a tomb's endowment, and the confiscated deeds become the substance of his house. *Human: fear of death; supernatural: the rite.*
9. **The dead king (c. 815).** The rite succeeds; the palace's inner courts are sealed with the deeds inside. His stewards collect rent from the allotment households in a dead king's name.
10. **The soldiers' crown (815–814).** Zaheret of Tenaret, boat-owner's daughter and widow of an allotment captain, leads the allotment households. Stewards, Radiance officials and anyone associated with Aseret are killed; their families lose house and land. Zaheret is crowned at Tenaret in 814 and keeps the temple fields for the soldiers. Temples recover estates piecemeal over a century, often on White Measure credit.
11. **Aseret (from 815).** Inhabited, paying rent to a sealed palace, avoided by boatmen after dark. The deeds inside are still cited in 350 BR.
12. **Ashkur (814).** The dead king's fled stewards reach Arqeshite road stations with copies of their deeds, which are good only if the dead king's house confirms them. Merod II sends Ashkur to escort them to Aseret. Ashkur and three exiles enter the sealed courts with their deed-boxes, and the doors close. Arqeshite histories say murder; Qahuri histories say the dead king kept him. Merod widens an intervention already under way.

## Qiryath judges and kings

### Old beat sequence

1. **Tovan (c. 1290; Gideon and Abimelech).** Messenger; father's idol; two signs with a clay measure overnight; small night force; horns and lamps in grain jars; raiders kill one another; refused kingship; a ring from each household made into an oracle standard; many wives; a lesser son murders his half-brothers on the threshing floor.
2. **Hador (c. 1170; Samson).** Strongman; marriage feast; toll "paid" with the cloaks of men he killed; burns the fortress fields; his bride and her father burned by the enemy; private war; his own elders bind him and hand him over; set to grind at a mill; dies killing his captors at their feast.
3. **Othan and Seleth (c. 1140; Jephthah).** Vow to give the first who greets him; his daughter comes out with a hand drum; killed; women's lament.
4. **Barakhel (Gideon again).** Feigned dismissal, small night force, stampede.
5. **Yedran (c. 985–925; Saul to David, Bathsheba, Nathan, Amnon, Absalom).** Goat-keeper; sanctuary singer; kills a champion; serves the protector; marries his daughter; protector's jealousy; outlaw years with debtors; protector killed by foreign-backed raiders; returns as Guardian. Officer Uriad starved at his post while Yedran feasts a marriage; widow's public accusation; confession; a son rapes their sister at the grain rooms and a half-brother kills him; another son revolts.
6. **Qoram, Zimra, Tobel, Haviel (c. 842–835; Ahab, Jezebel, Naboth, Elijah).** Foreign queen with her god's diviners; vineyard refused; false witnesses accuse its owner of cursing the ruler; executed; land taken. Drought; contest at a threshing yard; white fire on a soaked offering; rain; diviners killed at a dry channel; queen swears to kill the prophet; flight; fed by the queen's own people; return; the gate.
7. **Sameth (exile; Psalm 137).** Refuses to perform before the captor's image and loses two fingers.

### New sequence and its causes

1. **Tovan (c. 1290).** House Sereq pens pledge-children in a dry wadi. In an abandoned cistern older than the villages, something in the deep water answers Tovan's name and offers a bargain: the streams run where he names while his line calls them at each first rain. He names the camp; the stream comes out of season; raiders, animals and the penned children drown; villagers flail to death the raiders who ask for mercy. The road judges refuse his household an office (no office on a bargain). The villages reckon a household's line through mothers, so at his death the water answers his sister Imra's children. His sons seize Imra's youngest son on the old threshing floor to make him call the water for them; the boy misses the rite; the stream drowns the boy and four of the sons. Imra's daughter keeps the rite; *feed the cistern house; do not follow it*. *Supernatural: a named-petitioner Menhir bargain (exact performance, line as reckoned that day). Human: the judges' law, the sons' greed.*
2. **Tirzah (c. 1210).** Unchanged; native.
3. **Hador (c. 1166).** Gedran's false scales. Hador kills toll guards over his father's mules. Nareh's father, the weigher, gives the orchard carts true weight; he and Nareh are shut in the grain pit; Hador fires the store-house to free them and they die in the smoke (each side blames the other; the lament blames both). Private war (guards, labourers, villagers killed); Gedran closes the road; the elders unhouse Hador; Gedran hires him as gate captain; he weighs the orchard carts for life; his war's prisoners are sold east and many die on the winter roads. *Human throughout.*
4. **Othan and Seleth (c. 1137).** A harvest truce with Gedran, with Seleth as surety under the road custom that a surety's life is forfeit if her side breaks the peace. A herd boy reports a half-manned gate on feast night; the judges beg Othan to ransom her or take her place; he refuses lest the fortress be warned and attacks early. The aged Hador kills the sureties, Seleth and two elders' children, before the gate falls, and dies at it; Gedran's tolls end. Disputed versions (mistaken night, secret ransom, a servant girl). Seleth's Bread; no commander's child is accepted as surety again. *Human: a broken truce, a misheard or misreported night.*
5. **Barakhel.** Tarkhan camel riders hold three wells on the raiders' road home; the raiders surrender thirsty on safe conduct and are executed at the wells; his sons sell the wells' water to the families he rescued. *Human: land and water.*
6. **The stair (from c. 1020).** The guardian carries the appointed offering; the crowd reads cool stones as his confirmation; the stones are always cool, so an emergency command becomes a reign. *Supernatural sign, human misreading.*
7. **Yedran at the stair (c. 959).** Shaqel Oram, guardian and of a custodial house, collapses with fever on the stair; Yedran, an altar wood-carrier climbing behind, catches the basket; an appointed offering cannot be halted, so he carries it; the stones are cool; Shaqel dies before spring; the crowd carries Yedran to the council. *Contingency: a fever, a dropped basket.*
8. **The ford (c. 945).** Yedran leaves a ford post in mid-fight to carry the spring offering and takes his best spearmen to the paved yard; Hanel bar Sem and most of the garrison are killed; villagers behind the ford are sold. *Human choice made because of the sign's reading.*
9. **Rava's question (c. 945).** At the autumn festival Hanel's widow climbs ahead of the offering and asks the custodians aloud whom the stones answer. Mikalet bat Oram, Shaqel's daughter and eldest custodian, answers: only the offering. The bearer is chosen by lot thereafter. Mikalet's motive is disputed in-world.
10. **The grain rooms (c. 940–934).** Drought; each son holds a grain room. The keeper of the lower rooms lets his guards take women drawing their portion, his half-sister among them. The women of the lower town break the door and kill him on the threshold. Yedran hangs four of them. Villages and road houses feed the quarters directly; the remaining sons fight; one raises rural followers; the house breaks after Yedran's death (c. 934). Government by council, custodians, road judges and an emergency guardian follows.
11. **The image on the stair (c. 829–826).** Qoram marries Zimra of House Keresh; her portable image of the Crowned Countenance gets a court shrine. Her diviners ask that it stand on the stair at the autumn offering; Qoram argues from Rava's answer that it cannot matter. Tobel bar Yamin, elder of the third custodial house, refuses to witness beside it; soldiers kill him in his doorway for the witness-staff; a pliant cousin takes the seat; the stones are cool; the diviners proclaim the image received; the news goes downriver. Levy defaulters lose terraces. Haviel denounces it; spared; workshop destroyed; two labourers sold. *Supernatural: a real sign that answers only the offering; the Countenance fed by the proclamation. Human: Qoram's argument, Tobel's refusal.*
12. **The cistern shaft (c. 823–821).** Drought; the diviners wash the image daily with the lower town's cistern water while its ration falls to a cup. Haviel leads the water-carriers into the image-house; the diviners are thrown down the shaft of the cistern they drained while he watches. Rain four days later, claimed by three parties. The road judges condemn Haviel on witnesses for killing without trial and unhouse him; half his followers leave.
13. **The gate (c. 817).** Qoram shuts out the families living beyond the walls; Haviel is among them; the rain-swollen beam rises for six women. The Lower Stair counts it a sign; his followers dispute whether it pardoned him.
14. **746.** River courts remember the image's cool morning; Neshkar II's demand relies on it ("as it had once before"); Elior's council refuses because it remembers what Zimra's diviners made of that morning. The Withholding follows as canon.
15. **Sameth (exile).** A sluice winch in a Nemeru canal gang takes two fingers; overseers send him back to the winch; he invents a three-fingered pipe fingering; his best-known tune sets the Book of the Unanswered Years' empty lines; his daughter sings at Neshkar's court and ransoms relatives.

## Authorial truths

- **Khaseret** directed the foreclosure through her stewards and factor; her claims were real under the House's own terms and the crown had pledged what it could not deliver. **Iddar Yasharet** died in the harbour vault; whether his soul was bound as an attendant is protected.
- **The Radiance** keeps its Package B identity: a dependent Pride Mask fed by praise of the sun-king, neither the sun nor any Menhir. It lost its centre when Aseret was sealed and starved in the garrison towns within a generation; no Idol succession.
- **Deshren's rite succeeded.** His complete human soul is bound in the **Court at Noon**, a Second House anchored in the sealed palace and the stripped tomb metal of Aseret's treasuries, with the confiscated deeds as part of its substance. He is the only crowned founder. His stewards' rent demands are his. His accessibility in 1360 is protected.
- **Ashkur Ubar** died inside Aseret's sealed courts with the three exiled stewards; whether any of their souls were bound is protected (replacing "the killer of Ashkur Ubar").
- **Tovan's bargain** was with a place-bound Menhir court person of the Reqa shelf's karst water. It bound his line as the shelf villages reckoned it on the day of the petition (through mothers). The drowning on the threshing floor was the bargain's exact performance failing: the rite was missed and the water ran where no one named it. Menhir law decided who drowned; it proved nothing about justice.
- **Qiryath's sign before 743** was never withheld at an appointed offering. Its constancy is what made the stair a throne. The cool stones under Qoram's image (c. 826) answered the appointed offering, as always; the image's presence had no bearing on the sign. The Crowned Countenance fed on the proclamation. This does not bear on the cause of the Withholding, which stays unresolved.
- **Mikalet's answer** was true doctrine; her motive was both doctrine and her father's grievance.
- **Haviel's earlier flame and rain** are withdrawn. The rain four days after the killings was weather. The gate's lifting (c. 817) remains a genuine sign answering the families' rescue; it did not pardon Haviel.
