# Causality Pass — 11A1 Second Bend: Gateway Wars and the Late Republic

> [!note] Record of a second bend written into canon, 14 November 2026
> The first pass ([[Causality Pass - 02 Gateway Wars and the Late Republic]]) gave these sequences native mechanisms. An adversarial reading still called the beats in advance, because the skeleton survived: the same people met the same fates in the same order, and the late republic ran at a near-constant +326 years from its analogue. This record lists each predicted beat and what replaced it, the new sequence and dates, a table of rename proposals for the creator, and the integration patch for files this writer does not own. Rules: [[Causality Pass - Direction]]; Standing Recontextualization Rules 6–8 in [[Real-History Concordance]]. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## Summary

Two engines already in canon now decide the order of fates.

- **The names open the doors.** The Provider's granaries opened only to a child's name entered in the collectors' books. The towns' carriers win their strike and the council has to break its oath; Qarzeth's assembly votes to surrender the books and its god's doors stay shut. The third war begins from a surrender that hunger undid.
- **A Warden cannot close a Field without his named enemy.** Harod never enters Caleran. Casvilar accepts the Red Field at Kellovar in 383 against Drenoc, who walks into a river-court's ford and is never produced. For thirteen years Casvilar cannot be recalled, discharge his soldiers or go home, and his augurs count first his banker and then the senate as contestants. In 370 he comes down to the muster ground to meet a young man entered as Drenoc's son.

**Outcomes kept.** Caleran becomes hegemon of the Compact. Qarzeth's capital is destroyed in 525–520; the spared towns survive and their Table Oath founds the Nerethan kingdom. The republic breaks down into rule by commanders. Casvilar is killed in 370 by seven challengers who strike before the contest opens. The Red Field and its taboo exist from 609–607. Calvena is alive. Rethmar is a northern disaster decided by water. Every victim and atrocity of the earlier text is still on the page, re-caused where its cause changed. The protected items stand untouched: the bitten wax, the two lists, "it broke when the last name was spoken", the Noon Roll hour and its rotting harvest, the empty seat.

## Authorial truth

The rulings are in [[Authorial Notes from Reader Articles - Atherian West to 350 BR]] (dated notes under *The Gateway Wars* and *The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic*) and in the 11A1 amendments to [[Atherian Palace and Maritime Histories - Integration Review]] and [[Atherian Formation and Republic - Delivery Review]]. In brief:

- The collectors' books were the dependent Gluttony Mask's invitation route. No power refused Qarzeth's surrender; the feeding arrangement went unfed.
- The draw fell on Zamaret's camp because every table laid in the Provider's name belonged to the arrangement.
- The ford below Kellovar is the river-court of Hecatara's grammar in [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]] §9. Drenoc was a named petitioner; the price was the delivery of a traveller (himself) and surrendered destinations. He died among the southern watch halls about 375. The claimant of 370 was not his son.
- In Astaroth's grammar the contest at Kellovar ended when Drenoc ceased to contest the ground. The thirteen-year open Field was augurs' law. The acceptance of the claimant in 370 was a new, real contest, and the seven broke its bounds.
- Counting unwilling men as contestants (the nine senators, Ostar, the signatories of 377) is human law and broke no taboo, because none of them was killed as a helpless substitute. The Writing-In of 367 is where that changes.

No entry in [[The Weird of History — The Unexplained Register]] is touched or explained.

## What the founding writer must know (370 BR onward)

1. **Casvilar** is born in 431 and dies at sixty-one. He has been Warden since Kellovar (383), thirteen years, not since a frontier altar in 376. He slept in a tent in the court of his own house.
2. **Two Fields.** His Field against Drenoc dies with him, as Varroth's ruling of 367 already implies. The fouled contest of 370 is the challenge of the seven, which he had accepted in order to close the old Field. Varroth's ruling and the twenty-year hunt are untouched.
3. **The seven.** Mavren Seneth commanded the river line at Kellovar, watched Drenoc enter the ford, fought for the senate at Velos and took the Warden's pardon. This is compatible with "accepted pardon after fighting for Veyr" if "for Veyr" becomes "for the senate"; Veyr was not at Velos. Corial Draven is a ship-owner who held a third of Ostar's paper when the ledgers burned, not a commander with frustrated ambitions; his going to sea afterwards fits. The seventh name in the register is "the son of Drenoc of Kellovar", a young man of the river halls. He is the natural identity of the challenger who "went south to Darneth" and died there about 362. Deceran Tolm is quartermaster of the muster ground and keeper of the register; he entered seven lawful names and fixed the day, and whether he knew is disputed. He is no longer a trusted friend who betrays.
4. **A ladder to the Writing-In.** Augurs counted the nine senators of 452–449, Ostar in 379 and the signatories of 377 as contestants. The pardoned of 371 had their names left in the augurs' book "with a line through them, still legible". Velsar watched all of it. The Writing-In can start from that book.
5. **Veyr** never fought Casvilar. Velos is 377. Atheria had two governments from 377 to 373, and the eastern one ate the gateway kingdom's grain; Veyr came to Sarqen in 373 for that grain, as a named contestant of the Field but not as a beaten fugitive. Ilamar 373–371 is unchanged. Calvena's marriage is 389; her refusal (early 377) is unchanged.
6. **Ostar** never commanded, was alive until about 374 and died at Vorthara. The Spire Pursuit and the Kept Doors are **410**, not 399.
7. **Harod** never marched on Caleran and made no confiscations. He killed two senators on the muster ground in 452–449, so "no blow had been struck" on his Field is no longer true, though his named enemies never came. After his death his Field lay vacant and armies were raised and paid off as before; that contrast with the fouled Field of 370 still holds. Thalion's fear of the Red Star can draw on his uncle's thirteen years as well as on Harod's twenty-five.
8. **The veterans** on the Noon Roll lands held their plots undischarged, as soldiers of an open Field, and could not sell or bequeath them. The great discharge of 350–346 is the first time those plots become property.
9. **The Chain Peace** put every harbour's chain under its own wardens' oath never to raise it at another city's order or against a vessel in distress. Melvo's use of Pyrassa's chain at Marelith in 355 stands, and gains an edge: Pyrassa's wardens lent the chain they had sworn to keep.
10. **The names of 609** read out in 353 are those of children delivered as a pledge of peace two years before they were killed.
