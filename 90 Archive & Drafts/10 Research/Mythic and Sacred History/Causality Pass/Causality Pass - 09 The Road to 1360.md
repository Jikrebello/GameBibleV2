## Summary

The present day was predictable because the road to it followed real 1340–1580 in order: plague, bank, bought pontiff, posted theses, burned reformer, peasant war, systematiser, council, territorial peace, prophet-preacher, ocean route, apparition to shepherd children, and a "stagnant" sultanate. This pass keeps every anchor on the 1360 board and most named dates, and replaces the engine under them with five world-native pressures:

1. **The Unsent Dead.** The Grey Sweat was a failure of burial before it was a failure of medicine. Everything after it turns on who can send the dead and what a paid promise to send them actually does.
2. **The test nobody will run.** In 1227 Nikandros I refused to let the Sanctum try the Great Lamp of Veridun. From that refusal, through Bruckmoor, the Ossuary Crown, Sarnwell and the Council of Veridance, the world learns that a proof can win or destroy a church, and by 1360 nobody tests anything that matters.
3. **The fading grace.** Grey Sister's hymn-blessed horses were thinning towards ordinary. A captive Velian farrier's blessing on one black colt in 1249 renewed the grace in a single line, and that colt made Qarayel. The Black Wind's rise, its extraction, its 1300 limit, the Partition and the four Hordes all follow the grace's clock.
4. **The witness bench.** The Sweat killed the Covenant's witnesses faster than its debtors. Widowed instruments opened frontier forts, the Pageless Prince walked out of a lost page, and the Ahdanite Guard became the bench without which no Sultan-Caliph can accede. "Guard-brokered" is a legal description, not a decline.
5. **Credit and the paper of the dead.** The Medori rose by clearing the estates of the plague dead and the payments of the living for them, bought a Primarch, took the assurance registers as surety and became the bank of every side. The unsung assurance letters, "dead paper", became a market in claims on named souls.

The supernatural drives or bends about three-fifths of the turning points (see [[#Blend check]]). The period gains three Lives in 1250–1305: **Saint Gerlind of Lindenfurt**, who sang the plague dead out for bread; **Gavrin the Farrier**, the Grey Uncle, whose mercy on a sick foal armed the Black Wind; and **Silvio Quarne, the Bridge-Seller**, who sold the Sanctum's unperformed promises to people who knew what they were worth.

## The five pressures

### 1. The Unsent Dead

- **Start (1232).** The Sweat kills faster than priests can bury. Eclessian quarantine doctrine judges a village by its rites: a sealed village with a living priest is clean, a healthy one with a dead priest is not. Bodies burn on riverbanks unblessed; the forty licensed ferries close. The ferry queues. Some of the dead walk, and the thin places multiply. Where the Ash-Walkers process and speak the names of the dead, some places close; where Bruckmoor's weeping Lady takes their offerings, grief is eaten.
- **Turning point (1252–1297).** The Sanctum answers the terror with the **Chancery of Sendings** and its Sending Houses: chapters paid to sing rites for named dead, sold by sealed letter. The trade works where the rites are sung, and that is why it grows. Under Alessior VI and his Cardinal-Treasurer Silvio Quarne it outgrows its singers. By the 1290s most letters are never sung, and the unsung ones are sold on as "dead paper". **Ansgar's Count** at Brenholt (1296–97) sets letters sold against rites sung and against the dead still walking in his parish.
- **Present (1360).** Assurance sales are abolished (Council of Veridance), but the gray market persists, the Order of the Lantern still sings the Great Arrears from registers it has to buy from the Medori, brokers press claims on named dead ([[G-49 - The Two Tags on One Dead Man|the workhouse tags]]), and the Unburnt Psalter sits in a lead vault.

### 2. The test nobody will run

- **Start (1227).** Nikandros I asks the Sanctum for soldiers. The price is that the Great Lamp of Veridun be carried to the Sanctum and tried by its relic court. He refuses: "A lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned." It is the first time a power refuses a test because the refusal is its sovereignty.
- **Turning point (1243–1352).** Bruckmoor's exposure (1243) shows that a test can destroy a shrine; the Ruling of the Hollow Mouth on the Ossuary Crown (1263) shows a test can disappoint a continent; Aldersbruck (1305) shows a test can go wrong in public, because Ansgar asked for fire and the Psalter did not burn; Sarnwell (1311) shows a test can vindicate. The Council of Veridance codifies the five tests (1337–52) and closes in the year of Vessing.
- **Present (1360).** Deterrence by unopened proof. The Sanctum has not ruled on Vessing; the Psalter stays in its vault; the Weiss water-test of 1342 stays sealed; Veridun's Synod will not authenticate the burning-archive vision; Caedros IV's flame-silences are watched and not examined.

### 3. The fading grace

- **Start (before 1225).** The First Catch horses carry the old remount blessing. Blessed stock stands before bound things; ordinary stock bolts. Each generation thins it. By the 1230s the wind-speakers' genealogies show the high lines failing, and clans that priced debts, dowries and word-prices in sacred foals face ruin. The steppe calls this **the Thinning**.
- **Turning point (1249–1304).** A Velian farrier, taken from a Covenant well-fort the Sweat had emptied, sings the old remount hymn over a dying black colt. The colt lives and its get stand like the First Catch horses. Its herd-master takes the horse's name, Qarayel, and builds a coalition by stud-right. The Black Wind (1265–1300) is the window of a renewed grace: Covenant well-wardens fail before it, so Iqashar pays. The grace thins again; in 1300 Iqashar's wardens hold and the payments stop. In 1304 the herds are partitioned by grace-line.
- **Present (1360).** Four Hordes whose characters follow what they inherited: White the numerous herds, Iron the best-kept lines leased to the Covenant, Moon the Standard of nine manes, Ash the poorest lines and the eastern road. The grace is thinning again everywhere.

### 4. The witness bench

- **Start (1236).** In the Covenant every bound person's death needs a witnessed release, and every garrison instrument needs living witnesses to hold. The Sweat kills the witnesses. Names pile up unreleased; heirs inherit the debts of the dead; garrison bonds are widowed and forts open their gates.
- **Turning point (c. 1240–1252).** Suhrab's most gifted son swears wavering garrisons to himself to stop the gates opening, and Suhrab lodges him on the Guard's evidence; Suhrab's last book (1243) lets a sealed register stand as witness and names the Guard's officer bench as competent witnesses for the Threshold; the **Pageless Prince** (1249–52), a lodged cousin whose page was lost, walks free under the law and has to be killed. From 1252 the Cull uses the knife and the blinding needle.
- **Present (1360).** Every accession since 1243 has been witnessed by the Guard. Marzuq III was placed by it in 1349 after a Cull. The Living and Deep Vault registers have never balanced since the Sweat. The Guard will not march far from Iqashar while the Threshold could fall vacant, which is one half of the armed frontier peace.

### 5. Credit and the paper of the dead

- **Start (1238).** The Sweat empties a third of the houses in Fiorenzia's Golden Book and the senate sells seats to keep a quorum; Bartolo Medori buys one. His counting-house clears the estates of the dead and, from 1252, carries the payments of the living to the Sending Houses across a dozen jurisdictions.
- **Turning point (1281–1346).** Bartolo's credit buys the Primarchy for Alessior VI (1281). When Alessior dies in debt (1296) the Bank takes the Chancery of Sendings' master registers as surety. In 1345 a Sermoran pamphlet calls the Medori vault "the Bank of the Dead"; Fiorenzia expels the family and Frate Severin takes the city.
- **Present (1360).** The Medori, restored in 1349, lend to Veridun, the Sanctum, the reformed duchies, Castara and, through intermediaries, the Ash Horde. They hold the registers the Lantern needs to sing the Arrears. Sestara finances everyone because everyone owes it for something the dead were promised.

## The re-caused history

Each turning point is marked **Keep**, **Bend**, **Cut** or **Add**, with the reason.

### I. 1225–1247: The Restorer and the Sweat

**Nikandros I and the Refusal of the Lamp (1225–1227).** **Keep, bend.** Nikandros, of a cadet Aetherion branch in Kedrava, had brought the Kedravan monastic contingents home in order from Qarath's Well because they refused to drink sworn water, and was crowned at Virelos in 1225 with the monasteries and Vlastrava behind him (the seam writer's [[High Noon and the Qarath Disaster]]). In 1227 he swallowed two centuries of schism and sent envoys to the Sanctum for soldiers. The Sanctum's price, under the Kindling Question, was concrete: the Great Lamp of Veridun would be carried east and tried before the Sanctum's relic court, and the Patriarch would accept the verdict. Nikandros refused in a letter whose last line every Thalmyric schoolchild learns: *a lamp that is tried is a lamp that is owned.* He fought on alone. *Why:* the analog (Byzantium begging the West) supplies the humiliation; the world-native bend makes the price a **test**, which is the seed of the Held Proofs engine and gives the liturgical line "the East sold us for a doctrine" a specific object.

**The Grey Sweat (1232–1247).** **Keep, bend.** Dates, mortality and the Ash-Walkers stand. The bends:
- *Rite-integrity quarantine* (already in the Torque Register) is told through the land: forty licensed ferries closed, bodies burned on riverbanks, villages sealed by rite. 
- *Thalmyria.* The Sweat reached Thalmyria by the Sestaran sea lanes c. 1234 and killed perhaps a sixth. Thalmyric parishes had married, local priests who died with their flocks, but laity sang the funeral hymns over their own dead where no priest survived, and Thalmyria came out of the Sweat with fewer haunted villages and a hardened conviction that the Flame answers the singer, not the office. *Why:* the review found the Sweat stopping at a confessional border; this gives the Kindling Question an empirical plague-year root and gives the later Freeflame a model it could see across the frontier.
- *The Covenant: the Emptying of the Benches (1236–1243).* As 06.6, plus the **Open Gates**: garrison instruments on the Qarath frontier and the Tarkhan wells needed living witnesses; when the witnesses died, commanders held their men's bonds widowed (the 931 precedent) and marched home. *Why:* this explains how Nikandros "won back half the loss" without Eclessian help.
- *Sestara.* The Golden Book lost a third of its houses by 1238 (see pressure 5).
- *The steppe.* Dispersed camps suffered least; the wind-speakers say the steppe's dead go with the wind. Tarkhan raiders took the craftsmen left in opened forts.

**The Open Gates (1238–1246).** **Add.** Nikandros marched into the Southern Deeds districts the bill for the water had taken and found forts open and garrisons gone home. He retook about half the loss in eight campaigning seasons, mostly without sieges, and resettled them with veterans. *Why:* a native cause for "won back half"; the other half explains itself in 1243.

**Suhrab's son and Suhrab's last book (c. 1240–1243).** **Keep the seam writer's bend; add the cause.** The seam writer has replaced Named Figures' execution (Suleiman and Mustafa) with a lodging: Guard officers swore that the prince had taken soldiers' oaths in his own name, Suhrab lodged his son's name in the Deep Vault, and the prince died in its cloister within the year, of fever according to the Vault and by the Guard's hand according to the Iqashar bazaar. This file supplies why the prince did it. In the Emptying, the garrisons of the Basin and the Qarath frontier were walking home on widowed instruments, and forts were opening to Nikandros. The prince, commanding there, swore the wavering garrisons to himself to hold them, which the Ledger's book against armies sworn to a living man forbade. The officers who testified belonged to the Guard houses that would gain most from his fall. Date: c. 1240. Suhrab's last book (1243) then let a sealed register stand where no witness survived, which re-bound the widowed garrisons and stopped the Open Gates, and (this file's addition) named the Guard's officer bench a competent witness for the Threshold's own instruments because the jurist colleges had been gutted. He died that year; his surviving son **Tahmur** *(new name)* acceded before the Guard bench. *Why:* the prince's crime grows out of the Sweat's custody crisis, the forts he tried to hold explain Nikandros's half-recovery, and "guard-brokered" becomes literal from 1243. The prince stays unnamed, as the seam writer left him; if a name is wanted, *Mihrzad* is offered in the patch.

**The Schism of the Twin Cardinals (1240–1242).** **Keep** from Aurelia's Figures of Power: plague-thinned conclaves elected rival heads who issued incompatible burial and succession rules. Lord Primarch **Gelasin II** *(new name)* closed it in 1242. *Why:* already canon; it is the Sweat entering the Sanctum's own succession.

**Bruckmoor (exposed 1243).** **Keep** as told in [[The Hunger Wars]] (c. 1203–1243; fed on Sweat grief in its last decade). Bruckmoor is the first great public test, and it destroyed what it tested. *Error to fix:* [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]] dates the apparition's beginning to 1243 (see patch).

**The Quiet Ninth (1247).** **Keep, unexplained.** The Sweat stopped everywhere within nine days of the Feast of Souls, and the Ash-Walkers disbanded mid-march. Covenant registers record the date and no cause. No document in this pass explains, redefines or attaches a mechanism to it. See [[The Grey Sweat - Authorial Truth]], rewritten to comply.

### II. 1247–1281: The Sending, the Black Colt and the Pageless Prince

**Land after the Sweat.** **Keep, bend.** Tenancy expands in western Eclessia because every lord needed hands and every village needed a priest. In Virelia the sabre-right magnates held the patronage of most parishes, and they used the quarantine rule: a village with no living priest was unclean and could be sealed. Survivors of sealed villages were resettled onto demesne under bond in exchange for a priest, a cemetery and burial in rite, **the bond of the clean village**. *Why:* the second serfdom now grows from the soul-logistics torque, and it explains why Virelian runaways go where Freeflame elders bury anyone.

**The March of the Redeemer's Sword (1258–1261).** **Keep, bend.** Southern lords with emptied estates marched into the deep cold forest to take Folk captives as labour; winter and the Grobi broke the march. *Why:* gives the existing failed crusade a plague-economy motive and keeps slavery on the page.

**Gerlind of Lindenfurt (c. 1248–1279).** **Add.** Great saint; see [[#Saint Gerlind of Lindenfurt]].

**The Ossuary Crown (1251–1263).** **Add** (Top-40 item 25, GP-IV-02). In 1251 salvagers who would not say where they had been brought a crown of fused bone, wire and orchard wood to an abbey fair in Brauvalis. It spoke in thousands of voices, naming plague dead and their last wishes. Families paid to hear their dead; claimants carried it into the plague-gap title suits, and its voices testified to wills no notary had drawn. The Sanctum seized it in 1254; a Brauvalis abbey, a Virelian magnate and a voice claiming to be the crown's own lawful keeper all sued for it. In 1263 the relic court gave the **Ruling of the Hollow Mouth**: *the dead do not testify through things.* The ruling was correct and nobody was comforted by it, because the voices knew things only the dead could know. If the dead could not speak, someone had to speak for them, and the Sending Houses grew on that despair. *Authorial truth:* the crown is the coherence-piece of a mature Ossuary Mind, an Astral person assembled from wrecked plague refuges and abandoned route offices in the Egg White, not the dead and not their collective soul ([[Great-Power Mythic Ecology and Planting Matrix]], GP-IV-02). Its custody in 1360 is split between a Sanctum reliquary, an abbey claim and a Patron's suit; whether the crown is the Mind's core stays open.

**The Chancery of Sendings (1252).** **Add, bending chantries.** Gelasin II reorganised the old assurance letters into the Chancery of Sendings: Sending Houses (chapters endowed to sing named rites for named dead), sealed letters sold to families, and the Medori counting-house to carry payments across jurisdictions. Where the rites were sung, the walking dead often stopped walking. That is why the trade spread. *Why:* the real chantry boom after the Black Death is the springboard; the world-native fact is that intercession works, so the trade is not fraud at birth.

**Gavrin and the Black Colt (1241–1249).** **Add.** Great saint, contested; see [[#Gavrin the Farrier, the Grey Uncle]]. *Why:* Qarayel's rise "has no cause" (review). The fading grace gives the steppe a crisis; the colt gives it an answer only this cosmology could produce.

**The Pageless Prince (1249–1252).** **Add.** **Prince Qaydar** *(new name)*, a Qahirid cousin lodged in the Vault under Suhrab for a palace plot, lived under guard in a walled garden at Al-Qahur. The register volume that held his lodged name had been burned with plague bedding in 1240. In 1249 he walked out of the garden, and the Court of Breaches held what the law said: *no page, no lodging.* The Basin's military households, whose fixed-term leases of 938 had run out and whose sons had died in the Emptying holding forts for nobody, rose for him. The Guard bench, having witnessed Tahmur, fought for the Threshold it had witnessed. Qaydar was taken in 1252 and killed. A Guard-Master's sentence became law in practice: *the Vault can lose a page; the grave cannot.* From then on the Cull of Brothers killed, blinded or lodged, and lodging was the mildest of the three. *Why:* this is the event inside the seam writer's statement that jurists date the Cull's turn "from the reigns that followed, when princes had learned that a court could undo a renunciation and the Guard could reach a lodged man": a court could also free one. It bends the Ottoman fratricide law into a consequence of the Sweat's bookkeeping catastrophe.

**The Kurultai of the Long Grass (1261).** **Keep, bend.** Qarayel did not unite the banners by conquest. He offered stud-right under guest-right: any clan that came to his fire could put mares to the Black Colt's sons. By 1261 the colt had sired nine proven sons, and the **Nine-Mane Standard** was braided from their manes. The kurultai was the clans coming to keep the grace in their herds. *Why:* the Genghis analog supplies the assembly; the cause is horses that stand before bound things.

**The Ruling of the Hollow Mouth (1263).** See the Ossuary Crown. **Nikandros I dies (1263); Evandros the Sower** *(new name)* succeeds. **Add.** Evandros spent his reign resettling the Open Gates districts and the emptied interior with Kedravan mountain families and veterans; Thalmyria was in no condition to exploit the Covenant's Black Wind years, and the Wind burned both sides of the Velian frontier.

**The Black Wind years (1265–1300).** **Keep, bend.**
- *1265.* The first long ride burned the largest of the Velian fairs, where Covenant name-brokers sold Tarkhan captives taken on the Nine Wells frontier. The fair paid thereafter, and so did the others.
- *Iqashar's escort fees.* The northern wells were held by bound wardens whose terror depended on enemy horses panicking. The Black Colt's get did not panic. Tahmur began paying in 1265, and his son **Marzuq I** *(new name; r. 1268–1301)* kept paying, calling it escort.
- *Sestara* accepted the grass-toll on the northern routes and moved trade to the sea, which made the Boiling Sea coast worth sailing.
- *Extraction without occupation* has two causes now: grace-horses win nothing in a siege, and the wind-speakers' genealogies told Qarayel the window would close.
*Why:* keeps every canon fact and gives each one a native clock.

**Gerlind's trial (1266) and death (1279); the embassy for Gavrin (1279–1281).** See the Lives.

### III. 1281–1305: Dead paper and the Black Wind's noon

**Alessior VI (1281–1296).** **Keep, bend.** A Malvora of Sestara, elected with Medori credit. *Correction:* the purchase was **Bartolo's**, not Lauro's (Lauro was born in 1289). Alessior's wars (Aurelian succession quarrels, the Primarchal share of the War of the Crimson Banner, 1288–95) and the rebuilding of the Sanctum's dome were paid for by industrial assurance sales. Alessior is the gaudy prince of the story. The engine is his treasurer. *Why:* keeps the Borgia surface and moves the cause to the setting's own soul-logistics.

**Silvio Quarne, Cardinal-Treasurer (1282).** **Add.** Great sinner; see [[#Silvio Quarne, the Bridge-Seller]]. Printed letters, the schedule of rates (the Twenty-Second Grievance's cheaper rate for a child), the blank letter, the household bond, the Gerlind seal (1284), the Saint-Leon burning (1285) and the sale of dead paper (from c. 1288).

**Meshalem (1288–1291).** **Keep.** The Jirahar claimant's return movement rode on roads the Black Wind had emptied of Covenant garrisons; his arrest and conversion in 1291 under Marzuq I stand as in Named Figures. No change to Jirahar canon.

**Stavren the Patient (1290–1329).** **Add.** Evandros's son. He avoided war for thirty years, paid for it with Sestaran loans, founded the war college at **Vatrin** c. 1300 (where Caedros IV trained), and lived to see his own line fail.

**Alessior's death and the registers (1296).** **Add.** Alessior died owing the Medori more than the Primarchal State could pay. Bartolo, near the end of his life, took the Chancery of Sendings' master registers as surety: every letter sold, every name, every House assigned to sing it. The Sanctum kept the right to redeem them and has never been able to afford it. *Why:* fulfils [[Medori Family - Authorial Truth]] ("what the family acquired through Alessior VI's Primarchy") with a concrete object and sets up 1345.

**Ansgar's Count and the Hundred Grievances (1296–1297).** **Keep, bend.** Ansgar of Brenholt, friar and Vestibule-doctrine lecturer, set the Sending House letters sold in Brenholt parish against the rites its House had sung: by his count, more than ten letters for every rite. He also counted the dead still seen at the mine-heads and the ford, and found them among the unsung names. His friars sang those names out themselves, and the walking stopped. The Grievances posted in autumn 1297 are an audit as much as a sermon, and their Hundredth Grievance is a challenge to a Fire test. *Why:* the posted theses are kept; the bend is that Ansgar's case rests on evidence only this world could give, and his challenge sets up Aldersbruck as a test.

**Escort fees stop (1300); Qarayel dies (1303); Partition of the Herds (1304).** **Keep, bend.** By the third generation from the colt the grace was thinning, and in 1300 Iqashar's wardens held against Tarkhan horses again; Marzuq I stopped paying. Qarayel, old, was preparing a war when he died in 1303, undefeated and unsucceeded. His grave stays a weird by policy. The Partition divided **the herds** by grace-line among his children's lines:
- **White** (the eldest line, through Temuchar): the most numerous herds, the least managed lines.
- **Iron** (the second line): the colt's daughters' lines, kept close and bred back by wind-speaker genealogy; the only horses on the frontier that will stand beside the Covenant's own bound things.
- **Moon**: the Standard of the nine manes, the oldest mares and the wind-speakers; the kurultai belongs to whoever keeps the Standard.
- **Ash**: the eastern grass, the poorest lines and the roads.
*Why:* bends "partition among sons" into a partition of a fading sacred resource, which gives each Horde its 1360 character.

**Aldersbruck (1305).** **Keep, bend.** Ansgar asked for fire and they burned him. His psalter, thrown on the pyre after him, came out of the ashes unburnt. His ashes were shovelled into the Weiss. **Lucian Ferro**, a twenty-six-year-old jurist from the Latinate south, sat as junior notary of the Synod and wrote the record. Rainald, the fourteen-year-old heir of Ironvale, watched beside his father. *Why:* the burning is kept; the bends put the systematiser and the sheltering prince at the pyre, so the movement's two architects are witnesses, and make the result of Ansgar's own challenge ambiguous.

### IV. 1305–1329: Water, oaths and the frontier

**The oath at the Weiss and the Ashenmen Risings (1306–1329).** **Bend.** In the spring of 1306 miners and farmers of Brenholt and the upper Weiss waded into the river below Aldersbruck, smeared their faces with its grey silt and swore to fight until the last false seal was burned. They were the Ashenmen. Their oaths held strangely well: no Ashenmen band ever broke a sworn word, deserted or sold out. That made them the hardest infantry in Eclessia, behind wagon-forts and literate quartermasters, and it made them impossible to negotiate with, because the oath named an end that no prince could grant. The Risings were twenty-three years of wagon-fort war won by nobody. They smashed Marian images (the guilt the movement's second generation handles badly), burned Sending Houses and were burned in turn. *Why:* the Peasants' War springboard is kept for its social causes (bond, tithe, Sending fees), but its length and its end now turn on the Preacher's Water. *Authorial:* why the water holds oaths is deliberately unresolved, even here, as is Ansgar's sainthood (the Psalter is a held proof).

**Sarnwell (1311).** **Keep, bend.** The Mother appeared to a crippled washerwoman in a valley the Ashenmen had stripped of images; the spring that opened there passes every relic-court test. The old Church read it as a rebuke to the image-breakers; reformed preachers called it bait. It became the template the Council of Veridance later wrote its five tests around. *Why:* Lourdes becomes the proof that protocol can vindicate, and the five tests acquire a model.

**Ferro and Rainald (c. 1312–1341).** **Keep, bend.** Ferro reached Ironvale c. 1312 and wrote *The Order of the Open Flame* (c. 1316): the Flame kindles wherever sanctity gathers, "the Fire keeps no landlord" (Ansgar's Eighty-Eighth Grievance), no assurance trade, congregations under tested elders, and the Sending sung by the congregation for anyone who dies among them. Rainald, duke from 1309, was converted by the book and by the record of the pyre. *Why:* keeps the canon ecclesiology and gives it the Sweat and Gerlind as its empirical ancestors.

**Iron Horde companies and the night riders (c. 1305–1318).** **Add.** **Marzuq II** *(new name; r. 1301–1349)* hired Iron Horde companies on word-price from 1304. Their horses stood beside devil-mantled riders and small contract engines, which Covenant horses would not. From c. 1310 mixed companies raided Thalmyric chapel-forts on the Velian frontier by night. *Why:* the review notes the Covenant files say nothing of 1318–25; this gives the Covenant an offensive and the Iron Horde its road into Covenant offices.

**Orinel (1318).** **Keep, bend.** The Night of Seven Flames: the chapel-fort's seven signal braziers failed one by one under a night assault by Iron-mounted night riders; Caedros led the dawn counterattack after the last went out. Songs say the bound riders smothered the fires and a bird of fire rose from the smoke. A Saronikan quartermaster's ledger, found a generation later, records the fort's oil bought from a contractor who cut it with water. The Church records an improbable victory. *Why:* a contingent cause sits beside the wonder and neither is ruled out.

**The Crimson Pilgrimage (1319–1322) and Zehrim (1324–1325).** **Keep.** As in [[Emperor Caedros IV Aetherion]], financed by Sermoran and Medori loans against Saronikan customs and antiquities concessions. **Add:** in 1326 the Court of Breaches ruled that the bound of Zehrim, whose local instruments Caedros burned on the altar, remained bound in the Living Register at Iqashar. Thalmyria calls them freed; the Covenant calls them absconded. Any of them who crosses north can be seized. Their descendants are among the mixed communities whose belonging Thalmyria still argues about. *Why:* gives Zehrim a legal-metaphysical consequence on both sides and roots a present tension.

**Stavren's sons and the adoption (1327–1329).** **Add.** Stavren's two sons died within a month of each other in the summer flux at Veridun in 1327. In 1328, over the objection of the Caleran houses, the old emperor adopted the hero of Zehrim, Caedros of a provincial Aetherion branch whose grandmother was Nikandros I's daughter, and died the next year. Caedros was crowned at Virelos c. 1329. *Why:* explains "acceded" and "claims this line", and makes the 1360 adoption faction cite Caedros's own accession.

**The Held Breath crossing (1328).** **Add.** A Castaran salt-hauler, the *Naris's Hand* under pilot **Ottavia Sele** *(new name)*, was caught at sea on a Breath day and crossed the flat hurricane belt to the Shattered Isles. She came back with green crystal, Expanse hardwood and a Viscaran chart-case from a wreck. Her log says a week; the calendar says otherwise, and Castara's pilots do not discuss it. The University rebuilt the Viscaran storm-keel from the wreck's lines, and by 1331 Castaran ships crossed in hurricane-season windows of six to ten weeks. *Why:* a weird is used as an occurrence, not explained; the regular route rests on recovered technique. This replaces Columbus with a world-native door.

**The Burning of the Seals (1329).** **Add.** Rainald seized the Sending House chests in Ironvale and burned their sealed letters in Brenholt's market square. The Ashenmen bands of Ironvale held their oath fulfilled, came down from the wagon-forts and took service under his banner: "the heirs of the Risings" in Named Figures. The Risings ended; the princely Reform Wars began. *Why:* the war ends by the letter of an oath rather than by defeat, and the transition from peasant war to princes' war gains a cause.

### V. 1329–1349: Settlements that hold by fear

**The unsigned peace (from 1329).** **Add.** As emperor, Caedros codified frontier amnesties and provincial audits and did not draw Ashreaver. Since the Court of Breaches ruled in 1215 that Markellos's bill for the water bound the Throne, which does not die and is not deposed, no Thalmyric emperor has signed a Covenant instrument: whatever he swears binds every successor. The Covenant does not make peace without one. The frontier therefore has no treaty to keep or break, only raids, fear and the cost of war. Zanthir's alternating tribute is the one instrument both courts accept, because it is the emir's.

**Celestin IX and the Council of Veridance (1330–1352).** **Keep, bend.** The Council abolished assurance sales, but sales were the smaller problem. Millions of letters had been sold and never sung. The Council ordered every unsung rite performed, **the Great Arrears**, and the Order of the Lantern, founded by Father Ivo the Lamed, a crippled veteran of the Zanthiri border wars, began as the order that walked haunted parishes with lanterns singing names out of the registers. The registers were in the Medori vault. The Council bought copies, sheet by sheet. The five tests were written around Sarnwell; by deliberate drafting none can be applied to a saint who left no remains, which Veridun read as a verdict on Caedros I. *Why:* Trent is kept as the shape (abolition, tests, seminaries, a new order); the bends are the Arrears and the bank.

**Falkendorf (1340), Rainald's death (1341) and the Grand Ministry.** **Keep, add.** Falkendorf, a Freeflame-majority canton resisting an old-Church settlement, brought Korrheim to the edge of civil war; Rainald's last son died in the fighting. Rainald died in 1341 without heirs, and Ironvale's guild ministries chartered the Grand Ministry. A quest-level atrocity, the forged no-quarter order of 1338 at a prisoner exchange ([[The Accord's Hollow Keep — Full Adventure]]), stays local.

**The water-test (1342) and the Korrheim Accord (1343).** **Bend.** The Sanctum's relic court tested the Preacher's Water in 1342; the result was inconclusive and the file was sealed. In 1343 the Accord was drafted at Heldenheim and sworn on the Weiss, with the signatories' hands in the water on Ironvale's reach, because every party trusted that river more than any relic it owned. The formula stands. Three bends:
- *The Unsworn.* Radical congregations refused to swear on the river, because an oath on a heretic's ashes made the river a relic. They are the excluded radicals.
- *The silence on reunion.* No legate would put the reunification mandate into an oath on water that holds oaths, for fear of being bound to a reunion nobody could achieve or to abandoning it. The silence is deliberate.
- *A princeless signatory.* Ironvale's Grand Ministry signed as the keeper of a realm without a prince, so the formula's "whose" came to cover cantons, ministries and chapters.
*Why:* Augsburg supplies the shape; the Preacher's Water explains why a peace nobody believes in has held seventeen years.

**Lauro, Vetti and the Bank of the Dead (1341–1349).** **Keep, bend.** Lauro the Magnificent died in 1341; Vetti's *Mirror for Sovereigns* (c. 1340) is dedicated to him. The Accord ended the war-loan boom. In 1345 a Sermoran pamphlet printed extracts from the Chancery registers held in the Medori vault, with the names of Fiorenzian dead whose Sendings were never sung, and called the vault **the Bank of the Dead**. Frate Severin preached that the Medori held the city's dead in pawn and that the carnival masks of the merchant houses had become doors for the Hungers. In 1346 the city expelled the Medori and burned its vanities in the Pyre of Masks. Severin held Fiorenzia until 1349, when a Medori-restored government hanged and burned him. *Why:* Savonarola is kept as the shape; the trigger is the registers.

**The Cull of 1349.** **Keep, bend.** Marzuq II died in 1349. The Guard bench witnessed Marzuq III; his brothers were killed, blinded or lodged. The Cull is now a century old and every party knows its rules, which is why Kavus and Ardavan are already recruiting.

### VI. 1349–1360: The present assembles

- **Sophron (1351)** enthroned at Caleran; **Vessing (1352)**, in the Council's last year: three shepherd children, a figure of banked fire, three testimonies. One says the figure told them the Fire dwells where it is welcomed (Veridun's case); one that it is carried by the hands of the Church (the Sanctum's); one that it said nothing, wept, and pointed at the graveyard, which reformers read as "sing for them". Weeping Courts papers claim it. The Sanctum has not ruled. **Keep, bend:** Fátima's shepherds are kept as the image; each child's testimony belongs to a different party, which is why nobody can rule.
- **Ferro dies (1352); Celestin dies (1354); Alistair III.**
- **The Closed House (1357–1360).** **Keep** as told in [[Saints and Sinners of the Chronicles]]; its historical effect is that the Inquisitors of Saint Malveris, under Lantern review since the Council, could be reformed or split.
- **Vasilia (1357) and the flame-silences.** After her fortieth-day memorial the emperor began keeping silences of a day or more before the Great Lamp. Courtiers report that the flame stands still while he kneels. Nobody has examined it. **Add:** the silences are left undecided under the Forward Rule.
- **Prince Nikandros dies (1358)** in a skirmish with Iron-mounted raiders in the Velian marches. **Keep;** contingency.

## Lives for 1250–1305

### Saint Gerlind of Lindenfurt

*(c. 1228–1279; great saint. Feast kept by the Sanctum; claimed by the Freeflame.)*

**Earthly life.** Gerlind was a miller's daughter of Lindenfurt, a ford-and-mill town on a tributary of the upper Weiss. In 1236 the Sweat killed the parish priest, her mother and her two brothers. The ferries were closed and the bodies burned on the bank. Her mother was seen at the mill-race for nine nights. On the tenth, eight years old, Gerlind gathered the neighbours, made each of them say aloud the names of their dead, and sang the one funeral hymn she knew. Her mother was not seen again.

She worked the ford as a ferrywoman. After the Quiet Ninth she began to walk, c. 1248, to parishes where the priest had died and the dead still walked. Her rule was short: name every dead person aloud, by the people who knew them, then sing. She took bread and refused coin; her one remembered saying is *the dead are named, not bought.* In the 1250s she crossed into Virelia's sealed villages and was flogged at a magnate's gate in 1257 for burying bond-folk without leave.

In 1266 a Sanctum relic court tried her for unlicensed rites and for undercutting the Sending Houses. The court's own observers had watched a thin place close at her singing. She was acquitted narrowly, on condition that she sing only with a priest present, and she obeyed by bringing whatever priest she could find; several Sending House chaplains sang with her for nothing and lost their places. She died of a winter fever at Lindenfurt in 1279 and was buried by the mill-race. A Brenholt miner's boy whose father she had named in 1271 was Ansgar.

**Use after death.** Alessior VI canonised her in 1284 with unusual speed and without the Adversary's Counsel. Quarne's Chancery printed her face on its letters, and the Gerlind seal became the best-selling letter in the trade. Her grave was enclosed in a Sending House chapel that charged pilgrims, and in 1290 her bones were translated to the Sanctum. Her followers in Aurelium's Saint-Leon quarter, who sang the dead out for free, were burned out in 1285 after an apparition of Gerlind herself was seen condemning them.

**In-world disagreement.** The Sanctum honours her as the obedient saint who sang with a priest present. The Freeflame claim her as the first of their elders and quote her saying against every Sending. Lantern investigators found the Saint-Leon apparition staged. Lindenfurt people still go to the mill-race to say names.

**Authorial truth.** Gerlind was a genuine saint. The closings at her singing were the truthful naming of the dead and her sanctity working together, as the Ash-Walker torque describes; neither her voice nor any rite owned the result. The Saint-Leon figure was a hired woman in a grey cloak and a lamp behind gauze, arranged by Quarne's office. Gerlind's intercession after death is reported at Brenholt in 1296, where Ansgar's friars said a woman's voice joined the singing; this stays a report.

### Gavrin the Farrier, the Grey Uncle

*(c. 1215–1281; great saint in the Thalmyric calendar as Saint Gavrin of the Halter; cursed by Velian congregations as the Wind-Saddler; honoured by the Tarkhan at foaling.)*

**Earthly life.** Gavrin was a Velian farrier and deacon of a village church in the Covenant's client belt. His name was entered in the Living Register as a bound craftsman, and c. 1235 he was sent north to shoe and doctor horses at a well-fort on Iqashar's Tarkhan frontier. About 1241, in the Emptying of the Benches, the garrison's instruments were held widowed and the soldiers marched home. The craftsmen, bound to the fort rather than the garrison, stayed. Tarkhan raiders took them, and Gavrin was traded twice before he came to the herd-master of a minor clan.

In the late winter of 1249 a grey mare of Grey Sister's line dropped a black colt too weak to stand. The herd-master's small son wept over it. Gavrin knelt in the snow, laid his hands on the colt and sang the old remount hymn that Velian villages had kept as a blessing for sick animals. The colt stood that night. It grew into a stallion that would walk up to a bound well-warden as if it were a fencepost, and its foals did the same. The wind-speakers said the old grace had come back into the grass. The herd-master took the horse's name, Qarayel, and was called by it for the rest of his life.

Gavrin would bless sick foals and sick children and refused to bless anything for war. The grace went into the colt's line anyway. Qarayel offered to fire-name him into the clan; Gavrin said he would accept when the other slaves of the camp were freed, and stayed a slave. The camp called him the Grey Uncle. He doctored captives, buried the dead of three faiths, and kept a calendar of feasts in knots on a bridle.

In 1279 envoys of Evandros the Sower reached the Black Wind's camp to buy him for the imperial studs. He refused to leave. In 1281 he was found in the horse lines with his throat cut. Qarayel killed the envoys and sent their heads to Veridun in a feed-bag. The Moon circuit raised a cairn over Gavrin's body.

**Consequences.** The Black Wind's grace-horses are the reason Iqashar's wardens failed and Iqashar paid; they are the reason the Velian fairs burned, Gavrin's own people's fairs; their thinning is why payments stopped in 1300; their partition shaped the four Hordes; and Iron Horde horses carried the night riders to Orinel in 1318.

**In-world disagreement.** Velian congregations call him the Wind-Saddler and keep no feast. Thalmyria canonised him in the 1290s as a martyr of the Covenant's name-hunters. The Tarkhan say the envoys killed him. Covenant registers list him as an absconded bound craftsman whose name was never released, which is the Thalmyric case for name-hunters. Some steppe tellers say Qarayel did it himself, to keep the grace from being sold. The Moon Horde keeps the cairn and will not let it be opened.

**Authorial truth.** The blessing was a genuine kindling: the hymn answered the righteousness and purpose of the singer (the Concordance ruling), mercy for a foal and a child, and the grace persisted in the line and thins by generation like Grey Sister's. Gavrin was killed by the Thalmyric envoy, acting on a sealed order from Evandros's logothete: *if he will not come, he must not bless again.* Thalmyria's swift canonisation buried its guilt. No document of the order survives in Veridun.

### Silvio Quarne, the Bridge-Seller

*(c. 1238–1299; great sinner. Freeflame name from Ansgar's First Grievance, "you have sold the poor a bridge that is not yours".)*

**Earthly life.** Quarne was born in Fiorenzia to a notary's household that the last wave of the Sweat left with a son and a ledger. He was clever with figures, entered the service of the Malvora as a clerk, and came to the Chancery of Sendings c. 1265. He made it efficient. He introduced the printed letter, the schedule of rates by age and estate (the cheaper rate for a child that the Twenty-Second Grievance asks about), the blank letter sold with the name left open, and the household bond covering a family for three generations. Under Alessior VI he became Cardinal-Treasurer in 1282. He put Gerlind's face on the letters in 1284. In 1285 his office arranged the apparition that condemned the singers of Saint-Leon, and a hired riot burned the quarter.

By 1288 his own audits told him that the Sending Houses sang perhaps one rite in ten of those sold. He did not reform the Houses. He found that brokers in Vorthara would pay for bundles of unsung letters, and he sold them, discreetly and in quantity, to pay for Alessior's wars and the Sanctum's dome. When Alessior died in 1296 and the Grievances appeared in 1297, Quarne left Aurelium with his private ledgers. He died in 1299 in a rented room in the Black Spire wards of Vorthara. His ledgers were gone when the room was opened.

**In-world disagreement.** Sestaran historians call him a gifted administrator made scapegoat for a Primarch's appetite. The Lantern traced dead paper in brokers' houses to his office's countersign. Fiorenzian counting-houses teach his methods without his name. Vortharan tellers say his ledgers still add up at night.

**Authorial truth.** An unsung assurance letter is an unresolved sealed promise over a named soul. It is not a trap: it touches no one's judgement and no letter can route a soul. It is a **lien**: a retaining claim that a Patron collecting soul-liens, the Undertow or an infernal broker can press to delay or contest a soul's passage ([[Soul Routing and the Vestibule]]: "a retaining lien or false passage can delay arrival"). Quarne knew this by 1290 from what the buyers were willing to pay and from one buyer who told him. His buyers included Undertow brokers and agents of the Under-Ledger. Letters bearing his countersign are the most prized dead paper in 1360. The workhouse broker in [[G-49 - The Two Tags on One Dead Man]] and the Guardian at the Workhouse (OBA-N11) press exactly this kind of claim. Quarne is damned in the sense the Saints and Sinners hub uses: his name keeps doing harm. His private ledgers' whereabouts stay open.

## Why 1360 looks like this

Each element of the present board traced to its wound (Commandment 9).

### Eclessia split three ways, and the self-parodying mandate

- **Sanctum loyalists** hold the Latinate south and the old-Church duchies because the Council of Veridance reformed enough (sales abolished, seminaries, the Lantern) to stop collapse, and because Sarnwell gave the old Church a proof that passed.
- **Accord-protected reformed duchies** exist because the Ashenmen oath could not be bargained with, Rainald burned the seals and turned the bands into an army, and the Accord was sworn on the Weiss, where nobody dares break an oath. The Unsworn are outside it because they would not swear on a relic.
- **The schism with Veridun** is 345 years old, and every attempt to close it in this span was priced as a test: the Lamp in 1227, Caedros I's remains in the five tests of 1352.
- **The mandate** must reunite a church that refused to be tested, reabsorb duchies whose peace was sworn on water that forbade mentioning it, and police heresy with an order whose founding work is singing the Sanctum's own unpaid debts to the dead from registers it rents from a bank. It is self-parody with a paper trail.

### Thalmyria's grieving emperor and three-way succession

- **The emperor** was a provincial soldier adopted because a summer flux killed the last Nikandran sons in 1327. He is the first emperor since the Restorer who did not inherit, and the adoption faction cites him.
- **Grief.** Vasilia, his wife of forty years, died in 1357; after her fortieth-day the flame-silences began. Prince Nikandros, named for the Restorer, died in 1358 in a frontier skirmish against Iron-mounted raiders, the same kind of riders who attacked Orinel.
- **Three ways.** Alexane is Vlastrava's soldier-governor, Sophios is Caleran's scholar, and adoption is Caedros's own road. The Nikandran grievance (1227) still makes Eclessian help unthinkable.

### The Covenant cracked and Guard-witnessed

Re-caused natively, without harem decline:
- **Guard-witnessed.** Since Suhrab's last book (1243) the Guard bench witnesses every accession. Since the Pageless Prince (1252) the Cull kills. Marzuq III owes his throne to the bench and to the Cull of 1349.
- **Cracked.** Qazmarid has been independent since 933. Al-Qahur's military households, whose leases ran out and who rose for the Pageless Prince, contest the canal councils that the Sweat left holding the water. Zanthir's alternating tribute is the one instrument both empires accept. The Velian belt was broken by the Crimson Pilgrimage and Zehrim. The Iron Horde holds frontier offices because only its horses stand beside the Covenant's bound things. And the Living Register and the Deep Vault have not balanced since the Emptying of the Benches.
- **Seized, rather than stagnant.** The Covenant is strong (three great engines, Mazrakan's customs, the Court of Breaches, Iron cavalry) but every great act needs witnesses who will not leave the capital.

### Sestara financing everyone

### Errors found

- **Lauro and Alessior.** Named Figures says Lauro (b. 1289) made "the quiet purchase of Alessior VI's Primarchy" (1281). Impossible; the purchase was Bartolo's. Fixed in [[Historical Spine of the Sestaran Concord]]; patch above for Named Figures.
- **Bartolo's dates.** "c. 1235–1301" with a Golden Book purchase in 1238. Changed to c. 1215–1301.
- **Bruckmoor's start.** [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]] has the apparition beginning in 1243; [[The Hunger Wars]] and the Weeping Courts file have it exposed in 1243 after forty years. Follow the Hunger Wars.
- **Earth-style century.** The Ironvale overview's "until the fourteenth century".
- **Montesse.** "Henri de Montesse… the Phoenix household" (Figures of Power) against "Grandmaster Henri d'Montesse of the Order of the Phoenix" (Courts and Crowns).
- **The Undertow's span.** "Three hundred and fifty years" since Wolfsmere (993) is 367 in 1360; "for more than three and a half centuries" would fix it.
- **Public links to author-only pages.** [[The Age of Held Proofs]] no longer links [[Present-Day Political Board]] (fixed here); its Atlas navigation still links the author-only Torque Register and Atlas of the Left Hand, as before.
- **The Medori's Golden Book "followed" plague-era changes** (Index follow-up): fixed in the Sestaran spine; the admission was a plague-time seat sale.

### Naming recommendations (not applied)

- "Whose the realm, his the rite" is *cuius regio* verbatim. The village name used in [[The Korrheim Accord]], "every roof under its lord's lamp", could become the formula: **the lord's lamp lights the lord's land**.
- "Golden Book" is Venice's *Libro d'Oro*. Suggest **the Gilt Roll**.
- "Alric IV von Caldria", "Henri d'Montesse", "Ysabel de Braxford": suggest **Alric IV of Caldria**, **Henri Montessan**, **Ysabel Braxenford**.
