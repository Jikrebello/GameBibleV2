# Causality Pass — 01 Deep Past and the Ancient East

> [!note] Proposal for approval
> Nothing here is canon until approved. Tick an item's box to approve it; strike it through or annotate it to reject or change it. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## Summary

Nine turning points, each letting something only this cosmology decide who eats, rules, wins or believes, within the ontology already in canon.

- **01.1** The Glass provision ends. A people fed by miracle for a century cannot farm, and takes farmers.
- **01.2** Dilution makes Giant continuity cost more human bodies each generation. That bill is the root of the collapse; ritual catastrophes become legend.
- **01.3** A Menhir bargain binds the named petitioner's line, which makes the Narashi crown-mothers a dynasty. Ark refusal of bargains keeps Jirahar and Ahdari leaders judges.
- **01.4** The Prophet gets four incompatible public deaths.
- **01.5** Qiryath's sign is withheld in 743 and returns in 654 for Yaelith's mixed households. The Jirahar have outlived one silence.
- **01.6** A failed White Measure rite lets the Crowned Countenance speak as Eryndor's corpse, and its grants outlast his heirs.
- **01.7–01.9** cover three uncaused transitions, the Siege and the open rulings, followed by the cuts.

## Proposals

### 01.1 The provision ends

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.** The provision belonged to the refuge's commission, not to the people. When the host was commanded south (author-only: c. 2180 BR), it thinned: stages once met in full were met late or in part. It ended at the threshold. The crossing was staged over several seasons, so provision ceased company by company. The Prophet died near the threshold while the rear companies were still inside. This resolves Appendix A: he died leading the people out, and most of the people left after his death.

The host had eaten provision at the appointed stages, milk and meat from its herds, and produce from gardens at the anchor camps, which were watered from provided springs. Seed was kept alive by token plantings. Three or four generations on, the Glass-born could herd, repair, judge, count, bury and garden. Few had cleared a field, timed a flood or stored grain against a second bad year. Author-only: the commanded three-generation sanctuary was the time needed to become farmers under protection. Its failure meant learning farming by taking farmers.

Author-only order of magnitude: about forty thousand entered c. 2265 BR, and with low mortality under provision, 100,000–120,000 reached the threshold. No district south of the Glass could feed them. Reader texts keep "unknown".

The consequences are these.
- **The Vacant Commission is famine politics.** Provisioners had distributed what was given; now their office decided who lived. The contested "stores" were chiefly the seed reserve.
- **The Five Staffs levy takes seed.** The rear camps were eating seed to survive the drought, and the expedition needed it to plant. The commander and the judge were both right, which is why the quarrel never closed.
- **The Southern Seizures take farmers as much as land.** Forts seized fields together with their cultivators. Hostage-taking, adoption and marriage targeted farming households. Enslaved Orphaned farmers taught their captors the farming year.
- **Atherian religious drift has a root.** The saying of the southern camps ran: *"The Lord who stopped feeding us; the stars that still answer."* Menhir correspondences answer any compatible rite. The Lord had answered by commission. A people fed daily for a century read a completed commission as abandonment. Astronax follows.
- **A camp layer survives in three languages.** Camp speech leaves a substrate under Atherian, Jirahar and Ahdari, densest in measure, oath, water, law, grave and name. All three keep the whole layer with drifting meanings: the provision-portion becomes an Atherian wage, a Jirahar portion owed to the dead and an Ahdari guest's share. The "three tidy kits" symmetry breaks.
- **The Ark-versus-Orphaned grievance is about bread.** A Folk saying runs: *"Heaven fed them a hundred years and sent them to eat our harvest."* The Ark reply: *"They were not fed because they were not called."*

**Why.** The commission ended (world-native: grace comes by commission, not on demand). Field skills had decayed, the host had grown, drought struck the rear camps, the Prophet's death removed the only authority over every company, and the southern land was inhabited.

**What it changes downstream.** The Seizures become a labour conquest; Atherian slavery and adoption law grow from the need for farmers. It is the first case of the principle [[Causality Pass - 04 Who May Authenticate a Sign, 0 to 411 AR|04]] builds on. In 1360, Folk land claims around Grenzburg still call the Seizures the time "the fed came for the farmers".

**Analog bent or replaced.** In Joshua 5 the manna ends once the people can eat the land's produce. Here it ends before they can farm, and the cost is a conquest of farmers.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
**The Provision Ends (c. 2180–2165 BR — the manna ceasing at the border).** TORQUE: the miracle stopped on schedule — its commission was the refuge, not the people — and it stopped on a host that had forgotten how to farm. The Vacant Commission was a fight over seed; the Five Staffs took the seed the rear camps were eating; the Southern Seizures took farmers with their fields. The southern camps' saying, *the Lord who stopped feeding us; the stars that still answer*, is the root of Atherian star-worship: a people fed daily for a century could not tell a finished commission from abandonment, and the Menhir answer whoever performs the rite.

### 01.2 Giant dilution and the cost of continuity

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.** The declared root of the collapse. Every Giant continuity system was built by Titans and early High Giants whose blood gave easy access to ancestral structures. These were Vathra's sky routes, Ishkatar's title-estates, Xolotl's Blood Sun, Qianglong's recurrence vaults, Ansharan blood-memory and Nythalum's berths. Canon already rules that dilution reduces "ease of access to ancestral structures". Each diluted generation could keep the same structure working only by paying more in from outside: labour, captives, sacrifice, bodies and grain. By the Closing-Court crisis (c. 2400–2300 BR), the bill exceeded what subject districts could pay. Tomb crews ate seed grain, canal crews were moved to vault works and children were taken for recurrence. That is the cry the Lord answered. The plagues struck the systems through which continuity was bought with bodies, and revolt began where the bill was collected.

Each dossier is to be rewritten in the Arsuvan register: granaries, seed, towpaths and named human crews.
- **Ishkatar.** Estates of the dead absorbed river land, and dike crews were moved to tomb works. The plague severed ownership. Canal crews revolted and the Naga houses fought over the regulators. In a high flood the unmaintained river broke into a lower bed, and the Spine dried along its old course. *Survivor legend:* a Naga lord's ritual drank the river.
- **Xolotl.** Diluted rulers needed more blood each year to keep the lineage-memory stored in the Blood Sun legible. The plague broke the captive supply and the calendar. The Sun starved, its human vent-keepers fled, and the untended volcanic works vented. *Legend:* an Obsidian lord fed it the rebels and it burst.
- **Qianglong.** Too few caste-born bodies remained for the recurrence vaults, so the Death Caste conscripted humans. The Famine Caste emptied its granaries to feed vault-bodies and [[The Unending Muster]]. The War of the Four Horsemen was a war over the last granaries. "The retreat into the vaults" was the rulers entering recurrence. *Legend:* the Horsemen called their children home.
- **Nythalum** keeps its physical launch, with one added line: launch logistics stripped the coast of food. **Anshara**, which "drank itself", already fits.
- **Vathra.** The sky routes were opened under Hecatara correspondences that recognised the First Road-Mother's Titan-continuity. Hecatara displaces the distance of every shortened route onto the country below, which is why canon's roads lengthen. Diluted heirs could not meet the access condition with blood, so they paid in tolls Hecatara recognises: anchors, crews, tribute and captives "delivered" along the routes. In the Closing-Court years the distance came due under the failure clause, "displaced distance returns all at once". Ozhur IV's requisitions were arrears paid in bodies.

**Why.** Dilution (world-native, already canon), the escalating cost of continuity engineering, revolt aimed at the collectors, the Lord's answer to the cry, responsible Giant civil war, and contingency: a flood year, an untended vent.

**What it changes downstream.** Later cultures fear continuity relics because keeping something past its time is how the Giants ate people. The bill recurs at human scale: Meresha's White Measure from an Ishkatari plate, the Copper Orchard's diversion of 903 BR, Aramen's fall (01.7), Eryndor's corpse (01.6) and the Reclaiming (01.7). In 1360, Grenzburg's Unending Muster is the same bill still being paid.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The three "national costume" dossiers each ended in one ritual catastrophe; Egypt, the Aztecs and China give way to one shared world-native mechanism.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
**The Cost of Continuity (c. 2400–2260 BR — the fall of the god-kings).** TORQUE: Giant blood diluted, and the ancestral structures answered thinner blood only if more was paid in from outside — in labour, then captives, then seed grain, canal crews and children. That bill is the cry the Lord answered and the line along which the plagues struck. The famous catastrophes — the Naga's ritual, the Blood Sun bursting — are what survivors told; the cause was an account that could not be paid. Vathra's sky roads failed the same way: Hecatara's displaced distance, paid in blood while the blood was strong and in bodies after it thinned, came due.

### 01.3 First kingship through named-petitioner pacts

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.** An authorial rule generalised from the Pale Household. A Menhir bargain binds its **named petitioner**. Where the petitioner speaks for a line, the Boundary attaches the gift to that line *as the petitioner's community reckoned descent on the day of the petition*. The gift lasts only while the line pays. A community that needs the gift therefore needs the line, and the crisis leader becomes a dynasty because the bargain made that blood the account-holder. Succession law is frozen with it, since changing how the line reckons descent breaks the Boundary. Pacts are one route among several; protection-into-rule and conquest remain.

**Example: the crown-mothers of Narash.** In the first winters after the Unyoking (c. 2255 BR), abandoned Vathran anchors looped caravans back to their starting points, and raiders struck the Hundred Wells along surviving route fragments. **Meya of Narash**, an existing figure who had sold captives in Vathra's wars, went to the dead anchor field above the wells, where a Way-Weaver had formed, and bargained under Hecatara's law.
- *Gift:* a sealed threshold. Anyone approaching by a road shorter than the walked road arrives back where they set out.
- *Price:* the obligation to return by the long road. Her line travels only on walked roads. At accession, each holder walks the whole circuit of the wells and names each well at its threshold; this is the Long Circuit (proposed name).
- *Boundary:* the Hundred Wells and Meya's line, reckoned as her caravan house reckoned it, through mothers.
- *Failure:* the threshold opens for the unwanted traveller.

The Narashi wells became the safest water in the western dry country. The maternal houses outlived their Giant-age authority because the seal needed a daughter of Meya, and succession stayed maternal while every neighbour's changed. The bargain made her house indispensable. It did not make it good.

**Ark refusal.** The Prophet refused the bargains of desert powers, as the Covenant monomyth already says. Ark-derived law made it a rule that no public office may rest on a bargain, though private household bargains continued. No Ark leader held a gift only his descendants could keep, so protectors stayed removable. That is why Tovan refused the protectorate, Yedran's dynasty broke within a generation and Qiryath's guardians served for stated emergencies. Ahdari wells beside Narash had to be held by armed protectors, oath and compensation, which gives the Ahdari institutions a cause rather than a temperament.

**Why.** Menhir law treats names and scope as binding (world-native); post-Unyoking famine and route chaos; Meya's standing and ambition; Ark doctrine on where authority comes from.

**What it changes downstream.** Narashi houses later enter Ahdari communities as keepers of a road custom without sovereignty. Atherians accepted bargains after the provision ended (01.1), so [[Causality Pass - 02 Gateway Wars and the Late Republic|02]] may give the southern leagues petitioner kings. Every pact dynasty inherits a frozen succession law and a crisis whenever an heir cannot pay.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The textbook path from protection to rule (Abimelech, Saul) stays for some peoples. A contract-made dynasty now stands beside it.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
**The Long Circuit of Narash (c. 2255 BR — the war-chief who becomes king).** TORQUE: a Menhir bargain binds the named petitioner's line *as it was reckoned that day*, so a crisis leader becomes a dynasty because only that blood can keep paying. Meya of Narash sealed the Hundred Wells against every shortcut; her daughters have walked the circuit at accession ever since, which is why Narashi authority still runs through mothers. The Ark peoples would found no office on a bargain, so their leaders stayed judges: no gift made their sons necessary.

One line survives in all four: **no one is owned.** In-world scholars cite it as evidence of a real teacher behind the four.

**Why.** Each people needs a death that licenses its institutions. For the Jirahar it is duty to the slow and the dead. For the Ahdari it is obligation binding even the lawgiver. For the Atherians it is allotment as a commission. The Orphaned need a refusal of Ark claims. The world-native cause is 01.1: the threshold was the moment the bread stopped, so every account of the death is an account of who was fed.

**What it changes downstream.** The Jirahar empty seat ([[G-56 - The Empty Seat Facing Outward|G-56]]) becomes the seat of the man who turned back. Covenant jurisprudence gains its founding case. The Ascent sands away the harder demands, and Eclessian iconography inherits it. In 1360, Folk satirists still use the Guest in land disputes.

**Analog bent or replaced.** Moses on Nebo sees the land he may not enter. Here no one sees the land, and four incompatible deaths are each tied to famine.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
**The Four Deaths of the Prophet (c. 2165 BR — Moses on Nebo).** TORQUE: the Prophet died in the season the bread stopped, and every people tells his death as an account of who was fed. The Jirahar say he turned back for the last cart; the Ahdari, that he opened a well he had not paid for and was held to his own law; the Atherians, that he never died and went up among the stars that still answer; the Folk, that his captains killed him for forbidding them our fields. All four keep one line — *no one is owned* — and the spine keeps the rest unanswered.

### 01.5 Qiryath's sign of acceptance, 742–660 BR

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.** *Form (recommended):* an accepted appointed offering burned without smoke, and the altar stones were cool to the hand afterwards. Witnesses from three custodial houses touched the stone and entered the result in the register. The sign attached to the sanctuary's appointed offerings, not to individuals. People read it as a verdict on whatever the city was doing; that inference was human. Haviel's white flame was a separate sign outside the city.

*Before 746.* Road courts announced canal-district settlements at the festival offering, and cool stones made a settlement feel unappealable, so Arqeshite provincial disputes drifted to Qiryath. Neshkar II's demand for his image in the upper sanctuary was a test he could not lose. If the sign continued beside it, his kingship shared the altar's authority; if it stopped, the rival court was broken. Iltara wanted to buy the merchants instead.

*The Withholding (proposed name).* In the siege's first winter (author-only: 743 BR), the council expelled foreign residents to save grain, and several died between the lines. At the next appointed offering the stones were hot and the smoke rose. The council split, households slipped out through the burial passages and Maraiah's party gained hearers. The city fell in 742. Whether the withholding answered the expulsion, the envoy's killing or the coming destruction **stays unresolved, even authorially**.

*Exile law, 742–684.* The exile courts built a theology of absence.
1. No court may claim the sign's authority; judgement rests on witness alone.
2. Each year "no answer" was entered in the Register of Unanswered Years (proposed name).
3. Prayer was made facing Qiryath.
4. The sign's return depended on an altar, not on a king.

*Arezan's bet.* Qiryath's sign was the one verifiable answer in the east. Arezan's decree and first grants funded the rebuilding, and Daryav completed it. A return under Vasharan protection would show every province that heaven confirmed the Right Order against the Lie of Arqesh. Without one, the crown had still bought a road and a market. The royal consecration offering (c. 660) smoked.

*The return.* The severe faction blamed the continuing silence on the spouses and children of Arqeshite settlers, who ran Yaelith's water and labour. Yaelith refused to expel them, and several old houses departed. The next appointed offering was carried by those households, and the stones were cool (author-only: 654 BR). The departing houses said the witnesses were Neram's kin, and Vasharan inscriptions credited the crown. The register records the order: royal offering unanswered, households' offering answered.

**Why.** An objective sign with a limited meaning (world-native); provincial loyalty draining to Qiryath's courts; the siege council's choice; Vasharan interest; Yaelith's ruling.

**What it changes downstream.**
- Jirahar leadership stays judicial. "The Path can be kept without sovereignty" has a cause: the sign returned to a city with no king. Yaelith's admission rules hold.
- Vashar, refused, takes up a counterfeit: the Witness of Dawn begins under Daryav.
- In 66 AR the militants expect the sign "when foreign rule ends", misreading a withholding that ended *under* foreign rule.
- In 1360 the Jirahar have outlived one silence, so the mystics' "first sign of the Mesakha will be restored acceptance" rests on precedent. Usage keeps "the Withholding" for 743–654 and "the Silence" for 0 AR, which avoids a collision with 04's proposed "Second Silence".

**Analog bent or replaced.** The Babylonian exile: Psalm 137, Cyrus and Ezra. Here the altar, not the decree, settles the return, and the sign answers the households Ezra's counterpart wanted expelled.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
**The Withholding and the Cool Stones (743–654 BR — the Babylonian exile and return).** TORQUE: Qiryath's sign was physical and witnessed — an accepted offering burned without smoke and left the stones cool — and it stopped during the siege, in the season the council drove foreigners out to die between the lines. Nobody, including the spine, knows which act it answered. The exile courts kept a register of unanswered years and learned to judge without the sign. Vashar paid to rebuild the altar as a bet on its return, and the crown's consecration offering smoked. Six years later the stones went cool under an offering carried by the settler-spouses the severe party wanted expelled. The Jirahar have outlived one silence, and they remember that it ended.

### 01.6 Eryndor's corpse and the Crowned Countenance

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.**
- **688 BR.** Kassur III's killers brought Eryndor the body and the **portable royal images**. Eryndor kept the images as regalia of succession, and the Crowned Countenance, a dependent Pride Mask anchored in them, attended his ceremonies.
- **685–684 BR.** With the seep failed and his death foretold by the Root-Minds, the ailing Eryndor consented to the White Measure rite at Arqesh. Neresh of Qemaret supplied the masters and Serevan paid. The regimen had barely begun when the masters attempted the final rite at his death. It failed, and his soul went the human road, leaving his true name closed into an empty anchor. The estate-office built around his plans, his attendants' memories and the Countenance, present through Kassur's images, filled it. Within months the corpse spoke in his voice.
- **The Voice at Delkos** (proposed name). Serevan diverted corpse and images to Delkos. The Voice declared that the king had not died and had no heir, and it issued grants that Serevan's scribes sealed. Veterans believed it because it knew things only the king's companions knew.

Three consequences follow.
1. **The heirs became disposable.** A reigning king needed no half-brother and no infant, so Demeran's hostages lost their value and he killed them.
2. **The war ran long.** The Countenance fed on rival petitioners' praise and granted the same provinces twice; canon's "incompatible grants" now have a source.
3. **The end.** Around 657 Neresh, refused a grant, had the Qemaret masters testify that the rite had failed, citing Nemeret's counterfeit. Serevan's grandson sealed corpse and images in the tomb (c. 655), the grants lost force and the living successors settled c. 650.

The ontology is unchanged. Eryndor's soul was never present. The Voice is a dependent Mask using an office and residue, with no Archmask and no accession.

**Why.** The rite, the Mask's appetite and the images as anchors (world-native); Eryndor's fear; Serevan's ambition; Neresh's grievance; the contingent timing of the death.

**What it changes downstream.** The Wars of the Inheritance gain a reason to last thirty-four years, and Delkos gains its prestige. The White Measure gains a reputation for counterfeits. Grant-paid sailors drift to Caleran (see Interfaces). In 1360 the sealed tomb is a lost site, and its location stays protected.

**Analog bent or replaced.** Ptolemy seized Alexander's body, and the joint kings were expendable. Here the corpse governs, and the heirs die because the dead king refuses to.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
**The Voice at Delkos (684–c. 655 BR — Ptolemy and the body of Alexander).** TORQUE: Eryndor died mid-rite. The Qemaret masters closed his name into an anchor his soul had already left, and the Crowned Countenance — riding Kassur III's portable images, which Eryndor kept as regalia — learned his voice. At Delkos the dead king declared himself alive and heirless and sealed grants. The heirs were murdered because a reigning corpse made them worthless, and the wars ran thirty-four years because a Pride Mask fed on rival petitioners and granted the same provinces twice. It ended when Qahuri embalmers testified against their own failed rite and Serevan's grandson walled the tomb.

### 01.7 Uncaused transitions

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.**

*The end of the Aramen empire (c. 858 BR).* By the late tenth century, royal mortuary estates and White Measure Second Houses held a growing share of the Basin's best fields, metal and skilled crews. It paid no levy because the dead owned it, and it still demanded upkeep because some of the dead were awake. White Measure lenders financed campaigns, and the interest went into necropolises; the Copper Orchard's diversion of 903 BR was the visible symptom. When Kassur I's provinces pushed Arqesh to Qahur's eastern caravan stages, the crown could pay its southern garrisons only in land the dead already owned. The garrisons sold their allegiance to temple and military houses, and the desert mines were abandoned. By c. 858 the empire was three crowns and many estates, and the strongest landlords in the Basin were dead.

*Eryndor's motive.* After 742, Arqeshite governors seized Compact ships in eastern harbours, Terys among them, held their crews hostage and deported an Atherian merchant quarter to the river cities (c. 740–730). Valeron II used the grievance to win the eastern mandate. Compact contributions, partly struck as Ortheian silver (01.9), paid his permanent infantry. At his assassination in 706, the mandate was the only lawful ground for those contributions, and cancelling the war meant an unpaid army facing adult claimants. The sources disagree.
- Ortheian songs speak of vengeance for the deported.
- Caleran writers speak of an army that had to be paid.
- Olyssaran lists describe a war made so that the cities would pay.
- Vashari stories say Arezan invited him.

Author-only: pay set the timing, and the deportations made the war possible to declare.

*The Bronze Roads: the Reclaiming (proposed name), c. 1450–1325 BR.* Long-distance exchange relied partly on salvaged Ishkatari tomb metal, recast. Some of it still bore title marks linked to dormant recovery systems: Gilded Wardens, Processional Coils and Estate Dead. When ruin traders opened deeper estate-vaults (Meresha's plate came from one such dig), linked Wardens woke. They recovered "misplaced property" along the old ceremonial roads, which the Bronze Roads had reused as trunk routes. Caravans carrying marked metal were collected, and palace stores were visited. The existing causes worked alongside: failed floods, palace wars, armed sailors, requisition and unpaid soldiers. Trunk roads failed first and short roads survived. Boran Tall-Hand's copper at Daram Ford was salvage, which his porters knew.

**Why.** Each transition has plural causes and one world-native cause: dead creditors for Aramen, and Ishkatar's property continuity for the Bronze Roads. Eryndor's motive stays human. Its world-native consequence comes in 01.6.

**What it changes downstream.** Iron becomes the road metal because no dead owner claims it (01.9). Qahur enters the Arqeshite century already owned by its dead. The deported Atherian quarter gives [[Causality Pass - 02 Gateway Wars and the Late Republic|02]] an eastern grievance.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The Ramesside temple estates become estates whose owners are dead and active. The motive borrowed from the Persian invasions becomes a paid army under a lapsing mandate. The Late Bronze Age collapse keeps its human causes, but its famous part becomes the Giant dead reclaiming their goods.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
**The Reclaiming and the Dead Landlords (c. 1450–858 BR — the Bronze Age collapse; the Ramesside temple estates).** TORQUE: twice the ancient world was bankrupted by the dead. On the Bronze Roads, salvaged Ishkatari metal still bore its owners' title marks, and when deeper vaults were opened the Wardens woke and walked the old ceremonial roads collecting it — which is why bronze became a metal someone dead might own and iron became the road metal. In Qahur the Aramen crown borrowed against the estates of waking Patrons, and in the end it paid its garrisons in land the dead already held.

### 01.8 The Siege and the Returnings

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.** The war was fought over a speaking copy-king. High Harbour's "figure in the likeness of an ancestral king" is the founder's infrastructure-office, speaking through a palace image, with no soul and no Giant continuity. It is the named party to the tide mistress's inherited pact. Her price, the seasonal "travellers and matter" sent through the old channel, was paid with the people taken in collection raids. Whoever holds the image holds the channel and the tide.

The camp's quarrel is over the image. The commander wants it whole, to inherit harbour and tide; the withholding captain, the shipmistress and the inland ally want it broken, since any new holder would keep feeding the channel. **The famous part:** High Harbour's own craftspeople, fleeing the fire, broke it, so neither heroes nor commander decided the war. The pact defaulted, and its failure clause fell on everyone carrying the harbour's goods out by sea. The victors' prize silted and lay open to storms.

**Why.** An inherited court pact attached to an office, which canon allows (world-native); the coalition's greed; the captives' kin; the craftspeople's choice; the tide.

**What it changes downstream.** Southern sea law forbids binding a sea pact to an image or office "that cannot drown". The rule seeds later refuge law, which [[Causality Pass - 02 Gateway Wars and the Late Republic|02]] may use at Kelarion. The bronze "god's armour" relics are re-read as fragments of the broken pact.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The *Iliad*'s war over Helen becomes a war over a speaking office, and Poseidon's grudge becomes a contract's failure clause. **Recommended cuts:**
1. The captive-and-ransom quarrel (Chryseis and Briseis), replaced by the quarrel over the image.
2. The blinding of the court being and the captain's boast (Polyphemus).
3. The hanging of the enslaved women at the homecoming.

Keep the father's crossing, which already runs on Hecatara's grammar, and the grief-likeness.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
**The Copy-King of High Harbour (c. 1980–1960 BR — the Trojan War).** TORQUE: the coalition besieged an office, not a king. A speaking image of High Harbour's founder was the named party to the tide mistress's pact, and the captives taken in collection raids were her price. The commander wanted the image whole, to inherit the harbour and its tide; the shore households wanted it broken. The harbour's own craftsmen broke it in the fire. The pact defaulted, and every ship carrying the harbour's plunder inherited the failure clause: that is the Returnings.

### 01.9 Open rulings

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.** Each question carries a recommended ruling.
- **Writing.** Giant estate scripts survived among former scribes. The Ark kept registers in a camp script that became the Scrolls. Qahur and Arqesh wrote in palace and temple. Folk and Ättar used tallies, cords and memory specialists until City-States contact. *Ruling:* the era ladder's "writing" becomes "**public writing**", meaning civic inscription and citizen records, c. 1400–800 BR. The Ättar "registers" of c. 1600 become law-speakers' remembered registers and tally-sticks. The Folk "repair lists" become repair tallies.
- **Iron.** Iron is smelted early and locally where salvage metal was scarce: Folk, Ättar, Ossen and Odrani. It becomes the road metal of the north and east only after the Reclaiming (01.7). *Ruling:* both sets of files are right, as a regional difference.
- **Coins.** The first struck coin is Ortheian silver, c. 730 BR, made to pay Valeron's infantry, and the Successor courts spread it after 684. Brael's "foreign coins" of c. 1450 become stamped ingots and ring-silver.
- **Vathra's lost cities.** There are four. Sairat, Molqara and Yazuran were lost in the war, and Dazhur vanished within three years after it. Northern counts give "three or four", and that disagreement stays in-world.

**Why.** They remove contradictions without new events; iron and coin reuse the causes in 01.7.

**What it changes downstream.** Folk and Ättar law has a reason to stay oral, and Tarkhan songs can keep "three cities" against the scholars' four.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The real Iron Age transition and Lydian coinage are replaced by native causes, at dates that do not match theirs.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** None; these are rulings. If they are approved, add to the Reclaiming entry: "iron spread because nobody dead owned it."
