# Causality Pass — 02 Gateway Wars and the Late Republic

> [!note] Proposal for approval
> Nothing here is canon until approved. Tick an item's box to approve it; strike it through or annotate it to reject or change it. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## Summary

The Gateway Wars follow Polybius in order and the late republic follows Plutarch, and no Mask, Menhir or sign changes a decision. This file re-sequences both eras around engines canon already contains:

- **The Provider's real plenty.** Qarzeth recovers fast because its Gluttony Mask draws the Gateway country's harvest into the capital, and the Three Collections are the bill. The capital falls by the Gluttony defeat grammar, and the meal that seals the defeat becomes the Spared Towns' **Table Oath**.
- **Astaroth's Red Field.** A war rite goes wrong when Caleran sacrifices its Gateway hostages in 609 BR. It is reformed into an office its holder cannot lay down while a named opponent contests the field. The office traps Harod, binds Casvilar and gives his killers their opening.
- **Kelarion's Roll.** Its refuge custom enters the Oar League's charter, is turned against allies in arrears, and returns as general law after a partial recurrence of the Shoreless King.
- **Calendar, sighting and record.** Caleran rules the Compact as keeper of its calendar and its rolls. Damar's crisis turns on who may sight public boundaries, and the sighting is answered.

**Pressures, c. 680–370 BR.** (1) Food: dry Aurin springs, Gateway grain, the Provider's draw, public land after Ilyessa. (2) Ships and the money for them: Compact levies, Oar ship money, harbour rights. (3) Records: the common calendar, the Compact rolls at Orthea, sighted boundaries. (4) Powers with prices: the Provider, Astaroth, Father Sun, the Shoreless King and the Unwritten Worm.

**Anchors kept.** Qarzeth's destruction c. 525–520; Tasar 514; Ilyessa 493; Rethmar 381; Casvilar's assassination 370; the three-command government 367; Marelith 355; Thalion's settlement c. 350; Samyra's line. Dates elsewhere between 495 and 371 BR move where noted.

### 02.1 League into hegemony

Three episodes turn the league into Caleran's hegemony, and the Oar's ship money finishes the work.

*The Olyssar seat, c. 680–672 BR.* Eryndor had divided Olyssar's land among loyal households. After his death Caleran revived the Compact's meetings and bought ships from his unpaid commanders. It used them to carry its own unpaid sailors onto Olyssar's fields, drove off the Ortheian grantees and seated the settlement in the Compact as Olyssar. The Olyssaran exiles in Caleran, keepers of the lists of their dead and sold, claimed the seat. The arbiters ruled that a seat went with land and a festival, not with lists. From about 672 Caleran spoke with two voices of twelve.

*Verion struck from the reckoning, c. 652–649 BR.* Verion was ruled by heirs of the soldier whose emergency command had become a dynasty (the Bent-Key tradition). In the Tammur war (02.2) its ruler withheld his levy, and Caleran, as keeper of the common calendar, left Verion's festival and contract days out of the year. The Common Sky taught that an offering on an unreckoned day went unseen. Verion's own crews would not sail, member harbours turned its ships away and arbiters refused its merchants' suits. Within three seasons its ship-owning houses expelled the ruling family and asked to be "restored to the sky". Caleran agreed, on condition that Verion's magistrates be confirmed at Caleran. Exclusion from the calendar became the Compact's standard sanction, and only Caleran could impose it.

*The rolls come to Caleran, c. 513 BR.* Orthea kept the Compact's archive. When the archive-valley was collapsed to seal the Unwritten Worm (02.6), the rolls were rebuilt from Caleran's copies, and from then on every Compact dispute went through Caleran's archive.

*The Oar's ship money, 491–469 BR.* (Revised on approval.) The League of the Eastern Oar (497) was a separate alliance with its fleet at Velathene. Velathene's pilots learned Kelarion's Roll of Refused Hulls with their approaches, and the League's first custom came from it: no member harbour closed its chain against a League hull in distress (02.6). After 491 the fleet stayed in service against Orvaz I, and Caleran's assessors fixed what each member owed. Thesra and Olveth, with no ships, paid in silver and sons. A city that fell behind learned it at sea. In the autumn blow of c. 480 a Pyrassan grain convoy running for Velathene found the chain up and the mole lined with watchmen under orders; three hulls broke on the outer rocks in sight of the harbour, and their cargo washed up for the beachcombers. Cities that refused outright lost walls and magistrates (existing canon). By 470 the money went into Caleran's treasury, and sailors called the League's chains "Caleran's purse-strings".

**Why.** The review found no named members, no wars among them and no tyrants. These episodes use roles Caleran already held (calendar host, record-keeper, fleet assessor), so a host becomes a master without one decisive conquest.

**What it changes downstream.** The calendar returns in Damar's crisis (02.5); custody of the record makes the Worm's loose years matter (02.6); conditional refuge produces the War of the Closed Chains (02.5).

**Analog bent or replaced.** The Delian League's treasury and its punishment of defectors survive as pressure, worked through calendar, record and refuge. No Latin War beat is used.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
> **The Unreckoned Days (c. 652 BR — the Delian and Latin leagues).** TORQUE: Caleran ruled its allies through the sky before it ruled them by the sword. As keeper of the Compact's calendar it could leave a member's days out of the year, and the Common Sky held that offerings on unreckoned days went unseen, so the city's own crews would not sail. Verion's tyrant fell to the calendar in three seasons.

### 02.3 Qarzeth's Mask really provides

- [x] Approved — real plenty makes Caleran's fear true, keeps every atrocity of the sack, and gives the spared towns an oath older than their crown; it is told as full storehouses, short jars, a broken face and a meal of whatever people had.

**Rulings on the open questions.** Only deserters sit at the meal; Atherian soldiers under orders do not. "No guest served by worth" is read publicly as covering suppliants from 457, when the towns make Arenes eat at the unranked table. Hesara's concealment moves to the siege years, 526–520. The Gluttony dossier note goes into the Integration patch, since that file is read-only to this pass.

**What happens.**

*The plenty.* The Bronze-Faced Provider is a dependent Gluttony Mask anchored to the sanctuary, its estates and gardens and the serving chambers. Like the Guest with Shark Teeth at Tidewood, it concentrates real food through its feeding arrangement. The capital's stores fill while the dependent towns' sealed stores fall. Qarzeth's recoveries after 646 and 601 were real. Caleran's hawks feared a city that healed faster than it could be hurt, and the dependent towns knew the same plenty as stores emptying without theft.

*The bill.* Each provision demanded a larger "irreplaceable return". By the 550s the sanctuary's doors opened only when a living name had been entered on the collectors' household lists. The Three Collections (548–540) are the bill coming due. By the last of them the capital held a bounded dominion overlay, in which tables lengthened as guests disappeared. As at Tidewood, there was no Archmask promotion and no Regnant.

*The defeat*, in the order of the existing Gluttony grammar:

1. **The routes are cut.** The Rope Camp stoppage (536–531), the towns' defection and the blockade follow one another. In the siege the capital went hungry for the first time in living memory, and the priests' further killings brought no plenty.
2. **The named remains are recovered.** Ilya's list, copied from the collectors' own lists, matched remains, clothing and tokens to names after the breach. The dead were buried by name at Esharat.
3. **The organ-object is broken.** The bronze face was broken after the names were read. Atherian annals credit a soldier and Esharat credits Sapha's carriers; Ilya's fragment says "it broke when the last name was spoken". The point stays open.
4. **A meal without hierarchy of worth.** Some days after the sack, at Sarqen's landing, which had sheltered refugees, rescued children, carriers, farmers, freed slaves, ransomed people from the capital and a few Atherian deserters ate together. Each brought what they had; no one was served by rank and no seat was kept empty. Sapha set the rules and refused to preside.

*The oath.* The meal's rules became the Spared Town League's **Table Oath** (proposed name): *no child for the table; no seat kept empty; no guest served by worth.* The League renewed it yearly at Sarqen. In 457 the towns made Arenes eat at the unranked table and swear it before recognising him, and Tavren, Olymes and Samyra swore it at their accessions. An oath older than the crown is what stands behind canon's line that the towns' contribution "preceded the crown and imposed responsibilities on its heirs". It is the root of Samyra's legitimacy (02.5, 373).

*The salt* was Gateway salt from the pans of Hazreth and Sarqen, the same salt that stood on the meal's table. The towns asked for it to be laid where the plenty had been concentrated: the sanctuary, the gardens and the store compounds, which are exactly the districts canon salts. Nothing grows on salted ground, so nothing can be offered back from it and no table can be laid with its produce. Atherian annals call the salting a punishment, and Gateway tradition calls it closing the table: "The salt of the table and the salt of the ruin were one salt." The authorial position is that salt worked as the last act of a defeat already complete; on its own it does nothing (02.7, Vorthara).

*Why Sarqen and not Qarzeth.* Materially, the cisterns were broken and the ground salted. Ritually, a court over the sanctuary would be a place kept for the Provider, and Gluttony forms re-form from such places. Politically, the oath was sworn at Sarqen.

**Why.** Commandment 2 and the Tidewood precedent require real plenty (the review's hinge 4). The sack keeps its full weight: a real Mask was defeated, and Atherian soldiers still murdered, raped and sold people.

**What it changes downstream.** It grounds the hawks' fear and gives the towns' legitimacy a sworn form. It explains the royal seat and supplies the answer to the accusation of 353 (02.4).

**Analog bent or replaced.** Carthage's swift recovery, Cato's figs, becomes Mask plenty. The salting, unattested for Carthage, gains a world-native reason.

**Canon notes.** *The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth* hedges the plenty ("even where weather and ordinary labour explained the plenty"); delete the hedge. The meal "without hierarchy of worth" belongs to the Banquet Tyrant dossier, while the Provider is a dependent Mask. The proposed reconciliation is that the meal closes a **dominion overlay**, not an Archmask court, and that this be recorded as a note in the Gluttony dossier. Canon has Hesara concealing children "during the Years of Three Collections" (548–540), but she was born after c. 548. Move her act to the siege years, 526–520.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
> **The Unranked Table (c. 526–520 BR — the destruction of Carthage).** TORQUE: Qarzeth's notorious recoveries were real plenty, drawn by the Bronze-Faced Provider out of its dependent towns, and the Three Collections were the bill. The capital fell as a Gluttony form falls. The towns stopped carrying, Ilya's list gave the dead back their names, the bronze face broke, and the survivors sealed the end at Sarqen with a meal at which no one was served by worth. Its rules became the Table Oath that every Nerethan ruler swore at accession. The salt on the ruin came from that table and was laid where the plenty had grown, so that nothing could be served from that ground again.

### 02.4 Caleran's own sacrifice, 609 BR

- [x] Approved — the case against Qarzeth becomes an argument between two cities that have both killed children, and the Red Field is a native engine for two centuries of politics; Corven Ilar keeps his name.

**Rulings on the open questions.** The hostages were children and adolescents, about eight to sixteen. Valeor's family has no direct link to 609.

**What happens.** In 609, after a second army had been lost to Zamaret, Caleran killed captives at the Common Sky festival ground. Ilaron and Tarsenna had built that ground, and captives had been sacrificed there once before. Dema Varin presided. The victims were captured Gateway soldiers and the hostages held under the peace of 646, children and adolescents of the Council Houses. They were offered to Astaroth, the Red Star, "to give the Star a contestant". Atherian annals call the rite **the Lustration of the Field** (proposed name) and name no victims. The Gateway families kept the hostages' names, the first of their two lists.

The authorial truth is that the offering broke Astaroth's law, under which substituting a helpless victim for the named contestant is taboo. The failure clause followed: in 608 more allies defected and the war reached the islands. The augurs drew the Menhir's lesson, not a moral one. The victims had been the wrong kind, so the Field could henceforth be accepted only by a living commander in person, the Warden. No one in Caleran ruled the killing wrong.

Orsen Valeor demanded Qarzeth's destruction in a city that had killed Gateway children within his grandfather's lifetime, and the exiles answered him with the hostages' names. Mera Castren drew the opposite lesson: Caleran had stopped, so Qarzeth could stop too, under inspection. The survivors who broke the face of Valeor's statue knew both lists. In 353, when **Sabren Ilar** (renamed from Corven Ilar) tied Samyra to the sacrificial capital, the spared-town envoys answered with the Table Oath and the hostages' names (file 03).

**Why.** This is the review's hypocrisy hinge, and the precedent of Ilaron and Tarsenna makes it native to Caleran's own religion.

**What it changes downstream.** It creates the Warden (02.5, file 03). The case against Qarzeth becomes an argument between two cities that have both killed children.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The Forum Boarium burials become a broken Menhir taboo with a recorded consequence, a reformed office and a list of names.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
> **The Lustration of the Field (609 BR — the Forum Boarium burials).** TORQUE: Caleran killed its Gateway hostages and captives at the festival ground to give Astaroth a contestant. The Red Star's law forbids substituting a helpless victim for the named contestant, and in the following year the war spread exactly as that law's failure runs. The augurs concluded that the victims had been wrong, not the killing. They created the Warden of the Red Field, a living commander who must stand in person. Every case Caleran later made against Qarzeth's altars was made from that festival ground, and the Gateway towns never let it forget.

### 02.5 The late republic, re-sequenced (c. 495–370 BR)

- [x] Approved with changes — the anchors survive and every copied Plutarchan link breaks; Damar's crisis becomes noon shadows and a harvest that rotted on the stalk, Harod a Warden living in a tent, Casvilar's murder a challenge struck early.

*Damar and the Noon Sighting, 489–487.* The rowers and soldiers of the campaign against Xarun were mostly small citizens and allied crews. They came home in 491 to public land leased in great blocks to the families who also supplied the augurs. The land had been entered "on the sky": its boundaries were star-lines sighted from the old stones at Sarven, and they could be re-sighted only on days the augurs declared open. Eshren Damar, magistrate in 489, proposed to cap renewals and settle the veterans of Ilyessa on the surplus. The augurs inserted **the Long Month** (proposed name), an intercalation that carried the renewal day past his term; Irenai's observers disputed it publicly. At midsummer 488 Damar took veterans to Sarven without augurs, and Vardeni hill soldiers among them performed an upland rite for asking the Sun to show a boundary. A noon-bound place-mind of **Father Sun** answered (proposed). For one hour every shadow in the lower Aurin pointed along the old lines, showing where estate walls had crossed onto public land. The veterans paid the recognised price, a season's labour bareheaded under open sky. Some petitioners also asked that the land "bear for us" without limit, which is the Sun's taboo, and that autumn the public-land harvest ripened in days and rotted. In the hungry spring of 487, retainers of the leaseholding houses killed Damar in a bread crowd at the public grain stores. In 486 the senate passed **the Licensed Sighting**, under which only augurs may seek or record a sign over public things. The surveyors' record of the noon hour, **the Noon Roll**, stayed with the veterans' families.

*The War of the Closed Chains, 468–461.* Ship money (02.1) fell on cities with no ships and no voice. Thesra, Olveth and Orthea's former valleys (02.6) refused it; Velathene, the fleet's own harbour, refused the assessment of 469, and Pyrassa, Verion and the Elmarai pilots followed. Orvaz I sent timber and money and gave the rebels' leaders houses at Avesh. Caleran ordered League harbours to close their chains to defaulting hulls; in the gale of 466 Pyrassa obeyed, and the Shoreless King partly recurred there (02.6). The same winter a rebel fleet stood off the Aurin mouth, and Caleran revived the Red Field: **Vespen Harod** accepted it in person and named the rebel captains. The **Chain Peace** of 461 admitted loyal households and some rebel cities to citizenship on unequal terms, dismantled Velathene's walls and made refuge general law. The named captains were by then at Avesh.

*Harod, 466–c. 441.* His named opponents had neither fallen nor submitted, so the field stayed open, and a Warden who lays it down is fleeing the field. He refused the senate's attempts to replace him in 452–449. Senators who formally set themselves against him became named contestants under the rite's own grammar. In 448 he brought troops into Caleran, executed some of them and sold their property; Ostar's family were among the buyers. He spent his last years demanding the captains from Avesh for a fair contest, and Orvaz, who preferred Caleran tied to its own Warden, refused. (Revised on approval.) Harod would not sleep under a roof, lest the Red Star read it as leaving the field; for twenty-five years he governed from a tent on the muster ground below the river stores, and senators came out to him through the mud. Around 441 he died there of a winter fever, still Warden. The augurs said the Red Star had taken its Warden; his enemies spread a story of Vasharan poison. Authorially it was fever, and his acceptance ended with his death, but Caleran believed for two generations that the Field stood open.

*Calvena* lives. (Revised on approval.) From 379 Casvilar's returning veterans claimed the public land marked on the Noon Roll, held for more than a century by the leaseholding houses who were Veyr's senatorial allies. In 377 Casvilar offered Calvena a divorce from Veyr, and she refused it publicly. Her refusal, not a death, marks the end of the partnership. She goes east with Veyr's household, nurses him at Ilamar, and after 370 carries terms between her cousin Thalion and her stepson Ardel (the agreement of 357).

*Casvilar takes the Field, 376.* Casvilar had called Darneth's broken frame heavenly confirmation, though in canon it was a genuine Astaroth correspondence about a structure, not a commission. Ordered to lay down his command, he dedicated arms at a frontier altar and named the senators who demanded his surrender. Astaroth's courts accept a genuine contest whatever its justice, so the acceptance was real, and it bound him. That supplies the cause of his extending his command "beyond the circumstances used to justify it".

*Velos, the empty seat and Ilamar, 374–371.* At Velos in 374 neither army broke. Veyr lost at sea, where the Oar ports remembered the closed chains and refused him ship money. At Sarqen the minister Vardek, ruling for the boy Nethar, invited him to land and eat. An Esharati elder warned him that the table was laid with an empty seat, which the Table Oath forbids. Veyr turned back to sanctuary at Ilamar, which Casvilar would not storm because his own grain certifications ran through its court. Arest Corven went with him and there began the history of the republic later used against Casvilar's heirs. Veyr died of fever in 371. The towns, learning that Vardek had laid a table to kill a guest, rose for Samyra. Casvilar, who needed Esharat's grain, backed the side that already held it. Nethar died in 372, and the towns judged Vardek under the oath before Casvilar's officers executed him.

*The challengers, 370.* As Warden, Casvilar had to receive any challenger in person at the muster ground. Seneth, Draven and their fellows entered their names in the arms register kept by **Deceran Tolm**. They struck before the contest was opened. The contest's bounds broke, and Astaroth's failure clause ran: conflict spread until every faction had proved itself. File 03 decides whether the succession wars are that failure.

**Why.** This breaks every copied link except the protected ones: Rethmar, the distinction between the informal partnership and the three-command government, and the anchors. Domestic and foreign history now run on one clock.

**What it changes downstream.** The Licensed Sighting is the earliest form of the augurs' monopoly over signs (file 04). The Noon Roll grounds Casvilar's veteran grants, which his enemies call theft. The open Field explains why commanders would not lay down command.

### 02.6 Kelarion and Orthea reconnected

- [x] Approved with changes — the era's two native dragons become working causes: a drowned harbour's roll of names becomes the sea's refuge law, and a lost valley explains why nobody can say who sold the rescued children of Qarzeth. The rename is withheld.

**Changes made on approval.** No rename in this pass: "Orthea" stays in authorial files, and reader prose says "the archive valley", whose name the copies disagree about (Option B in effect, without a coined title). Reader prose never names the Shoreless King or the Unwritten Worm; it uses the coast's own name, "the King beneath the Breakwater", for what Pyrassa's sailors saw in 466, and gives the loose years only as the gap in the records that historians argue over.

**Rulings on the open questions.** The two counts of the loose years disagree (seven harvests by the Witness Stones, four by Caleran's annals) and stay that way. The Worm did not touch the Oar's charter rolls.

**What happens.**

*Kelarion's Roll and sea refuge law.* The beacon keepers kept the Roll of Refused Hulls distributed after c. 1160, and Velathene's pilots, from the nearest Compact port to the reef, learned it with the approaches. In 497 the Oar charter took its first clause from the Roll (02.1). After 491 Caleran made that clause depend on ship money, which repeats Kelarion's refusal in law: shelter only for those who pay. In the gale of 466 Pyrassa closed its chain to rebel hulls on Caleran's order, and the King partly recurred. The harbour admitted no one, Pyrassa's own grain ships circled on the same wave, and the signals contradicted one another. Ships that steered by Kelarion's beacon lived. Pyrassa's wardens cut their own chain, the remedy canon allows, and the recurrence ended. The Chain Peace of 461 made refuge law for every vessel in distress, of any allegiance, and deposited a copy of the Roll with **Ilamar's custodians**, who from then on heard refuge disputes. Marelith, which never anchors and so has no chain, took in rebel crews that winter. It became known as "the harbour without a chain", and that reputation draws Ardel's fugitives to it in the 350s (file 03). This is the ancestry of later Thalassian refuge law.

*Orthea's league and the Worm's loose years.* Orthea led the **Nine-Valley League** (proposed name) within the Compact and kept the Compact's archive. In 526–520 Caleran's levies for the Qarzeth siege passed through Orthea to its valleys. One district, stripped of men and grain, killed the collectors, and the campaign and erasure followed (existing canon); the witnesses killed were allied units marching home from Qarzeth. Between the erasure and the sealing came **the loose years**, seven harvests by the Witness Stones and four by Caleran's annals. In those years the Worm moved through the archive while two sets of records were being copied there: the war rolls of Qarzeth's sack and the founders' genealogies Caleran was recasting. Both came out with their attributions cut. Caleran's rolls name no officer for the sale of rescued children, and Esharat's ransom registers name officers whom no Caleran roll places at the siege. Founder lines carry names without cities. The two sets of records cannot be reconciled, and the cause lies in the archive, not in scribal error. After the sealing the rolls were rebuilt at Caleran (02.1). The league dissolved, and its valleys, now assessed directly by Caleran, joined the rebels in 469.

*Rename option.* **Option A (recommended):** rename Orthea **Ambreth** (proposed; no current use) and keep Ortheia. **Option B:** keep "Orthea" in authorial files, and call it "the Unnamed Valley" in public prose. That fits a site the Worm worked through, but it leaves the collision in the author's files.

**Why.** These are the era's two most world-native events, and both are currently quarantined. Neither dragon is a Weird, and nothing here explains a Weird.

**What it changes downstream.** Ilamar gains a refuge court, and Marelith is a refuge before it is a target. The gaps in the sack's records become something historians argue over. The Palace review's ruling of "no causal connection to Orthea" is replaced.

**Analog bent or replaced.** There is no analog. These events are native and now carry weight.

In the gale of 466 the Shoreless King partly rose at Pyrassa. The closed harbour admitted no one, not even its own ships, until its wardens cut their chain under the light of Kelarion's beacon. The Chain Peace made refuge a law for any vessel in distress, and Ilamar's custodians have kept a copy of the Roll ever since.

> **The Unreckoned Days (c. 652 BR — the Delian and Latin leagues).** TORQUE: Caleran ruled its allies through the sky before it ruled them by the sword. > **Zamaret's Bread (c. 612–601 BR — Hannibal in Italy).** TORQUE: the invader's gift was the weapon that failed him. Zamaret fed wavering Atherian towns with grain from the Provider's table, and every town that ate it found its own sealed stores short at the next count, the seals unbroken and the wax bitten. "Do not eat the Gateway's bread" became the allies' proverb, and the alliance held out of fear of the gift more than loyalty to Caleran. Caleran's answer was the reverse of delay: Celor Marath, first Warden of the Red Field, was bound to offer battle and stood below Thesra. [[The Gateway Wars]]

> **The Lustration of the Field (609 BR — the Forum Boarium burials).** TORQUE: Caleran killed its Gateway hostages and captives on its festival ground to give Astaroth a contestant. The Red Star's law forbids substituting a helpless victim for the named contestant, and the next year the war spread exactly as that law's failure runs. The augurs concluded that the victims had been wrong, not the killing, and created the Warden of the Red Field, a living commander who must stand in person and may not quit the field while his named enemy holds it. Every case Caleran later made against Qarzeth's altars was made from that festival ground, and the Gateway towns read the children's names back to it. [[The Gateway Wars]] · [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth.md|Astaroth]]

> **The Unranked Table (c. 526–520 BR — the destruction of Carthage).** TORQUE: Qarzeth's notorious recoveries were real plenty, drawn by the Bronze-Faced Provider out of its dependent towns, and the Three Collections were the bill. The capital fell as a Gluttony form falls. The towns stopped carrying, Ilya's list gave the dead back their names, the bronze face broke, and the survivors sealed the end at Sarqen with a meal at which no one was served by worth. Its rules became the Table Oath that every Nerethan ruler swore at accession. The salt on the ruin came from that table and was laid where the plenty had grown, so that nothing could be served from that ground again. [[The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth]]

> **The Noon Sighting (488 BR — the Gracchi).** TORQUE: Damar's veterans asked the Sun, not the senate, to show the public land's boundaries, and for one noon hour every shadow in the lower Aurin pointed along the old lines. When the petitioners also asked the land to bear for them without limit, the harvest ripened in days and rotted, and Damar died in the bread crowd that followed. The augurs' Licensed Sighting of 486 turned the disaster into a monopoly over signs, and the surveyors' Noon Roll waited a century for Casvilar's veterans. [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] · [[Father Sun]]
