# Causality Pass — 13 Anchor Date Proposals

**The recommended package on this page was applied on 15 November 2026.** The next section says what went in and where it differs from the tables as first drafted. The lighter and optional rows that were not taken are unchanged.

After the two bends and the renames, the clock is the last thing that points at the real analogues. Wherever an anchor date was kept, a sequence still sits a near-constant number of years from its source, so a reader who finds the distance can date the next event. This page proposes which dates to move, what each move costs and what it buys. It also says plainly where dates cannot help.

Five researchers each took one stretch of the history. Their full tables are in [[Causality Pass - 13A Anchor Dates - The Ancient East|13A]], [[Causality Pass - 13B Anchor Dates - The Atherian West|13B]], [[Causality Pass - 13C Anchor Dates - Tower Revolt to the Reconquest|13C]], [[Causality Pass - 13D Anchor Dates - Two Crowns and High Noon|13D]] and [[Causality Pass - 13E Anchor Dates - The Road to 1360|13E]]. All 464 offsets in those tables were reproduced by machine. This page reconciles the dates the stretches share and makes one recommendation.

## The short version

- **Most clocks can be broken.** The Gateway Wars, Qiryath's exile and return, the last years of Arqesh, the Great Tower Revolt, the Covenant's rise, Adalmar's crown, the Breaking and the Nikandran reigns all come off their lines.
- **Two can be broken only by changing how long a reign lasts.** Eryndor needs a reign of thirty-six years, not twenty-two. Astrion needs eleven years at Caleran, not three. Both are in the recommended package, and both are yours to refuse.
- **Three cannot be broken by dates at all.** These are the last thirty years of the republic (380–350 BR), the Eastern Sea Wars and the road to 1360. The reasons are under [[#What dates cannot do]].
- **The recommended package changes about 850 lines.** A lighter package without the two reign changes is about 515. No line in the Grenzburg or Caleran material changes in either.
- **No event or fate changes.** The moves change when things happen, and where a gap opens they add a sentence of cause. A few pairs of events that do not depend on each other change order; each is named under its stretch below.

Line counts are lines of canon, story and research text that cite the year or encode it in a phrase such as "six years later". They are estimates from searches, not from reading every file.

## The test

- **Offset.** The setting's year minus the analogue's year (BR minus BC, AR minus AD).
- **Line.** Three or more beats of one sequence whose offsets fall within 8 years of each other. A line shows when somebody tabulates the sequence.
- **Run.** A line whose beats follow one another. A run is what lets a reader date the *next* event, so runs matter most.
- **Interval.** Two successive beats should not stand the same distance apart as their sources (within a third, or within two years where the source's gap is short).
- **Reign lengths** are the same fault in another form. The three Nikandran emperors reign 38, 27 and 39 years against the Komnenoi's 37, 25 and 37.

The researchers were asked to break every line. In three places that is arithmetically impossible, because too many dated beats sit between two fixed ends: Cassian to Vestian (27 beats where ten would fit), Darneth to the Unification (26 beats in a band 17 years wide) and the Reformation (ten beats where six would fit). In those places the proposals break the runs and the matching intervals and declare the lines that remain.

## Decisions for the creator

Each has a recommendation. Answering "apply the recommended package" accepts all of them as recommended. The creator did so on 15 November.

1. **Which package?** Recommended (about 850 lines), lighter (about 515), or a tier named stretch by stretch. *Recommended: the recommended package, applied one stretch at a time, the road to 1360 first.*
2. **Eryndor's long reign (13A).** His clock breaks only if he reigns thirty-six years (709–673 BR). He spends seven years on the river throne with the images answering in his voice and four at the seep whose water "mended only those who stayed beside it", and dies at fifty-two (canon has him thirty at the loan of 695, so he is sixteen at Pelatha). His daughter Mehrava is eighteen at his death, not seven. *Recommended: yes. It removes the most recognisable fact about him, that he died young at the height of the conquest. Say no and the stretch falls back to three dates.*
3. **Cassian's fifteen years and Astrion's eleven (13C).** Cassian would die in 307, fifteen years after the ice, not twenty-four. Astrion would succeed in 340 and sit at Caleran for eleven years before the Cut of 351, not three. *Recommended: yes. The deathbed baptism a generation after the sign and the short reign of the emperor who restored the old rites are the two best-known facts in the sequence.*
4. **The funerals in 1290 (13E).** The Blackwater funerals and the reading of the *Brenholt Margins* would move from 1297 to 1290, and Alessior II's death from 1296 to 1289. The present crisis would be seventy years old in 1360, and the label "1297–1360" would become "1290–1360" on 25 lines. *Recommended: yes. It is the only move that breaks the opening run (the reading, the book-trial, the rising).*
5. **Adalmar's age (13D).** Moving Kaltfurt to 960 makes him seventy-six at his coronation in the ford and gives him a reign of fifty-eight years. Kaltfurt in 963 makes him seventy-three and clears the same lines by a narrower margin. *Recommended: 960.*
6. **Qarath's Well (13D).** Not in either package. The three landmark dates of High Noon (Caedros III crowned 1121, Qarath 1214, the Poured Cup 1225) still sit 143 to 145 years above their sources, a century apart. Breaking that line moves Qarath to 1219, cuts the War of the Seal from ten years to five, touches three present-day files and has no year to spare. *Recommended: leave it, and fix the Nikandran reigns that follow, which is where a reader can in fact date the next event.*
7. **The Accord.** 1338 and 1343 were treated as fixed because the Grenzburg game is built on them. Freeing the Accord would not clear the Reformation's band, because the 1360 board's own dates sit 210 to 217 years below their sources in the 1560s. *Recommended: keep both.*
8. **Three note titles (13C).** `Cassian and the Four Sees - The Settlement of 292 to 316 AR`, `Astrion and the Road to Vestian - 348 to 377 AR` and `After Cassian - The Succession of 316 and the Forty Flames` carry dates that decision 3 would change. *Current Focus Board* and the Vestian draft link the first two, and this pass edits neither. *Recommended: rename them yourself inside Obsidian, which updates those links.*

## What dates cannot do

- **The road to 1360.** The present day is a portrait of the 1550s to 1580s by design, and the Concordance says so. The Accord stands seventeen years behind it at 212 below Augsburg, and the board's own dates (1349, 1354, 1357, 1358) stand 210 to 217 below theirs. Between the Grey Sweat and the Accord, ten Reformation beats have to fit in a band of offsets 21 years wide, which has room for six. After the moves above six beats still share an 8-year window; today it is seven. Loosening is all dates can do here. Breaking it would take a third bend of the sequence itself, so that two to four of its beats (the bought conclave, the rising, the council, the chartered order) stop being recognisable. That is event work and is not proposed on this page.
- **Darneth to the Unification, 380–350 BR.** The Unification is the epoch of Atherian reckoning and is cited on about 950 lines. Darneth is the precedent for Astaroth's answers to engineers. The queen's childbearing (Hadram in 372, Lethia in 351) caps the length of the succession war. Twenty-six dated beats share offsets between 313 and 329. Moving the killing of Casvilar would widen that band by about five years at a cost of about 240 lines, so it is not proposed, and the earlier instruction to keep 370 stands.
- **The Eastern Sea Wars.** They are pinned from outside by Qarzeth's fall (520) and by the veterans' return and the Noon Sighting (491, 488). Every arrangement inside those pins leaves the revolt, the King's Bread and the withdrawal within 8 years of one offset.
- **Kellovar to Vestian, 372–398 AR.** Held for the Vestian draft.
- **Adalmar and the Folk Wars read as one clock.** Within each sequence no line survives. Read together their eleven beats still fall between 184 and 217 above, because the Drowning (993), the Split (1015) and the vaulting of the Crown (1104) are fixed.
- **Echoes hung on a fixed date.** The reigns around 742 and 690 BR; the four years before Velos (288–292 AR); the second Gateway war's opening (612–608 BR) and the last war (528–520 BR), each a single source moment.

## How it was applied

1. **One stretch at a time, one commit each:** the road to 1360, Two Crowns, the Tower Revolt to the Reconquest, the Atherian west, the ancient east.
2. **Each move was five kinds of edit:** the year, wherever it means that event; spans, headings and era labels; relative phrasing ("six years later", "in his nineteenth year"); ages and reign lengths in [[Named Figures of the Known World]] and the Chronology's sanity checks; and the registers.
3. **Nothing was written until it was checked.** Each writer produced a patch file of exact old-text and new-text pairs. A patch whose old text did not occur exactly once was refused. Number-only swaps were tallied against the approved pairs, and every change of wording was read.
4. **After each stretch** the Chronology's date order, the links and the remaining mentions of each old year were checked.

## Method and limits

- **How it was done.** Each researcher read the brief in [[Causality Pass - Direction]], the Concordance's rules and mappings, the second-bend record for the stretch, the Chronology and Named Figures, and the reader articles needed to check dependencies. Each tabulated every dated beat with a recognisable analogue, counted citations, listed what pins each date and designed two tiers.
- **Real-history years were not looked up.** They are from memory, checked by a second reading when this page was assembled. Where sources disagree (the fall of Jerusalem, Masada, Covadonga) the detail records give both.
- **Line counts are estimates.** They come from searches for the year and for the phrases each researcher thought to look for. Other sentences will depend on these dates.
- **Many ages are inferred.** Canon gives no birth year for Eryndor, Cassian, Sevrin, Lucar, Corlan, Corvenas I or the three Nikandran emperors.
- **Which beats a reader recognises is a judgement.** The blind readers' own notes were not available, so each researcher rebuilt the lines from the published scores.
- **Some pairings are a researcher's inference** and are marked so in the detail records: the imperial and Velian pairings in 13C, and Alessior's death and Falkendorf in 13E.
- **The research found lines no earlier record had declared:** the last years of Arqesh (13A), the start of the Unsealing (13C), the Impounding with the Rekindling and Korinna (13D) and the Nikandran reigns (13D). Others may remain.
