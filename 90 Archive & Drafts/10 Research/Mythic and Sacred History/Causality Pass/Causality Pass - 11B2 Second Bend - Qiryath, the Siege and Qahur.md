# Causality Pass — 11B2 Second Bend: Qiryath, the Siege and Qahur

> [!note] Record of applied bends, 14 November 2026 (second bending pass)
> Written under [[Causality Pass - Direction]] and Rules 6–8 of the [[Real-History Concordance]], after an adversarial historian read the first bend ([[Causality Pass - 10B2 Bending Qahur and the Qiryath Kings]]) and could still call beats in advance. The first bend made mechanisms native. This one breaks skeletons: who lives, who wins, who never arrives, and the order and number of judges, kings and dynastic phases. The bends are already written into the reader articles this writer owns ([[#Files changed]]). Everything other files must change is in the [[#Integration patch]]. No person or place was renamed; suggestions are in [[#Rename proposals]]. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## Summary

- **Qiryath (adversary 7.5/10).** Twelve of the thirteen incident beats are removed or inverted, and the Alesia-like ditch of 743 is re-caused. The judges now form one chain: a deliverer's grandsons are the next cycle's raiders, and the water that drowns them passes by blood, through a surety who is carried east alive, to the Arqeshite wife of a later guardian, who ends the "drought". The kings lose David entirely: a custodial house holds both the basket and a witnessing hand, the old guardian survives his fever by thirteen years, the wood-carrier founds no house, and Rava's question is asked over a lamp-filler crushed on the stair.
- **The Siege and the Returnings (7/10).** Seven of eight beats are removed or inverted and one is re-caused. The cycle keeps its two native hinges (the craftspeople break the image; ships that carried nothing came home) and is rebuilt around them: the blockaded harbour pays its tide from its own streets, a woman the commander confined carries his intentions into the town, the craftspeople open the gate and break the image, and the Returnings are told by cargo.
- **Qahur (4.5/10).** Nine of twelve beats are removed, inverted or reordered. The first division is three-cornered with the dead ruling the middle; the "foreign" house builds the empire; the foreclosure ends in a marriage and two king-lists; the sun is taken out of the Radiance, which is now older dead set on newer; the heir dies before his father; a river crown replaces the soldiers' crown; and the vanished prince comes back.
- **Exodus (3/10; light touch).** The provision is fixed as springs and bearing gardens in the Glass, so its end is a channel going dry. The "morning portion" is withdrawn.
- **Kept, as instructed.** The Sojourn's provision ends at the threshold and the Prophet dies there; the judges become protector-kings of First Qiryath; the sign and the Withholding (743–654 BR); Arqesh destroys First Qiryath in 742; the exile and the return under Vashar; Yaelith's mixed households; Qahur's houses (Senareth, Yasharet, Aramen, Zaheret), the White Measure, the dead landlords and the 903 stoppage; High Harbour's Giant court destroyed c. 1980–1960 by a mixed maritime coalition. Protected lines kept: "whom do the stones answer?"; the mixed households' offering answered after the king's smokes; the craftspeople break the image; ships that carried nothing came home; Iddar entered as returned cargo; "paid his spearmen in graves".
- **Slips fixed.** Qoram now argues from Mikalet's answer. Hador is an old man because Gedran falls forty-three years after his youth (c. 1171 to c. 1128).

### The new sequence and its causes

1. **Barakhel (c. 1335 BR).** Tarkhan camel riders hold three wells on a raiding household's road home; the raiders surrender thirsty on safe conduct and are executed. His sons keep the wells as a toll station, take pledges of grain, animals and children in dry years, and within two generations are **House Sereq**. *Human: protection becomes rule.*
2. **Tovan (c. 1290).** A pledged herd boy, hidden in the old cistern, is answered by the deep water. He names the Sereq camp; the stream comes out of season; raiders, animals and the penned children drown, his two cousins among them; villagers flail the survivors. The bereaved sue. The judges lay the debt on the gift: his line calls the streams for the three villages and eats their bread, and holds no office. The water follows the mothers' line to Imra's children; his sons seize her youngest, the rite is missed and the stream drowns the boy and four of them. *Supernatural: a named-petitioner Menhir bargain. Human: a mother's lie, the collectors' substitution, the judges' sentence, the sons' greed.*
3. **Tirzah (c. 1210).** Unchanged; native.
4. **The scales of Gedran (c. 1171).** The weigher Dovan gives true weight in secret and is killed under the fortress weights on his own scale-pan. Nareh is shut in the grain pit to be sold. Hador's fire, meant to draw off the guards, burns the labourers' huts and the families in them; Nareh walks out with the honest stones. Hador's private war closes the road for two hungry springs while Nareh re-weighs every household's old tallies. The elders unhouse Hador and Nareh speaks the sentence. Gedran hires him as gate captain; his prisoners are sold east and many die on the winter roads. *Human throughout.*
5. **Gedran falls (c. 1128).** Othan swears a harvest truce. Gedran asks for his daughter Seleth by name, because her mother is of the cistern house. Nareh begs him to send a judge instead. A herd boy reports the gate half-manned; Othan attacks before the truce ends. Hador, old now, kills the two elders' children and takes Seleth out by the postern and down the river road. Nareh sets the honest stones in the fallen gateway. *Human; the supernatural stake is the girl's blood.*
6. **House Oram and the grain rooms (c. 962).** A custodial house holds the guardianship for three generations: one family carries the basket and lays one of the three hands on the stone. The keeper of the lower rooms, Shaqel's brother, lets his guards take women drawing portions; the lower town's women kill him; Shaqel hangs four.
7. **The basket (c. 953).** Shaqel falls with fever on the stair; the wood-carrier Yedran catches the basket; the stones are cool; the lower town carries him to the council, which names him guardian for the emergency. **Shaqel lives**, unable to climb, for thirteen years. Yedran opens the lower rooms and cannot leave the city on a festival day. *Contingency and the sign's misreading.*
8. **The stair (c. 940).** Shaqel dies in the winter of c. 941. His sons strip the river posts to claim the basket at the spring festival; the ford they left falls, its garrison is killed and the villagers behind it are sold. On the stair the two parties crush people against the wall. The basket goes up from hand to hand and the lamp-filler Hanel bar Sem carries it to the altar with broken ribs and dies three days later. The stones are cool.
9. **Rava's question (c. 940).** Hanel's widow asks whom the stones answered: Oram, the wood-carrier or her husband. Mikalet bat Oram, sister of the men who fought, answers that they answer only the offering. The lot follows. Yedran's command lapses and he returns to the wood road; the Oram sons are starved out of the grain rooms when villages and road houses feed the quarters directly.
10. **Qoram's marriage and the wandering streams (c. 829–824).** Keresh's credit is secured on shelf terraces; Qoram's levy takes terraces and strips the three villages that feed the cistern house; its last woman leaves the shelf and dies; nobody goes down at first rain. For four autumns the streams run astray. *Human greed causes the supernatural default.*
11. **The image on the stair (c. 826).** The diviners promise the streams if the image is received. Qoram argues from Mikalet's answer. Tobel refuses and escapes over the roofs with the staff; his wife and brother are killed at the door; a cousin climbs without a staff. The offering burns clean; the roll gives the day two names and a blank; the diviners proclaim; the streams do not return. The lower town's cistern is drained for the image's washing.
12. **The queen at the cistern (c. 824).** House Keresh has kept, by marriage, a line of daughters descended from the shelf girl sold east when Gedran fell. Zimra goes down Tovan's cistern and the streams answer her, by the names of Seleth's day: old orchards flood and newer terraces pay Keresh's stewards for a name. Tobel, living in the empty cistern house, bears true witness for the woman whose soldiers killed his wife. The judges rule as for Tovan: feed her, follow her in nothing. The diviners proclaim that the Countenance did it.
13. **Haviel (c. 824).** He has the diviners drowned in the refilling cistern. The villages do not rise. The road judges unhouse him. Tobel dies at the cistern house; Zimra's daughters keep it.
14. **The gate (c. 817).** Unchanged, with Tobel's granddaughter carrying the true staff back in among the families.
15. **746–742.** The river court cites the clean fire of c. 826; Qiryath's custodians cite the blank. In the siege a Keresh woman of the line names the streams away from the city. The council declares the foreign residents unhoused and they starve in the caravan yard. The Withholding follows; its cause stays unresolved, and the three in-world readings stand (the envoy's murder, the unhoused, a destruction foretold).
16. **The return.** The Vasharan grant lapses with the walls at shoulder height; the city finishes the sanctuary; the king's offering smokes (c. 660); Yaelith's households are answered (c. 654).

Blend: of the sixteen steps, the supernatural decides or bends 2, 7, 8–9, 10–12, 14 and 15 (the sign and the water), always through human misreading or greed.

### The new shape

1. **The harbour and the channel.** Unchanged: a detached Giant house, a speaking image of the founder, a tide mistress who keeps the water for travellers sent through the channel, lately the people taken in collection raids.
2. **The coalition closes the shore.** The raids stop. The commander distributes captives from the dependent villages and confines a woman seized from a headland sanctuary. The quarrel is over the image, whole or broken. The challenging captain sails home before the second winter with his share of villagers.
3. **Four wreck seasons.** The pact holds: storms on the besiegers outside, calm inside. The harbour has no travellers to send, so the image names its own: prisoners, debtors, servants, and craft families by lot.
4. **The woman who went in.** In the fourth winter the shipmistress frees the confined woman, and the two carry into the lower town what the commander means to do. The craftspeople offer the water gate for the lower town's lives; the commanders swear.
5. **The gate and the hammers.** The harbour falls on the craftspeople's night. The Giant ruler dies; the image calls for resistance; the craftspeople break it. The tide runs wrong; the breakwater road falls on an inhabited quarter. The oath is broken: the inland ally's men, urged by likenesses of their taken children, murder families who opened the gate, and every contingent kills, rapes and enslaves.
6. **The sea's due.** The default falls on whatever leaves the harbour by sea as property, people included. A captive set free ashore stops being cargo.
7. **The count of hulls.** The ships that carried nothing come home. The commander drowns bound women and children and is lost with his fleet. The captain of the bay loses his captive at the first wreck, pays the shore court in bronze after it eats several companions, and is drowned by his own bay; the women he held make the next bargain. One ship's people buy changed bodies from a Faewild hostess. The shipmistress gathers the put-ashore and settles them.

Blend: the pact (3, 6), the image and its naming (3, 5), the likenesses (5), the guide (4), the hostess and the new pact (7) are supernatural; the blockade, the commander's crime, the craftspeople's choice, the broken oath and every drowning are human.

### The new sequence and its causes

1. **Senareth (c. 1880–1580).** Unchanged.
2. **Three corners (c. 1580–1412).** Two living houses and the Answering Kings. Meresha grows up a day's sail from statues that answer through priests, and builds the White Measure on her distrust of a voice whose speaker cannot be shown. *Supernatural in its effect on people; authorially, office patterns worked by priests.*
3. **Khevar II (1412).** Named by three statues at the flood festival. Treaty, marriage, contract, then the killings. House-Builders say nobody can know who spoke; he closes two of their workshops.
4. **The Yasharet empire (c. 1248–1084).** Gateway pilots and caravan masters carry garrisons to the mines, the caravan stages and the first gateway ports, on White Measure credit. Khaseret's House takes the grain fleet, store courts and gate tolls in pledge.
5. **Default and foreclosure (c. 1084–1076).** As 10B2, with one cut (Iddar's priests preach that the dead can hold no pledge over the living; the sun line is gone). Iddar is walled in as returned cargo.
6. **Two king-lists (from c. 1076).** Aramen marries Iddar's sister. The empire is widened to pay the dead; 903 and c. 858 stand.
7. **The seal (c. 842–840).** Ithren inherits a middle crown whose best land the dead own. The metal in their walls is recast estate bronze with title marks. He digs a receiver's seal out of the vaults under Aseret; Nemeret's tomb tells him where. *Supernatural: Ishkatari recovery works. Human: a king who cannot tax the dead; a Greed Mask's advice.*
8. **The Single Radiance (c. 840–829).** One gilded serpent on runners, masked guards, small many-armed markers. It walks the old estate roads at noon and takes marked metal out of tombs and temples; Patrons are wounded; cleared fields go to the garrisons. Communities clear its roads and carry out their metal. Custodians who hide plate are marked and taken with their children. Temple towns go hungry. Houses hide anchors on islands and barges because the procession will not cross water. Aseret ferries its dead to the pits because anything buried with old metal is dug up.
9. **The price (c. 840–829).** The estate claims whoever wears the seal. Ithren hangs it on his six-year-old son. Deshren wastes.
10. **The rite (c. 829).** At seventeen Deshren calls the Copper Orchard's masters himself, returns the Orchard's fields, takes the regimen in the estate-house and dies. The stripped metal and the allotment tallies become his house; the seal is sealed in; the Radiance coils round the palace and stops. His stewards, the Orchard's people, bill his father first.
11. **The Noon Rent (829–815).** Ithren is his son's tenant for eight years and loses two attacks on the gate. He dies c. 821 without an heir, and the stewards claim the middle crown for a dead prince.
12. **The river crown (815–814).** The guards keep to the estate roads; the rent needs boats. Zaheret's boat households refuse the cargoes and carry the allotment harvest to island granaries. Stewards, markers and people who served at Aseret are killed and their families dispossessed. She is crowned on a grain barge.
13. **Ashkur (c. 810).** In with three exiled stewards, out alone with a copper-sealed tally. Arqesh collects east of the river in a dead prince's name and sends a tenth to Aseret.

## Authorial truths

**Qiryath**
- House Sereq descended from Barakhel's sons.
- Tovan's bargain was with a place-bound Menhir court person of the Reqa shelf's karst water. It bound his line as the shelf villages reckoned it on the day of the petition, through mothers, and lasted while one of the line went down at the first rain and named the streams. A missed rite sends the water where no one names it; that is what drowned his sons and what ran the streams astray from c. 828.
- Seleth's mother was of Imra's line. Hador sold Seleth to an Arqeshite river house, the forebears of House Keresh, which kept her daughters' line by marrying it back into the house. Othan refused to ransom or replace her and did not arrange the sale; the women's account is false as to his intent and true as to the result.
- Zimra's descent was real and the streams answered her. The image and its diviners had no part in it; the Crowned Countenance fed on both proclamations. A Keresh woman of the same line named the streams away from Qiryath in 743. In every case the water answered blood and exact performance and proved nothing about anyone's justice.
- Before 743 the sign was never withheld at an appointed offering. It answered the offering Hanel carried (c. 940) and the offering made under the image (c. 826) alike. The blank in the custodians' roll is their law about witnesses, not a sign. Mikalet's answer was true doctrine; she chose the altar over her house.
- Yedran lived, and died an old man in a river timber-yard (authorial date c. 921). The royalist murder story is false.
- Tobel lived, witnessed truthfully for Zimra and died at the cistern house (c. 820).
- Haviel's killing came after the water returned and was murder under Jirahar law. The gate's lifting (c. 817) was a genuine sign for the families' rescue and did not pardon him.
- The unhousing of 743 was the council's act. The cause of the Withholding stays unresolved, as everywhere. The streams' diversion is no fourth candidate.
- The Vasharan grant lapsed under Daryav I and the city finished the sanctuary; the king's offering was unanswered.

**The Siege**
- The image is the founder's infrastructure-office speaking through a palace image: an office pattern with no soul and no Giant continuity, the named party to the tide mistress's pact (as 01.8). Its naming of the harbour's own people in the siege was the pact's exact price. The dependent Pride Mask that appeared through it is unchanged.
- The tide mistress performed throughout the siege. After the image broke, the failure clause fell on whatever left the harbour by sea as property, persons held as goods included; a captive set free ashore fell outside it. This is exactness and carries no verdict.
- Astaroth's figure was a bounded manifestation supported by the old pier bronze. It ended when the bronze was dug out. Nobody wounded it.
- The shore guide is the Hecatara-associated court person of the old ruling: one short approach, paid by a long inland return. The wind envoy is withdrawn.
- The likenesses were one dependent Wrath Mask wearing the faces of children sent through the channel. No dead soul was present and nobody was compelled.
- The commander ordered the rape and confinement, drowned bound captives at sea and was lost with every hull. The drownings paid nothing.
- The refuge-violating officer and the captain of the bay are one man. His drowning paid nothing. The women's new pact is real: a living-party pact with the same sea court, priced in harbour goods put through the channel and in a keeper who stands in the water at each season's turn; its failure term takes the keeper.
- The hostess's alterations do hide a body from the pact's pursuit. She also changed people who refused.

**Qahur**
- The Answering Kings were copied court personas worked through tomb images by the mortuary priests. No dead king's soul was present. Khevar's proclamation was the priests' act.
- Both king-lists are true records of the same kings.
- The estate under Aseret was a lesser Giant court's burial estate on the Ishkatari pattern. The Single Radiance was its one Processional Coil, with Serpent-Mask Guards and Tally Hands: constructs with no person inside and no moral agency. Everything they did is Ithren's and his markers' responsibility. People the Coil collected were carried into the vault and died there; no soul was held.
- A receiver's seal transfers the estate's claim to its bearer. The estate's recurrence works drew on Deshren as such works once drew on Giant blood. Ithren's line had no Giant blood.
- Nemeret's Greed Mask told Ithren where the seal lay, to see its rivals stripped.
- **The Radiance's Pride Mask is withdrawn.** There was no cult to feed one.
- Deshren's rite succeeded: a complete bound human soul, the Court at Noon, anchored in the estate-house, the stripped metal and the allotment tallies. He was never crowned. The seal lies inside with him, so the Wardens hold his last order and guard the house. The rent demands are his. His accessibility in 1360 is protected.
- Ashkur's tally was Deshren's genuine grant. What Ashkur agreed inside, and what became of the three exiles, are protected.
