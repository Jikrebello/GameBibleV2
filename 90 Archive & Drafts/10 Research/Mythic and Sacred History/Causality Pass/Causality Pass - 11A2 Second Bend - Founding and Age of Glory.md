# Causality Pass — 11A2 Second Bend: Founding and Age of Glory

> [!note] Record of a second bend written into canon, 14 November 2026
> [[Causality Pass - 10A Bending the Founding]] made the founding's mechanisms native. An adversarial reader who knew Roman history could still call beats in advance, because the skeleton survived: the same people met the same fates in the same order, and the Age of Glory still read as a parade of Roman dynasties. This file records each beat the adversary called and what replaced it, the new sequence and dates, a clock check, rename proposals for the creator, and the integration patch for files this writer does not own. Controlling rulings: [[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review#Causality Pass 11A2 amendment (14 November 2026)]] and [[Age of Glory - Authorial History and Integration Review#Causality Pass 11A2 amendment (14 November 2026)]]. Rules: [[Causality Pass - Direction]]; Concordance rules 6–8.

## Summary

**The founding, 370–350 BR.** The great battle now comes first and settles nothing. The speaker written in by his partners lives and goes on selling bread to the man who agreed to his death. The priest-partner chooses the rival's field and dies on it, and the book of the seven is lost in the salt. The eastern march is a summer's pursuit of one man up a road that outruns an army. The sea-heir rows into the harbour whose chain burned his ally and cannot be touched there. The queen's sister is a hostage for seven years and dies in her own husband's blockade; her son is counted by the towns every spring and first meets his father at the table. The sea bargain brings grain in. The speech against the queen fails.

**The Age of Glory, 350 BR–0.** Succession runs on three makings that belong to different people: the **board** the armies swear to, the **table** at Sarqen, and the **arch**. Each crisis is one of them failing. A wife holds the table while her husband hangs on the board. The board speaks when no heir has been painted, governs for two winters and starves its capital through its own clerks. The general who covers it refuses to be painted and is made under an arch; his hostage, not his grandson, follows him. In a fever year the carters of Sarqen seat the next emperor. The famine administrator opposes the crown estate. The emperor who will not stand under drowns beneath his own banks while his guard faces the portrait.

**Anchors kept.** Casvilar killed 370; the Four at the Stores 367; Marelith destroyed 355; Thalion supreme and Sarqen under a governor c. 350; Meren born 358, reigning 318–286; Caelor 326/324; Thalion's death 318; an unbroken empire to 0 AR; the speaking portrait in 247 and the veil; the Crown of Olveth 219; the Sleepwalkers' Summer c. 200 (untouched and unexplained); Arcelon c. 190–182; Raveth's famine from 119 and the crown estate; Elian's citizenship grant and its oath; the Qiryath charter under the late Serrats; the low door 61; Isfir born 33 under Othran I; Othran II at 0 AR. Every victim and atrocity account is kept, re-caused where the cause changed. No person was renamed and no article title changed.

**Gaps closed.**

- *Harod's open Field.* Harod's Field after c. 441 lay **vacant**: its Warden died with his named enemies still at Avesh, and it bound no one. Casvilar's own Field against Drenoc died with him. What the seven left is **fouled**. The camps' proof is concrete: two companies paid off after Darsil, the first soldiers discharged since Kellovar, carry the war home to the upper Aurin and re-enlist in the spring. Wording for [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]] is in the patch.
- *The partnership after 358.* Velsar's officers no longer kill Thalion's wife. From 358 the queen holds Merath and Meren; Thalion sends Velsar his grain and writes each year to ask after the boy. The partnership also runs on soldiers unwilling to fight fellow avengers and on Venn's bread, and it ends when Velsar names Thalion on the salt in 353.

## Alignment with the late-republic bend (11A1)

[[Causality Pass - 11A1 Second Bend - Gateway Wars and Late Republic]] was written the same day and changed what stands behind 370. Its section "What the founding writer must know" has been followed in the founding articles:

- Casvilar carried a Field from Kellovar (383) against Drenoc, who was never produced. That Field died with him. The fouled contest of 370 is the seven's challenge.
- The seventh name was entered as Drenoc's son. He is the fugitive who dies at Darneth about 362. Seneth commanded the river line at Kellovar and fought for the senate at Velos; Draven is a ruined ship-owner; Tolm is quartermaster of the muster ground, and whether he knew is disputed.
- The Writing-In starts from the augurs' book, which already held counted contestants and the pardoned of 371 "with a line through them, still legible".
- The Kept Doors are dated 410; Calvena's marriage 389; Veyr comes to Sarqen in 373 for grain and not as a beaten fugitive.
- Harod killed two senators on his Field, so the contrast drawn here is vacant against fouled, and no article says that no blow was struck on Harod's.
- Casvilar's veterans held their Noon Roll plots undischarged; the discharge of 350–346 makes them property.
- Pyrassa's wardens had sworn at the Chain Peace never to raise their chain against a vessel in distress, and lent it to Melvo. Admitting the survivors is the other half of that oath.

Two things run the other way. 11A1's note 10 dates the reading of the names of 609 to 353; it is now **354**. Its residual-clock note keeps 373–371; see the clock check below.

**Order of application.** Both passes patch some of the same entries. The patch below is written to be applied **after** 11A1's and touches only the later part of each shared entry.

Order is also broken: the great battle precedes the collective government (source: follows it); the speech against the queen precedes the eastern march and the blockade and fails (source: follows and succeeds); the sea-heir's terms follow his defeat (source: precede it). The Salt Field (353) and Thalion's second marriage (360) have no source event since 10A cut the Donations and Octavia.

*Residual, outside this pass.* Samyra's restoration (373), Amesar's birth (372) and Veyr's death (371) sit at 326, 325 and 323 against 47, 47 and 48 BC. With 370/44 they still form the late republic's +326 run that Concordance rule 7 names, and two of them now coincide with offsets used above. They belong to the late-republic writer. If they stay, moving Amesar's birth to 371 and Veyr's death to 372 would clear both.

**Age of Glory.** BR dates run against AD, so the test is the sum. Olarion's death and Samor's accession were 247 + 68 = 315 and 246 + 69 = 315; they are now 315 and 314. No BR date equals an AD year of its source (69, 79, 193, 212, 222 are all unused; 33 BR is deliberate and is not an Age of Glory analogue). Velen's death in 61 no longer has a source event.

### 1. Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)

In the bullet "**370 BR — Casvilar's assassination.**" (11A1 replaces its first sentence), replace the last sentence ("The Field is held to stand open while any of the seven lives, and no commander dares disband an army.") with:

> Harod's Field had lain vacant since c. 441 and bound no one; Casvilar's is held to be fouled while any of the seven lives, and no commander dares disband an army.

Replace the five bullets from "**367 BR — The Four at the Stores and the Writing-In.**" through "**353–350 BR — [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession|The Salt Field and the table at the landing]].**" with:

> - **369–366 BR — Darsil and the quartered winters.** Velsar destroys the army of the seven's provincial friends at Darsil in the summer of 369, and none of the seven is there. Two companies he pays off carry the war home to the upper Aurin and re-enlist, and no commander disbands a company afterwards. His army is quartered on Darsil; the town rises in the spring of 366 and his brother Tarsen hangs eleven organisers. Velsar's wife Miretha brings grain carts every autumn for ten years and dies there in 356.
> - **367–364 BR — The Four at the Stores and the Writing-In.** Varroth, as First Celebrant, rules the Red Field fouled and unclaimed (367). Velsar, Thalion, Varroth and the speaker Belisar Venn govern from the store-house above the muster ground. Their enemies are written into the register of challengers and killed on the muster ground. In the winter of 366 Venn is written in at Velsar's demand with Thalion's consent; he stands on the muster ground with a borrowed practice sword, the squad sent to kill him walks away, and he keeps the river stores for the rest of the war. The war spreads, and in 364 Varroth's own pass garrisons burn his magazines.
> - **362–358 BR — The guest and the counted child.** Seneth sits at the spared towns' table at Sarqen from 362. Thalion's first wife Ilvara dies in 361; in 360 he marries Merath, Samyra's elder sister. Iren Sallas, one of the seven, walks out of Ilamar's sanctuary to his death (359). In 358 Merath bears Thalion's son Meren at Esharat, and Samyra's officers bring mother and child to the palace at Sarqen; Esharat lets the boy go on condition that the queen show him at the landing every spring.
> - **355–353 BR — Marelith and the guests of Pyrassa.** In one linked 355 BR operation Melvo holds [[Marelith]] fast with Pyrassa's harbour chain, burns it with Corial Draven aboard and defeats Ardel Veyr's nearby main force among the central Thalassian anchorages. Ardel reaches Pyrassa with eleven hulls and the city's survivors and is admitted under the Chain Peace; Melvo refuses to take a guest from his own city's quay. Calvena makes terms there in 353, and Ardel lives out his life on the mole. Marelith, "the harbour without a chain", had sheltered families fleeing the Writing-In.
> - **354 BR — The names at Caleran and the Dawn Road.** Corven Ilar asks the assembly to declare Samyra an enemy; spared-town envoys answer with the Table Oath and the names of the hostages of 609, Thalion stands while they are read, and the assembly does not vote. Velsar marches on Avesh to seize the fugitive of the seven sheltered there; Vasharan relay riders carry the man up the Dawn Road ahead of him all summer, and he returns without a battle and without his man.
