# Causality Pass — 10D Bending the Long Frontier and the Covenant

Record of the bend applied on 14 November 2026 under [[Causality Pass - Direction]] to seven sequences that the whole-history pass had kept close to their real analogues ([[Real-History Concordance#Sequences still close to their analogues]]). Each sequence below gives the old beats, the new beats and their causes, and what was bent and why. Reader articles owned by this pass were rewritten in place; [[#Integration patch]] gives exact wording for every file this pass may not edit. Authorial truth sits in [[The Fourth Seal - Authorial Truth]], [[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]] and [[The Covenant after Caleran - Authorial Truth]].

## 1. Bir Qassim (603 AR)

**Old beats.** Disputed succession; "Uthar took the caliphate; Husan took the desert"; Husan and seventy-two of his household accept safe conduct to arbitration; a flood forces them off the named road; a travelling court voids the protection; they are killed at the dry wells; seventy-three dead by sunset; the escort digs the graves and its commander pays for the stones.

**New beats and causes.**
1. Husan spends six years among the well-clans of the dry country, where Mujalib's household sealed the station wells.
2. The safe conduct names fifty-eight travellers; the hired drovers are unnamed.
3. A flash flood takes the causeway at Bir Nadim; the escort goes six miles round by a drover's track.
4. Every station well on the North Well Road is kept by a bound office of the 4,444 that draws for those under the Covenant's protection. At Bir Qassim it draws for the escort and drovers and not for the fifty-eight: the bucket comes up wet and empty.
5. The captain asks the travelling court at Qafra. On the second day its presiding jurist, **Jarud ibn Sulayt**, rules the protection void (the route is an essential term); water given to the travellers becomes aid to rebels. The drovers go on passing skins at night.
6. Seven die of thirst, the oldest and youngest.
7. On the third night Husan reads the instrument at the well-mouth, clause by clause; after each clause the bucket comes up dry. This is the Mourning of the Wells.
8. At dawn the escort comes for their arms; they refuse; the escort kills thirty-four, Husan among them. The captain sends the seventeen women and children west with the drovers, water and camels. He pays for thirty-four stones; the mourning houses pay for seven more.
9. Within the year the well is dry for everyone (mourners: that morning; Witnessed registers: the keeper was recalled and the shaft silted).
10. The survivors reach the strait and seed the Sealed-Blood households at Qazmarid.

**What was bent and why.** The number (72/73 becomes 58 named, 41 dead, two honest counts of 34 and 41). The encounter (no army encamped against a river for days; a well-yard, a keeper, a court's letter and a dawn disarmament). The thirst mechanics (water present and refused by a bound devil reading its instrument cruelly; drovers drink in sight of the named). The martyr beats (no infant at the river, no brother killed fetching water, no head carried to the caliph, no captive women paraded: the women go free, and the liturgy is a reading). The contract torque grows stronger: the line is now literal. New in-world consequences: Sealed-Blood villages draw by hand and let no bound office keep their wells; Witnessed registers and mourning houses disagree about whether thirst counts as killing. *Uthar took the caliphate; Husan took the desert* is kept.

## 2. Rab Yequtiel (c. 748–831 AR)

**Old.** Physician to a Banu Mizan emir; "the Path's greatest philosopher"; the *Guide for the Wandering* reconciles Scroll legalism with Atherian philosophy. Title and role read straight through to Maimonides.

**New.** Covenant law requires a witness at every bound deathbed before the name is released. Jirahar physicians serve as witnesses in Zanthir's bond-hospitals and salt pans because both Covenant courts and Velian families accept them. Yequtiel sat at such beds for fifty years and recorded them. ***What the Register Keeps*** argues that a register keeps the name and lets the person go: the bound die as the free die. Reception: custodians quote it at bedsides; Keepers of the Path call it a licence for bondage; Velian copyists translate it. After 885 the foundry's name-cages make it false in one case nobody outside the foundry can identify, so it cannot calm occupied Caleran's funerals. Authorially it is right ([[The Covenant after Caleran - Authorial Truth]]: custody never touches routing; only a foundry cage closed at death does). Epithet proposed: **"the Witness at the Bed"**, replacing "the Lamp of the Exile".

## 3. The Fall of Caleran (905 AR)

**1453 furniture found.** The young conqueror at the walls (Idris at twenty-six leading in person); two weak points in the land walls; the last emperor's three possible deaths (in the breach with a borrowed spear, a body among the relics, walking out unrecognised); the great church sealed (the martyrs' precinct sealed "under glass" as evidence).

**Kept.** The breach-court train serving districts; Qahir's engines; the Red Star showing the weakness; the Ättar crews leaving; the Aurin flood; the marches bound; the Impounded Ember; Corvenas III's trap.

**New beats and causes.**
1. Idris accedes in 902 at twenty-three with no son. Every oath in the army names Qahir, then Idris, then nobody; re-swearing costs a season's musters. One arrow could widow the army, so his jurists keep him at Iqashar. He never sees Caleran; *al-Fattah* is a jurist's "Opener", which Thalmyric chroniclers render "Conqueror". This foreshadows 931 exactly.
2. Qahir's engine-master **Barzin**, in his sixties, and the train's chief jurist take the city.
3. The engines come to **one** span: the Star's share built into the imperial land wall in the Age of Glory to break in the wardens' test, rebuilt untested after the colleges were dissolved in 383. Thalmyric tradition: the Red Star showed it, and defenders saw red light on their stones; Covenant engineers: a dead college's archive bought at Sermora. Both are true.
4. On the last day Corvenas III carries the Velos Standard onto the wall and calls on the Fire before both armies. Nothing comes (the third public silence of that Standard). The holding quarters take terms that afternoon; those that have not by dark are stormed with killing, rape and enslavement.
5. The breach-court holds his charter void since 884 and accepts only his person and household. The **Instrument of the Ash Hall** surrenders him and the Hall's relics, listed one by one, and names no other person. He lives as their captive keeper and dies of a winter fever in 908; the Covenant inventory's "body among the relics, lamps unlit" is accurate.
6. While he lives the refuge governs in a captive's name; his death releases it, so the Mountain Rebellion begins in 908 and Caedros's coronation argues continuity without legality. Veridun strikes him from its prayers for the dead; Eclessian preachers read the Instrument aloud.
7. Idris's courier-borne ruling makes the Ember a deposit kept where it stands and open to view, so the basilica is never sealed or converted and its liturgy runs fifty-two years under guard.

**Why.** Every remaining 1453 image is replaced by a consequence of the world's own engines (the personal oath, Astaroth's grammar and the Star's share, Covenant law's view of a void charter, the law of deposit). The last emperor's death stops being a mystery; the emperor's failure becomes public and specific.

## 4. The Veil over Veridun (906 AR)

**Old.** Watchers saw a woman's figure spreading a veil of pale fire over the city before a night storm broke the siege: the Pokrov image and the storm of the robe legend. It also sat badly with Veridun's own rule that the Mother appears "in icons and dreams, never in fields".

**New beats and causes.**
1. A Qahirid column with a breach-court and lesser Surrender engines comes up the valley in winter.
2. The refuge council orders the store-cave flour burned rather than lose it.
3. The hearth-keepers of Veridun Minor (the households of Amaryah's lamp) refuse, and the city bakes all night; everyone eats.
4. At dawn the ovens' smoke lies over city and valley for three days, smelling of bread and cedar (the Dormition scent). Soldiers on the walls stand vigil instead of watch; the engines proclaim terms into fog.
5. Refugees dream of a woman banking an oven; the column's levies dream of their mothers' hearths.
6. On the third morning levies walk home; the commander turns back without reading a charter.
7. Feast: every household bakes through the night and opens the oven door at dawn; soldiers keep vigil under arms.

**Why.** It keeps a Marian miracle and the feast, makes the miracle native to Amaryah's hearth tradition and to Veridun's no-apparition rule, and adds a human chooser (the women who disobeyed). Sender, and whether the levies' going home was the answer, stay unresolved. In-world: Eclessian "dream of a starving city"; Covenant "fog and desertion".

## 5. Caelmount (917–918 AR)

**Old.** Caedros lets the vanguard through a pass, collapses road cuts behind it; militias hit supply animals; a refuge lamp burns through the ash storm (later art: a wall of divine fire); the victory gives Caedros legitimacy, and the crown follows.

**New beats and causes.**
1. Caelmount's hereditary road-menders have kept the wardens' load-test on their ash road in secret since 383.
2. Amir Qazem's column (levies sworn to Idris, a lesser engine, custodians with name-rolls) comes to destroy the store caves.
3. The valley carries its grain to the high pastures and burns the rest.
4. The menders ask the Red Star where the road fails under load; it shows the span over the ravine and names the terraces as price. They soak the span's ash with terrace water for a month.
5. The span breaks under the heaviest wagons in mid-column (engine, custodians); terraces slide after them. Shepherds take two custodians with their rolls.
6. Qazem's van reaches empty caves; snow closes the road behind it. He will not leave without the granaries; the van winters on camels and terrace timber under falling ash.
7. Without custodians or rolls, levies cannot say whose instrument binds them, and desert to the amnesty; the starving valley feeds them. A hermit keeps the shrine lamp, and they surrender their instruments in its light.
8. Qazem dies of lung-sickness; his officers surrender in spring 918.
9. Caedros, at Veridun when the road broke, had promised to feed the valley until its terraces were rebuilt. He cannot, so he takes Virelos and its granaries (921), and is crowned there (922) by leagues that watched him pay.

**Why.** No ambush, no landslide on a fleeing enemy, no miracle of the lamp; a road, a Star, a winter and a debt. The same Star that showed Barzin the Star's share answers the refugees twelve years later, which makes Astaroth's moral blindness a story. In-world disagreement: Church art (the lamp), founding sermons ("the Valley Ambush", Caedros's tactics), the menders' families (the Star and the terraces), Eclessian polemic (the founder's first victory bought from the Red Star).

## 6. Caleran 957 and the separately sworn amirates

**Old.** Durayd, with no relief, surrenders Caleran in his own name with the protected named first (Toledo 1085's capitulation by its own ruler, who keeps a throne elsewhere); his surrender widows the other districts' oaths; a dozen amirates are taken one at a time by siege, amnesty and purchase (the taifas).

**New beats and causes.**
1. The engine kneels (956); the funeral revolt (957), as before.
2. Durayd, unable to return to Iqashar empty-handed, sends his guard to carry the Ember to his ships.
3. The third generation of Covenant keepers, names pledged that it never be moved, bar the basilica; eleven die at the lamp; funeral crowds and deacons drive the guard out.
4. Durayd flees by sea to Qabir (d. c. 971, the emir's guest); the garrison families are left behind.
5. The parishes open the river gate. The surviving keepers open the forum precinct only when the heralds hear their terms: the abandoned garrison families, the surrendered quarters, the deposit's discharge. Horn players sound the three broken notes. Caedros II keeps two terms, Veridun pays the third; Caedros I dies at the Pillar.
6. The other garrisons are sworn to an absent living man, neither widowed nor servable; each amir re-swears locally. Durayd writes from Qabir offering releases for silver.
7. Thalmyria takes most amirates at their canal heads: Caedros II's engineers seize the heads in hills held since the 910s and set water-courts with the Banu Mizan hour-cup; registered fields get water; amirs keep walls above dry fields. River citadels and anchor works are besieged; one hill citadel holds eleven years on cisterns; some towns are sold.
8. No tribute passes either way. The Southern Houses' bronze tokens of 1052 are the occupation's canal shares.

**Why.** The ruler does not capitulate; the city is lost through a theft and saved by the enemy's honest keepers, an inversion with a moral edge. Fragmentation keeps its native cause (personal oaths) with a different trigger, and the Reconquest gains a land-based engine (water) that explains the water-tokens and the later War of the Southern Deeds. The parias have no counterpart.

## 7. Suhrab (1190–1243 AR)

**Old.** "Suhrab the Lawbinder"; the *Ledger of Ledgers* harmonising every provincial code (the kanun); the gifted son lodged on the Guard's evidence and killed in the cloister, recorded abroad as an execution (Mustafa).

**New beats and causes.**
1. The 1151 commission sits forty years unable to rank Qahuri and Iqashari codes; the young Suhrab is one of its clerks.
2. 1189 the stand collapses at Mazrakan; 1190 the renunciation is voided (kept).
3. Suhrab abandons ranking and writes the ***Ledger of Ledgers*** as a book of debts: each book is one occasion on which Covenant law killed the Covenant's own people, entered with its dead, wells, fields and silver, closed by the clause owed, to which every code yields in that matter only.
4. 1194: he reads the first book, Bir Qassim, at the dry well. Sealed-Blood mourners answer every clause *And yet the well was dry*. His diggers go down eighty fathoms and bring up dust. The crowds call him **the Debtor**; he keeps the name.
5. Later books: the Stand (Mazrakan), the Dye-Yard (931; the clause against armies sworn to a living man), the Wrong Well, the Two Readings, the Southern Deeds (closed by the Unburdened Deeds of 1212, which leads to Qarath's Well), the Benches (1243).
6. The Emptying: **Prince Mihrzad**, the most gifted son, rides the Qarath-frontier forts as a living witness while garrisons re-swear to the Threshold; forts he reaches hold, the rest open (Nikandros's half-recovery). His escort carries the Sweat into three clean forts. He dies in winter 1241 at a well-fort where only the Guard escort survives; his name cannot be released.
7. 1243: the book of the Benches lets a sealed register stand where no witness survives and names the Guard bench a competent witness, so that the prince can be released; he is the first release under it. Suhrab dies; Tahmur accedes before the bench.
8. The Cull's turn to the knife is caused by the 1190 voided renunciation and the Pageless Prince (1249–1252) alone.

**Why.** The epithet no longer echoes "Lawgiver"; the *Ledger* is native to a contract culture's habit of reckoning in debts; the son is neither accused nor imprisoned; the Guard's constitutional power comes from grief and the dead jurists, not palace intrigue. The Qarath's Well sequence (another writer's) is touched only by naming the *Ledger*'s book that caused the Unburdened Deeds.

## Blend, contingency and people

Fourteen turning points across the seven sequences. Supernatural cause or consequence in eight: the keeper at Bir Qassim; the Red Star at Caleran; the failed call on the wall; the Ember's deposit; the smoke and the dreams of 906; the Red Star at Caelmount; the engine's kneeling (kept); the custodians' rolls lost with the wagons. Human in six: Jarud's ruling; Idris kept at home for want of a son; the Instrument of the Ash Hall; the women's disobedience; Durayd's theft and flight; Suhrab's *Ledger* and Mihrzad's ride. Contingencies: a flash flood, a dead college's archive, snow a week early, a commander who would not go back, an escort carrying plague.

## Dates and spacing

No new date coincides with its analogue's AD year: 603 (Karbala 680), 748–831 (Maimonides 1138–1204), 905 and 908 (1453), 906 (the Pokrov c. 910–911; the robe 860), 917–918 (Covadonga 722), 921–922, 957 (Toledo 1085), 1190–1243, 1194 and 1241 (Suleiman 1520–1566, Mustafa 1553). Corvenas III's death three years after the fall and Mihrzad's death two years before his father's break the analogues' spacing.
