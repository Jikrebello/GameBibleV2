# Causality Pass — 10E Bending the Breakings and Qarath

> [!note] Record of a bend, 14 November 2026
> The creator asked for the sequences still close to their analogues to be bent ([[Real-History Concordance#Sequences still close to their analogues]]). This file takes five from 984–1231 AR: the Breakings (Iconoclasm), Ioan's work (John of Damascus), Strazgorod (Vladimir's embassy), Caedros III (Basil II) and Qarath's Well and after (Manzikert, Romanos IV and the Komnenian rise). It follows [[Causality Pass - Direction]] and builds on the spine in [[Causality Pass - 08 Two Crowns and High Noon, 962 to 1232 AR]], which it supersedes where they differ. The owned reader articles have been rewritten to match; shared files are untouched and their changes are in [[#Integration patch]]. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## 1. The Breakings, 984–1052

### Old beat sequence (08 spine §§1, 4, 8)

1. 983–984 Ashfall Omen; Ilarion dies; Leovar III acclaimed.
2. 987 First Breaking.
3. 1011 Leovar III dies; child Leovar IV under the regency of his widow Anthe.
4. 1013 Anthe summons the Council of the Thousand Lamps (needs: army, burial, grain); lamps restored.
5. 1029 Meliton burning; Leovar IV, "raised by the empress who restored the lamps", begins the Second Breaking.
6. 1049 Leovar IV dies on the ice; daughter Theodosia succeeds.
7. 1052 Theodosia opens the lamp-houses and institutes the Rekindling; d. 1071.

Beats 3–7 kept Iconoclasm's shape: breaking emperor, restoring empress-regent at a council, second breaking emperor, second restoring empress with a feast.

### New sequence and causes

1. **962–987 (kept).** The re-endowed church's lamps gutter (Pass 05's rule); Mount Askre, the lung-fever, the Great Lamp shrunk to a bead; Ilarion dies (984); Leovar III, whose own war-lamp went dark, reads it as the Fire refusing vessels; the First Breaking (987); the hood; households hide lamps in wells and ovens; the Reconquest slows; the march without mourners.
2. **1008–1011, the dry years.** Three dry summers sink the highland wells. Families deepening their shafts lift the lamps their mothers hid in 987; witnesses swear some come up burning (the well-lights, kept contested). Women carry them back to the parish churches. Leovar's soldiers, sons of those households, refuse to break them in several villages. *Cause:* weather (human/natural) bends a supernatural pattern into public view.
3. **1011, Dryma.** Leovar III, seventy-six, goes himself to break a lifted lamp at Dryma below Mount Askre, drinks from the sunken well and dies of the flux. Households: the well judged him. Soldiers: the drought. *Contingency.*
4. **1011–1013, the hesitation.** The army acclaims his adult son **Leovar IV** (35), who has carried his own war-lamp hidden in his saddle-bag for twenty-four years; it never kindled in his hand. He needs kindling lamps (the Covenant army is free again after the Pledged Custodian), monks to bury the dead and, in a drought, the monasteries' grain: the 08 writer's three native needs, kept and given to a man. His mother **Anthe**, Caedros II's daughter and the Breaker's widow, holds the palace guard and the hood; he allows lamps in houses only.
5. **1013, the Night of the Thousand Lamps.** The highland households walk down with their lifted lamps, a thousand by the chroniclers' count. Anthe orders the guard to hold the cathedral doors; its captain **Arsen**, whose mother is in the square, opens them. The crowd unhoods the Great Lamp and finds it burning low after twenty-six years (open: wonder, or a vent and the keepers). Leovar IV kneels, recalls the monks; the Synod receives Ioan's sayings; the lamp-carriers acclaim **Makarios** Patriarch (about 35; past 80 at Strazgorod). Anthe lives in unlit rooms until 1027. *Driver:* human (crowd, a captain's choice), with a contested wonder.
6. **1015, the Split (kept).** Makarios, made by the lamp-carriers, will not let Aurelium examine a lamp the households unhooded with their own hands.
7. **1013–1029, the Master of Lamps.** Leovar IV, loving and fearing for the lamps, creates a court office to keep them and gives it to his saddle-bag companion **Kallion**. In 1029 Kallion forces the Pelin's Bridge lamp before Meliton to prove a restored lamp can serve an army, and burns (B4-05, kept).
8. **1029–1049, the Impounding.** The man who let the lamps in locks them all away "until the Fire is safe": crown lamp-houses, flogged and killed monks, households back to the wells (kept). The keepers, paid in oil by the lamp, sell it.
9. **1049, the cold houses.** Leovar IV falls on the cathedral ice (kept). Custom requires the capital's lamps to pass the bier; the seals are broken and the houses found cold. Household fire-lines older than the Fall are dead in the crown's keeping. Crowds hang keepers from the lamp-house beams, honest with thieves. Thalmyric preachers: the Fire left what was owned. Eclessian canonists: a lamp without oil goes out. *Driver:* human greed.
10. **1049–1052, the Rekindling from necessity.** **Theodosia** (24) reigns in her own right and has nothing to restore. Families walk to Caleran to beg a light from the Ember, the only fire with an unbroken line, and are robbed and killed on roads that still pass the last amirates. She presses the last sieges; in 1052 the last amirate surrenders and the roads join; every lamp is put out at dusk and relit hand to hand from the Ember. The Master of Lamps' office, keeper of the cold houses, is remade to run the relay. She walks the first mile barefoot as penance. The feast's dark eve is the **Night of the Cold Houses**. Theodosia dies in **1076** (moved from 1071, a forbidden analogue year).

### What was bent and why

- **No empress-regent and no council.** Irene and Nicaea II were the most legible beats. Restoration now comes from the land (drought, wells, women carrying lamps), a contingency (the Breaker's flux) and a guard captain's choice. The Eclessian apologetic loses "a lamp-breaker's widow and her infant" and gains **the hooded throne**.
- **One man restores and impounds.** Leovar IV's love for the lamps he could never make kindle explains both acts, so the second phase is a character's tragedy, not a second iconoclast reign.
- **The second phase ends in death, not reversal.** Theodora's feast celebrated a decision; the Rekindling now answers a loss, and its relay was invented by walking families before it was a crown office. This also explains two existing customs: the household lamp "tended, not lit", never handed to another, and the Master of Lamps trusted with the relay and never with a lamp.
- **Kept:** Leovar III, the Ashfall Omen, 987, the hood, the well-lights, the march without mourners, the Pledged Custodian timing, 1013, the Split, Pelin's Bridge and Meliton (1029), the cathedral ice (1049), Theodosia the Kindler, 1052, the relay from the Ember and the receipt remark.

### In-world disagreement

- Whether the well judged Leovar III (households) or the drought did (soldiers).
- Whether the Great Lamp burned untended under the hood (monasteries) or through a vent its keepers fed (Leovarists).
- Whether the Fire left the impounded lamps (Thalmyric preachers) or the oil ran out (Eclessian canonists).
- Whether the Rekindling is a triumph (liturgy) or a penance (march-town grandmothers).

### Authorial truth

- The Breakings remain the Guttering's second and third generations (Pass 05).
- Leovar III's flux was ordinary drought sickness. No judgement.
- Leovar IV's saddle-lamp never kindled because he never carried it to protect anyone; the Flame answers purpose, not love.
- The cold houses were theft and the seal: a sealed lamp is an untended lamp. No supernatural withdrawal.
- Left open, as in 08: whether any well-light burned untended for decades, and whether the Great Lamp did.

## 2. Ioan of Velia: the *Sayings for the Foot of a Lamp*

### Old

Ioan "the Lamplit", a Thalmyric monk under Covenant protection in the Velian belt, writes the *Three Orations on the Housed Fire*: John of Damascus's three treatises on images, written from Umayyad territory.

### New

Ioan is a Velian potter's son who took vows in a Thalmyric march monastery and was driven home in 987 to his father's kilns at **Keramion**. He and the exiled monks learn the wheel and make cheap one-spout clay lamps, each stamped on its foot with one of forty sayings. Velian traders sell them north by the crate under Covenant customs seals; Iqashari jurists let the trade run and tax it. When the Breakers smash a lamp the foot survives, and households mortar the feet into their walls; children trade them to complete the forty. The Synod receives the forty in 1013. Sayings quoted in reader prose: *the Redeemer had a body; the Fire may have a lamp* (the formula, kept); *clay is the Fire's porch and not its prison*; *nobody lights a lamp to command the dark*. Named Figures' epithet becomes "the Potter".

### What was bent and why

The analogue's iconic detail was a learned treatise from enemy territory. The new form keeps the Covenant's embarrassing role (the court's bet that the Breaking would wreck Thalmyria) but makes the defence physical, cheap and archaeological, and gives farmers something to find in walls. The argument now travels as trade at a Covenant tariff.

## 3. Strazgorod, 1059–1061

### Old beat sequence

Radomir I sends sworn envoys to test four faiths (Covenant: "a marvel and a cage"; Path: "a god who wanders owns no land"); in Veridun their lamp kindles unbidden before witnesses including a Sanctum examiner travelling with them; report: *"We went to see their fire, and it came to see us"*; Radomir asks for the lamp; Makarios refuses; Radomir chooses the Sanctum for Lotharic's heiress and swords.

### New sequence and causes

1. **Vesari hearth-law (new custom).** A house's eldest woman keeps its fire; a house whose fire dies must relight from a fire greater than its own and becomes the client of whoever lends the light.
2. **1059.** Grass-Tarkhan riders burn Radomir I's hall at Strazgorod; the house-fire dies. Borrowing from a neighbouring prince would make him that prince's man while he is gathering the princedoms. He sends for the greatest fire anyone on the Orzva has heard of: the Ember that relit Thalmyria in one night (1052). *Cause:* the Rekindling's fame plus a raid.
3. **1061.** His aunt **Zdena**, the fire-keeper, carries the cold lamp to Veridun with sworn hearth-envoys. Makarios refuses to light an unbaptised hearth from the Ember. While they argue, the cold lamp takes the Flame in her hands, lit by no one, before witnesses of both churches and a Sanctum examiner in Veridun on the Pelin's Bridge business, who records it answering.
4. Makarios: the Fire has chosen its dwelling; no hand may move it. Zdena: *"It chose my hand. I carried it."* She stays to tend it; her descendants keep it in the cathedral close.
5. Under hearth-law Veridun now keeps Radomir's fire, so he is Veridun's client. He refuses. The Sanctum, whose examiner registered the lamp as the house of Radomir's, offers baptism, Lotharic's heiress and swords; his new hall is lit from Lotharic's hearth at the wedding. Their son rules Virelia.
6. **1103 (reinforced).** The Council of Sabres argues that a house whose first fire burns in another's hall cannot pass its seat by blood alone. Veridun's standing offer to return the lamp is a trap under the same law: a fire handed back from another's hall is a lent fire.

### What was bent and why

The embassy and test were the analogue's skeleton; both are gone. The torque (a kindled Vesari lamp kept in Veridun, offered back at every election since 1103, honoured hostage for three centuries) stands. The Kindling Question gains a sharper edge: the Fire came unbidden to a heathen house's lamp, lit by no priest in no rite, which neither Veridun's "consents where sanctity prepares" nor the Sanctum's "entrusted to the apostolic body" predicted. The 1103 election now has a second native cause alongside the famine-fever.

### In-world disagreement and authorial truth

- Veridun: the Fire chose its cathedral. Sanctum: examined, answering, the house of Radomir's. Vesari villages: it chose Zdena. Neither church keeps her day.
- *Authorial:* genuine kindling. It answered Zdena's purpose: she had carried the house's fire to give the raid's widows and orphans, sheltered in Radomir's household, a hearth again. It was not a sign for either church or for Radomir's politics.

## 4. Caedros III, 1121–1167

### Old beat sequence

Crowned 1118, a descendant of Theodosia raised in frontier monasteries; military districts; the War of the Southern Deeds won by kindling; Kolonna (1134); the ransom of 1139 refills the treasury; never married, soldier-ascetic; after Kolonna believes the Fire watches the throne; dies childless 1167 and leaves the succession to the Fire. The 1118–1167 reign also matched Basil II's forty-nine years.

### New sequence and causes

1. **1121.** Crowned at Virelos (moved from 1118; 46-year reign). Raised in the frontier monasteries, left at sixteen for the garrisons; big, loud, dances at district weddings, godfather to some four hundred district children. As a garrison captain he married **Chrysanthe**, a Saronikan admiral's daughter, whose father's ships now carry the settlers' grain. Children: **Orestan** (c. 1114) and three daughters.
2. **1121–1139 (kept).** Districts owing a lance and a lamp; the 1126 ruling; the mercy towers; the district lamps kindle; Kolonna (1134).
3. **1137, Vresna.** After the Day of Two Readings, the stranded flank's levies throw down their arms at the ford of Vresna; Orestan, commanding the district horse, has some four hundred cut down. The district riders cheer; his father does not.
4. **1139, the Kolonna Declaration.** At Kolonna, before the district families, Caedros declares that his heir will be the claimant whose lamp the Fire kindles at his bier: a way to bar Orestan without naming him. Synod protests; Chrysanthe argues for her son until her death (1163); districts cheer.
5. **1139–1167, High Noon on credit.** The Covenant's ransom refills the treasury (consistent with the Covenant writer's "helped refill"); Caedros spends it on roads, aqueducts, district towns, stone monasteries and a plough-team for each godchild's wedding, then borrows from Sestaran houses to finish the Kolonna aqueduct. In debt by the 1160s. In his last years he sits up with the Kolonna lamp; it never kindles again.
6. **1167, the Unanswered Funeral (kept, re-caused).** Seven claimants: Orestan, two sons-in-law, three nephews, the captain of the palace guard. None kindles. Orestan's flares with bought script-fire; the warlock smells it; the other six blind him on the steps (still the first imperial blinding). Despina's lamp kindles; the War of the Seven Lamps; Andrion. Orestan lives twenty years more in a Kedravan monastery.
7. **1169 onward.** Andrion inherits an empty treasury and Sestaran debts; the district levy grows to pay them, which feeds the 1212 Unburdened Deeds.

### What was bent and why

Unmarried, ascetic, full treasury, childless and forty-nine years were all Basil II. He is now married, convivial, indebted, a father who uses the Fire against his own son, and his reign is forty-six years. The funeral, Despina and the districts' emperor (the 08 writer's native succession) stand, but now have a motive and a victim. The 1360 board gains a precedent: the last emperor who sat up with a flame left his succession to it and started a war, which the court will remember while Caedros IV keeps his flame-silences.

### In-world disagreement and authorial truth

- Preachers: did the Declaration save the empire from the butcher of Vresna, or drive a barred son to buy script-fire?
- *Authorial:* the Kolonna kindling was genuine and answered his rescue of the children, not the throne. The Declaration was his own misreading (theophany's temptation). Despina's lamp answered her prayer for her sons (08, kept). Vresna was a real atrocity.

## 5. Qarath's Well and after, 1212–1231

### Old beat sequence

1212 Unburdened Deeds; 1214 drought, fouled outer wells, sworn well, the army drinks; dawn sickness; Tarkhan ride through; Nikandros's Kedravans (who did not drink) withdraw; Markellos stays, is captured, held with contract-courtesy, ransomed for the districts; the regent Sergian blinds him to "widow" the ransom; the Court of Breaches answers that an instrument sworn to the Throne cannot widow; Markellos dies of the wound-fever within the month; civil war; Sergian killed (1217); Nikandros wins with the monasteries and Vlastrava and is crowned (1225).

### New sequence and causes

1. **1212–1214 (kept).** Unburdened Deeds; drought; fouled outer wells; the sworn well with a bound warden.
2. **The curb.** Its three-script text now has two clauses: *Drink, guest, and be at peace with the house of this well until the sun sets again. The guest who lifts his hand against the house before then owes the house the price of the water, and the house names the price.* Most officers take the Atherian for a dedication. **Nikandros**, schooled in a refuge monastery, reads it aloud to the council as a bargain and is overruled. Markellos hears him and drinks anyway, last, from his men's bucket. The Kedravans keep the rule and do not drink.
3. **Dawn (kept).** The drinkers' water turns when they take up arms; Tarkhan companies ride through; Nikandros withdraws in order with the wounded; Markellos stays with the men who drank.
4. **Sunset of the second day: no captivity.** The peace runs out; Suhrab's officers bring water-skins, not chains. Under the curb the drinkers are guests who broke the peace, not prisoners of war. Suhrab keeps the emperor three days in the well-house tent with contract-courtesy and sends him home with his surviving men and **the bill for the water**, in the curb's three scripts: the Southern Deeds districts.
5. **Winter 1214–1215: the court's crime.** Markellos refuses to pay. His uncle **Sergian**, the Caesar who keeps the empty treasury, argues that the drinkers are the Covenant's guests still, with its water in them, and that a holder who laid down his arms as the enemy's guest has surrendered, which district tenure law punishes by forfeiture. The **Forfeiture of the Drinkers**: every district holder who drank loses his farm to Sergian's hired companies, who must be paid in land. In spring 1215 the council and a frightened Synod depose Markellos by the same reasoning, and Sergian takes the throne, arguing the bill left the palace with him.
6. **1215, the Covenant's answer (kept in substance).** The Court of Breaches: the emperor drank under his standard, so the bill is the Throne's, which does not die and is not deposed (citing the Sublime Threshold and the Ember deposit of 957). Garrisons occupy the Southern Deeds districts. Sergian's companies evict the drinkers' families in the rest (burned barns, men too weak to lift a spade beaten off their threshing floors). This answer is what 09 relies on: no emperor signs a Covenant instrument since 1215; the reader article adds that none drinks as a Covenant guest.
7. **1215–1225, civil wars (kept, re-caused).** Markellos shelters with the Kedravan monasteries, which refuse forfeited farms: *we did not drink, and we will not eat*. 1217: drinkers' families who walked to Veridun with their door-lamps kill Sergian in the Crown Hall; Caldrian captains take three valleys. c. 1220: the Peace Not Sold.
8. **1225, the Poured Cup.** At Virelos Markellos (39, still sick) pours out a cup of water untasted before the altar and gives his claim to Nikandros: *"I drank. He did not."* The price is Nikandros's oath to return the drinkers' farms. Every emperor crowned at Virelos since pours out a cup. Nikandros keeps half the oath, evicting Sergian's companies violently, and cannot reach the Covenant-held districts (recovered in part in 1238–1246 by 09's Open Gates). Markellos dies a monk at Kedra in 1231.

### What was bent and why

- **Captivity, ransom, blinding, death of the wound** were Romanos IV's sequence. The emperor is now a guest with a bill; the court's crime is a land seizure from its own veterans by their own tenure law, plus a deposition; nobody is blinded; Markellos lives seventeen years more. The 1167 blinding is now the only imperial blinding in Thalmyric memory.
- **The 1215 doctrine is preserved** in a new form (bill instead of ransom; deposition instead of widowing by blinding), so the 09 writer's "no instrument since 1215" and the Open Gates stand.
- **The next line's origin** is a renunciation by the deposed emperor in favour of the man who read the curb, bought with a half-kept oath, and it leaves a living coronation rite. The "reserve commander who withdrew" is now the one who warned.
- **The court made it worse** is now concrete and land-based: the district law that won the War of the Southern Deeds turns on the districts.
- Markellos's epithet changes from "the Captive" to **"the Guest"**. Sergian becomes an emperor (1215–1217), not a regent.

### In-world disagreement and authorial truth

- Drinkers' songs: Markellos ordered them to drink. Kedravan chronicle: he drank last. Covenant: the terms were posted in the drinkers' own tongue.
- Drinkers' songs: the cup was poured freely. Sergianist chronicles: the Kedravan abbot held a sick man's wrist. *Authorial:* both partly true; the record does not rule.
- *Authorial:* the well-peace is lawful contract magic (consent by drinking after posted notice) enforced by a bound warden. The debt clause is genuine contract text but the warden enforced only the peace; the bill was enforced by courts and garrisons. The binding ended at sunset. The lingering water-sickness was the warden's breach-sickness and bound nobody: Sergian's claim that the water was still in them was false, and he knew his jurists could not prove it. Nikandros read correctly. Markellos's drinking was loyalty, not ignorance.
