## Summary

The missing hinge in this span is a matter of law. Signs in this world really work, but no sign carries its author's name, and Atherian law gave one interested body, the augur colleges, the sole right to say which signs existed. Every turning point from 0 to 411 AR becomes a fight over that right. The colleges' registers fall silent at the Binding, and the Great Purge is fought to recover them. Tervan's edict answers a real Mask. Cassian gains a sign that needs no register, which crown and bishops then dispute for eighty-five years. Astrion stages a counter-sign, and Vestian hands the authenticating power to the bishops. In 385 the Church finds that the Flame will not authenticate a compelled profession, and the Man-Bearer party grows from the congregations that knew how to read that silence.

The years 249–398 AR are re-sequenced. No beat keeps its analog's AD year, and 292 stays. The firm dates become **377** (Vestian's accession), **385** (compulsion) and **398** (his death). Crispus and Fausta, Jovian's forts, Procopius, 375–376, the Maximus war and Julian's last words are cut or bent. The genuine bends remain: no massacre of princes in 316, the Forty Flames inside a slave rising, and Vestian harsher on the Jirahar than his analog was on the Jews.

## Proposals

### 04.1 Why proof does not compel belief

- [x] Approved with changes — it answers "why 290 years?" without downgrading a single miracle; the doctrine lives on its own author-only page, and readers meet it as augurs' blank answer-books and a magistrate's fear of lamp cults, never as a statute lecture.

> **Decision notes (14 November 2026).** Doctrine placed in [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]] (its own page; [[White Flame Doctrine]] gains a cross-reference through the integration patch). Option A, the Three Silent Days, is adopted. The registers record only the colleges' petitions; the authorial truth is that every Menhir sovereignty held still, with no ruling on Idol Masks. Clause 6 follows Direction decision 2: Astaroth's structural answers to builders are standing practice, and he answered *Astrion's public test of legitimacy* once.

**What happens.** An author-only doctrine, *The Commission Rule and the Crowded Field*, is added to [[White Flame Doctrine]] and cited by every history article in this span.

1. **Grace acts by commission, not on demand.** The White Flame answers the righteousness and purpose of a person and the commission under which it acts. It cannot be summoned to a hearing or produced to save a defendant; [[The Fireless Trial]] is the type case. A manifestation proves its commission and nothing beyond it. Because the Flame demands sanctity and coherence (Commandment 7), it does not ratify a lie, including one spoken under a seal.
2. **The field is crowded.** A light on the dead, a fire that will not take or a lamp without oil has at least four possible authors:
   - the Flame;
   - Shell-fire from kiln wards, signal scripts or forge furnaces;
   - a Menhir's bounded omen, which genuinely answers within its law and price;
   - an Idol's Mask, whose counterfeit kindling feeds on the devotion it draws. Virella's two veils name this danger in the first generation, and Bruckmoor proves it later.

   A wonder shows that something acted, not what acted. [[Mesakha Traditions]] already requires signs that "cannot be staged by warlock script, contract, Shade, relic fraud, or Idol pressure".
3. **Law names signs.** From the Common Sky onwards the colleges kept the civic calendar and the register of answers ([[Causality Pass - 03 Age of Glory and the Bridge to 0 AR|Pass 03]] supplies the origin). In imperial law a sign existed only once a chartered college had entered it, with its terms, its witnesses and the name of the answering Star. A registered answer could open a campaign, void an election, or consecrate or condemn ground. An unregistered wonder was either fraud or an unlicensed cult feeding something.
4. **What a magistrate saw.** A cult that met at executed men's graves, kept lamps for them, reported lights on the dead and refused the colleges' examination matched, point by point, the profile the empire had learned from real Mask cults: the ruler-cult appetite, Qarzeth's Gluttony Mask, the grief cults of garrisons. Many persecuting magistrates were sincere sacred hygienists who had seen a Mask feed.
5. **Why witness carried the faith.** Proof could not compel belief: the believer cannot produce a sign on demand; the sceptic can always assign it to a rival author; the law gave the naming to an interested body; and belief is assent, which neither force nor calculation supplies. The faith spread by people, its signs as rumour among the already persuaded. Hence 290 years, and hence a self-authenticating sign (the Aurin at Velos, which anyone can watch fail to freeze) broke the monopoly when three centuries of testimony had not.
6. **The Stars keep the same rule.** Astaroth answers inside his bargain grammar (a field, a load, a price) and gives nothing to "victory without resistance". He answered the 350 test because it offered a load, and gave no terms for a second test, for Caleran's last augurs in 383 or for the eastern command in 384. The Church learned this rule as a defendant and forgot it as a ruler.

**Did augury go dark at 0 AR?** Permanent darkness is ruled out: it contradicts the 350 answer, the Menhir bargain canon and the Road-Keys of 1360.

- **Option A, recommended: the Three Silent Days.** No college register in the empire records an answer between Isfir's death and the third morning; Severan's is the best known only because he read at Qiryath. Augury resumes on the fourth day unchanged. Authorial truth, never stated in reader prose: *the fled powers held still while the Judge was among the dead* (Menhir fusion as flight from judgement). In-world the registers prove only that no answer came, and the colleges sealed the days.
- **Option B: only Severan's readings failed.** The Purge then needs another cause.

Severan never read again; his college recorded that he "laid down the staff", from grief, fear or faith.

**Why.** It answers the review's central question (why 290 years?) without downgrading any miracle, and gives the persecutors sincere motives.

**What it changes downstream.** Later conflicts change from "is it real?" to "who says so, and what may they hold because of it?": the relic courts, the Kindling Question, the Empty Charter's clause, the Grey Sweat's rite-integrity doctrine. Tyros gains something to carry (04.2) and Cassian a reason to keep the colleges (04.3).

**Analog bent or replaced.** Celsus's charge of sorcery becomes a statute with a competent court. The cessation of oracles (Plutarch, "Great Pan") becomes three dated register entries with a legal afterlife.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** **The Three Silent Days (0 AR — the cessation of oracles).** TORQUE: from the hour of the Binding to the third morning, no augural register in the empire records an answer. The colleges sealed the days. Tyros carried them into the open, and the Great Purge was fought to recover them. The registers prove a silence and nothing more. The bishops have held the originals since 383 and never published them.

### 04.2 The Great Purge, c. 36–45 AR

- [x] Approved with changes — the Purge finally has a cause and a victim list that makes sense; told as augurs riding into towns with paid informers and burning "forged registers" in the Forum, and the statute survives only as the Underground's "gag", not as a named law.

> **Decision notes.** "The Law of Registered Answers" is dropped as a reader-facing name: reader prose says "the commission's edict" and the Underground's slang "the gag" (which Velmion's muzzle literalises). "The Deep Register" is kept as an in-world name. The emperor stays unnamed. Aquilan was tried as a citizen as an accessory to the theft of sacred records and beheaded. Virella's lapsed-warrant death (47) is adopted.

**What happens.**

- **c. 1–30 AR.** Tyros, the Companion who had been an astrologer-priest, learns of the sealed days from former colleagues and takes them back to the colleges. His Dialogues never claim that the silence proves the Resurrection. They argue that the colleges have recorded something they refuse to discuss, and that he met the man. Junior augurs and register-keepers in several provincial colleges come over; these are canon's "whole priestly colleges". Proposed: one was the observatory college whose building became the Lantern Cradle. Its coloured lanterns continue the old register marks.
- **Why that is an emergency.** A college carries legal competence. A defected college could enter the Flame's signs in public law and read its registers aloud, the silent days included. By the 30s copies are travelling the Ember Underground.
- **c. 36.** The High Augurate at Caleran obtains a commission of inquiry. The court grants it for three reasons: the colleges' jurisdictional emergency, the Othran succession troubles that make the colleges' ceremonies indispensable, and Menos's risings in the Ironvale forges that supply its arms. Augurs staff it, pay informers from confiscations and charge defendants with forging or stealing sacred records, not with belief.
- **The victims.** Thalos at Virelos (36), for staging a funeral of the Star Pantheon. Naris at Halmenth (38), for public baptism. Dorn (40), who would not name the stations. Jareth (41), a former forger, burned in the Celestial Forum with a half-copied Gospel the commission calls a forged register. Erom at Velos (44). Menos (45), for the risings. Tyros and Aquilan (c. 45): Tyros's beheading closes the Purge.
- **Why it ends.** The defected colleges are dissolved, notables complain of the commission's extortion, and the court has its registers.
- **Virella.** The commissioner who began poisoning her at Sermora outlives his commission. She dies in 47 at the hands of a man acting on his own. Tyros therefore remains the last Companion of the Purge, which resolves an Appendix A conflict, and her Life records the first martyrdom by a persecutor whose warrant had lapsed.

**Why.** The Purge currently has no cause. This one grows from 04.1 and from Tyros's canon defection, and explains why it fell on witnesses and scribes rather than on crowds.

**What it changes downstream.**

1. **A statute, *the Law of Registered Answers* (proposed name).** Publishing an unregistered sign becomes a crime; for 250 years a congregation that wrote down a miracle wrote its own indictment.
2. **The Underground records signs as practical judgement or hymn.** The Ashway coal test and the Lantern Cradle lamps survive so. The downgrading the review found is an in-world survival practice, and no one can now tell which entries were miracles.
3. **The Deep Register at Caleran** (proposed name) holds the recovered registers. It passes to the bishops in 383 and ends in the Sanctum's deepest registry beside the 350 record.
4. **Method enters the Church.** Defected augurs' terms, witnesses and registers, joined to Virella's "test the fire before you warm yourself", become the Church's testing of signs: the root of the relic courts.
5. **The Four Nails reach the Pillar** (04.8).

**Analog bent or replaced.** Nero's scapegoating and Domitian's purge become a jurisdictional war over sacred records. There is no fire of Caleran and no single tyrant.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** **The Great Purge (c. 36–45 AR — the first-century persecutions).** TORQUE: a records war. The High Augurate recovered the registers carried off by the colleges that followed Tyros and broke the Redeemer cult's capacity to authenticate signs. Its statute made an unregistered wonder a crime, which taught the Underground to write miracles as common sense. That is why the faith's earliest records understate its own signs.

**a. The Kept Lamps.** In the arrears after the Vashar war, an Envy Mask spreads through unpaid eastern garrisons, promising that comrades who died unpaid are "kept" by lamps over their graves. The lamps lean towards mourners and burn without oil; the Mask feeds on the grievance, and the mutinies draw heat from it. Augurs break its central image c. 243–244. Tervan's 245 edict therefore demands a witnessed offering and a licence for every household's graves and lamps, and the Redeemer burial fraternities fall under the licence. After 248 the reconciliation dispute turns on licensed lamps; most congregations relight from a flame never licensed. Amaryah's keepers had hidden her lamp in a covered firepot in a neighbour's oven, so theirs becomes the flame everyone seeks, and the daughter-flame custom spreads from that winter (proposed detail).

**b. 288–291.** The capital's augurs void the frontier command's omens; Cassian has few augurs of his own. The capital's seizures aim at the congregations' books of signs, treated as counter-augury that also carries dispatches. A commander with no register listens when Ironvale's guards talk (04.5).

**c. 292: the first sign the crown owns.** The river at the bridge never freezes again, and anyone can verify it every winter. River and bridge are crown property, but the law still knows only registered answers, so Cassian has the Caleran augurs enter the reach as a sign answered to the emperor. **This is why he keeps the colleges:** they are the machinery that makes his miracle official.

**d. Crown against bishops, 292–377.**

- **292–296.** The four sees refuse to commemorate an augural entry that calls the Flame an "answer". The Velos congregation, keeper of Erom's chains, claims the sign for its martyr's city.
- **c. 296, the Velos Concord.** The crown builds a chapel at the bridge, appoints a **Warden of the Reach** (proposed) to keep the winter ice-line register, and pays the clergy who accept him. In return, restitution is decreed empire-wide. The bishops trade the miracle for property; Caleran signs first, Halmenth last.
- **316 (option).** Dying, Cassian is carried to Velos and baptised in the unfrozen water by the crown-paid chaplain, since no bishop of the four sees would do it there. The Wager then asks two questions: did a deathbed absolution answer his reign, and can the crown's river baptise?
- **316–350.** Sevrin refuses every petition to transfer the wardenship. Astrion has the augurs re-enter the reach as a Star answer (proposed: Hecatara, of crossings), then stages the 350 test to give the Stars a sign of equal standing.
- **352–377.** Corlan returns the wardenship to crown officers. Lucar sells the chapel's revenue to bishops who accept his terms for army chaplains.
- **377.** Vestian gives the bishops the register and the chapel as the price of Caleran's recognition. River and bridge stay crown property, so it is crown water under a Church register, and the Torque Register's "it continues" stands.

**e. Cassian's household, c. 309.** Because the crown's own sign stands in an augural register, the heir asks the High Augur of Astaroth for an answer on the succession; an entry naming him would be the strongest title in the empire. Consulting on the emperor's life is a capital offence. The entry is sealed, and Cassian executes his son and the High Augur; the empress lives. From then on a crown registrar seals every answer touching the crown, a precedent Vestian extends in 383, and Sevrin inherits a register he refuses to open.

**f. Astrion and the east.** Astrion carries the signed 350 record in his baggage. The field record gives no last words; it notes only that he asked whether the baggage with the register had crossed. Preachers read this as an apostate clinging to a Star answer; the eastern army reads it as fidelity.

- **351.** **Pellan** (proposed name), Adran's younger son and Astrion's brother, brings water trains along the eastern depot road under the bronze standard of the 350 test, now the **Answered Standard** (proposed name). The column survives. Savel Orren, acclaimed in the field, abdicates at the depot line in 352 when Caleran declares for Corlan.
- **c. 357.** Pellan recognises Corlan. Corlan confirms the eastern command's revenue share on Sevrin's 316 terms, its augurs and the Standard. Eastern units take a Star oath; western units take Cassian's service oath.

In thirty-three years Astaroth never answers the east a second time, and its augurs say so honestly; that is why Pellan never claims the throne. He remains a real rival: a paid command, a sign he can show, the Vasharan frontier, and soldiers whose fathers he saved.

**g. The south.** In 369–371 hard winters and Grobi raiding drive Folk households to the fort line. Lucar admits them (371–372); his officers divert grain and sell dependants, and his chaplains confiscate household tokens and bargain-objects as unlicensed sign-objects. War runs 372–377. *Optional torque:* the Kellovar ford belongs to a river-court whose toll is paid in those tokens. Lucar's army crosses unpaid, while the Folk who kept their tokens cross dry. The river exacts without judging, and the defeat proves only the ford's law.

**h. Vestian: one civil war.**

- **383, the Dissolution at Caleran** (scene in Notes). By statute **the authentication of signs passes to the bishops of recognised communion**, and the Deep Register goes with it.
- **383–384.** Pellan refuses to dissolve the eastern augurs. Vestian wins with port grain and the sees' networks. At the decisive crossing the eastern augurs ask the Red Star for terms and get none, the Standard goes into the field unanswered, and the eastern Redeemer units negotiate. The Standard and the 350 record go to Caleran; the record goes on to Aurelium.
- **385, the Profession.** The east's peace oath becomes imperial law (04.4).

Vestian won his war because the Stars would not answer his enemy, and then the Flame would not answer his law.

**Why.** Unsynchronised dates alone would be cosmetic; the causes have to change. The Velos dispute gives 292–377 a single through-line in place of seven imitated reigns.

**What it changes downstream.**

- The bishops enter the Long Frontier as the legal court of signs, which is the Guttering's temptation.
- Eastern veterans remember a Star that answered once, the source of the Stars' survival "in military customs".
- The confiscated tokens leave the southern Folk a property wound.
- Velos gains a recurring ceremony and a standing dispute.

**Analog bent or replaced.** See the table. In short, Milan becomes a trade of property for a miracle, Jovian's treaty a rescue and Procopius an accord. The Frigidus moves before compulsion and is decided by a refused answer, not a wind, and Thessalonica follows a war instead of preceding one.

**Draft Torque Register entries.**

- **The Kept Lamps (245 AR — Decius).** TORQUE: the edict answered a real Mask. Its lamp licences caught the burial fraternities. The congregations relit from flames never licensed, and the daughter-flame custom that carried Amaryah's lamp to Caleran began there.
- **Cassian's Vision**, add: the augurs entered the sign in the crown's name; from c. 296 a Warden of the Reach kept its winter register, in exchange for restitution. In 377 Vestian gave the register and chapel to the bishops and kept the water.
- **The Two Standards (351–384 AR — Valens, Procopius, the Frigidus, bent).** TORQUE: Astrion's single answer outlived him as an army. For thirty-three years the command that watered his column kept augurs and the Answered Standard, though no second answer came. In 384 it asked the Red Star for terms and got none, and its own Redeemer units negotiated.
- **The Dissolution at Caleran (383 AR).** TORQUE: by statute the authentication of signs passed from augurs to bishops, along with the Deep Register and the three silent days. *(Optional: **the Kellovar Ford (377 AR — Adrianople)**: the army that had confiscated the crossing tokens crossed without paying.)*

### 04.4 Compelled professions fail to kindle: the Second Silence

- [x] Approved — the strongest world-native beat in the span: a decree followed by a consequence only this cosmology could produce, seen as thousands of yellow lamps in town squares.

> **Decision notes.** "Second Silence" is the gateway-port congregations' name; the churches say "the yellow lamps" or "the unkindled professions". Aurelium's sealed register survives incomplete in the Sanctum.

**What happens.**

- **Before 385.** Since Solana's coal, many congregations keep a reception lamp, lit by the newly baptised from the assembly's flame. Now and then it takes a white, unconsuming fire before the assembly. This **kindling** is irregular and never owed; the Underground wrote "the lamp took well". Velos and the kindlings at public baptisms under Cassian led the court to expect the Flame to confirm a Redeemer empire.
- **385, the Profession.** Every officeholder, then every household head in the directly governed provinces, professes before recognised clergy and lights a reception lamp from a church flame. The chancery advertises the ceremonies as the Flame's confirmation of the realm.
- **385–c. 388.** At state professions lamps by the thousand burn yellow, while parish baptisms of the willing go on kindling at the old uneven rate. The Flame has not left the Church; it does not answer the compulsion. The court cites a handful of state kindlings; the confessors note that most fell to people who had sought baptism before the decree.
- **The court's explanation** is that hidden rites pollute the households: Star images in cupboards, "Jirahar blindness" in descendants. That produces the ban on household rites (c. 387) and faster tower orders, a world-native cause for the escalation.
- **c. 389, the lamp leaves the oath.** State professions switch to a sealed written oath, and the silence can no longer be observed.

**Does the Church record it?**

- **Aurelium** compiles a register of the unkindled professions, district by district, and seals it.
- **Caleran** denies that a valid profession needs a sign; this is the first statement of protocol theology.
- **Halmenth** refuses the lamp at any profession made under compulsion.
- **The gateway ports** say it aloud (04.6).

Recommended ruling: the Church recorded the Silence and sealed it. As a body it did not lie; it hid.

### 04.6 Roots of the Man-Bearer party

- [x] Approved — it makes Halmenth an effect of the era's own wound instead of a borrowed council, and gives Nasriel a burned house to be absent from.

**What happens.** Since the first Qiryath agreement some Redeemer congregations, strongest in Sermora's harbours and the gateway ports (Qabir among them), have been largely of Jirahar descent. They keep maternal registers beside their baptism rolls, and several pray in the Jirahar dialect. Under Vestian three things fall on them at once.

1. **Their records make them findable.** Through tribute rolls, Astrion's survey petitions and the maternal registers, tower-order officers reach baptised families. Governors treat descent as evidence even against the baptised, as the Vestian draft already says.
2. **They name the Second Silence.** Their families had argued over a silence for 385 years; Jirahar schools read the Silence of Acceptance as defilement, hidden judgement or a test. 3. **They take their depositions to a Jirahar court.** When the bishops' courts refuse testimony on the unkindled professions, copies are lodged at the Jirahar hearing house at Raveth, a court practised in recording silences. ### 04.7 "Before the Mesakha" as Vestian's clause

- [x] Approved — one man's pen-stroke carries a live dispute into 1360; the only law in the world a sign can repeal.

> **Decision notes.** Copies at the gate-house and in the Caleran chancery agree; the original's custody is disputed in-world and left open.

**What happens.** The chancery draft of the Empty Charter (c. 396) forbids habitation and rebuilding "in perpetuity". Vestian strikes the phrase out and writes *before the Mesakha* in his own hand, using the Jirahar word. His motives:

- the Second Silence has persuaded him that the Return is near, and he wants the city kept empty for the Redeemer's coming back to the place where he rose;
- any successor can revoke a perpetual prohibition, but a conditional one binds until the condition is met;
- the Jirahar word is a sneer and a creed at once: their Awaited One had come, and when he came again the city would be his.

Caleran's canonists after 398 read *never*: the Mesakha has come, and his only future coming ends history. The Jirahar custodians read the condition of return in their persecutor's hand, and say it aloud at the gate stair. Jirahar jurisprudence glosses the clause as "before **recognition** of the Mesakha", the wording [[Mesakha Traditions]] already uses. The charter never says who recognises him. Since 383 the Church has held the authenticating power, and the Keepers hold the Mesakha tests.

**Why.** It turns an inherited phrase into an act with a motive, and carries the hinge into the present.

**What it changes downstream.** The warmth beneath the Silent Gate stair in 1360 is a legal emergency as well as a theological one. A recognised sign would lift the ban on rebuilding, and two authorities claim the right to recognise it. Hence the Eclessian call for an investigation, and the custodians' fear of "an attempt to void the charter".

**Analog bent or replaced.** There is no Theodosian analog; the clause is world-native.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** **The Empty Charter (c. 396 AR).** TORQUE: Vestian struck out "in perpetuity" and wrote "before the Mesakha". The Church reads never, and the Jirahar read a promise in their persecutor's hand. The charter does not say who recognises the Mesakha. It is the only law in the world that a sign can repeal.

**Articles to update on approval.** In `01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Jirahar/`: `60 Realms and Peoples/Qiryath Reqa.md` (clause and readings), `20 Faith and Worldview/Mesakha Traditions.md` (origin), `10 Foundations/Jirahar Canon Register.md` (Locked History 377–398, charter c. 396), `70 History and Conflicts/Vestian Persecutions and the Long Diaspora.md`.

**Open questions.** Does the original in Vestian's hand survive, and in whose custody? Has an Eclessian court ever claimed jurisdiction over the condition?
