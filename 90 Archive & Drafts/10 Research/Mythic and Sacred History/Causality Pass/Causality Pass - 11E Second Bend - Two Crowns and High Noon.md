# Causality Pass — 11E Second Bend: Two Crowns and High Noon

> [!note] Record of a second bend, 14 November 2026
> An adversarial historian scored the 10E versions of Two Crowns at 7/10 and High Noon at 5/10 for predictability: the mechanisms were native, but the skeletons of Iconoclasm, the Carolingians, the Saxon Wars, 1054, Jogaila and Manzikert survived. This pass changes who lives, who wins, who never arrives, and the order and number of events and reigns (Rules 6–8 of [[Real-History Concordance#Standing Recontextualization Rules]]). It supersedes [[Causality Pass - 10E Bending the Breakings and Qarath]] and [[Causality Pass - 08 Two Crowns and High Noon, 962 to 1232 AR]] wherever they differ. The owned reader articles are rewritten; shared files are untouched and their final wording is in [[#Integration patch]]. No person has been renamed; see [[#Rename proposals]]. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## New sequences

### Thalmyria, 962–1076

1. **962–979.** Caedros II re-endows the Church; lamps gutter (kept).
2. **979–984, Ilarion.** He sells the sees. **982, Anavra:** riders from the amirate of Meliton, devil-mantled and name-bound, take the town when the chaplain's war-lamp stays dark; some three hundred are carried off. The lamp-sergeant **Varkas** leads the survivors to break the lamp on the bishop's doorstep: "It did not burn for us. Show us one that does." The **Breakers** spread. **984:** the Vlastravan march army, sent to protect the sees, breaks its own war-lamps; Leovar breaks his first; Ilarion dies of a lung-fever (contingency); the army acclaims **Leovar III**.
3. **986, the hood.** The Breakers come for the Great Lamp, shrunk to a bead under court-appointed keepers. Leovar had been acclaimed under it. His wife Anthe has a hood cast from her father's clergy's broken lamps; he tells the Breakers the emperor has put out its eye himself.
4. **The test turns on everyone.** The Flame does not come for the asking, so true flame-monks fail too and are driven out; household lamps are smashed as painted fire; lamps go into wells and ovens. The Reconquest slows; the march without mourners (kept). Covenant jurists let it run.
5. **Ioan of Velia** at Keramion: forty sayings on lamp-feet; kilns pulled down twice; d. c. 1024.
6. **1009, Sarmene.** The hospice lamp kindles for the monk Makarios as he stands between a Breaker band and forty sick. The Breakers follow him; Leovar makes him Patriarch. He teaches that he passed no test. **1012:** the Synod receives Ioan's sayings. The Breaking fades into the 1020s with no end date.
7. **1011.** Leovar III asks to see under the hood; Makarios refuses; he dies in an unlit room. **Leovar IV** (35), who carried a hidden lamp that never kindled.
8. **1012–1029, the lamp-houses as refuge.** Households may leave lamps under the crown's seal for a clay token; Kallion is Master of Lamps.
9. **1015, the nails** (see below).
10. **1029, Meliton** (kept): Kallion burns; the refuge becomes compulsory: the **Impounding** (monks flogged and killed; kept). The term "Second Breaking" is retired.
11. **1046–1048, the dry years** (moved from 1008–1011): well-lights lifted. **1048, Dryma:** a family with a lifted lamp asks the district lamp-house for the grandmother's lamp; the village breaks the seal; the house is cold. **1049, Veridun:** a thousand lifted lamps in the square; **Arsen** breaks the seal himself; Leovar IV (73) finds his saddle-bag lamp cold on the first shelf; keepers hanged, honest with thieves (kept).
12. **1049–1052.** Families walk to the Ember and are robbed and killed on the amirate roads (kept). **Theodosia** (24) walks with them, returns with a lit lamp and a list of the dead, and is given the southern armies. **1052:** last amirate; the Rekindling; Leovar IV (76) walks the first mile barefoot; Makarios draws his nails and lifts the hood to light the Great Lamp, and it is burning.
13. **1055.** Leovar IV dies. **Theodosia the Kindler** reigns 1055–1076.

### The east, 974–1104

1. **974–975.** Kaltfurt; Adalmar king (kept).
2. **975–994.** Register grants; **987** Weylan's baptism; **985–994** the Folk Wars; **993** the Reciting and the Drowning; **994** the Standing of the Water; **999** the Peace of the Bent Oak, a judgement of the bishops' court against their king.
3. **1006.** Adalmar, called the Drowner, asks for an examination. Silvane II's court credits the banner to him; Hedwen testifies; Silvane will not crown. Hedwen d. c. 1010, Silvane d. 1012.
4. **1013.** Bonifac III's chancery strikes the testimony through.
5. **1015, the Split.** Bonifac's offer: lift the hood and be examined, and Leovar IV is the emperor who proves lawful; refuse, and the trust passes to Adalmar. Leovar IV is afraid to know; Makarios will allow no test. Four nails; anathemas; the Bells.
6. **1017.** Coronation at Kaltfurt ford; the Crown of Embers (a new circlet set with red glass coals); the mud. Adalmar d. **1019**.
7. **1019–1050, Ludovar the Mild.** Silent; never crowned. The crown lies in the royal chapel at Aurelium. **1027–1041** the banner wars of his sons and lords; **c. 1040** Grauwacht (kept whole).
8. **1041, Drossfeld.** The blank lot; the truce line; the smudge. Proverb: *a Drossfeld lot*.
9. **1047.** Lotharic killed at the Orzva fords. **1047–1049** Gervin's and Aldwin's men fight over the smudged line (this is the war whose wounded the Pelin's Bridge lamp shelters c. 1048). **1050** Ludovar dies in Aldwin's house.
10. **1059–1064.** Strazgorod as in 10E to the kindling; then the Sanctum's altar-fire and the moot of sabres.
11. **1068–1102** the lintels (kept). **1101–1103** the Two Winters (kept, unexplained). **1102** Ermbold and the grain cart. **1103** the Apology; Virelia's first contested election. **1104** the trust.

### High Noon, 1121–1231

1. **1121–1159, Caedros III.** As 10E, except: Chrysanthe d. 1155; in debt by the 1150s; d. **1159**.
2. **1159, the Unanswered Funeral.** The warlock is walked out; the court acclaims Orestan on the flare; Despina's lamp kindles (kept). **1159–1162, the War of the Seven Lamps.** Orestan beaten under Kolonna; Andrion acclaimed; Despina: "let him keep a grave"; Orestan keeps the chapel at Vresna c. 1162–1182.
3. **1162–1198 Andrion** (the Threshing Floor, 1171–1173, still falls in his reign; untouched). **1198–1209** his son.
4. **c. 1201** the Keys, as above.
5. **1209–1225, Markellos.** 1212 Unburdened Deeds; **1214 Qarath** to the dawn rout as before. Sunset of the second day: *a well for every cup*. Each drinker owes the well of his own holding and keeps the water-sickness until he gives it. On Southern House land the custodians collect farm by farm with the bronze water-token families. The Throne's cup is held to be owed the Throne's well: the aqueduct head at Kolonna.
6. **Winter 1214.** The **Forfeiture of the Drinkers**, under the emperor's seal in Sergian's hand: a man who owes his well to the enemy holds his farm for the enemy.
7. **1215.** Markellos leaves the palace for Kedra. Sergian tells Iqashar the cup was the man's. The Court of Breaches: the Throne drank; it does not die, and it did not leave the palace when the man did. Garrisons take the aqueduct head and the districts. The 1215 rule stands.
8. **1215–1225, the War of the Seal.** 1217 Sergian killed in the Crown Hall (kept); the three valleys (kept); the Peace Not Sold and the Poor Engineer (kept).
9. **1225, the Poured Cup** (kept): now also Markellos's abdication after sixteen years as emperor. The abbot's hand on his wrist has a plain cause.
10. **1227, the Refusal of the Lamp:** the Sanctum's unasked offer of the Crown of Embers.
11. **1231.** Markellos dies at Kedra, the debt unpaid.

**Clock check.** No date sits on an analogue year (726, 730, 754, 787, 815, 843; 768, 772, 782, 785, 800, 804, 814, 840, 843, 887; 988; 1025, 1054, 1071, 1072, 1081, 1095; 1386). Intervals against the sources:

- *Iconoclasm* (61, 28, 28 years between its four turns): one arc, 982 → 1052, with no restoration in the middle.
- *Charlemagne to Charles the Fat* (king → massacre 14; → coronation 18; → death 14; → son's death 26; → partition 3; → deposition 44): 18; 24; 2; 31; the lot falls nine years *before* the father's death; 61 to a surrender that is no deposition. The coronation now follows the schism instead of preceding it by two centuries.
- *Saxon Wars* (32 years, massacre in year 10, baptism 3 years after): 9 years, massacre in year 8, baptism 6 years before.
- *Basil II to the Komnenoi* (reign 49; death → Manzikert 46; → new line 10): 38; 55; 11.

**Residual, declared.** Because 993 and 1104 are fixed and Adalmar's reign must straddle 993 and 1015, his acclamation (975) and death (1019) still sit about 205 years after Charlemagne's. The intervals and the order are broken; the two end-dates are not. Moving them means moving Kaltfurt (974). 1214 → 1225 stays at eleven years (anchors).

## In-world disagreements

- Whether Varkas asked an honest question or taught a country to test God.
- Whether Leovar III hooded the Great Lamp to save it or to hide that it was dead; whether it burned untended for sixty-six years (monasteries) or through a vent the keepers fed (sceptics); whether it was relit from the Ember in 1052 (Synod) or lit the runner's taper (cathedral keepers).
- Whether Makarios's lamp passed the Breakers' test (the Breakers) or answered for the sick behind him (Makarios).
- Whether Bonifac's offer of 1015 was generous or a trap; whether the Kaltfurt millers shut their sluices or the autumn was dry.
- Whether the legate felt for the blank at Drossfeld.
- Whether Weylan led four thousand to the water or made the drowner's law fence the lake.
- Whether the well-lights burned in the wells or were lit on the ladder; whether the Fire left what was owned or the oil ran out.
- Whether the Kolonna Declaration saved the empire from Orestan or drove him to script-fire; whether Vresna was penance or prison.
- Whether Suhrab entered Qarath in his *Ledger*; whether the cup was poured freely.

## Authorial truth (author-only)

- The Flame answers the righteousness and purpose of a person. It did not answer the chaplain at Anavra (office), any Breaker's demand (a test), Kallion (force), or any Adalring coronation banner (office). It answered Makarios at Sarmene for the forty sick, as it answered Hedwen for the column. The Breakers were right that the bought lamps were empty and wrong that a lamp can be made to prove it. Makarios's "there is no test" is half the truth; Aurelium's "a fire nobody may examine shelters forgers" is the other half.
- Whether the Great Lamp burned untended under the hood stays open, as in 08 and 10E.
- The cold houses were theft and neglect. No supernatural withdrawal.
- Bonifac's chancery struck the testimony through on his order. Silvane's refusal was honest.
- The blank at Drossfeld: not ruled. The legate could have felt an uncut tablet.
- What the lake did in 994 and what the Lakekeepers pay stays unspecified. The Drowning's cause changes (archive murder at a betrayed recital); its nature as a mass soul-route atrocity at a sacred site, and so the thin space, does not.
- Qarath: the well-peace and the price are lawful contract magic (consent by drinking after posted notice in the drinker's tongue), executed by the well's bound warden. The warden wrote the price and holds each breaker in breach-sickness until the price it named is given; it is bound to the instrument, so neither Suhrab nor the Court can lower or remit it. The sickness lifts when the well is given, and binds nothing else: Sergian's claim that a drinker "holds his farm for the enemy" was false. The Throne's-well ruling is the Court's reading, not the warden's; Markellos stayed sick because he never gave a well at all. Daily drinking from a surrendered well creates no new peace: only the sworn well at Qarath has a warden.
- Despina's lamp, Kolonna and Zdena's lamp remain genuine (08, 10E).
