# Causality Pass — 11D Second Bend: The Covenant's Rise and the Fall

Record of the second bend applied on 14 November 2026 under [[Causality Pass - Direction]] and Rules 6–8 of [[Real-History Concordance#Standing Recontextualization Rules]]. The first bend ([[Causality Pass - 10D Bending the Long Frontier and the Covenant]]) made the mechanisms native. An adversarial historian still scored the Covenant side of the Long Frontier at 5.5/10 and the Fall and Reconquest at 4/10 for predictability, because the skeleton survived: the same people met the same fates in the same order. This pass changes who lives, who wins and who never arrives, and breaks the clock. Reader articles owned by the pass were rewritten in place. [[#Integration patch]] gives exact wording for every file the pass may not edit. Authorial truth sits in [[The Fourth Seal - Authorial Truth]], [[The Guttering - Authorial Truth]] and [[The Covenant after Caleran - Authorial Truth]].

The Locked Rulings are kept: Mujalib's revelation in 541, the Witnessed-Seal and Sealed-Blood divide, the unresolved Fourth Seal, the Creditor behind Malak al-Ahd (author-only), the Nine Wells seized in 558, Qazmarid, Zanthir, the Velian States and the 1360 board. Nobody was renamed.

## The new sequences

### The Covenant's rise, 514–612

1. **514–523, the Toll War and the two awards.** Each crown voids the other's well-awards in the dry country and grants the same wells again. Neither withdraws a grant at the peace.
2. **541, the revelation** (fixed), beside a well with two masters. **541–549, the kept wells:** Mujalib seals the shares of some sixty disputed wells on clay tablets, and something keeps them.
3. **546–552, the Thin Floods; c. 549, the Bread of the Bound.** The 4,444 dig Al-Qahur; bread goes to sealed names. Every name on the roll is owed bread in a failed year.
4. **555–560, the end of Vashar** (unchanged). **558, the Nine Wells** (fixed): taken by the guard of the oasis wells under its young captain, Uthar ibn Qays.
5. **563, the Three Seals.** Mujalib takes no title and gives his authority away: Water to Uthar and the guard of the wells, Names to the college of scribes, the Roads to the marshal Qatada. He lives another thirty-three years beside the first channel.
6. **567–568.** A thin flood shows the roll larger than the Basin's bread. The bound carriers lift only what is given, so breach-courts go north under Qatada's escort to make grain a gift. Terms: the roll, the Given, a bound keeper at the town well.
7. **575, the Dry Ford.** The Atherian field army wins and dies at the wells.
8. **587, Leovar I; 594–598, the crusade of seals** (unchanged).
9. **596, Mujalib dies. 597,** two instruments: the college's Fourth Seal names Uthar, who becomes the first caliph; Ruqaiya's Counter-Instrument names Husan, who takes the desert.
10. **598, Qatada halts the northern army** until the seals can be tried. The Banu Mizan go on serving the Velian road in Uthar's name (601 Zanthir; c. 610 the Velian compacts, when the Banu Mizan take the keepers off the belt's wells and measure water by the hour-cup).
11. **603, Bir Qassim** (below). **604,** Qatada dies on the road back; the Seal of the Roads passes to the college. **609,** Uthar dies.
12. **611, Tamsar; 612, the Suspension** (unchanged). One more reason for the halt: the 4,444 are a finite number and by about 610 every one is placed.

### Bir Qassim, 603

Fifty-eight are named in Qatada's safe conduct, sealed by all three Rightly-Sealed offices with the fourth space empty. A flood at Bir Nadim forces a six-mile detour. The keeper draws by the road measure for the escort and the unnamed drovers and not for the named. The drovers give the children their camels' measure. On the fourth day Jarud ibn Sulayt's court at Qafra rules the protection void and its bailiff stops the soldiers' skins. Twelve are dead by the sixth evening. That night Husan reads the instrument at the well-mouth, and the bucket comes up dry after every clause. At dawn the escort, Qatada's veterans, refuses to take the travellers' arms and carries thirty-seven west on the drovers' camels. Ruqaiya, too weak to ride, orders her son to go and stays with eight others and fifteen drovers. Qatada receives the caliph's order to arrest his men, answers nothing and rides after them to the strait. A column of the guard of the wells reaches the well four days later, finds Ruqaiya and three more dead, and kills the other five as rebels and fourteen drovers as abettors; one camel-boy escapes. Sixteen dead of thirst, nineteen killed: the Witnessed registers count five, the mourning houses thirty-five. Husan lives veiled at Qazmarid to 631, and the Wasiyin veil after him.

**What the Mourning mourns.** The Prophet's daughter refused water by her father's bound servant; the others who died of thirst; and the drovers killed for the one righteous act done at that well, whose names the mourning houses spent a generation recovering. *And yet the well was dry* survives unchanged. New custom: the drovers' measure (a filled skin set out for strangers; animals watered last).

### The Atherian successions

- **447.** Vestian's son dies after forty-nine years. His only son is an abbot, tonsured at eleven by the regency's bishops, and will not reign. The succession war of 447–452 follows as before.
- **709, the Fever Week.** The fever kills Leovar II in nine days and leaves his son too weak to ride the marches within the year and a day. Corvenas, the sick emperor's brother-in-law, rides them in his place, and the marches keep their oaths with the man who knelt. The "Caleran claimant" of 709–713 is the lawful emperor.

### Qahir's step, 878–885

Qahir (b. c. 848) commands the northern horse that saves the Velian belt. Nobody can pay it. He takes the Second Crusade's nine thousand unransomed prisoners instead, enters them under his own name at Iqashar, sets them to cut the cisterns and furnace pits of the Chain Furnace yard and hires them to the Basin's canal councils. By 881 his is the only army in the Covenant paid to the day, and the caliph at Al-Qahur gives him the Seal of the Roads, unheld since Qatada, and the new title of Sultan. The ruling of 884 also forfeits the prisoners' names. Idris is the first styled Sultan-Caliph.

### The Fall, 902–908

Idris is thirty-one, sonless and kept at Iqashar. Barzin and the breach-court train split Caleran. The engines come to the Aurin dyke; the Depth-Core frames wade the flood. Corvenas III's public call fails on the dyke. The dyke breaks at dusk and the poor quarters, which held no charter and were offered no terms, drown. The Instrument of the Ash Hall, the captivity and the death in 908 stand from 10D.

### The Veil, 906

As 10D, with three changes: the smoke lies for the five days the bread lasts; the court can serve nothing because it can find no gate or witness; and Veridun calls it the Night of the Ovens.

### Caelmount, 917–918: the Breath of Askre

Mount Askre breathes a heavy dead air into the valley's lava-gallery granaries, which is why they keep grain. Hereditary cave-wardens manage it with stone breathing-doors and a lamp held low. Qazem's engineers test the ash road under the Red Star, shore the weak span and cross safely. The wardens shut every breathing-door the night before the column arrives. Some six hundred men and every name-custodian die the first night. The rest winter beside grain they cannot reach; their levies, whose rolls lie with the dead, desert to the amnesty; the Surrender engine calls terms to an empty valley. Qazem dies; a few hundred surrender in spring 918. About eleven hundred dead are drawn out, and the valley walls up the lower galleries (the Sleepers) and never stores bread there again. Caedros promises to feed the valley until it has granaries, takes Virelos in 921 and is crowned there in 922.

### The heir who would not hold, 929–938

In 929 Ruzbeh ibn Idris, fourteen, is brought to the Chain Furnace yard to take up his father's instruments. He has been taught by a jurist of the people of the Clause. At the second feeding-plate he asks what the frame holds, is told that it holds a condemned man, and says he will hold nothing that was not given. Idris spends two years trying to change his mind, sets a muster for spring 932 to write in Mahdar (b. 923), and dies of fever in autumn 931. The instruments name a dead man and a boy who refused: widowed. In 932 Basin captains try to force Ruzbeh to take them up; the Regency lodges his name, the first lodging. He lives thirty years in the walled garden at Al-Qahur.

### Caleran 957 and the captive's releases

The funeral revolt, the keepers at the lamp and the eleven dead stand from 10D. New: the dockers burn Durayd's galleys at the mole; he holds the harbour fort for nineteen days and is taken alive; the keepers' terms leave him outside the protection as the thief of a deposit. Caedros II keeps him at Veridun until about 971 and has him sign the releases of the garrisons sworn to him by name. An amir who takes a release and terms opens his gates lawfully; one who refuses swears his garrison to himself. The canal heads take most of the rest.

### Zanthir's seasons, 1044

Qabir has two doors: the sea in the sailing season, along a coast Thalmyria holds by the 1040s, and the Basin caravan road in the cool season. In 1044 Thalmyric squadrons close the coast to the Zanthiri grain barges feeding the last amirates. The emir withdraws the barges, the amirates starve, and he seals an instrument valid while the sea is open: Veridun is paid in the sailing season, Iqashar in the caravan season. "Timelier treachery" is the Thalmyric word for it.
