# Causality Pass — 06 The Covenant after Caleran, 905 to 1190 AR

> [!note] Proposal for approval
> Nothing here is canon until approved. Tick an item's box to approve it; strike it through or annotate it to reject or change it. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## Summary

Between 905 and 1190 the Covenant exists only as the party Thalmyria defeats. This file gives it a clock of its own: a sovereign line, five pressures that run through the period, and turning points produced by its own institutions. Those institutions are contract law, name custody, a single engine foundry and a horse frontier it never expected.

**The spine.** Idris's instruments named him and his heir; the heir drowned and Idris died before re-swearing, widowing them all (06.1). The occupied south, re-sworn to its own governor, fell in 957 while Grass-Tarkhan riders on blessed Atherian horses pinned Iqashar's army in the north (06.2–06.3). Thalmyria then fought separately sworn districts while Iqashar lost its Vault to a creditor and audited the south away (06.4). The classical age was one of custody, not conquest, and its one war was a lawsuit fought with engines; Suhrab reached the throne because a court voided his renunciation of it (06.5).

**Canon tensions resolved or flagged.**
1. The Vault and the Fourth Seal lie at Al-Qahur, yet the Custodian serves at Iqashar (06.5).
2. No Surrender Engine stood among the five great engines at the Fall, though Qahir standardised three lines (06.1).
3. Qahir had several foundries; 1360 has one (06.1).
4. Zanthir's "timelier treachery" is never explained (06.4).
5. The Banu Mizan dynasty ends c. 885, yet "Banu Mizan territory" persists after 987 (06.4).
6. The Cull of Brothers, and Suhrab's execution of his son "on the Guard's evidence", remain near-verbatim Ottoman beats. They are flagged, not fixed.

### 06.1 The sovereign spine and the Widowed Seals

**What happens.**

| Reign | Sovereign | Why the reign matters |

| 931–979 | **Mahdar ibn Idris** *(proposed name)* | eight at accession; the Regency of the Bench to 940; refuses to arm the south |

*The Widowed Seals, 929–938.* Qahir was a new man (see [[#Interfaces]]). He bound his levies, foundry masters and engine crews to himself by name, with Idris written into each instrument as successor-holder, so 902 passed without dispute. Idris did the same, naming his eldest son, and the surrender instruments served on the southern cities in 905–906 named Idris in the same way.

In 929 the boy drowned in a palace cistern. Re-swearing meant musters, witnesses and fees across a season's march of country. Idris set it for the spring of 932 and died in the autumn of 931. Jurists called an instrument whose holder and successor-holder were both dead **widowed**. Whether it still bound was open, and every interested party answered for itself.

- **The north.** Levy commanders refused to re-swear without land; some levies went home, some sold their service. The **Regency of the Bench**, jurists and the Custodian of the Vault ruling for the boy Mahdar, devised a holder that cannot die. This was the **Sublime Threshold**, an office in whose name instruments are sworn, witnessed at Al-Qahur so that its continuity rests on the Covenant's oldest custody. In 938 the commanders re-swore to the Threshold for Basin estates on fixed-term leases. Their descendants are Al-Qahur's military households. "Armed tenants who forgot the lease had an end" was a legal description before it was a Qahuri insult.
- **The first binding.** A Qahirid cousin who claimed to be holder in his own right was not killed. His name was surrendered into the Vault and he lived out his life bound. The Cull begins here as a mercy.
- **The south.** Durayd ibn Sahl *(proposed name)*, amir of occupied Caleran, re-swore the cities and garrisons to himself in 931–932 as **Amir of the Southern Threshold** (06.3).
- **Qazmarid.** In 933 Qazmarid argued that if instruments die with the man, the Qahirid claim had only ever been a man's, and that the Fourth Seal itself had been widowed with Uthar. Only blood continues, so its ruler was proclaimed Caliph-Wasi and tribute stopped. Witnessed-Seal jurists answered that a living community's attestation cannot be widowed. Both traditions' present emphases sharpened here; the Seal stayed undecided.

*The Covenant's own pressures.*
- **Garrisons.** Caleran is a full season's march from Iqashar. The occupation was meant to pay for itself from estates worked by contract-bound survivors. From 908 the rebels gave amnesty to anyone who surrendered their instruments, and the labour that paid the garrison drained into the hills.
- **Sealed-Blood opportunism.** Qazmari naval stations tolled the convoy lanes that fed the occupation (map check needed) and sheltered fugitives from every Qahirid succession. Qazmarid never fought Iqashar in this period. It charged it.
- **One foundry.** In 905 Qahir ran three yards: the great foundry at Iqashar beside the Chain Furnace and the Ruzbeh Weight House, a Basin yard that went to the commanders in 938, and a forward yard lost with the south. From c. 960 one remained, needing about a generation per great frame. After 907 it built pure-pattern Surrender Engines first, for three reasons:
  - Crown Gate had shown that the enemy could open a Slave-Soul engine;
  - penitential jurists called the execution rite "lawful and wrong";
  - new Depth captures rested on a disputed loophole.

  The first great "mercy tower" was finished c. 944. That is why none stood among the five at the Fall: Qahir standardised the line, but no great frame of it existed yet.
- **Shell extraction.** The four anchors set the foundry's pace. To finish the first mercy tower the Chain Furnace was overdrawn against warnings and ran at reduced output for a generation. Its crews were name-bound, and every draw for engines was a draw taken from waterworks.
- **Soul custody under occupation.** Bound survivors' names sat in local name vaults. Covenant practice required a release entry at death; occupation custodians rarely made one, because a released debt-name was a debt written off. The occupied clergy taught that a held name might hold the soul. *Authorial truth:* custody alone does not touch routing; only a foundry name-cage at the moment of death does. The fear still had a real case behind it: the foundry had taken condemned prisoners, and nobody outside could tell which deaths were which.

**Why.** Personal instruments served a dynasty without old sacred standing for two reigns; a drowned child and a postponed muster made them a crisis. Contract law shaped the rest: names were needed, consent had a price, and loopholes went to whoever moved first.

**What it changes downstream.** It explains the court's name and Al-Qahur's veto, gives the military households literal leases, starts the Cull as binding, gives Qazmarid a legal argument, and reaches the 1360 foundry and engine counts without unexplained losses.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The Ottoman Interregnum and fratricide law become a legal-metaphysical widowing. The Abbasid *amir al-umara* pattern is avoided: commanders get leases, and Al-Qahur's role grows from witness, not protection.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
> **The Widowed Seals (929–938 — the Ottoman Interregnum).** TORQUE: the Qahirid army was bound to the sovereign *by name*, with an heir written in as successor-holder. The heir drowned and Idris died before the re-swearing muster, widowing tens of thousands of instruments at once. The remedy was a holder that cannot die, **the Sublime Threshold**, witnessed at Al-Qahur. It is why the Qahirid court bears that name, why Al-Qahur's acknowledgment still matters, why the Basin's military households hold leases whose terms have run, and why the Cull began as a mercy.

### 06.2 The First Catch boomerang: the Long Reach

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.** Idris's administration counted the Atherian border studs among the estates it would take over. Their handlers drove them north instead, so the occupation never received its remounts and stayed camel- and foot-heavy. The Grass-Tarkhan caught and bred the herds in 905–920, and by the third generation of riders, camps could reach roads that had been out of range. In 951 the first raid season reached the Covenant's north-eastern caravan roads and the outer wells of the escarpment towns; Tarkhan memory calls it **the Long Reach**. The raids peaked c. 975–1010, in what Iqashari registers call **the Horse War**.

*The torque.* The Atherian remount lines had been hymn-blessed in the Long Frontier for a practical reason: ordinary horses panic before bound things, and blessed stock stands. The Covenant's northern defence depended on that panic:
- bound well-wardens at the frontier wells;
- devil-mantled escorts on the roads;
- a terror doctrine learned against camel-borne Well-Tarkhan.

Against Grey Sister's early lines all three failed. The blessing thins by generation, so the threat peaked while the herds were young (c. 960–1020) and declined after. Tarkhan genealogy measures exactly this, which is why the sacred lines are priced above provinces.

*Tarkhan agency.* The raids came from Tarkhan pressures:
- remount wealth reordered prestige, and young riders needed captives and animals to found households;
- the Nine Wells wound of 558 gave raiding a cause;
- mounted camps followed the great-horn herds into new range.

No khan united the banners, and the same riders struck Eclessian and Sestaran roads. A Thalmyric chronicle claims Caedros I sent envoys to the khans. Tarkhan genealogists answer that the horses were Atherian, the choice was theirs, and no envoy drank at their fires.

*The Talking Hill.* In the 990s Iqashar sent its first mercy tower north. It predicted flight routes and broadcast terms adapted to local titles, all calibrated for cities. The camps moved away. For a season it proclaimed terms to empty pasture, and the Tarkhan named it the Talking Hill. Iqashar concluded that the north was cheaper to buy than to beat (06.5).

**Why.** The studs were lost; herds and a riding culture took two generations; Tarkhan raiding grew from Tarkhan pressures; blessed steadiness met a defence built on panic, and the Covenant had no cavalry to answer with.

**What it changes downstream.** The field army is pinned in 951–957 (06.3) and 975–1010 (06.4); Tarkhan companies and Mazrakan follow; the threat fades by the 1100s, so Qarayel in 1261 must win by organisation; and blessed Atherian stock in Covenant pay later fights Thalmyria at Qarath's Well.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The rise of a steppe horse power (the Huns, Seljuk migration, the Mongols) is bent in three ways. The horses are the fallen empire's own blessed stock. No unifier appears for three centuries. The advantage decays through metaphysical dilution.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** Amend **The First Catch**:
> The blessing is a steadiness: horses of the early lines do not shy from devil-mantles, Depth beasts or speaking engines. That is why the Long Reach of 951 broke a Covenant frontier built on bound well-wardens and terror, and why the Horse War (c. 975–1010) peaked while the lines were young. Tarkhan genealogy tracks a grace that once decided wars and now mostly decides prices.

### 06.3 Why Caleran was lost in 957, and the Ember seen from the occupiers' side

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.**

*The Southern Threshold, 931–957.* Durayd ruled through instruments sworn to himself. He named Mahdar in the Friday formula and paid tribute irregularly. Iqashar refused him engines unless he re-swore the south to the Threshold. He would not, because that would make him a governor who could be recalled. His regime rested on garrison families, transplanted subjects, contract-bound locals and sea-borne tribute from the eastern island cities. Its labour drained into the rebel amnesties.

In 948 he offered to return the Ember to Veridun against payment of its account, and Caedros I's court refused to acknowledge Covenant custody. *(Optional beat.)*

*The descent.*
- **951 onward.** The Long Reach holds Iqashar's army in the north.
- **955.** Mahdar sends the last Slave-Soul engine south by sea under its own Threshold-sworn crew.
- **956.** Before Caleran's outer works, kindled war-lamps open its name-cage. The captive soul goes on towards judgement and the frame falls where it stood, in view of the occupied parishes on the walls.
- **957, the release riot.** A custodian refuses a release entry at a bound dockworker's funeral, and the funeral becomes canon's "revolt within occupied districts".
- **957, the surrender.** The cities had sworn to Durayd, so he could release them. He surrendered Caleran in his own name. His terms were the lives of the garrison families, protection for the surrendered quarters (which Caedros II kept), and discharge of the Ember instrument on payment.

*Contested.* Iqashari chroniclers say he "surrendered what he had stolen"; the Southern Houses say Iqashar abandoned them; Thalmyric histories barely mention him. His end stays open: dead in the forum the day Caedros died, or old in Qabir.

*The Ember from the occupiers' side.*
- **The ruling.** Soldiers tried to put the Ember out in 905 and failed. Idris ruled it neither holy nor his own, but undecided. Under the Covenant law of deposit, a thing whose ownership no mortal court can settle is impounded with a depositary until the competent judge decides. Idris named the final audit as judge and the keeper office as depositary.
- **The keepers.** They pledged their own names as surety that it would not be divided, removed or extinguished. The ledger records fuel, the watch and every attempt on it, day by day: the account a depositary may charge the owner at judgement.
- **Survival of the Widowed Seals.** The instrument was addressed to the final audit and not to Idris, so it was the one instrument of his that did not widow in 931. Three generations of keepers answered to it, not to Durayd.
- **Division forbidden.** No lamp could lawfully be lit from the Ember. A Thalmyric tradition says one keeper let a deacon light a single lamp each Feast of the Mother, and the ledger shows an unexplained second measure of oil on each such feast. That stays unexplained.
- **The discharge.** Veridun's payment in 957 discharged the deposit and released the keepers' names. Covenant jurists call it proof that even the People of the Lamp honoured the law of deposit. Thalmyric memory holds that it acknowledged preservation, not ownership. Both stand.

**Why.** Divided sovereignty, leaking labour, the Long Reach, Qazmari tolls, a custody revolt, and a refugee army that kindled where Covenant doctrine held Atherian holy fire spent (see [[#Interfaces]]). Any one was survivable; together they were not.

**What it changes downstream.** Durayd's surrender widows his instruments everywhere else (06.4). The Ember instrument becomes the classical precedent for the Threshold, since an instrument addressed to an undying witness survives its maker; the famous invoice is the legal ancestor of the Covenant's constitution.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The recapture of a capital is bent: the city is surrendered by a governor who legally owned it. The relic held hostage by infidels becomes a deposit kept at the keepers' own risk and paid for by the claimant.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** Amend **The Fall of Caleran (905) and the Impounded Ember**:
> Idris's ruling was a deposit pending the final audit, with the keepers' own names as surety. Addressed to the audit rather than to Idris, it was the one instrument of his that survived the Widowed Seals. Veridun's payment discharged it and released the keepers' names, and classical jurists cite it as the model of the undying Threshold.

Add a new entry:
> **The Southern Threshold (931–957).** TORQUE: the occupied south was sworn to a man, not to the Covenant. Its amir could therefore surrender Caleran in his own name, and the Covenant never conceded it.

### 06.4 Why a divided Thalmyria still won to 1052

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.**

*The instrument provinces, 957–1052.* Durayd's surrender widowed his instruments for every other district, and each amir re-swore locally. By 962 the occupied heartland was a dozen separately sworn amirates, claimed by the Threshold but owing it nothing enforceable. After 957 Thalmyria never faced "the Covenant" in the heartland. It faced districts one at a time, each with its own price, which is why the Reconquest ran through amnesties, sieges and competing registers.

*The First Breaking, 987–1013.*
- **The Horse War** at its height (06.2).
- **A bet.** Iqashari jurists read lamp-breaking as Thalmyria breaching its own covenant, and the court let the Breaking do its work. Exiled flame-monks were sheltered in the Velian belt under the Banu Mizan compacts, which outlived the dynasty's fall c. 885. Ioan of Velia wrote the *Three Orations* there. The bet failed: his formula ended the Breaking. The Covenant's shelter supplied the doctrine that reunited Thalmyria.
- **The Pledged Custodian, 1003–1011.**
  - In 1003 the Custodian of the Vault pledged his own true name to an Iqashari lending house to ransom a son taken in a Tarkhan raid.
  - The loan was built to default. By 1006 the house held the Custodian, through him the Vault, and through the Vault every bound levy's name. No commander knew whose instrument his soldiers were under, and the army stayed home.
  - In 1011 the Custodian surrendered his true name into the Vault's deepest custody, so the pledge attached to nothing, and the Vault burned the lending desk and its books.
  - Tradition gives him three different former names. Every Custodian since has surrendered his name at investiture.
  - *Author-only:* the desk was an Under-Ledger cell, the first of the two the Vault has burned.

*The Split, 1015.* Covenant courts had to assign every Lamp parish to a communion, so Covenant registrars hold the oldest record of which parish chose which side.

*The Second Breaking, 1029–1052.*
- **The Audit of the South (1033).** Tamim's treasury showed that each southern amirate cost more than it returned. The south was declared "held in the Threshold's name without obligation of relief": the claim was kept and the defence disowned.
- **The Muster at the Wrong Well (1041).** The war party's one relief army was ordered to muster at a well a copyist had confused with another two days' march away. The columns arrived piecemeal, ran short of water and dispersed. The war party never won the argument again.
- **Zanthir's two seasons (1044).** Zanthir withheld its fleet and grain from the last amirates and signed with Veridun an instrument valid in alternate seasons, paying tribute to each in turn. Thalmyria accepted Zanthir's autonomy as the price, and Iqashar, with no army to spare, could only register it. This is canon's "timelier treachery" and the origin of Badis's alternating tribute.
- **Money to the north.** From the 1040s Iqashar spent on Tarkhan companies what the war party wanted for the south.

*1052.* The last amirate surrendered to Theodosia's armies. Occupation-descended families who rejected return law moved north with their Covenant deeds and became **the Southern Houses**.

**Why.** A refugee league won a 144-year war against an enemy that was legally fragmented in the south from 957, engaged in the north from 951, paralysed in 1006–1011, and fiscally decided against the south from 1033. Each condition is world-native: widowed instruments, blessed horses, a pledged name, an audit. A copyist's error in 1041 closed the last window.

**What it changes downstream.** The Southern Deeds lead to 1131 (06.5); the Unnamed Custodian and Zanthir's tribute gain origins; the Velian belt becomes the frontier. Thalmyric memory credits Thalmyria alone; Covenant registers record a war Iqashar stopped paying for. Both are partial.

**Analog bent or replaced.** Taifa fragmentation and the Reconquista keep their structure, as the Concordance allows, but the fragmentation comes from widowed instruments. The expected rescue by a zealous desert dynasty (the Almoravid beat) is deliberately absent.

**Draft Torque Register entry.**
> **The Pledged Custodian (1003–1011).** TORQUE: a creditor holding the Vault chief's true name made every bound levy's instrument doubtful. The southern war stopped for want of certainty about whose soldiers they were, not for want of soldiers. The Custodian freed the Vault by surrendering his own name beyond reach. Every Custodian since has done the same, which is why the Unnamed Custodian is the one officer no court can blackmail.

### 06.5 The classical age, 1052–1190: Iqashar, the Vault, the Southern Deeds and the road to Suhrab

- [ ] Approve

**What happens.**

*Iqashar becomes a centre.* The southern defeat discredited the commanders. Iqashari registrars, auditors and jurists took over government, and the city earned its reputation for keeping "the registers, granaries, arsenals and drains working".

*The two houses of the Vault (1059).* After 1011 no single custodian could be allowed to hold the whole key, so Tamim divided the Vault:
- **the Deep Vault at Al-Qahur** keeps the dead, the founding instruments and the Fourth Seal;
- **the Living Register at Iqashar** keeps the names of the living bound (levies, bondspeople, debtors and office-holders) under the Unnamed Custodian.

The Unnamed Custodian's own surrendered name lies in the Deep Vault. Iqashar governs the living, and Al-Qahur holds Iqashar's chief archivist hostage. This is why canon places the Vault and the Seal at Al-Qahur while the Custodian serves at Iqashar, and why Al-Qahur's silence can wound a Sultan-Caliph.

*Author-only:* compartmentalisation means nobody can audit the architecture upward. The reform that shielded the Vault from mortal creditors shielded the Creditor too. Its fragment rests in the Deep Vault's lowest stratum. Whether the 1059 recataloguing noticed an unaccessioned entry stays open.

*The Court of Breaches.* The Unsealing's portable breach-courts were seated at Iqashar as a standing court. It heard frontier instruments: Nine Wells access, the Velian compacts, Zanthir's seasons, Tarkhan contracts and the Southern Deeds.

*Mazrakan (1067).* Mazrakan was chartered at the end of Iqashar's paved road to turn the horse frontier into customs. The Covenant bought blessed lines, and Tarkhan houses sold on their own terms. Companies were hired on **word-price**, because Tarkhan refuse name custody. They therefore stayed outside the Living Register: less controllable, and for two centuries unable to capture the offices the Iron Horde reaches for after 1304.

*The frontier hardens.* The Velian belt, now the southern frontier, hardened under tribute, garrisons, bondage and name custody, as canon states.

*The Two Winters (1101–1103), which remain unexplained.* Al-Qahur's grain failed, and the Well of Seven Pressures was turned to grain preservation, idling the foundry for most of a decade. The Court gave **the grey-sky ruling**: impossibility suspends an instrument rather than voiding it. Suhrab later wrote it into the *Ledger* as flood clauses, the answer to Bir Qassim centuries late. Covenant registers record the winters without a cause.

*The War of the Southern Deeds, 1131–1139.*
- **The claim and ruling.** The Southern Houses held deeds under the 957–1052 surrender terms, which had protected occupation-descended holders. Caedros III resettled frontier military districts on some of that land, and in 1126 the Court ruled the surrender instruments breached. Both sides had a real case.
- **The serving.** Khurzad treated the ruling as a writ. In 1131 the Covenant "served" the districts as the Unsealing had served the north, with mercy towers proclaiming terms adapted to local titles.
- **Why the terms failed.** The terms offered property for surrender, but Caedros's settlers held land on condition of defence, so surrender meant forfeiture under their own law. The towers' models, trained on Long Frontier cities that bargained, kept predicting a capitulation threshold that never came. They escalated terror instead, and the terror hardened the districts. Kindled war-lamps and the counter-engine corps did the rest, and one tower was lost.
- **Two ordinary failures.** In 1135 dysentery in the siege camp killed Khurzad's heir, the commanding prince, and a third of the Guard contingent. In 1137, on **the Day of Two Readings**, Qahuri and Iqashari jurists read one order two ways under their own provincial codes, and a flank never moved.
- **The ransom, 1139.** Thalmyria had captured custodians with registers that held the names of living levies. The Covenant ransomed them at a price that helped Caedros III refill his treasury.

*Towards Suhrab.*
- **The commission.** The Day of Two Readings proved the provincial codes could not command one another, and Khurzad's son opened a commission in 1151 to harmonise them.
- **The renunciation.** Suhrab, a younger son, was placed in the colleges and took the jurist's oath renouncing the throne. This was a classical-age protection for younger sons, gentler than binding.
- **The accident.** In 1189 the heir died when a stand collapsed at the Mazrakan spring fair.
- **The ruling.** In 1190 the Court of Breaches held that a renunciation made to save one's life is coerced and therefore void. It was the reformers' doctrine winning its largest case. Suhrab took a throne whose title rested on a ruling, and spent his reign turning the commission into the *Ledger of Ledgers*.

**Why.** Defeat empowered the clerks; the Pledged Custodian made compartmentalisation urgent; the Long Reach made purchase cheaper than war; the Southern Houses supplied a grievance the courts could certify. Incompatible codes, a camp fever and a collapsed stand did the rest.

**What it changes downstream.** Recommend that the "border military districts" Suhrab takes for Markellos in 1214 be the Southern Deeds districts, so that Qarath's Well enforces the 1126 ruling 88 years late. The flood clauses, the Living Register, the Court of Breaches, Mazrakan and the Iron Horde's late rise all gain causes.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The Ottoman classical age, the House of Wisdom and the Seljuk takeover are avoided as sequences: there is no harem, no guard-led decline and no conquest of the sacred centre. The great defeat is an engine misreading a people, and the Lawbinder is a renunciant made king by a consent ruling, not a governor racing to the capital.

### 06.6 Interface note: the Grey Sweat in a civilisation with a Vault of Names

- [ ] Approve

**What happens (recommendation).**
- **Arrival.** The Sweat reaches Covenant lands c. 1236 by the Velian fairs, Zanthiri ports and Mazrakan. This is a route only; its origin stays unknown.
- **The custody crisis.** Every death of a bound person needs a witnessed release in the Living Register and transfer of the name to the Deep Vault. Deaths outran witnesses, unreleased names piled up, debts of the dead rolled onto dying heirs, and the levy rolls collapsed. The Covenant's name for it should be **the Emptying of the Benches**, after the witness benches of the courts.
- **Suhrab's last book.** Dying in 1243, Suhrab adds a last book to the *Ledger*: where no competent witness survives, a sealed register may stand as witness. Reformers object that the Vault has become witness to its own custody.
- **A third labour answer.** Eclessia answered the mortality with tenancy and Virelia with demesne binding. The Covenant answered with name-inheritance, which strengthened the Basin's canal councils, holders of the water survivors needed, against the military households.
- **The soul side (author-only).** Custody without a name-cage does not touch routing. The Unreleased are a bookkeeping catastrophe, not a soul trap, but the Vault cannot know that, and its books never balance. Neither the Vault nor the Church's relic courts knows that the other's books also fail to balance.
- **Keep the weird intact.** Do not tie the Vault's discrepancy to the Quiet Ninth. Covenant registers record the Sweat's end as a date, not a cause.
- **An Undertow hook.** The Undertow's attempts to buy the Unreleased from Vault clerks stay a hook.

**Why.** The Sweat's torque is soul logistics, and the Covenant is the setting's most logistical soul culture.

**What it changes downstream.** Thinned levies and the debt of the Unreleased help explain why Iqashar pays Qarayel's escort fees in 1265–1300, and why canal councils contest the military estates in 1360.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The Black Death in the Mamluk world is not followed: there is no dynastic collapse and no great historian's lament.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** Add to **The Grey Sweat**:
> In Covenant lands the Sweat is *the Emptying of the Benches*: deaths outran the witnesses needed to release bound names, and the Vault's living and dead registers have never balanced since.
