## Summary

Between 905 and 1190 the Covenant previously existed only as the party Thalmyria defeats. This file gives it a clock of its own: a sovereign line, five pressures that run through the period, and turning points produced by its own people, beasts and engines.

**The spine as approved.** Idris's instruments named him and his heir; the heir drowned and Idris died before re-swearing, widowing them all (06.1). On the ground that meant levies walking home, southern garrisons waking unsworn, silt hounds climbing out of the northern wells and a great engine loose in Iqashar's dye-yards, stopped by a feeder whose formula named the Sublime Threshold. The occupied south, sworn to its own amir, fell in 957 while Grass-Tarkhan riders on blessed Atherian horses pinned Iqashar's army in the north (06.2–06.3). Thalmyria then fought separately sworn amirates while Iqashar lost the use of its army to a pledged name and wrote the south off (06.4). The classical age was one of registers, horse fairs and one disastrous lawsuit fought with engines; Suhrab reached the throne because a court voided his renunciation (06.5).

**Wonders and horrors added for the Covenant (the inventory's gap).** The winter the wells barked (931–932); the Elder in the dye-yard and Gulnar's formula (931); the speaking engine broken before Caleran (956); the Unnamed Custodian's name-wound (1011); the Talking Hill (990s); the signal-house governor of the Taken Trumpet as a Covenant sinner (c. 910–913).

**Canon tensions resolved.**
1. The Vault and the Fourth Seal lie at Al-Qahur, yet the Custodian serves at Iqashar: the two houses of 1059 (06.5).
2. No Surrender Engine stood among the five great engines at the Fall: the first great mercy tower is c. 944 (06.1).
3. Qahir had several yards; 1360 has one foundry: one from c. 960 (06.1).
4. Zanthir's "timelier treachery": the seasons of 1044 (06.4).
5. "Banu Mizan territory" after 885: read as lands still under the Banu Mizan compacts (06.4).
6. The Cull of Brothers and Suhrab's executed son, bent into native forms per Direction decision 7: the Cull begins as **lodging** a rival's name in the Vault (931); Suhrab **lodges** his son on the Guard's evidence and the prince dies in the Deep Vault's cloister, which foreign chroniclers record as an execution (06.1, 06.5).
7. Grey Sister drinking at the Nine Wells "generations after the First Catch": her daughters' riders water there in 951 (06.2).

## Proposals

### 06.1 The sovereign spine and the Widowed Seals

- [x] Approved with changes — the oath-by-name cause is world-native and explains 1360; retold through the wells, the dye-yards and the Basin estates, with the Cull bent into lodging.

**What happens (as approved).**

| Reign | Sovereign | Why the reign matters |
|---|---|---|
| 902–931 | Idris al-Fattah | canon; heir Ruzbeh ibn Idris drowns 929 |
| 931–979 | **Mahdar ibn Idris** | eight at accession; the Regency of the Bench to 940; refuses to arm the south |
| 979–c. 1018 | Mahdar's son *(name open)* | the Horse War; the Pledged Custodian |
| c. 1018–1029 | *(name open)* | a short reign |
| 1029–1068 | **Tamim the Auditor** | the Audit of the South; the divided Vault; Mazrakan |
| 1068–1112 | one or two reigns *(names open)* | consolidation; third mercy tower |
| 1112–1142 | **Khurzad** | the War of the Southern Deeds |
| 1142–1190 | Khurzad's son *(name open)* | the code commission; father of Suhrab |
| 1190–1243 | Suhrab the Lawbinder | canon |

*An army sworn to a man.* Qahir, a soldier without old sacred standing, bound levy captains, foundry masters, engine crews and beast-handlers to himself by name, with Idris written in as successor-holder. Idris did the same, naming his eldest son Ruzbeh; the surrender instruments served on the southern cities in 905–906 named Idris the same way.

*The Unsworn Years, 929–938.* In 929 Ruzbeh, fourteen, drowned while swimming at night in a palace cistern. Idris set the re-swearing muster for spring 932 and died of an eleven-day fever in autumn 931. Every instrument naming the two was **widowed**, and each party decided for itself whether it still bound.
- **Levies and garrisons.** Northern companies held their own musters and marched home or sold service to Basin landholders for grain. Southern garrisons woke owing nobody. Two Velian-belt governors rode north with their pay-chests.
- **The wells.** Silt hounds bound into the frontier well-shafts were fed under names written to Idris and his son. The names failed; the hounds climbed out. The escarpment towns remember "the winter the wells barked": silt fans on thresholds, breathing under floors, families who went for water after dark. Townspeople ringed wells with dry stone; several outer towns emptied.
- **The Elder.** The Depth-Core engine that escaped Crown Gate broke out of the Chain Furnace yard on the ninth night after the funeral, went downhill to the dyers' cisterns and killed nineteen people. **Gulnar**, a Vashari-born feeder, stood on the threshold stone of the Dyers' Hall and gave the feeding name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir, whoever stands in it". The frame lay down in an indigo vat; the blue stayed in its seams. Dyers credit her; foundry masters said the beast drank itself quiet; Qazmari preachers said Iqashar invented a miracle.
- **The Regency of the Bench**, jurists and the Custodian of the Vault ruling for Mahdar to 940, made the dyers' formula law: instruments are sworn to the **Sublime Threshold**, witnessed at Al-Qahur. (The term stays as the established translation convention.)
- **The first lodging.** A cousin of Idris claimed the widowed instruments as the nearest grown man of the house. The Regency lodged his name in the Vault, so that nobody could swear to him, pray for him by name or hold him to anything he signed; he lived thirty years in a walled garden at Al-Qahur and was buried under a blank stone. The Cull begins here as a mercy. *(This replaces the Ottoman fratricide-law beat.)*
- **Qazmarid, 933.** Its lord took the title Caliph-Wasi on the argument that the Qahirid claim, and the Fourth Seal with Uthar, had been widowed and only blood continues; Witnessed-Seal jurists answered that a community's witness cannot be widowed. Qazmari stations tolled every Covenant convoy in the western strait.
- **The leases of 938.** Basin captains re-swore to the Threshold for estates on fixed-term leases and took the Basin engine yard: Al-Qahur's military households.
- **The south.** Durayd ibn Sahl swore the southern cities to himself as Amir of the Southern Threshold (06.3).

*Pressures kept.* Garrisons a season's march south, fed by bound labour that drained into rebel amnesties from 908; Qazmari tolls; one foundry from c. 960 (the Basin yard went to the households in 938, the forward yard was lost with Caleran); mercy towers built first after 907 because Crown Gate showed a name-cage could be opened, penitential jurists called the execution rite "lawful and wrong", and fresh Depth captures rested on a disputed reading; the first great tower c. 944 by overdrawing the Chain Furnace, which left Iqashar's lower cisterns low every summer for a generation; soul-custody fear in the occupied south (authorial truth moved to [[The Covenant after Caleran - Authorial Truth]]).

*Great-engine ledger (approved).*

| Date | Event | Great engines |
|---|---|---|
| 905 | the Fall | 5 (3 Depth-Core, 2 Slave-Soul) |
| 907 | Shattered Engines | 2 (one Depth-Core, later **the Elder**; one Slave-Soul) |
| c. 944 | first mercy tower | 3 |
| 956 | Slave-Soul engine broken before Caleran | 2 |
| c. 1000; 1080s | second and third mercy towers | 4 |
| 1131–39 | one mercy tower lost | 3 (the Elder and two towers in 1190) |

**Why.** Personal instruments served a dynasty without old sacred standing for two reigns; a drowned boy and a postponed muster made them a catastrophe in the streets and at the wells.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The Ottoman Interregnum becomes a widowing of oaths with beasts loose; fratricide law becomes lodging; the *amir al-umara* pattern is avoided (commanders get leases; Al-Qahur grows as witness).

**Torque entry (final).** See [[#Integration patch]] § Torque Register.

**Open questions resolved.** The Cull turns to the knife after 1243 (recommended to the later pass; [[The Covenant after Caleran - Authorial Truth]]). Qazmarid's prior status stays as 05 decides; 933 is written to work either way.

### 06.2 The First Catch boomerang: the Long Reach

- [x] Approved with changes — blessed horses meeting a frontier built on panic is world-native and vivid; told at the well-lip with Tarkhan motives, the Talking Hill and the Nine Wells wording fixed.

**What happens (as approved).** Idris's clerks listed the Atherian border studs among the estates to be seized; their lay brothers and handlers drove them north, and the occupation stayed camel- and foot-heavy. Grass-Tarkhan hunters caught and bred them (905–920). In 951 the first raid season reached the north-eastern caravan roads and the outer wells of the escarpment towns: **the Long Reach**. Raiding peaked c. 975–1010: **the Horse War** in Iqashari registers.

*The torque on the ground.* The northern defence rested on hound-wells, hound-handlers with the road escorts, and the panic a Depth beast causes in ordinary animals. Blessed stock stands before bound things. The hounds came up the shafts spitting silt; the grey horses stood; riders lanced the hounds at the well-lip while the garrison's horses tore their tethers. That season riders on Grey Sister's daughters watered at the central well of Tergai without any fort's leave. The blessing halves roughly each generation, so the danger peaked c. 960–1020.

*Tarkhan agency.* Remount wealth reordered prestige and young riders needed captives and animals to found households; the Nine Wells wound of 558 gave a grievance; the camps followed the great-horn herds. No khan united the banners. A Thalmyric chronicle's claim of envoys from Caedros I is answered by Tarkhan genealogists: no envoy drank at their fires (authorial: envoys went, and changed nothing).

*The Talking Hill.* In the 990s Iqashar sent its first mercy tower north; its models, learned on walled cities, proclaimed terms to empty pasture for a season. Iqashar concluded the north was cheaper to buy than to beat (06.5).

**Downstream.** Field army pinned 951–957 and through the Horse War; Mazrakan and word-price companies follow; the threat fades by the 1100s, so Qarayel in 1261 wins by organisation; blessed stock in Covenant pay later fights at Qarath's Well.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The steppe horse power is the fallen empire's own blessed stock, with no unifier for three centuries and an advantage that decays.

**Articles updated.** [[First Catch and Grey Sister]]; [[Nine Wells of Tergai]] ("generations after" replaced: the 951 watering); [[Covenant Armies and the Tarkhan Frontier]]; [[Mazrakan, Market of the Nine Measures]]. Tarkhan spine and Torque Register: patch.

**Open questions resolved.** The steadiness covers bound things only. The blessing is a grace on the line laid by the stud brothers (matches 05's march-stud interface).

### 06.3 Why Caleran was lost in 957, and the Ember seen from the occupiers' side

- [x] Approved with changes — the city surrendered by one man's voice is the best version of 957; the deposit stays, told through the keepers' ledger, and the speaking engine and funeral riot carry the turn.

**What happens (as approved).**

*The Southern Threshold, 931–957.* Durayd ruled through instruments sworn to himself, named Mahdar in the Friday prayers and paid tribute when he could. Iqashar refused him engines unless he re-swore the south to the Threshold; he would not become a recallable governor. His country: the Aurin estates, Caleran's port and a dozen walled towns, held by garrison families, transplanted subjects and contract-bound locals whose names sat in local name vaults. Custodians seldom made release entries at death (a released debt-name is a debt written off); the parish clergy taught that a held name might hold a soul. In 948 he offered the Ember back for payment of its account; Caedros I's court refused. Hostage exchanges with the banner valleys were abused on both sides (the AF-R15 false rescue is told there).

*The descent.*
- **951 onward.** The Long Reach holds Iqashar's army in the north.
- **955.** Mahdar sends the last Slave-Soul engine south by sea under a Threshold-sworn crew; Qazmarid charges its passage.
- **956.** Before Caleran's outer works the kindled war-lamps open its name-cage. The occupied parishes on the walls said it cried out in a Caleran voice and knelt. The Warden of the Last Redoubt spends companies against the outer works.
- **957, the funeral.** A custodian refuses a release entry at a bound dockworker's funeral; the mourners carry the coffin into his court and the quarter rises: canon's "revolt within occupied districts".
- **957, the surrender.** The cities were sworn to Durayd, so he could release them. In the glazed courtyards of the occupation quarter he surrendered Caleran in his own name; horn players sounded the three broken notes and the protected were named first: the garrison families, the surrendered quarters (Caedros II kept both) and the Ember's account on payment.

*Contested.* Iqashari chroniclers: he surrendered what he had stolen. The Southern Houses: Iqashar abandoned him. Thalmyric histories barely mention him. Forum death or old age in Qabir both stay in reader prose (authorial: Qabir, c. 971).

*The Ember from the occupiers' side.* Soldiers tried water, sand and a wet cloak in the first week. Idris ruled it neither holy nor his own but undecided: a deposit held for the final audit, the keepers pledging their own names that it would be neither divided, removed nor put out. Their ledger records oil, wicks, the watch and every attempt (a drunken sergeant with a bucket, a zealot bound north, Caleran's faithful trying for a coal). Addressed to the audit and not to Idris, it was the one instrument of his that did not widow in 931. No lamp could lawfully be lit from it; the second measure of oil on each Feast of the Mother stays in reader prose as a disputed tradition (authorial: true). Veridun's payment discharged the deposit and released the keepers' names; both peoples' readings stand.

**Why.** Divided sovereignty, leaking labour, the Long Reach, a funeral, a kindling army where Covenant jurists believed the Atherian fire was failing. Any one was survivable; together they were not.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The recapture of a capital becomes a surrender by the governor who legally held it; the relic held by infidels becomes a deposit kept at the keepers' own risk.

**Articles updated.** [[The Reclamation of Caleran]]; [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]]; [[The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)]]; [[The Fall and the Long Reconquest]]. *The Invasion and the Fall* and the Torque Register: patch.

### 06.4 Why a divided Thalmyria still won to 1052

- [x] Approved with changes — the amirates, the pawned name and the wrong well give the Reconquest a real enemy; the instrument-province language is cut to what people fought over, and the Custodian becomes the Covenant's name-wound wonder.

**What happens (as approved).**

*The amirates, 957–1052.* Durayd's surrender released Caleran and left every other district sworn to nobody; each amir re-swore his garrison, and by 962 the heartland was a dozen amirates with their own walls, debts and prices (one river citadel held eleven years on horse-meat and rainwater; another amir sold his town for passage north and a chest of his own deeds). Iqashar claimed them and armed none. Mahdar refused to arm the south to 979.

*The First Breaking, 987–1013.* Iqashar's jurists read lamp-breaking as Thalmyria breaching its own covenant and let it run, sheltering exiled flame-monks in the Velian belt under the old Banu Mizan compacts; Ioan of Velia wrote there; his formula ended the Breaking. The bet failed.

*The Pledged Custodian, 1003–1011.*
- In 1003 Tarkhan riders took the Custodian's son on the road north of Iqashar. He pledged his own true name to a lending house below the Ruzbeh Weight House.
- The loan was built to fail. By 1006 the house held the Custodian, the Vault's keys and through them every bound levy's name; the war party's army never left its camp while Thalmyria broke lamps.
- In 1011 he surrendered his name into the Vault's deepest custody. The ink of it faded from every page, from the ledger to his marriage contract; his wife could not say it; the bond had nobody on one side. That night the Vault's guards burned the lending house. His ransomed son came home to a father he could not name.
- Registrars give three former names, none in any document older than 1011. Every Custodian since surrenders his name at investiture. Registrars keep his niche, where petitioners leave blank slips; Qazmari preachers call it the suicide of a soul; Thalmyric polemicists counted him damned.
- *Author-only:* an Under-Ledger cell, the first of two the Vault has burned.

*The Split, 1015.* Covenant registrars entered every Lamp parish in Covenant lands under one communion, so they hold the oldest record of which parish chose which side (one line, in [[The People of the Lamp]]).

*The Second Breaking, 1029–1052.*
- **The Audit (1033).** Tamim's treasury read each amirate's accounts aloud in the Threshold's courtyard: every one cost more than it returned. The south was declared claimed but not defended; garrison families learned it by letter.
- **The Muster at the Wrong Well (1041).** The relief army was ordered to a well whose name differed by one stroke from another two days on. The columns arrived piecemeal, ran short of water and dispersed. The war party never won again; money went to Tarkhan companies.
- **Zanthir's seasons (1044).** The emir at Qabir withheld fleet and grain barges from the starving amirates and signed with Veridun an instrument valid in alternate seasons. Thalmyria accepted Zanthir's autonomy as the price; Iqashar registered it. Canon's "timelier treachery"; Badis's alternating tribute.

*1052.* The last amirate surrendered to Theodosia's armies. Occupation-born families who rejected return law went north with their deeds in cedar boxes and their house keys: **the Southern Houses**.

**Why.** A refugee league won a 144-year war against an enemy fragmented in the south from 957, engaged in the north from 951, paralysed 1006–1011 and fiscally decided against the south from 1033. A copyist's error closed the last window.

**Analog bent or replaced.** Taifa fragmentation keeps its shape, but comes from widowed oaths; the Almoravid rescue is deliberately absent.

**Articles updated.** [[The Long Reconquest (908-1052 AR)]]; [[The Founding Flame]]; [[The Fall and the Long Reconquest]]; [[Zanthir and the Unfinished Reconquest]]; [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Ahl-e-Ahd/60 Realms and Peoples/Emirate of Zanthir/Emirate of Zanthir Overview.md|Emirate of Zanthir Overview]]; [[The Qahirid Suzerainty and Iqashar]]. Named Figures, Present-Day Courts, the Under-Ledger and the Chronology: patch.

**Open questions resolved.** Name surrender binds the Custodian for life (he holds nothing and signs nothing in his own right). Ioan's Covenant shelter is public.

### 06.5 The classical age, 1052–1190: Iqashar, the Vault, the Southern Deeds and the road to Suhrab

- [x] Approved with changes — the Vault split, Mazrakan and the Southern Deeds explain 1360; the Court of Breaches is reduced to a building and its cases, and the war is told through settlers, towers and a camp fever.

**What happens (as approved).**

*Iqashar becomes a centre.* Defeat discredited the commanders; registrars, auditors and jurists took over, and the city earned its name for working registers, granaries, arsenals and drains.

*The two houses of the Vault (1059).* Until then one house under one Custodian: old deep halls at Al-Qahur (founding instruments, the Fourth Seal) and Qahir's working halls at Iqashar. Tamim made them two houses. The **Living Register** stayed at Iqashar under the Unnamed Custodian; the names of the dead and the Custodians' surrendered names went south in a train of sealed chests (eleven days on the Basin road, villagers covering their wells) to the **Deep Vault** at Al-Qahur under the shrine custodians. Iqashar governs the living; Al-Qahur holds the name of Iqashar's chief registrar. *Author-only:* the Creditor's fragment and the unaccessioned-entry question stay open.

*The Court of Breaches.* The Unsealing's riding breach-courts got a building at Iqashar and heard the frontier's quarrels: the Nine Wells, the Velian compacts, Zanthir's seasons, Tarkhan contracts, the Southern Houses' deeds.

*Mazrakan (1067).* Chartered at the end of Iqashar's paved road; Covenant buyers paid Tarkhan houses' prices for blessed lines; companies hired on **word-price**, outside the Living Register, which made them harder to command and kept them from imperial offices for two centuries.

*The Two Winters (1101–1103), unexplained.* Basin grain failed; the Well of Seven Pressures was turned to preserving grain, idling the foundry for most of a decade; the **grey-sky ruling** suspended impossible contracts instead of voiding them, saving Basin farmers; jurists call it the answer to Bir Qassim. Covenant registers record no cause.

*The War of the Southern Deeds, 1131–1139.*
- **The claim.** Caedros III settled frontier military districts on land the Southern Houses held under the 957–1052 surrender terms; in 1126 the Court ruled the surrender instruments breached. Both sides had a case.
- **The serving.** Khurzad treated the ruling as a writ and sent two mercy towers to proclaim property-for-surrender terms in the settlers' own village titles.
- **Why it failed.** The settlers held land on condition of defence, so surrender meant forfeiture. The towers kept predicting a breaking point that never came and escalated: marked stones on threshing floors, counterfeit orders called at dusk, bombardment of mills. Settler women sang the proclamations back from the terraces. Kindled lamps and the counter-engine corps did the rest; one tower was lost.
- **Two ordinary failures.** 1135: dysentery killed Khurzad's heir and a third of the Guard contingent. 1137: on **the Day of Two Readings** Qahuri and Iqashari jurists read one order two ways and a flank never moved.
- **The ransom, 1139.** Captured custodians carried registers holding living soldiers' names; Iqashar ransomed them, helping Caedros III refill his treasury.

*Towards Suhrab.* Khurzad died 1142; his son opened the code commission in 1151. Suhrab, a younger son, took the jurist's oath renouncing the throne. In 1189 the heir died when a stand collapsed at the Mazrakan spring horse fair; in 1190 the Court of Breaches voided the renunciation as coerced. Suhrab spent his reign turning the commission into the *Ledger of Ledgers*.

*Suhrab's son, bent (Direction decision 7).* Late in his reign the Ledger's book against armies sworn to a living man was turned on his most gifted son: Guard officers swore the prince had taken soldiers' oaths in his own name. Suhrab lodged his son's name in the Deep Vault; the prince died in its cloister within the year (fever, said the Vault; the Guard, said the bazaar). Foreign chroniclers, who could not tell a lodging from an execution, recorded that Suhrab executed his son on the Guard's evidence.

**Why.** Defeat empowered the clerks; the Pledged Custodian made a divided Vault urgent; the Long Reach made purchase cheaper than war; the Southern Houses supplied a grievance the courts could certify; incompatible codes, a camp fever and a collapsed stand did the rest.

**Downstream.** Recommend that the "border military districts" Suhrab takes for Markellos in 1214 be the Southern Deeds districts, so that Qarath's Well enforces the 1126 ruling 88 years late. The flood clauses, Living Register, Court of Breaches, Mazrakan and the Iron Horde's late rise gain causes.

**Analog bent or replaced.** No harem, no guard-led decline, no conquest of the sacred centre. The great defeat is an engine misreading a people; the Lawbinder is a renunciant made king by a ruling; Suleiman and Mustafa become a lodging.

**Articles updated.** [[Historical Spine of the Covenant]]; [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]]; [[The Qahirid Suzerainty and Iqashar]]; [[Al-Qahur and the Sacred Basin]]; [[Mazrakan, Market of the Nine Measures]]; [[Covenant Government and Imperial Suzerainty]]. High Noon articles, Disputed Fourth Seal, Named Figures, Chronology: patch.

**Open questions resolved.** The 1059 recataloguing question stays open (authorial file). The college renunciation survives as a custom until the Cull's turn after 1243. The 1139 ransom is public.

### 06.6 Interface note: the Grey Sweat in a civilisation with a Vault of Names

- [x] Approved with changes — the custody crisis is the right Covenant face of the Sweat; written into the Covenant spine and the Status article only, with Grey Sweat wording passed to its owners in the patch.

**What happens (as approved).** The Sweat reached Covenant lands c. 1236 by the Velian fairs, Zanthiri ports and Mazrakan (route only; origin unknown). It fell lightly on the dry country and heavily on the Basin and the Iqashar barracks. Every bound death needed a witnessed release in the Living Register; deaths outran witnesses, debts of the unreleased dead rolled onto heirs, and the levy rolls collapsed: **the Emptying of the Benches**. Dying in 1243, Suhrab added a last book to the *Ledger* letting a sealed register stand as witness where none survived; reformers object that the Vault became witness to its own custody. Covenant registers record the Sweat's end as a date, not a cause. *Author-only:* custody without a name-cage never touches routing; the registers never balance; do not tie the discrepancy to the Quiet Ninth; Undertow purchases of the Unreleased stay a hook.

**Articles updated.** [[Historical Spine of the Covenant]]; [[Status, Slavery, and Manumission in the Covenant]]. Grey Sweat articles and Torque Register: patch.

*Add* in date order:

> **The Widowed Seals (929–938 — the Ottoman Interregnum).** TORQUE: the Qahirid army was bound to the sovereign *by name*, with the heir written in as successor-holder. The heir drowned in a palace cistern and Idris died before the re-swearing muster, widowing every instrument at once: levies walked home, southern garrisons woke unsworn, the silt hounds of the northern wells slipped their feeding names, and the Depth-Core engine later called the Elder walked through Iqashar's dye-yards until a feeder gave its name "to the threshold of the house of Qahir". That formula became **the Sublime Threshold**, a holder that cannot die, witnessed at Al-Qahur. It is why the court bears that name, why Al-Qahur's acknowledgment still matters, why the Basin's military households hold leases whose terms have run, and why the Cull of Brothers began as lodging, not killing. [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]]

> **The Southern Threshold (931–957).** TORQUE: the occupied south was sworn to a man, not to the Covenant. Its amir could therefore surrender Caleran in his own name, the protected named first, and every district beyond Caleran fell apart into separately sworn amirates that Thalmyria took one at a time. [[The Reclamation of Caleran]]

> **The Pledged Custodian (1003–1011).** TORQUE: a lending house holding the Vault chief's true name made every bound levy's oath doubtful, and the Covenant army sat in its camp through Thalmyria's First Breaking for want of certainty about whose soldiers they were. The Custodian freed the Vault by surrendering his own name into its deepest custody, and the ink of it faded from every page. Every Custodian since has done the same, which is why the Unnamed Custodian is the one officer no court can blackmail.

> **The Two Houses of the Vault (1059).** TORQUE: custody was split between the living (Iqashar) and the dead and sacred (Al-Qahur), with the Living Register's chief's own name held in the Deep Vault. The capital and the sacred city cannot rule without each other, and, unknown to anyone in-world, the architecture can never be audited from inside.

> **The War of the Southern Deeds (1131–1139).** TORQUE: a lawsuit fought with engines. Mercy towers trained on cities that bargained proclaimed property-for-surrender terms to settlers for whom surrender meant forfeiture, and escalated where they should have stopped. The Covenant lost, then ransomed its captured custodians, because their registers held the names of its living soldiers.

*Append* to **The Grey Sweat**: In Covenant lands the Sweat is *the Emptying of the Benches*: deaths outran the witnesses needed to release bound names, and the Vault's living and dead registers have never balanced since.
