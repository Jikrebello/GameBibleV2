# Causality Pass — 10A Bending the Founding

> [!note] Record of a bend written into canon, 14 November 2026
> The [[Real-History Concordance#Sequences still close to their analogues|Concordance]] listed the imperial founding as still following its analogue beat for beat. This file records the old sequence, the new one and its causes, what was bent and why, and the integration patch for files this writer does not own. The controlling author-only rulings are in [[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review#Causality Pass 10A amendment (14 November 2026)]]. Rules: [[Causality Pass - Direction]]. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## Summary

From 370 to 350 BR the founding ran: Second Triumvirate and proscriptions, Philippi, Perusia and Fulvia, Octavia's marriage, Naulochus and Lepidus, the Donations, Actium, the double suicide, Caesarion's death, Octavia raising Cleopatra's children, Augustus's restored republic, adoption and apotheosis. A reader who knew Rome could call every beat.

The bend uses engines canon already held and makes them decide the sequence:

- **The Red Field's seven names.** Varroth's ruling of 367 is read as Atheria read it: the Field stands open while any of the seven challengers of 370 lives, and no commander dares disband an army. The war becomes a hunt for seven men through every refuge of the inner sea.
- **The Table Oath's suppliant clause.** A stranger who reaches the unranked table cannot be sold from it. It shelters the last of the seven at Sarqen, unmakes the queen's house, saves the queen's life and binds the conqueror.
- **Astaroth's grammar.** The proscriptions break his substitution taboo and spread the war; Thalion uses the counterplay of refusing a field before accepting it; Velsar binds himself to a field of his own.
- **Mask appetite.** Feeding a bound army by worth reopens a Gluttony sign in the towns' jars; the painted boards that go to war in Thalion's place are the first feeding of the Pride Mask that answers in 247.
- **The sea's covenants.** Marelith dies because its founding covenant forbids an anchorage and Melvo brings a chain; Sarqen's sea bargain keeps its nature and moves.

**Anchors kept.** Casvilar's assassination 370; collective rule 367; Darsil 363 and Velsar's Avesh campaign 358; Calvena alive and her 357 terms; Marelith destroyed 355; Ardel executed 354; Corven Ilar's speech 353; Thalion supreme and Sarqen under imperial rule c. 350; Caelor 326/324 and the wardens' test; Meren 318–286, Valeran, Olarion; the speaking portrait 247; every victim and atrocity account.

**Blend.** Of fifteen turning points, eight are driven or decisively bent by the supernatural (the fouled Field, the open Field's government, the Writing-In, Marelith's covenant, the Salt Field, the Fourth Collection, the sea bargain, Velsar's binding), with a ninth carrying a supernatural consequence (the meal at the landing). That is a little over half.

**Messy people.** Velsar devises the Writing-In; Thalion consents to Venn's death and leaves Velsar bound; Venn walks down armed; Varroth sells and refuses; Tarsen hangs; Miretha feeds; Iren Sallas walks out; Esharat's women hide a baby; Samyra asks for her sister's death and perhaps the baby's, keeps a murderer at her table, saves her treasury over her wounded, and sits down below a rower; Amesar kills at the table to end a war; Melvo disobeys out of decency; Calvena pleads for a half-brother she has never met; Tobal sits in the queen's seat.

**Contingency.** Ilvara's death in childbirth; the bridge where two foraging columns meet; a garrison's unpaid winter; the fever at Darsil; Tarsen lost in the snow.

## What was bent and why

- **The triumvirate** becomes four men in a grain store, one of them the senate's speaker. The collective rule exists because armies that may not disband must still be fed, which is a cause the world made, not a copied office.
- **The proscriptions** keep their victims and their ugliness but gain a reason the soldiers obeyed and a reason the killing made things worse. Cicero's death is replaced by an old speaker walking down to the muster ground with a borrowed practice sword; Astaroth honours the armed stand and is indifferent to its justice.
- **Philippi** becomes a battle with no assassin in it. The assassins' deaths are spread over twenty years and seven refuges, each a test of an institution canon had already built (Darneth's grievance, the Kept Doors, Ilamar's precinct, Marelith's open harbour, Avesh's houses, the Table Oath).
- **Perusia and Fulvia.** The rival's wife leads nothing. The town is eaten by the victorious army because it may not disband; the wife brings grain and dies of fever.
- **Octavia** is cut. Thalion's second marriage is to the queen's rival sister, which makes the Merath execution personal and gives the empire a Nerethan heir.
- **Naulochus and Lepidus.** Marelith still burns in 355, but its death comes from its own covenant meeting Melvo's home harbour's chain, and the city is the target because a named challenger is aboard. Varroth loses his military weight to his own unpaid garrisons and keeps his priesthood by refusing, not by surrendering; his refusal explains the Red Star's precedence.
- **The Donations** are cut; Velsar's equivalent public act is accepting a Red Field, which traps him.
- **Actium** is cut. A blockade, a famine levy and a Gluttony sign break the queen's country from inside.
- **The suicides** are cut. Velsar dies fighting on a field he cannot leave; Samyra lives because the oath that unmade her also protects any stranger at its table.
- **Caesarion** lives, and his act ends both the Field and his mother's crown. He finishes the hostile history on Ilamar, which later emperors' informers hunt.
- **Selene and Helios** stay with their mother among the towns.
- **Augustus.** Thalion is heavy, short-sighted, a poor rider, afraid of the Red Star and patient to the point of cruelty; he never commands in a battle. He does not pretend to restore the republic; he rules from the store-house. His heir is his own son. His death comes at the unranked table at Sarqen, not in a bed at Caleran, and no ascent is claimed. His founding role is unchanged.

## What a reader of Roman history would now fail to predict

That the assassins would hide rather than fight, and that their hiding would hold every army under arms; that the proscriptions would be a religious device that backfires; that the great battle would leave the war exactly where it was; that the conqueror's wife and the third partner would fall to hunger and to mutiny rather than to the conqueror; that the conqueror would marry the queen's sister and lose her to the queen; that the rival would bind himself to a field of salt; that there would be no sea battle; that the queen's son would kill the last assassin at his mother's own table and so bring her down; that the rival would die fighting a sailor; that the queen would live, by sitting down to dinner; that the conqueror would have to swear her people's oath to have her country; and that the founder's heir would be his son by the queen's sister, carried out of her town in a grain bin.

## Clock check

No date between 370 and 318 BR coincides with a BC or AD year of its analogue. Spacing differs: assassination to joint rule 3 years (analogue 1); to the great battle 7 (2); to the burning of Marelith 15 (8); to the queen's fall 20 (14); founder's reign 32 years (Augustus 41 from Actium).
