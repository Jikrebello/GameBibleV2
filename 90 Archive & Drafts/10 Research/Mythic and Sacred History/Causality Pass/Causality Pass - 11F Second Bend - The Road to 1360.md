# Causality Pass — 11F Second Bend: The Road to 1360

> [!note] Author-only record
> [[Causality Pass - 10F Bending the Road to 1360]] made the mechanisms of this stretch native. An adversarial historian then scored the road to 1360 at 7/10, "a Forward Rule failure", and the 1360 board at 6/10, because the skeleton and the clock survived: add about 212 years and the Accord is Augsburg, the Council is Trent, and the Reformation still runs founder, martyr, systematiser, new order, council, peace. On the creator's instruction (14 November 2026) this file breaks the skeleton and the clock under [[Causality Pass - Direction]] and Rules 6–8 of [[Real-History Concordance#Standing Recontextualization Rules]]. Changes to files this writer owns are made. Changes to shared files are in [[#Integration patch]]. Where 09, 10F and 11F disagree, 11F governs. Index: [[Causality Pass - 00 Proposal Index]].

## Summary

The Reformation no longer has a living founder. A Sending House chaplain drowns with his miners, and the movement starts when a tally-keeper reads his psalter's margins to their widows. For sixteen years it is a funeral strike led mostly by women, with no doctrine. The Sanctum tries the dead man's book, voids the test and bills the country twice for its funerals. The doctrine is then written by the Sanctum's own prosecutor. The old Church's council sits early and briefly, before any prince has reformed, and its honest attempt to pay the dead what it owes them is what starts the princes' war. The Church's reforming order is older than the Reformation and its priest dies in an Inquisitors' cell before it is chartered. The peace is drawn by sitting up in churchyards at night: every yard goes to the rite that has kept its dead sent, and no prince chooses. The movement's first congregations bury in the river, keep no yard, and are left outside their own settlement.

In Fiorenzia the bank is never expelled and never returns. The senate burns the wax faces of the city's dead, a mask-maker's son who married into the bank opens the vault to the crowd, and the citizens vote his house a hereditary chair over the senate's head. The friar preaches against the gift and is found dead in a wax face. The banned book is a factor's manual for lending to princes, and its author sold the extracts that started the crisis and was drowned for it.

Every outcome the present depends on is kept: Eclessia's three-way split; the Freeflame and *The Order of the Open Flame*; the Order of the Lantern, its seminaries and its review of the Inquisitors; the Accord's date (1343), its territorial peace, the oath in the Weiss, the Iron Fast and the silence on reunion; the Unburnt Psalter; the Great Arrears; the Medori's registers and ducal chair; Severin, his four years, the Pyre of Masks in 1346 and the panels; a Vetti line; the four Hordes and the grave's prohibition; Castara; the Sweat's dates and the Quiet Ninth; every 1360 ruler.

## The bends in detail

### Ansgar, Irmel and the Blackwater

Ansgar (1264–1297) never leads anything. He counts because he is the one who sings: handed ten letters for every rite he has time for, he sings the rest at night for nothing and writes what he cannot say in his margins. His father sells the letters at the House door. He drowns going down to sing for trapped men. The founding act belongs to **Irmel** (c. 1275–1344), the pithead tally-keeper, and to the widows; the movement's relic is a shift board and its first leaders are women because the men are underground. *In-world disagreement:* Freeflame say the House sent him down alone and sold over him in the morning; some old-Church teachers say he died the Sanctum's own priest and that the Church has prosecuted a book and never its author; bargemen say the river took back a man who named its drowned; the hill parishes say he came out by the adit. *Authorial truth:* he drowned. He was never tried, so the Sanctum has no sentence against him, which sharpens the Psalter as a held proof. Whether river burial sends the dead is not ruled.

### Aldersbruck and the second fee

The book is tried because the man cannot be. The legate's second device, charging every unbought funeral to be sung again at the family's cost, repeats the Chancery's offer of 1252 to re-sing Ferry Rule rites and is the direct cause of the Risings once the unpaid fees are sold to brokers.

### The Council before the princes

The Council sits 1319–1324 because the old Church can see that the reformed yards lie quiet and its own walk. It answers Ferro's book with Ferro in the room (kept from 10F), and its central decree turns every unsung and every free letter into a debt. The debt needs registers and singers, so it needs money, and the Arrears-levy of 1326 reaches princes who have been fighting the Ashenmen for the old Church. Rainald is not a sheltering prince and has no reformer to shelter: he is an old-Church duke who needs iron, is billed for the Sanctum's fraud and finds his own father unsung. The Counter-Reformation therefore comes first and causes the princely Reformation.

### The Lantern

Its root is Gerlind's condition of 1266, which is also the fork between the two churches: city choirs that brought a priest became the Lantern, and country chaplains who sang alone became the Freeflame. Ivo's death in Malverine custody is why the Council puts the Inquisitors under Lantern review, and it gives the Closed House hearing of 1357 a wound to open. No control says whether it is the same house.

### The Accord by graveyards

The rule comes from Falkendorf and a Korrheim war-priest, which canon already credited with "the burial articles later copied into the Accord". The Night Count is a human instrument on a real signal: a quiet yard shows truthful naming and singing, by either rite, and nothing about doctrine. The second line of the oath is ambiguous about whose dead a lord stands under, his land's or his house's, and that ambiguity is the present's succession crisis. The lapse clause (a yard unsent for a year and a day is open) moves the war to the graveside: races to deathbeds, starved-out clergy, staged and hidden hauntings, corpse-carrying across borders under Warden escort. The canonists' *whose the realm, his the rite* survives only as a half-quotation princes prefer.

### Fiorenzia

The wax faces are a plague custom: with the dead burned on the banks, a face on the wall is the Fiorenzian grave, and the city's portrait art grows from copying wax. *Authorial truth:* a grief-Mask of the Weeping Courts' kind fed through the faces once the pamphlet named the city's unsung dead; it spoke each household's own suspicion back to it, which is why it was so often right. The Pyre burned its doors and not the Mask, which then worked through paint; the master hanged in 1348 had been painting for it, knowingly or not. Severin was genuinely kindled while the Vigil discharged the liens. In 1349 he put on the Mask's last face, from the moulds, meaning to carry it out of the city, and it killed him. The Medori did not kill him. They did kill Vetti. Ginevra let only fair copies of names leave the vault; the Vigil discharged every Fiorenzian lien the family held, and the family has never agreed whether Lauro saved the bank or gave away its best asset.

### Sarnwell and Vessing

Both stay apparitions of the Mother. Sarnwell's proof rests on the Ashenmen's unbroken oaths, so the Sanctum's proudest modern test depends on the Freeflame's river. Vessing's three testimonies still match the three parties, but each witness speaks against their own communion, so no church can use its own witness or dismiss another's.

### The Partition

The herds come home in four bands to four pastures and the assembly of 1304 names what the horses did. White keeps its paternity quarrel because [[White Horde]] carries it in the present.

## Present-day proposals for the creator

Done in owned files, as texture only: the Covenant's succession is told as a contest of registers, using the brother already recorded as "lodged" in 1349; Virelia's election crisis is re-caused through the Accord (an elected crown has no royal dead); "second serfdom" and "confessional auction" are dropped from owned files. Not done, and proposed:

1. **Sayyida Nurhan (Nurbanu).** Option A, the adversary's: remove the queen-mother office and let Al-Qahur's Deep Vault be the court's fourth power, since whoever its custodians un-lodge is a claimant. Option B: keep her but make her the lodged prince's mother, Marzuq's stepmother, living at Al-Qahur as hostage-guardian of the garden; her "Veiled Ledger" becomes the record of who has visited it.
