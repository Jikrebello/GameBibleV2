# Causality Pass — 10C Bending Kellovar, Halmenth and the Last Fortress

> [!note] Record of applied bends
> 14 November 2026, under the [[Causality Pass - Direction|Direction]]. The whole-history pass kept its anchors, and the [[Real-History Concordance]] listed these sequences as still close to their analogues. This record bends five of them in the owned reader articles and in [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]], and gives exact wording for every file outside this writer's ownership. Isfir, the Companions, Aquilan's conversion and the First Church's founding beats are untouched by design.

None of the AR dates coincides with the analogue's AD year (68, 245, 351, 377 and 411 against 73/74, 250, 363, 378 and 431). Spacing: the edict runs three years against Decius's one; the Kellovar crisis runs nine years from the seizure (c. 368) against the two from the Danube crossing to Adrianople; Halmenth falls 13 years after Vestian's death against Ephesus's 36 after Theodosius I's. **Residual:** the Tower Revolt's fixed dates (61, 65, 68, set by the Direction) keep the analogue's four-then-three-year spacing. Inside them the shape now differs: Shahar is besieged continuously from Qiryath's fall, where Masada was invested only after a gap of years, and its siege lasts three years where Masada's lasted months. A later pass may move 68 if the creator wants the spacing broken too.

## 1. The last fortress (Migdal Shahar, 65–68 AR)

**Old beats.** Qiryath falls (65) → a rebel community holds a cliff fortress → camps enclose it → a ramp reaches the wall → assault and mass death → "competing accounts of heroic refusal, killings ordered by its own leaders and imperial massacre" and disputed speeches. A Josephus reader predicts every step, down to the speeches.

**New sequence and causes.**

1. After Qiryath falls, the war party and several thousand refugees go down into **Migdal Shahar**, the Dawn Tower, two days east on the Dawn Road towards Vashar. Like every great tower it is a small gatehouse above a large excavated city, and its deepest chamber holds a Way-Gate paired with an older tower in Vasharan country where the houses that refused the Qiryath charter settled c. 80 BR.
2. The legions take the gatehouse within a month and find the city underneath. **The siege goes down, not up.** Atherian siege engineers use Astaroth's standing practice (Darneth precedent): a siege frame loaded to breaking before a priest of the Red Star shows the failure line of the outer gallery roofs, and piled quarry stone drops them on the refuge halls. Survivors say the Star showed the enemy where to dig; the legate's engineers call it survey.
3. Winter 67: Shahar's council asks the partner bench to open. Opening reveals the anchor and can transfer danger; Vashar might be dragged in or sell the refugees back. The bench answers in spring 68: **households, and no one who has borne arms.**
4. The war captain **Eshkar bar Hanan** refuses terms that leave his fighters to die. The gate keeper **Yaltah bat Ozer** opens on the bench's terms anyway and passes the households over nine days while the fighters hold the stairs. Some fighters go through as fathers with empty hands.
5. Day ten: the soldiers break in; Yaltah breaks the scroll-keys; the gate has not opened since (closed for want of keys, not silent). The legions kill the fighters, the keepers and the old and sick who would not leave their dead, and sell the living they take. The legate claims the rebels killed their own wounded; the receiving tower's mourning court records the dead as the legions' victims. Atherian envoys ask Vashar for the households for years; Vashar knows of no gate.

**What was bent and why.** The ramp is inverted into a downward siege, which is what a Jirahar deep tower would actually demand. The suicide pact becomes a choice made by other Jirahar (the bench) and by a keeper against her captain, using the people's own power under its own rules ([[Prophet's Roads and the Underways]]: two benches, revealed location, transferred danger). The supernatural element is Astaroth's morally blind structural answer, already standing practice. Every victim class survives: the crushed in the galleries, the killed in the lower halls, the sold, and the contested account of the rebels killing their own (now the legate's claim). The outcome holds: charter government gone, a diaspora scattered, with one more cause for the Jirahar to argue among themselves. Shahar becomes the precedent gate benches cite for refusing another tower's danger.

## 2. Tervan's edict (245–248 AR)

**Old beats.** A grave-lamp Mask cult (native) → empire-wide witnessed sacrifice at market altars → written certificates → officers hunt households without certificates → certificates bought → after suspension, the Church argues over restoring those who sacrificed, bought certificates or betrayed names, over penance length and confessors' intercession. That is Decius, the *libelli* and Cyprian beat for beat after the trigger.

**New sequence and causes.**

1. The Kept Lamps' grave-lamps burned without oil and leaned towards mourners. Tervan's answer is to make every lawful lamp visibly lawful.
2. Every household carries the lamps it keeps for its dead to a market altar, puts them out in the Star's basin, relights them from the college fire and names the Star. The commissioners fill them with college oil salted with verdigris scraped from temple bronzes, and the lamps **burn green** for as long as the household buys that oil.
3. For three years the empire's burial grounds glow green at night. **Night watches** walk the graves; a yellow flame or a dark family grave is a door to knock on in the morning. The burial fraternities are found first, Jirahar graves second (charters honoured unevenly). The college stores' oil books know who bought.
4. Tinkers sell ***pot-green*** scraped from old copper. A household can go to the altar with a soldier at its elbow, colour its own flame at home, or let its dead go dark and risk prison, confiscation or death.
5. After 248 the quarrel is over **who may be given fire**: households that went to the altar, households that kept their flame under a bought colour, and the few who walked with the watch. Some congregations give fire after confession before the assembly, some after a winter, many never to watch-walkers. *Refused fire* still means shut out in the river towns. The fire given comes from Amaryah's oven-hidden lamp and spreads by river road and by sea to the gateway ports (Qabir among them), seeding Halmenth.

**What was bent and why.** The certificate is replaced by a colour, the thing a magistrate frightened of counterfeit grave-lamps would actually check, and which is checked by night in graveyards rather than at a desk (Direction rule 1). The penance-and-confessors' dispute is replaced by a native question about flame lineage that already existed in canon (the daughter-flame custom). The truth (Signs and Proof §5): the green is copper only; it proved purchase, and a Mask can counterfeit any colour.

## 3. Astrion's death (351 AR)

**Old beats.** Apostate scholar-emperor → disastrous desert campaign → retreat → rides to a disordered crossing during an attack → spear in the side from an unseen hand → contested last words (*Vicisti, Galilaee* as "You have won, Carpenter").

**New sequence and causes.**

1. On the retreat, well-house riders harry the column and the baggage jams at a dry wash. The army's carpenters bridge it in a night with roof beams from the captured store.
2. Every public work must be loaded before the Red Star until it cracks (the Star's share, 326 BR), and its opener stands under it while the first wagons cross; emperors have excused themselves since Velen I. Under arrows there is no time to load it.
3. The master carpenter offers to stand under it in Astrion's place. Astrion refuses (the substitution taboo of the builders' tales) and climbs down into the wash himself: the restorer of the Stars will not take Velen's excuse.
4. The register wagon crosses; the span breaks under the wagons of wounded behind it. Astrion, crushed, lives through the night. The field record's only words are his question whether the register crossed. It had.
5. Readings: soldiers (he stood there so the wounded could cross), eastern army (he kept the Stars' law), preachers (the Stars killed their own), engineers (green timber and haste), some augurs (he made his body the test of victory they had refused). "You have won, Carpenter" stays a later Church attribution and gains a native second hearer: the master carpenter. The soldiers call the wash **the Emperor's Cut**.

**What was bent and why.** The unseen spear is gone; the death now follows from the era's own engine (Astaroth's law, the 350 test, the refused second test) and from a choice only Astrion would make. It is the builders' standing rule, not a second answer: the Torque Register's "once" stands (Signs and Proof §8).

## 4. Kellovar (368–377 AR)

**Old beats.** Hard winters and raiders drive Folk households to the fort line → Lucar admits them (371–372) and recruits fighters → officers divert grain, take hostages, sell dependants → chaplains take ford-tokens (native) → armed revolt from 372 → Lucar marches before reserves are ready → battle at the ford, army broken, Lucar dies there → Vestian takes power. The token torque was native; the shape was Adrianople.

**New sequence and causes.**

1. **The guides.** Since Cassian's market treaties the southern army has crossed the river-courts' fords behind allied Folk guides from the fort districts: the guide wades first with his household's token at his wrist and the column follows on a rope. (Native institution; Commandment 6.)
2. **The seizure, from c. 368.** Lucar's new army chaplains, men of the bishops who accepted his terms, see idols at the head of a Redeemer army and take the tokens from guides and from every Folk trader at the fort gates. Bone and hair tokens burn in the fort yards; officers sell the silver ones in Caleran.
3. **The winters, 369–371.** Grobi come down the frozen rivers. With no guides the forts can reach the far banks only by bridge, and watch allied villages burn. Survivors who reach the forts are fed for **one pledged child per household** to the crown estates; stewards sell some on; hostages are taken beyond the treaties.
4. **The war, 372–377.** No revolt that ravages provinces: the fort-district households slip away across the fords at night on hidden tokens (sewn into hems, plaited into children's hair), stealing pledged children back in grain carts. The forts hang token-carriers at the fords; Folk war-bands burn estate barns. Few towns change hands.
5. **Kellovar, spring 377.** The last and largest company gathers below Kellovar (Casvilar's starving hill) and crosses by night, a token to each chain of people. Lucar has come south to bring back guides his forts cannot do without. At dawn his army follows behind the chaplains' blessing, unpaid, and the river takes it.
6. **The emperor crossed dry.** A confiscated silver otter, the Otter house's token, hangs on Lucar's horse as a trophy. The court does not ask whose token it is. He comes out alone among the households whose children his officers sold, eats their salt, gives his true name, and **Cathra**, mother of the Otter house, keeps him three days under hearth-law (kin, servant or gone on the third morning: [[Folk Daily Life and the Thirteen Moons]]). She sends him back across on the otter.
7. **The near bank.** His surviving rearguard, who watched him ride dry out of the river that drowned their comrades, pull him from the saddle and kill him. One throws the otter into the ford.
8. **Vestian** has meanwhile held the depots, paid the survivors and been recognised at Caleran **while Lucar was still alive across the river**. He tries no one for the killing, traces pledged children through ledgers, and will not buy back idols.

**What was bent and why.** Admission, the classic abuse-and-revolt arc, the hasty march and the emperor's death in the field are all gone. The confiscation of tokens becomes the cause of the whole crisis, because it disarms the empire's own crossing system; the war becomes a war of escapes; the drowning keeps the approved torque; and the emperor's death turns on the court's moral blindness (it carries the thief dry) and on hearth-law. The outcomes hold: Lucar dead, Vestian in power in 377, the Folk settlement by 379, Folk contingents for the War of the Two Standards, and the tokens left as a property wound.

## 5. Halmenth (411 AR)

**Old beats.** A council over the Mother's title ("Fire-Bearer" against "Man-Bearer") → the bishop condemned → his communion flees beyond the empire → founds a church abroad (the Church of the East). The roots (Second Silence, Raveth, the Jirahar-descended gateway congregations) were native; the council's mechanics and outcome were Ephesus.

**New sequence and causes.**

1. **The money.** From 398 the regency sells tax freedom to relic estates ([[The Long Frontier and the First Unsealing]]). Nasriel will certify no relic in his province: a reliquary is another *lamp made by hands*. If his teaching spreads, no one buys exemptions.
2. **The flame on the altar.** A warship brings a daughter of the Eternal Ember from Caleran. The Fire-Bearer party points to it: Amaryah's lamp has burned unbroken from the Qiryath coal.
3. **The charge.** Nasriel reads Qabir's count of the unkindled professions aloud and asks for Aurelium's sealed register. Refused, he is charged under the Purge commission's edict against publishing an unentered sign, never repealed and inherited by the bishops in 383: the persecuted Church's own gag turned on its last critic.
4. **The verdict.** The teaching is condemned (rightly, on the Redeemer); a canon forbids ordaining any man whose mother's register is kept beside his baptism roll (prejudice). Communion is sealed by fire: each assenting bishop lights his see's lamp from the Ember's daughter. Qabir's deacons keep their own flame, a daughter of Amaryah's lamp from the bread oven in 248, older in Qabir than the Ember in Caleran, and leave the church with it burning and out of communion.
5. **The escape.** That night Halmenth's ferry crews (their bishop **Ostrel** having voted to condemn) row Nasriel out to a Sea Fleet ship below the Sea Tower; his mother's line is of the fleets, and the captains refuse the regents' search of their docks.
6. **The outcome.** No siege, no exile, no flight abroad. The regency strikes Qabir and the cities in communion with it off the roll of recognised sees. Their clergy can certify no relic, so their land stays on the tax rolls while the rest of the empire's goes into sanctuaries; the regents leave them alone as revenue. The condemned gather there; Nasriel dies at Qabir c. 440. This explains why the Velian cities met the Covenant with intact charters and taxable granaries, and why Velian and imperial clergy refused to witness each other's charters (the Pass 05 interface).

**What was bent and why.** No rival council, no deposed capital patriarch, no exile to a desert oasis, no flight to Persia. The council turns on the era's engine (who may authenticate a sign), on the regency's relic economy, and on the daughter-flame custom founded in 248. The Velian Church grows inside the empire's edge as a tolerated, taxed pariah, which makes its later Covenant history more causal.

## What a reader of late Roman or early Church history would now fail to predict

- That the edict's proof is a flame's colour checked in graveyards at night, and that the reconciliation quarrel is about giving fire.
- That the apostate dies not in a skirmish but by choosing to keep a builders' rite his predecessors dodged, under a bridge, while the register he cared about crosses safely.
- That the "Gothic" crisis begins with chaplains disarming the army's own crossing guides; that there is no admission, no pitched battle and no death in the field; that the emperor survives the disaster, is a guest of the family he wronged, and is killed by his own men for having survived; and that his successor is recognised while he is still alive.
- That the council is decided by the regency's tax market and a lamp-lighting, that the condemned bishop escapes on a Jirahar ship, and that his church survives inside the empire because it is the only part still paying its taxes.
- That the last fortress is fought underground, that the Jirahar's own gate bench chooses who lives, and that there is no ramp and no suicide.
