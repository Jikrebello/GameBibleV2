## Summary

The review called this stretch "set-pieces without sinew": Charlemagne, the Saxon Wars, iconoclasm, Irene, 1054, Verdun, Vladimir, Basil II, Manzikert and Romanos IV, renamed and laid end to end. This spine keeps the pressures those analogs supply (two claimants to one empire, a quarrel over whether holy fire may live in made vessels, a forced-conversion frontier, a partition among heirs, a great defeat and a blinded emperor) and drives them with the World Egg's own causes from the second beat onward.

**The chain in one paragraph.** After Caleran is retaken, Thalmyria puts the Fire back into endowed office and its lamps gutter again, exactly as they did before 905 ([[Causality Pass - 05 The Guttering, 400 to 905 AR|Pass 05]]). An eruption, an ash-plague and a Great Lamp shrunk to a bead convince a soldier-emperor, Leovar III, that the Fire refuses to be housed; he breaks the lamps. In the east a march banner kindles for a miller's widow at Kaltfurt and the marcher lords crown the man beside her; the eastern bishops, who have held the provinces' oaths "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful" since 711, examine the banner, credit the sign to Adalmar and crown him emperor in 1002, while Veridun has destroyed its own proofs. Adalmar's register war on the Folk ends at a lake that stands up and takes his fort. Anthe restores the lamps; Aurelium demands to examine them; Hubald's nails and eleven towns' unrung bells make the Split. The two churches then learn opposite lessons from the same fire. Thalmyria impounds its lamps after one burns the officer who forced it, and releases them in 1052 by relighting every lamp from the Ember. Eclessia's examinations, honest in Simeon's day, become the means by which Ludovar's sons buy crowns, and the empire is divided by lot. At Strazgorod the Virelian envoys' lamp kindles and Veridun refuses to let it go home, so Virelia goes west. The Two Winters, which nobody can explain, decide nearly everything that follows: they depose the last Adalring emperor, put the Crown of Embers into the Sanctum's vault, make Virelia's crown elective, hang Sermora's chain-tender and empty the Thalmyric frontier that Caedros III resettles with soldier-farmers. Those farmers' lamps kindle in defence of their homes and win the War of the Southern Deeds. Caedros, who believes the Fire watches the throne, leaves his succession to it; it answers a district widow instead, and her district army makes its own emperor. That army's levies, a census stopped by an angel, and fifty years of district burdens make the frontier families open to Suhrab's offer of land without service. Markellos marches to stop his own people accepting it, his thirsty army drinks from a sworn well, and Qarath's Well becomes a battle lost to a contract cut into a well-curb. His regent blinds him to void the ransom with a Covenant doctrine, and the Covenant answers with another. The civil wars end with Nikandros, who did not drink.

**What the reader articles now carry.** More than twenty named saints, sinners and wonders, each with a historical effect: Hedwen's banner, the Standing of the Water, the Lakekeepers, the well-lights, the march without mourners, the Bells, the Judge Beneath the Black Eaves, the Saint of the Two Ferries, the Two Weeping Advocates, the Saint Who Guarded the Enemy Gate, the Lamp Answered Twice, the Virelian Lamp, the purge of the lintels, the Two Winters and the Apology, the Saint of Ashen Wings, the Knockers, the Saint above the Harbour Wall, the Unanswered Funeral, the Threshing Floor angel, the Hunt of 1163, the Walking of the Small, the well-peace at Qarath and the Peace Not Sold.

## The re-caused history

### 1. 962–987: the Fire goes back indoors

Caedros II the Road-Maker inherited a sacred victory and an unfinished war. To hold Caleran and pay for the road-building that let the Reconquest move, he re-endowed the Church: estates returned to monasteries, sees were filled by the court, choir places went to the families that had paid for the return. The refuge leagues had chosen chaplains by service; the restored empire chose them by rank and loyalty. Pass 05's rule then applies with no new metaphysics: the Flame answers the righteousness and purpose of persons, not offices. The war-lamps that had kindled reliably in the 950s, carried by people fighting to protect the defenceless, kindled less often in the 960s and 970s, carried by people fighting to keep what they had been granted. A refuge chronicler's line from the 930s was revived: *in the palace the lamps guttered, and in the passes they burned.*

In 983 Mount Askre, the cone above the Caelmount valley, erupted. Ash lay on Veridun for a season; a lung-fever followed in the crowded highland towns; and the Great Lamp of Veridun, the refuge's lamp under which Caedros I had been acclaimed, shrank to a bead of flame and smoked for forty days. In 984 the young emperor Ilarion Aetherion, Caedros II's son, died of the fever. The army acclaimed Leovar of the Vlastravan march, Caedros II's son-in-law, a veteran who had carried a war-lamp before Caleran at twenty-two and seen it kindle. He took the regnal name of the old oath-holding emperors of the Long Frontier. He had watched his own lamp go dark in the years since, and he read the Omen in the only way his experience allowed: the Fire was refusing the vessels men had made and bought and sold. In 987 he began the First Breaking.

- **Keep:** Leovar III, the Ashfall Omen, the dates 984–1011 and 987, his soldiers' love and the monks' hatred.
- **Bend:** the Omen becomes the opening hinge, and Leovar's theology grows from his own guttered lamp. Iconoclasm's cause (Leo III's volcanic omen and military reverses) is kept as a pressure but re-sourced in the Guttering, so the reader sees a man misreading a real pattern.
- **Add:** Ilarion Aetherion (979–984) fills the canon gap, and his death is the plain contingency that puts a soldier on the throne. Mount Askre is new.
- **Authorial truth:** the Omen was the Guttering's second generation. The eruption and fever were ordinary; the Great Lamp shrank because nobody tending it at that time was under commission. Leovar's reading is his own, not the spine's.

### 2. 974–1002: a king in the east, and the examination

The east had governed itself through bishops since the tax fleet sank in 653, and since Simeon's examination of 711 the eastern governors' oaths had been held at Aurelium "in trust for the emperor who proves lawful" ([[Causality Pass - 05 The Guttering, 400 to 905 AR|Pass 05]], 05.6). By the 970s Grass-Tarkhan riders on Grey Sister's early lines were reaching the eastern roads as well as the Covenant's ([[Causality Pass - 06 The Covenant after Caleran, 905 to 1190 AR|Pass 06]], 06.2), and the bishops' levies could not catch them.

At the ford of Kaltfurt in 974 a raid caught a column of refugees and grain carts. The march chaplain fled. **Hedwen**, a miller's widow who had been walking with the carts, picked up the march banner from the mud and stood with it between the riders and the children in the ford. The banner took the Flame. The riders' horses, which do not shy from bound things, shied from that; the raid broke on the ford. The commander on the bank was Adalmar, a Markish margrave's son. In 975 the marcher lords acclaimed him king of the eastern marches, and within a generation he had joined the bishops' principalities by war, marriage and the register: the bishops gave him an examined law and a single register of land and oath, and he gave them a sword that could reach the raiders.

In 1001 Silvane II's relic court at Aurelium examined the Kaltfurt banner, recorded it **answering**, and credited the sign to the king. Hedwen, by then old, came to Aurelium and testified that the banner had never burned in Adalmar's hand or anyone's since the ford. Her testimony was entered in the register and is struck through in a later hand. In 1002 Silvane crowned Adalmar Emperor of the Atherians, declaring the trust of 711 discharged: the eastern oaths had found the emperor who proved lawful. Veridun's emperor was at that moment breaking every lamp that could have been examined on his behalf. The chronicle's line that Adalmar "knelt surprised" became an Eclessian proverb for feigned innocence.

- **Keep:** Adalmar (975–1019), the 1002 coronation by Silvane II, "knelt surprised", the Two Emperors problem, Anthe's backdated regency as Eclessian apologetic.
- **Bend:** Charlemagne's coronation becomes the discharge of Simeon's trust. The Irene pretext survives only as the apologists' backdating. Adalmar's legitimacy rests on a true kindling that was not his.
- **Add:** Kaltfurt (974) and **Hedwen of Kaltfurt** (d. c. 1010), a Markish saint with a crossed-out testimony. She refused to carry the banner south into the Folk Wars. Her cult is strong among millers and in Markish reform country, which later reads the struck-through line as the first protest against the Sanctum's examinations.
- **Authorial truth:** the banner kindled for Hedwen's purpose at the ford, protecting the column. The Sanctum's examination was honest about the kindling and wrong about its subject: the protocol church's founding verdict (Simeon's) condemned a true relic for the wrong reason, and its imperial verdict crowned a king for a true sign that was not his. Neither church knows both halves.

### 3. 982–1008: the Folk Wars, Wolfsmere and the lake that stood up

The Folk Wars began as a register war. The eastern registers still listed the imperial frontier forts and estates abandoned after 905. Adalmar granted them to his Markish lords. The Green March clans had held that ground for three generations by seer-oath and grove boundary, and had sworn the treaties that bound clan to clan at Wolfsmere, their oath-lake. The registers said one thing and the groves another.

The war ran twenty-six years: settlement drives, punitive expeditions, baptisms at spear-point, treaties, clan civil wars, missionary protection and land seizure. Baptized clans kept returning to their unbaptized kin when called, because the oaths they had sworn at the lake held them to their kin before any later vow. Adalmar's bishops called it relapse. In 993 Adalmar had four thousand captive oath-keepers and seers drowned in Wolfsmere "to drown the oath with its keepers". Nine seers were held under and did not drown; they were pulled out breathing at dusk, pale-eyed and cold-handed, and became the first Lakekeepers.

On a windless night in the winter of 994 the lake stood up. The water rose in a wall and came down on the Lake Watch, the Markish fort Adalmar had built on the shore; the garrison was found in the spring on the ice, and the fort's timbers in the reeds. The nine Lakekeepers went to the water's edge and the lake lay down. The Markish advance never crossed the lake country again, and Eclessia's southern line has run beside it ever since. Weylan Oath-Bent, who had fought Adalmar for a decade before Wolfsmere, fought another decade after it, and around 1005 accepted baptism with his surviving clans. His price was the lake: under the **Peace of the Bent Oak** the march charters forbade any bell within hearing of Wolfsmere, any boat on it and any iron in it, and Adalmar's bishops, whose priests would not go near the shore, agreed. The Folk Wars were declared ended in 1008.

- **Keep:** the war's dates, Wolfsmere's date and dead, the nine and the Lakekeeper line, Weylan and the Bent Oak, the lake-law, the thin space.
- **Bend:** Verden's "relapse" becomes the lake-oath holding baptized clans to their kin. The Saxon wars' end becomes a treaty bought with the lake's sleep.
- **Add:** the register war; the **Standing of the Water** (994) as the reason the frontier stops where it does (Grenzburg sits at that line); the Peace of the Bent Oak.
- **Cut:** any suggestion that Wolfsmere converted the Folk by terror. It hardened them.
- **Authorial truth:** the Drowning made a mass soul-route atrocity at a sacred site, which is why the thin space does not close ([[The Reed Court of Wolfsmere]]). What the lake did in 994, and what the Lakekeepers pay to keep it asleep, stays unexplained in reader prose and is not specified here; the Standing of the Water is a recurrence of the wound, not a Menhir campaign or an act of Folk war.

### 4. 987–1013: the First Breaking, and why Anthe ended it

Leovar's soldiers smashed icon-lamps, melted relic-braziers, broke the army's war-lamps and drove the flame-monks out of the march monasteries. Leovar did not dare break the Great Lamp of Veridun: the army had been acclaimed under it and the city would have risen. He had it hooded in bronze. Households hid their lamps and icons in wells, ovens, walls and granaries ([[Icons, Churches, and Sacred Space]] already records the caches). The exiled monks found shelter in the Velian belt under the old Banu Mizan compacts, where **Ioan of Velia** wrote the *Three Orations on the Housed Fire*; the Covenant's jurists let the Breaking run, reading it as Thalmyria breaching its own covenant (06.4). The bet failed: Ioan's formula ended the Breaking.

The Breaking had two costs Leovar did not foresee. Without war-lamps, the Reconquest's sieges went back to Shell-fire, hired warlocks and starvation, and the province-by-province return slowed to a crawl. And without the flame-monks, who had buried the dead of the return, burial lapsed in the reconquered valleys. In one of them a commander forbade burial altogether, and a corpse-working emissary rebuilt the unburied dead into a road gang. A mother recognised her son's repaired coat among the workers ([[AF-R11 - King of Unburied Hosts]]). The families smashed the fittings and burned the gang; soldiers killed mourners for it. The story travelled faster than any oration.

Leovar III died in 1011, leaving a child, Leovar IV, under the regency of his widow Anthe. In the same year the Covenant's Custodian freed the Vault of Names from its creditor (06.4), and its army was free to move again. Anthe needed an army whose lamps kindled, monks to bury its dead, and the monasteries' grain. In 1013 she summoned the **Council of the Thousand Lamps** to Veridun. The households that had hidden lamps for twenty-six years carried them in from wells and ovens. Some were found still burning when they were lifted out of the wells: the *well-lights*. Leovarist officers said the monks had relit them on the road. The council lifted the bronze hood from the Great Lamp, found it burning low, received Ioan's formula ("the Redeemer had a body; the Fire may have a lamp") and restored the vessels.

- **Keep:** the First Breaking, Ioan, Anthe's regency and the Council's date.
- **Bend:** Irene's motives are replaced by three concrete needs (army, burial, grain) created by the Breaking itself and the Covenant's recovery.
- **Add:** the hooded Great Lamp; the well-lights; the march without mourners (AF-R11, already dated c. 970–1010 "in a minor reconquered valley", promoted as a Breaking consequence).
- **Authorial truth:** the well-lights are left open. Some were relit; whether any burned untended for decades is not ruled.

### 5. 1015: the Split and the Bells

After 1013 the Thalmyric lamps were restored but unexamined. Bonifac III demanded two things of Veridun: acknowledgement of **Simeon's primacy**, the right of the see that examines over the see that keeps, to be exercised first on the Great Lamp itself; and acknowledgement of the Sanctum's trusteeship of the imperial title, which had produced Adalmar's crown. Patriarch Makarios the Adamant would not let an eastern court examine the refuge's lamp, and called Bonifac "the keeper of an empty crown". The legate Hubald of Caldria, received with calculated insult, nailed the bull of anathema to the Great Lamp's stand with four nails, an open reference to the Four Nails of the Pillar. Makarios pronounced the counter-anathema before the nails were cold.

At that hour, by the letters of eleven towns along the watershed border written before the news could have reached them, bells rang with no ringers. Each side's chronicles record the other's bells grieving. A Brauvalis prior wrote that they were struck "from the inside". Border preachers on both sides took the bells as Heaven's verdict on the other church, and a belief grew on both banks that whichever church lifted its anathema first would be confessing that the grieving bells were its own. Neither has lifted it. Covenant courts, which had to assign every Lamp parish in the Velian belt to one communion or the other, keep the oldest surviving record of which parish chose which side (06.4).

- **Keep:** Bonifac, Hubald, the four nails, Makarios, the date, the anathemas never lifted, the Bells as an unexplained event.
- **Bend:** 1054's legate becomes a demand to examine a lamp. The Kindling Question is born at the moment of the Split rather than grafted on.
- **Add:** the consequence of the Bells (neither church can lift first). This is in-world belief, not an explanation.
- **Weird rule:** nothing in this spine explains the Bells. The Saint Who Guarded the Enemy Gate, the Lamp Answered Twice and the Last Common Road do not cause them.

### 6. 1015–1080: the border after the anathemas

The watershed border became a country of rite-changing valleys. Shrines were seized, clergy imprisoned, pilgrims beaten, graves opened in searches for relics, and travellers who had shared a road became mobs on opposite banks. The reader articles carry five episodes:

- **The Saint of the Two Ferries** on the river the Thalmyrics call the Lissos and the Markish the Lessen (SB-P04). A pre-Split ferryman who had carried enemy dead across in both directions was claimed by both banks. In 1022 a mob seized the east-bank shrine and threw the bones into the river; the west bank imprisoned east-bank clergy in reprisal; rival apparitions blessed both. One annual crossing is still made without either church's banners. Authorial: at least one apparition was counterfeit; whether the saint ever manifested stays open.
- **The Judge Beneath the Black Eaves** at Dvorna, a border town with two courts that no longer recognised each other (GS-13). A roof-runner carrying warrants between them began to see crimes before they happened, became the town's examiner, then tortured for crimes not yet committed. Both churches bought Black-Eaves forecasts of the other's future crimes, and preventive persecution hardened the Split for two generations. Two endings are told: the Judge forbade conviction for "the future fact" after the cooper's killings; or let his own assassin in (c. 1080) to prove every prediction fixed. The prohibition became law in both churches' courts, one of very few things they share. Authorial: GS-13 holds both the earthly life and the damned Judge's later activity.
- **The Two Weeping Advocates** (B4-06): two laments written on opposite sides in the voice of a grieving widow-church after real sieges, starvation and rape, which became two dependent Envy Masks directing mourners toward retaliation. Reader prose names the laments and the killings, and says investigators on both sides suspected the images; it does not name the Throne.
- **The Last Common Road** (G-15): already public; linked.
- **The Saint Who Guarded the Enemy Gate** at Grauwacht (UH-06), placed in the war of Ludovar's sons (§7).

- **Keep:** the existing border texture and the War for the Common Saints.
- **Add:** Dvorna, the Lissos/Lessen and Grauwacht as places; dates for the ferry riot (1022).
- **Cut:** nothing; these were all author-only and are now told.

### 7. 1019–1049: Ludovar's silence and the lots of Drossfeld

Adalmar died in 1019. Ludovar the Mild was devout, earnest and recorded **silent** at every examination the Sanctum held for him. He accepted the verdicts with a humility his barons read as weakness. His surviving sons, each wanting the inheritance confirmed in his own name, found bishops who would examine their banners, and banners that would answer: some were honest mistakes, some had Shell-fire scripted into the staff-head. By the 1030s three sons held three examined banners, each certified by a different see, and the examinations that Simeon had made honest because honesty was cheap had become the price of a crown.

The sons' war produced the siege of **Grauwacht** (c. 1040), a mountain fortress holding a grain road and three winter passes in the middle march. Both armies venerated the same early military saint, a physician who had refused to certify tortured prisoners fit for execution; one called him the Physician of the Western Host, the other the Keeper of Captive Soldiers. A Patron had leased the attackers an armoured apparition in the saint's likeness against captured winter stores, and it showed them an old supply postern. Inside, an Envy Mask fed on the garrison's grave-robbing and its sweating campaign icon. During the withdrawal the garrison commander sealed prisoners from both armies, porters and suspected collaborators in a burning granary by the refuse gate. The genuine saint broke the gate's windlass, dropped its iron screen between the captives and a revenge company, carried children out through the ash, and named three tortured messengers missing from both armies' rolls. When soldiers carried the gate key to the command tower it was ordinary iron. Each army kept the part that flattered it.

Ludovar died in 1046. His grandsons Aldwin, Lotharic and Gervin fought for three years. In 1049 at Drossfeld, a thrush-field below the middle-march hills, with their armies drawn up and none able to win, they agreed to draw lots for three portions written on wax tablets and sealed in Adalmar's helmet. Aldwin drew the south with Aurelium and the imperial title; Lotharic the east; Gervin the middle march. The wax of Gervin's lot was smudged along its eastern line before anyone copied it. Every later border suit in the middle march begins with what that smudge covered.

- **Keep:** Ludovar (1019–1046), the sons' rebellions, the three grandsons, 1049, every border claim citing Drossfeld.
- **Bend:** Louis the Pious's penances become silent examinations; the sons' wars become bishop-shopping and counterfeit banners. Verdun's negotiated treaty becomes a drawing of lots, a contingency every later lawyer must argue about.
- **Add:** Grauwacht and the UH-06 episode told; the smudged lot.
- **Authorial truth:** Ludovar was silent because a coronation examination's purpose is office, not protection. He was a good man and the Flame was not withholding approval of him. The genuine saint at Grauwacht is UH-06's; the Patron contract and the Envy Mask are separate counterfeits.

### 8. 1029–1052: the Impounding and the Rekindling

The wayhouse lamp at **Pelin's Bridge**, on the watershed road, had been repaired by its hosts from fittings blessed under both jurisdictions. Around 1020 it kindled while the host sheltered fugitives from a mob, and its fame reached Veridun. In 1029 Leovar IV's Master of Lamps seized it and carried it south to the siege of **Meliton**, one of the last southern amirates, whose garrison was name-bound. Before both armies he forced the lamp against the walls. The fire took him and burned him to death, and maimed the handler who kept on after the first flare. The court did not read the burning as a judgement on forcing the Fire; it read it as proof that lamps were dangerous in private hands. Leovar IV, who had been raised by the woman who restored the lamps, began the **Second Breaking**: every lamp in the empire except the Great Lamp and the Ember was gathered into sealed crown lamp-houses. Thalmyrics call it the Impounding. Monks who refused were flogged and some killed; households went back to the wells and ovens.

The Impounding mirrored what the Covenant had done to the Ember in 905, and Covenant jurists said so. The Pelin's Bridge lamp went back to its wayhouse in the confusion of the 1040s and kindled a second time (c. 1048), under Eclessian custody, while its keepers sheltered wounded from both of Ludovar's grandsons' armies. The two churches each now claim one answer and hold part of the lamp.

Leovar IV died in the winter of 1049 of a fall on the ice of the cathedral steps. His daughter Theodosia succeeded. In 1052 the last southern amirate surrendered to her armies and the heartland's roads were joined. Theodosia opened the lamp-houses, had every lamp in the empire put out at dusk, and had them relit from one flame carried on foot from the Ember at Caleran along the newly joined roads. She walked the first mile herself. The office of Master of Lamps was remade to run the relay. A Covenant jurist's remark that "every lamp in Thalmyria now burns on our receipt" is still quoted on both sides of the frontier.

- **Keep:** the Second Breaking's dates, Theodosia (1049–1071), the Rekindling and its relay, 1052.
- **Bend:** the second iconoclasm becomes an impounding provoked by a burning. Theodora's Triumph of Orthodoxy becomes a relay that ties the Kindling to the one fire even the Covenant could not extinguish.
- **Add:** the Lamp Answered Twice (B4-05) as the Second Breaking's trigger; Meliton; the Master of Lamps' origin.
- **Authorial truth:** the burning was the bounded judgement on a coercive test that B4-05 records. It was not a verdict on the Thalmyric Church, the Kindling Question or the Meliton garrison.

### 9. 1061: Strazgorod and the lamp that stayed

Prince Radomir I of Strazgorod, ruler of the Vesari river princedoms, sent sworn envoys to test four faiths. They refused the Covenant ("a marvel and a cage"; it would hold their souls' names in a vault) and the Path ("a god who wanders owns no land"). In Veridun's Ember-Cathedral the lamp they had carried from Strazgorod took the Flame unbidden before witnesses of both churches, including an examiner of the Sanctum travelling with them, who recorded it answering. The envoys' report: *"We went to see their fire, and it came to see us."* Radomir asked for his lamp. Makarios the Adamant, now over eighty, refused: the Fire had chosen where to dwell, and no hand could move what the Fire had chosen. Radomir, who had been offered an Adalmarid marriage and western swords against the Tarkhan, chose the Sanctum, married Lotharic's daughter and heiress, and their son ruled the Vesari princedoms and Lotharic's eastern kingdom together as Virelia.

Strazgorod made the Lamp Controversy public as the Kindling Question. The Sanctum's own register held a kindling it could not deny in a vessel Veridun would not release; Veridun held a sign that proved the east had been drawn to it and a refusal that had lost the east. Veridun first offered the lamp back in 1103, at Virelia's first election (§10), and has made the offer at every election since.

- **Keep:** the four faiths, the weeping envoys, the lamp kindling unbidden, the lamp in Veridun as honoured hostage, Virelia's western hierarchy and eastern piety.
- **Bend:** Vladimir's choice is caused by Veridun's own theology. The offer to return the lamp becomes a reversal of the 1061 refusal.
- **Cut:** "we knew not whether we stood on earth".
- **Add:** Makarios bookends the Split; Lotharic's heiress resolves Appendix A.

### 10. 1060–1104: the marches, the lintels, the Two Winters and the crown in the box

**The marches.** After Drossfeld the marcher lords of Gervin's and Lotharic's lots pushed south again in the First Southern Crusade (1060–1067), around the lake country, not through it. Folk captives were sent to the silver mines of the middle march.

**The lintels.** Radomir II won the Virelian throne in a war with his kin. The losing houses, Vesari maternal households that painted ancestors' faces above their door lintels and kept the district grain, demanded restitution through the duties of Strazgorod's old protector image (a gate, recognition of those houses, a famine store), and a rival "Elder Face" grew among them. From 1068 to 1102 Radomir II's officers burned the faces, executed keepers and witnesses, took and renamed children and forbade one kin-name; the protector returned with a harder, military face, and its famine store never opened again. This follows [[The Hunger Wars]] ("The Painted Lintels") and adds only the king's name and the consequence for Virelia's crown. Authorial: ID-R10R, an Archmask destroying its own former biography against an Envy rival anchor, which stays protected; its identity is named nowhere public.

**The Two Winters (1101–1103).** Two winters with no summer between; ash-grey skies, no eruption anywhere, Thalmyric volcanoes quiet. The register entry stands and is not explained. Its consequences decide the next century:
- In Virelia the districts emptied by the purge could not feed themselves; Radomir II's sons died in the famine-fever of 1102; the Council of Sabres revived the Vesari moot-right and held the first royal election in 1103. Veridun offered the lamp back to the "eastern candidate", who lost by three sabres.
- In the middle march the lordships without granaries submitted to those with them. Brauvalis's abbeys fed and absorbed their neighbours; Veridancia's towns bought Sestaran grain and took on the first Sestaran debts. Famine drew the Gervinian duchies' map.
- The flagellants walked, and three heresies failed: the **Grey Brethren**, who walked to the watershed to ring the bells by hand and answer them; the **Debtors of the Lake**, Markish penitents who went south to drown themselves in Wolfsmere for Adalmar's debt and were turned back at the shore by the Lakekeepers ("the lake does not want more"); and the **Unkindlers**, who put out every lamp in their villages to starve the false fire and wait for the true one, and dispersed when the harvest came.
- In 1102 Sermora closed its harbour chain against famine ships from the Eclessian coast. A foreign-born chain-tender lowered it; some ships forced the entry, and harbour workers and refugees died in the crush. Sermora hanged him (SB-P05R).
- On the Thalmyric frontier, settlers and Southern House deed-holders alike went north or died, and the frontier land lay empty (§11).
- In the Covenant the Basin's grain failed and the foundry was idled for most of a decade; the Court gave the grey-sky ruling (06.5).

**The crown in the box.** Ermbold, king of Aurelia and titular emperor of Aldwin's line, would not carry the Crown of Embers in a penitential procession through the ash: "It is a hat, not a relic." The other kings met at Drossfeld in the ash-snow of 1102 and deposed him as idle. Nobody could agree on a successor and the crown went to the Sanctum while they argued. In 1103 came the Apology, the finest harvest in a century. In 1104 the kings left the crown in trust at the Sanctum Divinitatis "until the realm is worthy of one head". The Primarchy's partisans dated the harvest from the crown's entry into custody. Farmers called it the Apology and still do.

- **Keep:** the Two Winters as an unexplained weird; 1102; 1104; the trust formula.
- **Bend:** Charles the Fat's deposition becomes a refusal to treat a crown as a relic in a sunless year; the crown's custody begins as a deadlock.
- **Add:** Virelia's elections from 1103; the purge of the lintels (human event only); the three heresies; Sermora's chain-tender; the Two Winters as cause of the Thalmyric military districts.
- **Weird rule:** the Two Winters are a cause here, never an effect. No faction's claim about them is endorsed.

### 11. 1104–1167: the Wall of the World

Caedros III was crowned at Virelos in 1118, a descendant of Theodosia raised in the frontier monasteries. He found the frontier empty: whole valleys abandoned in the Two Winters, including land the Southern Houses still held by deeds under the surrender terms of 957–1052. He resettled it as **military districts**: families received land on condition of service, owing a lance in war and a lamp lit each year from the Rekindling relay. In 1126 the Covenant's Court of Breaches ruled the surrender instruments breached. In 1131 the Covenant served the districts as it had served the north in the Unsealing, with two mercy towers proclaiming terms adapted to local titles (06.5; [[The Covenant from Idris to Suhrab (905-1190 AR)]]).

The terms offered property for surrender. The district families held their land on condition of defence, so surrender meant forfeiture under their own law, and they did not bargain. The towers kept predicting a capitulation that never came and escalated to terror; the terror hardened the districts. The district lamps kindled, carried by people defending their own houses. At **Kolonna** in 1134 Caedros himself dismounted to carry a district family's children out of a burning granary, and the lamp at his saddle took the Flame before the town. The Covenant lost a tower, lost its commanding prince to camp dysentery in 1135, lost a flank on the Day of Two Readings in 1137, and in 1139 ransomed custodians whose registers held the names of its living levies. The ransom refilled Caedros's treasury.

What followed was High Noon. Roads were repaired, aqueduct branches reopened, the district towns filled, monasteries built in stone, Aversian ore and Sestaran credit flowed. Caedros never married. He lived as a soldier-ascetic and believed, after Kolonna, that the Fire watched the throne.

- **Keep:** Caedros III (1118–1167), 1131–1139, the treasury, childlessness, "Wall of the World".
- **Bend:** Basil II's frontier is re-caused by the Two Winters; his military lands become districts owed a lance and a lamp; his victory is the settlers' refusal to bargain and their kindling.
- **Add:** Kolonna (1134); the district lamp; Caedros's belief.
- **Interface:** consistent with 06.5 (the Southern Deeds, the mercy towers, the 1139 ransom).

### 12. 1104–1180: High Noon in the east and the bill under the mountains

- **Korrheim (1116–1124; 1146).** In the silver mines of the Heldenberg, worked by convicts, Two Winters bread-rioters and the children of Folk captives, a courier among the slaves led a revolt through the prison tunnels and freed several mining valleys. Facing the counts' winter reconquest in 1123, he sent condemned prisoners (overseers and guards the rebels' own assembly had sentenced) through **the Cold Way**, a broken passage in the mountain that runs elsewhere for part of its length. Some came back, altered and dangerous; through them the rebels learned the passage, struck the counts' army from behind in the snow and broke it. The leader killed some of the altered survivors with his own hand and hid the experiment. In 1124 the free valleys swore the **Oath of the Shafts**, the first of Korrheim's cantons. Around 1137 the Shaft-Captain confessed before the canton assembly, surrendered office and went into exile; he rescued people from similar experiments and died closing a breach in 1146. He is the **Saint of Ashen Wings** (GS-14). Korrheim keeps the liberation feast and, the next morning, the reading of the experimental victims with black bread made from the feast's ashes ([[G-37 - The Feast Followed by Ashen Names|G-37]]).
- **The Knockers (1110–1170)** in Aversia's high mines (MN-R08, G-68): told as High Noon's ore boom and its dead.
- **The Saint above the Harbour Wall (1166).** Two generations after hanging the chain-tender, Sermora was besieged by hired Ättar keels and a rival's fleet carrying a leased storm-engine. The executed chain-tender was seen above the harbour wall, the engine broke, and the siege failed. Afterwards Sermoran soldiers murdered foreign residents blamed for the breach, and the apparition's rebuke is remembered with the defence. At the feast the harbour chain is lowered for one foreign or refugee vessel before it is raised for the procession (SB-P05R).
- **The Great Southern Purge (1158–1163).** Sylvan marcher lords, the Knights of Saint Aveline and a relic-court ruling that the seers' shrines were unexamined fire led a campaign to destroy seers and shrine settlements; the clans remember it as a campaign against land memory. The marchers cut the boundary groves. In midwinter 1163 riders without breath-smoke went through the marcher camps on three nights; an Aveline chapter was lost, and so were Folk households near the cut groves. The Purge ended. The Elsin tradition's missioners won the argument about Folk policy for a generation. Authorial: a court's Hunt answering broken boundaries; Menhir law is blind to justice and took Folk and marchers alike.
- **Aurelia** became the Primarchs' to rule when Aldwin's line died out in 1166.

- **Add:** Korrheim's founding by GS-14's revolt, the Cold Way, the Oath of the Shafts (1124); Sermora's 1102 execution and 1166 siege; the Purge's end by the Hunt; 1166 for Aurelia.
- **Authorial truth:** GS-14's afterlife (ash-dark wings against the Second Gospel's routes) is not in reader prose beyond the cult title.

### 13. 1167–1209: the Unanswered Funeral, the Widow's Emperor and the Threshing Floor

Caedros III died childless in 1167 and left his succession to the Fire: at his bier in Veridun's cathedral, the claimant whose lamp kindled would rule. The Synod objected and was overruled by a dead man. Seven claimants (nephews and generals) held lamps. None kindled. One flared: a warlock in the choir smelled script-fire under the wick ("Shell-fire smells of hot coin"), and the other claimants blinded the counterfeiter on the cathedral steps, the first imperial blinding in Thalmyric memory. In the nave, among the district honour guard, the lamp of **Despina of Kolonna**, a soldier's widow who had walked to Veridun to pray for her sons on the frontier, took the Flame. The court suppressed it. The district soldiers did not. In the **War of the Seven Lamps** (1167–1169) the district armies decided the issue and acclaimed their own commander, **Andrion**, whom the court called the Widow's Emperor. Despina went home to Kolonna and refused to come back.

Andrion (1169–1198) was the districts' emperor. He extended the district levy and counted district households for it. In Elenara, the river-and-monastery province, the prince used the same years to try for a royal crown of his own. His census (c. 1171–1173) was meant to seize dependants for road labour, list taxable refugees and take hostages from doubtful households; warned that it exceeded the law, he went ahead because a complete register would prove him a king. Clerks and soldiers broke houses and dragged people to the counting stations. A mortality then ran along the census couriers' roads, striking count-houses and barracks within hours of each sealed schedule's arrival. Witnesses saw a dark, immense raptor outline filled with white fire above the road's leading edge. The prince blamed foreigners, then confessed, released the hostages, burned the schedule, and came barefoot to the threshing floor of a household at **Kremna** that his census had already bereaved. The mortality stopped there. Elenara stayed a duchy. Thalmyric and later Eclessian law codes require a census to state its purpose and forbid its use for seizure without a new public act: jurists call the limit "the floor beyond the count" (OBA-N04; [[G-48 - The Threshing Floor beyond the Count|G-48]]).

The district burden grew under Andrion and his son (1198–1209). The **Ransomed Standard** formed c. 1185–1195 to recover bodies and pledged prisoners on the frontier. Around 1201 the **Woman Who Returned the Keys** broke a Velian coalition's siege of a district town by killing its commander herself.

- **Keep:** "the succession scramble he left behind"; the Ransomed Standard; the Keys.
- **Bend:** Basil II's childlessness and his successors' weakness become a theophany test that answers the wrong person and lets the districts make an emperor.
- **Add:** the Unanswered Funeral; Despina of Kolonna; the War of the Seven Lamps; Andrion (1169–1198); the Threshing Floor in Elenara, explaining why Elenara is a duchy in 1360.
- **Authorial truth:** Despina's lamp kindled for her own prayer, not as a sign about the succession; the district armies misread it. The Threshing Floor angel is OBA-N04's Greater Judgement Angel under a bounded commission; the prince's confession did not buy the stopping point.

### 14. 1180–1213: the east under an empty crown; the Unburdened Deeds

- **The Walking of the Small (c. 1180).** In Brauvalis, Caldria and Veridancia, after a local famine, children walked west toward Caleran carrying bread and were turned back at the watershed. Forty did not come home and have no records. The Church ruled it a fever-dream of grief. The register entry stands (sealed). Consequence only: the parents of the forty, refused by their bishops, became the first pilgrims to a weeping Lady at Bruckmoor around 1203, an apparition exposed in 1243 as an Idol-feeding operation (the other writer's span). Eclessian preachers tied the 1183 definition of the Mother to the Walking; Thalmyric polemicists said the Sanctum defined a doctrine to change the subject.
- **The Mother defined (1183, 1204).** The Unkindled Conception defines the Mother as a vessel made pure by grace before she bore the Fire: protocol's answer to Veridun's uncreated Fire that consents to dwell. Veridun rejected the Sanctum's authority to define either doctrine.
- **The crown's condition.** Kings who petitioned the Sanctum to bring the Crown of Embers out were answered with one condition: reunion first, meaning Veridun's submission to examination. This is the price Nikandros will be quoted after 1225.
- **The Unburdened Deeds (1212).** Suhrab's Court of Breaches, harmonising the codes in the *Ledger of Ledgers*, revived the 1126 ruling with a new offer to the district families: confirmation of their land under Covenant deed with no levy owed. After two generations of Andrion's levies, some families accepted and registered at the frontier bench beside **Qarath's Well**.

- **Add:** the Walking's consequence at Bruckmoor; the crown's condition; the Unburdened Deeds. The last touches the Covenant's history after 06; see the patch for the Covenant spine.

### 15. 1214: Qarath's Well

Markellos, Andrion's grandson, acceded in 1209 at twenty-three, a soldier raised in the districts. In the drought summer of 1214 he marched to Qarath's Well to stop his own frontier families accepting the Covenant's deeds. The outer wells on the road had been fouled with carcasses by Tarkhan companies in Covenant pay. Qarath's Well was a sworn well: a bound warden, and a well-peace cut into its curb in three scripts, Atherian among them. Whoever drank as a guest was under the well's peace until the next sunset and could not lift a weapon against the host. The officers who could read took the inscription for a dedication. The army drank at dusk.

At dawn, when the drinkers took up their arms against the Covenant line, the water in them turned: men vomited water, their arms hung like wet rope, some drowned standing. The Kedravan monastic contingents had not drunk, because the refuge monasteries' rule since the occupation forbade drinking sworn water. Their commander, **Nikandros**, of a cadet Aetherion branch in Kedrava, formed them up and withdrew in order with the wounded he could carry. The Tarkhan companies on Grey Sister's lines, whose horses do not shy from bound things, rode through what was left. Markellos stayed with the men who had drunk and was taken at sunset on the second day. Suhrab received him with elaborate contract-courtesy and ransomed him for the military districts that had been settled on the Southern Houses' land: the 1126 ruling enforced eighty-eight years late (06.5). The Ransomed Standard recovered the dead from the salt flats under truce.

- **Keep:** 1214, the capture, courteous captivity, the ransom in military districts, Suhrab's apex.
- **Bend:** Manzikert's battle is lost to a contract cut into a well-curb; the withdrawing reserve did not betray the emperor but obeyed a monastic rule that saved half an army; the captive emperor chose to stay.
- **Add:** the drought, the fouled wells, the well-peace, the Kedravan rule, Nikandros at Qarath.
- **Authorial truth:** the well-peace is lawful contract magic (consent by drinking after posted notice) enforced by a bound warden. The monks' rule was prudence learned under the occupation's name-vaults and sworn wells, not a kindling. The Fire is not said to have left the army.

### 16. 1214–1225: the wound made at home

Markellos came home without the districts. The regency council under his uncle **Sergian** blinded him in the Crown Hall at Veridun, beneath the Great Lamp and the Virelian Lamp. Sergian's jurists argued that an emperor made unfit for office widowed every instrument he had sworn, the Covenant's own doctrine of 931 turned against it. Within the season the Court of Breaches answered that the ransom was sworn to the Throne, which does not widow, and cited the Sublime Threshold and the Ember deposit of 957. Covenant garrisons occupied the districts in 1215. Markellos died of the wound-fever within the month. The blinding bought nothing but shame.

The civil wars ran 1214–1225 among Sergian's regime, the dispossessed district lords, the Kedravan monasteries and Nikandros, the Saronikan admiralty and the Elenaran duke.
