## Proposals

### 03.1 The Year the Portrait Spoke (247 BR)

- [x] Approved with changes — the age's best world-native turn, kept whole; the household that would not kneel joins the searches, Astaroth's priests keep the provincial copies, and no second copy speaks in 247.

**Decision (14 November 2026).** The Face is a painted accession panel in the audience hall at Caleran. The Celebrants of the Veil keep it through 0 AR. Astaroth's priests keep the provincial copies and the army's portrait standards, so the governor at Qiryath travels with a copy and an augur of the Red Star. The household of `atlas-b3-04` is placed in the searches of 247. Written into [[The Year of Four Eagles]], [[The House of Thalion]], [[The House of Veloran]], [[The Atherian Empire – Age of Glory]] (the public telling of the Emperor after the Emperor) and [[Star Religion and the Imperial Cult]] (author-only).

**What happens.** Olarion dies with no recognised heir. In the first days of the vacancy the accession portrait in the palace audience hall at Caleran answers questions in the voice Caleran expects of "the Emperor". Every emperor since Meren has been confirmed before this Face. Avaren Mott, who holds the senate and part of the guard, governs through it. Decrees are read before it, the guard swears in its presence, and it addresses him as the magistrate governing in the Emperor's name. Within its sight obedience is fervent. Beyond the walls it counts for nothing, and Mott learns the difference only when Veloran and Halven stop the grain. Asked where the grain is, the portrait answers as an emperor must: no granary of the Emperor is empty, and those who say otherwise are hiding it. Mott orders the searches. The portrait is carried ahead of the search parties so that households kneel before the soldiers enter, and the demonstrators outside the inner market die in its sight.

Samor Veloran's river army has never stood before it. He promises the guard its arrears and an end to the denunciations that Olarion's informers had sworn before the same image. In the audience hall he has the Face covered with a soldier's cloak before he speaks to the guard, and he refuses to be addressed by it. Mott, running for the hall as his sanctuary, is killed at its threshold by guardsmen. Caleran's accounts disagree over whether the cloak had fallen before the blow, and that question stays open.

**Why.** A century of standardised imagery and Olarion's loyalty rites before the portrait had fed the dependent Pride Mask; at his death the office had no living face to carry that praise, and the Mask answered on its own. Mott needed a claim beyond the senate. Samor's army was loyal to its pay, and his promise against denunciations was a promise against the image.

*Authorial ruling.* The Mask acts where its image is seen and addressed, intensifying obedience to the represented office. It knows nothing of stores or armies; the grain answer was the expected imperial sentence. Covering one image interrupts its pull in that room without starving it. Olarion, Thalion and each claimant remain distinct persons with complete souls; the Mask served whoever stood beside it.

**What it changes downstream.**
- **The veil.** Samor's first law keeps the Face veiled except at accessions and on the founding anniversary, and makes questioning it a capital offence. Custody passes to the Common Sky's First Celebrant, whose college had refused Mott's rites, and away from Astaroth's ceremonial priests, who had performed them. The Celebrants of the Veil (proposed name) become a check on the Red Star's precedence.
- **Acclamation order.** The Face is unveiled last, after the armies and senate agree. 151 is the first full test (03.3).
- **The appetite moves outward.** With the central image covered, the Mask feeds through copies in governors' courts, town halls and army standards.
- **Memory.** The plays' "talkative magistrate" is the magistrate who talked with the portrait.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The Year of the Four Emperors (AD 69). Kept: four constituencies, and grain decides. Bent: the capital's claimant governs through a speaking image, whose assurance causes the searches. The victor's famous act is a refusal.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** **The Year the Portrait Spoke (247 BR — the Year of the Four Emperors).** TORQUE: in the empty succession the accession portrait at Caleran answered in the Emperor's voice, the first autonomous response of the dependent Pride Mask later called the Emperor after the Emperor. Mott governed through it, and its assurance that no imperial granary was empty sent soldiers behind the carried image into the poor districts. Samor Veloran had the Face covered with a soldier's cloak and would not be addressed by it. Since then the Face has stayed veiled in the Common Sky's custody, unveiled last at an accession, and the Mask feeds through provincial copies. Whether the cloak fell before Mott died is disputed. ### 03.2 The Threshold Law: Astaroth's tests as an imperial institution

- [x] Approved with changes — cause kept, engine retold through the land: the Star's share, the sound of stone under load and the official who stands under the arch first; liability clauses, priced contracts and "test-warden" procedure are cut.

**Decision (14 November 2026).** The fallen building is the new treasury hall on the forum at Caleran. The testers are simply *wardens*, rune-trained engineers with a priest of the Red Star beside them; the Weird register's "wardens' records" of the Sleepwalkers' Summer are theirs. Sending a slave or a condemned man to stand under in an official's place is the Red Star's taboo of the substituted victim, and builders tell what happens then. Under Direction 2 Astaroth's answers to builders are standing practice, so the rite does not lapse after 292 AR. The Arcelon rule survives as the road crews' "tested in the dry". No warden's record comments on the Via Somnia.

**What happens.**
- **Before 326 BR.** Military engineers have used the Red Star's test since Darneth (380 BR): a valuable frame is loaded to failure under the rite, and the crack shows where force will carry. Thalion's elevation of Astaroth's priesthood adds a ceremonial practice in which priests dedicate new public works and ask the Red Star that they stand.
- **326 BR.** A new treasury hall at Caleran (proposed placement) is dedicated but never tested. It gives way during Caelor's inspection. The heir is crushed and dies two years later; workmen and enslaved labourers die with him. The engineers say what the priests will not: the dedication asked the Red Star for strength without trial.
- **The law.** Thalion cannot disown the priesthood on which his victory rites rest, so he divides its work.
  - No one may enter a public work before a witnessed test.
  - A test-warden (proposed term), a rune-trained engineer, performs the test, with a priest of Astaroth as witness. Dedication follows the test.
  - Every contract prices the breaking portion (proposed term): a span, member or valuable fitting built to be broken.
  - An official who opens an untested work answers for the dead with his property and office. A warden who records a false test answers with his life.
- **274 BR.** Valeran's aqueduct needs a breaking span in each run of arches. The managers extend compulsory quarry service through the harvest to cut stone "for the Star to break", as the strikers put it, and troops kill the strikers. Valeran keeps both the assessment and the test: a house that had lost its heir to an untested hall would not open an untested aqueduct.
- **Arcelon, c. 190–182 BR.** The embankment passes a dry-season test under loaded wagons, then rain loosens it onto the labourers. The repair a generation later learns the groundwater from the workers' song. Thereafter a test names its load and season, and discharges liability only for what it tested: the Arcelon rule, or in the road crews' words, taking the second road.
- **Velen I.** The palace exempts the temporary banks of the river spectacles as festival works, overruling the wardens because the works are the Emperor's and the Face is unveiled there (03.3). The banks fail and drown workers and spectators. Doran I abolishes the exemption.

**Why.** Astaroth reveals the weakness of whatever is genuinely submitted to force. His bargain grammar forbids demanding victory without resistance, and substituting a helpless victim for the named contestant. He is morally blind: who dies follows from human choices about what is tested and who pays. Thalion turned his heir's death into a law that blamed no priest.

*Authorial ruling.* The 326 failure follows from the dedication's breach of Astaroth's taboo. A structure asked to stand without trial is forced to prove itself. That is Menhir law, not punishment.

**What it changes downstream.**
- **A profession (Commandment 6).** The test-wardens form a college from the rune schools, and their registers become evidence in court. Imperial religion now has three authorities over proof: the Red Star's priests keep the victory rites, the wardens keep the tests, and the Celebrants keep the Face.
- **A legal habit.** The Arcelon rule, that a test answers only the question asked, is the Atherian ancestor of 04's principle that a sign proves only what it proves.
- **Pride against test.** Velen's exemption is the first time the Face's claim to stand beyond judgement overrides a test.

**Analog bent or replaced.** The amphitheatre collapse at Fidenae (AD 27) and the senate's building rules that followed. Bent: the founder's heir dies, the cause is a cultic breach of Menhir law, and the remedy's price causes the next disaster.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** **The Threshold Law (from 326 BR — Fidenae).** TORQUE: Caelor died under a hall dedicated to the Red Star but never tested; under Astaroth's law a work asked to stand without trial is forced to prove itself. Thalion made the test compulsory: a test-warden breaks a priced portion of every public work before anyone enters it, and the official who opens an untested work answers for the dead. The breaking spans drove the quarry strike of 274; Arcelon's embankment, tested dry and failed wet, fixed the rule that a test answers only the question asked; Velen's exempted banks drowned his audience. Astaroth remains morally blind; the law decides who pays. [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/10 Second Circle/Astaroth.md|Astaroth]]

### 03.3 Four pressures, 151 BR–0 AR

- [x] Approved with changes — all four pressures kept and told through the land; Raveth's towns were tributaries under the Avesh command's canal duty before they joined; a fever summer is added to 151 as contingency.

**Decision (14 November 2026).** Raveth joins by negotiation c. 160 BR. The hunger is dated "from 119": two failed harvests, a fair third shipped out, blight, and relief wound down by 113, with no seven-year shape. The fever summer of 151 kills Orent II, thins the river camps and spares Elian's eastern army; crowds bring their sick to the doors of the audience hall and the Celebrants refuse to lift the cloth. The veiled oath survives to 0 AR; its end belongs to 04 (Tervan's edict of 245 AR is recommended).

**What happens.** The period gains four long-running pressures, each with a start, a turning point and an end state; 03.5 narrates them.

**(a) Crown estates: Raveth bent away from Joseph.**
- **Start.** Raveth's towns join the empire by negotiation late in Orent II's reign, after the Arcelon road pulls their trade west. Their first assessment is set in a good year.
- **An integration famine.** The road levy takes the men who kept the flood channels under the old Vasharan canal duty. Two harvests fail, a fair third is shipped out under contract, and blight follows. Public text: "the Raveth hunger, from 119 BR", relief ending by 113, with no seven lean years.
- **Turning point and torque.** Neriah's releases are countersigned by local witnesses. When the debt conversion is extended, Lethara's officers replace the countersignature with an oath before the emperor's image, and Neriah resigns rather than administer it. Countersigned households argued and kept part of their land. Households sworn before the Face walked their animals to the estate offices unasked: "the loan before the Face".
- **End state.** A crown estate across the eastern basins, worked by resisting dependants, and a memory that blames the image as well as the Jirahar administrator.

*Authorial ruling.* The Mask intensified compliance where debts were sworn before its image. It created no debt and chose no policy. Neriah advised the estate, Elian II authorised it, and Lethara's officers chose the oath.

**(b) Elian I's citizenship grant.**
- **Motive.** Citizenship for council households is the eastern cities' price for Elian's recognition. He needs those households to staff Raveth's assessments and the Arcelon road and to carry council costs: hence "able to meet public obligations".
- **The oath.** Each new citizen swears before the emperor's image, binding the grant's holders to the office rather than to a provincial emperor. Jirahar scribes, who will not swear before an image, are allowed Samor's precedent and swear with the copy veiled: the veiled oath (proposed term). The house of Qoreth gains citizenship this way, and its chancery service is the "family's imperial service" behind Sha'il bar Qoreth's citizenship.
- **Effects.** Thousands of oaths before provincial copies; townsmen who cannot meet the obligations shut out; and citizen pilgrims a growing share of Qiryath's donors, so that Atheria has citizens to protect inside a Vasharan sanctuary.

**(c) The Mask's appetite** begins with the veil of 247, grows through citizenship and debt oaths and towns' costlier portraits, and turns when Velen I unveils the Face at his spectacles, removes popular commanders and exempts his festival works from test. Doran re-veils the Face, and his officers enter by the low door (03.7). At 0 AR the Face in Caleran is quiet, the provincial copies are hungry, and governors hear cases beneath the image, so a claim to kingship offends the office it represents. The Jirahar, with their veiled oath and imageless sanctuary, are the empire's most visible refusers of the Face.

**(d) The eastern frontier.** Orvaz II keeps Avesh, the Ubar and Qiryath in 350. The Arcelon road (c. 190–182) is Atheria's answer to the Dawn Road (03.6). Raveth joins by negotiation, and the Withheld Flame opens Qiryath (03.4). At 0 AR Vashar is a sovereign peer that regards Qiryath as leased.

**The vacancy of 151.** Orent II dies of fever without an heir, and three things hold the commanders still:
- Samor's division of the river army means no single commander holds every approach to Caleran, so a seizure needs several district commands to agree.
- The veil law means no claimant can be acclaimed by the Face before the others consent.
- 247 is still family memory in the poor districts and the senate.

The negotiation chooses Elian Serrat and sets his price, and the Face is unveiled last.

**Why.** The review found a frozen golden age with one formula per reign. Two of these pressures turn on world-native causes (the oath before the Face, the veil); two are human and fiscal.

**What it changes downstream.** 0 AR gains a dependent countryside that blames an image, Jirahar scribes who are Atherian citizens, hungry provincial copies and a leased sanctuary.

**Analog bent or replaced.** Joseph (Genesis 47), bent by the integration famine and the oath against the countersignature. Caracalla's grant (AD 212), whose tax motive becomes a negotiated price with an oath. Nerva, Commodus and Severus, bent by the river commands, the veil and the exemption.

**Draft Torque Register entry.** **The Loan before the Face (Raveth, from 119 BR — Joseph in Egypt).** TORQUE: where relief debts were sworn before the emperor's image, the dependent Pride Mask intensified compliance. Those households gave up land and animals with an obedience their countersigned neighbours never showed. Raveth remembers its dispossession through the image as well as through the Jirahar administrator, who refused to administer the oath and resigned.

### 03.4 The Charter of Referral: Qiryath passes from Vashar to Atheria

- [x] Approved with changes — renamed **the War of the Two Dawns** (Direction 4) and told through the cistern stair, the silent court and the exile caravans; reader prose says "the Qiryath charter".

**Decision (14 November 2026).** Two Tiravan claimants each held a dawn audience; the keepers of Daryam carried the royal flame to the Ruzhan-backed claimant and left the one holding Kavresh to rise in lamplight. Reader prose calls the dead office-holder the sanctuary's Keeper; whether the Keepership of the Upper Room continues is 01's call. The purchaser's house, the interim Keeper, the governor at Arcelon and the Qoreth scribe stay unnamed. Isfir's birth town lies among the towers the charter protects. Author-only working dates: the Keeper's fall c. 82 BR, the trial c. 81, the charter c. 80, the Vasharan treaty c. 74.

**What happens.**
- **Vashar's crisis.** Late in Cassen I's reign a Tiravan succession splits Vashar. The keepers of Daryam carry the royal flame (an ordinary lamp that opens a reign) to the Ruzhan-backed claimant and withhold it from the claimant holding Kavresh: the Withheld Flame (proposed name). The victor purges the Avesh road command, which backed the loser, and sells confirmations of offices in the western sanctuaries to raise silver.
- **The vacancy and the sign.** Qiryath's Keeper of the Upper Room dies in a fall. The council names an interim Keeper from the tower houses, whose pilgrims are now largely Atherian citizens; a river-quarter house in Vasharan road service buys the confirmation. At the interim Keeper's pilgrimage offering, before the purchaser arrives, the sign of acceptance is given and recorded. The purchaser then takes the altar with a Vasharan escort. One side says heaven answered its Keeper; the other says the sign answers the people's offering, whoever stands at the altar.
- **The Trial before the Stars (proposed name).** The tower houses appeal to the Atherian governor at Arcelon in letters a Qoreth scribe, Ezrahel's grandfather (unnamed), writes in the chancery's forms. The governor's augur proposes the Atherian remedy: test the claim, with each claimant offering and the sign deciding. The purchaser refuses to make the Lord answer a foreigner's question. The interim Keeper offers, and no sign is given. The governor rules on the sanctuary's written record and the purchaser's refusal, so the record, which the Qoreth scribes kept, carries the day.
- **The charter**, drafted by the Qoreth scribe:
  - sacrifice, communal courts and burial jurisdiction are protected, including in the province's tower communities;
  - no image enters the sanctuary;
  - the council chooses the Keeper and the governor confirms him;
  - an offering is made daily on behalf of the emperor;
  - the governor holds capital jurisdiction over offences against imperial rule, and sits at the pilgrimage, with an augur for public rites, to hear cases the elders refer;
  - the sanctuary's dues stand surety for the tribute and the payment to Vashar, and arrears may be taken from its treasury;
  - one copy lies in each archive, sanctuary and chancery, and disputes go to Caleran.
- **Vashar's settlement.** Early in Velen I's reign the victor accepts the charter by treaty for the Dawn Portion (proposed name), a yearly share of pilgrim dues paid to Avesh. Vasharan scribes record a lease; Atherian scribes a cession.
- **The losers go east** to Jirahar communities in Vasharan territory, carrying their account of the Trial.

**Why.** A contingent death; a Vasharan civil war and its need for silver; Qiryath's revenue shifting west after Elian's grant; a scribal family fluent in two archives; and the sign.

*Authorial ruling.* The sign proved that one offering was accepted, and chose no Keeper. Its absence at the trial proves nothing either: lawful offerings are not made to answer a governor's question, and grace is not given on demand. It went on answering ordinary offerings under the confirmed Keeper and under priests of the defeated houses who stayed.

**What it changes downstream.**
- **0 AR.** Every step of Isfir's trial uses the charter: the governor at the pilgrimage, the augur (Severan), the referral, the threat to complain to Caleran, and the elder's petition for the body. Ezrahel, Lord-Scribe and keeper of the sanctuary copy, knows the terms better than any governor. His fear for "the towers' protections" is fear for his family's instrument.
- **61–68 AR.** The surety clause becomes the governor's "pretext of arrears", and the offering for the emperor is what the militants stop. Their claim that the sign will return when foreign rule ends has its precedent in the Trial. The refugees of 65 go to communities founded by the charter's losers.
- **Vashar.** The Dawn Portion ends in 65 AR. Vasharan claims to Qiryath's revenue join the grievances behind the war of c. 94–103 AR.

**Analog bent or replaced.** Pompey's arbitration of the Hasmonean succession (63 BC), the sacrifice for the emperor, and Florus's seizure of the Temple treasury (AD 66). Bent: there is no siege and no general enters the sanctuary. The decision turns on a refused trial and a scribal record, and the rival empire leases rather than loses. The author-only working date, c. 85–80 BR, stays clear of 63. Public text says "under the late Serrats".

**Draft Torque Register entry.** **The Trial before the Stars and the Charter of Referral (under the late Serrats — Pompey and the Hasmoneans).** TORQUE: the sign of acceptance was given at an interim Keeper's offering while a rival held a Vasharan purchase of the office. The Atherian governor's augur demanded that the sign decide between them. One claimant refused the trial; the other offered, and the sign was not given. The governor ruled on the sanctuary's written record. The charter drafted by a Qoreth scribe governed Qiryath until 65 AR: an imageless sanctuary, an offering for the emperor, referral of capital cases, the dues as surety, and copies in two archives. Its clauses returned in 0 AR and 61 AR. Vashar took the Dawn Portion and called it rent.

### 03.5 The bridge, 151 BR–0 AR: the world Isfir is born into

- [x] Approved with changes — the reader version is written into [[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]], led by the pressures; the author-only narrative moves into the Age of Glory control review; the charter gets no article of its own.

**Decision (14 November 2026).** The Last Dynasties grows to about 2,700 words. [[Age of Glory - Authorial History and Integration Review]] carries the narrative with its exact author-only dates.

**What happens.** *Author-only narrative. Exact years are kept for 151, 119, 61, 33 and 0.*

**The vacancy.** Orent II died of a fever in 151 with no son and no named heir, and for some weeks the empire had no quick way to make an emperor. Samor's division of the river army meant every approach to Caleran answered to a different district command, each fed from different country. A commander who marched first would march without the others' grain. The Face hung veiled in the audience hall, and the Celebrants would lift the cloth only after the senate and the armies agreed. In the poor districts old people still told how the portrait had been carried ahead of the search parties. Senators whose grandfathers had sat with Mott did not want to be seen near it. So the commanders talked.

The eastern cities came to the talks richer than ever. Raveth's towns had lately joined the empire after the Arcelon road pulled their trade west, and the eastern councils now handled grain, tolls and garrison contracts on two frontiers. They named their price: citizenship for the households that ran their councils, courts and roads. Elian Serrat commanded the eastern army and came from such a household, so he could pay it. The Face was unveiled last, before a ruler already agreed, and every later accession kept that order.

**The grant and its oath.** Elian kept the promise early in his reign and shaped it to his needs. Citizenship went to officers, teachers and municipal families able to meet public obligations: the people who would sit on councils, collect Raveth's new assessments, keep the Arcelon stations and pay for festivals the treasury could no longer fund. Each new citizen swore before the emperor's image in a governor's court or town hall, so that a provincial emperor's grant bound its holders to the office rather than to him. The eastern negotiators carried one exception. Jirahar scribes and interpreters, a large part of the class Elian needed, would not swear before an image. They were allowed Samor's precedent and swore with the copy veiled. The house of Qoreth, scribes in the eastern chancery, became citizens on those terms. Townsmen who had walked in the same processions but could not meet the obligations watched their neighbours enter the courts and the contracts.

The oath multiplied. Every town that wanted standing commissioned a better portrait for its hall. The Mask that had first spoken at Caleran was now fed in a hundred halls, by thousands of small acts of obedience to a ruler beyond judgement.

**Raveth.** Raveth's first assessment had been fixed in a good year. The road levy took the men who had cleaned the flood channels under the old Vasharan canal duty, and the channels silted. Two harvests failed. The third was fair, and the managers shipped it out under contract. Then blight took the fields, and the last harvest was thin. From 119 the province starved.

Elian II gave the relief to Neriah bar Sorek, a Jirahar store examiner who carried the measures of three provinces in his head. He bought grain, requisitioned boats, opened military stores and had every release countersigned by local witnesses. Relief went out as debt. Boats reached the river towns first, and hill villages died waiting for the pack trains.

When the worst had passed and the debts could not be paid, Neriah advised one estate large enough to repair the channels, and Elian approved it. Lethara's officers ran it, and they replaced the neighbours' countersignature with an oath before the emperor's image at the store. Neriah would not administer that oath and resigned. Where debts had been countersigned, households argued, delayed and kept part of their land. Where they had been sworn before the Face, families walked their animals to the estate office unasked. Later writers who blamed the Jirahar Keeper for everything could never explain why Raveth also blamed the image.

**Cassen's prosperity.** Cassen I, Lethara's son and Elian II's adopted heir, inherited the estate and its officials. Its revenue built baths and theatres in the central cities. Its dependants resisted with unworked labour days, burned seed stores and absconding families. Raveth's grain reached the garrisons through estate contracts, so towns like Lacaris paid in prices what Raveth had paid in land. The Lacaris riot, from which six servants climbed into office, belongs to this economy.

**Vashar breaks.** Late in Cassen's reign the Vasharan crown passed in dispute. The keepers of Daryam carried the royal flame to the claimant backed by the Ruzhan horse houses. They withheld it from the one who held Kavresh, whose mirrors, his enemies said, showed him nations kneeling. The war of the Withheld Flame lasted years. The road command at Avesh chose the losing side. The victor purged it, pulled garrisons east and, needing silver, sold confirmations of offices across the western sanctuaries.

**Qiryath.** In those years Qiryath's Keeper fell on the cistern stair and died. The council named an interim Keeper from the tower houses, whose pilgrims now came mostly from Atherian provinces and were largely citizens. Their gifts paid for much of the sanctuary's upkeep. A river-quarter house long in Vasharan road service bought the confirmation. Before the purchaser arrived, the interim Keeper made the pilgrimage offering, and the sign was given and recorded. A month later the purchaser took the altar with a Vasharan escort.

The tower houses wrote to the Atherian governor at Arcelon, in letters a Qoreth scribe drafted in the chancery's own forms. The governor came to the next pilgrimage with an augur of Astaroth. The augur proposed what Atherian law did with disputed claims: test them. The purchaser refused to make the Lord answer to a foreigner's question. The interim Keeper made the offering, and no sign was given. The governor took his decision from the sanctuary's record of the earlier sign and from the purchaser's refusal. The Qoreth scribe drafted the charter that followed. His house kept the sanctuary copy for three generations and trained its sons in both archives.

The purchaser's house, and the river-quarter families who would not serve under the charter, went east into Vasharan country. They told the story of the Trial before the Stars in the towers that took them in. At Qiryath the sign went on answering the appointed offerings, under the confirmed Keeper and under priests of the defeated houses who stayed. Each side cited it.

**Velen and the Face.** Early in Velen I's reign the Tiravan victor, still fighting in Ruzhan, accepted the charter by treaty in return for the Dawn Portion, paid yearly to Avesh. Vasharan scribes wrote "lease"; Atherian scribes wrote "cession". The palace scarcely noticed. Velen ruled through a narrow circle and removed commanders whose popularity alarmed him. He had the Face unveiled at his river spectacles, so that crowds acclaimed image and emperor together. The banks built for those displays were exempted from test as festival works, and the wardens were overruled because the works were the Emperor's. The banks failed and drowned workers and spectators. In 61 his guards killed him, expecting a reward from one of his relatives. The relatives divided the commands and argued.

**Doran.** Doran, governor of the central military district, moved first because the road stores and the capital's armour workshops declared for him; two of the Lacaris servants held them. He punished the assassins, re-veiled the Face and abolished the festival exemption. His officers entered their commands through a low door, stripped of insignia and carrying a bowl, instead of swearing before the image as Velen's guards had. He paid his supporters with southern farms, reopening claims that older surveys had seemed to settle. His daughter Mira married Othran, of an eastern house that knew the charters. As Othran I he governed from the provinces and kept away from the audience hall.

**33 BR.** Isfir was born in a Jirahar tower town of the eastern provinces, whose communal court sat under the charter's protection of the towers. The governor's hall in the nearest city held an imperial portrait, and much of the farmland around the town was crown estate. Imperial roads ran past the tower. Jirahar citizens swore with the image veiled. The sign was given each year at Qiryath, and Vashar collected the Dawn Portion and called it rent.

**Othran II and 0 AR.** Othran II succeeded in 24 BR to a state that worked. Its estates fed the cities, its eastern citizens staffed the courts, and its provincial portraits were hungrier than the Face in Caleran had been since 247. Lawyers in every province knew that a test answered only the question asked. At Qiryath, Ezrahel bar Qoreth, Lord-Scribe and keeper of the sanctuary copy, knew the charter better than any governor. When a teacher from the towers drew crowds at the pilgrimage, every procedure Ezrahel needed already existed:
