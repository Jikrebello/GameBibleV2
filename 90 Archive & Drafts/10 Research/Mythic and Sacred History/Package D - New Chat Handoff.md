## Package D's selected architecture

### Peoples and geography

The Folk and Ättar are **Orphaned peoples**. Their principal ancestors survived the Giant collapse outside the Glass Ark. Their old populations include settled southern communities, human subjects of several Giant states, rebels, fugitives, fishing populations and people displaced from the polar country. Later migration and captivity add further ancestry. They possess no single founding ancestor, exodus column or ancient united nation.

The reconstructed name **Fäskogarfolk** may describe an old language-and-custom continuum. It should never function as the name of a pure race or lost state. Package D requires period self-names for actual peoples and coalitions.

Three overlapping Folk constellations should be developed:

1. **Western and Atlantic Folk** inhabit western coasts, islands, estuaries, hills and river corridors. Their history draws selectively upon continental Celtic, Brittonic and Goidelic comparisons. Kindreds, fosterage, learned houses, cattle wealth, hill towns, island lordships and dangerous Menhir relationships are central.
2. **Middle Forest Folk** inhabit woodland, marshes, river crossings and northern approaches. Germanic comparisons contribute farm districts, assemblies, retinues, compensation customs, longhouses, wetland offerings, war leaders and relationships with Atherian auxiliaries.
3. **Eastern River and Upland Folk** inhabit eastern forests, cold shores, rivers, mountain valleys and connecting passes. Carefully separated Slavic and Baltic comparisons contribute villages, gords, assemblies, princely followings, mixed farming, ovens, beekeeping, river trade, household spirits, epic strongmen and the war-voice schools.

These constellations are centres of historical gravity. Customs and people cross their edges. “Celtic”, “Germanic”, “Slavic”, “Baltic”, “Reachman” and “Nord” remain source descriptions and never become World Egg ethnonyms.

The **Ättar** emerge gradually along the coldest coasts and protected valleys from populations closely related to the Middle Forest and western maritime worlds. Winter stores and halls, boats, Grobi pressure, the War of the Empty Halls and Mother Moon's bargain produce the historical separation. The four hereditary pact-kindreds arise around the accepted **c. 1600 BR** bargain. They did not exist from the beginning of southern settlement.

### Civilisation and material life

Package D should reject the premise that southern peoples lack civilisation because their monuments differ from Caleran stone cities. Their history should include timber halls rebuilt on old footprints, earthworks, cliff forts, ringworks, winter boathouses, shipyards, harbour barriers, mounds, weapon deposits, assembly stones, mining landscapes, charcoal woods, river diversions, markets, gords and occupied Giant ruins.

Wet ground, fire, Grobi warfare and later Atherian preferences explain the incomplete survival of this material. The southern peoples still possess agriculture, fleets, law, craft, learned traditions, humour, pleasure and political ambition.

### The polar Giant crownland

Package D should develop a **lesser southern Giant penal crownland**, comparable in depth to Arsuvan and Vathra without becoming a sixth Imperial Horizon. Several Giant states use its ring-fortresses, mines, geothermal estates, harbour prisons and warded roads. Its inhabitants include exiled God-Kin, condemned Giant rebels, human slaves, skilled deportees, older southern communities and client households profiting from confinement.

During the Great Unbinding, Giant gaolers and prisoners recruit from the cells, execute hostages and fight for heatworks and supplies. Human labour communities withhold food and fuel, occupy tunnels and destroy causeways. Some Giant rebels restore slavery after promising freedom. Ring walls split, mines burn, heated galleries flood or freeze and evacuation ships abandon dependants.

At least one fortress becomes the setting of the **First Rewriting**. The Crownland, its houses, settlements and human revolt leaders remain unnamed. Their development is the first required Package D history.

### Grobi

Existing canon controls Grobi metaphysics:

- Wrath performs the First Rewriting.
- The human souls of the first victims separate and enter the proper judgement road.
- Modern Grobi possess `wrath-rewritten-life` and reproduce as a true living lineage.
- Their five food-driven bodily stages remain bodily stages rather than species or castes.
- Individual Grobi can suffer, reason, choose, die and bear responsibility.
- They are neither dependent Wrath Masks nor humans with damaged souls.
- Pale-Skins and Hollow-Kings retain independent origins.

Giant experimentation may assemble victims, select bodies and create the conditions for mass violence. It does not author the new continuity. Wrath hijacks the undertaking.

The principal missing subject is **Grobi civilisation**. Package D needs connected histories of child-rearing, food shares, rationing, adoption, desertion, captives, craft, hide and metal work, stage-gland medicine, drum communication, religion, resistance to Wrath, market truces, winter refuges, settled occupations and rival political ambitions. Warhammer supplies energy, size-and-prestige loops, gathering war-hosts, specialists, exuberance and the rebellion of a weapon against its makers. Fungal reproduction, inherited technical knowledge, species-wide psychic unity and the claim that belief makes all machinery function were rejected.

The War of the Empty Halls requires named Grobi actors with intelligible aims. Grobi remain dangerous neighbours and historical agents rather than a faceless recurring wave.

### War-voice

The **Eastern River and Upland Folk** are the selected home of a spectacular learned voice tradition. **War-voice** and **the Deep-Voiced** are working handles.

War-voice uses the setting's existing resonant account of magic. A speaker aligns breath, posture, understood words and the resonant properties of place. The art principally addresses Material substance through Air-and-Earth structures of the Elemental Shell and sits close to unaccompanied [[01 Canon Reference/Magic & Systems/40 Rune and Shell Magic/Harmonic Shellcraft.md|Harmonic Shellcraft]]. Menhir bargains and inherited Giant techniques may affect particular schools without owning the whole tradition.

The likely historical origin lies in the polar crownland's command galleries, storm horns, resonant bridges and stone chambers. Human workers learn syllables used to open vents, shift loads, punish labour gangs and carry commands. Rebels use them against Giant works and carry the knowledge into the eastern uplands.

Package D may establish ancient masters capable of breaking formations and gates, carrying speech through storms, loosening earth, calling avalanches, altering local weather, interrupting other resonant workings and producing destructive physical force. One or two extraordinary feats may be historical. Others may survive as partisan song. Different masters possess different arts.

Training is long and physical. Failure can tear throats, crack teeth, burst vessels, deafen companions and collapse the wrong wall or hillside. The Voice cannot return a human soul, make victory inevitable or command a Menhir. Access follows teaching and school membership rather than ancestry.

The source structure distinguishes two Elder Scrolls traditions:

- Vivec's *Sermon Nine* supplies an heroic age of named Tongues with distinct and extravagant powers.
- Jurgen Windcaller's later Red Mountain defeat supplies catastrophe, moral argument and the division between restrained and martial schools.

Ättar retain Moon-blood, the four pact-kindreds, skinwalking, berserkers and Iron-Skin champions as their principal extraordinary traditions. This division ensures that mystical power is distributed among the southern peoples.

The following boundaries remain fixed:

- Tidewood's completed history remains local and is the required standard for depth and prose.
- Teren Casvilar's campaigns around **390–379 BR** remain the principal established late republican contact.
- G-61, the Hall Facing the Grobi, remains one local tragedy rather than the origin of every Folk–Ättar division.
- The south is colder; old wording which places the Grobi threat in the north requires correction when encountered.
- The current map remains relational and provisional. Package D may use coasts, rivers, forests, ranges and sailing relationships without fixing exact borders or distances.
- No Package D event explains a protected Weird, creates an Idol succession or fixes history after 1360 AR.
