# World History Causality Review — November 2026

A whole-history review of the World Egg's historical canon from the Age of Chains to 1360 AR. It asks two questions: does the history have **clear cause and effect**, and does it **read like real human history**? Grenzburg is excluded and will be revised to fit.

**Method.** Six separate readers each took one era band and applied the same six-part test: causation, human texture, premature precision, analog transposition, internal consistency, and prose register. A cross-era measurement pass of word density, process overhead and legend templating was added. The six readers worked independently, without seeing one another's notes. The findings that recur across eras below were each reached separately by several of them, which is why they are treated as structural rather than local.

## The verdict

The history is **morally serious, socially textured and often beautifully observed**. When it is good (Vathra's tether crews, the Three Collections, Rethmar, the White Measure, the Jirahar guilt architecture) it is better than most published fantasy lore. But across every era it has the same four structural faults:

1. **The world's real metaphysics almost never changes what happens.** Delete every sentence mentioning a Mask, a Menhir, a sign or the Flame and, in most eras, the political history plays out identically. Several control files actively *forbid* the supernatural from altering outcomes.
2. **Real-history analogs are followed beat for beat, not used as an entry ramp.** A reader who knows Homer, Kings, Alexander, Polybius, Plutarch, Suetonius or Ammianus can predict the next event, and from 375 AR the AR year matches the AD year.
3. **Precision is inverted.** Deep-time margins are exact: the 2,014 captives, phased year-ranges in 1600 BR, birth years for minor figures. The causal hinges are blank: the Prophet has no recorded deed, Qiryath changes masters with no account, 151 years before the Redeemer get 838 words, and 400–905 AR is nearly empty. The closer the history gets to the present, the thinner it gets.
4. **The author's control voice has leaked into public prose.** Negations ("does not prove", "no single", "remains distinct"), soul-taxonomy rulings and the authorial secrets appear as plain narration. A corrective narrator always rules on which source is right. That is the opposite of how Elder Scrolls lore works.

None of these require throwing work away. They require **re-pointing** it: a small number of world-native causal hinges written first, the analog beats demoted to background, and public articles handed to in-world voices.

> **The Elder Scrolls comparison.** TES lore feels like history for three reasons this vault currently inverts. (a) Its metaphysics is *causal*: the Numidium, the Towers and the Dragon Breaks are why things happened, not decoration on things that would have happened anyway. (b) Its history reaches the reader through **biased in-world books**, with no omniscient encyclopedia. (c) Its deep past is myth-fog while its recent eras are dense. The World Egg has a better-grounded human layer than TES, and that layer deserves a TES-grade causal and narrative layer.

## Five cross-era problems

### 1. The metaphysics is decoration, and the control files keep it that way

Every reader independently reported that the supernatural "influences beliefs and moments" but never moves an outcome. The cause is partly editorial. Control rulings written to prevent *misreadings* now prevent *consequences*:

- **Package B:** "Supernatural powers influence particular beliefs and moments without directing the campaigns"; supernatural actors "provide information or passage only."
- **Palace and Maritime Histories review:** Qarzeth's Mask is "a conventional regional war with local Mask activity," and the salting is barred from becoming "a reliable anti-Idol weapon."
- **Imperial Founding review:** a Pride apparition is rejected because it "would duplicate a device already present."
- **Three Collections:** the Mask's plenty is hedged: "even where weather and ordinary labour explained the plenty." That contradicts Commandment 2 and the Tidewood canon, where a Gluttony Mask demonstrably concentrates the catch.
- **The Torque Register has no entry before 292 AR.** More than 2,500 years of history carry no canonized world-native consequence.

Commandment 6 (*magic creates society*) is honoured in culture files but barely in the political narrative. Rune-script siege engines and aqueducts exist in [[Origins of Warlock Rune Magic]], yet never appear in three centuries of Atherian war. Astaroth is defined by power over structural weakness and his priesthood is pre-eminent, yet four imperial structural collapses never involve him.

**Fix.** Rewrite the supernatural controls in Packages A–E and the integration reviews so they govern **what is true** (author-only) rather than **what is allowed to happen**. Then give each era at least one torque that changes an outcome, and register it. Concrete candidates from the era reviews are listed under [[#The missing hinges]].

### 2. Analogs are followed in sequence, and the research method is why

| Ark and Hearths tales | Jacob and the household gods, Dinah, Joshua, Moses on Nebo, David and Uriah |

| Gateway Wars | Messana, the Mercenary War, Sardinia, Hannibal, Fabius, Masinissa, Cato, the salting |

| 400–1360 AR | see [[#400–1360 AR — the Long Frontier to the present]] |

The cause is visible in `90 Archive & Drafts/10 Research/Mythic and Sacred History`. About **756,000 words** of research include deep dossiers on Alexander, Caesar, Carthage, Amarna, Ptolemaic Egypt and late Rome. The Concordance then cites a primary source per beat: Dio, Pliny, Eusebius, Lactantius, Ammianus. Citing a source per beat imports that source's beat. The research is excellent; the problem is that it is used as a **script** rather than a **quarry**.

The setting's own rule already covers this. The Real-History Concordance's Rule 1 says: "If the analog works only because the reader knows the real history, it isn't done yet." The Forward Rule allows analogs to govern the past, but not this tightly.

**Fix.** Keep the analog as the *structural pressure* (an overextended republic, a successor scramble, a persecuting fiscal state). Cut the incidents that exist only because the source has them: Cleitus, Philotas, the Zannanza affair, Samson, the Shahnameh cycles, Bathsheba, Julian's spear line, Crispus and Fausta. Then bend at least one beat per sequence with world physics, and make the bend the famous part. The surviving divergences prove this works: Arezan and Eryndor as allies, Qarzeth's fall triggering the Persian-type war, Yaelith reversing Ezra, Rethmar's water politics in place of Parthian archers, and Marelith as a living reef city.

### 3. Precision is inverted: exact margins, blank hinges, a thin present

**Measured density in the History folder**

| Span | Words | Words per century |
|---|---|---|
| Deep past to 350 BR (`20` and `30` folders) | ~94,000 | ~4,000 (2300–350 BR) |
| c. 414 BR–0 AR (`40` folder) | ~35,800 | ~9,000 |
| 0–400 AR (`50` folder) | ~25,500 | ~6,400 |
| 400–1360 AR (`60` and `70` folders) | ~11,900 | **~1,200** |
| *Control and review documents (`10` folder)* | *~247,000* | — |
| *Mythic and Sacred History research drafts* | *~756,000* | — |
| *In-world voiced texts (`03 Stories`)* | *~17,600* | — |

Real historiography and TES both run the other way: the recent past is densest because it is best remembered and drives the present. The 960 years that produced every present-day wound get about a seventh of the per-century coverage of the founding generation. The vault's process documents outweigh its canon by roughly four to one.

**Exact where it should be fuzzy:**
- the "2,014" Grobi captives (in 11 files);
- the War of the Empty Halls phased 1608 / 1607–1603 / 1602–1596 BR;
- a hobgoblin quartermaster's ration policy;
- Gharuk's height to the foot;
- Ivara's fort falling "c. 1972 BR";
- judges of Qiryath dated to the decade;
- birth years for every founding-era figure;
- about 25 exact years between 244 and 395 AR;
- a Vashar reign list that is arithmetically impossible: Mithara I, Daryav's daughter, reigns 584–536 BR when her grandfather Arezan was born c. 742 BR.

**Blank where it should be load-bearing:**
- the Prophet, who has no saying, deed or quarrel;
- what ended the Glass provision;
- the twelve members of the Celestial Compact;
- how Qahur's greatest empire ended;
- why Eryndor went east;
- how Qiryath passed from Vashar to Atheria (found independently by two readers);
- 151 BR–0 AR;
- why a faith whose miracles work took 290 years to legalize;
- 400–905 AR.

**Fix.** Set a *precision budget*. Exact years and figures for the deep past belong in author-only notes; public text uses traditional forms such as "two thousand, by the Nhalduran count" or "king-lists name between six and ten rulers". Precision is spent on hinges and on the last five centuries. Freeze new named minor characters until the causal arc of their era is settled.

### 4. The control voice is in the public prose

- **Negation density.** The core Exodus files average a negation every 70–100 words, and "No single X" occurs 16 times. Island and Vorthara dossiers were expanded under a no-new-facts rule, so length came from caveats ("No individual isle has been fixed to a precise chart position by this account").
- **Authorial secrets stated as fact in public files.** Examples: "Most named powers were Menhir encountered through imperial astronomy"; "each man retained his complete human soul"; "a rare child born a true Giant retained Giant creaturely continuity"; production notes ("This Package D account… before Package E").
- **A corrective narrator.** Source disagreement almost always takes one shape: the inscription omits, the song remembers, and the narrator rules. Real sources disagree in ways that are *not* resolved.
- **Legend boilerplate.** 65 of the 75 local legends carry an identical "Stories told" callout and an identical "History and belief" paragraph. 73 carry the same "Its survival does not make every singer's version a complete account" line. 24 have a template slot that starts a sentence in lowercase.

The tales underneath are often excellent: *The Three False Processions* and *The Shelter That Outlived Its Heir* are as good as anything in the vault. The template wraps them in disclaimers, so they read as case files rather than legends.

**Fix.**
- **The negation rule:** public prose states what happened and what people believed. It may say what something is *not* only when a named in-world source claims it is. Every other "not / does not prove / remains open" moves to author-only notes. On a first pass this should cut 20–30% of the words in the affected files.
- **The legend template:** move the epistemic boilerplate into the MOC once. Let each legend be told by a teller with an agenda, followed by a short authorial note.

### 5. Templates and symmetry

- **The same incident in three reigns.** In each one, a prophet condemns the king, is spared, his workers are sold, and the chronicle omits them (Haviel, Mazhara, Rathava).
- **Killing the surrendered or breaking safe-conduct** appears at least six times.
- **A competent imperial woman manages the stores** in nearly every reign of the Age of Glory.
- **The paragraph ledger.** Nearly every paragraph closes with "X benefited; Y suffered."
- **Overused words.** "Household(s)" appears about 157 times in the Age of Glory canon.
- **Symmetric peoples.**
  - Each Ättar chief brings exactly his animal's resource.
  - The All-Thing is cloned across the Folk, Ättar and Cold Belt files.
  - The Sojourn's legacy is split into three tidy cultural kits: Atherians get crafts and measures, Jirahar get commandment and names, Ahdari get oath and water.
- **Name crowding.**
  - Lethia / Lethara / Lethren
  - Mira / Miretha / Merath / Meren / Mariel / Mirsa
  - Velsar / Velen / Veloran / Velcara
  - Orthea / Ortheia
  - two Saphas
  - three uses of "Aster"
  - three uses of "Corven"

**Fix.** Let outcomes sometimes come from plain contingency: a drowned heir, a lost harvest, incompetence. Break at least one symmetry per people. Leave some disagreements unresolved even at authorial level. Do a name-differentiation pass.

## What is working — protect these

- **Vathra and the Great Unyoking.** Flight needs human tether crews, which gives the workers leverage and makes their revolt possible. Horses concentrated on royal estates die with the kingdom, leaving the Tarkhan horseless until the First Catch in 905 AR. That is a 3,000-year causal thread driven by the setting's physics. It is the model for everything else.
- **The Grobi winter war.** An annual ecological pressure that directly produces beacons, refuge law, assembly calendars and store rules.
- **Mother Moon's firstborn bargain.** The contract terms produce succession law, changelings, Moon-Seers and grievance.
- **The White Measure.** A dead woman's estate demanding grain causes the 903 BR tomb-workers' stoppage. Nemeret's counterfeit raises a real problem of evidence.
- **Arqesh's rise and fall.** A canal flood creates demand for a gate monarchy; overextension, debt and insiders bring it down.
- **The Three Collections and the Fall of Qarzeth.** Ilya's list, rescuers who disagree with one another, rescue and atrocity in the same sack, and a statue whose face survivors broke. This is the register to aim for.
- **Rethmar.** Carrhae rebuilt through water politics. It is the best divergence in the imperial founding.
- **The Jirahar guilt architecture.** Named-council guilt against hereditary blame, and the Silence of Acceptance as an observed fact with contested meanings. "The carpenter we mourn. The scribe we cannot forgive."
- **The Ember's chain of custody,** ending in Veridun's paid invoice.
- **Ordinary life done concretely.** Tasar's winter song, the repair-banded pot, Kassilon's seizure turning into joint store control, the Naris roof-bribe the stewards refuse.
- **The best lines.** "A cook could become a commander without the kitchen children becoming free." "Workshop songs celebrated hungry workers who made a dead millionaire wait." "Tether cables became marriage ropes."

## Era by era

### Deep past: Age of Chains to Age of Hearths

**Core problem.** The Glass Sojourn is the one event only this cosmology could produce: a century of divine provision for a refugee host. It currently causes nothing. No file says what the host ate or farmed, when provision ended, or what its ending did. The Vacant Commission and the First Fission are explained by lists of contested nouns.

**The hinge.** The provision thins and ends at the threshold. After three or four generations fed by miracle, the Glass-born can herd, repair and judge, but few can farm. Several consequences follow:
- The Vacant Commission becomes **famine politics**.
- The Southern Seizures take *farmers* as much as land, which also gives the Five Staffs grain levy a cause.
- "The Lord who stopped feeding us; the stars that still answer" becomes the root of the Atherian religious drift.
- Fix an author-only order of magnitude for the host.
- Give the camp language descendants: a shared creole layer under Atherian, Jirahar and Ahdari, with cognate words for oath, water and law.

**Other findings.**
- **Giant collapse.** It still runs on legacy "national costume" dossiers, each ending in "one Giant's ritual backfired":
  - Ishkatar as Egypt,
  - Xolotl as the Aztecs,
  - Qianglong as China.
  The real engine is buried in one sentence: diluted dynasties paying ever more human bodies for continuity. Promote dilution and the cost of continuity to the declared root cause, rewrite the four dossiers in the Arsuvan register (granaries, seed grain, towpaths), and demote the ritual endings to survivor legend.
- **First kingship.** It uses the textbook "protection becomes rule" model. The Ättar file already holds a world-native theory: the Pale Household binds the *named petitioners*, so a crisis leader becomes a dynasty. Generalize it, and let Ark refusal of bargains explain why Jirahar and Ahdari leadership stays judicial for longer.
- **Writing.** It needs a ruling. Ättar "registers" in 1600 BR and Folk "repair lists" contradict the era ladder, which puts writing in the City-States age.
- **The Prophet.** Give him three or four incompatible public traditions of his last command and death (Jirahar, Ahdari, Atherian, and Orphaned mockery), while the authorial truth stays sealed.

### Ancient east: Qahur, Arqesh, Vashar, Qiryath, Eryndor

**Core problem.** Package B's controls keep every Mask, Menhir and sign out of the turning points. The research draft for Eryndor had a genuine torque, the Successors fighting over the Crowned Countenance's images, and canon dropped it.

**The hinge.** Qiryath's authenticated sign of acceptance between 742 and 660 BR. Canon says sacrifice at Qiryath carried an objective sign until 0 AR, yet no pre-Atherian file mentions it. It supplies the missing causes:
- Neshkar wants his image in the sanctuary because the sign draws loyalty away from his provinces.
- Exile law becomes a theology of absence.
- Arezan funds the rebuilding as a bet on the sign's return.
- If the sign returns first at an offering by Yaelith's mixed households, heaven decides her disputed ruling instead of the narrator.

The big gain lands in 1360. The Jirahar have already lived through one silence, and it ended. Their present expectation of return becomes historically grounded rather than stubborn.

**Other findings.**
- **Eryndor's corpse.** Serevan hires White Measure embalmers, the rite fails, and the Crowned Countenance learns the dead king's voice. A corpse that issues grants explains why the living heirs became disposable and why the inheritance wars ran 34 years.
- **Uncaused transitions.**
  - the end of the Aramen empire ("imperial contraction");
  - Eryndor's motive, now that the Persian-type invasion comes two centuries later. Give Arqesh western aggression c. 760–735 BR, or make the motive an army that had to be paid;
  - Kassur III's portable royal images vanish after 688 BR.
- **Vashar's reign list.** Keep Arezan, Daryav, Xarun and Orvaz II. Replace the rest with "king-lists name between six and ten rulers; Qiryathi and Tasaran lists disagree on order and on whether Mithara reigned or regented."
- **Cuts.** Cleitus, Philotas, Narethi and Ashkur, Hador (Samson), and the Shahnameh cycles. Collapse the Romance incidents into "the romances". Keep Tovan, Othan and Seleth; the women's fast is excellent.
- **The Chronology.** Its lines should state structures, not incidents. The Tenaret stoppage earns its place in an article, not at the same level as the fall of Arqesh.

### Atherian city-states to the late republic

**Core problem.** The Gateway Wars follow Polybius, and the republic's crisis follows Plutarch. No metaphysics appears in any decision; a word search of the republic files finds no Menhir, Mask, Shell or warlock acting. The integration review says the war has "no… mechanically mapped Roman campaign," but it is one.

**The hinge.** Make Qarzeth's Mask actually provide. Qarzeth's notoriously fast recovery is real Mask plenty, which is why the hawks fear it, and the Three Collections are the bill coming due. Existing Gluttony canon already supplies the defeat:
- cut the supply routes (the spared towns defect);
- recover the named remains (Ilya's list);
- break the organ-object (the image);
- seal it with a shared meal "without hierarchy of worth".

That meal becomes the Spared Towns' anti-sacrifice oath, repeated by Nerethan kings at accession, and so the root of Samyra's legitimacy. Salt then has a world-native reason, and it explains why Sarqen, not Qarzeth, becomes the capital. Separately, give Caleran its own panic sacrifice of captives during Zamaret's invasion; Ilaron and Tarsenna already sacrificed captives. That makes Valeor's moral case hypocritical in a way that is historically grounded and world-native.

**Other findings.**
- **The missing hinge.** Nothing explains how a league of twelve equals became Caleran's empire: no named members, no inter-city wars, no tyrants. Name the twelve, narrate two or three episodes of league becoming hegemony, and fuse the Social War with the League of the Eastern Oar ship-money revolts. That splice is the best anti-predictability move available, because no textbook reader can guess it.
- **Silos.** Domestic and foreign history run separately. Damar's land bill (495–491 BR) runs through Xarun's invasion (497–491 BR) and never mentions it. Make Damar's constituency the veterans of Ilyessa.
- **Kelarion and Orthea** are the most world-native events in the era, and both are quarantined. Make Kelarion's Roll of Refused Hulls the ancestor of Thalassian refuge law, and let the Worm's loose years explain why the records of Qarzeth's sack cannot be reconciled.
- **Islands and Vorthara.** Eight files and about 5,500 words, with almost no events. Fold them into the Elmaric Atlas and into Vorthara, and add two or three human events per surviving article or cut it.
- **One republican torque.** Give each phase one: a Menhir-sanctioned emergency office Harod cannot lay down (which breaks the Sulla beat), or augury rights over public land at the heart of Damar's crisis.

### Imperial founding and the Age of Glory (active frontier)

**Core problem.** Qiryath's move from Vasharan supremacy to an Atherian governor has no cause anywhere. Vashar appears zero times in about 25,000 words of Glory canon. The 151 years before the Redeemer get 838 words, and the empire is a frozen golden age with one formula per reign.

**The hinge.** Write a 151 BR–0 AR causal bridge on four converging pressures:
- Raveth's crown estates and rural dependency;
- Elian I's citizenship grant;
- Qiryath's transfer;
- the ruler-cult Mask's growing appetite.

At least two need world-native causes. One is the sanctuary's real sign: a disputed priestly succession the sign appears to favour drives a faction to seek Atherian arbitration, and that becomes the charter Ezrahel's faction knows how to use in 0 AR. The other is Astaroth's structural testing, or the speaking portrait. Date the bridge in relative terms.

**Other findings.**
- **247 BR should be "the year the portrait spoke".** The review says the Mask first answered then; the Four Eagles article never mentions it. Let it bend the outcome: Mott's claim leans on the image, and Samor wins partly by seizing it or refusing it.
- **Astaroth's load tests** should become an imperial institution with liability. Caelor's 326 BR collapse is the scandal that made them compulsory; Velen's riverbanks are a skipped test.
- **The Principate is used twice.** Once 350 BR–0, then again for Tavian to Marek. Egypt's annexation is reused (Sarqen 350 BR, proposed Qahur 33 BR, citing the same Dio passage). Spartacus is reused (Ostar 399 BR, Sarqen c. 189 AR). Raveth's seven-year famine is Joseph in Egypt, which the Concordance doesn't record. Pick one Egypt.
- **Public files state the Lorkhan-grade secret** (the Star Pantheon is misread Menhir) as plain narration. Move it to author-only callouts and show it through rival in-world voices.

**Before the live drafts are promoted:**
- *The First Imperial House biographies.* Keep the structural idea (good government plus southern occupation fund the Four Eagles) and the Ossen failure, and cut the rest to one page. Cassara's death in 263 contradicts the companion review; "Tavren Arel" collides with King Tavren of Sarqen.
- *From Thalion to Isfir.* The strongest draft, and its "what it leaves" column is real causal chaining. It leaves the Qiryath transfer open while settling Dovren crews, and it contains instructions to the author and a Bible to-do list that must be stripped.
- *The Constraint Sheet.* Its tables still have Isfir executed "at Caleran" by the High Augur's sentence, contradicting its header and canon.
- *The New Chat Handoff.* It instructs "Use named phases, rulers and local actors", which is the over-specificity instruction in writing. Replace it with the working rules below.

### Redeemer and Conversion, 0–411 AR (active frontier)

**Core problem.** Nothing in canon explains why a faith whose signs objectively work needs about 290 years to be legalized and 380 to be compelled. The articles avoid the question by quietly downgrading their own miracles: the Ashway coal test becomes practical judgement, the Lantern Cradle lamps become later hymns. Meanwhile 249–395 AR follows late Rome beat for beat. From 375 the AR year is the AD year: 375, 376, 380, 391–392 and 395 all match.

**The hinge: who may authenticate a sign.** Write one author-only page on why proof does not compel belief in this cosmology:
- grace acts by commission, not on demand; [[White Flame Doctrine]] and the saints research already say this;
- the Material Realm is crowded with rival wonders: Shell-fire, Menhir answers, Idol counterfeits (Bruckmoor);
- the augur colleges hold the legal monopoly on authenticating signs. A flame on the dead looks, to a magistrate, like an unlicensed and possibly Idol-feeding cult.

Then route the era through that page:
- the Great Purge (c. 36–45) becomes the augurs answering Tyros's defecting colleges, and it currently has no cause;
- Tervan's edict adds an Idol-cult panic to the arrears;
- 292 is the first verifiable sign the crown owns;
- 292–377 is crown against bishops over who owns the Velos miracle, with Astaroth's 350 answer as the Stars' one counter-proof. Let it leave a Star-recognising eastern command that outlives Astrion, so Vestian defeats a real rival rather than issuing a tidy edict;
- 380 brings compelled professions that **fail to kindle**, a Second Silence the Church must decide whether to record (Commandment 7);
- 411 becomes the Man-Bearer reply from Jirahar-descended congregations, so the borrowed Ephesus becomes an effect of this era's own wound.

**Other findings.**
- **The torques are thin.** Lead with them, and move restitution tables, hearing mechanics and edict procedure into development records. The Cassian article never mentions the river that won't freeze or the fight over who owns a miracle.
- **Four legacy files** with the old "AE" dating are marked canon and contradict current canon. `The Apostle's Bones.md`, `Saintmaker's Forge.md`, `The Temple That Would Not Burn.md` and `Codex of The Era of Blood and Ash.md` contain unmappable AE dates, three locations for Calira's blade, a "post-Imperial" Church, and two leftover chatbot sign-offs (verified). Recast the Codex as a late, biased in-world compilation; re-date or archive the rest.
- **The Companions' dates conflict.**
  - Amaryah's Dormition, c. 55 AR, gathers "the surviving Companions", but only Solana is alive by then.
  - Elsin "arrives" there, but he died in 52.
  - Tyros is called the last martyr in 45, but Virella dies in 47.
- **The Third Nail weird** assumes the Four Nails reached the Pillar of Concordance before 219 AR. The accepted Binding proposal moved the execution to Qiryath and left the timber "unrecorded". The weird needs a route from Qiryath to Caleran, or a rewrite.
- **Vestian proposal.** Its motive is one line, and the supernatural is absent from all 14.6 KB. The dissolution of the only body that ever made a Star answer should be a scene. Cut one of the two civil wars (Maximus or the Frigidus). Make "before the Mesakha" Vestian's own clause, which the Church reads as "never" and the Jirahar as hope. Promote only 377, 380 and 395 as firm dates. Keep the reuse of Astrion's survey records to locate tower property.

### 400–1360 AR — the Long Frontier to the present

**Core problem.** 400–905 AR has almost no causes, not just few words.

| Period | Chronology words per century | Narrative history words per century |
|---|---|---|
| 0–395 AR | ~235 | ~5,800 |
| 400–905 AR | ~44 | ~520 |

What the period does have is mostly parable: about 12,800 words of tales and saints' lives against about 2,600 of political history.
- **One ruler in 510 years.** The only named Atherian ruler is Corvenas III, and he is absent from Named Figures.
- **Vashar disappears.** It is Atheria's peer through every earlier age (last war 222–233 AR), then vanishes, so the Covenant rises into a vacuum the world never created.
- **"Weak emperors" is the only cause.** It is Gibbon's explanation, long abandoned by historians. It is also the only explanation offered for 905 (Corvenas "devout and strategically incapable"), so both great transitions bottom out in a bad man on the throne.
- **No end and no survival.** The Decadence has no end event on Atheria's side; it ends in 540 only because Mujalib begins in 541. And nothing explains how the rump empire survives 293 years after losing the north.

**The hinge: the Guttering, plus a Covenant clock of its own.** Write a 3,000–4,000-word author-only causal spine for 400–905 on five threads:

1. **The Guttering.** As sanctity becomes office, the state church's kindling yields fall: hymns misfire, relics stop answering. The court substitutes warlock Shell-fire and hired troops. This follows directly from Commandment 7 ("White Flame demands sanctity and coherence"), and both ends are already seeded: the Conversion file's "temptation to treat successful custody as ownership of the Flame", and Prelude's "treating possession of the past as protection from the future". It explains:
   - why the Flame did not save Caleran but did save Veridun in 906;
   - why the refugees kindle reliably in the 950s ("Weaponized Holy Flame first appears reliably in this campaign" currently has no cause);
   - both sides of the later Kindling Question (theophany against protocol).

   Keep it contested in-world.
2. **Fiscal and territorial squeeze.** The north's grain and water provinces are lost. Provincial commands keep their own revenue, a habit seeded in 253–288. Relic and monastery estates are exempted from tax, and the Sestaran cities and the east withhold theirs. That turns "tax registers overstated recoverable wealth" into a consequence, and explains why the east does not march in 905.
3. **A sixth-century shock and Vashar's exhaustion.** An Atheria–Vashar war, then a drought, dust veil or plague around 541 (real history had all three before Islam). The 4,444 dig the oasis as famine relief. Vashar falls first, and its converted elites later produce the Qahirids, which would also explain Qahir ibn Ruzbeh's Persian-sounding name.
4. **The legal arms race.** Rewrite the Unsealing around the breach-courts the Torque Register already canonizes; no history article mentions them. 612 becomes a legal-metaphysical stalemate: Atherian chanceries re-seal charters with Flame-witnessed oaths, and the Fourth Seal schism (597–603) makes breach-court authority itself disputable. That is the missing reason the conquest stops, and the Velian "fireproof chests" become part of it. Commandment 7's cost structure should explain why 4,444 bound devils dig an oasis and then the Covenant fights 340 years without devil-powered war: for example, the founding covenant bars its bound from conquest, and Qahir's innovation in 885 is a loophole, since Depth beasts from mapped Hell are not party to it.
5. **The eastern drift.** Bishops become civic governors in the east, and Aurelium's protocol culture grows as Caleran's influence fades. That explains why the east survives 905 alone, why it develops protocol theology, and why Adalmar is possible by 975. As written he comes from nowhere.

Add one plain contingent accident per century so history does not run as pure mechanism.

**Other findings.**
- **The Covenant only acts when Atheria needs a villain.**
  - No dated Covenant event falls between 905 and 1131, and no ruler is named between Idris and Suhrab, a 259-year blank.
  - Its whole account of losing Caleran is one sentence.
  - The Reconquest has a refugee league win a 144-year war while fighting two lamp civil wars and a schism, which only works against a distracted enemy.

  Give the Covenant its own 905–1190 history: Idris's death in 931 and a succession war; Sealed-Blood opportunism; the cost of garrisons; one foundry replacing the engines lost in 907. Add the **First Catch boomerang**: the studs scattered by the Fall make the Tarkhan mounted raiders "within three generations", c. 950–970, just as Caleran is retaken. That unintended consequence is already canon-adjacent and unused.
- **The Grey Sweat stops at a confessional border.** No Thalmyric, Covenant, Sestaran, Tarkhan, Jirahar or Velian file mentions it. A Covenant with a Vault of Names should experience mass death as a custody crisis, which is the most native plague story the setting has.

  - Karlmann (a real Carolingian name);
  - three grandsons partitioning the empire (Verdun);
  - the envoys' "we knew not whether we stood on earth" (the Russian Primary Chronicle);

  - "Whose the realm, his the rite" (cuius regio);
  - the Golden Book;
  - Qarayel's grave hidden by a thousand horses (the Genghis legend);
  - "second serfdom".

  Present-Day Courts and Crowns breaks the Concordance's own naming rule with "Alric IV von Caldria", "Henri d'Montesse" and "Ysabel de Braxford".
- **The Forward Rule is at risk.** The 1360 board reads as the textbook 1570s: harem-and-guard "stagnation" (itself a discredited trope), an elective crown, the Augsburg freeze, an Atlantic rush. The Concordance forecasts the analog future outright ("Atlantic trade eclipsing the Mediterranean… ruins whoever stays landlocked"); delete that. For each trajectory now on rails, canonize one native disruptor tied to a Held Proof: the Accord toward a Thirty Years War, the elective crown toward paralysis, the harem toward decline, the New World toward a price revolution.
- **Set-pieces without connecting tissue, 1052–1360.**
  - Sestara's "Competitive Independence, 910–1234" gets two sentences.

  - Eclessia's 908–975 gets one.
  - Thalmyria 1263–c.1329 names no ruler.

  - *The Grey Sweat - Authorial Truth* redefines the Quiet Ninth, breaching the Weird Register's rule that no document may explain a weird. The register defines it as the week the plague stopped.

## Working rules for the next drafting passes

1. **Pressures before people.** Every era first gets three or four long-running pressures, each with a start, a turning point and an end state. Names are added only where a person changes the result.
2. **One torque per transition.** No transfer of territory, change of dynasty or religious turn is canon until it has a stated cause *and* a world-native consequence registered in the Torque Register.
3. **Analog as quarry, not script.** Research supplies structure and texture. A borrowed incident needs a reason to exist beyond its source having it. If a reader who knows the analog can predict the next beat, bend it.
4. **Precision budget.** Deep-past numbers and dates are author-only or traditional. Exact years are reserved for hinges and for the last five centuries. No new named minor characters in an era whose causal bridge isn't written.
5. **The negation rule.** Public prose says what happened and what people believed. It says what something is *not* only when a named in-world source claims it is.
6. **Disagreement that stays open.** At least one disagreement per article is left unresolved even in the author's notes, and not every disagreement takes the "inscription omits, song remembers" shape.
7. **Contingency is allowed.** Some outcomes come from a drowned heir, a lost harvest or incompetence, not from structure or doctrine.
