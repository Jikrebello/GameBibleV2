# World Egg — Inherited Places and Institutions Review

**Review date:** 13 November 2026.

The vault inventory covered the six canon subject areas and the playable and in-world libraries. This is a whole-setting review by representative source chains, with closer reading of Atheria, the Redeemer, and their later inheritances. Caleran, Grenzburg and independent adventures provide examples of how the inherited setting becomes playable. Drafts provide evidence of plans where identified. No project-specific Moldy Worldbuilding study was supplied or found in the vault. The review uses the skill's questions about past purpose, present work, custody, cost, disagreement and new uses.

## The strongest existing chains

| Inheritance | What the sources establish | Why it matters in ordinary life |
|---|---|---|
| **Tidewood's shore and work** | Survivors broke a Giant wall to drain water and reused masonry in hearths and banks. The position of Brackenmouth followed the new settlement on the shingle ridge. Later households developed replaceable boat parts and kept a common loft for repairs. The Tarred Hand grew from local workers, freed captives and foreign residents. Later genealogies made it sound like one ancient bloodline. [[History of the Tidewood Coast]] · [[Sails, Fish and Winter Fire]] · [[Taking of the Dry Fleet]] | The past survives in where people build, what they can repair and who sails before the next weather change. A patched sail shows the order of damage, repairs and shortages. The loft gives sailmakers a decision when cloth is scarce. Jokes about owners who scorn sewing give working people a way to talk about mutual dependence. This is the strongest chain from first survival through present work, pleasure and new social arrangements. |
| **Arcelon's road and songs** | Orent's army took winter stores, burned a civilian settlement and used captives to build the road. One song preserved grades and distances. The workers' verses preserved springs, unstable ground and deaths omitted by officials. After a washout, crews required both songs before repairing the road. [[The Two Roads of Arcelon]] | A public memory changes because people need the disfavoured account to keep travelling. Repeating a verse has consequences for which culvert gets repaired and whose testimony officials have to hear. The sources identify a surviving expression in modern speech. The present repair authority and condition of the surviving stretches remain unspecified here. |
| **Raveth's relief and estates** | Neriah requisitioned transport and issued seed and food against obligations. Local countersignatures checked theft. Default transferred farms and tools into the imperial estate. The court later stripped some local signatures from its account. Elian II authorised the policy. Households remembered the Keeper's loan as rescue or as a warning. [[The Keeper of Borrowed Grain]] · [[The Last Dynasties of the Age of Glory]] | One set of records lets neighbours challenge theft and lets the state convert help into permanent control. The estate changes where children work and whether a household can leave. Cassen inherits a revenue source and people who have reasons to resist it. Any longer survival of specific farms, records or obligations needs its own evidence. |
| **The Nine Wells of Tergai** | Tarkhan houses deepened old sources with lifting knowledge from former Vathran tether workers. Maintenance, burial and seasonal use supported claims. Covenant forces seized the wells in 558 AR. Pilgrimage remembers voluntary submission, while Tarkhan graves and pasture remain inside present security zones. [[Vathra and the Great Unyoking]] · [[Nine Wells of Tergai]] · [[The Tarkhan before the Horse Hordes]] | Control requires keeping the draw gear, water, grazing and roads usable. It affects guards, herders, pilgrims, merchants and families with graves to visit. The smallest addition when a scene calls for one is to identify who can admit a party or call for a repair at a particular well. |
| **Qiryath's sanctuary and charter** | Two tower cities left foundations, courts, cisterns and records. Sacrifice continued after the Resurrection without its former sign. The 70 AR destruction moved continuity into teaching, households and travelling courts. Present Jirahar custodians maintain paths and masonry while Eclessian officers enforce the boundary. Reports of warmth at the Silent Gate increase pilgrimage and dispute. [[Great Tower Revolt and the Second Destruction]] · [[Qiryath Reqa]] · [[Jirahar Memory of Isfir]] | The ruin supports pilgrimage, care of the dead and legal claims. The next repair can be interpreted as rebuilding, and a supposed sign can prompt a change in who controls the site. The charter and the surviving rites give the parties something concrete to contest. |
| **Caleran's water and Ember** | Caleran inhabits republican, imperial, occupation and Reconquest fabric. Repair crews keep parts of the old waterworks in service. Covenant custodians kept the Eternal Ember under guard and recorded its upkeep. Veridun paid their invoice after recovering the city. [[The Living Waterworks]] · [[Caleran Great-City Dossier]] · [[Caleran and the Eternal Ember]] · [[01 Canon Reference/History/60 Fall and Successor Empires/The Reclamation of Caleran.md|The Reclamation of Caleran]] | Renters, millers and residents need old structures to keep working. The Ember supports pilgrimage and church authority, and the receipt records a debt to the former occupier's custodians. Follow the distinction between maintaining the lamp and commanding the Flame. The waterworks offer a separate everyday scene test through inspection, repair or a dry street. |

## Coverage across the setting

| Area sampled | Evidence of consequential inheritance | Next question or limit |
|---|---|---|
| **Deep history and cosmology** | The Glass refuge's households preserve distribution, testimony, labour and care. Its people carry obligations into later societies. Menhir retain transformed commissions, courts and bargains. The restored soul-road changes the condition of the dead. [[The Glass Sojourn]] · [[Menhir Sovereignty and Bargain]] · [[The Lord and Redeemer]] | The mechanisms are strong. When one reaches a household, show the actual obligation, refusal or burial decision. A later people may preserve one practice from the Glass generations without inheriting the whole covenant. |
| **Nsizwa and the distant world** | After Giant rule, some households stripped workshops for metal and repaired useful channels. Later strongholds controlled storage, graves, shelter and grazing. An awakened body can reproduce the signs and tactics of an inherited office. The people who keep the tombs and the authorial sources have different evidence for whose identity continues. [[Historical Atlas of the Nsizwa]] · [[Izivukwa Continuity]] | Repair and custody give the distant history a local material basis. Keep the question of personal continuity open where the source evidence is inconclusive. A future account can follow who provides maintenance and service at a particular stronghold. |
| **Imperial technology and creatures** | Early runes serve kilns, irrigation and well clearing. Later colleges standardise techniques. Surviving Atherian water wardens still read rights and writs using dead civic categories and may redirect flow towards the wrong district. Jirahar Underways preserve routes and caches through human engineering. [[Origins of Warlock Rune Magic]] · [[Atherian Ward and Trial Engines]] · [[Underworld, Underways, and Corpse-Roads]] | The mechanism is specified at creature or system level. For an individual use, name who relies on it, who may alter the writ and what stops working when it is shut down. Keep genuinely operational relics rare, as the Caleran corpus already does. |
| **Elmaric ports and Vorthara** | The future cities form from different harbours, fairs and settlement histories. Vorthara's reagent growers, carriers, repair yards and water access join the mage precincts to imported grain. At Crown Gate in 907 AR, broken engines changed later military practice and the victory supported the Mountain Rebellion. [[The Making of the Sestaran Cities]] · [[Vortharan Working Precincts before 350 BR]] · [[The Battle of the Shattered Engines (907 AR)]] | Ancient supply and later civic identities both work. The bridge is thin where imperial charter, occupation or trade rights determine who can levy a later port. Establish the instrument for each important harbour before assigning a modern institutional descendant. |
| **Covenant and the surviving churches** | After condemnation at Halmenth, Velian congregations survive under negotiated protection and preserve a Jirahar liturgical dialect associated with Isfir. Recognised communions retain worship, property and internal courts under tribute and legal restrictions. [[The Velian Church]] · [[The People of the Lamp]] | A liturgy carries a language across political and confessional change. The restrictions matter at inheritance, marriage and testimony. The first link is less developed than the last. Follow who taught that language and who copied, translated or financed the texts before Halmenth. |
| **Hostile institutions** | The Left Hand's separate economies tie predation to choices over debt, shelter, bodies, promises and access. Caleran's procurement and body-theft chains place the abstract cosmology inside local markets and burial practice. [[The Atlas of the Left Hand]] · [[Secret Networks of Caleran]] | Regional use gains force when a specific office or household needs the service that makes exploitation possible. The general atlas describes a mechanism. It leaves the owners, intermediaries and surviving evidence to the region. |
| **In-world testimony** | A count's letter is a genuine disputed source carrying his land claim. Eclessian, Thalmyric and Sestaran accounts preserve different institutional interests in the Fall and Split. Quarry families in the Empty Grave story scatter stones used by rival shrines to claim their dead. [[Reliability Register]] · [[A Letter from Count Rhael Tovarin of Velos]] · [[G-70 - The Empty Grave and the Two False Shrines]] | Bias has force when a witness is protecting a title, relic, grave or livelihood. The quarry family's observable act gives this more weight than a disputed label alone. This legend concerns an Underground-era prisoner. It supplies a contrast in how burials are used and gives no evidence for the Redeemer's own tomb. |
| **Playable settings and adventures** | Caleran already shows water crews, reused barracks and old property disputes. The Grenzburg Road-Key network has maintainers, finite stock, access rules and transport limits. Celion Light Station relies on an Atherian rainwater cistern and a trained household for a lamp important to current shipping. The Stone Before the Covenant gives several groups reasons to claim a buried legal instrument. [[Caleran Great-City Dossier]] · [[Road Keys and Menhir Paths]] · [[Celion Light Station]] · [[The Stone Before the Covenant]] | The strongest examples can enter play through a repair, a closure or a document with a living claimant. At Celion, a missed supply boat affects the keepers' food and fuel and eventually the ships that need the light. The historical provenance of particular Grenzburg institutions belongs to later history work. The legal instrument's deeper provenance remains disputed. |

## The gaps that matter for the Redeemer pass

1. **Which imperial authority may take a life in Qiryath?** Existing history gives the sanctuary courts and sacrifice under imperial government and the elders' need for an imperial execution. It leaves the charter's mechanism, the officer with capital jurisdiction and the reason a case reaches Caleran largely unwritten. Developing these details connects conquest to the Passion through a law people already use. It makes the distinction between Ezrahel's faction, dissenting Jirahar and the imperial officials visible in the sequence of decisions. [[Historical Spine of the Jirahar]] · [[The Rise of the Redeemer]] · [[From Thalion to Isfir - Imperial Expansion and the Redeemer]]
2. **Which people keep the first assemblies supplied?** The Underground has ordinary and specific evidence, including burial fraternities, law-book bindings, fostered children and letters. The bridge from Isfir's gathered witnesses to those local institutions is thinner. Start with one existing household, one place to meet and a person who feeds or houses a visitor. Later growth can leave an actual practice, a copied text or a dispute about who may join the table. [[The Age of the Redeemer]] · [[The Ember Underground]] · [[Amaryah the Fire-Bearer]]
3. **Which southern obligations survive a change of ruler?** The first dynasty proposes stores, transport, assessments and charters. The south later contributes to Eclessia. The missing middle is a local example of what an arrangement required, who kept its records and whether a later ruler renewed it, seized it or let it lapse. Start with a crossing or store already in the draft. Allow different outcomes at independent Tidewood landings and directly assessed inland districts. [[The First Imperial House - Proposed Character Biographies]] · [[Historical Atlas of the Southern Cold World]] · [[History of the Tidewood Coast]]
4. **Where does the next large gap appear?** Once the apostolic generation is anchored, follow one inherited office or property through Underground, Legalization and compulsion, and one institution across the eastern frontier. The first track gives the growth of the Church a material history. The second keeps imperial and regional history moving independently of Caleran's religious changes. [[The Ember Underground]] · [[The Conversion of the Empire]] · [[The Velian Church]]

World Egg already has strong models for this kind of history. Tidewood shows how an inheritance can produce work, humour and new kinship. Arcelon shows how practical dependence can preserve testimony a government prefers to lose. Qiryath and Caleran show how people use the same place under incompatible claims. The priority is to give the imperial expansion and the first churches that same level of local cause and consequence. This review records evidence and questions. The separate expansion draft contains the new story proposals.
