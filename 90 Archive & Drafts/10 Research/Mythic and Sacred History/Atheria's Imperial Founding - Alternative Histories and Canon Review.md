# Atheria’s Imperial Founding — Alternative Histories and Canon Review

Atheria already possesses the conditions from which an imperial founding can develop: competing commanders, unequal incorporation, military settlement, provincial wealth and a religious tradition through which victory can be celebrated as public order. The Roman research supplies a connected generation of people whose relationships turn those conditions into events.

This dossier preserves two researched imperial-founding alternatives. **Alternative A and Route M have now been selected and integrated:** Thalion is the Augustus-like heir and founder; the queen descends from an incoming maritime governing house joined to a spared-town family. [[Imperial Founding - Authorial History and Integration Review]] controls the canonical names, dates, relationships and selected supernatural events. Alternative B and the locally originating dynasty remain unselected research, not competing canon.

The [[#12. Pre-promotion alignment after the Gateway integration|pre-promotion alignment below]] records the recommendations approved before integration. Statements that a choice remains open refer to that earlier stage. Earlier dates, geographic alternatives and supernatural possibilities apply only where the canonical review expressly adopts them.

The source sequence is in [[The Roman Republic's Last Generations - Conquest, Civil War and Imperial Settlement]]; individual comparisons are in [[Caesar's World - People, Households and Rivalries]]. The resulting histories begin with [[The Last Generations of the Caleran Republic]], [[The House of Nerethan]] and [[The Wars of the Atherian Succession]]. Further delivery concerns the wider Age of Glory rather than another choice of founder.

## 1. What the present history establishes

The [[The Celestial Compact and the Caleran Republic|republican account]] describes a political world whose commanders increasingly obtain the resources to challenge its institutions. [[The Rise of the Atherian Empire Overview]] says that civil wars ended when **Aurelius Thalion seized Caleran**, retained republican offices in ceremonial form and made command hereditary in practice. It assigns him a standing army, centralised provincial appointments, regular taxation and the elevation of Astaroth’s priesthood.

[[Chronology of the Known World (AR Dating)]] places Unification around **350 BR** and the Age of Glory at **c. 350 BR–0 AR**. Atherian old-style dating also uses the Unification as its epoch. These are authorial statements, not merely an imperial claim which can be silently exposed as false.

The new Caleran articles establish earlier human families, the Common Sky settlement, three related Orphaned peoples and a regional Giant kingdom. They do not give Thalion a childhood, parents, marriage, assassination, adoption or individual campaigns. Considerable room therefore remains for a dramatic founding generation.

The following are distinct inherited facts:

- Caleran stands at the Aurin’s mouth on the north-western outer coast.
- The Thalassian lies east of Thalmyria, between its coast, Sestara and western Eclessia.
- Folk lands lie southward into colder country; separate clans and regional powers precede imperial conquest.
- Modern Tarkhan horse power follows the much later First Catch. Early western camel-and-well societies and eastern foot-mobile communities already exist.
- The Serpent’s Spine dried up at Ishkatar’s collapse. Its old river cannot be restored merely to furnish a convenient Egypt.
- Modern churches, the Covenant revelation, the Sestaran Concord and current dynasties belong to later history.

## 2. Common historical material for both alternatives

The proposed founding generation would contain two successive forms of partnership. The first joins men who need one another’s money, reputation and political support. The second follows the conqueror’s murder and gives three surviving commanders a public claim to govern a fractured republic. Their territories and responsibilities change as armies, cities and allies change sides.

Conquest receives several campaigning seasons. A regional rebellion follows years of garrisons, recruitment, trade and requisition. Local rulers have opportunities to cooperate, miscalculate or resist. The conqueror accumulates officers who know how he feeds an army and followers whose property depends on his victories. His growing power becomes a domestic problem because losing command threatens people besides himself.

The assassination draws participants from both former enemies and trusted followers. Its aftermath preserves the dead man’s army, promises, appointments and household. A younger heir has to make an inherited claim effective; an older lieutenant believes experience and service entitle him to lead. A third commander supplies resources neither can safely ignore.

The queen governs a distinct kingdom with its own household, cultivated land, trade, revenue and religious relationships. Its rulers have long dealt with Atherian supervision through garrisons, payments and support for competing claimants. In both proposed founding stories, her partnership with a commander changes that existing dependence while securing her position against rivals. It also exposes her subjects to a wider war. The fate of her children matters because claims can survive military defeat.

Ordinary people encounter these events through the departure of family members, requisitioned grain, payments, confiscations, changing garrisons and fear of denunciation. Public celebrations and real relief after peace belong to the same account as the people who cannot return to land granted to veterans.

## 3. Alternative A — Thalion inherits the conqueror’s unfinished settlement

### The founding generation

In this version, the principal conqueror is an older Atherian whose early political standing depends on a wealthy partner and an established commander. The older commander has secured eastern sea routes and arranged settlements with provincial rulers. His supporters expect recognition and land; the wealthy partner expects access and distinction. The conqueror offers political cooperation, then obtains a southern command.

Over several winters he extends Atherian power across the inhabited forest approaches. He draws supplies from allied districts, demands hostages and places trusted officers in fortified towns. His reports describe a spreading order. Families sold after a captured settlement and allies forced to provision troops remember a different expansion. A large resistance coalition eventually draws him into a siege whose outcome gives him extraordinary prestige at home.

The first partnership weakens as its members pursue separate commitments. The wealthy partner dies in a northern expedition. The older commander’s eastern clients and Caleran’s prominent houses become his alternative coalition. A marriage joining the conqueror’s family to the older commander had provided personal access; its loss makes misunderstandings harder to repair, but appointments and command decisions produce the final break.

Civil war carries the conqueror through the heartland and towards the older commander’s supporting harbours. Defeated, the older man asks refuge from the queen’s contested royal household. One faction kills him while expecting the conqueror to approve. Instead, intervention secures the queen’s position and binds her government to a new patron. The conqueror returns to Caleran with authority too concentrated for the arrangements he has promised to restore.

His assassination leaves Thalion, a young kinsman designated as heir, competing with an experienced lieutenant. A trusted serving officer and a respected former opponent have both participated in the murder for different reasons. Thalion obtains resources through his mother’s relations and the dead man’s officers. Several older politicians help him because they expect to direct him. He joins the lieutenant and a third commander once their cooperation offers a stronger prospect of survival.

The three authorise executions and confiscations, defeat the principal republican armies and divide responsibilities. Thalion’s work in the Aurin and western districts includes settling soldiers on occupied holdings. The lieutenant acquires eastern revenues and a closer alliance with the queen. The third commander holds an important connecting military district before losing his forces and retiring into a surviving priestly office. Their arrangements repeatedly change; there is no single map that remains valid throughout.

A maritime enemy descended from the older rival shelters fugitives and interferes with inner-sea supply. Thalion depends on a trusted naval organiser to overcome him. The final conflict with the lieutenant and queen is fought through ports, grain movements, highland approaches and shifting allies. Victory in the east allows Thalion to return to Caleran around 350 BR with undisputed military predominance. His final seizure and settlement preserve the existing foundation date; earlier participation in a joint government does not become another sole imperial foundation.

The lieutenant could be born around 408 and the queen around 392, placing both within ordinary adult lives throughout. These are arithmetic checks, not fixed birthdays or genealogies.

### Relationships and resulting government

Thalion’s connection to the conqueror supplies inheritance; service and material rewards supply allegiance. An independent naval organiser supplies victories Thalion cannot achieve alone. A sister or close female relative supports peace through marriage to the lieutenant, then has a continuing household to protect after that alliance fails. The queen’s children possess claims dangerous to the victors, while some dependants can survive into other political lives.

Thalion’s settlement must deal with the people it has displaced. Taxes, provincial appointment and army organisation make rewards more regular; they also keep resources under his command. Astaroth’s priesthood lends religious language and public ceremony to the settlement. Older cults remain and can resent the new priority. Ordinary gratitude for peace does not settle the historical guilt of the killings which preceded it.

**Strength:** the existing founder acquires an extensive personal history while retaining his established date and reforms. The dead conqueror and the living ruler leave competing reputations, families and provincial memories.

**Cost:** the conqueror will require substantial space and a distinct name when drafting begins. Readers must be able to distinguish him from Thalion before meeting the assassination.

## 4. Alternative B — Thalion is the murdered conqueror

### The founding generation

In this version Thalion rises within an older republic and gains southern command through cooperation with a wealthy patron and an established eastern commander. His family can inherit Aurin civic standing without descending directly from Avren, Caleth or the religious couple. His distinction emerges from an adult career rather than a prophecy connecting every founder.

Thalion’s campaigns enlarge the republic’s power and his own following. He becomes known personally among officers who have shared winters, wounds and difficult withdrawals. He also authorises executions, slave sales and punitive mutilation. A returning veteran may love him for remembering a dead comrade while a provincial family knows him chiefly through the relative who came home without hands.

The wealthy partner’s failed northern war removes one source of balance. The older rival gains support from cities and senators frightened by Thalion’s growing command. The relationship deteriorates through specific decisions about demobilisation, prosecutions and the disposition of allied communities. Thalion fights his way to Caleran around 350 BR and begins the reforms currently assigned to him.

His victory leaves the older rival seeking protection abroad. The foreign dynasty’s intervention and the queen’s return to power establish a relationship with Thalion which later makes her both useful and vulnerable. Back in Caleran, he assigns offices, honours veterans, changes provincial government and elevates Astaroth’s public standing. The government becomes more effective in some respects while access increasingly passes through him.

Thalion is assassinated around 342 BR. One killer owes his career to him; another had accepted pardon; a third believes his continued power makes ordinary civic independence impossible. Their coalition agrees more completely about removing Thalion than about paying his armies or governing his provinces. His death interrupts reforms already in operation and gives loyalists a founder whose public image can be developed without his correction.

A young heir, the experienced lieutenant and a third regional commander eventually establish joint rule. The heir claims that protecting Thalion’s settlement requires punishing his murderers. He also accepts killings and confiscations which Thalion’s later memorial cult conveniently attributes to other partners. The lieutenant continues an eastern relationship with the queen, whose children may claim descent or recognition from the dead ruler. The surviving family can therefore dispute both personal inheritance and imperial legitimacy.

The succession wars continue into the 320s. Naval supply, veteran settlement, a mediator’s marriage and the third partner’s removal furnish distinct stages. The heir’s naval organiser helps defeat a dispossessed rival and the queen’s coalition. Around 322 BR the heir establishes lasting imperial government, incorporating Thalion’s reforms while altering their operation. Atherian public ceremony increasingly calls the result the fulfilment of Thalion’s intentions.

A queen born around 373 is about 23 during Thalion’s seizure and about 51 at the final defeat, allowing an adult royal alliance and a substantial independent reign. The longer southern sequence contains separate campaigns and intervening commands rather than eighteen years of continuous fighting.

### Relationships and resulting government

The heir’s need to preserve Thalion’s reputation becomes a source of conflict with witnesses. A celebrated act of clemency can conceal earlier violence; a former officer can remember a command the public funeral account omits. Thalion’s surviving household may resist what is done in his name. The queen’s connection to him adds another claimant with access to a distinct polity’s memories and resources.

The lasting founder inherits a working army and provincial experiments but must create his own settlement. His imperial government gains stability through institutions attributed to Thalion, changes made during the three-command years and further adjustments after final victory. The private comparison should name who actually introduced each measure even where later inscriptions credit the dead founder.

**Strength:** Thalion becomes the central tragic conqueror, and his contested memory shapes an entire successor generation. A murdered founder’s household and cult receive exceptional historical importance.

**Cost:** current authorial statements identifying Thalion as the man who ended the civil wars and made command hereditary must change. The Age of Glory and the meaning of the Unification require an explicit chronological decision.

For a consistent comparison, this model treats **350 BR as the later commemorated founding epoch**, **350–322 as founding and succession wars**, and **322 BR as the beginning of settled imperial predominance**. It does not pretend that those distinctions are already canon.

The south is colder. A further southern expedition analogous to Caesar’s British ventures can reach an outer coast or island community, exact destination unselected, and withdraw without occupation. It must not establish early contact with the Last Houses, the distant mountain people or another uncontacted world.

A central Thalassian blockade interrupts eastern trade and military supply. Its effect on Caleran passes through interrupted inland deliveries, reduced revenues and allied ports. The final campaign also needs a landward route to the Aurin; the inner sea does not suddenly contain Caleran.

The failed northern expedition borrows Carrhae’s overconfidence, enemy organisation, loss of a son or close companion and failed withdrawal. It does **not** place the later Tarkhan horse hordes in the fourth century BR. Defenders could combine camel-supported archers, foot ambushes, defended water and mobile supply. Tactical particulars require regional development before promotion.

### Q-1: a kingdom in the later Qabir–Zanthir gateway

The existing Zanthir overview establishes irrigation, shipping, river customs and caravan exchange where desert and Thalassian meet. This is the strongest physical combination for an independently wealthy queen whose support matters to an eastern commander.

The ancient polity now has an established mixed population and a history of republican defeat and royal recovery. Its proper name and dynasty remain open. The spared towns and Second Harbour supply its cultivated revenue, shipping and local institutions. Royal rivals can compete for those resources, temple patronage and foreign recognition; the imperial-founding draft must begin from that inherited country.

A coastal or inland ruling family of maritime origin could claim an older local sacred inheritance, recalling the distance between Ptolemaic dynasty and the larger country it governed. The current emirate, Banu Mizan, Velian Church and Covenant institutions remain much later developments.

**Giant inheritance:** a local canal estate, reused stone or recovered royal imagery is plausible, but the sources do not locate Ishkatar’s capital or its full river here. Any Ishkatari object must have a demonstrated local or traded history. There is no restored Serpent’s Spine, undiluted Pharaoh-Serpent line or automatic Avarix patronage.

**Outstanding requirement:** choose the local or incoming dynasty route, then locate its seat, immediate supporting towns and campaign approaches through the existing geography. Preserve the distinction between Second Harbour and the ruined republican capital. Neither is automatically the modern city of Qabir.

### Q-2: a river-and-estate kingdom in the later Fiorenzian country

Fiorenzia has an established pre-Atherian river city, fertile highland valleys, vineyards and surrounding estates. It offers a queen whose power rests on cultivated land, workshops and access to neighbouring ports. The kingdom’s naval capacity would come through negotiated or coerced harbour relationships.

Its inhabitants and dynastic divisions would be part of the peninsula’s pre-Concord history. Atherian financiers could support rival claimants, making debt and personal alliance important without importing the modern Medori Bank. A queen can maintain a learned court and substantial army while remaining dependent on coastal allies.

**Giant inheritance:** none is securely assigned to this specific ancient polity. Ruin reuse may be proposed individually. The queen’s public lineage can draw on older human founders rather than requiring Egyptian-looking tombs.

**Cost:** the region is inland; a major royal fleet requires additional coastal allies. This produces a distinctive political setting but weakens the direct combination of irrigated kingdom, eastern command and independent naval power.

### Q-3: an Elmarai island crown linked to Naverion

The existing Saronikan record supplies island production, defended harbours, naval work and ancient Atherian communities. A temporary crown over selected islands and allied ports could command pilots, provisions and strategically important winter anchorages.

The queen would belong to a maritime-derived world already related to Caleran. Her independent royal household could interpret the Common Sky differently and maintain local sea practices. Marriage into inland or island families might support her rule without implying a distinct biological civilisation.

**Giant inheritance:** the available material does not establish a local Giant kingdom or a continuous ancient royal line. Any such addition would need separate support. The later Saromen dynasty and Queen Thaleia IV are not projected backwards.

**Cost:** island cultivation gives less reason for overwhelming grain wealth, and the queen’s cultural distance from Atheria is smaller. The resulting story becomes a conflict between related royal and civic traditions, with useful possibilities but a different emphasis from Cleopatra’s Egypt.

### Geographic recommendation

**Q-1's broad location has been selected and integrated.** Its agriculture, shipping, caravan connections and spared-town history now govern the comparison. Q-2's land-rich kingdom and Q-3's island crown remain records of earlier alternatives; neither receives a duplicate royal history through this programme.

None of these candidates is Ishkatar surviving unchanged for nearly two millennia. The comparison deliberately avoids assigning unplaced ancient geography simply because a real-world resemblance is attractive.

## 7. Four limited supernatural possibilities

### A siege bargain involving Astaroth

An engineer serving the conqueror seeks the weakness in one fortress’s load-bearing structure. The agreed test risks a valuable engine under genuine resistance; the result reveals a particular fracture. Soldiers still have to approach it, build works and survive the defenders.

The commander publicly calls the breach proof of his right to rule. Surviving defenders know that stone failed under force. Astaroth’s actual involvement is limited to testing and structural knowledge; the sack and treatment of captives remain human decisions. This proposal uses the existing sovereignty’s bargain terms and creates no new war god.

### A winter route involving Hecatara

A retreating force accepts a shortened passage through one wooded crossing. Its commander agrees to return by the long road and gives up another local exit as the price. Wounded soldiers survive the shortened march; inhabitants relying on the closed route have to move food elsewhere, and some cannot escape a later raid.

A guide may warn explicitly of the cost. The choice still belongs to the commander. This is a limited ancient passage, not a permanent teleport network, a human soul-road or the explanation of a protected disappearance.

### A royal fleet’s agreement with Thalassor

During a dangerous withdrawal, the queen’s pilots obtain one safe current by surrendering cargo and allowing a named abandoned harbour-work to flood. Officers then load treasure onto the vessels which should carry wounded people, leaving some behind. The current works for the agreed crossing; it guarantees no victory after arrival.

The sea does not judge whether the queen’s cause is rightful, and drowned people do not become its property as souls. The officers’ selection of passengers creates the culpability. This remains separate from the Court of Salt and Teeth and the mythic ship of the Thirty-First Crew.

### A flattering image exploited by Pride

An unnamed dependent Mask adopts imagery from a conqueror’s victory cult and supplies convincing dreams to selected followers. It rewards deliberate humiliation, comparison and the claim that a ruler’s greatness excuses injury. Belief contributes images and practices; it does not create the Idol.

After the conqueror's death, the same victory imagery could become the object of competition among heirs, officers and civic patrons. The current preferred use concerns the living people's appropriation of his achievements and the Mask's exploitation of public superiority. The earlier idea of a dead ruler's apparition urging revenge is set aside in this draft because High Harbour already contains that particular deception. No supernatural impersonation is needed to make the successors choose murder or confiscation.

This proposal is **not** the Sarven Mask, a return of the Giant kings or the protected Crowned Reflection. It establishes no autonomous Archmask or Regnant succession. Its exact cult and incidents would be selected only during drafting.

### Common limits

All proposed rulers, assassins, spouses, children, engineers and soldiers remain complete human-soul persons. A human’s final judgement is unassigned. Neither a throne, a title, an inherited name nor an image preserves or reproduces that soul. All apparent apotheosis must be analysed as public worship, false identification or a separately classified manifestation.

A court envoy is an individual person under an existing ecology, not the Menhir in full. Astaroth, Hecatara and Thalassor remain personally continuous sovereignties. No pre-Redeemer manifested Saint, inherited prophetic commission, new divine mandate for conquest or invented soul-return is introduced.
