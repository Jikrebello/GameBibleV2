# The Glass Sojourn — Refuge, Ethnogenesis, and the Last Witness

> [!note] Sequence superseded — 2026-08-31
> This dossier remains the research basis for the long Sojourn and formation of one Glass people. Its older shorthand of three direct successor crystallisations is retained as provenance but has been superseded by [[The Two Fissions - Canon Promotion Review]].

> **Promotion state:** the recommended long-sojourn ruling was approved and promoted into [[The Glass Sojourn]], [[The Great Unbinding]], [[Age of Exodus]], [[Great Glass Desert]], [[Age of Scattering]] and the controlling historical spine. This dossier retains the research, alternatives and retcon audit; the canon notes control.

## Research Question

Could the Lord have sent the Glass Ark deliberately into the [[Great Glass Desert]] as shelter from the collapse of the Giant world, sustained its mixed population there for several generations, and formed it into one people before the Great Prophet's death and the later division into proto-Jirahar, proto-Ahl-e-Ahd and proto-Atherian peoples?

**Research ruling:** yes, and this is stronger than treating the Glass as a difficult transit corridor. It gives the Ark a purpose beyond evacuation, makes the Prophet's people a historical society rather than a queue of refugees, and explains why three later civilizations inherit recognisably related law, measures, stories and institutions without having entered the Glass as three nations.

The cleanest form is a **two-sanctuary distinction**:

1. the **Glass refuge** is commanded, providentially sustained and substantially successful at preserving and forming the host;
2. the later **southern sanctuary for three generations** is not successfully established after the people are commanded to leave the Glass.

The Prophet dies at or near the outward threshold. The already-formed people then moves south without its last living witness to the Giant world and divides through route, counsel, marriage, ecology, political conflict and competing interpretations of the inheritance.

Nothing in this proposal explains the origin, nature or purpose of the Glass. Providence uses a pre-existing place; the proposal does not claim that the Lord created the Glass for the Exodus or that its hostility to Giant settlement reveals what it is.

# I. The Scriptural and Orthodox Grammar

## 1. A mixed host may become one people

Exodus does not present escape as the work of an already simple, socially homogeneous caravan. A mixed multitude accompanies Israel in Exodus 12:38. The wilderness narrative then orders households, assemblies, camps, judges, priestly duties, craftsmen, feasts, rations, purity rules, inheritance and collective memory. Its governing literary movement is not merely **slaves travel to land**, but **a delivered population is taught how to live as a people**.

That makes the strongest World Egg transformation:

> many subject populations answer one call → one camp life binds their children → one people leaves the Glass → several later peoples divide the inheritance.

Modern scholarship disputes the historical reconstruction of Israel's origins, but even competing models regularly use mixed-population ethnogenesis to explain how displaced, indigenous and migrant communities can acquire one identity. The useful point for worldbuilding is not to import one archaeological theory as fact. It is that shared law, ritual, danger, marriage, memory and institutions can make a real people without requiring pure descent ([Oxford Handbook overview of early Israelite ethnogenesis](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/early-israels-origins-settlement-and-ethnogenesis/), [*Becoming Israel in the Wilderness of Numbers*](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/34438/chapter-abstract/292230572)).

## 2. The wilderness is preservation, discipline and disclosure

The Orthodox Church's own survey of Numbers describes the wilderness people as precarious, murmuring and rebellious while also being guided, sustained and disciplined so that they learn dependence upon God. Deuteronomy makes the forty years a remembered road of humbling, testing, manna and bodily preservation. The Orthodox liturgical tradition repeatedly reads the forty-year wilderness as a pattern of struggle and preparation rather than effortless refuge ([OCA Old Testament survey](https://orthinc.oca.org/article/The%2BOld%2BTestament), [OCA on Great Lent and the forty years](https://orthinc.oca.org/article/Great%2BLent%2Band%2BPascha), [NETS Septuagint edition](https://septuaginta.uni-goettingen.de/ioscs/nets/edition/)).

This prevents the Glass sojourn from becoming an idyllic hidden age. The Lord's provision keeps the people alive; it does not make every choice righteous, prevent all death, or spare them the consequences of treachery, hoarding, cowardice, idolatry and violent faction.

## 3. Provision forms institutions

The useful scriptural patterns are concrete:

- **Exodus 16:** daily provision is gathered under rules; attempted hoarding fails; the rest-day changes the ration cycle.
- **Exodus 17 and Numbers 20:** water is a public crisis involving complaint, leadership and commanded provision rather than private wonder-working.
- **Exodus 18:** delegated judges prevent one prophet from becoming the whole government.
- **Exodus 25–40:** artisans, stores, measurements and portable sacred space turn craft into common service.
- **Numbers 1–10:** counting, camp order, offices, burdens and departure signals make movement governable.
- **Deuteronomy 6 and 8:** parents teach children who did not personally see the first deliverance; provision becomes memory and warning against pride.
- **Nehemiah 9:** later confession holds together pillar, instruction, manna, water, mercy and the people's repeated disobedience.

The World Egg should therefore join miracle and labour. High-sign provision answers what human work cannot. Bakers still ration, herders still breed, smiths still repair, midwives still deliver, judges still hear disputes, diggers still bury the dead and the [[The Last Cart|Last Cart]] still carries people whom stronger households would abandon.

## 4. The New Testament warns against triumphal memory

Saint Paul reads the passage, food and drink of the wilderness typologically, but immediately stresses that many fell through idolatry, lust, testing and complaint. Hebrews similarly treats the wilderness generation as a warning about unbelief rather than proof that participation in mighty events guarantees faithfulness ([1 Corinthians 10](https://www.oca.org/readings/reference/1-corinthians/10), [Hebrews 3 in the OCA lectionary](https://www.oca.org/readings/daily/2043/01/19/1)).

For the World Egg, this means:

- the pre-Redeemer people are a real commanded assembly, but not the post-Redeemer Church;
- provision proves the Lord's particular commission, not the moral innocence of every recipient;
- the Prophet remains a servant and mediator, not the source of the signs;
- later Jirahar, Ahl-e-Ahd or Atherian claims cannot convert Ark ancestry into automatic righteousness;
- the Redeemer fulfills and judges the inherited witness rather than merely inheriting the Prophet's office.

# II. Promoted Historical Architecture

## Stage 1 — Convergence under collapse

The Prophet's warning, plagues of unbinding, local revolts, Giant civil wars and heavenly severance still produce several moving columns rather than one nation leaving one capital. Households arrive with different languages, bodily adaptations, trades, injuries, Giant administrative habits, local cult memories and degrees of complicity in the old order.

The host is selected by response and practical gathering, not by ethnicity. It includes former slaves, free client populations, defecting soldiers, repentant collaborators, giant-blooded humans with complete `human-soul`, artisans, herders, physicians, burial fraternities, children and dependants.

## Stage 2 — The commanded withdrawal into Glass

The Lord directs the Prophet into the Glass because the Giant collapse is not a single defeated army. Outside the refuge, irrigation systems fail, war-broods escape, dynasties fight, Titan fragments are bound or scattered, pursuit coalitions form, subject cities starve and abandoned continuity machines continue their last commands.

The Glass is deadly to ordinary settlement, but the Giant powers never established a permanent imperial road, dynastic city or self-sustaining colony within it. This should become an empirical historical limit:

- Giants may reach the margins;
- pursuit forces may enter and die;
- temporary survey camps, corpses, instruments or failed stations may survive;
- no Giant horizon ever holds a permanent tax district, stable lineage estate or functioning city in the deep Glass.

Why this is so remains unresolved. It must not become a discoverable anti-Giant frequency, Titan poison, Menhir weapon or hidden design feature of the Glass.

## Stage 3 — A long refuge, not one continuous march

The survivable road leads into a sequence of protected anchor regions rather than requiring a century of uninterrupted walking. Camps split for grazing, salvage, sanitation and disease control, then reassemble at appointed seasons or signals. Some sites last months; others hold a generation. The people move because provision, water, safety or command moves—not because every day is a travel montage.

The Glass refuge is therefore analogous to an ark in a deluge of kingdoms:

- the catastrophe occurs chiefly outside it;
- the refuge remains dangerous and cramped;
- survival requires preservation of humans, animals, seed, tools, records and crafts;
- the inhabitants cannot make the surrounding world habitable by their own power;
- the refuge produces a new common life while old political identities lose practical meaning.

## Stage 4 — The one camp-formed people

By the later sojourn, the descendants of Qianglong estates, Ishkatari river households, Xolotlan subject cities, Nythalum harbours, Ansharan escapees and lesser Giant states no longer function as separate refugee nations. They remember origins, but marry, litigate, worship, work and bury through common institutions.

They become one people through observable mechanisms:

1. **common worship and calendar:** appointed assemblies, fasts, feasts, rest and recitation order time;
2. **common law:** no Giant court remains as appellate authority; the camp's rulings become the only workable public law;
3. **common ration and water discipline:** survival depends upon rules applying across former status;
4. **common register:** births, deaths, marriages, households, skills and dependants are recorded under camp categories rather than imperial caste;
5. **common service:** burden-bearing, defence, sanitation, herding, burial, craft and care mix origin groups in recurring duties;
6. **common vernacular:** interpreters create a camp speech that children acquire as their first public language;
7. **intermarriage and adoption:** households join across former imperial and servile identities;
8. **common graves and remembered enemies:** children inherit the same dead, deliverance and exterior catastrophe;
9. **one generation of teachers:** camp-born adults receive the old world through testimony rather than personal allegiance to its states;
10. **boundary maintenance:** admission, expulsion, restitution and return define who belongs without pretending that ancestry alone decides it.

This is ethnogenesis, not bodily homogenisation. Families keep different appearances, foods, private memories and crafts. “One people” means a durable shared identity, law and historical consciousness.

## Stage 5 — The last witness

By the Prophet's final years, he is the only living human who remembers the Giant world from direct adult experience. The children born before entry have died; the Glass-born know the old empires through testimony, objects, wounds and surviving records.

This makes his old age historically dangerous as well as sacred:

- he alone can correct a confident but false reconstruction from memory;
- rival secretaries can truthfully preserve different parts of his final recollections;
- younger leaders may honour him while quietly believing his political instincts belong to a dead world;
- stories collect around ordinary remarks because every remembered comparison becomes unique evidence;
- his death changes not only leadership but the human relationship to the past: history becomes entirely mediated.

His longevity is a bounded personal mercy or burden. It does not make him more than human, grant immortality, establish a long-lived bloodline or prove that later prophetic claimants share his commission.

## Stage 6 — Departure, death and failed southern sanctuary

The Glass is refuge and school, not the promised permanent human homeland. After the outside collapse has passed into successor history, the people are commanded to leave and establish a sanctuary in the southern lands capable of holding common law and peace for three generations.

The Prophet dies at or near the outward threshold, before the settlement is securely founded. Possible traditions may still accuse him of refusal, describe murder, remember exhaustion, or argue that the people rejected the last command. The authorial retcon need not settle that dispute.

The people then moves south and divides. The failure is not that they never became one. The wound is that **a real unity formed under deliverance does not survive land, succession and possession**.

Three major crystallisations emerge:

| Later family | What it principally carries from the common people | Characteristic distortion or loss |
|---|---|---|
| **proto-Jirahar** | commandment, name registers, portable courts, road memory, burial obligation, expectation | may identify faithful memory too tightly with its own custodians |
| **proto-Ahl-e-Ahd** | oath, water allotment, guest protection, witness, restitution, enforceable camp obligation | may turn covenantal relation into autonomous contract machinery |
| **proto-Atherian** | crafts, measures, military service, archives, public works, civic deliberation | may preserve the form of ordered life while sanding away the Lord and Prophet |

None carries the whole inheritance, and none is merely a degraded copy of another.

# III. How Long Should the Sojourn Last?

The biblical forty years is thematically powerful but does not meet the strict memory goal. In the biblical account Moses dies at 120 after forty wilderness years, while other people who entered as children or younger adults can still remember Egypt. World Egg should use forty as a liturgical and narrative grammar, not copy the number automatically ([OCA Life of Moses](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/1985/09/04/102490-holy-prophet-and-god-seer-moses)).

| Model | Historical result | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **about 40 years** | one wilderness generation; strong transparent Mosaic parallel | too many living entrants remember the Giant world; host remains visibly a coalition of parents' peoples |
| **about 70–90 years** | almost every adult eyewitness is dead; two or three Glass-born generations hold office | the Prophet can be the last **full adult** witness, but a few very old people may retain childhood memories |
| **about 100–120 years** | three to four camp-born generations; the Prophet can literally be the last human with direct memory | requires exceptional but finite prophetic longevity and moves the traditional death date substantially |

**Recommended bracket: approximately a century, with the exact count left disputed.** Authorially, the Prophet is the last direct living witness. Later traditions can remember forty camps, forty judgements, forty law-readings or forty years because sacred number and historical duration need not be the same claim.

The approximately century-long version gives enough time for:

- the camp vernacular to replace several first languages in public life;
- intermarriage to cross most founding origin groups;
- apprentices trained in Glass to train their own apprentices;
- children with no memory of slavery to become judges and commanders;
- former imperial caste categories to become antiquarian or polemical labels;
- the Prophet to become a living discontinuity between the old world and the new.

# IV. Providence without Hand-Waving

## What the Lord supplies

The sustained commission may include:

- food appearing or becoming gatherable according to a fixed daily and rest-cycle;
- water at named crises and appointed stations;
- shade, orientation and safe timing through lethal conditions;
- preservation of clothing, leather, seed, medicines and essential tools beyond normal endurance;
- protection against the final Giant pursuit and particular Glass phenomena;
- commands identifying when a camp must remain, divide, rejoin or move.

## What human beings still do

- gather, mill, cook and distribute food;
- dig drains, latrines and graves;
- repair carts, harness, tents and containers;
- maintain herds and seed stocks;
- isolate sickness and care for the injured;
- translate testimony and teach children;
- adjudicate theft, assault, adultery, debt, homicide and inheritance;
- defend the rear and recover stragglers;
- confess, rebel, hoard, reconcile, murder, forgive and remember.

The signs do not provide ready-made cities, self-writing law or automatic obedience. The historical mechanism is:

> exterior collapse makes return impossible → the Lord commands entry and supplies impossible necessities → camp institutions allocate provision and labour → generations born under those institutions acquire one identity → the command to leave reintroduces land, possession and succession → the Prophet dies → the people fractures while carrying a common civilizational witness.

## Hard edge retained

Provision does not mean nobody dies. The Glass contains graves. Children die of ordinary illness and accident; people are killed in faction, punishment and crime; some abandon the camp and perish; some try to recreate Giant rank; some hoard what should be distributed; some make or invite false sacred powers; some leaders use the emergency to enlarge their office.

The exact rebellions and judgements should be planted separately so that the sojourn does not become a renamed compilation of Exodus and Numbers. What must remain is the source's moral pressure: the delivered can prefer bondage, witnesses of miracles can commit idolatry, leaders can fail under commission, and a generation can be sustained without being vindicated.

# V. The Civilizational Witness

The Glass people do not emerge merely with better technology. They carry a tested **grammar of free civilization**:

- a human being cannot be owned as Giant property;
- rulers, judges, priests and commanders hold differentiated offices rather than divine blood-right;
- names and graves belong in public memory;
- weights, rations and measures answer to law;
- strangers, widows, orphans, debtors and dependants possess claims that strength alone cannot erase;
- worship places political power under judgement;
- skilled work is a public inheritance transmitted by apprenticeship;
- emergency authority must eventually explain why it continues;
- rest, feast, mourning and teaching structure time beyond production;
- no miracle gives its human recipient unlimited authority.

This witness is better than much of what survives outside the Ark, but Ark descendants can betray it and Orphaned peoples can independently recover true law, mercy and craft. The Ark explains an institutional advantage, not superior human nature.

# VI. Ancestry Architecture beside the Sojourn

| Historical class | Current author-only ruling | Relationship to the Glass people |
|---|---|---|
| **Ark-derived** | proto-Jirahar, proto-Ahl-e-Ahd, proto-Atherian | direct later fissions of the one camp-formed people |
| **Orphaned remnants** | Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan, Nsizwa | principally descend from human communities surviving the Giant fall outside the Ark; each has an independent reassembly history |
| **Braided peoples** | Sestaran harbor populations, Vesari, later Eclessians | later historical mixtures of Ark-derived, Orphaned and other local peoples |

Eclessian braiding should be stated particularly clearly. Atherian provincial institutions and settlers join Folk substrates; Markish peoples form through frontier mixture; Vesari river populations later enter through Virelia; migrations, conquest, conversion and refuge add further layers. The Celtic–Germanic–Italic comparison is useful creator-facing structure, not an in-world claim that one present Eclessian nation equals one real people.

Nsizwa Orphaned descent should shape, but not prematurely explain, their ancestor traditions. Their communities may have preserved bodies, names, war offices and recurrence procedures through the orphaned world. Every later apparition still requires the established distinction among human soul, residue, tomb-bound office and constructed pattern.

# VII. Recoverable Evidence in 1360 AR

The approved long sojourn should leave evidence stronger than one holy chronicle:

1. **occupation archaeology:** several anchor camps show rebuilding, cemeteries and waste layers spanning generations rather than a brief crossing;
2. **demography:** early graves preserve many adult birth-origins; late graves show almost nobody born outside the Glass;
3. **language:** Jirahar, early Ahdari and archaic Atherian share a public-administrative substrate absent from Orphaned languages, while domestic loanwords retain several Giant-horizon sources;
4. **measures:** later rival systems reduce to one camp ration, cord and burden family;
5. **law:** separate traditions preserve recognisably related rulings on witness, water, burial, adoption and emergency office;
6. **craft transmission:** tool marks and apprenticeship signs repeat across anchor camps and the earliest southern settlements;
7. **register form:** early lists classify households by origin; later lists classify them by camp duty, district and kinship;
8. **palaeopathology:** skeletons show chronic Glass travel stresses followed by later camp stability, then renewed southward movement;
9. **negative Giant evidence:** Giant survey debris exists at margins, but no deep-Glass site contains the demographic and fiscal layers of a permanent Giant settlement;
10. **conflicting death traditions:** every early successor tradition knows the Prophet as an old man at the outward threshold but disagrees about the failure and death.

No single relic proves the full reconstruction. A credible 1360 conclusion requires at least archaeology plus one independent linguistic, legal, biological or textual carrier.

## Material revised through promotion

1. **Chronology:** the Great Unbinding's c. 2300–2260 BR collapse window is now distinct from the longer c. 2300–2160 BR Age of Exodus; the Prophet's death moves to c. 2165 BR and Ark Scattering begins by c. 2160 BR.
2. **Great Unbinding sequence:** the long Glass refuge now stands between entry and the failed southern sanctuary.
3. **Age of Exodus:** former transit language now states refuge, formation and eventual departure; Folk and Sestaran are no longer direct memory communities.
4. **Age of Scattering:** the age now begins with division of one Glass people while separately tracking Orphaned reassemblies and later Braided ethnogenesis.
5. **Historical Spine:** the former one-Exodus origin now distinguishes Ark formation, Orphaned survival and later braiding.
6. **Culture histories:** Vesari are no longer their own Exodus fragment; Nsizwa are Orphaned; early Sestaran populations are Braided; Eclessian layering is explicit.
7. **Prophet's calendar:** any future exact conversion between BR dating and the Jirahar count must use the c. 2165 BR death tradition.

# IX. Promoted Authorial Ruling

> [!note] Subsequent sequence ruling — 2026-08-31
> Points 7 and the older “Ark Scattering” row below preserve the first promotion's research history but no longer control the order of ethnogenesis. [[The Two Fissions - Canon Promotion Review]] now fixes a first southern war-settlement fission followed generations later by the northern proto-Jirahar/proto-Ahl-e-Ahd fission. The long Sojourn, one-people formation, Orphaned classifications and Braided classifications remain unchanged.

The approved retcon fixes the following:

1. The Lord deliberately sends the principal remnant into the Glass to shelter it from the wider collapse.
2. Giant powers had never established permanent deep-Glass settlement, although temporary incursions and marginal remains may exist.
3. The Lord sustains the host through bounded high-sign provision joined to indispensable human labour.
4. The sojourn lasts approximately a century and forms the mixed host into one real people.
5. At his death, the Great Prophet is the last living human with direct memory of the Giant world.
6. The Prophet dies at the outward threshold before the southern sanctuary is securely established.
7. Proto-Jirahar, proto-Ahl-e-Ahd and proto-Atherians form only after this death and departure.
8. Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan and Nsizwa are principally Orphaned remnants.
9. Sestaran harbor populations, Vesari and later Eclessians are Braided peoples formed at different later moments.

Leave open:

- the exact number of years, camps and dead;
- the Glass people's endonym;
- the form and schedule of each provision-sign;
- the complete list of wilderness rebellions and judgements;
- whether the Prophet's death was failure, murder, exhaustion, judgement or mystery;
- the identity of the first post-Prophet faction leaders;
- the precise point at which each later people becomes recognisable;
- why Giants could not hold the deep Glass;
- every protected Weird associated with the Glass.
