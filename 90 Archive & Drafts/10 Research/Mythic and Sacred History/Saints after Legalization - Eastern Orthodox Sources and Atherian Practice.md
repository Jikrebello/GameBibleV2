# Saints after Legalization

In Cassian's reign, people can visit a grave together, read a prison letter in public or carry a sacred object openly. The risks change. A bishop needs to decide whether a relic offered to a new basilica came from the named martyr. A family needs to know whether giving custody to a church will cost it access. The imperial household can finance a feast or shrine and press for the emperor to appear in its prayers.

## Early accounts and Eastern Orthodox traditions

The first three entries below use early texts close to the communities that remembered the events. The other entries describe the stories received in Eastern Orthodox worship and devotion. The date of a martyr's death, the date of the surviving Life and the date when a shrine begins to report miracles can be centuries apart.

| Saint or tradition | Death, reported signs and what the community did |
|---|---|
| **Polycarp of Smyrna** | Smyrna's church writes that a fire curved around him at the pyre and that an executioner stabbed him. The authorities burned the body, and believers gathered the bones. The letter links their custody to an annual gathering on the anniversary of his death. The congregation also explains the difference between its love for martyrs and its worship of Christ. [*Martyrdom of Polycarp*, chapters 15–18](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0102.htm) |
| **Perpetua and Felicity of Carthage** | The Passion preserves prison visions in Perpetua's voice, including the ladder and the vision of her brother Dinocrates. Felicity, an enslaved woman, gives birth in prison. A fellow believer receives the child. Both women face the arena and are killed by the sword. The account preserves nursing, adoption, prison visits and the experiences of the prisoners alongside the extraordinary signs. [*Passion of Perpetua and Felicity*](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0324.htm) |
| **Blandina and the martyrs of Lyons and Vienne** | In the churches' letter, Blandina survives repeated torture, encourages the younger Ponticus, and is thrown in a net before a bull and finally killed. Earlier, companions see the suspended Blandina as an image of the crucified Christ. The authorities burn the remains and throw the ashes into the Rhône. The letter carries names and testimony that the authorities tried to extinguish along with the bodies. [Eusebius, *Church History* V.1](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250105.htm) |
| **The Forty Martyrs of Sebaste** | In the received Life, soldiers refusing sacrifice are exposed on a frozen lake. One leaves for warmth. A guard sees heavenly crowns and joins those on the ice. In the Greek Orthodox account the dying men are burned and their remains thrown into the river. The feast remembers them by name and prays for healing. Its setting under Licinius makes it a model for military and local persecution persisting amid shifts in imperial policy. [Greek Orthodox Archdiocese, Forty Martyrs reading and hymns](https://www.goarch.org/chapel/saints?contentid=454&language=en) |
| **Panteleimon of Nicomedia** | The Orthodox Life remembers a physician who treats the sick and poor, survives tortures and is ultimately beheaded. His Life tells of a sword that softens during prayer and of the olive tree at his execution bearing fruit. His continuing remembrance as a healer gives a martyr's shrine work with the sick as well as a public account of his death. [Orthodox Church in America, Panteleimon](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/1949/07/27/102099-greatmartyr-and-healer-panteleimon) |
| **Demetrios of Thessaloniki** | The received Life has soldiers kill him with spears while imprisoned. The same tradition associates fragrant myrrh with the crypt in later centuries. The narrative leaves room for a saint's relationship with a city to grow long after the execution. [Orthodox Church in America, Demetrios](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/2017/10/26/103059-holy-glorious-demetrios-the-myrrh-gusher-of-thessaloniki) |
| **Cyrus and John, Athanasia and her daughters** | Cyrus practises medicine without charge. He and John visit the arrested women and are themselves executed. The received Life specifies that the women are flogged and beheaded. Later generations associate Cyrus and John with healing and remember successive transfers of their relics. Their story connects the care of living prisoners with the later cult of the dead. [Orthodox Church in America, Cyrus and John](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/0579/01/31/100380-holy-wonderworkers-and-unmercenaries-cyrus-and-john-and-those-wi) |
| **Antony and Mary of Egypt** | In Athanasius's Life, Antony attributes healings to God and asks for a hidden burial when he dies. Mary's received Life tells of a penitent desert ascetic, of her walking across the Jordan to receive communion, and of a lion helping Zosimas bury her. Her Life is read in the Lenten cycle. These give later Atherian generations models for holiness through repentance, ordinary death, hidden graves and the discipline of remembering a Life. [Athanasius, *Life of Antony*, §§56–58 and 90–92](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2811.htm) · [Orthodox Church in America, Mary of Egypt](https://www.oca.org/saints/lives/20122/04/01/100963-venerable-mary-of-egypt) |
| **Constantine and Helen** | Constantine dies after receiving baptism. The Orthodox feast honours him and his mother Helen as Equals to the Apostles and remembers Helen's finding of the Cross. Eusebius describes the imperial shrine at the tomb and Helen's church patronage. The liturgical title and Cross tradition show how a ruler and an imperial household can become subjects of lasting devotion. [Eusebius, *Life of Constantine* III.25–47](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/25023.htm) · [Book IV.61–64](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/25024.htm) · [Orthodox feast and hymns](https://www.oca.org/saints/troparia/2035/05/21/101452-equal-of-the-apostles-and-emperor-constantine-with-his-mother-he) |

Orthodox glorification can begin with local remembrance and intercession, then gain a feast, hymns and an icon through recognition by a synod. Miracles can prompt inquiry. A holy life can be recognised in their absence. Eastern Orthodox practice also offers a later model for disputes about sacred images and veneration through the Seventh Ecumenical Council in 787. In the Cassian period, annual local observance, exchanging readings and tending graves can begin to grow in public. Formal synodal procedures, a settled calendar and later controversies over images need time to develop. [Orthodox Church in America, *Glorification of the Saints*](https://www.oca.org/fs/glorification-of-saints) · [Orthodox Church in America, the Seventh Ecumenical Council and sacred images](https://www.oca.org/reflections/misc-authors/fathers-seventh-ecumenical-council-relationship-image-person-redemptive-work-sacred-space)

## What each see can do with this

The deaths and signs already recorded in the Companions' Lives are the starting point. The acts of public commemoration below are proposals. A dated first feast can be settled when the local evidence is developed. [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]] · [[The Making of Saints]] · [[White Flame Doctrine]]

| See | Established life or object | Proposed practice after 292 |
|---|---|---|
| **Caleran** | Jareth was burned with his unfinished Gospel in 41. Its Ash Spine survived; its later home is the Sanctum. Calira died defending Amaryah's hearth in 29. Solana died in her sleep in 63 and her body remained free of decay for seven days. The daughter flame and the vessel venerated as Solana's jar reach Caleran during Cassian's reign. | Read Jareth's account aloud at the former forum, with the names of the condemned. Keep the flame tended through Calira's all-night vigil and let its household keepers instruct the public attendants. Solana's feast can honour a witness who survived and cared for others into old age. The court wants the bridge victory read at the forum. The bishop can give thanks for peace at the dedication while keeping the vigil for the dead. |
| **Aurelium** | Myra was stoned while sheltering children in a bakehouse. Later Myrine institutions receive foundlings. The records of weeping images belong to later cult and inquiry. | Read the names of children whose shelter is documented, then use a feast offering to fund bread, wages and beds. Retain accounts in the languages of the households that gave them. A patron can pay for the room and claim a cure at the opening; copyists preserve what the sick person, household and attendants actually report. |
| **Halmenth** | Soldiers drowned Naris in the Celion during a baptism. His body was never recovered. The Lives remember objects associated with him washing ashore. | Hold the vigil by the river and include the names of drowned people without graves. An object offered as Celion-wrack can be received as a local memorial. Custodians record where it was found, who identified it and which harbour brought it in before declaring it an authenticated relic. A founding donor offers to replace the hall roof if a purported bone of Naris is displayed. The stewards refuse the display and keep the vigil at the landing. |
| **Sermora** | Virella continued treating the imperial agent who slowly poisoned her. He converted at her deathbed and became the first writer of her Life. Aquilan's mission and memory already travel among the port assemblies. | Copy the agent's account alongside testimony from people she treated, and let ship chaplains and interpreters read it on the same annual day. Hold a day of free care for sailors and households. Keep the confession in circulation even when a patron offers to fund a shrine in exchange for a more flattering account of her death. |

## Two disputed cases worth carrying forward

- **The Forty Flames.** The canon account places the rising and subsequent furnace deaths in winter 290 AR. The prison roll records forty and the common burial records thirty-nine, because one family buried its dead privately. Public Ashwake observance begins under Cassian. Relic claims and the date of the Council of Second Flame remain open. The Sebaste comparison belongs with communal memory, names, annual prayer and contested access to remains. [[After Cassian - The Succession of 316 and the Forty Flames]] · [[The Forty Flames]] · [[The Twelve Companions of the Redeemer]]
- **The Witness at the Red Passage.** The Witness dies under torture c. 226 after holding a passage so prisoners and civilians can escape. The record also includes people abandoned or murdered during an earlier escape and names the Witness helped conceal. The identity of a red prison door is unresolved. A proposed early commemoration would read the names of the lost and collect aid for prisoners. A rival group might try to turn the feast into a list of alleged informers. This preserves the risk already present in the Life and gives bishops a reason to intervene before granting a shared feast. [[GS-01 - Witness at the Red Passage]]

A person can be honoured locally before other sees receive a Life or recognise a feast. Pilgrims can pray and receive help without possessing the body. A genuine manifestation in the World Egg has the scope of its particular commission, so it cannot settle every claim made by a relic's custodians. A signed deed can settle who keeps the building; the flame or saint remains free to answer. Cassian's patrons have reason to press for a shrine's most flattering account precisely because their titles and gifts are easier to record than the signs people report. ## Carried forward by Causality Pass 04 (14 November 2026)

- The closing principle above ("A genuine manifestation in the World Egg has the scope of its particular commission … A signed deed can settle who keeps the building; the flame or saint remains free to answer") is promoted as clause 1 of the Commission Rule in [[Signs and Proof - Authorial Truth]]. The cross-references for [[White Flame Doctrine]] and [[The Making of Saints]] are in the integration patch of [[Causality Pass - 04 Who May Authenticate a Sign, 0 to 411 AR|Pass 04]].
- The Halmenth stewards who refuse to display a bone in return for a new roof are the model scene for the bishops' sign hearings after the Dissolution of 381. The first such hearing is the Council of Second Flame (c. 382–383), which confirmed the Forty on the 291 sworn account. A hearing on the 297 model also settles the 320s dispute over [[The Apostle’s Bones]].
- In the Cassian period the Star colleges, not the sees, held the lawful power to say what a sign or relic was. The proposals in "What each see can do with this" therefore describe what the sees could do in communion; a find's standing in law stayed with the augurs until 381.
