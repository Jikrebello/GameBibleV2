# Primarch Function Atlas and World Egg Translation

> [!info] Atlas integration
> Current canonical destination: [[Great Saints and Sinners - Lives and Afterlives]]. This dossier retains its research history; former holds and promotion gates are superseded only where the [[World Historical Atlas - Integration and Coverage Ledger]] records an explicit outcome. Preliminary or rejected variants do not override their corrected descendants.

## Purpose and Date

This author-only atlas records the narrative function of all twenty Warhammer 40,000 Primarch designations as checked on **30 August 2026**. It distinguishes published setting fact from deliberately unresolved material and fan inference, then asks what each grammar can contribute to the World Egg without importing a race of demigods, a divine family, a twenty-person brotherhood, or a Horus-Heresy equivalent.

Nothing here enters World Egg canon. Warhammer supplies modern literary grammar only. Names, plots, Legion identities, genetic descent, distinctive visual designs, exact sequences, and protected setting terminology do not transfer.

## Confidence and Translation Rules

### Source confidence

- **A — current official:** directly stated in current Games Workshop or Warhammer Community material.
- **B — published canon:** stated in a Black Library, campaign, codex, or Horus Heresy source and consolidated by a reliable reference work.
- **C — seeded inference:** a plausible reading supported by published clues but not established as fact.
- **D — fan theory:** useful speculation with no controlling canonical answer.

### World Egg boundaries

1. A Greater Saint remains one redeemed `human-soul` acting through a bounded heavenly commission.
2. A Sinner remains a damned `human-soul`; devil office, incarnation, Mask, Patron role, residue, cult and legend-persona are separately classified.
3. No person is created as a demigod, genetic son of the Lord, substitute Redeemer, or member of a holy species.
4. Saintly power never validates every earthly act, follower, order, ruler or church claim.
5. Trauma, coercion and supernatural injury can explain pressure without erasing personal guilt.
6. Shared identity never merges, divides or manufactures human souls.
7. No translation creates one universal rebellion, one secret master fellowship, or a war that explains an age.

## The Twenty Studies

## I — Lion El'Jonson

- **Received source and current state:** **A/B.** The Lion has returned to the current era, moves through a supernatural forest-road, acts in Imperium Nihilus and receives some of the Fallen as the Risen. A future meeting with Guilliman is strongly expected but not yet resolved.
- **Essential function:** the secretive knight-king who is formidable against external enemies but damages his own house through distrust; age and loss create the possibility of mercy without making his earlier conduct harmless.
- **Hard edge:** secrecy becomes fratricidal suspicion. Loyal and guilty followers are punished together, the home polity breaks, and later descendants build an institution around concealing shame.
- **Institutional afterlife:** an order becomes more loyal to its secret and inherited hunt than to the founder's actual duty.
- **Return mechanism:** sleeping-king return combined with impossible travel and selective reconciliation.
- **World Egg lead:** a frontier hunter later manifested as a Greater Saint for one route-defence campaign, while an earthly order continues misusing his memory.
- **Hazards and collisions:** do not make a Saint privately patrol the Egg White, pardon guilt by fiat, or duplicate the Saint of the Two Ferries. Nearest bed: Long Rent War and War of the Last Map.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## II — The First Expunged Primarch

- **Received source and current state:** **B.** The Second was found, led his Legion and suffered a separate tragedy before the Heresy. Memories and records were suppressed. His warriors did not necessarily share his fate and were adapted to new circumstances. II Legion forces are now attested in the Rangdan Xenocides; that does not prove the war caused the Primarch's loss.
- **Established mystery function:** an erased founder whose followers remain historically useful, forcing later institutions to contain inherited practices without acknowledging their source.
- **Open theories:** **C/D.** Rangdan compromise, unacceptable mutation, ideological failure, execution by a brother, redistribution into other Legions, or imprisonment. None is fixed.
- **World Egg lead:** the institutionally erased person. Human authorities deliberately remove an identity after catastrophe and disperse followers and practices.
- **Hazards and collisions:** Heaven cannot forget a soul; the Lord does not falsify archives; erasure cannot become proof of greater guilt than every remembered evil.
- **Disposition:** `retain as absence study`; no invented biography.

## III — Fulgrim

- **Received source and current state:** **A/B.** Fulgrim is an active Daemon Primarch and returned to the current tabletop setting with the Emperor's Children in 2025. A physically convincing clone remains in Trazyn's collection, but no source establishes its release or the original person's return to virtue.
- **Essential function:** sincere artistry and love of excellence become comparison, appetite, possession and humiliation. The fall becomes irreversible when he kills the peer whose judgement mattered most.
- **Hard edge:** intimate betrayal, murder of a beloved friend, enslavement of followers to appetite, and the conversion of beauty into a permission structure for cruelty.
- **Institutional afterlife:** imitators preserve techniques and surfaces while eliminating restraint, work and truthful criticism.
- **Recurrence mechanism:** daemonic embodiment, cult performance and the dangerous independent existence of a convincing copy.
- **World Egg lead:** a conquering aesthete whose Sinner court manufactures perfected bodies and counterfeit reconciliations while the real human soul remains damned and distinct.
- **Hazards and collisions:** no possession may absorb all guilt; a copy is not the soul; do not duplicate Pride's Regnant question or make beauty inherently corrupt. Nearest beds: Crown-Market of Shadows and War for the Common Saints.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## IV — Perturabo

- **Received source and current state:** **A/B.** Perturabo is an active Daemon Primarch. Current 2026 material gives him the Infinite Citadel: linked fortress-worlds whose massacres thin reality so he can advance his project toward Terra.
- **Essential function:** the indispensable builder who keeps an exact account of every slight, refuses vulnerable petition, and eventually makes destruction the only proof that his work mattered.
- **Hard edge:** mass siege, expendable soldiers, punishment of subordinates for the commander's humiliation, enslavement of whole populations to construction, and the use of civilian death as architecture.
- **Institutional afterlife:** engineers inherit both genuine competence and a doctrine that suffering demonstrates seriousness.
- **Recurrence mechanism:** a fortress network supplies incarnation, jurisdiction and movement without becoming the person's soul.
- **World Egg lead:** a damned frontier mason whose infernal office turns linked bastions into a Material–Egg White siege geometry.
- **Hazards and collisions:** keep Patron anchor, infernal office, constructed body and human soul distinct; avoid duplicating the Long Rent War as a whole. Nearest bed: War of the Last Map.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## V — Jaghatai Khan

- **Received source and current state:** **B.** The Khan vanished while pursuing Drukhari into the Webway. Survival, captivity and return remain unconfirmed.
- **Essential function:** an outsider who recognizes that both respectable empire and obvious rebellion can become cages; speed and laughter protect a serious political commitment to freedom.
- **Hard edge:** conquest and raiding remain part of the culture he commands; refusal of tyranny does not prevent violent extraction or the abandonment of people who cannot keep pace.
- **Institutional afterlife:** descendants turn mobile adaptation into rigid ancestral performance.
- **Return mechanism:** absent rider beyond mapped roads.
- **World Egg lead:** a road-founder lost beyond a hostile correspondence who may have rejected both Church and Sinner claimants.
- **Hazards and collisions:** currently overlaps the Border Lamp and Keelman, wandering-duo material, sleeping heroes and existing Menhir roads without adding a sufficiently distinct pressure.
- **Disposition:** `defer`.

## VI — Leman Russ

- **Received source and current state:** **A/B.** Russ departed on a hidden quest and left a promise of return at the time of direst need; current Space Wolves material retains that expectation.
- **Essential function:** the cultivated barbarian and sanctioned executioner who is more perceptive than his public mask, but whose obedience still causes catastrophe.
- **Hard edge:** punitive war, destruction of a city of scholars, collective punishment and a later recognition that being ordered to strike did not remove responsibility for how he struck.
- **Institutional afterlife:** descendants imitate the boastful surface and inherit a return prophecy that can be politically exploited.
- **Return mechanism:** apocalyptic sleeping-warrior expectation.
- **World Egg lead:** a penitent war-founder whose promised return becomes a contested mobilisation doctrine.
- **Hazards and collisions:** overlaps the existing sleeping champion, Menhir hunt imagery, prophetic-return grammar and severe military saint leads. A commissioned Saint cannot remain on a private quest of his own choosing after judgement.
- **Disposition:** `defer`.

## VII — Rogal Dorn

- **Received source and current state:** **B/C.** Dorn is missing and generally presumed dead. Sources conflict over whether more than a hand was recovered. Later visions of his return are real in-world claims, not confirmation.
- **Essential function:** the perfect defender survives the fall of what he defended, then converts grief into self-punishment, fortification and an inability to stop fighting yesterday's siege.
- **Hard edge:** troops and civilians die within an inflexible defence; later penitential warfare consumes followers who mistake endurance for moral truth.
- **Institutional afterlife:** a hand or tool becomes the relic around which successor institutions argue about patience, revenge and rightful command.
- **Return mechanism:** ambiguous bodily death, contradictory remains and visionary resurgence.
- **World Egg lead:** a Fall-era redoubt commander later claimed as a wall saint, with one authentic bounded defence possible but no settled death tradition.
- **Hazards and collisions:** must remain distinct from Caedros I, the Returning Standard and existing imperial founder-saints. Nearest bed: Long Rent War.
- **Disposition:** `revise then retain`.

## VIII — Konrad Curze

- **Received source and current state:** **B.** Curze knowingly allowed his assassination and used it as the final argument that his visions and fatalism were correct. The Corona Nox is a genuine office-relic; claims that its jewel preserves his soul are **D**, not canon.
- **Essential function:** the terror judge who sees possible atrocities and commits them first, treating foreknowledge as absolution and his own death as courtroom theatre.
- **Hard edge:** torture, mutilation, collective terror, exemplary killing and the deliberate cultivation of criminal followers because fear produces faster obedience than justice.
- **Institutional afterlife:** police, executioners and killers quote only the effective part of his doctrine.
- **Recurrence mechanism:** office-relic, fear residue, counterfeit visions and possible Sinner manifestation—not a soul trapped in jewellery.
- **World Egg lead:** a schism-era Sinner prophet whose accurate warnings make his voluntary cruelty harder, not easier, to excuse.
- **Hazards and collisions:** prophecy must show possibility rather than metaphysical determinism; keep the soul distinct from crown, residue and Mask. Nearest beds: Crown-Market of Shadows and War for the Common Saints.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## IX — Sanguinius

- **Received source and current state:** **B.** Sanguinius is dead. The Sanguinor, visions and psychic inheritance associated with the Blood Angels are real setting phenomena whose precise identity remains disputed; none establishes Sanguinius's bodily return.
- **Essential function:** the radiant champion who knows the fatal place ahead, fears what his own household can become, and goes because somebody must occupy it.
- **Hard edge:** violent death, inherited rage, followers who kill innocents while identifying with the martyr, and institutions that use sacrifice to silence scrutiny.
- **Institutional afterlife:** the martyr's last stand becomes both moral centre and hereditary wound.
- **Return mechanism:** apparitions, herald figures and inherited visions whose identity must be tested rather than assumed.
- **World Egg lead:** an Underground martyr-protector later given one Greater-Saint commission, surrounded by genuine relic response, false manifestations and a violent cult he explicitly condemns.
- **Hazards and collisions:** avoid a substitute Passion, genetic curse, automatic martyr sainthood or universal apostle. Nearest beds: Siege of the Martyrs' Names and War for the Common Saints.
- **Disposition:** `revise then retain`.

## X — Ferrus Manus

- **Received source and current state:** **B.** Ferrus was killed by Fulgrim. Later clones and a compelled wraith-like appearance do not restore the original person to life. A connection to the Legion of the Damned remains speculative.
- **Essential function:** the strength-teacher who recognizes too late that followers have made a metaphysic out of the weakness he hoped eventually to renounce.
- **Hard edge:** violent conquest, contempt for perceived weakness, fatal anger, and successors who mutilate themselves while claiming fidelity to a founder who intended to reverse course.
- **Institutional afterlife:** the order becomes the opposite of its founder's final teaching while preserving his tools and slogans exactly.
- **Return mechanism:** counterfeit bodies, coerced image, relic memory and possible saintly correction must remain separate.
- **World Egg lead:** a frontier smith and severe penitent whose later genuine manifestation corrects rather than validates the order bearing his hammer.
- **Hazards and collisions:** do not duplicate the Founder Who Corrected the Order without a distinct craft, crime and institutional consequence. Nearest bed: War of the Closed Doors.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## XI — The Second Expunged Primarch

- **Received source and current state:** **B.** The Eleventh was found and later removed in a tragedy separate from the Second. XI Legion forces are attested in the Rangdan wars. Published hints support neither a settled mutation nor a settled execution account.
- **Established mystery function:** a historical person becomes unrecoverable because identity carriers themselves are damaged, allowing incompatible replacements to occupy the vacancy.
- **Open theories:** **C/D.** genetic failure, alien compromise, forbidden power, execution, secret survival, imprisonment or institutional absorption.
- **World Egg lead:** the supernaturally name-wounded person. Hostile action damages names, images and testimony but cannot erase or divide the soul.
- **Hazards and collisions:** an Idol cannot consume a human soul into nonexistence; a true name is a handle, not the person; no claimant is authentic merely because it knows secrets.
- **Disposition:** `retain as absence study`; no invented biography.

## XII — Angron

- **Received source and current state:** **A/B.** Angron is an active Daemon Primarch. Current lore gives him a fixed cycle of remanifestation after banishment.
- **Essential function:** an enslaved empath mutilated into a rage engine, denied solidarity and chosen death, who later inflicts his wound upon everyone beneath him.
- **Hard edge:** slavery, neurological mutilation, abandonment of companions, massacres committed knowingly after liberation became possible, and followers forced to reproduce the injury.
- **Institutional afterlife:** descendants install or ritualise the founder's wound because they cannot imagine loyalty without sharing it.
- **Recurrence mechanism:** timed infernal remanifestation tied to a hostile office or atrocity cycle.
- **World Egg lead:** a slave liberator who freely becomes the bearer of a wrath office after an imposed breach-engine has already deformed his choices.
- **Hazards and collisions:** coercion cannot absorb later guilt; the office cannot be his soul; avoid making Wrath's Idol Throne his direct owner. Nearest beds: War of the Last Map and Long Rent War.
- **Disposition:** `revise then retain`.

## XIII — Roboute Guilliman

- **Received source and current state:** **A/B.** Guilliman is alive and governing Imperium Sanctus after returning from near death. His central modern conflict is with the decayed religious and administrative structures built partly from his own legacy.
- **Essential function:** the legislator and organiser who can save societies through systems, then returns to discover that systems preserve mistakes more faithfully than intentions.
- **Hard edge:** conquest through administration, emergency rule, paternal coercion and laws that continue harming people after their immediate necessity has passed.
- **Institutional afterlife:** a manual becomes scripture; flexible emergency divisions become permanent borders and rival organisations.
- **Return mechanism:** founder returns to audit an institution that invokes him against reform.
- **World Egg lead:** a well-clerk and jurist later manifested to correct one inherited frontier compact, not to resume permanent government.
- **Hazards and collisions:** no Saint may become imperial regent after death or universally rewrite law. Keep distinct from the Four Who Brought Water and existing jurist companies. Nearest beds: Long Rent War and War of the Closed Doors.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## XIV — Mortarion

- **Received source and current state:** **A/B.** Mortarion remains an active Daemon Primarch of Nurgle. Published material gestures toward a remote possibility of future salvation but establishes no repentance or release.
- **Essential function:** the liberator from poisonous overlords who rebuilds their hierarchy around himself and becomes what he most hated while continuing to speak the language of endurance.
- **Hard edge:** poison warfare, coerced hardiness, abandonment of the sick, enslavement through dependency and acceptance of damnation to avoid the humiliation of helplessness.
- **Institutional afterlife:** clinics, armies and cults turn endurance into permission to expose others to suffering.
- **Recurrence mechanism:** diseased incarnation and domain dependency.
- **World Egg lead:** a frontier plague-fighter who becomes a Sinner by making survival conditional upon accepting his protective corruption.
- **Hazards and collisions:** illness is not moral failure; the Sinner does not cause or explain the Grey Sweat; no speculative redemption becomes fact. Nearest beds: Long Rent War and War of the Closed Doors.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## XV — Magnus the Red

- **Received source and current state:** **A/B.** Magnus is an active Daemon Primarch. His fall includes catastrophic forbidden intervention and multiple soul-shard narratives.
- **Essential function:** a brilliant scholar correctly perceives danger but believes knowledge and good intention exempt him from obedience, consent and bounded means.
- **Hard edge:** a warning destroys the barrier it was meant to defend; followers and civilians pay for the scholar's certainty; later grievance focuses on rejection while minimising the original breach.
- **Institutional afterlife:** schools teach that only ignorance, never pride, destroyed their founder.
- **Recurrence mechanism:** separate bodies, residues and knowledge copies may carry aspects of a public identity, but the human soul cannot shard in the World Egg.
- **World Egg lead:** a translator and breach scholar whose forbidden warning opens the route used by the threat, later bearing a Sinner office that markets dangerous knowledge as vindication.
- **Hazards and collisions:** expressly prohibit soul-fragmentation; distinguish the Fifth Translation, the School of the Open Cinder and the Unmirrored Interregnum. Nearest beds: War of the Closed Doors and War of the Last Map.
- **Disposition:** `revise then retain`.

## XVI — Horus Lupercal

- **Received source and current state:** **B.** Horus is decisively dead after the final Siege of Terra narrative. Cloning produced another body, not the restored original, and that clone was destroyed.
- **Essential function:** the beloved first champion whose greatest weapon is making rivals trust him; private grievance becomes universal rebellion because he can translate every person's resentment into one cause.
- **Hard edge:** fratricidal civil war, manipulation of friends, mass betrayal, sacrificial escalation and the demand that every subordinate turn personal loyalty into metaphysical allegiance.
- **Institutional afterlife:** later rebels fight over whether they inherit the person, office, rebellion or merely the grievance.
- **Return mechanism:** false heirs, clones, office claimants and historical charisma rather than actual survival.
- **World Egg lead:** a charismatic commander whose individual betrayal could seed several later claimants.
- **Hazards and collisions:** the user-approved architecture expressly rejects a common Heresy. Removing the universal rebellion leaves the current lead too close to several existing traitor-founder stories.
- **Disposition:** `defer`.

## XVII — Lorgar Aurelian

- **Received source and current state:** **B/C.** Lorgar is a Daemon Primarch. Reports place him outside long seclusion after the Great Rift, but his full modern campaign remains unresolved.
- **Essential function:** a religious seeker is humiliated for false worship, discovers genuinely responsive evil powers, and concludes that reality deserves worship simply because it is powerful and answers.
- **Hard edge:** forced conversion, purges, human sacrifice, deliberate corruption of trusted companions and the transformation of pastoral hunger into a recruitment system for damnation.
- **Institutional afterlife:** the founder writes texts used by both the order that rejected him and the counter-church he creates.
- **Recurrence mechanism:** heresiarchal scripture, cult succession and later damned manifestation.
- **World Egg lead:** an early preacher disciplined for claiming private revelation who builds a rival sacramental church after receiving real answers from hostile powers.
- **Hazards and collisions:** keep distinct from Mujalib, whose sincerity and Creditor deception are fixed; do not create a substitute apostle or make Church correction itself the cause of evil. Nearest beds: Siege of the Martyrs' Names and War of the Closed Doors.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## XVIII — Vulkan

- **Received source and current state:** **A/B.** Vulkan is a Perpetual but missing after the War of the Beast. Salamanders currently believe the recovery of nine artefacts will herald his return; five are recovered.
- **Essential function:** the humane smith-king holds compassion and catastrophic power together, then leaves followers a relic quest that can become either moral formation or treasure obsession.
- **Hard edge:** even the compassionate conqueror kills civilians and employs terrible weapons; remorse does not resurrect victims or make every later act merciful.
- **Institutional afterlife:** artisans preserve practical service, dangerous devices and a return expectation attached to scattered works.
- **Return mechanism:** relic convergence and bodily persistence in the source; World Egg translation uses posthumous commission rather than Perpetual biology.
- **World Egg lead:** a refuge smith whose dispersed tools encode duties to communities rather than pieces needed to summon him.
- **Hazards and collisions:** relics cannot compel a Saint; avoid a collectible resurrection mechanism and duplication of Saintmaker's Forge. Nearest beds: Long Rent War and War of the Closed Doors.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## XIX — Corvus Corax

- **Received source and current state:** **B/C.** Corax survived the Heresy and was last shown hunting Lorgar in the Eye of Terror in a transformed shadow-like form. The common phrase “loyalist daemon Primarch” is interpretation, not a settled species ruling.
- **Essential function:** a liberator compromises the principles of liberation, creates a failed successor project, then abandons rule to pursue the corrupter personally.
- **Hard edge:** insurgent killing, use of prisoners and unstable creations, destruction of failed followers and abandonment of a damaged political community.
- **Institutional afterlife:** later covert orders choose between rescue, assassination and the founder's self-punishing hunt.
- **Return mechanism:** frightening transformed appearance and continuing pursuit beyond ordinary geography.
- **World Egg lead:** a High-Noon liberator who repents, dies and later receives a severe but bounded breachward commission against one heresiarch.
- **Hazards and collisions:** heavenly glory may be frightening but cannot be corruption; a Saint cannot maintain a private endless vendetta. Nearest beds: War of the Last Map and Siege of the Martyrs' Names.
- **Disposition:** `retain`.

## XX — Alpharius and Omegon

- **Received source and current state:** **B/C.** Two Primarchs occupied one public identity. One twin was killed by Dorn and the other experienced the death. The alleged later death at Eskrador is deliberately questionable. Which private biography belonged to which user of the shared name remains contested.
- **Essential function:** two complete persons use one office-name until strategy, loyalty and identity become impossible to separate; later imitators turn uncertainty into institutional permission.
- **Hard edge:** deception of allies, expendable agents, murders committed to preserve plans no subordinate can evaluate, and a culture in which nobody can refuse an order because any contradiction may be intentional.
- **Institutional afterlife:** everyone can claim to be the founder, so accountability dissolves while the office survives.
- **Recurrence mechanism:** two souls, one name, an inheritable office and later counterfeits.
- **World Egg lead:** two humans sharing a political or covert office without soul-merger.
- **Hazards and collisions:** the World Egg already has Masks, Patron personas, copied ancestors, offices and true-name mechanics. This grammar is valuable but currently risks overloading the same distinction before the two erased-person mysteries settle it.
- **Disposition:** `defer`.
