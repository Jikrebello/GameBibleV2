# Great Saints and Sinners — Historical Placement and Relationship Matrix

> [!info] Atlas integration
> Current canonical destination: [[Great Saints and Sinners - Lives and Afterlives]]. This dossier retains its research history; former holds and promotion gates are superseded only where the [[World Historical Atlas - Integration and Coverage Ledger]] records an explicit outcome. Preliminary or rejected variants do not override their corrected descendants.

## Controlling Ruling

These fourteen figures are independent human lives. They are not one fellowship, species, holy dynasty, apostolic college, war council or complete list of history's greatest persons. Eight overlap during the Long Frontier because that period places unusual pressure upon law, conquest, translation, refuge and supernatural borders. They never form a permanent body and never divide into two universal camps.

The culpability and consequences in the earthly lives are controlled by [[Great Saints and Sinners - Primarch Source-Fidelity Hardening Review]]. Where that audit restores a source wound, the concrete act below supersedes any earlier softer phrasing; no review disposition, placement or trace count changes.

Every title below is a provisional authorial handle. Exact names, cultures, locations, dates, church recognition and institutional heirs remain nursery material.

A **becoming** and a later **active history** are separate claims:

- a redeemed Saint's earthly personality survives recognizably, but all posthumous action requires a fresh bounded commission;
- a damned Sinner may pursue a continuing project through an office, cult, body or realm, but none of those is identical to the human soul;
- an apparition, relic, copy, Mask, office or institution can use the same name without becoming the person.

# I. Underground and Conversion Precedents

## GS-01 — The Witness at the Red Passage

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Sanguinius's doomed defence, inherited wound and ambiguous posthumous heralds; source state **B**. Translation status `reviewed-revise` because the Redeemer, Companions and Forty Flames already occupy the sacred centre.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 180–226 AR, one inland prison manufactory and its transfer courtyard during the Underground persecutions. Culture and city remain open. It is not the Three-Lock Prison Road or a ferry tradition.
- **Continuity:** one complete `human-soul`; authorially a redeemed Greater Saint after judgement. Relics, herald apparitions, rage residues and later cult personas are separately classified.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Witness begins as a prison porter hired because a malformed shoulder makes ordinary military service impossible. The porter learns which chains fit which bodies, which guards can be bribed and which prisoners will be killed before trial. During a transfer riot the porter initially shuts one gate to save a smaller group, knowingly leaving another yard to the soldiers. The abandoned prisoners are beaten and several are executed. Inside the escaping group, frightened rescuers then murder bound guards, a captured informer and two prisoners wrongly accused of collaboration. The porter conceals those killings from the escape register because exposing them may destroy the prison network.

The guilt does not produce immediate heroism. The porter spends years carrying letters, food and burial names between prisons and unsuccessfully attempts to restore the murdered prisoners to the record. A later purge drives several households toward a narrow red-painted service passage. Knowing the defence will be fatal, the porter holds that passage while civilians and prisoners escape. The martyr is tortured and killed after capture. The abandoned yard and concealed murders remain part of the received Life rather than a secret excuse for the final sacrifice.

### Active-history seed

During one later movement in the Siege of the Martyrs' Names, a genuine manifestation holds a soul-road threshold while angels of witness recover names being converted into a Sinner's prisoner registry. The commission protects persons and testimony; it neither releases judged souls nor declares every prisoner innocent. The manifestation later confronts the Chain-Bearer of Sarrat at one infernal transfer and the General of the Glass Court when a perfected martyr-copy begins demanding retaliatory blood.

- **Hard edge and cost:** abandoned prisoners die; the escape cell murders captives and innocent prisoners; the porter suppresses their names; the Witness is tortured and executed; later admirers murder supposed informers under the Witness's banner. The Saint condemns the later killing as a repetition of the earthly sin rather than violence invented by an unrelated cult.
- **Legacy:** no surviving order. There are prison prayers, incompatible red-door processions, a militant cult dissolved after the riot and several organisations falsely claiming succession.
- **Primary rivalries:** GS-06 Chain-Bearer; GS-11 Glass General.
- **Competing interpretations:** flawless martyr, repentant coward, invented prison composite, Patron herald, common saint of several prisons or dangerous cult symbol.
- **Evidence carriers:** two independent prison transfer lists; an escape register with removed name-lines; letters naming the abandoned yard, murdered prisoners and later rescue; a wall layer showing the passage was narrowed before the final defence; hostile trial minutes; later angelic testimony that does not supply the earthly biography.
- **1360 AR trace:** a red-painted prison door kept unhinged in a courthouse, used in an annual reading of names but claimed by no lawful order.
- **Authorially fixed:** initial abandonment, concealment of the escape cell's murders, later attempt to restore the names, years of service, fatal defence, redemption and one genuine bounded manifestation.
- **Open:** personal name, gender, city, number saved, exact torture, relic authenticity, herald identities and present custodianship.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** never a Companion or substitute Passion; later cult violence remains human guilt. The mortal core belongs to one prison rather than a route network.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` incorporated — retain the abandonment, service, execution, cult violence and bounded manifestation after moving the earthly core inland and separating it from earlier prison-road placements.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `restored-at-parity` — the beloved martyr knowingly concealed murderous violence within the earthly network later followers idealise.

## GS-02 — The Preacher of the Second Gospel

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Lorgar's rejected worship, discovery of responsive evil and heresiarchal texts; source state **B/C**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 330–382 AR, moving between newly legal urban congregations, confiscated temples and displaced provincial households during Conversion.
- **Continuity:** one damned `human-soul`. The Second Gospel, later devil office, speaking images and copied preacher-personas are not the soul.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Preacher begins as a temple copyist who joins the Church because the new congregations feed displaced workers whom both imperial and old-cult authorities abandoned. The copyist becomes an extraordinary preacher and organiser but privately teaches that every real power deserves negotiated worship. A bishop removes the preacher from public teaching after witnesses establish coerced confessions and a secret rite addressed to the White Flame as though it were an appetite to be fed.

The correction is public but not a torture or imperial humiliation. The preacher chooses to interpret it as proof that the Church fears honest revelation. In closed sanctuaries the preacher asks hostile powers for an answer, receives several genuine but contradictory responses and builds the **Second Gospel**: a rival sacramental system in which effectiveness proves truth. The Preacher personally presides over the first captive sacrifice, orders that a child be used in the first possession trial after another teacher refuses, and orders a former companion killed so the original disciplinary record can be seized. Later initiates reproduce each act as proof of fidelity.

### Active-history seed

After damnation the Preacher bears a heresiarchal office that can speak through authorised copies of the Second Gospel. The Sinner participates in the War of the Closed Doors and Siege of the Martyrs' Names by offering displaced cult workers a coherent counter-church, real minor wonders and false judgement. Later conflicts with the Clerk of a Hundred Wells concern whether law can bind revelation; the Saint of Ashen Wings receives a bounded commission to close one route by which new annotated Gospels are being delivered.

- **Hard edge and cost:** coerced confession, child possession trials, captive sacrifice, ordered murder and sincere followers damned through chosen participation. The Preacher authors and commands the first acts; Church discipline neither causes nor excuses them.
- **Legacy:** several rival counter-churches, mutually hostile recensions and legitimate charitable practices stolen from the preacher's earliest congregation.
- **Primary rivalries:** GS-07 Hundred-Wells Clerk; GS-14 Ashen-Wings Saint.
- **Competing interpretations:** persecuted visionary, obvious fraud, tragically mishandled reformer, first modern Sinner, several preachers conflated, or a text written by later enemies.
- **Evidence carriers:** the bishop's disciplinary dossier; an early sermon shared with orthodox collections; sacrificial-site remains matching a late recension; murdered companion's correspondence; infernal copies that disagree where the original text is silent.
- **1360 AR trace:** three mutually exclusive “complete” Second Gospels circulate, while a fourth fragment is used innocently by hospitals unaware of its origin.
- **Authorially fixed:** genuine early service, just removal from teaching, freely chosen hostile consultation, personal authorship of sacrifice and possession trials, ordered murder, counter-church formation and damnation.
- **Open:** name, city, precise responsive powers, final earthly death, office title and which later recension is closest.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** never Mujalib, Aquilan, a hidden Companion or source of all heresy. Promotion requires a dedicated Conversion-era institutional review.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — the becoming, hard edge, counter-church, evidence and later rivalries remain distinct from existing prophets and apostles.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `restored-at-parity` — the heresiarch personally founds the sacrificial system instead of leaving its violence to unnamed initiates.

# II. The Long-Frontier Campaign Generation

The following eight people overlap in two loose clusters. The older four shape law, fortification, revolt and quarantine between roughly 641 and 735 AR. The younger four shape routes, breach scholarship, refuge craft and institutional discipline between roughly 708 and 804 AR. Some meet personally; none belongs to a common company.

## GS-03 — The Hunter of the Shutwood

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Lion's secretive knight-kingship, broken household, supernatural forest travel and late mercy; source state **A/B**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 708–781 AR, a mixed forest frontier containing Covenant roads, older Menhir jurisdictions and fortified refugee estates.
- **Continuity:** one redeemed `human-soul`; later Greater-Saint commission-bodies are not Menhir manifestations. Forest correspondence remains a route condition, not a private heaven.

### Earthly biography seed

The Hunter begins as a hostage-guide exchanged between a forest household and a frontier governor. Exceptional at finding people and detecting false trails, the guide becomes a commander but refuses to explain intelligence sources. During one pursuit a guilty scout is executed after trial and a coerced scout dies under an unlawful sentence. When the third scout refuses a secret interrogation and demands an open hearing, the Hunter kills the innocent scout in a sudden blow, then orders all three deaths entered as one lawful traitor case. Secrecy saves the route network from an immediate infiltrator while poisoning the household that operates it.

The Hunter eventually submits the records, resigns command and personally leads the recovery of families previously condemned as collaborators. The work ends during a Shutwood evacuation in which some genuinely guilty fighters are carried out beside their children and victims. Mercy does not erase trial, restitution or the innocent scout's death.

### Active-history seed

Much later, the Saint receives a commission within the Long Rent War to find households stranded between foreclosed correspondences. The manifestation can cross only routes necessary to that rescue and withdraws when the last named household reaches a lawful hearing. A separate threshold hunt against the Judge Beneath the Black Eaves occurs when a fear-court uses prophecies to mark refugees as future killers.

- **Hard edge and cost:** the Hunter personally murders an innocent subordinate, falsifies the case, permits another unlawful execution, sustains hostage politics and collective suspicion, and later rescues guilty people without acquitting them.
- **Legacy:** one later regional guide fellowship, several incompatible route manuals and a secret tribunal that falsely claims the Hunter authorised permanent concealment. The fellowship postdates the Hunter and possesses no authenticated institutional descent.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-13 Black-Eaves Judge.
- **Competing interpretations:** flawless ranger, foreign occupier, repentant killer, Menhir guest, Patron route-lord or fabricated founder of the Long Watch.
- **Evidence carriers:** execution orders in the Hunter's hand; the refused-hearing petition; coerced scout testimony; a false consolidated traitor entry; household evacuation registers; route marks that function only for named rescue parties; the innocent scout's family grave.
- **1360 AR trace:** an order of guides leaves one place empty at every route hearing for the scout whom its claimed founder killed.
- **Authorially fixed:** mixed hostage origin, personal killing of the innocent scout, falsified case, surrender of command, rescue work, redemption and one bounded route commission.
- **Open:** identity, polity, Menhir involvement, death, exact order inheritance and authenticity of later tribunal claims.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** not Saint of the Two Ferries, founder of all Long Watch practices or permanent Egg White ranger.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` incorporated — retain the executions, resignation, evacuation and bounded route commission while making the hearing custom later and non-genealogical.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `restored-at-parity` — secrecy now culminates in the commander's own sudden killing of a loyal subordinate, not only signed sentences at a distance.

## GS-04 — The Mason of the Ninth Bastion

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Perturabo's resentful builder, expendable warfare and Infinite Citadel; source state **A/B**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 646–716 AR, early Long Frontier quarry towns, breached hill forts and one infernal survey operation.
- **Continuity:** one damned `human-soul` bearing a later `infrastructure-office`. Bastions, constructed bodies, Patron contracts and route geometry remain separate.

### Earthly biography seed

The Mason begins as a quarry measurer responsible for preventing tunnel collapses. After saving a town through an improvised retaining wall, officials repeatedly assign impossible works while honouring noble patrons instead. The Mason records every death and every withheld recognition. Resentment becomes doctrine: only those willing to spend lives understand construction. When sabotage delays a major course, the Mason orders one worker in ten from every surviving crew killed by lot. The condemned include workers who exposed the sabotage and crews who never touched the damaged course.

During the ninth bastion project, the Mason knowingly shortens evacuation time to preserve a geometrically perfect foundation. A workers' quarter collapses and prisoners are sealed inside because reopening the course would delay the campaign. When confronted, the Mason publishes the names as proof of personal honesty but refuses guilt, arguing that the dead finally belong to an enduring work. The later infernal bargain does not corrupt an innocent; it rewards an already chosen account of human beings as structural material.

### Active-history seed

The Sinner's office now links several incomplete fortresses within the War of the Last Map. Each massacre supplies a temporary causal bridge through which a constructed command-body can enter another bastion. The project seeks one route toward Material cities, not dominion over all Hell. The Warden of the Last Redoubt later opposes one link by evacuating the people whose deaths the design requires.

- **Hard edge and cost:** decimation by lot, lethal labour, sealed prisoners, civilian districts sacrificed to geometry, execution of saboteurs and later followers repeating the commander's collective punishment.
- **Legacy:** a respected engineering school preserves safe measurements alongside a suppressed “necessary loss” appendix; an infernal cadre preserves the appendix alone.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-12 Last-Redoubt Warden.
- **Competing interpretations:** betrayed genius, mass murderer, founder of scientific fortification, infernal captive or Patron architect.
- **Evidence carriers:** quarry death rolls and lot lists; two incompatible ninth-bastion plans; sealed worker graffiti; office mandates; repeatable route failure when a casualty chamber remains empty.
- **1360 AR trace:** three ordinary fortresses still contain sealed ninth chambers which engineers refuse to open and Sinner cults try to fill.
- **Authorially fixed:** professional brilliance, decimation, deliberate abandonment, refusal of guilt, damnation and the bounded linked-bastion project.
- **Open:** identity, exact forts, bargain partner, number of active links, current command-body and ultimate objective.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** not an Anchor-Lord, Pride Regnant or architect of all infernal geography; no bastion enters a map before separate review.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — the ninth bastion remains one mortal atrocity and the later linked works one bounded War-of-the-Last-Map operation.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `restored-at-parity` — punitive expenditure of the Mason's own workers now precedes the use of civilian death as architecture.

## GS-05 — The Smith Who Broke His Hands

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Ferrus's strength doctrine, death before reform and successor self-mutilation; source state **B**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 729–792 AR, later Long Frontier foundries serving roads, hospitals and fortified estates.
- **Continuity:** one redeemed `human-soul`. Artificial hands, tools, copied workshop memories and an institutional founder-persona are not the Saint.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Smith begins as a foundry bondservant whose unusual resistance to heat becomes the workshop's excuse for giving dangerous tasks to other children. After manumission the Smith teaches that weakness must be exposed and overcome. During a test, a gifted apprentice rings the stop bell and begs release. The Smith personally holds the mechanism closed to prove that fear can be mastered, and the apprentice dies. Several maimed witnesses are expelled because their injuries supposedly prove unfitness.

The Smith eventually recognizes that the school has made injury into shame and strength into entitlement. In a public act neither miraculous nor painless, the Smith breaks the celebrated artificial gauntlets, confesses responsibility, sells the workshop interest for victim restitution and spends the remaining years designing tools usable by maimed workers. Former followers resist; some attack the new workshop and kill another apprentice. Repentance changes the Smith but cannot restore those already dead.

### Active-history seed

During one Closed-Door crisis, the Saint manifests before leaders of a later strength-order that has begun replacing healthy flesh and abandoning injured members. The manifestation names victims, breaks the chapter seal and protects its records, then withdraws without selecting new leaders. A later conflict with the Glass General concerns perfected replacement bodies offered as a way to erase culpability and grief.

- **Hard edge and cost:** child labour, the founder's knowing refusal to stop a lethal test, expulsion of maimed witnesses, founder culpability and retaliatory murder after reform.
- **Legacy:** a divided craft tradition, accessible tools, a coercive strength-order and no single lawful successor institution.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-11 Glass General.
- **Competing interpretations:** patron of strength, patron of disabled artisans, hypocrite, late penitent, fabricated reformer or saint captured in the broken gauntlets.
- **Evidence carriers:** apprentice death in a guild roll; a victim-family rule preserving the stop bell; sale and restitution contracts; surviving adaptive tools; the expurgated workshop rule; a chapter seal broken from the inside during the later manifestation.
- **1360 AR trace:** paired broken gauntlets are displayed in rival cities; neither pair need be original for the reform charter to be authentic.
- **Authorially fixed:** personal refusal to stop the lethal test, expulsions, repentance, restitution, final craft, redemption and one founder-correction commission.
- **Open:** identity, craft culture, exact injuries, death, relics, order and outcome of the later correction.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** do not attach automatically to Iron Martyrs, forge-convents or Saintmaker's Forge.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — one workshop family, one later strength-order and one correction commission remain independently evidenced and institutionally bounded.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `restored-at-parity` — the founder's strength doctrine directly kills the apprentice before later correction begins.

## GS-06 — The Chain-Bearer of Sarrat

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Angron's enslavement, mutilation, denied solidarity and recurrent daemonhood; source state **A/B**. Translation status `reviewed-revise`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 670–727 AR, one slave market, quarry frontier and portable breach-court system. “Sarrat” is provisional.
- **Continuity:** one damned `human-soul`; an imposed pain engine remains bodily machinery, while the later Chain-Bearer office and recurring bodies are separate continuities.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Chain-Bearer begins as a slave infirmary attendant able to recognise another captive's pain before it is spoken. Owners implant a breach-responsive chain engine that converts surrounding distress into strength and uncontrollable pain. The attendant helps organise a revolt and is removed from the final stand by officials who want the engine for frontier war. Companions die believing they were abandoned.

Later freedom is real but incomplete. The liberated commander kills surrendering overseers, orders new soldiers fitted with imitations of the engine and massacres a neutral household accused of hiding former owners. Offered removal at the cost of losing command, the commander refuses. The final infernal compact is chosen because it promises that the pain will always mean power and that nobody will ever again remove the bearer from the battle.

### Active-history seed

The Sinner's office creates a recurrence body whenever a specified chain of custody culminates in mass retaliatory killing. Breaking the body interrupts the campaign; preventing the qualifying transfer prevents recurrence. The Witness at the Red Passage later holds one prisoner route against the Sinner without claiming the Chain-Bearer's victims are innocent of every crime.

- **Hard edge and cost:** slavery, bodily mutilation, companion abandonment, murder of surrendering people, coerced imitation engines and massacre.
- **Legacy:** no lawful organisation. Slave revolts remember the healer; Sinner cults remember the engine; states cite the later massacre to discredit all revolt.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-01 Red-Passage Witness.
- **Competing interpretations:** betrayed liberator, mindless weapon, responsible mass murderer, patron of revolt, Wrath Mask or infernal jailer.
- **Evidence carriers:** infirmary tablets; implant scars in an exhumed body claimed as an early imitator; revolt testimony; massacre graves; custody formula repeated at later manifestations.
- **1360 AR trace:** abolitionists still wear broken chain links while recurrence cults secretly preserve the missing joining link.
- **Authorially fixed:** enslaved healer, imposed engine, genuine revolt, later freely ordered atrocities, chosen office, damnation and bounded recurrence rule.
- **Open:** identity, exact owners, fate of companions, office grantor, recurrence interval and present cult.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** neither Titan nor Wrath's offspring; trauma explains pressure but never erases command responsibility.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — bodily coercion, later command guilt, chosen office and bounded recurrence remain separate and causally explicit.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `intact-at-parity` — slavery, imposed mutilation, abandoned companions, voluntary massacre and compulsory imitation engines already carry the Angron wound without excuse.

## GS-07 — The Clerk of a Hundred Wells

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Guilliman's administration, emergency institutions and return to correct inherited law; source state **A/B**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 641–708 AR, one early Long Frontier water-court district and its mixed municipalities under unstable sovereignty. It is a different locality and legal generation from the Four Who Brought Water.
- **Continuity:** one redeemed `human-soul`. Legal office, copied manual, seal and later manifestation remain distinct.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Clerk begins copying well allocations for wages low enough to require sleeping in the archive. During drought and siege the Clerk devises a common register that prevents elite households from taking all available water. The emergency system saves thousands but also confiscates private wells, compels labour and converts several independent households into permanent dependants of the court. After being warned that several households will resist, the Clerk personally signs armed seizure warrants. Household defenders and impressed labourers die during enforcement, and one petitioner is executed under an emergency procedure the Clerk wrote.

When peace returns, the Clerk initially defends every measure as necessary. A dispute with the Bitter Reaper exposes people still trapped under health and water restrictions long after danger passed. The Clerk spends the remaining career restoring hearings, returning land where possible and writing expiration clauses that later copyists often omit. Some victims accept compensation without forgiving the original coercion.

### Active-history seed

The Saint later manifests during one Long Rent dispute in which a copied Hundred-Wells manual is being used to classify kidnapped people as infrastructure contributors. The commission preserves testimony, suspends one seal and compels a hearing; it does not legislate for every city. The Preacher of the Second Gospel offers a rival law based on effective wonders, while the Bitter Reaper argues that dependency is the only reliable form of protection.

- **Hard edge and cost:** confiscation, compelled labour, household dependency, armed seizure, execution under the Clerk's own procedure, deaths during enforcement and reforms arriving too late for the victims.
- **Legacy:** a family of water codes, expiration clauses, a clerkly feast and several governments invoking only the emergency powers.
- **Primary rivalries:** GS-02 Second-Gospel Preacher; GS-08 Bitter Reaper.
- **Competing interpretations:** perfect lawgiver, colonial administrator, repentant bureaucrat, founder of Banu Mizan law or later saint invented by clerks.
- **Evidence carriers:** layered well registers; signed seizure warrants; petitions and burial rolls from dispossessed households; the Clerk's revised clauses; Reaper correspondence; one later seal response independently witnessed.
- **1360 AR trace:** courts still ask whether a coercive order contains “the Clerk's ending,” meaning a date after which it must justify itself again.
- **Authorially fixed:** low origin, effective emergency law, personally authorised lethal enforcement, unjust persistence, restitution work, redemption and one bounded legal commission.
- **Open:** identity, city, faith, exact deaths, judgement chronology, relic seal and which codes descend directly.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** not a member of the Four Who Brought Water, author of all Covenant water law or permanent heavenly regent.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` incorporated — retain the successful emergency register, coercive persistence, restitution and bounded correction after fixing the administrative district as independent from B3-12.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `restored-at-parity` — administration now visibly acts through the Clerk's warrants, soldiers and an execution, while still saving the besieged district.

## GS-08 — The Bitter Reaper

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Mortarion's liberation from poison tyrants and recreation of protective tyranny; source state **A/B**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 676–735 AR, toxic marsh estates, quarantine farms and caravan clinics during the Long Frontier.
- **Continuity:** one damned `human-soul`; protective corruption, diseased body, clinic network and dependency office remain distinct.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Reaper begins carrying corpses from an estate whose owners live above the poison line. Through practical medicine and stolen equipment, the carrier leads workers into the high houses and kills the overseers. Survivors acclaim a liberator. The new regime nevertheless requires every household to accept repeated exposure rites so that nobody will again depend upon elite medicine.

The rites injure children, kill some adults and make survivors dependent upon treatments controlled by the Reaper's clinics. Refusal brings exclusion from water and burial. When the Hundred-Wells Clerk challenges permanent quarantine powers, the Reaper insists that vulnerable people are safer without choice. The final Sinner compact is accepted during an outbreak the Reaper did not cause, exchanging the population's dependency for the commander's continued control.

### Active-history seed

The damned Reaper now establishes refuge clinics along foreclosed routes. They genuinely protect travellers from local poisons while making departure lethal without continued treatment. This participates in the Long Rent War rather than causing the Grey Sweat. The Hundred-Wells Saint later suspends one inherited dependency seal, but does not cure every patient or destroy every clinic.

- **Hard edge and cost:** poison labour, killing of overseers, forced exposure, child death, medical dependency, denial of water and burial.
- **Legacy:** clinics divided between real medical descendants, exploitative cult hospitals and states that copied quarantine law without supernatural corruption.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-07 Hundred-Wells Clerk.
- **Competing interpretations:** liberator-physician, necessary tyrant, plague saint, Sinner, personification of disease or victim of an ungrateful court.
- **Evidence carriers:** estate elevation plans; worker burial rolls; exposure prescriptions; correspondence with the Clerk; patients whose dependency follows one reproducible contract rather than infection.
- **1360 AR trace:** a bitter cordial remains medically useful in small doses and ritually abused by Reaper cults; the recipe proves neither cult nor condemnation.
- **Authorially fixed:** low corpse-work origin, genuine liberation, chosen coercive regime, uncaused outbreak, compact and damnation.
- **Open:** identity, estate, exact medicine, compact party, surviving clinics and any possibility of future repentance.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** does not cause the Grey Sweat, make illness leftward or invalidate ordinary quarantine.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — medicine remains materially useful while forced dependency, not sickness, carries the Sinner project.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `intact-at-parity` — liberation, forced exposure, child death, water and burial denial, dependency and the freely accepted compact already preserve the Mortarion wound.

## GS-09 — The Reader Behind the Sealed Door

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Magnus's brilliance, catastrophic warning and later shard tradition; source state **A/B**. Translation status `reviewed-revise`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 714–779 AR, a separate frontier maintenance college and one layered closure complex; not the Fifth Translation company or Open Cinder school.
- **Continuity:** one damned `human-soul`. Memory copies, warning residues, discarded bodies and the later interpretive office do not divide the soul.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Reader begins as an underpaid aperture-maintenance assistant who compares heat, vibration and seal distortion at a closed correspondence. The assistant detects that the seal is being approached from the far side. Senior custodians believe the evidence but forbid the demonstration because the warning method itself completes the route. A preliminary address maims one student and proves that a living receiver bears the route's return pressure. The Reader conceals the severity, overrules students who withdraw consent and stations them as receivers for the prohibited final address.

The warning reaches distant authorities—and opens the path. Custodians, nearby households and the coerced student receivers die containing what crosses. The Reader then centres the injustice of being disbelieved and minimises the choices that injured and killed the students and made the danger material. Later attempts to preserve knowledge create memory copies with different portions of the Reader's public manner; none contains a fraction of the soul. The Reader chooses a Sinner office promising a realm in which knowledge cannot be refused.

### Active-history seed

Within the War of the Closed Doors, the Sinner offers perfect warnings whose receipt opens the warned-of route. A later friendship with the Refuge-Bell Smith becomes the principal conflict: the Smith once helped contain the mortal breach and later receives a commission to close one warning network without destroying legitimate scholarship.

- **Hard edge and cost:** knowing violation, concealed student injury, overridden consent, deaths of student receivers, custodians and families, suppression of dissent and later routes opened through coerced receipt.
- **Legacy:** no single order; scattered schools preserve valid observations, hostile warning texts and mutually accusing memory copies.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-10 Refuge-Bell Smith.
- **Competing interpretations:** rejected saviour, arrogant killer, several scholars conflated, victim of conservative custodians or living library divided among bodies.
- **Evidence carriers:** prohibition minutes acknowledging the threat; altered student and infirmary rolls revealing the preliminary injury; door geometry showing receipt completed the route; receiver death rolls; incompatible memory copies; later messages whose opening repeats the bounded failure.
- **1360 AR trace:** sealed warnings are stored unread in three archives, while scholars debate how to test them without becoming their destination.
- **Authorially fixed:** correct perception, concealed preliminary injury, coerced receivers, knowing violation, causal breach, refusal of full responsibility, office and damnation. The soul never fragments.
- **Open:** identity, college, threat, exact copied patterns, office and number of active warning routes.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** not Fifth Translation, Open Cinder, an Idol succession or source of all breach scholarship.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` incorporated — replace the textual-copy origin with low-status aperture maintenance while retaining correct perception, knowing violation, deaths, refusal and the warning office.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `restored-at-parity` — the correct warning now rests upon knowingly coerced human receivers, so brilliance and good intention cannot carry the guilt away.

## GS-10 — The Smith of the Refuge Bell

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Vulkan's compassionate smith-kingship, terrible weapons and relic-return tradition; source state **A/B**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 721–804 AR, mobile refuge forges and mixed households in the later Long Frontier.
- **Continuity:** one redeemed `human-soul`. Attributed tools, workshop recipes, a bell and later commission-bodies remain separate. No relic contains or summons the Saint.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Smith begins repairing cookware and cart fittings for refugees who cannot pay. Exceptional craft draws military patronage. During a siege the Smith builds a heat weapon intended to clear an empty approach. The first discharge reveals that civilians have been driven into the district. Given a chance to lock the weapon, the Smith instead authorises and personally triggers a second discharge, believing an enemy breakout will kill more refugees. Civilians burn with the fighters. Commanders conceal the second order, but the Smith cannot place the killing upon them alone.

The remaining life is spent dismantling copies, building bells and tools for evacuation, testifying against patrons and teaching that an artefact's rightful owner is the community bearing its danger. The Reader Behind the Sealed Door was once a friend and helped identify a threat, but the Reader's forbidden warning kills members of the Smith's refuge. The Smith refuses both revenge and the claim that knowledge alone excuses the breach.

### Active-history seed

A later Greater-Saint commission gathers several communities around one threatened route. Attributed tools activate only for their encoded duty—opening a hospice, disabling the heat weapon, sounding evacuation or proving workmanship—not when collectors assemble them. The Saint coordinates angels for one closure against the Reader's warning network and withdraws when refugees reach Material safety.

- **Hard edge and cost:** the compassionate Smith personally authorises and fires a civilian-killing weapon, followed by military cover-up, retaliatory seizure of workshops and refugee deaths during later dismantling operations.
- **Legacy:** distributed craft customs, dangerous devices, refuge bells and collectors wrongly expecting a nine-object return.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-09 Sealed-Door Reader.
- **Competing interpretations:** flawless merciful smith, war criminal who repented, founder of forge-convents, still-living craftsman or soul divided among tools.
- **Evidence carriers:** weapon commission; two-stage district burn pattern; a suppressed second-discharge order; the Smith's later testimony; dismantling accounts; workshop duty marks; independently responding tools whose effects remain task-specific.
- **1360 AR trace:** known institutions organise duty-marked tools into incompatible devotional sets. No authoritative count exists, and assembling any claimed set produces no manifestation.
- **Authorially fixed:** refugee origin, personal authorisation of the second discharge, civilian deaths, restitution, redemption and one closure commission. Collection never compels manifestation.
- **Open:** identity, polity, tool count, genuine artefacts, death, commission date and present route.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** not Saintmaker, source of White Flame or resurrection through relic collection.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` incorporated — remove the nine-object structure and recovery tally while retaining culpable invention, restitution, refuge craft and one closure commission.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `restored-at-parity` — compassion no longer places the Smith at one remove from civilian killing; the decisive discharge is the Smith's own act.

# III. Fall and Reconquest

## GS-11 — The General of the Glass Court

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Fulgrim's perfection, intimate betrayal, corrupted artistry and convincing clone; source state **A/B**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 910–951 AR, one refugee court and reconquest army outside the Six at the Cinder Inn. Exact allegiance remains open.
- **Continuity:** one damned `human-soul`. The Glass Court is a Patron-supported location later infiltrated by Pride and Lust Masks; perfected bodies and court personas are copies or constructs.

### Earthly biography seed

The future General begins as a camp tailor and armour repairer whose ability to make defeated people appear dignified earns access to a claimant's household. The tailor becomes a commander through logistics, theatre and personal courage. A fellow craftsperson and loyal field officer remains the one person able to criticise the General honestly.

As victory approaches, the General turns reconstruction into aesthetic cleansing: damaged veterans are hidden, mixed households expelled and surrendered rivals displayed before execution. During a private confrontation, the loyal officer threatens to reveal sexual abuse of office, diverted refugee funds and the cleansing programme. The General personally murders the officer, then fabricates assault orders and a field-death record so the body can be placed among soldiers abandoned in an unsupported action. A court offering perfected replacement bodies first acts as consolation, then becomes the chosen means of ruling without unwelcome witnesses.

### Active-history seed

The damned General presides over a mobile Glass Court in the Crown-Market of Shadows. It offers descendants flawless versions of dead heroes in exchange for surrendering contradictory records and bodily remains. Later contests involve the Red-Passage Witness, whose martyr-copy demands vengeance, and the Broken-Hands Smith, whose order is offered perfected bodies that erase disability and institutional guilt.

- **Hard edge and cost:** sexual abuse of office, personal murder of the beloved critic, execution of prisoners, expulsion of mixed families, theft from refugees and bodies used as patterns without consent.
- **Legacy:** a courtly school, military pageants, descendant claims, several constructed “returned” officers and no legitimate order founded by the General.
- **Primary rivalries:** GS-01 Red-Passage Witness; GS-05 Broken-Hands Smith.
- **Competing interpretations:** civilising hero, decadent traitor, victim of possession, several generals combined or beautiful Patron sovereign.
- **Evidence carriers:** tailoring accounts and refugee payments; fabricated unsupported-assault orders; officer correspondence and repair records contradicting the field death; graves missing specific remains; constructed returners with identical gaps in memory.
- **1360 AR trace:** formal portraits repaint wounds and mixed marriages out of the victorious generation; restoration reveals one repeated medium-and-contract signature beneath several faces. That signature supports coordinated revision but does not prove that every portrait belonged to one supernatural court.
- **Authorially fixed:** humble craft origin, real courage, cleansing policy, sexual abuse of office, personal murder and its falsification, chosen court and damnation.
- **Open:** identity, claimant, exact campaign, Patron, Mask involvement, current Court and whether any constructed person has acquired independent moral standing.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** not a Pride Regnant, Six at the Cinder Inn member or cause of the Reconquest.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` incorporated — narrow the portrait signature while retaining the craft ascent, cleansing policy, sexual abuse of office, personal murder and fabricated field death, chosen court and damnation.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `restored-at-parity` — the fall now turns upon the General's personal murder of the one beloved critic who could still speak truth.

## GS-12 — The Warden of the Last Redoubt

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Dorn's defence, grief, penitential warfare and contradictory remains; source state **B/C**. Translation status `reviewed-revise`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 915–965 AR, one provincial mountain redoubt during the early Reconquest, outside Caedros I's immediate command circle.
- **Continuity:** one redeemed `human-soul`. A recovered hand, full-body tomb claims, wall persona and visions are separately classified.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Warden begins as a refugee quartermaster who knows masonry because nobody else will inspect unsafe sleeping vaults. During a siege the quartermaster becomes commander and holds the inner redoubt by sealing an outer ward before every family crosses. The decision prevents immediate capture but leaves civilians and a relief company to be killed.

The Warden refuses celebratory promotion, records the excluded names and spends later years building evacuation depth into provincial forts. Grief hardens into punitive campaigning, and subordinates die in assaults whose real purpose is to prove the Warden did not choose cowardice at the gate. A final confession acknowledges both the original constrained decision and the later voluntary self-punishment imposed upon others. Authorially the body dies; the place, recovery of remains and public account remain genuinely uncertain within the nursery.

### Active-history seed

One authentic Greater-Saint manifestation is permitted as an authorial possibility and fixed for this research card: during a later Long Rent episode the Warden holds a Material redoubt while civilians evacuate, expressly opening the outer ward first. The commission defeats one link in the Ninth-Bastion Mason's project by denying it the required abandonment. It does not resume command of the Reconquest or prove any imperial claimant.

- **Hard edge and cost:** sealed civilians, dead relief troops, later wasteful assaults and descendants made to inherit disputed blame.
- **Legacy:** evacuation architecture, a provincial wall feast, contradictory body relics and rival military schools; not a sovereign order.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-04 Ninth-Bastion Mason.
- **Competing interpretations:** flawless defender, coward, penitent murderer, secret survivor, Caedros's hidden lieutenant or fabricated provincial saint.
- **Evidence carriers:** quartermaster repair rolls; sealed-ward remains; excluded-name book; assault casualty patterns; incompatible tomb inventories; later outer-gate opening witnessed by enemy and refugee records.
- **1360 AR trace:** several fortresses claim the Warden's hand, while the most credible relic is an ordinary measuring cord repaired by refugee children.
- **Authorially fixed:** low origin, gate decision, later culpable assaults, confession, bodily death, redemption and one bounded later defence.
- **Open:** identity, fort, death, remains, church recognition, exact commission date and heirs.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** never Caedros I, Returning Standard founder or proof that Reconquest atrocities were holy.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` incorporated — fix bodily death while leaving location, remains and public knowledge disputed; retain the provincial history and bounded later defence.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `intact-at-parity` — the deliberate sealing and later self-punishing assaults already spend civilians, relief troops and followers in the Dorn-shaped wound.

# IV. Great Split through High Noon

## GS-13 — The Judge Beneath the Black Eaves

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Curze's foresight, terror jurisprudence and chosen assassination; source state **B**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 1018–1080 AR, landlocked towns traumatised by the Great Split and subsequent jurisdictional violence.
- **Continuity:** one damned `human-soul`. Judicial crown, prophetic residue, execution office, fear-Mask and later apparitions remain distinct.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Judge begins as a roof runner carrying warrants between courts that no longer recognise one another. Genuine but incomplete visions reveal murders, riots and betrayals before they occur. The runner prevents several crimes and becomes a celebrated examiner. Gradually the Judge tortures people for acts only foreseen, displays mutilated offenders and terrorises whole households because fear appears to reduce visible crime.

The prophecies are neither invented nor inevitable. Several atrocities occur precisely because preventive executions destroy the relationships that might have stopped them. When an assassin finally enters the Black-Eaves courthouse, the Judge orders guards to stand aside so the death can be used as proof that every prediction was fixed. Victims remain dead; the theatrical death does not turn terror into truth.

### Active-history seed

The damned Judge later offers divided churches accurate visions of the other's future crimes, demanding preventive persecution. A crown office and Envy or Pride Masks imitate the Judge independently. The Shutwood Hunter receives one threshold commission to rescue families marked as future killers, demonstrating that protection need not equal acquittal and possibility is not sentence.

- **Hard edge and cost:** torture, mutilation, execution without committed crime, collective punishment, self-fulfilling massacre and deliberate use of assassination as propaganda.
- **Legacy:** terror procedures, abolition law, a crown-relic dispute and criminal fraternities claiming the Judge's permission.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-03 Shutwood Hunter.
- **Competing interpretations:** perfect prophet, mad judge, Sinner, murdered reformer, crown-bound ghost or several executioners under one title.
- **Evidence carriers:** warrants dated before alleged crimes; survivors whose conduct contradicts visions; courthouse architecture; assassin testimony; crown responses that continue when the Sinner appears elsewhere.
- **1360 AR trace:** courts prohibit conviction for “the future fact,” while secret magistrates still purchase Black-Eaves forecasts.
- **Authorially fixed:** real conditional visions, chosen terror, self-fulfilling outcomes, permitted assassination and damnation.
- **Open:** identity, town, vision source, assassin, office, crown status and present cult.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** prophecy never removes freedom; crown never contains the soul; the story does not explain the Great Split.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — conditional visions, chosen terror, self-fulfilling policy, office boundaries and the later rescue conflict remain coherent.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `intact-at-parity` — torture, mutilation, preventive execution, collective punishment and chosen assassination already preserve the Curze wound directly.

## GS-14 — The Saint of Ashen Wings

- **Source attribution and confidence:** Corax's compromised liberation, failed creations, abandonment and later transformed pursuit; source state **B/C**. Translation status `reviewed-retain`.
- **Era / region / culture:** c. 1087–1146 AR, High-Noon mines, prison estates and one early otherworld breach campaign.
- **Continuity:** one redeemed `human-soul`. Frightening wings and shadow are signs of commission, not corruption, daemonic nature or Menhir transformation.

### Earthly biography seed

The future Saint begins as a mine slave and covert courier. A successful revolt uses prison tunnels and wins freedom for several settlements. Facing immediate reconquest, the rebel leader authorises an experimental passage through a damaged correspondence and uses condemned prisoners as the first test party. Survivors return altered and dangerous; the leader kills some personally and conceals the experiment from the liberated settlements.

The lie protects the new polity long enough to survive but leaves families without bodies or truth. Years later the leader confesses, surrenders office and accepts exile rather than forcing victims to maintain the heroic story. In exile the former commander rescues people from similar experiments and dies closing one Material breach. Redemption does not make the original test lawful or require families to celebrate the penitent.

### Active-history seed

The Greater Saint later manifests with ash-dark wings during specific heavenly commissions against routes operated by the Preacher of the Second Gospel. The Saint may command angels where the charge grants it, enter hostile custody and destroy constructed gates. Each pursuit ends when the named route, captives or text-delivery is secured. There is no permanent celestial hunt or private vengeance.

- **Hard edge and cost:** slavery, insurgent killing, non-consensual prisoner experiments, killing of damaged survivors, concealment and abandonment of a vulnerable polity.
- **Legacy:** liberation songs, victim rolls, covert rescue techniques and competing breachward groups; no single surviving order owns the Saint.
- **Primary rivalry:** GS-02 Second-Gospel Preacher.
- **Competing interpretations:** immaculate liberator, unforgivable experimenter, redeemed penitent, hostile shadow being, Patron assassin or founder of every covert order.
- **Evidence carriers:** mine courier marks; prisoner allocations; altered remains; confession copies preserved by victim families; gate scars matching one later commission; angelic testimony limited to the commissioned operation.
- **1360 AR trace:** a liberation feast is followed the next day by an unofficial reading of experimental victims whom the public Life omits.
- **Authorially fixed:** slave origin, successful revolt, culpable experiment, killings, confession, exile, redemption and bounded later commissions.
- **Open:** identity, culture, polity, exact breach, number of victims, death place, manifestations and institutional heirs.
- **Collision and promotion gate:** not Ansgar, founder of a universal breachward order or a Saint permitted to pursue private revenge forever.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — the revolt, experiment, killings, confession, exile, redemption and separately bounded commissions remain historically and metaphysically distinct.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `intact-at-parity` — the liberator already authorises non-consensual experiments, kills altered survivors, conceals the programme and abandons the damaged polity.

# V. The Two Erased Positions

## ER-01 — The Institutionally Erased Person

- **Era / pressure:** c. 780–835 AR, after one late Long-Frontier catastrophe involving a human command, breach response and politically useful survivors.
- **Continuity:** one complete `human-soul`; judgement and posthumous status deliberately open.
- **Fixed event:** a coalition of human authorities removes the person's name from rolls, recuts monuments, divides followers among existing institutions and rewrites practices as independent discoveries. Some followers committed crimes; others opposed them and were preserved because their knowledge remained necessary.
- **Hard edge:** the underlying catastrophe killed captives, workers and noncombatants. Suppression includes imprisonment, coerced silence and at least one execution of a witness. It cannot be reduced to a harmless archival mystery.
- **Evidence carriers:** recut seniority lists; pay and ration sequences with one command layer missing; identical restricted practices appearing suddenly in rival institutions; a witness sentence preserved in a hostile court; monuments whose removed inscription was longer than the replacement.
- **1360 AR access point:** an inheritance dispute requires establishing why three unrelated orders all possess the same founder-era exemption but none can name its grantor.
- **Bounded test:** reconstructing dates from independent ration, weather and burial records proves coordinated alteration and redistribution; it cannot reveal the person's soul-state.
- **Plausible false reconstruction:** the person never existed and the gap merely conceals collective administrative failure.
- **Corroboration threshold:** one altered institutional record plus one independent material or hostile documentary class.
- **Permanently open:** identity, complete offence, judgement, final fate, whether the purge protected victims and which surviving practices were worth preserving.
- **Promotion gate:** not Ischar's empty chair, a hidden Companion, secret founder of all orders or cause of the Long Frontier's end.
- **Review disposition:** `retain` — coordinated human suppression and redistribution are recoverable while identity, guilt, judgement and final fate remain open. No trace is promoted in this pass.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `intact-at-parity` — captives, workers and noncombatants die in the catastrophe, while imprisonment, coerced silence and witness execution keep the absence from becoming a harmless archival puzzle.

## ER-02 — The Supernaturally Name-Wounded Person

- **Era / pressure:** c. 815–870 AR, one bounded Long Rent or Closed-Door conflict involving an attack upon testimony and identity carriers.
- **Continuity:** one complete `human-soul`; damaged names, copied memories, Patron personas, Masks, office claimants and frauds are separate.
- **Fixed event:** hostile supernatural action attacks the person's public identity across archives, portraits, spoken testimony and certain route records participating in one historical network. The effect removes or substitutes identifying handles but does not erase events, bodily consequences or the soul. Unconnected records may be missing for ordinary reasons. Later powers occupy the vacancy with incompatible claimants.
- **Hard edge:** witnesses who resist suffer memory injury, disappear into hostile routes or are killed by institutions that mistake inconsistency for treason. Families lose inheritance and burial rights because they cannot establish relationship to the missing identity.
- **Evidence carriers:** portraits altered in different media toward incompatible faces; grammatically complete sentences with the same absent name-space; route tolls paid by an unrenderable signature; descendants sharing material possessions but no stable genealogy; claimants with mutually exclusive bodies and identical borrowed memories.
- **1360 AR access point:** several claimant personas petition for control of one dormant refuge while displaced descendants seek recognition without restoring the dangerous name.
- **Bounded test:** the same name-position fails differently under ink, speech, icon, contract and Patron memory, proving targeted multi-carrier damage rather than ordinary censorship. No test identifies the attacker.
- **Plausible false reconstruction:** one immortal founder repeatedly changed bodies and is now returning through several claimants.
- **Corroboration threshold:** patterned failure in two independent identity carriers plus one material consequence that predates all present claimants.
- **Permanently open:** identity, allegiance, judgement, attacker, original appearance, ultimate fate and authenticity of any later manifestation.
- **Promotion gate:** a true name remains a handle rather than a soul; no claimant becomes original through knowledge, likeness or institutional acceptance.
- **Review disposition:** `revise` incorporated — limit the damage to one participating historical network while preserving the attack-pattern, complete soul and incompatible claimants. No trace is promoted in this pass.
- **Source-fidelity hardening:** `intact-at-parity` — memory injury, disappearance, institutional killing and dispossession keep the name-wound materially destructive without inventing the missing person's guilt.

# VI. Relationship and Campaign Matrix

## Mortal contacts

Only two primary rivalries begin through confirmed mortal contact:

1. **Hundred-Wells Clerk / Bitter Reaper:** emergency water and quarantine law become a dispute about whether protection may make dependency permanent.
2. **Sealed-Door Reader / Refuge-Bell Smith:** friendship and technical cooperation break when the Reader's warning opens the route and kills members of the Smith's refuge.

Other Long-Frontier figures may attend the same court, siege or road without becoming primary companions. No event gathers all eight.

| Conflict | Saintly or human act | Sinner project | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red Passage / Chain-Bearer | preserves prisoner names and one lawful exit | converts retaliatory custody into recurrence | rescue is not acquittal; no judged soul is released |
| Red Passage / Glass Court | rejects perfected martyr-copy and retaliatory cult | exchanges contradictory memory for a flawless body | copy never becomes Saint |

## Uneven inheritances

- **No surviving lawful organisation:** Red-Passage Witness and Chain-Bearer.
- **Distributed practices without exclusive order:** Shutwood Hunter, Refuge-Bell Smith and Ashen-Wings Saint.
- **Competing institutional descendants:** Broken-Hands Smith, Hundred-Wells Clerk and Bitter Reaper.
- **Counter-church or Sinner network:** Second-Gospel Preacher, Ninth-Bastion Mason, Sealed-Door Reader and Black-Eaves Judge.
- **Court or military memory rather than order:** Glass General and Last-Redoubt Warden.

# VII. Review and Promotion Gates

Every later promotion must confirm:

1. the earthly biography works without its posthumous manifestation;
2. the active history states commission or office beginning, permitted acts, protected or targeted persons and ending condition;
3. the person, soul, body, office, Mask, Patron role, relic, residue, copy, legend and institution remain distinct;
4. the source hard edge remains causally present without importing unrelated Warhammer plot or surface design;
5. victims retain names, claims, descendants, evidence and the right to refuse reconciliation;
6. no figure causes an entire age, founds every later order or secretly belongs to one master cohort;
7. no Saint falls, accepts worship, owns a private heaven or resumes permanent government;
8. no Sinner's trauma, possession or office removes mortal culpability;
9. no present manifestation or institutional outcome is fixed outside the Forward Rule;
10. no protected Weird, Great Split doctrine, Grey Sweat cause, Giant continuity or Idol succession is explained.
