# The Two Fissions and the Atherian Long Formation

> [!warning] Author-only research
> This dossier replaces no canon by itself. It tests a gradual two-stage fission, an earlier and harder proto-Atherian expansion, and a layered Atherian ethnogenesis. Canon changes require the separate promotion review recorded in [[Scattering Ethnogenesis and Inheritance Matrix]].

## Research Question

How does the one people formed during [[The Glass Sojourn]] become several historical peoples without reducing the [[Age of Scattering]] to one quarrel, one march, or three tribes walking away from the same camp on the same day?

The recommended answer is a sequence rather than a family-tree fork:

```text
One Glass People
├─ southern war-settlement leagues
│  └─ proto-Atherians
│     └─ heroic maritime cultures → city-states → Caleran republic → empire
└─ Near-Glass camp continuum
   └─ later northern fission
      ├─ proto-Jirahar
      └─ proto-Ahl-e-Ahd
```

Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan, and Nsizwa remain independent **Orphaned** peoples. Sestaran harbor populations, Vesari, and later Eclessians remain **Braided** peoples. The diagram shows institutional descent, not pure bloodlines.

## Proposed Controlling Rulings

### 1. The Prophet leaves no universal successor

The Great Prophet's commission is personal and non-inheritable. His death does not abolish government: commanders still command assigned forces, judges still hear disputes, archivists still preserve the received teaching, provisioners still account for stores, and household elders still answer for their dependants. None of those offices becomes the Prophet's chair.

The first crisis is therefore not simple leaderlessness. It is the attempt to answer a question for which the people possess several real but incomplete authorities: **who may decide what obedience requires when the commissioned witness is dead and the people must enter unfamiliar land?** Rival factions elevate military necessity, received judgement, archival precedent, household consent, or material provision. Each can cite a genuine responsibility. None may honestly say that responsibility makes its bearer the Lord's new universal mouth.

Some later rulers claim that an ancient office did inherit the commission. That claim is political theology, not authorial fact. No sign, victory, relic response, or survival miracle validates it.

### 2. The first fission is southern departure, not threefold separation

Between approximately 2130 and 2050 BR, companies led by captains, surveyors, craftsmen, younger household coalitions, and former Giant-world service specialists move farther south. They favor permanent possession, repair of captured works, fortified settlement, and offensive war against communities controlling the land they want. These leagues become recognizably proto-Atherian only through the movement itself.

Other camps remain closer to the Glass margins and northern roads. They continue seasonal movement between wells, graves, registers, and remembered stations. **Near-Glass camp continuum** is an authorial term only. Its inhabitants do not yet constitute either the later Jirahar or the proto-Ahl-e-Ahd.

The fission takes generations. Households divide internally; judges travel between both zones; southern settlements still send offerings and testimony north; northern caravans use southern workshops; soldiers desert in both directions. No assembly, battle, or boundary stone creates the separation by itself.

### 3. The second fission is a northern ethnogenesis

Between approximately 1950 and 1750 BR, water scarcity, seasonal distance, marriage, raiding, trade, and sustained contact with Orphaned Tarkhan and older desert communities make one Near-Glass system increasingly unworkable.

- The ancestors of the **Jirahar** answer insecurity with portable courts, translocal registers, route memory, protected burial obligations, and shelters whose legitimacy does not depend on owning the surrounding land. Their later tower towns make mobile law visible and durable.
- The ancestors of the **Ahdari and related proto-Ahl-e-Ahd communities** answer the same pressure with defended wells, territorial oath-coalitions, guest-right, blood-price, rescue debt, marriage alliance, and public enforcement. Their law becomes attached to specific water, witnesses, and protectors before it becomes the later Covenant's metaphysical contract system.

This is neither a Jirahar people expelling a deviant child nor an Ahdari people abandoning rootless cousins. Both descend from the Near-Glass continuum and absorb people who never belonged to the Ark. Later Jirahar and Ahdari traditions possess genuine but partial ancestral claims.

### 4. Proto-Atherian settlement begins as conquest

The southern leagues do not enter empty country. They encounter Orphaned villages, strongholds, harbor communities, old client populations, successor courts, and separate great clans later grouped under Folk histories.

Their advance is a hard mixed conquest:

- some gates are opened by local enemies of the ruling house;
- some towns surrender for protection or grain;
- some populations are massacred, expelled, sold, or divided among estates;
- captives and children are exchanged as hostages;
- local smiths, pilots, interpreters, healers, and landholders enter the new settlements by compulsion, advantage, marriage, adoption, or defection;
- some proto-Atherian forts are destroyed and their survivors absorbed into Folk communities;
- different leagues pursue incompatible policies and sometimes fight one another more bitterly than they fight their neighbors.

The result is durable proto-Atherian power without uniform extermination or one-directional replacement. The conquerors possess a valuable Glass inheritance of measures, archives, differentiated offices, organized service, and preserved crafts. They do not possess better souls, automatic righteousness, or a monopoly on human civilization.

### 5. No southern conquest is a commanded holy war

The Lord commands the Glass people to leave the refuge and establish human life beyond it. That command does **not** grant the proto-Atherians title to Folk land, authorize massacre, or turn victory into proof of divine favor.

Joshua and Judges supply author-facing grammars of entry, warfare, incomplete settlement, local alliance, disobedience, and moral consequence. They do not provide an in-world warrant. Atherian rulers later manufacture conquest texts which place their campaigns inside the Prophet's final command. The lack of an inheritable commission is exactly what makes those texts false even where they preserve accurate routes or battles.

### 6. Atherian identity changes its basis repeatedly

“Atherian” names at least four different historical relationships:

1. **war-settlement identity:** membership in southern companies, forts, ships, allotments, and shared campaign law;
2. **heroic-maritime identity:** participation in a network of competing ports, raid fleets, dynastic houses, mercenary markets, and founder cults;
3. **city-civilization identity:** language, games, star rites, archives, philosophy, leagues, measures, and civic law shared among rival poleis;
4. **republican and imperial identity:** citizenship, military service, provincial status, taxation, road law, office, education, and state cult under Caleran institutions.

These layers are cumulative but not identical. A person may become Atherian by adoption into a war-settlement household, admission to a city, military grant, manumission, provincial citizenship, or education without possessing an imagined pure descent from the Glass.

## Phase I — The Vacant Commission, c. 2165–2130 BR

The Prophet dies at or near the outward threshold after the Glass people have already become one people. He is the final living human with direct memory of the Giant world. The loss removes both the commissioned leader and the only witness capable of correcting every inherited account from personal experience.

The practical problem arrives immediately. The refuge was sustained for a bounded purpose; provision cannot be converted into a permanent entitlement, and the people must establish ordinary agriculture, roads, workshops, defenses, and civil government outside the deep Glass. Several camp offices have authority to perform parts of that work, but their mandates overlap once movement becomes settlement.

Commanders argue that hostile country requires unified obedience. Judges answer that emergency command cannot decide ownership and punishment without review. Keepers of testimony insist that the last received instructions must govern. Provisioners control the seed, herds, tools, and ration registers without which any route will fail. Household elders refuse to surrender dependants to projects they did not approve. The conflict is constitutional before it becomes ethnic.

Deaths caused by delayed departure, hoarding, punitive requisition, failed scouting, and factional violence prevent this from becoming an abstract debate. Families remember who starved after a storehouse was sealed and who was killed when a captain's guards dispersed a hearing. Later traditions preserve the offices but simplify the dispute into a contest between one wise ancestor and one wicked rival.

## Phase II — The First Fission, c. 2130–2050 BR

The first durable division follows geography. Southern routes offer rivers, arable basins, timber, coastal access, metals, and reusable ruins, but those assets are already inhabited or claimed. Northern routes remain poorer and more exposed yet maintain easier contact with Glass-margin graves, old camps, caravan stations, and the remembered road.

Southern leagues form around military protection and material competence. A captain can defend a convoy; a surveyor can divide irrigable land; a smith can keep tools and arms in use; an archivist can turn temporary allotments into inheritable claims. The leagues initially describe themselves as an expeditionary arm of the whole people. Permanent walls, seized fields, locally born children, and the refusal to return captured property make that claim progressively less credible.

Near-Glass camps do not simply remain still. They move among northern wells and routes, argue over which records travel, and sometimes send armed expeditions to recover people or property from the south. Their decision preserves memory while exposing dependants to drought, raiding, and the power of caravan custodians. Neither branch represents obedience without sin.

The first fission is complete only when children can grow to adulthood knowing the other zone principally through marriage negotiations, trade disputes, inherited accusations, and songs.

## Phase III — The Southern Seizures, c. 2050–1900 BR

Proto-Atherian forts spread along river crossings, defensible hills, road junctions, and coasts. The settlers seek food security and strategic depth, but their methods create new wars. Grain taken to feed one garrison leaves a local settlement unable to survive winter. Hostages taken to guarantee a treaty become bilingual heirs or permanent captives. A local clan which helps destroy a rival may discover that the new fort claims both territories afterward.

The southern world is not politically empty or culturally uniform. Some Orphaned communities retain Giant-era waterworks or burial knowledge; others possess iron, timber, navigation, medicine, Menhir bargains, or accurate local calendars the newcomers lack. Proto-Atherian victories therefore depend upon local allies and coerced specialists as much as Glass inheritance.

The first Sestaran braids begin in this contact zone. Harbors join local pilots, island households, Ark-derived migrants, captives, refugees, traders, and war-settlement garrisons in different proportions. No harbor's mixed origin makes every later Sestaran city the same age or the child of one colony.

Folk memory preserves this period as the arrival of disciplined strangers carrying straight measures, portable archives, hunger for land, and a habit of treating temporary roads as permanent title. Atherian memory turns the same campaigns into refuge, reclamation, or the defeat of monstrous kings. Both may preserve individual facts; neither should control the authorial account alone.

## Phase IV — The Northern Fission, c. 1950–1750 BR

The northern division begins late in the formal Scattering and completes during the Age of Hearths. It is driven less by one doctrine than by the cost of applying camp institutions to increasingly different ecologies.

Western and northern Tarkhan communities already possess seasonal well tenure, mobile pastoral practice, dry-route knowledge, kin alliances, and their own memories of Giant collapse. They trade, raid, hire guides, take and return captives, marry outsiders, and defend routes. They are not an anonymous “steppe pressure” produced for somebody else's origin story.

At shared wells, three kinds of claim repeatedly collide:

- an inherited local or Tarkhan right to use water in a season;
- the Glass-derived principle that emergency provision must answer to registered need;
- the emerging territorial claim that a community which fortifies, cleans, and defends a well may impose binding terms upon access.

Portable judges can settle one caravan dispute but struggle to defend a well for decades. Fortified oath-houses can protect water but convert emergency guardianship into inherited lordship. Raids make both systems harsher. Marriage makes clean separation impossible. The later Jirahar and Ahdari solutions emerge through repeated cases, retaliations, migrations, and institutional choices rather than one doctrinal council.

## Phase V — The Heroic Maritime Recovery, c. 1900–1400 BR

The proto-Atherian south becomes a network of sea-kings, fortified colonies, mercenary leagues, captive markets, shrine towns, and competing founder houses. This is the setting's Greek heroic and mixed maritime-migration grammar, not a claim that one historical “Sea People” founded Atheria.

Ark measures, preserved apprenticeship, military organization, and record fragments help reconnect mines, forests, workshops, harbors, and grain basins after the Giant collapse. Local ore knowledge, ships, seasonal winds, roads, and labor make the network possible. The Atherians therefore catalyze a **post-Giant recovery horizon**; they do not invent metalwork, navigation, urban life, or civilization in empty country.

The same network spreads destruction. Rulers demand timber, ore, slaves, tribute, and dependable grain. Mercenaries change sides. Settlers capture ports. Rival fleets burn warehouses that feed inland populations. Standard weapons circulate beyond the leagues that manufactured them. When several routes fail together, dependent settlements starve or abandon specialized works.

Heroic poems later place one founder at the beginning of each city. Archaeology should instead reveal older walls, mixed cemeteries, interrupted local occupation, several rebuilding horizons, and names in more than one linguistic family.

## Phase VI — City-State Atheria, c. 1400–800 BR

War-settlement identity gives way to city identity. Rival poleis codify citizenship, magistracies, land registers, cult calendars, public games, militia service, debt, enslavement, and rules for admitting outsiders. Shared language and ritual make wars between them intelligible without making them less savage.

Colonization continues, but it now takes several forms: citizen settlements on conquered land, merchant quarters by treaty, garrisons inside allied cities, refugee foundations, pirate ports regularized after victory, and mixed settlements which retrospectively claim a single Atherian founder.

The first sustained star synthesis grows inside this competitive world. Giant astronomy, route observation, Menhir correspondences, Idol Masks, and civic praise are sorted into locally different pantheons before the later Star Pantheon claims they always belonged together.

## Phase VII — Caleran Republic and Atherian Synthesis, c. 800–350 BR

Caleran begins as one participant in a multipolar Atherian world. Its advantage is not cultural purity but position: river, harbor, roads, archives, and an ability to arbitrate among cities which fear one another. The Celestial Compact joins twelve leading cities through calendar, levy, and arbitration before becoming a republic.

The republic converts earlier Atherian culture into scalable instruments: graded citizenship, colonial status, military land, cadastral survey, provincial taxation, standardized appeals, road obligation, and a state cult capable of subordinating local sacred powers without immediately erasing them. Conquered elites can enter the system, enslaved people can be manumitted into it, and subject communities can remain excluded for generations.

This produces a Roman-like transformation without requiring a separate “Roman” ethnicity. Atheria becomes a political and legal civilization whose citizens may descend from Ark war-settlers, Folk clans, Sestaran harbors, older city populations, freed slaves, provincial aristocracies, and many others.

Republican expansion remains violent and locally variable. It destroys governments, transfers populations, seizes land, enslaves captives, and privileges collaborators. It also leaves roads, courts, markets, military careers, urban rights, and mixed provincial cultures which survive the empire. Local adoption and imperial coercion occur together rather than canceling one another.

## Phase VIII — Empire and Successor Inheritances, from c. 350 BR

The civil wars and the victory of [[Named Figures of the Known World|Aurelius Thalion]] remain unchanged. He does not found Atherian civilization; he captures and centralizes a long republican and city-state inheritance.

Later successor identities inherit different layers:

- **Thalmyrics** are a post-Fall heartland ethnogenesis. They inherit the densest city-state, maritime, republican, imperial, Church, mountain-refuge, occupation, and Reconquest layers. Their Greek and eastern-Balkan flavor is primarily cultural and historical, not descent from a separate ancient “Greek Atherian” tribe.
- **Eclessians** are a later western and eastern-provincial braid in which Atherian civic and Church institutions join Folk, Markish, Vesari, Arqeshite, and other regional populations. Their Latin and western-imperial flavor comes through provincial inheritance and later Church politics.
- **Sestarans** are Braided harbor peoples, some older than Atherian conquest and others created by Atherian colonies. Atherianization becomes one common layer, not their common biological origin.
- **Vesari** remain a later far-eastern Braided river people. Ark-derived migrants participate in their formation, but Vesari are not a lost Atherian colony or a fourth Exodus branch.

The resulting map is an inheritance topology, not a genealogy. Roads, language, rites, law, archives, military practice, ancestry, and political memory can travel together or separately.

## Competing Historical Memories

| Tradition | Characteristic claim | What it preserves | What it tends to hide |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jirahar** | “We carried the road when others mistook possession for promise.” | portable courts, registers, burial obligation, the empty prophetic office | coercion by custodians, failed protection, and the shared ancestry of Ahdari rivals |
| **Ahdari / proto-Ahl-e-Ahd** | “We made promise enforceable where memory alone could not defend water.” | oath, guest-right, rescue debt, defended wells, public witness | conversion of guardianship into lordship and the northern continuum shared with Jirahar |
| **Atherian** | “We left the refuge in order to build.” | southward movement, repaired works, military coordination, urban recovery | seizure of inhabited land, captive labor, and dependence upon local knowledge |
| **Folk** | “The road people arrived already measuring what belonged to others.” | conquest, forts, hostage systems, land surveying, absorbed settlements | alliances with proto-Atherian leagues and Folk participation in neighboring wars |
| **Tarkhan** | “Our wells and seasons were old before the scribes argued over them.” | Orphaned continuity, dry-route expertise, raids, treaties, intermarriage | local participation in captive markets and the attraction of Ark legal instruments |
| **Sestaran** | “Every harbor was founded twice.” | mixed ports, translated law, multiple archives, civic rather than ethnic identity | enslavement, confiscation, and the unequal terms under which some braids formed |

## Orthodox Scriptural Lens

The sources govern moral grammar; they are not in-world documents and do not make the setting a renamed biblical sequence.

- **Numbers 11 and Exodus 18:** delegated authority can be real without becoming the Prophet's full office. This supplies the competing-offices grammar.
- **Numbers 27, Deuteronomy 34, and Joshua 1:** Joshua receives an explicit commission. The World Egg history deliberately lacks an equivalent universal succession; later claimants cannot infer one from administrative continuity.
- **Deuteronomy 8:** settlement, abundance, and technical success create a danger of forgetting the giver and attributing prosperity to one's own power.
- **Joshua and Judges 1–2:** warfare, local alliance, incomplete settlement, coexistence, generational forgetting, and disobedience provide the hard grammar of entry into inhabited land. They do not authorize Atherian conquest.
- **Judges as a whole:** local deliverers solve bounded crises but do not create a stable universal succession; victory and moral legitimacy repeatedly diverge.
- **1 Kingdoms 8:** fear and military need can make people demand concentrated rule; a ruler who protects may also take children, fields, harvests, and labor.
- **3 Kingdoms 12:** accumulated burdens and arrogant government can turn a shared inheritance into lasting political division. It informs the northern fission without reducing it to one speech.

## Comparative Research Controls

The “Sea Peoples” material is used only as a grammar of diverse maritime mobility, raiding, mercenary service, population movement, and settlement during systemic change. It is not evidence for one ethnically uniform invasion or a literal Atherian analogue. See [Bronze Weaponry and Cultural Mobility in Late Bronze Age Southeast Europe](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/warfare-in-bronze-age-society/bronze-weaponry-and-cultural-mobility-in-late-bronze-age-southeast-europe/F7C2C8ED8AA0B973866FE3A5159A5C09) and [Migration Myths and the End of the Bronze Age in the Eastern Mediterranean](https://assets.cambridge.org/97811089/64739/excerpt/9781108964739_excerpt.pdf).

Roman provincial history supplies a second grammar: violent conquest followed by uneven local participation, graded status, military service, municipal institutions, mixed identities, and regional differences which imperial categories never fully erase. See [Roman Imperialism and Local Identities](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/roman-imperialism-and-local-identities/E69132F012F654F1CCFB763EB7EB5C3A) and [Becoming Roman](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/becoming-roman/A33D558AE5D4B61CAE3B214EA06B783F).

## Human-Coherence Chain

**Cause:** the Prophet dies after the Glass has formed one people, leaving several bounded offices but no universal successor.

**Decision:** southern leagues privilege conquest and permanent settlement; northern camps privilege portable memory and continued route life. Generations later, northern communities choose different legal responses to water, territory, and Tarkhan contact.

**Mechanism:** physical distance changes marriage, trade, defense, property, language, worship, and which institutions remain useful. Forts create territorial authority; caravans create translocal authority; defended wells create enforceable oath jurisdictions.

**Effect:** proto-Atherians, proto-Jirahar, and proto-Ahl-e-Ahd emerge at different times through different processes. Orphaned peoples encounter and reshape them rather than waiting passively to be discovered.

**Cost:** conquest, enslavement, massacre, hostage-taking, dispossession, drought deaths, retaliatory raiding, coerced settlement, destroyed records, broken kinship, and political myths which make victims disappear.

**Consequence:** Atheria becomes a layered civic civilization; Jirahar and Ahdari traditions retain rival parts of a shared northern inheritance; Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan, Sestaran, Vesari, Thalmyric, and Eclessian histories preserve different mixtures of resistance, absorption, imitation, and survival.

## Authorially Fixed for This Research Pass

- the Prophet has no universal human successor;
- the first fission is the gradual separation of southern war-settlement leagues from a Near-Glass continuum;
- proto-Ahl-e-Ahd do not form as an immediate third branch at the outward threshold;
- the northern fission occurs generations later under geographic, legal, marital, economic, and Tarkhan pressures;
- early proto-Atherian settlement includes real conquest, massacre, slavery, hostage-taking, displacement, treaty, intermarriage, and absorption;
- no Atherian conquest possesses divine authorization merely because its participants descend from the Glass people;
- Atherian identity develops from war-settlement membership into maritime, civic, republican, and imperial forms;
- Folk, Ättar, Tarkhan, and Nsizwa remain principally Orphaned;
- Sestaran harbor populations, Vesari, and later Eclessians remain Braided;
- Thalmyrics and Eclessians are later ethnogeneses, not ancient proto-Atherian biological branches.

## Deliberately Open

- the names and number of post-Prophet offices;
- the Prophet's final words and whether any faction possessed an accurate private instruction;
- the first southern league, fort, ruler, and city;
- the number and exact routes of southward movements;
- which early settlements were destroyed, absorbed, abandoned, or refounded;
- the exact date when contemporaries first used a recognizably Atherian name;
- the personal actors behind the northern fission;
- the names of the shared northern camps and early desert coalitions;
- the final selection, names, and biographies of any nursery figures;
- every protected Weird, including the Year of Standing Stars and the nature of the Glass.
