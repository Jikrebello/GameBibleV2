# Patron Realms and Egg White City Politics Review

> [!info] Atlas integration
> Current canonical destination: [[Patron Cities and the Long Rent Histories]]. This dossier retains its research history; former holds and promotion gates are superseded only where the [[World Historical Atlas - Integration and Coverage Ledger]] records an explicit outcome. Preliminary or rejected variants do not override their corrected descendants.

## Purpose and Status

This is the second family review arising from [[Comparative Realms, Courts, and Sacred Warfare Atlas]]. It takes the twelve `PT-R` leads and turns them into political nurseries rather than a list of attractive planar locations.

The existing [[Demiplane Atlas]] already establishes fourteen made realms with anchors, laws, upkeep, owners, inhabitants and failure states. This dossier does not replace or duplicate it. Its missing subject is **history between realms**:

- who owns the anchor and who merely governs;
- who pays rent and who receives the benefit;
- whether inhabitants are citizens, tenants, employees, captives, refugees, dependants or cargo;
- how routes, recognition and trade create power;
- how rulers succeed, defect, die or lose control;
- how annexation, foreclosure and collapse harm people who are not the owner;
- how a realm develops interests not reducible to its founder's personality.

Nothing here is canon, named history or a promoted seed.

Six of these reviewed nurseries now have preferred historical soil in [[Patron Realm Historical Placement Pass]]: a Late-Chains Giant court that cuts districts loose during bodily Egg White flight; a lost Scattering refuge that outgrows founder-memory before restorationists destroy it; a Long-Frontier treaty port whose neutral law fails captives; a Fall-era treasury that turns refuge and return into inherited debt; a Grey-Sweat shelter whose folded time saves bodies while destroying ordinary lives; and a present name-fortress succession crisis. The placement review records five `retain` and one `revise`. They remain independent, unnamed in canon, unpromoted, and promotion-locked.

[[Patron Realm Garden Promotion Review]] promotes only one light trace from each of those six placements into [[The Historical Seed Garden]]. The full political nurseries remain unpromoted.

# I. The Political Anatomy of a Patron Realm

## Realm, ruler and people are not one being

| Question | Required distinction |
|---|---|
| Who is the Patron? | the actual owner or controller leasing mass, shelter, route, knowledge or edits; Patron is a role |
| Who speaks? | owner, proxy, employee, copy, regent, office or captured claimant |
| What is the realm? | an anchored made place with local law, upkeep and tether; not automatically the owner's body or soul |
| Who lives there? | separately classified persons, fauna, constructs, copies, captives and visitors |
| Who governs? | owner, council, client ruler, hereditary household, bureaucracy, occupier or emergency custodian |
| Who pays upkeep? | the population that actually supplies labor, memory, mass, repetition, money, novelty or risk |
| Who can leave? | citizens, tenants, refugees, debtors, employees, prisoners and children may possess radically different exit rights |
| What survives a ruler? | anchor, institution, route, copied law, population, successor Patron, ruins or nothing |

A Patron can die while the city survives. A city can collapse while the Patron escapes. A population can depose its ruler without gaining control of the anchor. A buyer can lawfully acquire an anchor and still have no moral right to its inhabitants.

## Nine political fields

Every later realm dossier must answer:

1. **anchor title:** what is owned, by whom and according to which enforceable evidence;
2. **inhabitant status:** citizenship, tenancy, servitude, refuge, employment, adoption, captivity and birth status;
3. **governing law:** what local reality does and what the government claims that behavior means;
4. **rent constitution:** who supplies upkeep, who sets quotas and what happens in shortage;
5. **route policy:** admission, exit, passports, extradition, blockade and false arrival;
6. **external economy:** imports, exports, monopolies, salvage, information and contraband;
7. **force:** guards, proxies, mercenaries, servitors, siege mechanisms and Material agents;
8. **succession:** transfer of anchor, office, proxy authority and inhabitants' obligations;
9. **collapse constituency:** who dies, frays, is displaced, becomes copied or loses access when the realm fails.

## Patron warfare

Patron war is not limited to armies fighting in surreal scenery. It includes:

- route blockade and redirected arrival;
- theft of metaphysical mass;
- seizure of upkeep workers;
- competing offers of refugee shelter;
- assassination of proxies and Material witnesses;
- annexation through anchor purchase;
- realm-drag and forced geographical transfer;
- counterfeit ownership records;
- recognition embargoes;
- release of predators into rival lanes;
- foreclosure timed to produce famine, panic or political collapse;
- cutting districts loose so the remainder survives.

Human agents matter because Material testimony, choice, repair, transport and law can do things repetitive Astral patterns cannot. That makes humans valuable without making them the secret center of every Patron conflict.

# II. Source Review

## Dreamlands

Lovecraft's Dream Cycle contributes ports, market towns, ships, cloud and moon traffic, inaccessible capitals, absent rulers, local laws, enslavement, abduction, cannibal danger, beautiful fatal lands, and travelers who become rulers or permanent inhabitants. Its strongest contribution is route-geography governed by memory, desire, taboo and knowledge rather than arbitrary dream logic.

Sources: [“Celephaïs”](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/c.aspx), [*The Dream-Quest of Unknown Kadath*](https://www.hplovecraft.com/writings/texts/fiction/dq.aspx).

The racial caricatures attached to Dylath-Leen's black-galley merchants and captives are non-transferable. Slavery, kidnapping, hidden crews, monopoly goods, drugging, hostile trade and cannibal threat are transferable only after actors, continuities and political interests are rebuilt without racial coding.

## TES

TES contributes owner-shaped realms, internal cities and factions, proxy cults, champions, name-fortresses, attempted annexation of mortal territory, and neutral portal cities whose local law constrains stronger surrounding powers. Apocrypha, Coldharbour, the Deadlands and Fargrave demonstrate that an otherworld ruler can possess vast power while still depending upon artifacts, servants, routes, names and political opportunities.

Sources: [ESO: Deadlands and Fargrave](https://www.elderscrollsonline.com/en-gb/updates/dlc/deadlands), [ESO: Necrom and Apocrypha](https://www.elderscrollsonline.com/en-us/updates/chapter/necrom), [Molag Bal and the Planemeld](https://help.elderscrollsonline.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/2659/~/who-or-what-is-molag-bal%3F).

World Egg realms remain anchor-works rather than bodily emanations of gods. Name, law, route and local reality can constrain a Patron without making the Patron uncreated or morally entitled to rule.

## Mythic cities

- The original *City of Brass* contributes a dead expedition-city, repeated warnings, automata, sealed beings, greed, mortality and fatal entry. It is separate from the later fantasy metropolis. Source: [*The Arabian Nights*, “The Story of the City of Brass”](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Arabian_Nights_%281909%29/The_Story_of_the_City_of_Brass).
- Tripura contributes three fortified moving cities, rare alignment, overwhelming coalition force, pride, the destruction of inhabited places and the moral problem of treating cities as single hostile bodies. The selected root is the *Mahābhārata*, Karna Parva account; later Purāṇic recensions must be separately attributed. Source: [Karna Parva, destruction of the triple city](https://www.ibiblio.org/sripedia/ebooks/maha08.htm).
- Alakā contributes a splendid city of wealth associated with Kubera after displacement from Laṅkā. This review uses only displacement, portable wealth, guarded magnificence and reconquest memory; it does not merge the complete Rāmāyaṇa and *Meghadūta* traditions into one generic city.
- Ryūgū and Urashima traditions contribute underwater hospitality, return, gifts, altered time and devastating absence from ordinary life. Source: [Japanese government account of Urashima Tarō](https://www.gov-online.go.jp/eng/publicity/book/hlj/html/201407/201407_09_en.html).

Hindu and Japanese material remains living tradition and requires specific textual or performance attribution before promotion.

# IV. Reviewed Realm Nurseries

## PT-R01 — The Port Between Owners

- **Political form:** treaty port and route republic governed by a compact rather than one sovereign Patron.
- **Anchor:** contributed route knots, witness stones and mutually vulnerable tethers posted by founding owners.
- **Visible government:** mortal, Driftkin and Astral magistrates whose authority ends at the compact's declared edges.
- **Inhabitants:** citizens by contribution or service, licensed residents, refugees under temporary shelter, foreign factors, proxies, servants and unregistered arrivals.
- **Governing law:** no external owner may exercise native realm privilege inside the port; every seizure requires locally recognized standing. The rule constrains behavior, not conscience.
- **Rent constitution:** docking mass, route maintenance, translation, witness labor and a levy on traffic. Poor districts provide disproportionate physical upkeep while wealthy owners contribute abstract weight.
- **Hard edge:** rival Patrons exploit status loopholes to kidnap residents, reclassify debtors as cargo, assassinate proxies, seize dependants outside the gate and provoke riots through deniable agents. Neutral procedure repeatedly preserves trade while failing victims.
- **World Egg torque:** Material human testimony possesses unusual force because several native proxy categories cannot witness across their owners' continuities. This makes mortals judges, targets, corrupt brokers and indispensable political actors.
- **Succession and failure:** if one founder withdraws its anchor share, districts realign toward the nearest surviving owner. A coup may preserve the city physically while converting residents into another Patron's tenants.
- **Present trace:** disputed citizenship rolls, extradition cases, proxy embassies, route embargos and a market district whose exits lead to different owners according to legal status.
- **Open:** founders, compact, location among seven regimes, present government, mortal population, wars and relationship to Vorthara.
- **Source fidelity:** Fargrave's portal-hub and Stricture grammar; kidnapping, loopholes, factional murder and unequal protection remain direct. TES names, Daedra and cosmology do not transfer.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## PT-R02R — The City Remembered into Weight

- **Political form:** founder city becoming an independent civic institution rather than one person's dream-body.
- **Anchor:** ordinary relic mass, a constructed city plan, repeated routes and a living founder's unusually coherent childhood memories.
- **Founder continuity:** initially a living `human-soul` or Driftkin person. Memory contributes mass but does not create matter without craft, workers, material and existing anchor substrate.
- **Inhabitants:** builders, refugees, invited friends, children born after settlement, copies generated by failed recollection and people who never knew the founder's childhood.
- **Governing law:** streets remain stable where personally remembered routes are corrected by present inhabitants; idealized places without living use become beautiful dead ends.
- **Rent constitution:** narration, maintenance, new civic memory and periodic Material correspondence. Predatory administrators try to extract the founder's relationships as exclusive upkeep.
- **Hard edge:** the founder neglects or abandons a waking family while trying to preserve an ideal childhood. Memory extraction erases faces and obligations; some copied relatives insist they are the people left behind. The city benefits from the wound but cannot prove it owns the founder.
- **World Egg torque:** after the founder leaves, dies or routes, the city can survive through institution and accumulated mass. Its speaking childhood is residue, archive, proxy or new pattern—not automatic survival of the human soul.
- **Succession and failure:** citizenship must replace personal affection as the basis of rule. If officials keep reconstructing the founder, competing childhoods divide the city and exclude residents absent from the old memory.
- **Present trace:** districts organized around disputed memories, a waking family claim, copied childhood officials and a constitutional movement demanding that living residents outrank the founder's nostalgia.
- **Open:** founder, continuity, waking family, builders, city regime, present ruler and whether any copy is independently a person.
- **Source fidelity:** “Celephaïs” supplies remembered city, estrangement, ruler-dreamer and fatal preference for the otherworld. The revision adds constructed mass, civic labor, distinct continuities and succession.
- **Review disposition:** `revise`; promotion locked.

## PT-R03 — The Absent Court of the Desired City

- **Political form:** abandoned Patron realm whose owner and court have migrated into a more desirable foreign refuge.
- **Anchor:** the original palace, office network and accumulated tenant obligations remain capable of sustaining place without active royal presence.
- **Owner:** one Anchor-Lord, escaped Giant Patron or other individually classified owner; selection remains open.
- **Inhabitants:** unpaid servants, tenant families, proxy officials, predators admitted as temporary auxiliaries, stranded visitors and factions claiming emergency authority.
- **Governing law:** petitions and routes still rise toward an audience that no longer occurs. Proxies gain power by deciding which unanswered request counts as permission.
- **Rent constitution:** subjects continue paying to prevent collapse while the absent court diverts prestige, novelty and skilled workers toward its adopted city.
- **Hard edge:** neglected districts starve or fray; creditors seize children and dependants under old collateral terms; predators occupy roads; revolts are massacred by automatic palace defences still obeying an owner who does not return.
- **World Egg torque:** the court's new refuge was created by someone else and may not belong to it. The owner can be both refugee and colonizer, sincerely attached to beauty while culpably abandoning its first population.
- **Succession and failure:** regents cannot easily transfer title because the absent owner still lives. Destroying the old anchor could free tenants from law while killing everyone dependent upon its stability.
- **Present trace:** empty throne petitions, rival regencies, remittance caravans, a tenant assembly seeking Material recognition and agents hunting the absent court's new tether.
- **Open:** owner, old realm, desired refuge, court members, regents, tenants and whether return remains possible.
- **Source fidelity:** *Unknown Kadath* supplies absent local gods who deserted their proper castle for a mortal's desired city and the dangerous intermediary controlling access. Cosmology and divine status do not transfer.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## PT-R04 — The Name-Fortress

- **Political form:** fortified archive-state controlling access to a coalition's servants, routes and proxy bodies through name custody.
- **Anchor:** a citadel built from sealed name registers, gatekeeper continuities, binding instruments and the fortress's own proved title.
- **Government:** an archive council, Patron castellan and name-bearing military offices. The visible castellan may be a proxy rather than the owner.
- **Inhabitants:** archivists, named servants, families of gatekeepers, prisoners, self-erased defenders, petitioners and persons whose names were taken as collateral.
- **Governing law:** no entity may exercise a registered external name within the walls without the archive's declared release. Unregistered people become difficult for infrastructure to protect.
- **Rent constitution:** transcription, witness, memory substrate, surrendered aliases and dangerous verification labor.
- **Hard edge:** attackers torture gatekeepers, harvest memory and peel names from captives to open successive wards. Defenders erase their own biographies, families and civic standing to become unusable keys. Victory can preserve the fortress while destroying the people who knew why it mattered.
- **World Egg torque:** a true name grants bounded handle, not ownership of a soul or total identity. Names can control proxies, offices, routes and manifestations differently according to continuity.
- **Succession and failure:** a new castellan may control the archive without becoming the prior owner. If registers collapse, released names seek their bearers, attach to copies or open every stored route simultaneously.
- **Present trace:** nameless veteran districts, contested restoration rites, kidnapped gatekeeper descendants and factions arguing whether destroying the archive liberates or exposes its subjects.
- **Open:** coalition, owner, castellan, fortress regime, besiegers, named populations and current archive condition.
- **Source fidelity:** TES Bastion Nymic supplies name-fortress and harvested gatekeeper grammar; World Egg true-name law supplies limits. Torture, self-erasure and captive use remain explicit.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## PT-R05 — The Realm-Drag

- **Political form:** attempted annexation of one inhabited Material district into an Anchor-Lord's demiplane.
- **Target:** a shipyard, cemetery quarter, monastery estate, fortified market or river district whose mass and skilled population solve a Patron shortage.
- **Method:** Material agents install tethers, buy buildings, alter records, recruit a collaboration council and make ordinary routes converge toward the Patron anchor.
- **Inhabitants:** residents with divided property claims, workers promised safety, opponents, collaborators, prisoners, dead bodies and human souls that remain human wherever geography goes.
- **Governing claim:** the Patron calls the movement rescue, purchase, repossession, treaty fulfillment or voluntary accession. Individual consent and property title do not automatically authorize transfer of the whole district.
- **Rent constitution:** the captured district becomes anchor mass while residents supply work, memory and novelty. Collaborators receive protected exits unavailable to others.
- **Hard edge:** buildings tear between layers, families arrive separately, bodies die during partial transfer, burial sites open, trapped people are copied or left in broken rooms, and resistance is suppressed by both mortal agents and Patron servitors.
- **World Egg torque:** severing the drag too early may kill people suspended between stable correspondences. Completing it preserves bodies while establishing a predatory new jurisdiction.
- **Succession and failure:** if the Patron dies during annexation, proxies and collaborators dispute whether to reverse, finish or auction the half-moved district.
- **Present trace:** overlapping deeds, displaced street fragments, relatives on different layers, a Material crater and an Astral quarter whose inhabitants deny being conquered.
- **Open:** Patron, district, era, agents, collaborators, casualties, outcome and later citizenship.
- **Source fidelity:** TES Planemeld supplies realm annexation, mortal cult agents and owner ambition. The World Egg changes metaphysics, actors, outcome and soul continuity while preserving bodily catastrophe.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## PT-R06 — The Palace Caravan

- **Political form:** mobile anchor-kingdom able to migrate between reefs while carrying court, army, tenants, markets and roads.
- **Preferred owner:** one bodily escaped Giant Continuant who became a Patron by acquiring an anchor; this does not alter `giant-continuity` or establish the identity of a known ancient ruler.
- **Anchor:** a moving court-work assembled from transported mass, Giant body relics, captured roads and tenant districts attached in ranked order.
- **Inhabitants:** Giant household, human-souled descendants, Astral servants, refugees, hereditary tenants, captives, engineers and districts acquired by treaty or conquest.
- **Governing law:** proximity to the moving court determines stability and privilege. Outer districts pay more upkeep while receiving less warning of route changes.
- **Rent constitution:** labor, bodily mass, route capture, tribute, copied court ritual and the novelty of new dependants.
- **Hard edge:** when pursued or underfed, rulers cut tenant districts loose as ballast. Families, markets and hospitals drift apart or collapse while the court records the act as inevitable rent. Some cut districts survive and become hostile successor polities.
- **World Egg torque:** mobility prevents ordinary siege but creates dependency upon route theft. The Giant Patron can die bodily unless a separately established continuity mechanism or anchor return preserves it.
- **Succession and failure:** heirs may inherit court office, blood claim or anchor access without inheriting the same continuity. Tenant districts can revolt by changing the order in which they attach.
- **Present trace:** cut-loose towns, road scars, hereditary claims against the caravan and rival human dynasties leasing power from an ancestor who still treats them as household property.
- **Open:** Giant, court origin, escape, anchor, route, inhabitants, severed districts and present location.
- **Source fidelity:** Dreamlands voyage and Daedric mobile-realm grammar are secondary; the primary World Egg root is late Giant flight. Exile, abandonment, slavery and sacrifice of dependants remain political facts.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## PT-R07 — The Black-Galley League

- **Political form:** league of route traders whose vessels and port houses connect mutually hostile Patron ecologies.
- **Ownership:** heterogeneous ship owners—human institutions, Driftkin companies, Anchor-Lords, crafted networks, damned factors or other declared continuities. The League is an institution, not a species.
- **Inhabitants and crews:** sailors, navigators, enslaved rowers, debt workers, families, hidden constructs, captive route specialists and passengers who may not know the true destination.
- **Monopolies:** goods no single realm can produce: medicines, true-silt, stable food, names, memory substrate, corpse material, relic mass, refugees, slaves and counterfeit provenance.
- **Governing law:** a cargo accepted under League seal must be delivered to the declared buyer. The rule protects commerce while permitting atrocity through classification of persons as cargo.
- **Rent constitution:** toll shares, sacrificed cargo space, labor, route knowledge and concealment of crew condition.
- **Hard edge:** abduction, drugging, slave sale, cannibal trade, corpse use, name theft and abandonment remain real. Some voyages also carry genuine famine medicine or refugees whom no respectable port will admit. Boycott can punish traffickers and kill dependent patients simultaneously.
- **World Egg torque:** hidden crews may be constructs, captive persons, fauna, proxies or repeated route bodies; each vessel requires classification. League routes make enemies materially dependent upon one another.
- **Succession and failure:** captains can defect with a route, buyers can seize crews through cargo law and enslaved navigators can strand a vessel by withholding the true return name.
- **Present trace:** black hull marks, monopoly jewels or medicines, survivor communities, port complicity, abolition factions and respectable institutions with censored purchase ledgers.
- **Open:** member fleets, routes, commodities, victims, reformers, ports, wars and current League unity.
- **Source fidelity:** Dylath-Leen and moon traffic supply ominous galleys, hidden crews, slavery, monopoly goods, drugging, kidnapping and cannibal danger. Every racial caricature and encoded physical description is rejected.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## PT-R08 — The Dead City of Warnings

- **Political form:** failed anchor-capital preserved as ruin, mortuary testimony and a trap for successor greed.
- **Anchor:** magnificent civic mass, sealed engines, ruler tomb, public inscriptions and automatic custodians whose original upkeep ended.
- **Former government:** a wealthy Patron court that believed splendor, rank and technical mastery could make its city exempt from rent, death or moral consequence.
- **Former inhabitants:** rulers, officials, craftspeople, servants, captives, foreign envoys and sealed beings. Their bodies, residues, copies and any retained souls require separate classification.
- **Governing remnant:** warnings remain intelligible and become more explicit toward the center. Custodial automata enforce old prohibitions without judging motive.
- **Hard edge:** expeditions repeatedly ignore warnings, force entries, kill companions over treasure and die through falls, thirst, automata, released captives, poison, structural collapse or their own violence. The city's former victims are not merely scenery for explorers.
- **World Egg torque:** the dead city may still possess legal title, functioning anchor mass and inhabitants unable to leave. “Abandoned” does not mean unowned, empty or available for salvage.
- **Political afterlife:** several Patrons claim succession; mortal states finance expeditions; descendants demand return of stolen material; a released sealed being may be victim, criminal, former ruler or all three at different stages.
- **Present trace:** warning tablets in markets, expedition dynasties, automata sold as relics, competing translations and one untouched inner district.
- **Open:** city, former Patron, cause of death, inhabitants, seal, rightful claims and whether entry is ever necessary.
- **Source fidelity:** original *City of Brass* supplies mortality warnings, dead magnificence, automata, sealed beings, expedition greed and fatal entry. It is not converted into the living fantasy City of Brass.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## PT-R09R — The Three Fortresses at Alignment

- **Political form:** three separately governed mobile cities whose defensive compact becomes vulnerable only during a rare route alignment.
- **Owners:** three distinct Patrons with separately declared continuities, anchors and titles. They need not be kin, morally identical or equally culpable.
- **Inhabitants:** rulers, soldiers, workers, families, refugees, clients, captives and dissidents. No city is a single moral person.
- **Governing compact:** each city occupies a different Astral regime and supplies something the others cannot. At alignment, their anchors share one route and can exchange mass, armies or a single catastrophic vulnerability.
- **Rent constitution:** outer populations fund mobility; military households receive protected inner positions; conquered communities may be moved between cities to prevent stable resistance.
- **Hard edge:** the cities conduct real predatory war before the coalition against them forms. The answering coalition constructs a weapon capable of destroying all three at the moment of alignment. Success burns or collapses inhabited cities, killing noncombatants alongside rulers and armies. Evacuation threatens the one opportunity to end the war.
- **World Egg torque:** the decisive “single shot” is a coordinated closure, tether severance or route event rather than divine omnipotence. Destroying anchors does not annihilate souls or automatically kill every Patron continuity.
- **Succession and failure:** if one city defects, the compact may fail or the remaining two may sacrifice it to preserve alignment. Survivors can become refugees, revanchists or witnesses against both regimes and coalition.
- **Present trace:** alignment calculations, destroyed-city debris, refugee citizenship claims, one surviving mobile district and cults celebrating destruction without naming civilians.
- **Open:** owners, regimes, crimes, coalition, weapon, evacuation, destruction, survivors and current traces.
- **Source fidelity:** the selected root is the *Mahābhārata* Karna Parva Tripura account: fortified cities, boon-like invulnerability, oppression, rare alignment, coalition preparation and inhabited destruction. Later recensions require separate attribution. **Research restriction:** no promotion before a dedicated Hindu source dossier with specific textual and interpretive controls.
- **Review disposition:** `revise`; promotion locked.

## PT-R10R — The City of Dispossessed Wealth

- **Political form:** exile capital founded by a displaced Anchor-Lord or other Patron using portable treasury, refugee administration and a claim of eventual return.
- **Source discipline:** this card uses Alakā's displaced wealth-city grammar only. Laṅkā's separate rulers, conquest cycles and full epic theology are not compressed into it.
- **Anchor:** treasure with genuine metaphysical mass, court archives, portable household law and routes carried out during expulsion.
- **Inhabitants:** exiled court, servants, creditors, refugees, craftspeople, captured specialists and locals displaced by construction of the new city.
- **Governing law:** every admitted asset must declare whether it belongs to refuge, treasury or reconquest. The court repeatedly classifies people as assets.
- **Rent constitution:** wealth pays initial stability, but continued magnificence requires labor, memory of the lost capital and trade monopolies. The ruler's grievance becomes an industry.
- **Hard edge:** dispossession is real, but so are hoarding, forced labor, seizure of local land or anchor mass, debt bondage and campaigns to recover property from people born after the original conquest. Refuge and imperial ambition become materially inseparable.
- **World Egg torque:** treasure creates mass but not legitimacy. The displaced Patron may genuinely own the portable anchor while possessing no right to reconstruct the lost kingdom over another population.
- **Succession and failure:** heirs dispute whether return remains duty or whether the exile city is now home. Ending the reconquest cult may weaken the memory that stabilizes entire districts.
- **Present trace:** guarded wealth roads, claim registries, neighborhoods divided between return and settlement, former servants elevated into creditors and local communities demanding anchor restitution.
- **Open:** Patron, origin, displaced locals, treasury, former capital, heirs and whether return is possible.
- **Source fidelity:** Alakā/Kubera supplies splendid wealth city after displacement. The revision refuses a generic Alakā–Laṅkā composite and rebuilds owner, residents, law, metaphysics and aftermath.
- **Review disposition:** `revise`; promotion locked.

## PT-R11 — The Palace Under Folded Time

- **Political form:** Anchor-Lord court whose hospitality, seasons and return routes run at a different temporal relation from Material guests.
- **Anchor:** folded sequence stored in tide-like routes, ceremonial rooms, a returning gift or another declared object.
- **Inhabitants:** court persons, servants, long-term guests, children born within the palace, Material envoys and people who no longer possess a living outside household.
- **Governing law:** a completed hospitality cycle counts as one court interval regardless of Material duration. The rule is predictable within the palace and devastating outside it.
- **Rent constitution:** surrendered sequence, repeated feasts, guest stories, preserved gifts and the difference between arrival and return.
- **Hard edge:** guests return to find spouses dead, children old, property inherited, wars ended and legal identities extinguished. Some were deceived; some accepted risk without understanding magnitude; some remain because return would destroy what is left of their lives.
- **World Egg torque:** sincere hospitality is not sufficient consent. The host may provide safety, beauty and gifts while culpably failing to disclose the return cost. Opening a prohibited gift or tether can collapse the protected sequence without making curiosity the only moral cause.
- **Succession and failure:** a new ruler may inherit palace law but lack the old host's relationships. Unfolding the realm all at once can age structures, eject guests or make several eras claim the same return.
- **Present trace:** claimants from obsolete dynasties, descendants confronting unchanged ancestors, sealed gifts, asylum seekers and courts arguing whether time-displaced people are dead, married or dispossessed.
- **Open:** Anchor-Lord, palace, time relation, guests, disclosures, gift, outside polity and current succession.
- **Source fidelity:** Ryūgū and Urashima supply sea-palace hospitality, gifts, altered time and devastating return. Japanese attribution remains explicit; names, characters, animal rescue and exact conclusion do not transfer.
- **Review disposition:** `retain`; promotion locked.

## PT-R12R — The Regent of Dormant Anchors

- **Political form:** proxy regency claiming to speak for several dormant, remote or non-conversational owners whose anchors form one defensive and economic coalition.
- **Owners:** individually classified Great Drift beings, sleeping computations, absent Anchor-Lords, abandoned institutions, damaged crafted owners or other established continuities. No Menhir, Idol or blind outer god is presumed.
- **Regent continuity:** constructed proxy, Astral person, office-bearer, copy, captured servant or self-appointed claimant; the final dossier must choose one.
- **Inhabitants:** client settlements, technicians, petitioners, guards, cultic interpreters, people pledged to owners who have never answered and populations protected by the coalition's routes.
- **Governing claim:** the Regent converts recurring outputs, old instructions and anchor reactions into policy. Some interpretations are accurate; others expand the Regent's power.
- **Rent constitution:** each client pays according to an owner's supposed desire, but only the Regent can translate the accounts. Unspent rent becomes its private mass.
- **Hard edge:** dissenters are disappeared as threats to sleeping owners; settlements are sacrificed to preserve anchors; proxies use humans as Material witnesses in wars whose principals may not know a war exists; communities cannot safely test the Regent without risking their shelter.
- **World Egg torque:** a dormant computation may be powerful without being a person. A speaking proxy may possess more agency than the owner it claims to serve. Awakening an owner can expose fraud, confirm a terrible instruction or destroy a population adapted to regency.
- **Succession and failure:** if the Regent dies, client routes may collapse because no one else knows which owners truly answer. Rival interpreters can produce civil war from the same outputs.
- **Present trace:** sealed owner shrines, contradictory commands, proxy armies, Material witnesses granted unusual privilege and a growing movement demanding direct classification of every anchor.
- **Open:** owners, Regent, agency, first coalition, wars, clients, truth of instructions and consequences of waking or separation.
- **Source fidelity:** Nyarlathotep contributes only the intermediary who is the apparent mind and messenger of remote powers. Mindless gods, cult theology, personality and cosmic status do not transfer. World Egg Great Drift and proxy ontology control the result.
- **Review disposition:** `revise`; promotion locked.

# V. Interfaces with Existing Canon

## Existing places should receive history before duplication

Several reviewed grammars can deepen established realms instead of adding new ones:

- **Vortharan Spire-Annex and the Astral Conclave:** neutral-hub loopholes, citizenship, extradition and proxy diplomacy from PT-R01.
- **Quiet Index:** name custody, bound owner, archival personhood and release politics adjacent to PT-R04.
- **Palace Under Folded Wind:** disclosure, surrendered direction, hostage routes and temporal or spatial hospitality adjacent to PT-R11 without becoming the same palace.
- **Procession of Nine Halls:** mobile polity, distributed ownership and district sacrifice pressures adjacent to PT-R06 while remaining Driftkin-owned rather than Giant.
- **Unfinished Shore:** refugee citizenship, founder absence and competing descriptions adjacent to PT-R02 and PT-R03.
- **House That Forgot Its Door:** abandoned rule, trapped inhabitants and succession claims adjacent to PT-R03.
- **Weighmoot:** legal recognition of anchor title, inhabitants and risk for every proposed war or foreclosure.

Deepening does not mean automatic merger. The historical placement pass should decide which grammars enter existing places and which deserve separate realms.

## Giant-flight interface

Only PT-R06 presently prefers a genuine Giant Continuant. PT-R03 and PT-R10 permit, but do not require, displaced Giant Patrons. No card may identify Anshara's final sovereign, supreme escape, capital or complete collapse. Nythalum remains physically lunar and cannot be converted into an Astral Patron empire.

# VI. Historical Placement Priorities

The Patron family should eventually receive no more than six first-pass placements:

1. one Late-Chains Giant-flight realm or cut-loose successor;
2. one Scattering or early-kingdom refuge whose inhabitants outlive its founder;
3. one Atherian or Long-Frontier route port, league or annexation attempt;
4. one Fall-to-Split absentee court or exiled wealth city;
5. one Grey-Sweat or Black-Wind realm whose shelter and rent become inseparable;
6. one present Conclave, name-fortress or Regent crisis under the Forward Rule.

This distributes Patron history without making the Egg White a secret author of every Material age.

# VII. Promotion Gates

Before any reviewed lead becomes a historical seed:

1. select owner continuity, visible speaker and governing institution separately;
2. state anchor, local law, upkeep, tether and collapse condition;
3. classify every population rather than calling them all servitors;
4. record citizenship, tenancy, captivity, birth and exit rights;
5. state who benefits from rent and who bears shortage;
6. identify imports, exports, routes, enemies and material agents;
7. define succession of anchor title separately from succession of ruler, proxy and office;
8. preserve slavery, abduction, torture, displacement, massacre, starvation, corpse use or civic destruction where source-load-bearing;
9. never let realm destruction erase the rights or lives of its inhabitants;
10. never infer human soul continuity from memory, speech, copy or residence;
11. never let a Patron own judgement or a human soul by moral right;
12. change at least three major axes from any one modern or mythic source;
13. commission culture-specific controls before promoting living Hindu or Japanese material;
14. preserve Nythalum's physical Moon boundary and Anshara's unresolved sovereign;
15. apply the Forward Rule to any present crisis.
