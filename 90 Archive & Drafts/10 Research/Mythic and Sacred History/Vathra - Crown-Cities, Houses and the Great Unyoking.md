# Vathra — Crown-Cities, Houses and the Great Unyoking

## The aerial dominion

Vathra was a confederated Giant kingdom whose rulers carried their courts through the Material sky. Its dominion flourished above the northern dry belt during the last two centuries of Giant rule and collapsed between approximately 2300 and 2260 BR. The great cities contained palaces, workshops, gardens, cisterns, prisons and human neighbourhoods. Smaller estates rose for a season or campaign, while stripped war-platforms carried soldiers and weapons.

The civilisation took its name from the royal itinerary rather than a single capital. A Vathran king governed by appearing over a subject district, receiving tribute and hostages, settling disputes among client houses and departing before the country exhausted its stores. Human settlements supplied nearly all food, water, fibre, timber and ordinary labour. The aerial court made that dependence difficult to see in its monuments and royal histories.

Vathra ranked below the Five Imperial Horizons. At its height, its rulers negotiated with them as peers, married into lesser Giant houses and sold cavalry, animals and captives across several frontiers. The kingdom never possessed the population, religious reach or continental infrastructure of a Horizon. Its distinctive power lay in moving concentrated force across country which could not support a large permanent court.

## The founding descent

Vathran royal genealogies began with the **First Road-Mother**, a Titan-Firstborn associated with a bounded manifestation of Hecatara and a human woman. The full Menhir sovereignty neither entered the conception nor diminished itself. The Firstborn possessed Titan continuity and no human soul. Later High and Dynastic Giants descended through marriages among her children, neighbouring Giant houses and selected mortal partners.

The oldest histories attributed three achievements to the Road-Mother: the opening of the first seasonal sky route, the relocation of a royal herd during famine and the defeat of a Giant prince who controlled the western wells. The first two may preserve genuine route correspondence. The third belongs to several incompatible dynastic versions. Package C need not choose the Firstborn's personal name or final fate.

Later rulers used this ancestry to claim ownership of movement itself. They taxed roads they had not built, demanded children from communities which had never accepted their protection and treated escape as theft from the crown. Hecatara's response to a prepared route proved only that the route could open under its actual conditions.

## How the cities flew

Vathran city-flight combined four systems.

**Lift bodies** were hollow foundations built from light stone, Giant bone, timber and metal lattice. Their chambers distributed the weight of walls and reservoirs. Builders renewed them whenever cracks crossed the principal ribs.

**Pressure mantles** were immense layered textiles stretched beneath and around the foundations. Plant fibre, hair, metal thread and treated membrane channelled an atmospheric pressure associated with Zephyraxis. Knots and seams controlled lift, load and direction. A torn mantle could raise one side of a city until buildings slid from their foundations.

**Route anchors** joined a city to prepared fields, towers and mounds. They used Hecatara correspondences to place distant destinations beside one another for a departure. Repeated use displaced part of the cost onto the country below: a road lengthened, a seasonal stream shifted or a familiar approach returned a traveller to its starting point.

**Human operation** kept the first three systems working. Pilots read pressure and weather. Weavers repaired mantles and tied destinations. Ground crews moved ballast, loaded water and cleared landing fields. Gardeners, cooks, waste crews and animal handlers sustained the population between descents. Giants controlled access and punishment; human skill supplied daily motion.

Small sky-carpets used the same principles. A courier cloth could carry one or two people along an open current. Tribute carpets moved chests, food baskets or animals. War carpets carried archers over a wall or between platforms. Most became heavy textiles when taken away from a prepared route. A damaged piece sometimes retained its last destination and carried an unwary rider into danger.

## Houses and rank

Vathran society divided its ruling dynasties by altitude and function. The ranks expressed culture, inheritance and power rather than separate Giant species.

### The House of Ozhur

The rulers of **Urzavad** held storm rank and claimed the closest descent from the Road-Mother. They controlled the high route calendar and the winter assembly of the crown-cities. Their court cultivated weather sight, long hearing and lightning weapons. Distance from the ground became a sign of purity in their ideology.

The last ruler, **Ozhur IV**, was a High Giant with sufficient inherited power to keep Urzavad above ordinary storm height for short periods. He inherited a failing route system and answered scarcity by increasing requisitions. His orders killed subject communities before the civil war began. The crisis explains his choices without excusing them.

### The House of Varakesh

The cloud-ranked house of **Dazhur** controlled herd exchange, tribute storage and most commercial flight. Its lower routes gave it better knowledge of human stations than the high court possessed. Its rulers maintained lavish suspended gardens, mist screens and menageries filled with captured animals.

**Varakesh of Dazhur**, a Dynastic Giant and Ozhur's cousin by two lines of marriage, believed that the cloud houses could save Vathra by displacing the high dynasty. He concealed food shortages, purchased tether-house loyalty and took over several war-platforms. His willingness to discard dependants made his proposed reform another form of predation.

### The House of Kel-Orun

Kel-Orun's rulers held a disputed place between cloud and tether rank. Their city contained the largest mantle workshops and trained pilots for several courts. Human households there possessed unusual influence because no dynasty could replace their accumulated craft quickly.

**Kelvara**, a Dynastic Giant of a junior line, supervised the city's descent grounds. She initially served Ozhur and allowed captive workers to be transferred to Urzavad. She changed sides after seeing the remains of Tal-Kesh and the people killed beneath Dazhur's discarded ward. Her later assistance saved thousands, yet it did not restore those whom her earlier service had harmed. If retained, she can occupy the existing ancient slot for a morally repentant Giant defector who receives temporal mercy. This decision remains subject to the actor-slot review.

### Tether houses and human officers

Tether houses governed wells, anchor fields and pasture districts. Some consisted of younger Giant branches. Others were giant-blooded human dynasties or ordinary human officers elevated through service. Court law blurred these distinctions because descent supplied rank; the authorial account must classify every person separately.

Tether rulers could prosper from landings. Markets gathered, craftsmen received work and local judges gained access to royal petition. They also enforced tribute, conscription and child-taking. Their mixed position explains why some supported the revolt, some defended Vathra and others changed allegiance according to which city approached.

## Six crown-cities and their fates

### Urzavad, the High Seat

Urzavad was the largest crown-city and the winter seat of the high kings. Its upper palace, weather galleries and lightning towers stood above human quarters packed around workshops and reservoirs. During the final battle, Ozhur tried to climb above Varakesh's weapons before his water and breathable corridors were ready. A damaged mantle tore across the eastern foundation. Urzavad struck a war-platform and fell over Tal-Kesh.

The crash killed Ozhur bodily, most of his court and thousands of humans in the city and on the ground. Later treasure stories placed the Road-Mother's heart beneath its central tower. The claim has never been established and cannot be used to resolve the Firstborn's fate.

### Kel-Orun, the Lowered City

Kel-Orun was brought down deliberately. Seyra's weavers retied the pressure mantle while ground crews opened a line of prepared fields. Kelvara prevented royal guards from closing the lower gates. The descent split two foundation chambers, crushed buildings and killed hundreds, but most inhabitants escaped.

Post-Vathran settlements grew among its cisterns and ribs. People quarried the palaces, turned a processional court into a market and used mantle cloth for shelter. The event gave later northern communities a durable image of power made useful by being lowered.

### Dazhur, the City that Cut Away its Ward

Dazhur carried tribute stores, royal herds and a large captive district. During an assault on its lift, Varakesh ordered the lower ward released. Houses, prisoners, animals and workers fell while their families remained inside. The discarded mass struck a tether settlement and killed people who had never entered the aerial city.

Dazhur survived the immediate battle and disappeared from reliable records within three years. Its act belongs to Vathra's War of Altitude. It remains separate from the later Egg White caravan which used copied ancestors to misdirect a supernatural pursuit.

### Sairat, the Returning Archive

Sairat contained Vathra's mobile archive, foster genealogies and route accounts. Its rulers entered a Hecatara path after rebels destroyed the destination anchor. Later northern traditions describe a city returning above the same empty tract at long intervals, with more of its stone missing each time.

Package C should establish Sairat's departure and the destruction of its anchor. Its later appearances remain unresolved. A returning structure could be the original city, an automatic route image or a copied procession. None proves the survival of its rulers.

### Molqara, the Blind Arsenal

Molqara began as a war-platform and grew around its prisons, armouries and stone-dropping galleries. Its crew lost steering during the final revolt while portions of the lift continued to function. Stories place an armed ruin in the distant northern sky, shedding metal and answering movement beneath it.

The platform's original existence is suitable for ordinary history. Its survival in 1360 remains a future placement question. Any present response might come from machinery, an office pattern or an imprisoned court person rather than a living Giant commander.

### Yazuran, the Vanished Crown

Yazuran carried a cadet dynasty which attempted a forbidden route during the last war. No confirmed wreck or landing has been found. Egg White Patrons, copied ancestors and drifting ruins have each claimed its name.

Yazuran remains protected. Its fate must be reconciled with the established slot for one Giant escaping bodily into the Egg White, the counterfeit escaped ancestor and the ancient Hunt of the Escaped Courts. Package C establishes only that the city departed and failed to reach any known Material anchor.

## Human households of the sky

The people who lived aboard the crown-cities did not share one status. Some pilot and weaving families held hereditary privileges, including property, servants and the right to petition a Giant ruler directly. Contracted merchants travelled between courts. Captive craftsmen worked under guard. Children born to hereditary dependants inherited their obligations unless a patron household secured release.

Women and men worked in the mantle houses. High-altitude cold damaged fingers and lungs, and maintenance continued through most nights before a departure. A skilled caller could hear a wrong knot from the change in the mantle's vibration. Pilots trained apprentices by sending small cloths between tether towers before allowing them aboard a city.

Life aloft also contained gardens, kitchens, games and religious processions. Human gardeners produced herbs, onions and fruit in protected courts. Children raced weighted cloth toys along safe currents. Musicians timed lifting ceremonies so that ground crews could coordinate by sound. Court art later represented these pleasures as gifts of the kings, although human labour created them.

## The human conspiracy

**Seyra Vatan** belonged to a privileged weaving household whose members had served Kel-Orun for four generations. Her mother supervised a royal processional mantle; her father died after being sent to repair a war-platform during lightning. Seyra learned the complete knot vocabulary and trained captive workers whom the court treated as replaceable hands.

**Dorun of Tal-Kesh** managed ground routes for Urzavad. The court had taken his elder child into Ozhur's household and fostered his younger child at Dazhur as security for his obedience. Dorun knew the order in which anchor crews received instructions and the points at which a false confirmation could delay a city.

**Meya of Narash** governed three western wells through a maternal caravan house. She had profited from Vathran protection and supplied captives taken in local wars. Increased royal seizure threatened the breeding animals and dependants on which her own authority rested. She joined the revolt to preserve them and never presented herself as an innocent opponent of Giant rule.

The three began cooperating after rival courts stripped the same station twice. Seyra supplied knot patterns. Dorun altered route notices. Meya placed food and camels beyond the tribute fields. Enslaved armourers at Molqara made cutting tools, and cooks carried messages between human quarters which royal officers rarely searched.

Their plan sought to ground Kel-Orun, abandon selected anchors and release the human trains. It did not envisage the crash of Urzavad or the massacres which followed. Local rebels pursued their own aims. Some wanted escape; others wanted the property of collaborators or revenge against Giant households. The Great Unyoking remained a coalition of overlapping revolts rather than the work of three secret founders.

## Reprisal, revolt and descent

Ozhur discovered the loss of western herds and ordered Tal-Kesh cleared. His guards executed route workers and threw their bodies from an anchored platform as a warning. Dorun's elder child died in the punishment. The order brought hesitant ground crews into revolt and destroyed any prospect of restoring the route schedule peacefully.

Rebels killed overseers, opened prisons and burned tribute records. At one tether estate they murdered the human family of a Giant official after he had fled. At another, a pilot protected the Giant child she had raised and was killed by neighbours who regarded all court dependants as enemies. Later northern songs removed many of these divisions in favour of a single glorious rising.

Kelvara reached Kel-Orun with orders to lift the city beyond the rebels' reach. She instead opened the lower gates and gave Seyra control of the mantle house. Royal guards fought through the human quarters while the descent began. Ground crews held the western moorings until the foundation settled, knowing that a broken cable would strike them first.

Dorun did not survive the final days. One account places him beneath Urzavad's crash; another says he returned to Dazhur seeking his younger child. No reliable history recovers the child's fate. Meya led displaced families towards the Narashi roads and spent her remaining years settling access among people whose former obligations had burned with the archive.

## The end of a Giant kingdom

Vathra ended when no surviving house could join court, ground labour, food and movement under a common itinerary. Individual Giants survived. Grounded structures remained inhabited. Human officials preserved techniques and records. These continuities did not restore the state.

The wider Great Unbinding influenced the collapse through failed Giant alliances, human revolt and the breakdown of supernatural systems. A commissioned heavenly action may have bound one Titan-Firstborn or severed an unlawful recurrence used by a royal house. The Phoenix did not fight Vathra's war for its human subjects, destroy every city or grant the rebels a universal mandate.

## Historical inheritance

The people below Vathra preserved camels, wells, animal medicine, weaving, metalwork, fosterage and portable assemblies. They lost city-scale lift, the central route archive and most royal horses. A royal mantle became a tent over a freed household. A tether cable became marriage rope. Fallen foundation stone became the curb of a human well.

The most enduring political memory concerned occupied sky. Later Tarkhan songs treated open air as freedom from a descending court, even while fearing lights, bells and shadows above. Their later interpretation of Kokh, the Blue Eternal, belongs to human religion. It does not reveal Zephyraxis under another name.

## Proposed authority layers

### Proposed authorial history

- Vathra consisted of several aerial crown-cities, rising estates and war-platforms supported by a larger human ground economy.
- Ozhur and Varakesh began the final War of Altitude through a dynastic struggle over failing routes and tribute.
- Human workers planned and carried out the Great Unyoking.
- Urzavad crashed, Kel-Orun was deliberately grounded and Dazhur discarded an inhabited ward.
- Human liberation included retaliatory killings and was followed by famine and displacement.

### Received histories

- Seyra, Dorun and Meya appear under different names and relationships in northern songs.
- Kelvara's repentance and the nature of any mercy she received remain disputed.
- The Road-Mother's remembered deeds mix dynastic genealogy with actual route effects.

### Protected questions

- the Road-Mother's personal name and final fate;
- the post-departure fate of Sairat, Molqara and Yazuran;
- whether any surviving sky carpet remains fully operational in 1360;
- the identity of any Giant who escaped bodily into the Egg White.
