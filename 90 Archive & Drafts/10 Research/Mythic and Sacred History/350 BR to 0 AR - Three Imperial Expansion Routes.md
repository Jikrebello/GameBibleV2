# 350 BR to 0 AR — Three Imperial Expansion Routes

> [!warning] Alternatives for review
> These are three ways to order the same required imperial growth. Every campaign, transfer and annexation below is a **proposal**, except for the established events identified as canon. No option changes a canon article or creates a canon date by appearing here.

## Selected direction

The creator selected **A: southern provinces first**, with **B's Sestaran/Elmaric naval network operating throughout the period**. The principal Atherian–Vasharan wars should occur **after 0 AR but before the Ahl-e-Ahd emerge from 541 AR onward**. Vashar therefore remains a consequential independent peer at the Redeemer boundary. Pre-0 eastern campaigns, above all Orent's established Arcelon operation, may involve local resistance, limited border war, defections and negotiated transfers, but should not be written as a decisive conquest or fragmentation of Vashar. C remains a rejected pre-0 architecture and a useful source of later-war questions.

The selected route still has to explain Atherian Raveth by 119–113 BR, Qiryath under Atherian authority by Isfir's trial, and Isfir's 33 BR birth in an eastern imperial tower town. It does **not** require the whole Ubar basin, Arqesh, Avesh or Vashari highlands to have passed to Atheria by 0 AR. Their precise 0 AR status needs a relational geography pass before any city-by-city chronology is settled.

If 0 AR is confirmed as Atheria's **all-time territorial maximum**, the later Vasharan wars should principally contest, defend or temporarily disrupt that inherited frontier rather than add a still larger durable province. "Before the Ahl-e-Ahd" means before the Covenant's revelation and state formation from 541 AR onward; Ahdari people and well societies already exist long before then.

## Fixed board and historical lesson

The [[350 BR to 0 AR - Imperial Expansion Canon Constraint Sheet|constraint sheet]] records the sources. At 350 BR Thalion rules Caleran and has annexed Sarqen; Qahur has three crowns under competing Vasharan and Atherian pressure; Ortheia is independent despite Atherian speech; Vashar holds Avesh, Arqesh, the Ubar, Qiryath and its highland core; the Elmaric sea is divided; Casvilar's southern conquest is only an uneven beginning; and most northern dry country lies beyond Atherian control.

Every route must preserve the Four Eagles in 247–246 BR, Orent's eastern campaign and Arcelon road around 190–182 BR, Raveth as an imperial river province by its famine in 119–113 BR, Doran's southern veteran settlements after 61 BR, Isfir's birth in an eastern imperial Jirahar tower town in 33 BR, the Star establishment and Atherian execution in 0 AR, and the Great Tower Revolt only after 66 AR. The creator's present working target is an empire at full territorial breadth by 0 AR, including extensive land later inherited by Eclessia and a directly governed Qahuri cultivated Basin by 33 BR. Its exact high-water claim awaits later-history review.

The real Roman record offers **mechanisms**, not an event schedule. Hispania took nearly two centuries of uneven occupation and war; southern Gaul became a province before Caesar's rapid conquest farther north; Egypt was annexed relatively late; eastern provinces coexisted with client kingdoms and an independent Parthian peer. Rome's maximum area came after Jesus's lifetime, whereas the creator's proposed Atherian maximum comes by 0 AR. Sources: [Metropolitan Museum of Art on Iberia](https://82nd-and-fifth.metmuseum.org/toah/ht/04/eusi.html), [Cambridge on the southern Gaul corridor](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/greece-and-rome/article/abs/caesar-de-bello-gallico-book-i-cc-141/0C3CA590FB44900F66F20974E673B139), [Metropolitan Museum of Art on Roman Egypt](https://www.metmuseum.org/de/essays/roman-egypt), [Metropolitan Museum of Art on eastern provinces and Parthia](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/The_Year_One_Art_of_the_Ancient_World_East_and_West.pdf), [Cambridge on Rome's later maximum](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/antiquity/article/urban-form-infrastructure-and-spatial-organisation-in-the-roman-empire/0A1433E14B6469E08B2B027B7C54ED6B).

## A. Southern provinces first, Qahur last — recommended

**Main logic:** Thalion's successors convert Casvilar's southern footholds into large provinces over generations. A continuous maritime network supplies and connects those gains. Southern provinces provide soldiers, timber, metal, estates and taxes for limited eastern campaigning. The Qahuri Basin becomes the final great annexation before Isfir's birth. Vashar loses selected western dependencies but retains the resources of an independent eastern empire.

| Period | Proposed development |
|---|---|
| **350–247 BR, Thalion and first house** | Stabilise Sarqen and the Aurin. Meren's canon southern campaigning grows into a series of negotiated and violent submissions among particular Belathi and Hartheni river districts. Cassara's roads, harbour stores and quarry programme make deeper occupation possible. Ortheia and Sestaran harbours move through separate treaties, bases and occasional annexations. Olarion's court leaves unresolved military and tax grievances; the Four Eagles interrupt consolidation. |
| **246–151 BR, Velorans** | Samor restores the frontier commands; Tessa funds depots, river crossings and municipal agreements. Southern provincial rule extends across substantial river, forest and hill country later inherited by Eclessia. Imperial fleets maintain the Elmaric and eastern-sea supply chain. Orent's fixed eastern campaigns take the Arcelon road and neighbouring approaches. Limited war, provincial defection or treaty begins the transfer of Raveth and other western river districts; Qiryath's Atherian accommodation can follow in this or the next phase. Vashar retains its highlands and substantial eastern possessions. |
| **151–61 BR, Serrats** | Elian's citizenship policy turns conquered southern and eastern local elites into imperial municipal partners. Raveth is indisputably an imperial province by its famine of 119–113 BR. Qiryath's negotiated Atherian supremacy must be settled before the Redeemer boundary without ending its courts or sacrifice. The Qahuri crowns become increasingly bound to Atherian grain contracts, port protection and succession arbitration while remaining formally separate. Sea harbours occupy varying direct, charter and allied positions. |
| **61–33 BR, Doran and Othran I** | Doran's restored army and Othran I's provincial command exploit a specific Qahuri succession or war among crowns, ending direct independence in Tenaret, Qemaret and Sakhret by 33 BR. Their canal and temple administrations continue beneath imperial supervision. Doran's canon southern veteran grants settle previously taken districts and provoke land disputes. |
| **24 BR–0 AR, Othran II** | The new Qahuri provinces and older southern/eastern territories are assessed, garrisoned and connected. Imperial roads, star establishment and provincial courts supply the mature world of Isfir's ministry and execution. |

**Strength:** The empire's later Eclessian provincial base has generations to mature. Qahur's late annexation gives the Egypt comparison its strongest timing while emerging from Qahur's own three-crown politics. Orent's eastern campaigns and the Raveth famine become causally useful. Vashar remains available for the larger post-0 conflicts the creator wants. **Cost to develop:** A continuous southern sequence requires named local coalitions and several campaigns rather than one emperor's victory. The government must afford southern, eastern and maritime commitments without claiming every victory happened at once; eastern transfers need convincing local causes despite the absence of a great interstate war.

## B. Sea first, then inland provinces

**Main logic:** Ships, harbours and food routes create the empire's first expansion system. Atheria takes or charters key Elmaric and eastern-sea landings, then advances inland from several shores. Qahur is tied to that network early but formally annexed late. This route gives the Sestaran and Delkosi worlds a larger role in the empire's formation.

| Period | Proposed development |
|---|---|
| **350–247 BR** | Thalion's Sarqen victory becomes the western base of a naval system. Caleran obtains selected Elmaric harbours and pilots, Ortheian access and Delkosi cooperation through distinct war and charter arrangements. Qemaret's lower-river houses gain favourable shipping and become Atherian clients, while Tenaret and Sakhret remain independent. Southern fleets hold landings and river mouths, but large inland Folk provinces are not yet secure. |
| **246–151 BR** | Samor and Tessa turn ports into supply bases. Orent uses them for his fixed Arcelon campaign, cutting Vashar's western access and bringing Raveth and Qiryath into Atherian rule or a tightly supervised settlement. Along the southern rivers, land armies join coastal footholds into broad provinces; the eastern and southern provincial bodies cannot be just strings of ports by the end of this phase. |
| **151–61 BR** | The Serrats regularise municipal rights for pilots, island councils, river cities and southern Folk elites. Rival Qahuri crowns contest Atherian fleet access and grain dues. Raveth's famine may deepen the court's interest in reliable Qahuri food supply. A naval defeat or major storm could expose how dependent the state has become on its sea network. |
| **61–0 AR** | Doran and Othran I convert the Qahuri client relationship into direct rule over the cultivated Basin by 33 BR. Doran's southern veteran settlements and Othran II's fiscal settlement complete the territorial system by 0 AR. |

**Strength:** Distinctive to World Egg's divided island and Gateway geography; explains imperial fleets, Sestaran autonomy and why Qemaret's shipping matters. **Cost to develop:** Sea power cannot itself govern the inland Folk districts or Sakhret's southern river. The sequence needs explicit inland armies, winter supply and negotiated local administrations so the Eclessian and Qahuri provinces are real territories.

## C. Eastern great war, then a southern surge

**Main logic:** Vashar remains the principal rival after 350 BR. A long contest for Ortheia, Avesh, the Ubar and Qiryath shapes the first two centuries. A decisive Veloran settlement breaks Vasharan supremacy in the western riverlands. The empire then uses eastern wealth and soldiers to conquer broad southern Folk districts and settle Qahur. Vashar may survive as a reduced highland peer or fragment into clients.

| Period | Proposed development |
|---|---|
| **350–247 BR** | Thalion and Meren preserve the 350 BR settlement while governors and independent Ortheian and Delkosi rulers play both empires. Atheria obtains limited southern districts and sea bases. Vashar backs one side in local eastern conflicts and may profit from Olarion's disorder and the Four Eagles. |
| **246–151 BR** | Samor's military restoration and Tessa's road system prepare a major eastern war. Orent's fixed Arcelon campaign becomes its turning point: Atheria wins the Ubar/Raveth districts, Qiryath and strategic coast. The terms imposed on House Tiravan decide whether Vashar keeps the highlands, becomes a client or breaks into several successor courts. |
| **151–101 BR** | Provincial eastern administrators and former Vasharan clients intervene in Qahur's crown politics. Atheria annexes its cultivated Basin earlier than in A or B; Qahuri temples and canal officers remain necessary to rule it. Raveth's famine then occurs within a broad but unequal eastern fiscal system. |
| **101–0 AR** | The major Folk provincial surge comes relatively late, using settled eastern and Qahuri revenues. Some campaigns fall amid Cassen's prosperity and Velen's court decline; Doran restores the army, finishes disputed southern districts and makes the canon veteran grants. By Isfir's birth the eastern and Qahuri provinces are old imperial territory; southern integration is newer. |

**Strength:** A dramatic interstate conflict grows directly from the canon 350 BR balance; the Arcelon campaign gains large geopolitical consequences. **Cost to develop:** Southern expansion becomes compressed into the last century and needs to leave enough time for the old provincial layers later visible in Eclessia. Breaking Vashar too completely may remove a useful independent eastern peer. Qahur's annexation loses the close late-Egypt timing, although its long provincial life can deepen Isfir's imperial setting.

## Comparison and recommendation

| Question | A — southern first | B — sea first | C — eastern war first |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earliest major investment | Folk river and forest provinces | Fleets, islands and coastal charters | Ortheia/Vashar frontier |
| Broad southern provinces | Grow across most of the period | Grow from ports during the middle period | Acquired chiefly in the final century |
| Qahur direct by 33 BR | Late annexation | Late annexation after long client ties | Earlier annexation |
| Vashar in 0 AR | Independent highland peer | Independent highland peer | Peer, client or fragmented courts — review choice |
| Isfir's eastern world | Old enough to be institutionally settled; Qahur recently annexed | Connected by sea and roads; Qahur recently annexed | Deeply provincial eastern world; newer southern frontier |
| Principal weakness | Requires patient, multi-reign southern history | Risks confusing naval reach with territorial control | Compresses the Eclessian predecessor provinces |

**Selected: A as the main spine, borrowing B's maritime infrastructure.** It gives the large later Eclessian provincial base sufficient time, preserves Orent's fixed eastern victory, lets Vashar remain consequential and makes Qahur's conquest a late but locally caused event before Isfir's birth. The decisive Atheria–Vashar conflicts belong to later imperial history. No option should treat the present Sestaran Concord, Virelia, Al-Qahur city or Covenant as existing in 0 AR.
