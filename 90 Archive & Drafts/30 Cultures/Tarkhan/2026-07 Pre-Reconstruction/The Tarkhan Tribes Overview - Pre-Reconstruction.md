# The Tarkhan Tribes Overview

The mobile peoples of the middle-north steppe — the grass road between the Covenant's desert and the settled south, flanking three empires at once. Mongol/Hun-coded with an Oghuz-Seljuk current, and one crucial wrinkle: like the plains peoples of another world, **the Tarkhan were not born horse-lords. They became them — fast.**

## Player-Facing Fantasy

Mobility, hard bargaining, and survival in open country. A Tarkhan ally can get a caravan through impossible country; a Tarkhan enemy can make an army starve without ever fighting a pitched battle. And under everything, the ache of a people fifty years past their greatest hour, arguing over a dead man's saddle.

## The People Before Horses

For two thousand years after the Scattering, the Tarkhan were the steppe's *poor* cousins: foot-herders, dog-sled clans, well-diggers between empires, raided by everyone with cavalry. Their own phrase for that age is a whispered humiliation and a sacred memory at once: **"we were slow once."**

Then Caleran fell (905 AR), and the Atherian remount system fell with it — the great border studs of the dying empire scattering ten thousand blooded horses into the grass. The Tarkhan caught them. Within three generations a walking people had become the finest light cavalry in the world, and every sacred horse-line the clans keep today (canon) descends, by chanted genealogy, from the **First Catch**. The horse-religion is thus young and ferociously intense — the way only a salvation within memory can be. The mare who "chose" to be caught first, **Grey Sister**, is the steppe's founding saint, and her line's foals are diplomatic gifts worth provinces.

## The Black Wind (1261–1303 AR)

For centuries the horsed Tarkhan stayed what canon describes: a political weather system, clans around khans. Then came **Qarayel, the Black Wind** — a minor clan's orphan who rose exactly when both neighboring empires lay exhausted from their own wars (the post-Qarath's Well decades). At the **Kurultai of the Long Grass (1261)** he did what no khan had done: united all the banners under the **Nine-Mane Standard**.

The **Black Wind Years** (1265–1300) were the steppe's apex: Iqashar paid him tribute politely renamed "escort fees"; the Velian border fairs burned twice and paid thereafter; Sestara instituted the **grass-toll** (Sermora's ledgers still carry the line-item, relabeled "northern route assurance"); and the one army the Covenant sent into the deep grass came back as a cautionary poem. Qarayel never besieged a great city — he had learned the steppe's oldest lesson, that walls are a trap in both directions — and instead perfected **extraction without occupation**: the whole settled north paid him to stay mobile.

He died in the saddle in **1303**, old, undefeated, and unsucceeded. His grave is the steppe's holiest secret: a thousand horses trampled the site, the burial riders never returned, and **whoever finds the grave — every Tarkhan believes — will hold the succession the kurultai could not settle.**

## The Partition of the Herds (1304) and the Four Hordes

The kurultai of 1304 broke on the question his life had suppressed, and the banners divided by his children's lines:

- **The White Horde** — line of **Temuchar**, the eldest, whose paternity the other lines have questioned for fifty years (he was born nine months after his mother's ransom from captivity — the steppe's Jochi wound). Holds the richest far-grass and the largest herds; nurses the bitterest claim. Present khan: **Batuqai**, Qarayel's great-grandson, patient as winter.
- **The Iron Horde** — line of **Berkan**, the second son. Covenant-facing, and *drifting Covenant-ward* along the old Oghuz road: Iron banner-lords sell heavy-cavalry service to Iqashar, take Covenant seal-faith in a horse-lord's key (an Aqid rides with every Iron war-camp now), and learn the law of the people they guard. Present khan: **Tegrul**, who has begun sealing contracts *in his own name* — and every Sestaran analyst who has read any history at all is watching the employer-becomes-conquest trajectory with professional dread. Present khan of the Guard's nightmares.
- **The Moon Horde** — line of **Alakha**, the daughter, keeper of the old sky-faith in its strictest form and custodian of the **Nine-Mane Standard** itself (no full kurultai can be called without it). Rules through khatun-regencies; keeps the grave-secret's *search* forbidden. Present ruler: **Khatun Sorqai**, called the Mother of Banners — the closest thing the steppe has to a shared conscience, and old.
- **The Ash Horde** — line of **Qutan**, the youngest. Sestara-facing: caravan-taxers, road-lords, and raiders of the Castara hinterland — the "nuisance" the Concord budgets for annually. Sermora quietly hires Ash lances against rivals and quietly insures against them the same season. Present khan: **Chilgir**, young, debt-ridden to the Under-Ledger's certain delight, and hungry for a name.

**The standing question (canon, now armed):** a unifier could make the Tarkhan a continental power again; a failed unifier scatters them into mercenary bands. Three paths to the Nine-Mane Standard exist — Sorqai's blessing, the grave's discovery, or a kurultai forced by common enemy — and all four hordes know all three.

## Society, Faith, and Law

Canon structures stand (clans, khans by success and gift-giving, oath riders, spirit keepers, bond markets), now grounded:

- **The sky-faith:** the Tarkhan worship **the Kokh, the Blue Eternal** — the endless sky — with the sun, moon, and winds as its household. *(Authorial spine: this is Menhir-worship in steppe idiom — the sky-regime read as one god; the wind-speakers are closer to right about the Second Circle than any augur ever was, and would be horrified to learn it.)* **Wind-speakers** (shamans) read weather, omens, and the dead; the horse-ancestor cult runs beside it, Grey Sister at its head.
- **Law:** the **word-price** — every oath has a stated cost for breaking, declared at the swearing. Cheap oaths are an insult; the settled peoples' unsecured promises strike Tarkhan as either sorcery or fraud. (Covenant jurists find Tarkhan law adorably primitive and completely sound; the Iron Horde is where the two systems are currently interbreeding.)
- **Hospitality:** three days at any fire, guest-right sacred — and its mirror: a guest who lies at the fire may be pursued *forever*. Several Sestaran factors have learned the tense of that verb.
- **Missionaries:** the Church (both rites), Covenant Aqidin, and Jirahar caravans all work the grass. Scripture-faiths baffle the clans ("a god who lives in a *box*"), but the Freeflame's landlordless fire gets a puzzled, interested hearing — and the Iron Horde's seal-conversion is the century's live religious drama.

## The Present Board (1360 AR)

To **the Covenant**: a hired sword growing opinions (Iron), and a northern flank it cannot fortify (White). To **the Sestaran cities**: the Ash Horde taxes Castaran approaches, Vardessa hosts the great horse fairs, Sermora hires lances, and Fiorenzia insures against the same people its clients employ. To **Eclessia and Thalmyria**: distant thunder, for now — both churches' strategists have noted that the last time the steppe unified, it took thirty-five years for the bill to reach everyone. To **the Jirahar**: the safest roads in the world, oddly — Qarayel ruled that the wandering people paid a flat peace-price, and the hordes still honor it ("they were slow once too, and never forgot how to move"). See [[Sestara and the Wider World]].

Along the arid Covenant edge, Tarkhan mobility changes with the ground. Horses remain sacred and dominant on the grass; camel-breeding border clans carry trade and raids across the driest well-country. Iron Horde companies work both kinds of remount in Qahirid service, while White Horde and dispossessed splinters test the roads beyond [[Mazrakan, Market of the Nine Measures]].

## Game Use

Canon hooks stand (escorts, arbitration, horse-theft, taboo mounds, the unifier campaign), plus: the grave-hunt (forbidden, priceless, and someone in every horde is quietly digging); an Iron Horde contract-crisis where Tegrul's seal conflicts with his banner-oath (word-price versus Covenant law — a jurisdiction duel); the Nine-Mane Standard as the ultimate steal-and-return quest; and Sorqai's death, whenever it comes, as the board-shaking event every chancellery already wargames.

## Naming Register (locked)

Turkic-Mongol filtered: coined names favoring **-ai / -an / -uk / -ul** endings and kh/q sounds (Qarayel, Batuqai, Tegrul, Sorqai, Chilgir); **khan/khatun** kept as translation-convention titles (genre-standard); banner-lord (not noyan), wind-speaker (not shaman-loanwords), the Kokh (the Blue Eternal). Forbidden verbatim: real steppe-historical personal names (Temüjin, Attila, Osman, etc.).
