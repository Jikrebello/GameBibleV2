At the heart of Thalmyric life lies the **Thalmyric Church**, a deeply mystical, ascetic, and militant interpretation of the **Faith of the Redeemer**. Born in the crucible of war and shaped by centuries of hardship, it differs greatly from the **Eclessian Church** to the east despite sharing the same spiritual origin. The Thalmyric form of the faith is steeped in personal suffering, spiritual fire, and constant preparation for divine warfare.

---

### **[[The Redeemer as Divine Warrior]]**
To the Thalmyric faithful, the **Redeemer** is not merely a benevolent savior but a **warrior-ascetic** who descended into the mortal world to wage a spiritual and physical war against the **Adversary**. His crucifixion is not just a sacrifice but a **combat miracle**—a cosmic act of martial defiance that burns sin and darkness alike.

- **Depictions** of the Redeemer often show him clad in simple robes, bearing the scars of the worlds sin as wounds, or surrounded by flames.
- In Thalmyric theology, the Redeemer's pain is an **initiation rite** for all believers. To follow him is to suffer, to struggle, and ultimately, to transcend.

---

### **[[The Holy Fire]]**
The **Holy Fire** is the central spiritual motif of the Thalmyric Church. It is both a literal and metaphysical flame said to originate from the Redeemer himself. It:

- **Purifies** the soul through pain and asceticism.
- **Annihilates** corruption, heresy, and demonic influence.
- **Illuminates** the path to divine truth in a world shrouded by spiritual darkness.

Miracles in the Thalmyric Church are often flame-related: swords that blaze with holy fire, censers that burn heretics with smoke, and priests who invoke heatless flame during exorcisms. Pilgrims often carry scorched tokens—small coals or ember-touched iron—as proof of devotion.

The phrase "**Burn to be redeemed**" is a common Thalmyric mantra.

---

### **[[Mysticism and Asceticism]]**
Unlike the bureaucratic structure of the Eclessian Church, the Thalmyric path is intensely personal and mystical.

- **Mystics**, often hermits or wandering monks, seek visions through fasting, silence, and exposure to the elements.
- **Ascetic practices** include sleeping on stone, ritual scarring, isolation in sacred grottos, or extended pilgrimage to fire-shrines in dangerous frontiers.
- **Divine communion** is believed to be possible not through sermons, but through transformative suffering.

Visions are considered one of the highest forms of divine communication. Entire theological doctrines have emerged from the visions of flame-bathed saints and scorched prophets.

---

### **[[The Patriarchate of Veridun]]**
The spiritual and administrative center of the Thalmyric Church is the **Patriarchate of Veridun**, seated in the holy city of **Caleran** within the **Kingdom of Veridun**. The **Patriarch of Veridun** is considered the spiritual successor to the Redeemer's own fire-born apostles.

- The Patriarch is elected by a conclave of senior ascetics and high clergy, usually after undergoing a period of isolated contemplation in the **Cavern of Smoke and Flame**, a volcanic shrine near Caleran.
- The Patriarch wears robes embroidered with phoenix feathers and a crown of black iron, shaped like a burning brazier.

#### **The Council of Flame**
This inner circle consists of:

- **Hierophants**: scholars of holy mysteries and flame doctrine.
- **Ember-Visionaries**: monks who have received multiple divine visions.
- **Flame-Marshals**: military priests who coordinate religious efforts across the frontier.

The Council of Flame interprets visions, declares heresies, sanctifies military campaigns, and guards ancient relics believed to hold the Redeemer's fire.

---

### **[[Church-Military Unity]]**
The fusion of faith and warfare is one of the most defining features of the Thalmyric Church. The Church sees the reconquest not as a worldly ambition but as a **holy war**, decreed by the Redeemer's divine will.

- **Priests and chaplains** bless armor, weapons, and soldiers with sacred oil and fire.
- **Paladins**, especially those of the **Order of the Phoenix Ascendant**, act as both knights and preachers.
- **Battle chapels** line the frontiers, offering blessings, healing rites, and last rites before battle.

The **Sacraments of Flame** include:

- **Anointing Before Battle**: Sacred ash and oil placed upon the forehead, invoking divine protection.
- **Communion of Ember**: Blessed bread and spiced wine infused with smoke or consumed beside flame.
- **Pyric Oaths**: Vows taken before a fire, binding warriors and commanders to spiritual obligations.

Every military campaign is sanctioned with a **Ceremony of Illumination**, where the flame of a battlefield altar is kindled with embers from Caleran’s eternal brazier.

---

### **[[Iconography and Worship Spaces]]**
- Churches are built of dark stone, often on high places, their spires crowned with flames or phoenix sculptures.
- Interiors are dim, lit by candles and braziers. Incense thick with charcoal and spice burns constantly.
- Altars are simple but scorched, marked with geometries meant to evoke burning wheels and celestial orbits.
- Worship includes **chanting in Old Jirharian**, meditative kneeling, and occasional acts of public penance (walking across heated stones, bearing chains, etc).

---

### **[[Saints and Relics]]**
The Thalmyric Church reveres a wide array of saints, many of whom died in agony or embraced fire as purification.

- **Saint Anthera of the Pyre**: Self-immolated to prevent a cursed relic from falling into enemy hands.
- **Saint Lucirion the Ember-Eyed**: Gained divine sight after gazing into the Eternal Flame for nine days.

Relics are highly sacred and include:

- Ashes of saints stored in glass vials.
- Ember-charged weapons or armor.
- Scrolls partially burned in divine visions, their surviving words believed to be direct revelations.

---

### **[[Theological Schism with Eclessia]]**
The Eclessian Church views the Thalmyric Church as dangerously mystical and overly militant. In turn, the Thalmyrics see Eclessians as decadent, detached, and too aligned with temporal power.

- The schism is more than political; it is **spiritual rivalry**, with both claiming to be the true successors of the Redeemer’s will.
- Both churches await a **prophesied unifier** who will restore the glory of the Atherian legacy, though their visions of this figure differ wildly.

---

### **Conclusion**
The Thalmyric Church is a powerful force in the empire—an engine of both faith and war, a keeper of mystical flame, and a crucible of divine vision. It calls its followers not just to believe, but to burn: with devotion, with zeal, and with the sacred fire of redemption.
