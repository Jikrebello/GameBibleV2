# Lives of Thalmyria — Seven Roads from Cradle to Grave

The grounded pass: what a life actually is in the empire, 1360 AR, role by role. The rule throughout — **the strange is real and rare.** An ordinary Thalmyric sees the Flame do something inarguable perhaps twice in a lifetime, hears of drakes the way we hear of avalanches, and will never knowingly meet anything from the Left Hand. The lore is the weather; these are the people standing in it.

## The Shared Spine (every life, any road)

Born at home, likely with a midwife, possibly with a priest if it goes wrong — **one child in five does not see a fifth birthday**, and every family owns that arithmetic. Presented to the **hearth-lamp** before the church font. Named on the eighth day, usually for a grandparent or a saint. Betrothed by family logic, crowned in iron, married into a lamp. Worked from age seven at *something*. Dead, on average, in the fifties if the childhood years are survived — later for monks and bureaucrats, earlier for soldiers and mothers (childbed is the empire's quiet battlefield; the widows' guilds keep those rolls too). Faced toward the lamp at the end, ember before the eyes, the ladder of memorials after: third day, ninth, fortieth, year's mind. Everyone — emperor to bandit — walks that spine. The roads below are what happens between the waypoints.

## 1. The Farmer (seven in ten of everyone)

**Born** in a two-room stone-and-timber house in a lowland wheat village or a highland terrace hamlet; the lamp-niche is the third thing built after roof and hearth. Childhood is work that grows with the body: goose-girl at five, gleaning at seven, plow-team by twelve. Letters only if the parish priest runs winter school (perhaps a third do); the catechism is learned by song either way.

**The decisions:** whom to marry (the parents propose, the young couple can refuse — twice gracefully, a third time with consequences); whether the second son takes the **soldier-tenure** (land for service in a frontier theme — the empire's standing offer and the village's pressure valve); whether to risk the **ash-vineyards** (triple the income of grain, one killing frost from ruin); and, once or twice in a life, whether to join a pilgrimage year to Caleran — the one journey most farmers ever take, and the thing they describe on their deathbeds.

**The texture:** taxes in kind at harvest (the theme's assessor, the Church's tithe-barn, the landlord's third if untenured — a farmer keeps perhaps half); the fast calendar shaping the plate more than wealth does; village law settled on the church porch by the priest and two elders before it ever reaches a magistrate. Fears, in honest order: frost, fever, conscription sweeps in a crusade year, the assessor's remeasurement, and only then — distantly — anything with claws.

**The strange, lifetime dose:** the Rekindling runner every year (miracle as *civic infrastructure*); one drought broken after the relic-procession came through (coincidence? the village has argued for thirty years); the field near the old barrow that nobody plows and nobody discusses.

**Old age and death:** the holding passes at the crowning of the heir, not at death — the old couple keeps the hearth-corner and the lamp-duty, which is honor and pension both. A farmer who dies with the fortieth-day feast paid for and no debt outliving him is accounted to have won.

## 2. The Soldier

**Born** anywhere; *made* at the muster. Three doors in: the **theme levy** (the farmer's second son, land-paid, local, defends what he can see), the **regulars** (coin-paid, posted anywhere, the poor man's ladder), and the **war college path** (for those with letters and a sponsor — Caedros IV's own road). Trained two years if the empire has two years; the frontier era means it often trains you on the wall.

**The decisions:** which god you fear more — the enemy or the paymaster's arrears; whether to take the **frontier posting bonus** (double pay, Zanthir border, actuarially honest); whether to marry (the regulars discourage it, the themes require it — a tenure without heirs reverts); and the one that defines the middle years: **re-enlist or take the plot** — every veteran's fork at each seventh year, and the pension-plot is real land in a real theme, which is why the empire's borders are farmed by men who can still form a shield line.

**The texture:** lentils ("the long war"), boot-repair as a survival skill, the unit as second family (it keeps your fortieth-day forever — canon), hymn-lamps in the field chapel that *work when the company deserves it*, which the men monitor with theological precision nobody taught them. Battle is rare; walls, roads, escort duty, and boredom are the actual career. The unspoken things: what weaponized Flame does to the men who carry it (the lamp-bearers rotate out young and pray old), and the crusade question every barracks avoids until it can't.

**The strange, lifetime dose:** more than most — a frontier man has seen contract-lights over Covenant works, heard the Unsealing's bound things named in enemy war-cries, maybe once watched a war-lamp answer a hymn with something that was not fire. He does not talk about it at home. None of them do.

**Death:** in bed at the pension-plot if the fork was taken in time; on the wall if not; and either way the regiment sends two men and the arrowhead-rings ceremony to the funeral, forty years on. No Thalmyric soldier is buried alone. It is the army's one unbreakable promise, and it recruits better than the pay does.

## 3. The Bureaucrat

**Born** to a guild family, a priest's brother's household, or a veteran's plot — the chancellery is the empire's true ladder, and it starts with **letters**: parish school, then the theme's registry as a copy-boy at eleven, then — for the sharp ones — the **Logothete's Examination**, open to any subject who can pay the sitting fee or win a parish sponsorship. Prokopia's office grades blind. The empire quietly runs on the fury of clever provincials this one honest door lets in.

**The decisions:** which ministry (the treasury is power, the lamps-office is honor, the army's commissariat is money, the registry of the dead is — genuinely — sacred work, feeding the memorial ladder and starving the Undertow, though the clerk knows only that the forms *matter*); whether to marry into the office (chancellery dynasties are real and resented); and the career-defining one — **whose signature you'll risk yours under** when the irregularity crosses your desk. Every clerk gets that day. The whole empire is shaped by ten thousand small answers to it.

**The texture:** ink-stained cuffs as a class marker; the wax-seal hierarchy memorized like scripture; salary in coin *and* grain-right (the empire hedges its own money); the annual audit-fast (the treasury fasts through its own reconciliation — half piety, half proof of nothing-to-hide). Fears: arrears rolling downhill, a patron's fall, and the Sestaran-style forensic audit that ended three careers in living memory.

**The strange, lifetime dose:** almost none, which is the point — except the registry men, who learn that certain filings are never late and never wrong, and choose not to wonder why the office of the dead has the chancellery's best attendance.

**Death:** pensioned, lettered, and — the profession's dream — *cited*: a form, a clause, a register format that outlives you. Bureaucrats build the only monuments that get used daily.

## 4. The Parish Priest

**Born** usually poor — the priesthood is the other honest ladder — marked early by the catechism-master, sent at fourteen to the theme seminary (fed, beaten, and taught real theology in that order), ordained at twenty-four. **Parish clergy marry** in the Thalmyric rite (a married man understands a village; the celibate path belongs to the monasteries), so the priest's wife is a parish office in all but name.

**The decisions:** the parish or the mountain (the monastic fork, taken young or after grief — never in between, says the proverb); how hard to press the lamp-tithes in a bad year (the bishop's letter says one thing, the widow's face another); what to do about the *almost-right* — the folk charm, the double-dated feast, the Velian family at the village edge — where doctrine has an answer and the village has a peace, and they are not the same answer.

**The texture:** the priest works — glebe-land, bees, winter school — because the tithe feeds the church before the churchman; his hymns *usually* work at the font and the deathbed, in the modest way ordinary sanctity earns (per the Kindling canon, though he'd never phrase it so); he keeps the parish register that is the village's whole legal memory; and he stands at every one of the spine's waypoints for every soul in the valley — presenting, crowning, embering, laddering. No one in Thalmyria knows more people or buries more friends.

**The strange, lifetime dose:** the second-most exposed road. A deathbed that went *wrong* once — the thing the keens warn of, arriving at the window; a hymn that answered with more than warmth, once, at a drowning; and the annual dread-duty of reporting anything odd up the ladder, knowing an Ember-Visionary might arrive, and what that does to a village.

**Death:** in the parish, ideally mid-office; buried by the bishop if lucky, by his own son-successor if luckier. Village memory keeps priests the way armies keep soldiers: the good ones get the lamp named after them.

## 5. The Bishop

**Made, not born** — though family helps: perhaps half rise from the monasteries (the Ember-Visionary path: holiness as credential), the rest through seminary brilliance or chancellery-adjacent families (the doctrine-men). The Conclave's arithmetic between those two currents *is* Thalmyric church politics.

**The decisions:** whose man to be — the Patriarch's, the Emperor's, or (career-endingly, occasionally sainthood-makingly) neither; how to spend the see's three currencies — **relics, appointments, and audits**; whether to push the Zanthir crusade in synod or count the veterans in the pews and hold; and the one that haunts the good ones: what to do when a **field report** crosses the desk that is either a miracle, a fraud, or a Left-Hand feeding architecture — because the Bruckmoor file is required reading, and the ruling is his.

**The texture:** a bishop's day is estates, courts, and correspondence — he is a magnate who happens to be ordained, with a household of forty, a stable he shouldn't enjoy, and a fast-calendar he keeps in public and negotiates in private. Above him: the Council of Flame's three-way pull (Marshals, Hierophants, Visionaries). Below him: two hundred parish priests whose letters he actually reads, because the empire's real intelligence service is the parish register.

**The strange, lifetime dose:** professionally curated — he has stood where the Flame did something inarguable (it is, quietly, half of why the Conclave chose him), and he has also signed the file that said *no miracle occurred*, and he knows which signature cost more.

**Death:** in office, entombed in his cathedral, his crozier broken over the grave and his lamp carried to his successor's enthronement — the office never dies, which is the comfort and the sentence of it.

## 6. The Criminal and the Bandit

**Born** on the same roads as everyone, diverted by the usual doors: debt, deserter's brand, a feud, a harvest that failed while the assessor's didn't. Thalmyria's underworld is shaped by its geography and its virtues — mountains for hiding, veterans for skills, and a society so oath-bound that **even crime keeps liturgy**.

**The forms:** city crime runs in **brotherhoods** with lamp-parodies of guild structure (the Caleran wharf-rings swear on cold braziers — the Church finds this blasphemous; the brotherhoods consider it *honest*); highway banditry clusters on the pilgrimage roads in the weeks after feast-days (piety moves coin); the mountain passes keep **mountain oath-bands** — half outlaw, half remembered-resistance, fed by villages that recall when men in those same caves fought the Covenant, and who still won't rob a Rekindling runner, because there are lines.

**The decisions:** what you will not do — every band defines itself by its refusals (pilgrims? corpses? the lamp-tithe wagon?); whether to take the **amnesty musters** (the army's standing offer: the brand traded for the frontier, the empire's oldest recycling program — half the Zanthir border garrison has a past); and when the time comes, whether to go to the priest, because Thalmyric bandits die *shriven* at rates that astonish Eclessian inquisitors. Refusal is for the Hollow Flame. A Thalmyric thief fully intends to settle the account late.

**The texture:** fences in the port quarters, Sermoran buyers who ask nothing, the Under-Ledger's men (met once, regretted permanently — the brotherhoods have learned the difference between crime and *that*), and the Red Tally's recruiters circling every feud-driven band in the Aversian borderlands.

**The strange, lifetime dose:** more than they wanted — tomb-robbing is the underworld's worst-paying good-sounding idea, and every band has a story about the barrow job nobody talks about, told exactly once, drunk.

**Death:** the rope, the wall, or — the folk-song ending — old and pardoned on a frontier plot, teaching pilgrims' sons to whistle. The gallows chaplain hears the empire's best confessions; two are in the seminary curriculum.

## 7. The Noble

**Born** to one of the great houses (old Caleran blood, mountain-rebellion blood, or Reconquest-grant blood — three vintages that never let each other forget the sequence) under a name chosen for dynastic signaling, with the lamp-presentation performed by a bishop and the wet-nurse chosen by treaty. Childhood is curated: letters at five, horse at seven, the war college or the convent-school at twelve, and the understanding — administered early, like inoculation — that **the person is the house's asset**.

**The decisions:** made mostly *for* them, and the interesting nobles are defined by which one they take back: the marriage (crowned in silver, negotiated in acreage; refusal is possible and priced); the career fork — **sword, synod, or stewardship** (the army theme-command, the church see, or the estates — a great house tries to hold all three in one generation, which is the whole game); whether to attend court at Veridun (presence is expense and exposure; absence is suspicion); and the generational one, live in every hall right now — **which succession candidate to quietly fund**, because Alexane, Sophios, and the adoption whisper each have a price sheet, and abstention is also a bet.

**The texture:** the estate as a small state (the noble's actual job is arbitration, granary policy, and keeping the theme's muster-rolls honest); the fast-calendar as competitive display; the Sestaran debt nobody admits (the Medori hold paper on a third of the great houses, per the family's own canon); the Diadem's beautiful invitations (declined by the wise, per the Left Hand); and honor-culture in its Thalmyric key — dueling is illegal, church-condemned, and *thriving*, conducted as "vigil-quarrels" at dawn with witnesses who were coincidentally all praying nearby.

**The strange, lifetime dose:** proximity without intimacy — a relic in the family chapel (audited twice a century), a great-aunt among the Ember-Visionaries whose letters everyone reads carefully, and the house's one *story* — every old house has one — about the founder's bargain or the sealed room or the portrait, which is usually embroidery and occasionally, per the Left Hand's files, is not.

**Death:** in the house's own chapel-crypt, with a fortieth-day feast that doubles as the heir's first political event, and the name carried forward — which is the entire theology of nobility, and why the Church's insistence that the Weighing takes no account of lineage is heard politely, annually, and not believed.

---

## Design Rule for All Seven

When writing any Thalmyric NPC, place them on their road's decision-points first — which forks they took, which they were denied, which is coming — and dose the strange honestly: **twice a lifetime for the farmer, curated for the bishop, once and never-discussed for the soldier.** The fantasy stays enormous because the lives stay actual size.
