# Quest Tier Doctrine

Three quest grades, matching the reference games named in the design brief, each with its own construction rule. Companion to [[The Conversion Library — Classic Adventures Recontextualized]] and the encounter pyramid in [[The Mundane Layer — Residue, Vermin, and the Delver Trade]].

## Tier W — the Witcher Grade (marquee side-quests)

**Rule: a moral knot, discovered late.** The quest presents as a Tier-R job (kill the beast, find the girl, clear the barrow); the middle reveals the knot (the beast is a punishment someone earned; the girl left on purpose; the barrow was sealed *from outside, recently, by someone who knew*); the end offers two or three closures, none clean, all of which the world remembers. Construction: start from a **Lives file** decision-point (a serf's flight, a debtor's arithmetic, a priest's almost-right village) and let the monster or mystery be the *symptom*. The Grenzburg three-layer rule (material / legitimacy / metaphysical) already enforces this for main-arc quests; Tier W applies it to side content.

## Tier F — the Faction Ladder (Skyrim/Oblivion guild grade)

**Rule: rank buys obligation.** Each ladder is 6–10 quests: three establishing competence, two exposing the faction's internal fracture (every canon faction has one built in), one forcing a side, and a capstone that changes the faction's board position. Grenzburg's ready ladders: the **Blackjack Corps** (dual-chain tension by design), the **Church** (Bishop/Inquisitor/Reliquarian triangle), the **Folk** (clan-by-clan trust with the Lakekeeper shadow), the **delver trade** (licenses, salvage law, the forty-first lamp), and the **garrison** (Hohenwall's wall-discipline against winter arithmetic).

## Tier R — Radiant Templates (repeatable generators)

**Rule: template × location × faction-stake = infinite honest jobs.** Twelve canonical generators:

1. **Collection writ** — a Bank debt to collect; the debtor always has a story (radiant Tier-W seeds).
2. **Corridor escort** — wagons, pilgrims, or Folk hostages through contested ground.
3. **Grave-watch** — Souls-month sexton duty; usually boring, which is the trap.
4. **Vermin bounty** — rattle-bones, leakspawn, harridans, warg packs; the bread of the trade.
5. **Ruin sweep** — clear and certify a barrow/undercroft for a charter-holder.
6. **Salvage claim** — recover named goods from a named wreck/ruin before rival crews.
7. **Missing person** — the Funnel eats people; most are findable, some shouldn't be found.
8. **Drake-sign survey** — confirm or refute lair-signs before panic prices grain.
9. **Relic transport** — authenticated cargo, Church escort clauses, three parties who want it.
10. **Debt mediation** — Bank vs. household, resolvable by coin, work-pledge, or bad precedent.
11. **Supply run** — winter logistics under weather, tolls, and theft; the Numbers doc prices it.
12. **Feast-day trouble** — calendar-driven: the bride-ale brawl, the Quiet Fire trespass, the Kindlenight count.

**Radiant discipline:** every template updates one number (per the Numbers doc design rule) and may, at low frequency, *escalate a tier* — one collection writ in twenty uncovers an Under-Ledger clause; one grave-watch in fifty goes genuinely wrong. That low frequency is what keeps Tier Three sacred.
