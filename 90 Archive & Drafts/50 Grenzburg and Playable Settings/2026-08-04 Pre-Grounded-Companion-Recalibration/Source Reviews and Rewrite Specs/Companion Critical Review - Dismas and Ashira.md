# Companion Critical Review — Dismas and Ashira

An adversarial read of CMP-06 and CMP-04, same standards as the previous two reviews. Quests treated as replaceable; character shapes held fixed.

**Fairness note:** these two sit at opposite ends of the production spectrum. Dismas is the roster's least-built companion — four outline quests, no campaign doc, no Field Familiarity, vocation "pending reassessment," empty relationship perks, seven of eleven slots reserved. Ashira is *nominally* the opposite — every piece "cleared and dialogue-approved" in the first paired pass — but see finding S2: her clearance happened at a fraction of the density the other cleared pair received. This review is therefore mostly prescriptive for Dismas and mostly a flesh-audit for Ashira.

**The one-sentence verdict:** these are the two best-*written* companions in the roster at the bible and banter level — and the two least-built at the quest level; the danger for the paired passes is not fixing bad material but failing to feed the best material in the vault.

# Part I — Systemic Findings

## S1. The arrival crisis, third instance — and the ruling that completes the principle

[[The Name on the Charge]]'s outline: "Marta Etten, sole known survivor of the crushed coach, reaches Grenzburg with a civil charge naming Dismas." His bio: "No survivor, former gang member, road rival, or Korrheim official physically arrives in Grenzburg." Third campaign in a row with a past-person arriving in defiance of the Remote Claims Standard; third pending-migration flag.

**Ruling: agree with the audit — cut the arrival.** This is the *opposite* of the Lio ruling, and the reason completes a principle worth writing into the Constitution. Dismas's flaw is flight from aftermath; his redemption theme is stated in his own frontmatter: "commitment can remain free when it is repeatedly chosen." An arriving survivor with a civil charge does the work *for* him — the world corners him, the court compels him, and his accountability becomes something that happened *to* him. His bio already knows this: "public accountability he chooses rather than a courtroom plot that corners him." A subpoena redemption is a domestication arc, which his own failure-mode list forbids ("resolves his story by placing him obediently beneath a good commander").

The three rulings together: **Lio arrives** (the wrong was relational; the climax is a bout that needs two bodies). **The Salt-Reeds stay away** (the wrong was control; the climax is learning that people continue without you — distance *is* the material). **Marta stays away** (the wrong was flight; the climax must be Dismas turning around *unforced*). The principle underneath, for the Constitution's exception clause: *the question is never "may the past arrive?" — it is "whose act is the climax?" If arrival would replace the companion's volition, keep them remote. If absence would replace the wrong's reality, let them come.*

What Marta gets instead: the Petra treatment, with a twist specific to Dismas — see D2.

## S2. Ashira is cleared at outline density — the audit status is masking a debt

Compare the cleared pairs. Elara's and Bernard's quest files run 900–2,400 words with named tracked casts (six Seven Mounds workers with jobs and voices; seven children of whom three own beats). Ashira's run 400–800 words, and her tracked people are anonymous: [[A Useful Terror]] tracks "eight captives individually" and names zero; "the captives' families" refuse the face-use as a category; [[When the Screen Comes Down]]'s "affected debtors" have no names. The one exception proves the standard she should be held to — Heda Morn, who gets a name, a grief, and the best victim-instruction in the roster: "Prove what they did. Leave my sister out of your mouth."

The `emotional_rewrite_status: cleared` flag is true of her *premises* — which are excellent — and false of her *density*. The Elara–Ashira paired pass visibly spent its budget on Elara. Recommendation: an explicit density pass for CMP-04 (named casts, scene business, tracked-state texture at the Elara/Bernard standard), scheduled independently of the pending pairs so it doesn't fall between passes forever.

## S3. The anonymous-victim pattern, worst case

Both companions harmed *categories*. Dismas's coach carried "civilians" — unnamed, uncounted ("the full human cost was never established"). Ashira's crime has the roster's most specific victim class — families in a raider camp who saw their dead kin's faces walk, night after night — and **not one of those people exists anywhere in canon**: no name, no letter, no testimony, not even a summarized voice. Her remote-claim anchor is an institution ("Qesheth human-roster judgement"), making her the only companion whose wronged parties lack even paper representation. Petra got an enum; Ashira's victims got a filing system.

Both need the local-face fix established in the earlier reviews — and in both cases the vault has *already promised it and not delivered*:

- Ashira's bio, Seasonal Movement, autumn: "confronts the first living owner of a face she retained." **No quest stages this.** Build the person: kin of one of the dead raiders — a widow or adult child who was *in the camp* during the terror, later displaced to Grenzburg. She recognizes a face Ashira's projector-thief circulated in [[The Faces She Kept]] — her husband's, her father's. She is not a claimant and files nothing. She simply exists, knows, and once says so. (The raiders were killers; their families were not. That asymmetry is the whole moral engine of Ashira's crime, and it currently has no body.)
- Dismas's banter file *already contains his version as a fully-written scene the quests never stage*: "Dismas returns to a family whose flight drew retaliation and gives them the names, supplies, and testimony needed to move safely... He accepts whatever task they assign without bargaining for forgiveness." That is a quest premise sitting in a calibration note. Build the family locally: they took one of his Grenzburg escape routes in autumn, the reprisal lands in winter, and the apology scene becomes playable exactly as drafted.

## S4. The banter files are the two best in the vault — and the gap to the quests is at its widest

"I counted because counting was easier than going back." is the best confession line in the project. "Then it isn't an escape. You sold them the first mile." is a complete ethics in eleven words. "I was accurate. I was not entitled." / "Do not enlarge yourself with what she said while dying." / "You are not required to improve this." — Ashira's calibration set is the strongest of the eight. The missing-queen card deck (denied theft proven because the queen turned up as a route marker) is the best recurring object in the roster, and Ashira's cherries-taken-only-when-you-look-away is the best micro-tell.

The pattern from all three reviews holds at its most extreme: the closer a file sits to a *person in a room*, the better this vault writes. The paired passes should treat both banter files as the quality floor, and in Dismas's case as a literal shot list — at least three of his calibration scenes (the apology, the reprisal anger, the second night) are unbuilt quests.

## S5. Credit where due, and small bugs

- **Ashira's campaign is the least administration-poisoned of the six reviewed.** Her climaxes are decisions about faces, custody, and consent — not hearings and inventories. [[No Human Shadow]]'s destroy/divide/weaponize/lose fork is the cleanest finale decision architecture in the roster, and Bellandi's role is correctly subordinated ("That account cannot determine which choice was right"). Whatever the density pass adds, protect this.
- Dismas's four outlines share verbatim boilerplate ("Required understanding never rests on a single social check..." ×4; "Completion grants a companion technique or field synergy..." ×4) — expected at outline stage, noted so the rebuild doesn't inherit it.
- **Name collisions, third and fourth instances:** Heda Morn (Ashira's grieving witness) vs. Heda Rusk (Ilyana's bandit leader); Marta Etten (Dismas's survivor) vs. Marta Vell (Ilyana's customs reader). With Mika/Nika this is now a pattern — the vault needs a name registry check before implementation.

# Part II — Dismas Raben (CMP-06)

## What is genuinely working

The bio voice is the best of the eight — "revolutionaries who cannot cook," "a horse that dislikes uniforms," "Captain Polished-Buckle has discovered weather," embarrassment that "makes him inspect a window." The wrong is well-chosen and rigorously un-romanticized ("He was not a folk hero unjustly accused... He still chose armed robbery"). And its sharpest feature is deliberately preserved: **he never went back, so he does not know who died.** "He heard people alive. Soldiers approached. He left." The not-knowing *is* the haunting — the Constitution even guards it ("Dismas's haunting is memory and trauma, not a supernatural entity"). The foil arc with Ilyana ("They must return together for the boring part") is the best-matched pairing in the ensemble: two connoisseurs of leaving, each assigning the other the maintenance.

## D1. The rebuilt introduction: pleasure before the wound (endorse the audit, with one salvage)

The current intro ([[The Sound of Falling Stone]]) opens on the trauma trigger and drives to a confession — the audit rightly replaces it with "a thrilling act of justified rule-breaking that makes his company desirable." Endorse: the player must *enjoy being his accomplice* before the rockfall means anything (this is the same enjoy-them-first sequencing that worked for Elara and Bernard). But salvage the whistle-and-collapse freeze and the counting tell — "Three voices. Two at the front. One pretending the wheel wasn't frightening him." — as the Act I finale's centre rather than the campaign's opening move. A player who has spent five hours with the charming man who always knows the exit, then watches him freeze at a quarry slide and start *counting voices*, learns the backstory through his body before anyone says a word.

## D2. The Act II finale inverted: he opens the case

With Marta's arrival cut (S1), [[The Name on the Charge]] should invert its transitive verb: **Dismas files the charge.** The quest becomes his voluntary commissioning of the truth — engaging Bellandi to trace the coach's passengers through Korrheim records, funding the search from his own service credit, against his every instinct and with the player watching him almost stop three times. The discovery has teeth on both branches: if passengers died, the count he never made is finally made, with names. And Marta — alive, one province away, having rebuilt — presents the finale's real question in the Petra pattern: the record can be *offered* to her, and she may read it, refuse it, or take material repair without contact. The cruelest available truth: she may not have known a person caused the fall at all. His confession would hand her a wound to relieve his own — which makes the last decision *whose need the truth serves*, converging beautifully with Ashira's theme (truth may be offered, guarded, or withheld without becoming less true) and giving the foil-adjacent pair one honest late conversation.

Marta's paper needs a voice regardless: her original claim filed years ago against "persons unknown," in her diction, is the campaign's Petra letter.

## D3. Containment is never staged — and the Yard should be the trap

The Rebel reference: his deep fear is containment — "if the jock fears weakness, the rebel fears ownership. Intimacy threatens sovereignty." The campaign has the *legal* containment (Medori owns his service) as inert background and never once stages the fear. Three missing scenes, in ascending order of cruelty:

1. **Koss exercises the contract.** One authored beat where his debt is used as a leash in front of the company — ordered, by right, publicly. His response (comply, slip it, or make the compliance cost Koss) is a direction beat and the campaign's only look at what "purchased service" feels like from inside.
2. **The Yard gives him a room.** Brina assigns him a permanent bunk, a roster title, a name painted on a board — kindness as capture. His embarrassment list already contains the tell ("being unable to leave because somebody trusts him"). Play it: he re-routes his bedroll for a week and cannot explain why.
3. **Someone ordinary is right.** His constituency note is the best unused sentence in his file: "Some want reliable law more than another clever smuggler." Stage the carter who wants the toll *and* the magistrate — who experienced Dismas's kind of freedom as the thing that got her cousin's wagon burned — and let her win the argument. The Rebel shadow ("contempt for ordinary stable life") needs one scene where stable life talks back and is correct.

## D4. The Act III problem the reserved slot must solve: what remains when the enemy falls?

His sworn-bond note names the deepest Rebel question in the file: the bond "requires a positive shared object or cause that remains after the authority they opposed has fallen." That is not just the bond's problem — it is *his*. A man whose worth is refusal faces annihilation not in defeat but in victory: the siege ends, the checkpoints come down, and there is nothing left to defy. The free guide network is his positive object, so the Act III operation should be **the network's first boring week** — post-siege spring, no villain, no pursuit: rosters, fee disputes, a route that needs re-marking, two guides quarrelling over territory, and a founder who is discovering that peace is a container too. His temptations are leaving and *manufacturing danger* — the retired outlaw's itch. (Convergent-design note: this is the third "institution must run without its founder" piece after Bernard's drill and Ilyana's ensemble — vary the texture. Bernard's crisis is indispensability, Ilyana's is authorship; Dismas's is that nothing is *wrong*, and he cannot bear it.)

Fiction anchors for the whole arc: The Outlaw Josey Wales (the unownable man who accretes a family and chooses the farm — the resolved shape), Mal Reynolds (the rebel whose war is lost and whose ship *is* the positive object), Hell or High Water (righteous robbery priced honestly), Kaz Brekker (their own reference list, for the private-code register).

## D5. Vocation note

Narrative input to the pending reassessment, whatever the mechanical answer: his identity should not read as *hunter*. Ylva hunts. Dismas is the escaped quarry who learned the hunter's habits from the wrong side — prey-minded, exit-first, trap-laying as counter-pursuit. If Hunter is retained for roster reasons, name the variant from that inversion rather than from "ambusher," which points his fantasy at the rockfall's worst reading.

## D6. Archetype audit

Rebel core is exact, and the file already refuses the two standard failures (the bio explicitly rejects both folk-hero innocence and domestication endings — "a bad rebel is a narcissist with better lighting" has been successfully avoided). The blend matrix's **Rebel → Goofball, "the Lovable Rogue"** — "defiance made charming; seriousness versus evasion; charming nihilism" — is already his observed texture: the card tricks, the nicknames, breakfast smuggled onto a forbidden roof, charm "as the first stage of departure." Adopt it formally; its named conflict (seriousness versus evasion) gives scene writers his precise failure surface — the joke deployed at the moment gravity was owed — and "charming nihilism" names his broken outcome better than "renewed fugitive" does.

## Priority prescriptions for Dismas

1. Rule Marta remote; invert the Act II finale into the case he opens himself; write her claim-paper voice.
2. Rebuild the intro as accomplice-pleasure; move the counting-freeze to the Act I finale.
3. Build the reprisal family locally (his banter apology scene, staged) — his Sandro/Lenka.
4. Stage containment three ways: Koss's leash, the Yard's kind trap, the carter who is right.
5. Act III operation = the network's first boring week; the enemy-shaped hole as final antagonist.
6. Preserve the not-knowing until *he* chooses to end it; the count of voices is the campaign's spine.
7. Adopt Lovable Rogue; keep the missing queen everywhere; fix the Marta/Heda name collisions.

# Part III — Ashira bath Melan (CMP-04)

## What is genuinely working

More than any other companion. The crime is the roster's most original — psychological warfare by likeness against a camp's noncombatant families, with a real-world analogue the campaign fully earns (Operation Wandering Soul, the Vietnam "ghost tape" broadcasts of faked dead soldiers' voices — her crime is that, with faces). The ontology/ethics split is stated with unusual discipline: "The roster contains no human souls. That fact matters and does not settle the moral question." The theme — likeness, consent, and who owns a face — runs coherently through all eleven pieces and lands on the roster's best finale fork (destroy / divide / weaponize / lose). [[Borrowed Faces]] is the best Act III operation concept of the eight: consent propagating through an institution, with the scout who "revokes consent after seeing her face continue without her" as its thesis-in-one-image — the campaign's whole argument, miniaturized, in a stranger. [[What the Ruins Repeated]] (survivor, mechanism, or residue loop — "treating all three as either hope or death will kill somebody") is grief-triage as gameplay and the best operation premise in the vault. "That is not a haunting. It may still be your fault." is the best line in the project. And the coda beat — "It died." / "It was never alive." / "Still dropped the spoon." / "This is why belonging is dangerous." — is the only summer coda of the six reviewed that lands its whole campaign in four lines.

The critique of Ashira is therefore almost entirely: **the flesh is missing, and the three people who would hurt most are absent.**

## A1. Her victims have no faces — in the campaign about faces

See S3. The irony should sting: the companion whose crime is unauthorized use of human likeness has victims who exist only as a category. The fix is the bio's own unkept autumn promise — "the first living owner of a face she retained" — built as a person (the raider's widow or adult child, in Grenzburg, displaced; recognizes a circulated face from The Faces She Kept; never files anything; once, quietly, tells Ashira what the nights in the camp were like). Ashira's whole arc turns on the sentence "destroying it felt, to her, like agreeing that frightened people had understood it better than she did" — so put one of the frightened people in the room, and let her have understood it *perfectly*. Not eloquent (the chorus rule from the first review holds; Ashira's cast must not seminar), just accurate: what it does to a child to see her father walk again, wrongly.

## A2. Her want is never exposed at cost

Her frontmatter humiliation: "exposure as someone who wants ordinary tenderness." The banter stages micro-versions perfectly (the cherries taken only when the player looks away; "Do not make me repeat it"), but every exposure is private, controlled, retractable — the campaign never once *inspects her* the way she inspects everyone. The Goth reference has the line: "She wants to be genuinely seen but resents being inspected." Nobody ever inspects her. Missing scenes:

- **The want, witnessed publicly.** One beat where her need for belonging shows in front of the *room*, uncontrolled — she keeps a debtor's supper seat for someone; she is visibly, catastrophically pleased to be included in something ordinary; the room notices; she cannot take it back. Two minutes. No dialogue lesson.
- **An agent of assimilation.** Her listed humiliation "sentimental correction" has no character attached — nobody in Grenzburg ever tries to *fix* her. Give the pressure a face: a well-meaning figure (Church steward, kindly debtor, one of Elara's orbit) who keeps trying to brighten, soften, and translate her — kindness as erasure. Her fear currently has no hands.

## A3. The temptation must actually tempt — mechanically

"It would work." is the right line and the only load it currently bears. In [[A Useful Terror]], the prepared nonhuman decoy substitutes for the human face at near-parity, so the ethical route costs nothing and the temptation is decorative. Same at Bone-Reed: "Weaponize... is tactically strongest now" is *asserted*. Apply the both-and lesson from the Elara spec: make the human face **visibly, materially superior** in play — the fight the player actually has without it should be longer, uglier, and cost something the docket shows (an injured captive, a lost branch, a worse route) — with the do-not-regress flag the other campaigns' exceptions carry. A refusal that costs nothing teaches nothing; the player should *feel* why she kept the roster.

## A4. The roster needs presence — her Things Not Mine

Bernard's compulsion lives in ordinary play (visible tells, a pouch, incidents). Ashira's retention — the same class of standing sin — is inert between quests: nobody sees her *keep* it. Give it a body: the maintenance ritual (relabelling, testing a branch alone at night in the screened corner, the case turned so the labels face the wall when someone enters), one readable tell when she is tempted to consult it, and one authored incident mid-campaign where the player catches her studying a face and she explains the category until the person inside it disappears — her own documented shame-tell, finally pointed at her crime. The banter file's "The scratching began after they sealed it" register proves she can carry uncanny self-disclosure without breaking voice.

## A5. The density pass (S2), itemized

- Name the eight Causeway captives; give three of them beats (the Seven Mounds standard). Their families' refusal of the face-use is currently a stated position — make it one named ferryman's, for named reasons, in his diction.
- Name two of the copied debtors in [[When the Screen Comes Down]]; cross-cast where possible (Hannel and Neda already exist and are loved — a decoy wearing *Neda's* face converts a mechanic into a violation the player feels).
- Give [[The Faces She Kept]]'s Pavel his ending — he currently decides "whether his name enters the report" and vanishes; he is her first believer and should recur (the Yard's uncanny-report clientele the bio promises her is Pavel's natural home).
- The Garden Under Rain and What We Keep Closed are the right shapes (the Rain Vault is the only relationship location in the roster with its own aesthetic logic; the NOT EVIDENCE puppet leg is perfect) — they need only the same density treatment, not redesign.

## A6. Archetype audit

Goth core is the cleanest fit in the roster — desire (truth outside approval), fear (assimilation), shadow (romanticized damage, gatekeeping) all check, and the file already dodges the reference's named failure modes (no funeral poetry, no perky-goth flattening, no secret softness that invalidates severity). The blend matrix's **Goth → Cheerleader, "the Midnight Socialite"** — "outsider identity with selective social fluency; difference versus recognition; cultivated elitism" — is her formalization: "secrets used to establish rank" is already in her bio's shadow list, and *selective* social fluency names exactly how she operates the Yard (the person debtors approach "when they need someone to believe an uncanny event without spreading it by supper" — that is a social network, curated). Adopting it also completes a quiet symmetry with her foil: Elara is Cheerleader→Tomboy, Ashira Goth→Cheerleader — each woman carries the other's primary as a secondary, which is *why* "each recognizes that the other's social intelligence is real." The foil arc has been running on that engine without naming it.

## Priority fixes for Ashira

1. Build the first living owner of a face (the bio's unkept promise) — her local, unresolvable person.
2. Run the density pass: named captives, named debtors, cross-cast decoy faces, Pavel's continuation.
3. Make the face mechanically tempting; flag the imbalance as intentional.
4. Give the roster presence between quests — tells, rituals, one caught-studying incident.
5. Stage the want witnessed publicly, and give assimilation-pressure an agent.
6. Adopt Midnight Socialite; fix the Heda collision.
7. Protect, verbatim: the finale fork, the coda's four lines, Heda Morn's instruction, the banter file entire.

# Part IV — The shortest path

If only five things get done across both characters:

1. **Rule Marta remote and invert Dismas's Act II finale** — the case he opens is the campaign; everything else in his rebuild follows from that verb.
2. **Build the two local faces** — the raider's kin for Ashira, the reprisal family for Dismas. Both are already promised (bio line; banter scene) and both campaigns' crimes stay abstract until they exist.
3. **Run Ashira's density pass** — she is the only companion whose problem is neither conception nor structure but simple flesh; highest quality-per-hour in the roster.
4. **Stage each unstaged fear** — Dismas's containment (the leash, the kind trap, the carter who is right), Ashira's inspection (the want witnessed, the corrector with a face).
5. **Mine the banter files as shot lists** — at least three of Dismas's calibration scenes and two of Ashira's are finished quest beats waiting for quests.

Of the eight, these two need the least invention and the most construction. The voices are done. The wrongs are the best in the roster. What's missing is rooms for them to happen in.
