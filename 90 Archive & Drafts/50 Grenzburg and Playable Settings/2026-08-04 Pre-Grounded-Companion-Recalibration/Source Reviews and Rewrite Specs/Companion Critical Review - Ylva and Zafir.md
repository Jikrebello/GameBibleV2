# Companion Critical Review — Ylva and Zafir

An adversarial read of CMP-03 and CMP-08, same standards as the previous three reviews. Quests treated as replaceable; character shapes held fixed. This completes coverage of all eight companions.

**The one-sentence verdict:** these are the two *healthiest* campaigns in the roster — Zafir's is the best-designed of the cleared four and Ylva's contains the best-handled death option and the best shame scene in the vault — so this review is less about repair than about two specific diseases: **Ylva's campaign teaches one lesson seven times, and Zafir's world is missing the people his instruments held.**

---

# Part I — Systemic Findings

## S1. The maturity gradient is now visible across the roster

With all eight read, the pattern is clean: migration debt and template density map directly to authorship order. Dismas (oldest) is boilerplate outlines with an arriving survivor; Tavio and Ilyana carry pending arrivals and the "campaign tests four truths" template; Ylva carries the template but no arrivals; Zafir and Ashira (newest-cleared) are fully remote-compliant with the template gone. Two consequences: first, the earlier reviews' arrival rulings affect only the older half — nothing here needs one, and both files honor the standard completely ("No person from the convoy appears in Grenzburg"; "Samira never enters the base-game worldspace"). Second, **Zafir's campaign should be treated as the roster's reference implementation** — Karel Reed-Hand is the present-tense local countervoice the Ilyana review had to prescribe from scratch, already built, with written exit terms; the Master Ring is the mechanically *real* temptation the Ashira review had to demand; and The Copyist's Answer converts a moral boundary into a technical gameplay constraint (derive severance *without using her unpublished work*), which is the single best theme-as-mechanics move in the project. When the paired passes rebuild the older campaigns, this is the model.

## S2. The anonymity disease persists in both

Zafir's nine chainworkers are tracked "individually" — as stations one through nine, no names. Ylva's evacuees are headcounts: "nineteen civilians; six wounded soldiers and laborers," "eleven civilians; seven wounded or exhausted defenders." Both finales move crowds. The Elara/Bernard standard (six named Seven Mounds workers with jobs and voices; three children owning beats among seven) applies: two or three named people per finale, minimum, with one beat each. A rescue the player can recount by name is a rescue; a rescue of nineteen is logistics.

## S3. The paternalism twins never talk

Set the two wrongs side by side: Ylva signed the whole surety so the crew "would not be drawn into litigation" — she protected them out of a choice they had a right to make. Zafir wrote humane clauses onto ownership — he protected the owned out of the harm while keeping them owned. **Both companions harmed people by protecting them.** That symmetry is sitting unexploited: their only banter object is "field evidence defeating a beautiful model," and the one place they touch thematically is buried in an autonomy table ("Zafir may formalize shared liability" for her Act III). Build the scene: the two reformed paternalists co-drafting the shared-liability instrument for her route teams — Zafir producing three drafts of exit clauses, Ylva striking every clause that speaks *for* the signers, each catching the other doing the old thing. It is the cheapest high-value ensemble scene the roster has left unwritten.

## S4. Banter-versus-quest gap, and the codas

The pattern holds a fourth time, gentler here: both banter files outclass both campaigns. Ylva's shame calibration — "Seven minutes to shelter." / "How long have you known?" / **"Eleven."** — with its design note ("the shame lies in the four minutes she stole from the team's decision") is the best shame scene in the vault. Zafir's "Excellent. It has objected." and the Brina evacuation-model scene ("They do not." — then clearing the centre of the board) are his campaign's thesis done in four lines and one gesture; the quests should quote that scene's method, not just its conclusion.

Small lattice items: **three companions now have "The Road…" codas** (The Road She Chooses; The Road Takes the Song; The Road Takes the Measure) — rename at least one alongside the Bernard-side coda fix. "Justified combat/resistance" boilerplate appears again (A Signal Shared). Ylva's campaign doc carries the same "campaign tests four truths" block flagged in the Tavio/Ilyana review. And one meta-note worth a smile: Ylva's preparation ladders are the most logistics-dense in the roster — fuel, rosters, soundings — meaning **the readiness system is her flaw externalized**; a campaign about a woman who over-prepares alone is itself a preparation checklist. Use it or at least wink at it (Brina, reviewing the player's five assets: "You are doing the thing she does").

# Part II — Ylva Winter-Track (CMP-03)

## What is genuinely working

The wrong is the most elegant in the roster: the rescue was *right*, and the sin is one signature — "She protected the crew from a choice they had a right to make." A flaw manufactured entirely out of virtue, with no crime-scene grimness to manage. [[The White-Sled Rescue]] is the tightest backstory file of the eight. The Honest Distress Call is a strong duo centerpiece; [[The Last Marker]]'s sacrifice-gating (only after alternatives visibly fail, explicit confirmation, "heroic but unresolved" — never counted as her best ending) is the roster's most disciplined handling of a companion death. [[No Quarry Today]] is the best friendship episode of the eight — "Nothing has to die for us to know who read the ground," and the beat where she divides abundant food into emergency portions on a homeward walk is exactly the right scale of symptom. A Hearth Chosen's confession — "she does not know how to ask for private time without making it useful" — is the campaign in one line. The banter file is superb throughout ("Your hands said yes."; "Name the injury. Weather later."; "That was not my decision alone. I made it alone.").

## Y1. The campaign is one lesson taught seven times

Walk the eleven pieces: the intro is four watches she shouldn't hold alone; A1-O is a relay that must work without her; the A1 finale is the honest distress call; A2-O is training independent station holders; the A2 finale is distributed liability and informed teams; A3-O is an atlas that must not have a single carrier; the A3 finale is accepting relief at the last marker. **Every single piece is "Ylva must share the load," varied only in scenery.** Her own banter failure-mode list prohibits exactly this — "makes every scene another lesson about shared burden" — and the campaign commits the violation at architecture level. Bernard's five reserve-declarations got flagged; Ylva runs seven delegation-beats. The audit's reframes ask for "more variety" without naming the monotony's cause: the *same facet* of the wound fires every time.

The unused facets are already in her bible, loaded and safe:

- **Contempt.** "Quietly despises people who ask for help sooner than she would" — never staged. She is never once ugly on screen about someone else's early surrender; her dark judgment of "weakness" is the shadow's actual teeth, and the campaign files them off. One scene: a debtor taps out of a route she'd have finished bleeding, she says something quiet and unforgivable, and the player sees the doctrine under the competence. (This is her Box Hill — every companion review has needed one.)
- **Paternalism inverted.** "Assumes a less experienced person has no right to choose risk" — the bio demands "a person whose different competence Ylva initially underrates," and the audit's A Signal Shared reframe gestures at it. Sharpen to a *person*, not a station: someone slower, chattier, differently skilled, whose informed choice to take a risk Ylva blocks — making her, for one scene, the patronizing officer from her own anger calibration.
- **Being wrong.** In every quest her field read is correct; the flaw lives only in self-allocation. A scout who is infallible except about herself is half a character. Once — once — her route call should be wrong and a local's read right, with material cost. Her Zafir banter object is literally "the right of field evidence to defeat theory"; turn it on her.

## Y2. Three loaded guns, never fired

Her file plants tells the quests never touch:

1. **The carved spoon** — the one non-utilitarian object she owns, defended "unreasonably" if called pretty. It appears once, in the bio, and never again. This is her prayer-rope, her missing queen — and it has no scene, no origin, no payoff. One quiet campfire beat (who carved it; why she keeps a beautiful useless thing; what she does when someone notices it kindly) is her "Without the Powder."
2. **"Not knowing a domestic skill someone assumes all women know"** — gendered texture the Tomboy reference centres (the compulsory-legibility problem) and the campaign, in its care to avoid the makeover cliché, avoids entirely. The reference's advice is not to skip the dress scene but to *understand* it. Her version needn't be a dress: one scene where the community demands she be legible as the approved kind of woman — a Grenzburg matron handing her mending at a shelter, assuming; the room waiting — and her response is neither shame nor speech but exact, visible discomfort, would honor the type without the cliché.
3. **Grey-Wolf women denied the war-scout office** — currently one conversation in The Sister Watch. This is the *engine* of her professionalization ("dangerous competence recognized as a profession rather than tolerated as household necessity") and deserves one more surfacing — ideally colliding with the Grenzburg institutions that keep proving her point (Koss citing her surety to assign extra watches is the Grey-Wolf restriction wearing a Medori seal; nobody ever says so).

## Y3. The local face: the man she saved from his own conscience

Full remote compliance has left the White-Sled crew as pure backstory. Her Sandro/Lenka, wound-specific: a convoy survivor or driver **in Grenzburg** — it was a Medori convoy; the quays are full of Medori freight people — who learned afterward what she signed. His grievance is the campaign's thesis from the victim's side: **not harm — exclusion.** He tried to co-sign; the papers were already closed. "You decided I couldn't afford my own conscience." He doesn't want restitution and isn't angry in any usable way; he simply will not let her carry his gratitude, which is the one weight she wants. Place him at the Ledger Quays; one fixed exchange; state never moves; in the resolved ending he signs the route board as one name among several, next to hers, and that is the entire payoff.

## Y4. Archetype audit

Tomboy core is exact, and the file already dodges the reference's named trap ("treats femininity as incompetence she escaped" is on her failure-mode list — the not-like-other-girls problem, pre-blocked). The blend matrix answer is **Tomboy → Cheerleader, "the Field Captain"** — "action combined with social command; directness versus diplomacy; **controlling protector**." That last term is her file's own sentence ("her care becomes controlling when she withholds information so others cannot choose to share danger") arriving as a named formation. Adopt it: it explains why her campaign is full of boards, rosters, and relays (command *is* social), gives writers her stress behaviour, and completes the foil symmetry — Tavio is Jock→Goofball, Ylva Tomboy→Cheerleader, each carrying a social term in the secondary slot, which is why their foil runs on public credit and halts. Fiction anchors: Brienne of Tarth (competence plus deflected tenderness; "protected into powerlessness" as the fear), Furiosa (the rescue-scout fantasy done wordlessly), and — for the delegation drama done with variety — Winters in Band of Brothers, whose promotions keep relieving him of the line he loves: relief experienced as loss, which is exactly her endgame feeling and nowhere in her files.

## Priority fixes for Ylva

1. Break the monotony: reassign at least three of the seven delegation-beats to unused facets — contempt, inverted paternalism, being wrong.
2. Fire the three loaded guns: the spoon scene, the legibility scene, the Grey-Wolf/Medori rhyme.
3. Build the excluded co-signer at the Quays.
4. Name two or three evacuees per finale (S2).
5. Stage her contempt once — her Box Hill — and let repair be harder than the distress call.
6. Adopt Field Captain; rename her coda out of the "The Road…" triplet; strip the template blocks in the paired pass.

# Part III — Zafir ibn Samad (CMP-08)

## What is genuinely working

The most, of any companion. [[The Copyist's Answer]] is the best act finale in the roster: Samira's four fixed terms ("no greeting and no counter-script") make refusal a *load-bearing structure*, Bellandi's "She has declined to work for you." is the perfect flat correction to "She has omitted the construction.", and the severance puzzle — free yourself without using her unpublished work — is theme as engineering constraint. The Ninth Lever's "It is functioning exactly as designed." / **"Then design faster."** is the sharpest two-line indictment in the vault. The Master Ring (HOLD POSITION UNTIL RELEASED BY MASTER) is a temptation with real mechanical teeth — the clean extraction is genuinely cleaner — which is what the Ashira review had to ask for and this campaign already does. Karel Reed-Hand, with written exit terms and the standing rule that he "is never the reward for Zafir behaving well," is the model local countervoice. And the legal register that poisons other campaigns is *earned* here: Zafir is a jurist; custody, severance, and release are his native tongue and his prison — the one campaign where compliance vocabulary is diegetic. The banter file is excellent ("You may amend the rule. You may not call my loss retroactive consensus."; the label `Rope's Objection`; the Brina scene).

## Z1. Samira's void is right — but it needs texture around its edges

Her blankness is correct design: unlike Petra, whose enum needed a letter, Samira's refusal-to-be-present *is* the characterization — she declines to exist even as prose, and the campaign honors it ("She does not return, reconcile, or finish Zafir's work"). Do not write her a letter. But her file is four sentences of function, and the risk is that she reads as a design pattern (The Refusal) rather than a person refusing. The fix is texture she cannot control:

- **The court record.** She testified at the Iqashari judgement — that's public, and Zafir would have read it a hundred times. Quote three lines of her testimony in [[The Inverted Surety]] or the Copyist's Answer staging: her diction under oath, dry, exact, entirely about the instruments and never about him. Personhood through the transcript she couldn't prevent existing.
- **The inversion as portrait.** "She found the dependent loop. I published three corrections and never saw it." is the right move — her craft as her voice, readable only by him. One more beat of this: a scene where Zafir catches himself *annotating her inversion with professional admiration* — studying her without leave, the old act in miniature — and stops. His retention-equivalent, made visible once.

## Z2. The missing people: everyone his instruments actually held

Samira freed herself. The *others* — debtors who served full terms under Zafir's humane restraints, petitioned through his clauses, were fed per his schedules, and were released on his timetables — appear nowhere: not a name, not a record, not a face. His local person exists, and the wound-specific version is the roster's most disturbing: **someone who served under a Zafir-designed instrument and is grateful.** A freed debtor now working Grenzburg who recognizes the notation style, seeks him out, and thanks him — the buckles didn't cut, the petitions were answered, the term ended on the day the clause said. She means it. And her gratitude should horrify him more than hatred would, because a person thanking you for a better cage is the cage-world testifying that your work made it livable — "reduced harm mattered to those forced to live inside the system" given a face who *agrees*, which is worse. She is not angry, not a claimant, not a lesson; she is a satisfied customer, and every conversation with her is quietly unbearable. No other companion's local face can invert the pattern this way; it is the Nerd-arc version of the differential-grace scene.

## Z3. He is never wrong in public with a body count

His humiliation list leads with "being publicly wrong after sounding certain," and the campaign stages only the comic version (the knot; the collapsing bridge — both delightful, both costless). Helplessness is well-staged (the execution-end arresting him while others act is used twice, correctly). But no authored beat ever makes his *confident error* cost someone else something — a model asserted publicly, followed, and wrong, with a named person paying. The zero-participation route gestures at it ("Bernard may catch a humiliating public failure in Act I") — it exists as a state and not a scene, the recurring vault disease. One scene, mid-campaign: his load map or route model fails in front of the Yard, a worker is hurt *because they trusted his certainty*, and the recovery he must perform is not technical correction but standing in the room as the man who was wrong. The campaign tests whether he shares power thoroughly; it never tests whether he survives being incorrect, and for a Nerd arc that is the floor under everything else.

## Z4. Karel needs one want that isn't curriculum

Karel is superbly protected by design law — exit terms, no proxy-reform, no reward-status. The protection has a side effect: he is close to a rights-bearing abstraction. His texture (charcoal floor-plans, revision instinct, the shouted route from the gallery) is good; what's missing is one appetite that has nothing to do with Zafir or learning — a boat he's saving for, a Chapel-Folk standing he wants, a rival his own age, the wish to beat Zafir at exactly one thing and retire the trophy. One selfish, ordinary want converts him from a perfectly designed apprentice into a person who happens to be apprenticed.

## Z5. Archetype audit

Nerd core is exact — legibility as desire, ridicule as fear, "confusing intelligence with wisdom" as the shadow his bio restates precisely ("confidence that humane administration could transform ownership into justice" is the smartest-guy-in-the-room failure mode in period costume). The blend matrix answer is **Nerd → Goofball, "the Eccentric Genius"** — "expertise through play and absurdity; precision versus spontaneity; irresponsible cleverness." The bird that lifts latches for no door, the folding bridge, "Excellent. It has objected." — the secondary is already in the text, unlabelled. Adopting it also names the pleasing inversion with his foil: Bernard is Goofball-primary with hidden weight (Jock secondary, per his spec); Zafir is Nerd-primary with hidden play. The two men are each other's blend reversed, which is *why* the Label Board scenes work — formalize it in the Chemistry Matrix. Fiction anchors: The Remains of the Day (dignified service to an indefensible arrangement, the retrospective horror of having done it *well*) and, above all, **Never Let Me Go** — Hailsham is Zafir's instruments as an institution: the kinder cage that made the system survivable and therefore durable. His grateful freed debtor (Z2) is a Hailsham alumna. That is the comparison that should sit in the campaign doc's margin.

## Priority fixes for Zafir

1. Build the grateful freed debtor — his local face, the inverted-valence version.
2. Add Samira's court-transcript texture and the caught-annotating beat; write her no letter.
3. Stage one public confident error with a named cost.
4. Give Karel one non-curricular want.
5. Name the nine chainworkers (S2); write the Ylva co-drafting scene (S3).
6. Adopt Eccentric Genius; register the Bernard blend-inversion in the Chemistry Matrix.

# Part IV — The shortest path

If only five things get done across both characters:

1. **Vary Ylva's lesson** — reassign three of the seven delegation-beats to contempt, inverted paternalism, and being wrong. This is the single largest quality delta available in her campaign.
2. **Build the two local faces** — the excluded co-signer at the Quays; the grateful freed debtor. Both wounds stay theoretical until someone in Grenzburg carries them.
3. **Fire Ylva's loaded guns** — the spoon, the legibility scene, the Grey-Wolf/Medori rhyme. All three are already planted in her bible.
4. **Give Zafir one costly public error** and Samira her transcript edges.
5. **Write the paternalism-twins scene** (Ylva and Zafir drafting the shared-liability instrument) and adopt the two blends — Field Captain and Eccentric Genius — which complete the roster's blend symmetry: every foil pair now carries a mirrored secondary.

With this, all eight are reviewed. The roster-wide picture for the rewrite phase: Elara and Bernard need **rework** (specs written), Tavio and Ilyana need **rulings then rebuilds**, Dismas needs **construction**, Ashira needs **flesh**, and Ylva and Zafir need **variety and faces**. The vault's constitutions are strong, its banter files are its proof of voice, and its recurring disease — everywhere, in every register — is that the campaigns trust systems to carry what only named people in rooms can.
