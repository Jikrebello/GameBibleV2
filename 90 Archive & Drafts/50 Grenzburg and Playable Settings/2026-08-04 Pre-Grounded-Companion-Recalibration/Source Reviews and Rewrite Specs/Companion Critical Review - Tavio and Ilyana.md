# Companion Critical Review — Tavio and Ilyana

An adversarial read of CMP-07 and CMP-02: bios, campaigns, all quest pieces, banter files, and the Lio/Salt-Reed support canon, measured against the same standards as [[Companion Critical Review - Elara and Bernard]]. The quests are treated as replaceable; only the character shapes are held fixed.

**Fairness note first:** unlike Elara and Bernard, these two are *not* cleared — both campaigns carry `pending-paired-pass`, and the [[Companion Emotional Rewrite Audit]] already flags many of the problems below with reframe/replace dispositions. This review is therefore better timed than the last one: its job is to steer the paired passes, not to blame drafts for being drafts. Where the audit's planned fix is right, I say so. Where the planned fix would trade one wound for a worse one, I argue against it.

**The one-sentence verdict:** both campaigns are sitting on the strongest raw premises in the roster — a maimed rival demanding one honest fight, and a forger who sold her own band — and both currently bury those premises under the vault's two chronic diseases (administration-as-drama, template symmetry) plus one crisis unique to this pair: **the half-finished Remote Claims migration, which will either be resolved thoughtfully in the paired passes or will quietly gut both campaigns.**

---

# Part I — Systemic Findings

## 1. The arrival crisis: these campaigns currently break the vault's own law, and the planned fix is worse than the crime

The [[Companion Constitution]] rules that "no person from a companion's former life enters the base-game worldspace." Now read the current texts:

- [[Lio Varcen]]'s file says, in consecutive sections, "Lio never enters the base-game worldspace" *and* "Lio enters Grenzburg under independent Crucible and medical protection during [[The Challenger's Road]]." Both sentences are live canon right now.
- [[Ilyana Veshkar]]'s bio: "None physically enters the base-game worldspace." Her quests: Celia is rescued from a barn in Act I, Nereo is forced-performing at Eel Bridge in the Act I finale, [[Reeds Under Ice]] "brings Mirella Corsi and Tomas Marin physically into Grenzburg," and [[The Free Refrain]] opens with "All four former Salt-Reeds are present." A state literally called `salt_reed_roster_in_grenzburg` confirms "all four former members are alive and physically present by winter."

So the current drafts import the wronged parties wholesale, the standard forbids it, and the audit's migration briefs plan surgical removal: "a present-day challenger," "a current local musician," "current volunteers." Here is the problem: **the Elara/Bernard review diagnosed exactly what happens when every person with an emotional claim on the companion is exiled to the mail.** The planned substitutions manufacture that disease deliberately — at the *climaxes* of both campaigns. A "locally appointed challenger" is a stunt double for Tavio's guilt. A "current volunteer" declining a gig weighs nothing against Nereo declining the woman who forged his name.

The two campaigns deserve different answers, argued in Parts II and III: **keep Lio's arrival under a scoped exception** (his design already satisfies the standard's *purpose*), and **cut Ilyana's parade but replace it with one developed local and genuinely voiced remote artifacts** (the audit's instinct is right; its replacement is under-designed). Either way, the decision must be made *before* the paired passes, because every other fix routes through it.

## 2. Administration-as-drama, third and fourth verses

Tavio's campaign vocabulary: filed, certified, batch register, recall, endorsement chain, inspection ledger, stop authority. Ilyana's: releases, claims, custody, packets, authorship records, registration. Both Act III operations are literally paperwork-logistics quests — [[Measure on the March]] is an inspection tour (the audit's own brief: "rather than conduct another inspection ledger") and [[Every Case Its Owner]] is escort-the-documents (audit: replace). Both campaign docs even share the identical thesis template: "The campaign tests four truths," followed by four numbered abstractions.

The compliance language is not always wrong — Part III shows one place where a customs procedure could be the most devastating scene in either campaign — but as the *default* register it produces the same flattening as before: climaxes about custody, stakes about registration, and emotion asserted in frontmatter (`pleasure_beat`, `care_beat`) rather than dramatized in scenes.

## 3. The lattice is showing worse than before

- **[[Five Cases at Customs]] is a near-clone of [[Names Beneath Lime]].** Same Customs Hall opening; the companion's incriminating past arrives in crates; a low-status dock worker (Pell One-Thumb ≈ Jonas Feld) takes or takes-the-blame-for one; a crane-and-net pursuit over the quays; a hanging-rescue set piece; the companion decides how the truth enters the record. Two different companions introduced by the same quest wearing different hats is the single clearest exhibit of lattice-generation in the vault.
- **The twin codas are more flagrant this time:** "The Road Takes the Measure" / "The Road Takes the Song." There is also a title collision *inside* Ilyana's own campaign: "The Road Without a Conductor" and "Duet Without a Conductor."
- Both Act II finales unlock the player vocation; both bios carry an eight-item "must include" list; the four-step virtue gradients, "captured, killed in justified resistance, or escape," the 2-of-5 asset ladders, and the mutually-exclusive-equal-safeguard relationship bonuses all recur verbatim.

As with Elara and Bernard: keep the invisible production lattice, but the *felt shapes* must diverge. A sports tragedy and a band breakup should not have the same silhouette.

## 4. Voices: the licensing-clone problem, and two crucial people with no voice at all

- **Hella Dorn and Marta Vell are the same person** — the incorruptible procedural woman who "refuses every attempt to substitute charisma for documentation" — and she is also Bellandi, Brina, and Greta. Five gatekeeper characters, one register. Differentiate or merge.
- **Nereo's one line is seminar-voice:** "I will not play because you have decided I must." The event (refusal) is the best beat in Ilyana's campaign; the diction is the boundaries-workshop chorus again. A drummer with a broken band says something angrier, smaller, or stranger than a thesis.
- **The two most important absent people have no voice whatsoever.** Lio's file is a rights-and-states document — meticulous about what he may do, silent on what he *sounds like*; he has zero written lines. Morante — the best villain-logic in the setting — exists only as summarized behavior. Both need the Petra treatment from the Bernard spec: real prose in their own diction, quoted, never paraphrased.
- **The banter files are alive again** — Tavio's sash scene and gold turnip, Ilyana's "Because the stew has surrendered" and the unfinished phrase — and again the banter files contain self-warnings the campaign files violate. Ilyana's failure-mode list prohibits "convert[ing] every consent problem into legal vocabulary" and requires that she "never sounds like a contract lecturer merely because consent is central to her arc" — while her campaign is wall-to-wall releases and claims. The standard knows; the quests don't.

## 5. The missing-beat disease continues

Elara's campaign promised a public loss and never staged it; Bernard's promised humor failing and never staged it. Same pattern here, once per character: **Tavio's bio promises "a public stop that feels like defeat" and a crowd-facing fear ("defeat that changes how the crowd sees him") that no quest commits to** — see Part II. **Ilyana's campaign promises "a present performer whose refusal she cannot rewrite as fear," but never stages her shadow actually firing in the present** — see Part III. The bios keep writing checks the eleven pieces don't cash; the paired passes should treat each bio's promise list as a scene-coverage checklist, which is how both reviews keep catching this.

# Part II — Tavio Meran (CMP-07)

## What is genuinely working

**Yield is the best mechanics-narrative marriage in the roster.** A vocation whose signature lesson is *safely abandoning loaded power* — "mechanically useful because the story regards stopping as skilled action" — is theme embodied in a verb. Nothing in Elara's or Bernard's kits dramatizes their thesis this well; protect it and build toward it. The banter file is excellent: the sash scene ("Say coward again." — the foreman does — Tavio drapes his champion's sash on the worker — "Now say it to both of us.") is the best anger calibration in the project; "The discharge was an accident. The ring was not." is exactly right; the gold turnip with the date carved in it is a real object. [[Games Without a Crown]]'s premise — competition with no crown, villain, or interrupted climax — is the correct friendship-episode shape. Lio's *design* (own terms, own second, own exit, right to refuse, "a thrown fight angers him because it steals his agency again") is the strongest victim-with-agency construction in the vault. The sole-blame temptation — confession that protects the corrupt institution — is the most sophisticated moral idea in his campaign. And the horn-before-glory finale shape is right.

## The Lio decision: keep him

The audit's brief for [[The Fair Defeat]] plans a bout "without requiring Lio to appear," with "a locally appointed challenger." Argued position: **this would be the single most damaging change either paired pass could make.**

The bout is not a subplot; it *is* the campaign. Two men entered a crooked contest; one of them rigged it and took a body from the other; the fair rematch is the only scene in which the wrong can be faced at the scale it was committed. Combat drama runs on the specific opponent — Creed II is about the Drago rematch, not a rematch; Warrior works because the man across the cage is the brother; The Wrestler's Ayatollah rematch matters because of the shared past. A proxy champion fighting "on Lio's filed rules" turns Tavio's climax into shadowboxing with a legal instrument — the enum-Petra problem, relocated to the emotional peak of the campaign.

And here is the thing: **Lio's current design already satisfies the standard's purpose.** The Remote Claims Standard exists to prevent two failure modes — the surprise visitor from the past who arrives to complete the companion's lesson, and the victim staged as a prop for the companion's redemption. Lio is neither. He "never enters Tavio's custody," arrives "under independent Crucible and medical protection," has his own lodging, witnesses, and exit route, may refuse the bout entirely, and "his future [is] separated from Tavio's need for forgiveness." The file even guards against the prop failure explicitly: he is not "a trial staged for Tavio." The standard's letter is violated; its spirit is fulfilled better than anywhere else in the corpus.

**Recommendation:** amend the Constitution with a narrow, named exception — call it the Lio Clause: *a wronged party may enter the worldspace only under their own power, for their own aims, with authored refusal rights, independent protection, and no reconciliation requirement.* One sentence of law, applied once. Then fix the Lio file's internal contradiction (both sentences currently canon), and give him what he actually lacks — a **voice**. Not more rights language: lines. A man who does pain management every morning has a specific relationship to small talk. Write his challenge notice in his own hand; write the three sentences he says to Tavio at the weigh-in; write what he says if he *wins*. (If the ruling goes the other way and Lio stays remote, then the challenger must be a full person with their own injury and their own stake in Red Brass hardware — a Grenzburg Lancewright hurt by the endorsed batch from [[The Signed Measure]] — and the campaign must admit the bout is no longer its climax and promote the Bench exposure in its place. That is a worse campaign, but it is at least a coherent one.)

## Where it is emotionally flat

**His fear is never staged — the crowd never turns, and nobody replaces him.** The Jock reference is explicit: the governing fear is "humiliation through weakness... replacement by someone stronger or younger," and "if the body is the foundation of identity, bodily damage becomes a metaphysical crisis." Now audit the campaign: no rising challenger exists anywhere; no young fighter uses his old style; no crowd is ever shown preferring someone else. The Fair Defeat's fail-forward states — "Tavio loses: the campaign advances normally. Tavio wins: the campaign advances normally" — are dramaturgical agnosticism: the quest refuses to *want* anything from its own bout. Meanwhile a campaign about a competitor contains almost no competition: aside from the one bout, Tavio never competes on-screen. A sports story with no sport, no rivals, and no stakes in its results. The Wrestler and Friday night's Jason Street exist because the genre knows where its pain lives; this campaign has the champion, the scandal, and the comeback fight, and has carefully drained each of the thing that makes crowds hold their breath.

**He has no body.** Thirty, uninjured, at peak — for a Jock arc, that's a missing organ. The cheapest, highest-yield fix available: **the Fifth Ring marked him too.** Nerve scarring in the gauntlet hand from the discharge — a tremor at maximum load — which he has concealed for four years. Not equivalence with Lio (Lio lost a hip and part of a spine; Tavio has a lie-sized flaw in one hand), but embodiment: his bio's private contradiction — "he believes every body deserves fair limits and built his identity on being the exception" — becomes flesh. It makes the Fair Defeat physically dangerous *for him*, gives the concealment theme a present-tense object (he is hiding a weakness the way the Bench hid the ring), and hands his "vulnerability compatible with excellence" lesson a place to live.

**The cheat has no motive.** The files establish that he "knowingly used" the ring and refuse to say *why*. A champion who was already winning doesn't need a hidden insert — so was he already losing? The strongest available answer, per the archetype's replacement-fear: **he was starting to lose to Lio,** or starting to feel the decline the tremor design formalizes, and the ring was a prosthetic against it. Cheating from fear of decline is human and Jock-core; cheating from "I carried the risk" hubris is a placard. One paragraph in the bio and one confession beat late in Act II ("You were better that season. I knew it in the third exchange.") converts his crime from generic to specific — and makes the fair bout unbearable, because this time there is no ring and the question the ring answered is still open.

**Maro Vezan is inert, and the sole-blame offer arrives as paper.** A Jock's coach is his father-figure, and Maro "organized the cover-up" — yet he has no scenes, no letters, no voice. The Bench's settlement offer ("accept sole blame, preserve the branch, and receive a path back") should arrive *in Maro's hand*, warm and fatherly and monstrous — the Vardek-letter treatment: "You carried the Bench on your back for six years. Carry it once more." Pair it with the crowd dynamic the campaign never uses: **the public *wants* Tavio to be the lone bad apple,** because one villain is easier to love a sport through than a corrupt institution. His martyrdom would be *popular*. Give Hella the counter-line — that taking all the blame is one more performance, the martyr's discount — and the sole-blame temptation graduates from bullet point to the campaign's best scene.

## Where it is boring

- **[[The Signed Measure]] and [[Measure on the March]] are the same quest** — locate unsafe endorsed hardware, recall it, chase the paperwork — played in Act I and Act III. The audit knows ("rather than conduct another inspection ledger"). The Act III operation slot should hold the material the campaign is missing: the replacement figure (a young Grenzburg fighter recreating Tavio's championship style with knockoff Red Brass gear — his name still selling the thing that maimed Lio, his legend now a safety hazard with a fan club), or the public-defeat beat, or both in one piece.
- **The arena world has no present tense.** Bout bills, bookmakers, a Crucible that supposedly spans nations — none of it lives in Grenzburg's streets. One recurring fight-night location (even a modest one — Low Lantern pit nights) would give his campaign a *room* the way the Yard serves Bernard, and give the crowd — the entity he fears — a face the player recognizes by winter.
- The betting-sabotage subplot in The Fair Defeat is orphan noir: "impossible betting positions" with no named beneficiary. Either attach it to the Bench's exposure math or cut it.

## Where it doesn't make sense

1. **Red Brass's motive and jurisdiction are never quantified.** They covered up, sold his debt, and washed their hands — why do they now spend saboteurs and hired disruptors in a besieged foreign city? What does a Grenzburg proof actually *do* to a Vortharan bench — what treaty, market, or Crucible sanction makes Hella's documentation dangerous to them? One paragraph ("the Crucible's certification travels; a proven substitution voids every Red Brass warranty from here to Vorthara") prices their aggression and makes the stakes legible.
2. **Winter-siege logistics:** Lio's convoy, Crucible clerks, and Red Brass agents all cross a war zone in Deep Winter with suspicious fluency — same heroic-postal problem as Elara's sixty-three households, but with bodies. Date the arrivals before the siege closes, or spend one scene on what the crossing cost.
3. **The Lio file contradiction** (never enters / enters) is a live canon bug regardless of which ruling is made.

## Archetype audit

Jock core is correctly built and — credit where due — the bio already refuses the dumb-jock trap ("makes him unintelligent because he is athletic" is a listed failure mode). The blended reference offers the formalization: **Jock → Goofball, "the Lovable Captain."** Its secret desire is *the campaign's thesis verbatim* — "continued love after defeat or injury" — and its fear list ("losing relevance," "being known only as persona") names what the campaign should be staging. Two payloads worth adopting: the stress behaviour — "jokes more, then becomes controlling or aggressive when humour fails" — is a register Tavio currently lacks (he is never allowed to be *unpleasant* under pressure; one scene of the festival host turning drill-sergeant when a game stops going his way would do more characterization than three recalls); and the shadow list ("bullying becomes banter; objectors are weak") supplies the present-tense small sin his campaign, like Elara's, currently lacks. Differentiation from Bernard is already latent in the material — Bernard's humor makes room, Tavio's humor *recruits*; keep that line bright.

## Priority fixes for Tavio

1. Rule on Lio (recommended: the Lio Clause; keep the arrival) and fix the file contradiction.
2. Write Lio's and Maro's voices — challenge notice, weigh-in lines, the sole-blame letter.
3. Give him the tremor and the motive (losing to Lio); make the Fair Defeat cost him something physical to fight honestly.
4. Stage the fear: a replacement figure in the Act III operation slot, a crowd that turns once, and a bout whose outcome the quest actually cares about.
5. De-duplicate the recall quests; give the arena a present-tense room in Grenzburg.
6. Stage the martyr's-discount scene; price Red Brass's aggression.
7. Adopt Lovable Captain; add the humour-fails-then-controlling register.

# Part III — Ilyana Veshkar (CMP-02)

## What is genuinely working

**Her wrong is the best in the roster.** Forging four friends' signatures, one by one, to sell them into military service — intimate, documentary, premeditated, and committed by the person whose whole identity is *permission*. "Ilyana used no magical compulsion. She did not need it." is the strongest single line in either campaign doc. The forged-approvals lining in [[Five Cases at Customs]] is the best physical prop in the vault. **Refusal-as-gameplay-constraint** — [[No Song by Command]]'s engine, where the rescue must be replanned around Nereo's no — is a genuinely novel design idea; whatever happens to the cast, keep the engine. The ask-each-voice structure of [[The Free Refrain]] (individual, specific, revocable consent as mission design) is the roster's best consent mechanics. The answer-reeds bond episode — two half-instruments, neither part complete or subordinate — is the best relationship-episode conceit of the eight. The Dismas foil sketch ("they must return together for the boring part") is the best foil beat in either file. And the banter file sings: "Because the stew has surrendered," "Not a curse. Onion. A more democratic enemy," "Keep it ugly. Pretty slips on ice," and the unfinished phrase that returns scenes later.

## The troupe decision: cut the parade, but the audit's replacement is under-designed

The current campaign imports the entire cast of her backstory: Celia by land (Act I operation), Nereo at the bridge (Act I finale), Mirella and Tomas by shipwreck (Act II operation), all four assembled by winter. This *is* the serial-visitor problem the standard was written against — Grenzburg becomes a stage onto which her past conveniently files, one arrival per quest, each timed to her reckoning schedule. It also collapses the story's real subject. Distance, silence, and unanswered letters are what a broken band *is*; assembling everyone in one city for a supervised reconciliation season converts irrevocability into scheduling. The audit's replace/reframe dispositions are right.

But "current volunteers carry the playable roles" is not a fix — it is the enum-Petra disease again: strangers' consent weighs nothing, so the consent drama dies with the cast swap. Two moves solve it together:

**First: one developed local, not four proxies.** Build a single Grenzburg musician — young, gifted, hungry, *not* a Salt-Reed stand-in but the next one: the person Ilyana begins absorbing **in play**. The player watches the Salt-Reed pattern start again from the outside — the borrowed instrument that becomes permanent, the "we" that arrives before agreement, the role assigned before it was asked for — and the Act I refusal Ilyana "cannot rewrite as fear" belongs to this person, in the present, about something the player witnessed her do. This converts the campaign's question from restitution-of-past (which paperwork can answer) to temptation-in-present (which only she can). It is also the missing dramatization of her resolved-outcome clause — "does not found another company during the game" — which currently forbids a temptation no quest ever stages.

**Second: the troupe stays remote and gets real voices.** Mirella's letters — the principal composer and scorekeeper writes devastating marginalia, not correspondence-summaries. And the *scores themselves* are the artifact class this campaign is sitting on: annotated margins, crossed-out shared bars, Mirella's hand and Ilyana's hand alternating on the same staff — musical documents as letters. Then rebuild [[The Last Common Measure]] around the strongest image available to a remote-compliant version: **the last piece is assembled by post.** Each former member sends — or refuses — their line. The finale performance is Ilyana and local volunteers playing the assembled score *with authored silences where the refused lines would be*. The absence is audible; the absence is the point. That is a better climax than four conveniently present ex-colleagues, and it turns the compliance constraint into the campaign's central artistic statement.

**And write the handwriting scene.** The compliance register can be devastating exactly once: Marta Vell needs to authenticate that the four approvals are forged, and the procedure requires Ilyana to *write the four names again* at a customs desk, before witnesses, so the hands can be compared. Her hand knows Mirella's signature better than its own. That is a paperwork scene that is also the confession the campaign never stages — proof the property-law vocabulary should be weaponized in one scene and evicted from the other ten.

## Where it is emotionally flat

**Property law has eaten a betrayal story.** The campaign engages the crime as cases, claims, releases, and packets; nobody ever asks what it was like to sign Mirella's name, and no scene lets the friendship that was broken *appear* — there is no account anywhere of what the five of them were like on a good night, which means the player is asked to grieve a band they never got to love. (Compare Tár, which is this exact arc — charisma, consent corroded by power, the institution, the fall — and spends its first hour making you feel the brilliance so the collapse costs you something; or Station Eleven's Traveling Symphony for troupe-as-family rendered warmly enough to lose.) One flashback is not needed; one *good-night story*, told by Ilyana and contradicted in Mirella's margin note on the same score, would do it.

**Her shadow never fires in the present — and the obvious target is the player.** Ilyana is the one companion whose flaw is *instrumentalizing people*, and she never once instrumentalizes the player. The romance episode even names the question ("whether Ilyana fears a relationship she cannot manage like a company") without dramatizing it. Design the beats: she publicly volunteers the player for things — a performance, a role, a plan — speaks for the player to third parties, assigns the player parts in schemes before asking. The refusal loop then runs on the player's *actual* agency — the one thing an RPG can do that no novel can. Her consent theme finally touches the medium it lives in.

**Morante is unvoiced — and she is Ilyana's dark mirror, which the text never notices.** Look at the symmetry sitting unstated in [[The Gilded Wake Season]]: Ilyana forged consent *before* success; Morante "treated the successful prize as retroactive consent" *after* it. Both women convert brilliance into permission. Morante's replacement-patronage offer (currently a bullet in the broken outcome) is Ilyana's Vardek-restoration equivalent and deserves the same treatment: one letter, nauseating because accurate, praising Ilyana in Ilyana's own logic — *"You understood what the timid never will: that greatness sometimes signs for its friends."* A temptation that flatters the companion's best self-image is the strongest kind, and this one is already latent in canon.

## Where it is boring

- **[[Every Case Its Owner]] is escort-the-paperwork** — the audit already says replace, and its brief ("a living creative project that must continue when Ilyana cannot remain its centre") is the right shape: make the Act III operation the proto-company piece — the local ensemble she built must run a performance *without her*, and her hardest playable action is the same as Bernard's drill: doing only her part. (Note the convergent design with the Bernard spec's indispensability drill; vary the texture — his is logistics, hers is authorship. Her version's knife: the ensemble *changes her arrangement*, and it's better.)
- **[[Reeds Under Ice]] is a shipwreck that exists to deliver cast members** — audit says replace; endorse. Its brief ("maintenance, repetition, art serving people after novelty has passed") should inherit the strongest existing beat from the winter material: the moment in her own banter file where the casualty cadence "keeps working after she moves away." Build the operation around *that* — a signal system she must maintain, repeat, and hand off through boring weeks of siege, with the temptation being novelty — "improving" the working system — rather than control.
- Five Cases at Customs needs de-cloning from Names Beneath Lime (see Part I §3) — different opening geometry, different pursuit, and above all a different *worker*: Pell One-Thumb should not be a second Jonas.

## Where it doesn't make sense

1. **Why does her entire past migrate to a besieged foreign war-city?** Celia travels alone through bandit country *to Grenzburg* — motivated by nothing except the campaign's need for her. Four separate people who have every reason to avoid their forger all relocate toward her within two seasons. The arrival ruling dissolves this entirely: keep them remote and the logic problem evaporates with the parade.
2. **Why were the cases shipped to Grenzburg at all?** Morante lodges a Sermoran seizure claim on instrument cases that then travel to the city where her debtor enemy happens to live. Fix with one chain-of-custody sentence, exactly like Elara's vault: Medori moved the securitized collateral to its administering branch — the cases followed the debt, not the drama.
3. **Timeline pressure:** company broke, court ruled, Medori purchased, Ilyana deported, claims filed, letters exchanged — all apparently within months, across a sea, into a siege. Date the collapse a year-plus back and the paper traffic breathes.

## Archetype audit

Bohemian core is well built — the bio already dodges the Manic Pixie trap the reference warns about (she has "desires, costs, work, contradictions"), and "her danger is assuming that possibility belongs to the person who named it first" is a strong thesis line. The blended reference offers the exact formalization: **Bohemian → Cheerleader, "the Creative Connector."** Read its shadow list against the Gilded Wake and the fit is eerie: *"Friends become cast members"* — that is the Salt-Reed wrong in four words. The distortion — *"If everyone is having an experience through me, I cannot be abandoned"* — names her fear of the empty measure better than her own bio does. The stress behaviour ("creates more plans, gifts, events and performances") gives scene writers her panic register, and the narrative arc line — *"Community requires limits, administration and presence when nobody applauds"* — is her maintenance lesson stated as one sentence. Adopt it formally; it also cleanly differentiates her from Elara (Cheerleader-primary who *manages* the existing room) — Ilyana is Bohemian-primary who *changes the room's weather*, and their banter object ("host and artist, audience and ownership") already knows this.

## Priority fixes for Ilyana

1. Rule on the troupe (recommended: remote; cut all four arrivals) and rebuild the finale as the assembled-by-post score with audible silences.
2. Build the local mirror-musician; move the Act I refusal to them; dramatize the second-company temptation the resolved outcome forbids.
3. Write Mirella's margins and Morante's letter; stage the handwriting-authentication scene.
4. Instrumentalize the player — her shadow must fire at the person holding the controller.
5. Replace the two paperwork operations per audit, with the shapes above (ensemble-runs-without-her; maintenance-under-siege).
6. One good-night story of the Salt-Reeds, so the loss has a face before the ledger does.
7. De-clone Five Cases from Names Beneath Lime; differentiate Marta Vell from the Hella/Bellandi register.
8. Adopt Creative Connector; fix the internal title collision (Road/Duet Without a Conductor) and the twin codas (Measure/Song).

# Part IV — The shortest path

If only five things get done across both characters:

1. **Make the arrival ruling** — the Lio Clause for Tavio; remote-with-voices for Ilyana's troupe. Every other fix routes through this decision, and the paired passes cannot start without it.
2. **Write the four voices**: Lio, Maro, Mirella (margins), Morante. The two campaigns' emotional cores are currently carried by people with zero written lines.
3. **Give each companion a present-tense wound-in-motion**: Tavio's concealed tremor and motive; Ilyana's absorption of the local musician and of the player. Both campaigns currently prosecute finished crimes; both need a live one.
4. **Stage each bio's unstaged promise**: Tavio's public stop that feels like defeat (with a bout the quest is allowed to care about); Ilyana's present refusal she cannot rewrite (aimed at someone whose loss costs her).
5. **Break the paperwork default**: keep the handwriting scene and the filed-bout rules (procedure as drama, used twice, on purpose); evict the register-escort-recall grammar from everything else.

The raw material here is stronger than Elara's and Bernard's was — a fair fight with the man you maimed and a song with your victims' silences in it are climaxes most games never get near. The current drafts know this; the audit half-knows it; the paired passes just have to stop the migration from sanding off the two things worth keeping.
