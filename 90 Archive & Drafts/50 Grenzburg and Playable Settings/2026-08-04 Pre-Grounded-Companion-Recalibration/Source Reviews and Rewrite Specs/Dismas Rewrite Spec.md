# Dismas Rewrite Spec

Execution spec for actioning [[Companion Critical Review - Dismas and Ashira]], Dismas scope. Companion to the Elara, Bernard, Tavio, and Ilyana specs — shared prose rules and verification inherited. **This is the roster's construction spec, not a retrofit:** seven of eleven slots are reserved, there is no campaign doc, no Field Familiarity doc, no combat package, and no backstory event file. Where the other specs edit, this one builds. It is the Dismas half of the Ilyana-Dismas paired pass; pair items are marked ⟨PAIR⟩.

## Locked Decisions

1. **Marta stays remote; he files.** The arriving-survivor courtroom plot is cut. [[The Name on the Charge]] keeps its title with inverted meaning — the name on the charge is his own. Marta's voice arrives as her years-old claim paper and a fixed response instruction. The Lio Clause remains scoped to Lio alone.
2. **Secondary archetype: Goofball** — Dismas becomes Rebel-primary / Goofball-secondary ("Lovable Rogue"): defiance made charming; conflict, seriousness versus evasion; shadow, charming nihilism. His precise failure surface: the joke deployed when gravity was owed.
3. **Vocation: Hunter, "Quarry-Wise" variant.** The escaped quarry who learned the hunter's habits from the wrong side — prey-minded, exit-first, counter-tracking, pursuit-breaking, reversible traps. Doctrine-distinct from Ylva's Rescue Scout (extraction vs. evasion); the frontmatter's reassessment item closes.
4. **The truth is fixed, mixed, and counterfactual-proof.** Two named people died in the coach; Marta lived because the soldiers he fled from dug her out; the record cannot resolve whether his staying would have saved the other two. No playthrough finds "nobody died"; none gets "they were doomed anyway." Do-not-regress flagged. His unfairness exception: the truth cannot be prepared into kindness, and the counterfactual is permanently withheld.

## The Re-Architecture in One View

The bio's nine rebuilt-line requirements, mapped to the new eleven:

| Bio requirement | Where it lands |
|---|---|
| 1. Thrilling justified rule-breaking that makes his company desirable | The Decorative Fence (new intro) |
| 2. An escape whose practical aftermath he initially avoids | seeded in the intro; detonates in The First Mile (new A1-O) |
| 3. The rockfall truth recovered without a past survivor arriving | The Name on the Charge (rebuilt A2-F) |
| 4. Friendship built around illicit pleasure, skill, and trust | No Railing (new FR) |
| 5. An urban winter operation where resistance requires maintenance | Passage (new A2-O) |
| 6. Public accountability he chooses | The Name on the Charge — he files |
| 7. Romance and bond organized around return | The Return Leg (new RM) / The Missing Queen (new BD) |
| 8. A free guide network that may continue without becoming his throne | built across Acts II–III; tested in Tuesday (new A3-O) |
| 9. A final road he answers because nobody owns him enough to force the answer | The Road That Answers (reframed A3-F) |

**Campaign shape statement** (the campaign doc's thesis; no "four truths" template): **the door stays open, and he keeps not leaving.** Every act ends with a clean exit visibly available and untaken; the campaign never contains him, because a contained Rebel proves nothing — his resolved ending is *uncontained return*, commitment as repetition. The gesture-spine is **counting**: he counted voices at the coach (and fled); he counts chimneys above a hamlet he secretly saved (No Railing); he makes the count of the dead at last (The Name on the Charge); he counts the network's living in spring. The campaign is one man learning to finish a count.

# Part 1 — New Canon Elements

## 1.1 The Korrheim Rockfall (new event file)

His White-Sled/Froststep equivalent does not exist; create it. Contents:

- **The fixed record (locked decision 4):** the band's prepared rockfall; his early trigger on an unconfirmed target; the coach; the two dead — working identities: the coach's driver and one passenger (names at execution, registry-checked); Marta Etten (renamed — see 1.2) the survivor, dug out by the soldiers whose approach made him run. The file states plainly that the record cannot resolve the counterfactual, and carries the do-not-regress flag.
- **The conviction:** armed robbery and road destruction, "the full human cost never established" — the gap in the legal record is what his Act II filing closes.
- **Present consequences** (White-Sled-style list): he counts under stress; he sleeps by exits; he tells stories with himself missing from the brave sentence; Koss cites the purchased service when assigning route work; the missing queen has marked seven routes he never signed.
- **Remote apparatus:** the absent cofounder (the old outline's "absent cofounder" retained as *remote pressure only* — one letter, Act II, offering to absorb blame conveniently or warning him off the filing; never arrives); no gang member, survivor, or Korrheim official enters the worldspace.

## 1.2 Marta's paper (and her rename)

**Rename:** Marta Etten → working name **Sella Etten** (kills the Marta×2 collision; Ilyana's Marta Vell survived her spec unrenamed). Registry-check at execution; propagate through her personal-history file and the old outline's archive.

**Her claim paper** (in-world text, quoted whole, ≤120 words): filed years ago in Korrheim against "persons unknown" — practical to the point of pain: the injury listed as expenses, lost work seasons, a replaced wheel, a hired man's wages. One line breaks the accounting register and is the whole person (final wording at scene pass; register: *"Item: I was under the stone from midday until the soldiers came. I claim nothing for this."*).

**Her response instruction** (Petra pattern, fixed, selected by the actual filing): accept the record without contact; direct any repair to Korrheim road-safety works; or no response. No option contacts her, and no state means forgiveness. Her personhood is the claim paper; her answer is administration; the gap between them is the design.

## 1.3 The reprisal family — his local faces

The banter file's apology calibration ("returns to a family whose flight drew retaliation and gives them the names, supplies, and testimony needed to move safely... accepts whatever task they assign") is staged at last, as continuity:

- A debtor family (surname at execution, registry-checked; 3–4 named members, at least one child with a voice) moved through Dismas's route in the intro caper. The escape is clean, thrilling, complete — and in winter the aftermath arrives at their door, not his: questioning, a stall license pulled, a neighbor's suspicion (The First Mile's engine).
- **Voice rule:** never eloquent about power; their anger is small-scale and specific (the stall, the neighbor, the child's school-line place) — the chorus prohibition holds.
- **The assigned task:** when he returns to repair (The First Mile's end), they assign him something ongoing and unglamorous — standing surety visits, a monthly carry, maintenance of the thing his exit broke. It recurs in ambient play through spring; whether he keeps the appointments is direction made visible.
- In the coda they are established, and their child knows his card trick. `reprisal_family_state` persists to summer.

## 1.4 The containment triptych

The Rebel's fear — never staged in the old outline — gets three authored scenes, distributed:

1. **Koss's leash** (in Passage): Koss discovers the underways route and exercises the contract — orders Dismas, by right, publicly, to run military cargo through it or cede its map. The one scene where "purchased service" is felt from inside. His response (comply / slip it / make compliance cost Koss) moves direction.
2. **The kind trap** (ambient + banter escalation): the Yard assigns him a permanent bunk, a roster title, his name painted on the route board. Kindness as capture; his existing embarrassment beats (the bedroll already under the old bunk) escalate into one scene where he re-routes his sleeping spot for a week and cannot say why.
3. **The carter who is right** (in The First Mile): a road woman whose cousin's wagon burned in a reprisal after someone else's clever escape wants the predatory toll ruling contested *lawfully* — process, magistrate, precedent. She is not naive; she has priced his kind of freedom and found who pays for it. She wins the argument on screen. (The review's requirement that ordinary stable life talks back and is correct.)

## 1.5 The missing queen, systematized

The best recurring object in the roster gets its rules written down (bio + banter file): the queen is his route-marker, his tell, and his held-back self — the card kept out of every game. Appearances are budgeted like Tavio's tremor: the unsigned exit (exists, banter); a route mark the player can learn to recognize in the world (ambient); **the bond rite** (The Missing Queen — he gives it away, 2.6); and in a resolved summer, the deck plays complete for the first time. Four beats, no more.

# Part 2 — The Eleven Pieces

Formats follow the Companion Constitution (intro + three operations + three duo finales + friendship + romance + bond + coda; ~10.5–11h; three act deadlines: The Last Convoy, Ghor at the Gate, The Deep Muster).

## 2.1 The Decorative Fence (new introduction — replaces The Sound of Falling Stone)

**Function:** accomplice pleasure. The player must enjoy being his partner in a righteous caper before the rockfall means anything — the enjoy-them-first sequencing all four prior specs enforce.

**Premise:** a technically-valid, materially predatory seizure ruling is about to land on a debtor family's goods at a Grenzburg checkpoint. Dismas proposes the caper: family and goods out before the writ arrives. His method is his banter competence scene as design DNA — no forgery, no violence: re-route reality, swap the visible cargo, leave every document untouched ("Lie to the paper if you like. The cart already knows"). Beats: roofline reconnaissance; the guard whose boredom is a resource; the child stashed with the cargo laughing at the wrong moment; the exit through the fence everyone forgot is decorative. **It works completely.** The quest's last beat is the hook, not a lesson: as they leave, a neighbor watches from a window — and says nothing. Yet. (Requirement 2 seeded; no narration flags it.)

Old material: [[The Sound of Falling Stone]]'s quarry-slide freeze and counting tell are **salvaged, not deleted** — relocated to the Act I finale (2.3). The outline file archives.

## 2.2 The First Mile (new Act I operation)

**Function:** the beneficiaries inherit the consequences (audit brief); the aftermath he initially avoids; the carter who is right; the apology staged.

**Premise:** winter's edge — the reprisal lands on the family (1.3). Dismas's reflex answer is another extraction ("I'll move them again"), and the quest's first movement lets him start building it, because it's what he knows. The counter-pressure: the family doesn't want to run — they want their stall back; and the carter (1.4.3) wants the ruling contested properly. The operation becomes the thing he has never done: **stay inside the aftermath** — witness statements, the magistrate's queue, protecting the family from Watch pressure *without* an exit, testimony under his own name. The end is the banter apology, staged: names, supplies, testimony, and the assigned task accepted without bargaining. Output: `dismas_first_mile_state`, the assigned-task thread, Act I prep asset.

**Title note:** from his own line — "Then it isn't an escape. You sold them the first mile."

## 2.3 The Easy Ambush (reframed Act I finale)

Keep: Split Pine Ravine, the medicine raiders, reversible traps, the civilian wood train entering early, the marcher lieutenant who "promises to falsify any civilian losses."

Reframe and additions:

- **The counting-freeze lands here.** The prepared trap line above the ravine; the whistle of stressed rock; his freeze; the count — "Three voices. Two at the front. One pretending the wheel wasn't frightening him." The player sees the body-memory *before* any explanation exists. He does not explain it in this quest.
- **The lieutenant is his cover-up in miniature:** an institution offering to hide his harm, again. Refusing the falsification offer — even at cost to the operation's official result — is the finale's direction beat.
- **The audit's cleanup clause:** after aborting or partially springing the ambush, someone must disarm the remaining traps in the dark while the raiders regroup. Staying for the dangerous cleanup — unwatched, uncredited, his own devices — is the played climax. The exit is, per the shape statement, visibly open the whole time.
- Duo format; prep threshold per standard; fail-forward per the outline's existing lines (they were sound).

## 2.4 No Railing (new friendship episode)

**Function:** illicit pleasure with no moral prosecution (audit brief) — and the campaign's secret heart.

Dismas takes the player up a route "that doesn't exist" — laundry lofts, two roofs, the unused watch platform, then beyond: an old smuggler's traverse to a view officials call impossible. He has brought breakfast. Cards on the parapet; the deck one queen short; no test, no villain, no confession. The payload he would never name: the view overlooks a hamlet that exists because people he moved settled there. He comes every year, anonymously, to check. The player catches him **counting chimneys** — the counting motif inverted: this time he finishes the count, because this count comes out right. If asked, he lies badly ("Waste of a good view otherwise") and deals the next hand. Outputs: friendship state; one Act III safeguard-adjacent memory beat; no reward that moralizes the afternoon.

## 2.5 Passage (new Act II operation)

**Function:** the audit's urban siege operation — underways and borrowed authority, where escape is easy and maintenance is not; the guide network's true founding.

**Premise:** siege winter. Dismas runs an underways passage linking two cut-off wards — the smuggler as civic artery. Opening it is one thrilling night; the quest is the *three weeks after*: props rot, water rises, handoffs fail, torch discipline slips, [[Mara Senn]] requires named handoffs, supplies, and return signals before the Low Lanterns will call it protected (her bio terms, enforced on screen), and volunteers must be trained to run it without him. Mid-operation: **Koss's leash** (1.4.1). The temptation architecture: at every failure, cutting a new route is easier and more fun than fixing this one — novelty as his version of flight. The win-state is the passage running a full night with Dismas above ground, doing nothing, which he experiences as a kind of death and the quest refuses to console. Outputs: `dismas_passage_state`, network-founding prep assets, `koss_leash_result`.

⟨PAIR⟩ The Ilyana winter foil beat ("they create an escape and a shelter; each tries to assign the maintenance to the other; they return together for the boring part") stages inside or adjacent to Passage — coordinate with her pass; the scene is jointly owned.

## 2.6 The Return Leg (new romance) and The Missing Queen (new bond)

**The Return Leg** (female player): commitment as a route chosen twice. He plans a two-day route with her and — the man who never announces returns — *announces the return leg at the start*. The middle is the intimacy his character permits: he shows her his exits. All of them — the habit, the count of doors, where he'd go. Telling her where the doors are is his vow; nothing about him promises the doors will close, and she is not asked to close them. Night camp; the plain question (register: what a second journey means when the first proved nothing was owed); fade to black per standard. Morning: back on time. Rewards: romance duo move + `dismas_return_leg_practiced` (one A3-F safeguard); perk **No Escape Route** (Constitution-named) earned at the finale per standard.

**The Missing Queen** (male player): the positive shared object that survives after the enemy is gone. The two men cut a *new* route — against no one; a shortcut to something good (a spring, a carter's saving of half a day) — and set its keeping-rules on a marker post. The rite: **he gives the player the missing queen** — the card he has kept out of every game, his route-marker, his held-back self — and the player gives a return signal both can sound and neither can overrule (⟨PAIR⟩ echo of the vault's bond grammar). The oath is the campaign in two lines: *call me back; I will come.* Rewards: bond duo move + `dismas_missing_queen_given` (equal, exclusive A3-F safeguard); perk **No Blind Turn** at the finale. The deck now plays incomplete forever unless the summer beat returns it — executor's choice whether the resolved coda has the player slip it back into the deck, which is the better ending.

## 2.7 The Name on the Charge (rebuilt Act II finale — he files)

**Trigger:** the assigned task is done, and doing it didn't quiet the older thing. Early spring, or deep winter's end. Dismas engages Bellandi to trace the coach — passengers, survivors, the count Korrheim never established — and to file the enlarged truth against his own conviction record, knowing it can only increase what he owes.

**Structure:**

- **The tracing is played:** his old route notation surrendered; physical evidence recalled against the record; Bellandi's correspondence with Korrheim (postal logistics honest — weeks, not days; the quest spans a season-edge); underworld brokers who offered to disappear a survivor in the old outline now offer to disappear the *inquiry* — cheaper, cleaner, nobody arrives. Koss separately wants the case closed: an enlarged claim complicates his asset.
- **Three authored exit ramps.** At three points, abandoning the filing is easy, clean, consequence-free in the short term, and visibly available (the shape statement enforced at the campaign's hinge). Nobody stops him but him.
- **The cofounder's letter** (1.1): remote pressure to let the absent partner carry it — the sole-blame temptation inverted (someone *else* volunteering to be the villain; accepting it is the fled ending wearing a gift's face).
- **The truth arrives** (locked decision 4): two names, Sella alive, the counterfactual withheld. An authored scene holds the count — the campaign's spine gesture completed: he counts, out loud, to the end, for the first time.
- **The choice** (bio requirement 6): public confession and restructured service; sealed admission under marcher oversight; restitution to the named estates with Sella's instruction honored — or suppression, which remains available and is the broken path. The old outline's best line survives as the relationship-commitment frame: *the player names what they will and will not carry for him.*
- Commitment gates open here per standard. Output: `dismas_charge_filed_state`, `sella_etten_response`, direction, Act II resolution.

## 2.8 Tuesday (new Act III operation)

**Function:** the network's first boring week — peace as the container he can't pick.

Post-siege spring. The free guide network runs its first normal week: a fee dispute, two guides quarrelling over territory, a route needing re-marking, a client complaint about a perfectly safe detour. No villain; nothing is wrong; **that is the antagonist.** His temptations, all authored and all open: leave (the exits never close); *manufacture* danger (spice a route report, refuse an escort to make a point — the retired outlaw's itch); or settle the quarrel by fiat and become the throne the network must not have (bio requirement 8). The resolved play is the dullest and hardest: arbitrate by the keeping-rules, re-mark the route, show up on the appointed day. ⟨PAIR⟩ the Ilyana spring foil beat ("Dismas arrives when promised") lands here. Convergence-variance note (fourth institution-piece in the roster): Bernard's axis is indispensability, Ilyana's authorship, Elara's dethronement — **Dismas's is that peace bores him**, and the quest says so without a lesson. Output: `guide_network_state`, A3 prep assets.

## 2.9 The Road That Answers (reframed Act III finale)

Keep: Three Horn Depot, the dead-march column, fen routes, training local guides, sabotaging the residue road-pattern, the sacrifice gated per vault standard.

Reframe (audit: independent commitment repeatedly renewed, not institutional domestication):

- **Three legs, three renewals.** The evacuation runs in three movements; at each, a clean exit is authored, visible, and genuinely takeable — the sealed-commission offer, the battle-cover flight window, the "you've done enough" release from Liese herself. Resolved play is choosing to stay *three times*, uncoerced — "a road chosen twice" scaled to thrice. The finale never contains him; that is the point and the test.
- The resolved outcome is re-specified: not "accept public marcher service" but **the free guide who stays** — under no one's command, with the network's people, to the last group. Supervised-scout remains the compromised shape; flight the broken; sacrifice the gated heroic-unresolved.
- The relationship safeguards (`dismas_return_leg_practiced` / `dismas_missing_queen_given`) each prevent one extraction or exhaustion failure, equal and exclusive, per vault grammar.
- Output: arc outcome, summer destination, evacuation states.

## 2.10 Passing Through Slowly (new summer coda)

Title from his own line — the banter embarrassment scene paid off. Reflects the fixed outcome: **resolved** — the network runs without being his; he guides, unowned; the bedroll is under the old bunk and nobody comments; the reprisal family's child demands the card trick, and (executor's choice, recommended) the player returns the queen to the deck and he deals the first complete hand of the campaign. **Compromised** — supervised scout under Liese's terms, useful and slightly caged, naming it himself. **Broken/departed** — word of him from far roads; a family he moved sends thanks he will never collect; the network runs anyway, which he would hate to know is the kindest outcome. No re-litigation; the coda shows.
