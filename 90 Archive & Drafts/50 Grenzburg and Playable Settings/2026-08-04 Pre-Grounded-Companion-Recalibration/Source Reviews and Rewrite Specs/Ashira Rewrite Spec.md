# Ashira Rewrite Spec

Execution spec for actioning [[Companion Critical Review - Dismas and Ashira]], Ashira scope. Companion to the five prior specs — shared prose rules and verification inherited. **This is the roster's density spec:** her premises are the strongest of the eight and her structure is untouched; what's missing is flesh — named people, staged fears, a temptation with teeth, and the three people her campaign promised and never delivered. Where Dismas's spec built, this one *populates*. Her `cleared` status is real about the bones and premature about the tissue; touched files flip to a working status during the pass and re-clear at Phase 5.

## Locked Decisions

1. **The widow is built.** Kin of one of the dead raiders whose face Ashira shaped and sent walking — she was in the camp during the nights of faces, later displaced to Grenzburg. Never a quest, never eloquent, state never moves. One finale roster branch becomes the face she owns.
2. **Secondary archetype: Cheerleader** — Ashira becomes Goth-primary / Cheerleader-secondary ("Midnight Socialite"): outsider identity with selective social fluency; conflict, difference versus recognition; shadow, cultivated elitism. The Elara foil symmetry is formalized: each woman carries the other's primary as her secondary.
3. **Full mechanical imbalance, flagged.** The human face is always visibly superior in play; the ethical route always costs more. Do-not-regress flag: *if a playtest reports the ethical route feels equally efficient, the imbalance has regressed.* Her unfairness exception.
4. **Present tense: caught studying.** Four budgeted beats make the retention visible — two ambient maintenance tells, one caught-studying incident, one pre-finale packing beat. Her sin stays custodial; the player finally sees her keep it.

The sibling rule to the other campaigns' spines: *she is the last thing in the campaign to be witnessed accurately.*

# Part 1 — New Canon Elements

## 1.1 The widow

**File:** new character note. **Role:** the person the campaign about stolen faces owes a face to.

- Working name **Mavra** (surname and cultural flavor at execution, registry-checked — nothing near Runa Moss-Crown or Vesna Rimek). Forties. Widow — or the review allows sister or adult child; widow recommended — of one of the raiders killed in the Qesheth reprisals, whose combat pattern Ashira shaped into a walking likeness. She was *in the camp* during the nights of faces. The camp scattered; she came west with other displaced people; she launders or mends in Lantern Ward among the war's other arrivals.
- **The asymmetry is the point, stated in her file:** her husband massacred a caravan. She did not. The terror was addressed to people who had committed nothing, in the faces of people they loved. Her existence makes the campaign's cleanest sentence ("It did [end the attacks]") cost something to read.
- **Recognition, canon-fixed:** she was on the quay during [[The Faces She Kept]] when the thieves' projector test-flashed a stolen face — her husband's. This is true in every playthrough regardless of quest outcome; the quest gains one line of crowd texture to seat it (a woman who does not scream).
- **Behavior rules (the Sandro/Lenka/Mavra pattern, fourth instance):** never a quest; never blocks content; refuses nothing because she asks for nothing; not eloquent — the chorus prohibition at full strength. Her one fixed exchange (between Acts I and II, at her mending stall or a shelter line, a few lines total): what the nights did — concrete, small-scale, one child-detail (register, final wording at scene pass: *the children stopped sleeping indoors; her son still wakes when a lamp moves*). No accusation shaped for use. She does not ask Ashira to do anything, which is worse than any demand.
- **The finale payoff:** the five roster branches gain identities (Part 2.5); one is her husband's. The destroy / divide / weaponize decision at Bone-Reed now contains her name. In the resolved outcome, word of the destruction reaches her through the consent-policy world — she does not thank Ashira, and `mavra_state` does not move. In the coda, if resolved, one beat: Ashira passes her stall and is not stopped; that is the entire transaction.
- The bio's Seasonal Movement autumn line ("confronts the first living owner of a face she retained") now points at a person.

## 1.2 The named dead and the named living (density substrate)

The registry-checked name budget this spec creates (all working names at execution):

- **The eight Causeway captives** (A Useful Terror): named; three carry beats — including the ferryman who refuses the face-use with a concrete reason in his own diction (he traded nets with the camp; the dead man fixed his boat's pump once; he will not be rescued by that face). His refusal replaces the anonymous "families refuse" position.
- **The four field prisoners** (No Human Shadow): named, one beat each.
- **The five roster branches** (No Human Shadow): identified — five dead raiders with names and one line of who they were to the camp; one is Mavra's husband.
- **The copied debtors** (When the Screen Comes Down): two or three named — and the cross-cast escalation: one decoy wears **Neda Volk's face.** A mechanic becomes a violation the player feels, because Neda is established and loved; her reaction afterward (fury without seminar — her deadpan register bent almost to breaking) is a required beat.
- **The dead woman in the wall** (What the Ruins Repeated): the repeated voice gets a name, and the family who asks about her gets one speaking member; Ashira's "the wall remembers badly" beat now lands on named grief.
- **Pavel Orr recurs:** the bio's clientele line ("the person debtors approach when they need someone to believe an uncanny event without spreading it by supper") becomes real ambient content, and Pavel is client zero — two or three small beats across the acts (his suspension resolved through her testimony; his next uncanny report, this one mundane; his presence at the remembrance meal). He is her first believer and the proof her practice can be trusted.

## 1.3 The caught-studying thread (locked decision 4)

Four beats, budget enforced (the tremor/queen/counting discipline):

1. **Ambient tell one:** the screened corner at night — relabelling, testing a cord, the case's labels turned to the wall when someone enters. Observable, unnarrated.
2. **Ambient tell two:** she declines to let anyone else carry the case, ever — noticed once by Neda in one line ("It has a handle." — no reply).
3. **The incident** (between Acts II and III): the player catches her studying a face by lamplight. Her documented shame-tell fires — she explains the category until the person inside it disappears — and the player may name it or let it stand; one beat, no repair quest, direction moves. If the player says nothing, she stops explaining mid-sentence anyway, which is worse.
4. **The packing beat** (pre-finale): loading the branches for Bone-Reed, she handles them like relics — wrapped, ordered, cared for. The care is the indictment; no one comments.

## 1.4 The remembrance meal — the want witnessed

One scene, Act II, following [[What the Ruins Repeated]]: the Yard holds a meal for the found and the lost Blackjacks. Someone sets Ashira's place unasked — in the middle of the table, not the edge seat with the sightlines. She sits there. She is visibly, catastrophically pleased, and the room notices, and she cannot take it back. Two minutes; no dialogue lesson; her frontmatter humiliation ("exposure as someone who wants ordinary tenderness") staged at last in the one register she cannot control — kindness done *to* her in public. The grief context is deliberate: it is the one social weather she trusts, inverted into belonging.

## 1.5 The corrector — assimilation with an agent

**File:** new minor character note. Working name **Dotte** (registry-checked). A Mercy laywoman or Yard cook — genuinely kind, genuinely erasing: she brightens Ashira's corner, gifts her colorful cloth, softens her introductions, translates her to third parties ("she means well, she's just —"). Three beats across the acts; no arc-payoff lesson — the corrector never fully stops, and what Ashira learns is to survive being translated without the scalpel (the growth register between assimilation and cruelty). One beat may go dark at the player's steering: Ashira's anger register is *intimate, not loud* — if she finally corrects Dotte, it should be one quiet sentence that ends the pattern and costs her the kindness too. Direction texture, not a conflict system.

## 1.6 The imbalance (locked decision 3)

- **[[A Useful Terror]]:** deploying the human face scatters the sentries — encounters skipped, all eight captives cleanly saved, the Causeway route preserved, the quest shortened. The ethical route is longer and harder: the nonhuman decoy misdirects *some* sentries; at two assets one material objective (route or property, never a life) is at genuine risk; at full preparation the ethical route still costs more time, more fight, and one visible scar on the outcome docket. "It would work." now has arithmetic behind it.
- **[[No Human Shadow]]:** Weaponize collapses the Muster Conductor's phases — the fight shortens dramatically and the extraction is clean. Destroy fights all five phases with the full escalation. Divided custody sits between. The tactically-strongest claim the quest already makes becomes mechanically true.
- **The flag, in both files and the campaign doc:** *intentional imbalance; do not rebalance toward parity; if the ethical route tests as equally efficient, the design has regressed.* Refusal that costs nothing teaches nothing.
