# Companion Critical Review — Elara and Bernard

An adversarial read of CMP-01 and CMP-05: bios, campaigns, all quest pieces, banter files, and support systems, measured against [[Companion Dialogue and Scene Standard]], [[Companion Constitution]], the eight-archetype reference, and the blended-archetype reference. The quests are treated as replaceable; only the character shapes are held fixed.

The one-sentence verdict: **the constitutions are better than the campaigns.** The Dialogue and Scene Standard correctly names almost every failure mode in this corpus — "characters politely presenting their design documents to one another," universal eloquence, arguments that resolve because both parties state the correct lesson — and the banter files obey it. The eleven formal pieces per companion largely do not. The life of both characters is currently hiding in their banter files while the campaigns dramatize administration.

---

# Part I — Systemic Findings (both characters, and likely all eight)

## 1. The campaigns dramatize administration, not relationship

Look at what the climaxes literally are. Elara's Act III finale ends in a hearing where a claims-factor reports "which instructions are authentic; which assets satisfy restitution; what remains legally owed." Bernard's finale ends in an inventory count. The load-bearing emotional vocabulary across both campaigns is *authenticated, escrowed, witnessed, bounded, custody, provenance, restitution schedule, settlement*. These are the climaxes of a compliance seminar.

Ledger-drama can work — *The Traitor Baru Cormorant* proves an accountant's arc can be devastating — but only because every line in Baru's ledger is a named person she loves and betrays. Here the ledger lines are strangers and paper. The design principle "no account state determines whether Elara has shared power" is stated correctly and repeatedly; the problem is that the *scenes* still spend their climactic minutes on the account states.

The tell that this is a flattening rather than a style: the moments everyone will remember from these files are tiny and physical — the spoon falling out of Bernard's boot, "You used the blue cups," "I asked for coal." None of them involve a document. The quests should be built out of that material; instead that material decorates quests built out of paperwork.

**Fix:** for every formal piece, ask "what is the last thing on screen?" If the answer is a state being recorded, restage. The count can *happen*; it cannot be the *climax*. The climax must be a person doing something to another person.

## 2. The Remote Claims Standard exiles everyone who matters

The standard (no person from a companion's former life enters the worldspace) is defensible production discipline and avoids the surprise-visitor cliché. But as executed it produces a fatal side effect: **every human being with an emotional claim on the companion — victim, mentor, judge, patron, betrayer — is off the map, and everything they feel arrives as authenticated mail.**

Petra Ilev, the woman whose fingers Bernard's caches cost, is literally an enum: `accepted-no-reconciliation | redirected-to-road-care | contact-closed`. Vardek is a letter with nice paper. Radena gets one relayed sentence. The docs even say the quiet part: "His physical absence keeps the decisive scene on Elara." But the decisive scene isn't on Elara — it's on the envelope.

*Disco Elysium* is the proof that absent people can carry a game — but its absent people arrive through devastatingly textured artifacts (the boots on the hanged man, Dora in the dream), not through summaries of their filing status. The medium can stay remote; the *voice* cannot stay procedural.

**Fix (cheap, high yield):** write the actual letters. One letter from Petra in her own diction — dictated to someone else's handwriting, because she lost fingers, a detail the current design leaves lying on the ground. One Vardek letter that demonstrates surveillance-intimacy (he knows the blue cups, knows the porter's name — menace without arrival). One Radena letter. Player-readable, in-world, un-paraphrased. This converts the campaigns' weakest structural constraint into their best texture at the cost of maybe three pages of prose.

**Fix (bolder):** each companion gets exactly one *local* person with a legitimate personal grievance — not from the old life's inner circle (standard intact) but downstream of the harm. See E4 and B3 below.

## 3. The entire support cast shares one voice

Put these speakers side by side:

- Jonas, a dock porter, is "uninterested in becoming Elara's evidence of compassion."
- Emri, a shoring lead: "You may say you listened. Do not say we followed."
- Hannel, a sixteen-year-old kitchen helper, "refuses to become 'the girl Bernard once cleared.'"
- A one-scene Chapel-Folk grandmother "values the name of the taker more than repayment."
- Radena "asks Bernard to stop repaying childhood shelter by trying to possess every future need."
- Bellandi: "That question is not in my portfolio."

Six characters, one speaker: a boundaries-workshop graduate with perfect insight into instrumentalization, refusing in aphorism to be used as a prop. Individually, several of these are good lines. Collectively they are the loudest LLM fingerprint in the corpus, and they do real damage beyond style: **because the world instantly and eloquently corrects every shadow-move the companion makes, the shadow never gets to work.** Elara's curation is refused before it lands; Bernard's charm is named before it deflects. Temptation that never tempts anyone is not a flaw, it's a costume.

This directly violates the corpus's own standard: "universal eloquence under fear, shame, exhaustion, or anger" is on the prohibited list, as are "mistakes about other people that may take more than one scene to correct" (required, and almost never present — nobody in either campaign is *wrong* in a way that persists).

**Fix:** redistribute articulacy. Some harm-bearers should grovel, some should want money, some should be successfully charmed and *like* their exploiter (the most chilling outcome for both arcs), some should be inarticulate and angry in a way that helps no one. The Wire's dockworkers, not a facilitation circle. Let at most one character per campaign own the surgical-refusal register — for Elara that's Ashira (it's her whole function); for Bernard it's Zafir. Everyone else must lose the scalpel.

## 4. Antagonists are defined entirely by disclaimers

The files keep announcing what antagonists are *not*: "Sera is a current hired professional, not a former acquaintance." "Vek has no connection to Froststep." "None is a returning figure from Elara's former life." "The bandits... know nothing about Bernard." Four quests, same sentence. This is defensive anti-cliché writing — and after the disclaimer, nobody gives the antagonist anything *to be*. The result is an interchangeable HR department of evil: "local agents," "hired abductors," "paid loyalists," broker, solicitor, advocate.

Jorek Sile has one flicker of a self ("he intends to burn the names because unnamed goods sell better" — that's an appetite, good). Nobody else does.

**Fix:** each named opposition figure gets one appetite, one fear, and one line that could not be transferred to another antagonist — the exact test the Dialogue Standard already applies to companions. Sera Vann offering the "clean bargain" in the vault should be the most seductive two minutes of Elara's campaign; currently she is a door with a contract.

## 5. The safety architecture leaks into the fiction and kills dread

"No single failed check kills either character." "Nika never dies off-screen or from one failed check." "No child dies through an off-screen timer." "Bernard cannot die autonomously." Every irreversible loss is quarantined behind an explicit, warned, confirmed choice, and — worse — the quest briefs keep *saying so*. As player-protection design this is defensible. As drama it means a first-time player who reads the world correctly will stop being afraid by mid-Act I, because the world visibly promises to catch them.

Note also the recurring legalism "captured, killed in justified resistance, or escape" — four bosses, same clause, and "justified resistance" is compliance language wearing a costume.

**Fix:** keep the no-unfair-death rule, but allow each campaign one *unwarned, unchosen, permanent loss that is not a death* — a friendship, an object, a place, a reputation — that the player only understands afterward. Dread requires at least one thing the design will not promise to protect.

## 6. Template symmetry is the deepest LLM tell

Both campaigns (and per the Constitution, all eight): eleven pieces in identical slots, identical "2 participate; 3 all material objectives; 4 add one safeguard" ladders, identical resolved/compromised/broken taxonomy, identical mutually-exclusive-equal-safeguard romance/bond rewards, three identical act deadlines, remote-only pasts, twin coda titles ("The First Honest Find" / "The First Open Table"), and a uniform title cadence (noun-preposition-noun: *Names Beneath Lime, A House Fed by Bones, Beds Before Banners, Embers Without Excuse, Watch Until Morning*). Any single instance is craft; the aggregate is a lathe. Players feel this even when they can't name it — the fourth companion campaign will play as a re-skin of the first.

Real ensembles are asymmetric on purpose: BG3 gives Lae'zel a short brutal arc and Shadowheart a long slow one; Astarion's is confession-shaped, Karlach's is a countdown. Shape *is* characterization.

**Fix:** keep the production lattice (slots, thresholds) as invisible scaffolding, but let each campaign's *felt shape* come from the archetype. Elara's campaign should be socially shaped — rooms, occasions, audiences, a rise-fall-rise silhouette (dethronement arc). Bernard's should be intimacy-shaped — small objects, kitchens, one room at a time, a spiral (relapse arc) rather than an escalation. Right now both are siege-logistics-shaped, because the *world* plot (siege, muster) dictated the shape of every personal story. Also: break the twin coda titles.

## 7. Thesis-first prose: the docs pre-chew every scene

Recurring habits worth a find-and-revise pass:

- **"X is not Y. It is Z."** — "Her brightness is not innocence. It is social force." "He is not comic relief. He is..." "The pouch is an accountability interface, not a magical container." "Triage determines location and urgency, not moral worth." Dozens of instances. Two are style; forty are a metronome.
- **Negation lists** — "White Flame is not: a battery... proof of innocence... an order's property..." The bio for Bernard contains the triple disclaimer "The order permits marriage. Bernard took no vow of chastity. He was never ordained." — a legal brief anticipating an objection nobody raised yet.
- **Self-grading beats** — "His strongest line is not eloquent:" and "The strongest scene is not a speech." The doc announces the quality of its own material, which is what prose optimizing for a rubric looks like.
- **Uniform four-step moral gradients** — shared / accurate-led / curated / concealed; public / witnessed / quiet / false-blame; declared / hesitated / discovered / concealed. The same virtue-slider re-skinned per quest. Players will learn the slider by Act I and stop reading the fiction, because the options are legible as grades.
- **Suspicious tidiness** — 63 households, 7 children, 4 tables, 4 teams, 4 sponsors, 3 routes, 3 cabinets, 3 delegates, 3 galleries. Nothing ragged, nothing left over. Reality (and the illusion of it) needs remainders.
- **Minor bugs that show the lathe slipping:** "Silkooks" for "Silkhooks" in Names Beneath Lime; and **Mika Reed-Kin (Bernard's child) vs. Nika Rimek (Elara's rescued runner) are different characters one letter apart** — rename one before implementation.

The deeper issue: because each bio fully theorizes its character's flaw ("Her shadow is curation"; "warmth becomes evasion"), scenes tend to *illustrate the stated thesis* rather than discover anything. Strong character fiction withholds the diagnosis — we assemble Jaime Lannister ourselves; BoJack refuses to summarize itself until "Free Churro." Design docs may state theses. But scene sketches must contain at least one thing the thesis does not predict, or the player's interpretive work is already done and the story is a proof, not an experience. The rescued sorter in Names Beneath Lime who says the crew "were locked in by the same people she is praising" is thesis-delivery with zero delay; let the player *find* that irony a scene later, un-narrated.

# Part II — Elara Velescar (CMP-01)

## What is genuinely working

The core conception is strong and unusual: a Cheerleader whose warmth is real *and* is the weapon — "using real warmth to decide who counts" is a better companion thesis than most shipped RPGs have. "The house required nothing" is the best sentence in either file. The banter file is excellent almost throughout: the blue cups, "No. Begin the sentence again," the pebble entered as a material witness, "Without the Powder." The Ashira foil arc is the best relationship material she has — "That is the prettier word" is a real scene between two real people. The wager-as-displaced-vulnerability tell is smart characterization. Keep all of it.

## Where it is emotionally flat

**She is never cruel.** Read all eleven pieces: Elara is charming-good or charming-blind, never once charming-*cruel*, in the present tense. Her cruelty is entirely quarantined in backstory summary. A Cheerleader shadow that never wounds anyone on screen with the social weapon is a costume shadow. Compare the scene her archetype is begging for — Box Hill in *Emma*. Emma, tired and status-anxious, is witty *at* Miss Bates — one sentence, huge laugh, real wound — and Knightley's "It was badly done, indeed!" lands because the victim was present, comic, pitiable, and harmless, and because the rebuke comes from the one person whose regard Emma cannot dismiss. Elara has no Box Hill and no Miss Bates: every low-status person in her orbit is too dignified (see Systemic §3) to be woundable by charm. Until the player has *laughed at* one of Elara's cruelties and then watched it land, her redemption has nothing underneath it.

**Her fear is never inflicted on her.** The bio: "an indifferent room can tell her she was never necessary" — fear of social death, correctly Cheerleader. The campaign promises beat 3: "a public loss she cannot style into victory." **No quest delivers it.** The Winter Company's failure state is about *other people* flailing without her; The Patron's Cabinet's crisis is logistical; the coal exchange is her being gently corrected, not excluded. Nowhere in eleven pieces does a room turn on her, laugh at her, or hold an occasion without her that she has to sit through. Cordelia Chase's arc works because the show actually takes the money and the status and makes her wear the prom dress she can't afford — the dethronement happens *on screen and hurts*. Elara's file even loads the gun — "under shame she becomes ceremonially polite and begins speaking for other people" — a beautiful playable tell that no quest ever fires under real pressure. Design the scene where that tell curdles into sabotage-by-etiquette in front of people she needs, and you have her best scene.

**The temptation is asserted, not felt.** "Elara hates how good it sounds" — but the restoration offer is paper the *player* has no reason to want. The strongest version makes the player complicit: attach the offer to something materially urgent (the live grain warrants are already in the neighbouring cabinet — use them; accepting Vardek's terms feeds the district *tonight*, refusing means visible hunger states), and for once do not let three preparation assets dissolve the tradeoff. Every current dilemma in her campaign is soluble by readiness; the files say so ("three assets make every material objective achievable"). A campaign about a woman who always finds the elegant both-and needs exactly one authored moment where there is no both-and, or her signature is never tested.

## Where it is boring

- **Act III duplicates itself.** The Hands That Hold It and No Lady of Velescar are the same quest — protect authentic documents and their bearers from substitution by anonymous agents — played twice consecutively. Merge the coalition material into the finale; spend the freed operation slot on the dethronement/Box Hill material the campaign actually lacks.
- **The romance episode is archetype-blind.** Raven Shelf at Dawn is a generic climb-rescue-camp; run the swap test and it works verbatim for Ylva. Nothing in it engages rooms, witnesses, performance, or being seen — her entire register. The radical intimate experience for a woman who lives in front of audiences is *being loved with no room to play to*: strand them (the Blue Ice Cave is already there), remove every exit she could arrange and everyone she could introduce, and let the terror of being un-witnessed be the scene. "Without the Powder" already proves this register works for her; the romance episode currently doesn't know it exists. The bond episode's public claim-board instinct is right — sharpen the pair into a deliberate contrast: bond = witnessed equals, romance = unwitnessed.
- **A Better Wager's race is stakeless by design** ("deliberately free of villains... compulsory moral tests") — good instinct, wrong execution: friendship episodes shouldn't be moral tests, but *play still needs cost* to be funny. Let her lose something petty and public she visibly hates losing (the pebble/wager motif is already there — spend it).

## Where it doesn't make sense

1. **What does Grenzburg know?** She's a convicted grave-robber debtor whose crime is semi-secret in Names Beneath Lime ("Concealment remains possible"), yet she's publicly celebrated at the Yard, and "public scandal" is deferred to winter. The knowledge-state of the city about her conviction is never pinned, and half her arc is reputation. Decide it at recruitment. (Recommended: everyone already knows — "the grave lady" — because managed notoriety gives her social genius something to do from scene one, and turns the winter scandal into a deepening rather than a reveal.)
2. **She never meets one person she personally harmed.** Her diggers, "workers she knew could not safely refuse," are all remote. The Seven Mounds workers are victims of *Branka and Vardek's channels*, not of her — the campaign quietly swaps in fresh victims so that she performs restitution toward people she never wronged. "Her respect becomes real only when it survives refusal" — but refusal from strangers is cheap. Plant one local: an adult child of one of her Virelian diggers, working the Ledger Quays, who recognizes her, refuses her coin, refuses her introduction, and — crucially — refuses to perform either anger or forgiveness for the rest of the game. An unresolvable person, not a quest. (Remote Claims Standard survives: he's downstream of the harm, not from her circle.)
3. **Vardek has no legible scheme.** Why does he spend a fortune restoring the scapegoat who is also the chief witness against him? The pieces exist — restored, she'd be his client again, socially incapable of testifying without destroying herself; the offer is witness-tampering wearing a gift's clothes — but no file says it, so his agents' Acts I–III activity reads as weather. One paragraph of stated strategy plus one surveillance-intimate letter (he knows her new friends' names) turns him from plot-device into presence without ever entering the map.
4. **Why are Velescar's land rolls, title seal, and burial provenance in a vault under Grenzburg?** The house is in Virelia. "Medori bought the portfolio" is gestured at but never sold; as written, the documents teleported to wherever the dungeon was. One sentence of chain-of-custody in House Velescar fixes it.
5. **Sixty-three households conduct authenticated multi-round correspondence across a war zone during a siege winter.** The campaign's central artery is postal, and the post is heroic. Either thin the traffic (one packet, arrived before the siege closed, delayed in Bellandi's backlog — which also adds a deadline) or hang a lampshade on the cost of getting it through.

## Archetype audit (against the references)

Her Cheerleader core is correctly built: desire (exemplary belonging), fear (public exclusion), currency (making people feel chosen), shadow (curation) all match the reference cleanly — arguably too cleanly; she is the reference with a rapier. The blended doc offers the sharpening tool the flat archetype lacks. Two candidates:

- **Cheerleader → Tomboy, "the Athletic Social Captain."** The reference's distortion line — *"If I earned my place under these rules, the rules must be fair"* — is precisely her sabre-right noble logic and would give her creed a sentence. Its stress behaviour ("tightens discipline and controls presentation") matches her ceremonial-politeness tell; its shadow ("cannot tolerate another woman at the centre") supplies exactly the present-tense cruelty her campaign is missing — aim it near Ilyana or a guest hostess in the Winter Company.
- **Cheerleader → Bohemian, "the Cultural Curator"** ("I will make belonging beautiful, exciting and culturally meaningful"; shadow: commodification) matches her ribbon-tying, expedition-naming, occasion-making self.

Recommendation: declare Tomboy secondary formally (the physical daring is already in the Delver-Duelist kit; "mud is a condition, not a philosophy" is already Tomboy-integrated language), and steal the Captain's distortion line and centre-of-the-room jealousy for her Act II material.

## Priority fixes for Elara

1. Write the Box Hill scene — one witnessed present-tense cruelty, one Knightley (Ashira or Brina), placed late Act I or Act II.
2. Stage the dethronement — a real public loss she cannot style, using the ceremonial-politeness tell under fire. This is the campaign's promised beat 3; it currently doesn't exist.
3. Add the unresolvable local victim (digger's kin at the Quays).
4. Make the restoration offer cost the player something immediate; refuse the both-and once.
5. Merge Act III's two document quests; rebuild the romance episode around witness-deprivation.
6. Give Vardek a stated scheme and one surveillance-intimate letter.
7. Pin the city's knowledge of her crime at recruitment.

# Part III — Bernard Valeon (CMP-05)

## What is genuinely working

The compulsion system is the best-designed thing in either campaign — visible tells, finite authored pool, Things Not Mine as a non-fungible conscience-inventory, no player-property theft, "the owner of the missing object is never the punchline." That is shippable design. The spoon falling out of the boot is the best cold open in the corpus, and "No joke. Not for this. She did not take that spoon." is exactly the register the character needs. The banter file is superb ("Water with a grievance"; "You may not count his shame as part of the ration"; "Prayer, company, or quiet?"; "My shame has acquired excellent handwriting"). The Zafir foil trilogy is the best sustained two-hander in the project — the Label Board scene ends with both facts standing, unreconciled, which is precisely what the Dialogue Standard asks for and almost nothing else achieves. A Blade Freely Given's core idea (the first object he may simply receive) is right. Keep all of it.

## Where it is emotionally flat

**His arc ended before the game started.** Walk the Froststep record: he confessed voluntarily, led the order to all four caches, accepted a fair investigation, signed the bond without contesting it, and the hospice holds "no secret scandal." By the standards his own campaign applies, Bernard arrives *already resolved* on the big question; what remains is a habit — small relapses, jokes at the wrong moment. A habit can absolutely carry an arc (recidivism is human and under-written in games), but a habit's native dramatic scale is a spoon, and the campaign keeps inflating it to warehouse scale: four shelters, three corridors, hospice logistics, sponsor negotiations. The best beats in his files are all spoon-scale (the boot, the mitten's child that wants *the* mitten, the blanket); the finales are all quartermaster-scale. Either give the drama back to the object scale, or give him something still unconfessed — one fact the fair investigation missed — so that present-tense discovery stakes exist at all. Right now nothing about Froststep can ever surprise anyone.

**His thefts are emotionally safe by system law.** The incident pool excludes player property, companions' relationship objects, quest items — in practice, *anyone with a claim on his heart*. Every theft is from a stranger or an institution, so every return circuit is administratively poignant and personally weightless. "The owner is never the punchline" was right; it over-corrected into "the owner is never someone we love." Dostoevsky's "An Honest Thief" hurts because Emelyan steals from the one man who shelters him — the theft *is* the relationship. Authored exception needed: one late incident whose owner is beloved (Hannel, Mika, Oda, the player's gift), so a return circuit finally costs him a face he has to keep looking at.

**Theft-as-love is never staged.** His own controlling excuse — "a little hidden away... then I can be generous with the rest" — says the hoard was always *for* people. The unifying, heartbreaking gesture is therefore Bernard stealing *for someone*: the last warming salve hidden for Tova or Mika while a stranger's child goes without, exposed by the count he built himself. That is Froststep truly re-run — love as theft, generosity and harm as one motion — and no quest ever stages it. The Open Hospice's hinge should be exactly this, with a face on it, instead of an unmarked Church crate.

**The silence after the joke is never staged.** His archetype file names his humiliation precisely: "the silence after the joke, when others may judge or discard him." Eleven pieces, and it never happens — his jokes always land, or he wisely withholds them. One scene: a room that needed the joke, the joke failing, nobody laughing, and Bernard having to remain in the room as himself. Ted Lasso works because the show eventually stages the panic *under* the charm on screen; Bernard's fear stays in his frontmatter.

## Where it is boring

- **The reserve-declaration beat runs five times.** Little Things Missing (caches), The Long Winter Table (packet under the table), Embers Without Excuse (salve and flasks), Watch Until Morning (urge at the chest), The Open Hospice (crate). It is the correct thematic spine and it is the *same scene* five times with escalating logistics. His bio lists other expressions of the same wound that never get a quest at all: "becomes indispensable so nobody can impose a boundary" and "makes a joke before the harmed person has finished speaking" and "treats forgiveness as the natural reward for confession." Indispensability alone could carry an act — a hospice drill fails *because* every procedure secretly routes through Bernard, and the fix is his removal, which he experiences as abandonment. That's a fresh dramatization of the same flaw, and it's sitting unused in his own character sheet.
- **Acts II and III are one quest at two scales.** Long Winter Table ≈ Beds Before Banners ≈ Open Hospice: build shared care network, sponsors try to own it, declare stores, defend against attack. Keep Long Winter Table (his best operation — the folding table becoming stretcher litters is the strongest structural image in his campaign), fold Beds Before Banners' negotiation into the finale's first movement, and free the Act III operation slot. (The rank tablet under the floor of Beds Before Banners is a naked excuse to add combat to a zoning meeting; cut it without grief.)
- **The bear finale doesn't involve his flaw — but the autonomous version does.** In the unplayed fallback, Bernard "carries an undeclared food reserve and delays naming the danger." That's the dramatic version! In the played version his compulsion touches the finale only through an optional prep flag, and the crisis is pure logistics. The engaged player gets the *safer* story than the absent one — exactly backwards. Wire the reserve into the played crisis causally: the group's count is short because of his hidden portion, a shelter decision gets made on bad numbers, a child's minor injury traces to it, and his supper confession costs him something in front of children who trusted the count. Froststep at child scale, on screen.

## Where it doesn't make sense

1. **The Froststep causal chain is fuzzy.** His caches were at the hospice (bakehouse floor, road-wall niche, stable box, bedding); the *sled* went out understocked. The implied chain — his concealment falsified the store count, so the manifest promised goods that were never loaded — works, but no document states it, and a reader who has to reconstruct the causality of the founding guilt will wobble on it. Two sentences in The Froststep Evacuation fix this permanently.
2. **The institutions behave as if his record is weightless.** A convicted relief-supply thief is routed by Medori into kitchen and store work, hospital supply escort, and ultimately founding and provisioning a hospice. Brina's witnessed-stores rule acknowledges the record once; nothing else does. Missing entirely: the scene where a donor, a patient's family, or the Church discovers who he is and pulls out — the ordinary social cost of a known past that the game keeps waiving. One withdrawal scene would do more for realism than every disclaimer in his bio.
3. **The bio argues with an absent lawyer.** "The order permits marriage. Bernard took no vow of chastity. He was never ordained." — three consecutive disclaimers solving a romance-compliance worry inside a character document. Move to a design note; the bio should carry voice, not liability language.
4. **White Flame is over-disclaimed and under-dramatized.** Six-plus repetitions of "kindling never certifies innocence," and not one scene of the actually interesting thing: *the crowd misreading it.* Visible grace in a religious society will be read as acquittal whether the metaphysics agree or not. Stage it once — the flame catches during the infirmary defence, a steward waves off his confession attempt, a mother asks him to bless her child, Deacon-level clergy start calling the debt divinely annulled, and Bernard has to fight to stay guilty in public while Petra's unopened packet sits in his coat. That converts six pages of doctrine into one unforgettable irony, and it is the *only* version of the theme that needs the player present.
5. **Petra is a record type.** She lost two fingers and retains cold pain; that's the totality of her personhood on the page, and her response to the central restitution of the campaign is a three-value field. See Systemic §2 — she is the single highest-value beneficiary of an actual written letter, dictated in someone else's hand.

## Archetype audit (against the references)

Goofball core is correct and unusually well-integrated: desire (worth through warmth and resilience), fear (serious exposure), shadow (evasion via charm) all check out, and — rare in this corpus — the *system* (compulsion tells, Things Not Mine) mechanically embodies the archetype rather than decorating it.

Two gaps against the reference:

- The reference's Goofball is "least drawn to symbolic domination and most forgiving of ordinary human weakness" — present — but his shadow list includes "manipulation through feigned incompetence," which Bernard never does and profitably could: a Bernard who plays dumber than he is to dodge a store audit is a colder, truer note than another hidden packet.
- The blended doc's **Goofball → Jock, "the Hidden Heavyweight"** (Po, Sokka, Mater — his exact comparison set) is already his physique and party role; adopt it formally. Its payload: stress behaviour "louder, then abruptly focused and physically decisive" (matches and sharpens his humor-vanishes-at-danger tell), and — the valuable part — its shadow: *"training feels oppressive; responsibility causes resentment."* **Resentment is the note Bernard's entire campaign is missing.** He is currently inexhaustibly willing. One scene of a tired Bernard resenting being the one who always cooks, always carries, always absorbs the room's fear — snapping at someone who didn't deserve it, then having to repair *that* — would be the most human minute in his twelve hours.

One more missing scene the archetype demands: **differential grace.** The dark side of being the beloved fool is that the world refuses to punish you. Stage it: a wronged stallholder publicly forgives Bernard *because* he's funny and everyone loves him — waves it off, makes it a story — while Hannel, charmless and poor, was confined and docked wages for less in the same act. Bernard watches the double standard operate in his favour and has to decide whether to insist on his own consequence. Falstaff inverted: the tragedy isn't "I know thee not, old man" — it's that nobody in Grenzburg will ever say it, so he must say it to himself.

## Priority fixes for Bernard

1. Rebuild the Open Hospice hinge as theft-for-a-beloved with a face, not an unmarked crate.
2. Wire the undeclared reserve causally into the played Act I finale (the autonomous version already knows how).

# Part IV — The shortest path

If only five things get done across both characters:

1. **Write the letters** (Petra, Radena, Vardek). Highest emotional yield per hour of work in the entire corpus.
2. **Give each companion one present-tense sin the player witnesses** — Elara's Box Hill cruelty, Bernard's beloved-theft. Both redemptions currently ask the player to take the crime on faith and paperwork.
3. **Give each a local, unresolvable harmed person.** Refusal from strangers is currently doing work it cannot do.
4. **Break one both-and per campaign.** One dilemma preparation cannot dissolve; one loss no warning precedes.
5. **De-synchronize the campaign shapes** — Elara's social-shaped (rise, fall, rise), Bernard's spiral-shaped (relapse, repair, relapse smaller) — so the lattice stops showing through the fiction.

The material underneath is good. The characters have real theses, the banter proves the voices exist, and the design constitutions are unusually self-aware. What the formal campaigns need is permission to be *unfair, untidy, and personal* in the specific ways the current architecture — remote pasts, dissolvable dilemmas, enlightened strangers, symmetrical lattices — has engineered out.
