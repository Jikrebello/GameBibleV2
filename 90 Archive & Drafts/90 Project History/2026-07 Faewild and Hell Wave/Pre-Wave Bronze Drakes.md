# Bronze Drakes

Bronze drakes are the Braided Court's reed-bed ambushers, formed where land, river, and salt-water law overlap. Their armor weathers from dark bronze to green-brown plates, making a resting adult difficult to distinguish from drowned masonry or a root-tangled wreck.

## Ecology and Lifecycle

They nest above the reach of ordinary tides but close enough to hunt both channels and dry ground. Fish, waterfowl, marsh pigs, carrion, and ferry animals form the normal diet. A bronze drake tolerates brackish water better than any other lineage and often follows a changing estuary inland over several generations.

## Signs

- reed lanes pressed flat without hoofprints;
- fish traps emptied but left carefully closed;
- green-black scales caught on pilings;
- livestock dragged into water and wedged beneath roots;
- long periods when birds and frogs go silent around one channel.

## Threat Pattern

Bronze drakes wait, isolate, and strike from concealment. They overturn small boats, use mud and current to split groups, and retreat through routes too shallow for larger pursuers. They defend eggs and heart-gem beds with exceptional persistence but usually abandon prey beyond their wetland boundary.

## Materials and Use

Weathered scale resists salt and damp, making it valuable for instrument cases, ferry fittings, and specialist armor. Heart-gems hold the Braided Court's boundary law and are dangerous when installed in canals or floodworks without a keeper who understands what boundary they will enforce.

## Story Hooks

- A new embankment has shifted a nesting channel into the only safe ferry route.
- Poachers steal an egg and the pursuing drake follows their scent into a pilgrim town.
- A bronze drake is keeping a residue-fed marsh predator out of occupied wetlands.
- A drained estuary exposes an old clutch beneath an Atherian road.
