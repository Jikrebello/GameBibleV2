# Gold Drakes

Gold drakes are the sun-cliff lineage associated with [[Father Sun]], heat shimmer, exposure, ordered glare, and hoarded reflective metal. Their scales resemble sacred gold, but the resemblance is court livery rather than holiness. A gold drake may be beautiful, ecologically important, dangerous, or—in the rare elder case—morally accountable without becoming a divine messenger.

## Range and Signs

Gold drakes favor exposed limestone ridges, high quarries, dry sea cliffs, ruined observatories, bright badlands, and slopes where stone holds the day's heat. They require water but avoid deep shade and enclosed wet ground.

Common signs include:

- bleached or heat-polished stone around a regular basking shelf;
- coins, buttons, foil, bells, tools, or mirror fragments sorted by brightness;
- livestock taken at exposed crossings near noon;
- shade-loving animals abandoning one side of a ridge;
- repeated heat shimmer on cool mornings;
- pale gold scale dust inside cracks and high gutters;
- wild plants opening toward an empty point rather than the sun.

## Ecology

Gold drakes eat meat, eggs, large insects, carrion, and mineral salts. They do not eat precious metal. Reflective objects help mark territory, distribute heat, attract particular court insects, and create confusing approach lines around the nest.

Adults usually maintain solitary territories. A breeding pair shares a ridge only through tightly divided basking and hunting hours. One to three eggs are laid in a mineral-lined clutch hollow. Hatchlings remain near heated stone and feed largely on insects before becoming active predators.

Their presence can reduce large carrion feeders and rival predators. Their glare-law may also keep damp corruption or shade-bound court growth from crossing an exposed ridge. Removing one therefore changes more than the local food chain.

## Creature Role

- **Encounter shape:** aerial and climbing territorial predator, glare hazard, lair guardian, or regional pressure.
- **Opening behavior:** watch from heat shimmer, isolate movement in open ground, and make a fast test strike.
- **Tactics:** use reflected glare, high shelves, heated stone, tail sweeps, and short dives rather than remaining in a crowded melee.
- **Retreat:** a non-nesting animal withdraws after severe injury if it retains a safe route. A nesting adult draws pursuers away or attacks until the clutch is no longer reachable.
- **Lair:** multiple sun shafts, at least two exits, a warm clutch hollow, reflective midden, water fissure, and one court-stone aligned with the law-gem.

Gold drakes are not naturally obedient. Food can create routines. Pain can create panic. Neither creates loyal military service.

## Territorial Law

A gold-drake territory favors exposure:

- shadows shorten or become poor hiding places at particular hours;
- polished objects turn subtly toward the nesting ridge;
- sounds from struck metal carry farther in clear light;
- heat accumulates along repeated paths;
- concealed rot, tracks, and physical flaws become easier to see.

The effect is not moral revelation. It exposes surfaces and patterns, not guilt or truth. Cults that interpret every revealed flaw as judgement confuse a predator's court ecology with divine justice.

## Rare Elders

Like any drake lineage, an exceptionally old gold drake may cross the elder threshold described in [[Drakes Overview]]. Gold elders often organize memory through repeated reflections, seasonal returns, metal arrangements, and the sound of bells across territory.

An elder can speak, bargain, deceive, preserve a grievance, and choose how to act upon it. It remains a gold drake rather than becoming a true dragon. [[Sovarath-of-the-Open-Noon]] demonstrates both the power and moral danger of such an emergence.

Signs of true elder sapience include abstract language, plans extending beyond immediate territory, recognition of individual responsibility, invented offices, deliberate restraint, and adjustment to arguments rather than conditioned cues.

## Law-Gem and Materials

- **Gold law-gem:** stores exposure, heat distribution, reflective alignment, and—in an elder—organized autobiographical memory.
- **Scales:** useful in glare-resistant lenses, heat shields, and instruments that reveal surface defects.
- **Eye membranes:** restricted material for anti-glare medicine and dangerous observation devices.
- **Gland salts:** used in kiln control, signal lights, and heat alchemy.
- **Eggshell:** distributes warmth and is sought by incubator makers and counterfeit relic dealers.
- **Midden metal:** ordinary material altered by long alignment; useful for mirrors and warning bells but unstable if removed in bulk.

Extraction from a living drake is fatal or mutilating. After a necessary kill, court-specific law may require stabilizing the gem within the lair before any salvage occurs.

## Human and Institutional Responses

- **Eclessian authorities:** license defensive hunts, dispute trophy ownership, and often require clergy or natural philosophers to witness law-gem custody.
- **Folk courts:** emphasize what local law the drake was holding and may require return of the heart-gem to the land.
- **Warlocks:** value gems as heat and exposure anchors; responsible schools prefer recovered defensive kills over commissioned hunting.
- **Collectors and cults:** pay heavily for sacred-looking scales, creating poaching and false-dragon legends.

## Encounter Variations

- a juvenile following kiln smoke into a craft town;
- a nesting adult whose hunting route crosses a new road;
- a wounded drake whose collapsing law exposes a hidden damp corruption;
- a poached clutch drawing repeated attacks against the wrong settlement;
- a rare elder whose legitimate territorial claim has become the foundation for chosen tyranny.

## Game Hooks

- A church bell contains aligned midden metal and calls a drake every clear noon.
- Quarry workers expose clutch stone after the old animal has migrated, and three factions claim the site.
- A supposed saint-sign is a drake territory whose glare reveals physical evidence but cannot judge its meaning.
- A gold law-gem stabilizes a hospital kiln while slowly stripping shade and sleep from the surrounding ward.
- A speaking elder offers a limited peace whose material terms are reasonable and whose human terms are evil.
