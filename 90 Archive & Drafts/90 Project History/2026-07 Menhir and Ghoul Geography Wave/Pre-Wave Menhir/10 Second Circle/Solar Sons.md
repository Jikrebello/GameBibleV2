## Star Children of Father Sun (Solzareth)

### Aurion

**Appearance / Manifestation**
 Aurion is seen at the first thin boundary where darkness stops being dominant. In the Faewild, Aurion manifests as a low, blade-bright line on the horizon that seems to “cut” fog and dew into retreat. In material lands, the sign is simpler: a sudden sharpening of edges, the moment when forms become legible again. Animals behave as if an invisible rule has been enacted.

**Core Function**
 Aurion governs dawn/ daybreak, the transition where night collapses and day becomes active. The repeatable opening of the day’s operating state—wakefulness, orientation, first movement, first pursuit.

**Omens / Phenomena**
 Common signs include dew withdrawing in a visible line, birds beginning in coordinated waves rather than scattered calls, and nocturnal predators “dropping out” as if a signal has sounded. In the Faewild, travelers may see short-lived “dawn paths”—routes that exist only during first light and vanish once the sun clears the horizon.

**Common Observances**
 Aurion is acknowledged by alignment rather than prayer: creatures and cultures that mark Aurion do so by **timing**—beginning migrations, hunts, departures, or openings precisely at first light. In wild places, cairns and markers at ridge-lines are set to catch the first ray, not as worship sites, but as calibration points.

### Zenithra

**Appearance / Manifestation**
 Zenithra manifests as the sense that the air has become heavy with directness. Shadows shrink into hard, minimal shapes; surfaces shimmer; the world looks flatter and less forgiving. In the Faewild, Zenithra may appear as a tall, still figure whose outline is difficult to hold—heat-haze forming a humanoid suggestion that never fully resolves.

**Core Function**
 Zenithra governs noon/ midday: the point in a day when exposure becomes costliest and concealment becomes territory. This is noon as geometry—angle, intensity, reflection, and the ecological behaviors that emerge under maximum overhead light.

**Omens / Phenomena**
 Zenithra’s presence is marked by mirage bands forming over ground, insects going still in exposed places, and animals choosing routes that cling to shade lines as if those lines were walls. In some regions, water surfaces briefly show “false depth”—a visual distortion that causes creatures to misjudge distance and risk.

**Common Observances**
 Zenithra is observed through **avoidance and design**: burrows deepen, canopies are respected, and travel shifts to edges and shade corridors. Where people are part of the ecology, they build narrow streets, overhangs, and high walls—not as devotion, but as behavioral adaptation to Zenithra’s pressure.

### Heliox

**Appearance / Manifestation**
 Solcarion is the Sun’s sudden excess rendered as a presence: a day that turns briefly too white, shadows that look washed-out, and a sky that seems nearer than it should. In the Faewild, Solcarion can appear as a crown-like bloom in the upper air—an over-bright rupture that fades before it can be stared at directly.

**Core Function**
 Solcarion governs **solar burst events**—short-lived surges that override ordinary daylight behavior. Where Solzareth is the enduring rule, Solcarion is the exception-state: an interruption that forces rapid recalibration in navigation, behavior, and sheltering.

**Omens / Phenomena**
 Signs include auroral veils appearing at incorrect latitudes, animals veering from known corridors, and water tasting faintly metallic after “white days.” In the Faewild, the air may briefly hum with a high, thin resonance, and insects may drop from flight as if their internal compass has been reset.

**Common Observances**
 Solcarion is observed through **hard sheltering and stillness**. Creatures seek deep shade, under-rock spaces, and dense canopy; movement slows. Where sentient ecologies exist, observances take the form of blackout practices—extinguishing reflective surfaces, covering bright metal, staying off open water—actions that reduce exposure during the burst window.
