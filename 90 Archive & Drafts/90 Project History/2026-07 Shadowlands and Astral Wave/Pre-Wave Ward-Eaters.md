# Ward-Eaters

## Classification

Small arcane leak-predators formed where layered wards shed rejected instructions into black-spire residue.

## Signs

- protective chalk ends in neat semicircular bites;
- locked rooms open while their physical locks remain intact;
- canary-lamps burn colorless near corners and doorframes;
- tiny glassy droppings contain reversed fragments of written sigils;
- household charms fail from the outermost layer inward.

## Appearance

A Ward-Eater resembles a hairless, six-limbed lizard made from translucent ash and slivers of black glass. Its mouth opens along the length of its throat. Script-light moves through its belly after feeding.

## Ecology

They do not reproduce biologically. Dense, contradictory ward systems produce them when discarded magical instructions acquire enough residue to seek more of the same. Most starve within hours outside an arcane district. Vorthara gives them an abundant food web.

## Behavior and Tactics

- They avoid unwarded living creatures unless cornered.
- They consume protective barriers, magical locks, circles, and prepared counterspells.
- A fed Ward-Eater discharges the last ward consumed when injured.
- Packs open paths for larger contained threats without understanding what follows.
- They retreat toward active ward networks and can be lured with disposable seal-strips.

## Practical Response

Leak-wardens isolate a pack with mundane shutters and stone barriers, then bait it into a ceramic proof-box. Casting more protection around civilians usually feeds the problem. This reversal makes them particularly dangerous during panic.

## Materials

Fresh belly-glass can reveal the last ward consumed and is valuable to proof-engineers. After a day it becomes inert black grit. Selling live specimens without a leak license is prohibited because a buyer can use them to strip vault protections.

## Quest Uses

- Evidence that an allegedly isolated ward system is projecting beyond its site.
- A vermin job where conventional magical defenses worsen the infestation.
- Deliberate release by thieves before a vault breach.
- A tracking tool: feed one a sample seal and follow it toward matching architecture.
