# The Bragging Vault — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** A rogue Vorthara impresario called the Vaunt has stolen three famous pit-champion weapons and posted a public boast: run his gauntlet under the Ashplume fumarole and the arms are yours to keep. The whole run is scried and sold to arena patrons. It is an audition, and the party did not audition on purpose.

**Beginning:** Three separate parties want the arms back — their registered owners, the arena bond-court, and the Vaunt himself, who wants a new stable more than he wants his stunt to fail. The party takes a recovery job and reaches the boasting door of the [[The Ashplume Vault|Ashplume Vault]].

**Middle:** Three branches, each a broadcast showpiece leading to one arm: the boiling-mud crossing to the dark vault where a [[Vault-Claimed]] champion keeps [[The Trophy-Arms|Cairn]]; the sunken glass bubble in the geothermal pool where a Fleshweaver crab keeps [[The Trophy-Arms|Ovation]]; and the stepped menagerie climb to the fleshwarped champion Gorreth, who is being eaten alive by [[The Trophy-Arms|Forfeit]]. The arms are prizes and liabilities at once.

**End:** Completion is a job offer. The Vaunt congratulates the winners and pitches a partnership. At the same moment the [[The Pale Hand]] under-factor [[Odalie Vench]] files her claim: the Pale Hand holds the mortgage on Ashplume and title to everyone who died below, including the last several teams. The party can take the arms, decline the offer, expose the audition, free or refuse the harvest, and settle the arms' liability histories — but custody, reputation, and debt each resolve on their own terms.

**Tier:** F/W. A faction-ladder audition wrapped around a Witcher-grade knot discovered late — the prize you were hired to fetch is the bait in someone's recruitment drive, and the floor beneath it belongs to necromancers.

**Expected length:** 3-4 substantial sessions: the boast and the way in, the three branches in any order, the champion, and the offer-and-claim finale.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; the Vaunt, [[Corsin Marlo]]; his stage-crew and scouts; the current champion Gorreth in the sword-vault; the Pale Hand under-factor [[Odalie Vench]] and her registrars; the three registered owners of the arms; and the [[Vault-Claimed]] dead who used to be contestants.
- **What:** retrieve one, two, or all three [[The Trophy-Arms|Trophy-Arms]], decide who is owed which arm, and reckon with the audition and the mortgage the recovery job never mentioned.
- **Where:** the Ashplume fumarole in Vorthara's geothermal district, a live vent shaped into a broadcast gauntlet with three branches, a slick corridor, against-current steam flumes, and a Cinder-Bound exit toll.
- **Why the party acts:** a paying recovery contract; a bond-court writ; the Vaunt's open dare; or a fallen contestant's family asking what happened to a team that went in and never came out.
- **Why the vault exists:** the Vaunt is between stables and needs a new headline act. The gauntlet is a talent audition disguised as a heist target, and its footage sells either way.
- **How it escalates:** the arms fight their own recovery, the champion will not surrender the blade eating him, the Pale Hand's claim lands the moment anyone declares victory, and every death during the run becomes collateral the party may be standing on when the offer is made.

## Moral and Metaphysical Ruling

The Vaunt stole registered property and staged a lethal audition without the consent of the people auditioning. Publishing the run for profit does not make the danger a game, and calling it a challenge does not make dying in it a fair loss. His charm is real and his offer may be genuinely good for the party's careers; neither cancels that people have already died to make it.

The Pale Hand's mortgage is legally valid and morally obscene. Holding title to whatever dies below a floor is a clean instrument built to catch desperate people at their worst moment. That the claim is enforceable in Vorthara says something true about Vorthara, not something exculpatory about the claim.

The three arms are dangerous but not evil in the way a person is evil. Forfeit consumes what it kills, Ovation gathers crowds toward its bearer, Cairn carries the dead who held it before. Each was made, sold, wielded, and insured by people who knew what it did. The liability for their histories sits with the market that traded them, not only with the last hand on the grip.

The [[Vault-Claimed]] are people. They contested the gauntlet, lost, and were raised under a clause they never read closely. Their present silhouette is a guard's, or a trophy's, or a set-piece's. It does not lower their claim to release, and defeating them a second time is not the same as freeing them.

The comedy of the piece is Vortharan swagger meeting its own machinery — a scried funhouse, a crab that knows better than the intruders do, a champion too proud to drop the knife killing him, and a host who congratulates you while a registrar counts the bodies behind him. The stakes underneath stay real.

## The Central Truth

Corsin Marlo built his name in Vorthara as a promoter, not a fighter. He discovered great champions, dressed their bouts as stories, and sold the spectacle to the factions and private patrons who bankroll the arenas. His last stable left him — some to rivals, some to the Fleshweavers, one to the grave — and a promoter without fighters is a voice without a stage.

Marlo needed a new stable and a headline to launch it. So he engineered both at once. Working through fences and a lapsed champion-broker's license, he acquired three of the most famous [[The Trophy-Arms|arms]] in the circuit — arms with such ugly liability histories that their registered owners were quietly relieved to have them stolen and were happy to post recovery bounties rather than admit what the weapons had done in their keeping. Marlo planted the arms at the far end of the Ashplume gauntlet and published a boast in the old style, daring anyone to come and take them.

He does not care whether the arms are recovered. He cares who recovers them, and how. Every run is scried and broadcast; every contestant is being scouted. The party is not raiding a lair. They are the finalists in an audition, and the prize for winning is that the Vaunt asks them to sign.

What Marlo underpriced was the floor. To fund construction cheaply he took a development loan from the [[The Pale Hand]], secured against the vent itself — and the Pale Hand's standard sub-floor clause grants it title to any body that dies below the mortgaged level. Several teams have already run the gauntlet and not come out. Their bodies did not vanish. Under-factor [[Odalie Vench]] raised them as [[Vault-Claimed]] guards and set-pieces, folding the labor cost of the dungeon into the mortgage. Marlo's audition is now staffed by its own failed applicants, and the moment anyone declares victory, Vench's claim files against everyone who fell to make that victory possible — the party included, if they fall.

## The Vaunt's Stable and the Pale Hand

### Canon Authority Ruling

Ashplume is governed by a compact among the Vaunt's stable, the Pale Hand lender, neighboring pit patrons, and the arena shrine whose Bishop-of-Bouts arbitrates their disputes. The bond-court is this compact's tribunal. It can impound the Trophy-Arms and void its own mortgage clauses, but it is neither Vorthara's government nor a court with universal city jurisdiction.

The [[Corsin Marlo|Vaunt's operation]] is not yet an institution — it is one impresario, a stage-crew, a broker's lapsed license, and a scried gauntlet. That is exactly its danger and its appeal: Marlo is building the stable out of whoever wins, and a party that signs becomes the founding act of a rising arena house with real money behind it.

The [[The Pale Hand]] is the canon necromantic faction of Vorthara, taboo but indispensable, its Black-Mud craft tied to the Adversary and its Reapers a fixture of the arenas. Here the Pale Hand is not raiding anyone. It simply lent money and wrote a clause, and the clause is doing the raiding for it. Vench is a mid-rank factor turning a construction loan into a body-farm, and she would genuinely prefer the party sign, win, and leave quietly so the harvest can continue.

## Adventure States

- **Broadcast Heat (0-4):** how invested the scried audience is. Rises with spectacle, clever solutions, and mercy shown on camera. High Heat makes the Vaunt's offer richer and the Pale Hand's claim more publicly awkward.
- **Vault Toll (0-4):** how hard the gauntlet fights to keep its arms. Each recovered arm raises it; at 4 the Cinder-Bound exit toll is unavoidable without a bargain.
- **Contestant Trust (-2 to +2):** whether the Vault-Claimed dead and any surviving prior contestants resist, stand neutral, or help.
- **Proof of the Audition (0-3):** scrying rigs, the scout's grading tally, and the broker's-license paper that ties the "theft" to Marlo.
- **Proof of the Mortgage (0-3):** the loan seal, the sub-floor clause, and the raised-contestant register that shows the guards are people.

## The Ways Through

The adventure supports several throughlines from the first hour, and most groups will braid them.

### Recover and Deliver

Run the branches, take one to three arms, and hand them to whoever hired the party. Clean on its face; it ignores the audition, the harvest, and what the arms will do in their next owner's hands.

### Win the Audition

Beat the vault well enough that the Vaunt makes the offer, then negotiate from strength — a partnership on the party's terms, or the arms as a signing fee, or the leverage to make Marlo dismantle the harvest as a condition of any deal.

### Expose the Show

Gather Proof of the Audition and turn the broadcast against its author: contestants died on camera for a talent search dressed as a dare. Public exposure in Vorthara does not end careers by shame, but it moves the bond-court and rival stables, and it changes what the arms are worth.

### Break the Mortgage

Gather Proof of the Mortgage and the raised-contestant register. Under Vortharan liability custom a sub-floor claim cannot rest on deaths caused by an undisclosed hazard the lender helped build. This can free the Vault-Claimed and void the harvest — and it makes an enemy of the Pale Hand.

### Settle the Arms

Reckon with the arms themselves: return each to a rightful claimant, destroy the ones no one should hold, or keep one under strict terms. Each arm's liability history points to a different just recipient, and none of them is simply "whoever hired you."

## Act I — The Boast

### Beat 1: Three Buyers for One Job

The party is approached about the same recovery from more than one direction, which is the first sign the job is bigger than a theft.

- A **registered owner's agent** (Sera Coyle, for Cairn) offers a clean bounty: bring the arm back, no questions, discretion valued above speed.
- The **arena bond-court** posts a writ through Bishop-of-Bouts Halric Onn to impound all three arms as contested property. This pays in standing, not coin, and comes with the right to enter Ashplume legally.
- **The Vaunt's own handbill** is pasted across the geothermal district in gilt letters: a public dare to retrieve the arms from his vault, framed as sport, signed with a flourish. The handbill is bait and everyone in Vorthara knows it and goes anyway.

Whichever thread the party pulls, the others are visible. A group that notices three separate parties want the same three objects has already learned the useful thing: the arms are hot, and their owners are more relieved than robbed.

### Beat 2: The Liability Histories

Any competent inquiry — a bond-court clerk, a champion-broker, a talkative armorer — turns up what the arms actually are, and why "stolen" is a convenient word for each.

- **Forfeit**, the victory-eater, has outlived four wielders in six years. Its last owner shelved it after a bout where it took the loser and then would not let the winner set it down.
- **Ovation**, the crowd-binder, made a mediocre fighter a sensation and then a demagogue; its owner sold the fighter's contract and reported the arm stolen the same week.
- **Cairn**, the memory-maul, is a roll-call of dead champions. Its registered owner used it to kill a servant and called it a training accident; the arm remembers otherwise, and the inquest died when the maul "vanished."

This is Proof-adjacent material: it tells the party that returning an arm to its "rightful owner" may be handing a documented weapon back to the person it should testify against.

### Beat 3: The Geothermal District

Ashplume sits in Vorthara's geothermal district, among the vents and fumaroles that heat the Flesh Pits and drive the arcane forges. The approach is industrial: pipe-galleries, steam-tolls, Crafters' Union valve-crews, and the constant low commerce of a city that sells heat.

The party can learn, before entering, that:

- the vent was dark for years and reopened suddenly under a new leaseholder;
- the leaseholder took a development loan the district gossips about because of who lent it;
- teams have gone in on the Vaunt's dare and not come back out, and the handbills keep going up anyway.

### Act I Output

The party knows the arms are liabilities, that at least three parties want them, and that the "dare" has already killed people. They enter Ashplume by dare, by writ, or by bounty — and every entry is on camera.

## Act II — The Ashplume Vault

### The Boasting Door

The [[The Ashplume Vault|Ashplume Vault]] opens through a bronze door cast in the Vaunt's own smiling likeness. It recites his boast aloud to anyone who approaches, grades their entrance with theatrical scoring, and will not open until the party gives it a *good enough show* — a clever line, a display of skill, a wager taken. This is the audition's opening question stated as a lock: are you worth watching?

Scrying-eyes are set into the door's crown. From here on, the run is broadcast. Sharp players notice the eyes; what they do about being watched raises or lowers Broadcast Heat.

### The Junction and the Three Branches

Inside, a heat-shimmering junction splits three ways under painted banners naming the arms. The promise is honest: three branches, three prizes, any order. Between them run the Vaunt's signature hazards, which a group can stumble into or exploit:

- **The Glazed Run:** a corridor floored in frictionless volcanic glass, studded with steam-pits. Kept from the original as pure play-tested cruelty; here it is dressed as an arena sport the audience bets on.
- **The Against-Current Flumes:** steam-tubes that carry water and riders *upward* against gravity, used by the Vaunt's scout **Wisp Aldery** to appear, grade the party live, and vanish. Catching her is a route to Proof of the Audition.

### Branch A — The Mud Gallery and the Dark Vault (Cairn)

A cavern where the floor is a slow sea of boiling mud, crossed only by brass disks hung on chains, while timed geysers erupt between them. The crossing is the original's set-piece, kept intact: timing, weight, and nerve against scalding.

Beyond it is a vault of permanent conjured darkness. Inside, a [[Vault-Claimed]] champion — a prior contestant raised by the Pale Hand — keeps **Cairn** in the black. It fights with a dead champion's skill because it *is* one, and because Cairn feeds it every wielder the maul remembers.

The fight can end without a second death: light disrupts its keeping, Cairn can be spoken to by name-of-the-roll, and a party that recognizes the guard as a person (Contestant Trust) may talk it down instead of destroying it. Taking Cairn raises Vault Toll and hands the party a maul that will start naming the newly dead.

### Branch B — The Boiling Pool and the Glass Bubble (Ovation)

A geothermal pool of near-boiling water, and sunk in it a great blown-glass bubble with **Ovation** displayed on a plinth inside. A Fleshweaver arena-crab guards the bubble and is careful never to crack it, because it knows the glass is the only thing between everyone and the boil.

The original's question, kept exactly: *the crab is that smart — are you?* Force the bubble and the pool comes in. The intended solutions are patience, misdirection, and pressure-management, not brute force. Ovation, once taken, begins gathering the room's attention toward its bearer — including the attention of things that should be watching something else.

### Branch C — The Stepped Menagerie and the Champion (Forfeit)

An inverted stepped structure rising through the vent's heat, each tier stocked with a dangerous, incongruous beast — Fleshweaver arena-stock and geothermal fauna arranged for spectacle rather than sense, exactly the original's menagerie logic. The climb is a gauntlet of set-piece fights the audience loves.

At the summit is **Gorreth**, the reigning champion of the vault and the current wielder of **Forfeit**. He is fleshwarped, formidable, and losing: the blade has been eating his victories and, lately, him. He cannot set it down — Forfeit does not permit its wielder to disarm after a win, and Gorreth has not lost in months.

Gorreth is a boss and a victim. He can be fought, but he can also be *helped* — a party that understands Forfeit can try to make him lose on purpose, or sever the blade's hold, or simply outlast the thing consuming him. Winning Forfeit means deciding what to do with a knife that will start the same clock on whoever picks it up.

### Act II Output

The party holds one to three arms, has crossed the vault's signature hazards, has met at least one Vault-Claimed guard and perhaps the scout, and has begun to feel the eyes. Vault Toll is now high enough that leaving will not be free.

## Act III — The Offer and the Claim

### The Green Room

Whoever clears the vault is funneled — the geometry makes it feel like escape — into a warm, lamplit chamber that is unmistakably a green room. The Vaunt is waiting, delighted, already applauding.

### The Vaunt's Offer

[[Corsin Marlo]] congratulates the party by name, replays their best moments from the broadcast, and makes his pitch: a founding partnership in a new arena stable, the arms as their signing fee, patrons already lining up because the run has been playing across Vorthara for hours. Broadcast Heat sets how good the offer is and how many patrons are watching him make it.

He is charming, specific, and genuinely offering something valuable. He also does not mention the floor.

### Vench's Claim

At the offer's high point, Pale Hand under-factor [[Odalie Vench]] enters with a registrar and files. She presents the mortgage on Ashplume and the sub-floor clause: the Pale Hand holds title to everyone who died below the mortgaged level. That includes the last several teams — now the guards the party fought — and, she notes without heat, it would include the party, had they fallen.

The three threads now stand exposed and separate:

- **Custody:** the party physically holds the arms.
- **Reputation:** the broadcast made them a rising act, whether they sign or not.
- **Debt:** the floor beneath the whole show belongs to the Pale Hand, and the harvest is legal.

Marlo did not want Vench to say this in front of his patrons. Vench did not want Marlo recruiting the labor she is trying to keep. The party can widen that seam.

### The Fault Between Them

The Vaunt and the Pale Hand are not allies; they are borrower and lender, and each has something to lose:

- Marlo is behind on the loan and cannot afford the harvest to become the story of his launch.
- Vench cannot afford the raised-contestant register to become public, because a sub-floor claim built on an undisclosed lender-built hazard will not survive the bond-court.

A party holding Proof of the Audition and Proof of the Mortgage can play these against each other: force Marlo to fund the release of the Vault-Claimed as the price of keeping his patrons; force Vench to void the harvest to keep the clause; or bring both to Bishop-of-Bouts Onn and let the court carve them apart.

## Act IV — Leaving Ashplume

### The Cinder-Bound Toll

Leaving with the arms is the original's exit challenge, kept and re-rooted. The vent's heat is worked by [[Cinder-Bound]] — fire-spirits bound into the geothermal district's service, here set as an exit toll. At Vault Toll 3-4, carrying two or three arms out lights the toll: the Cinder-Bound demand payment for removing prizes the vent was built to display.

Payment need not be blood. The toll can be met with coin, with a wager, with an equal spectacle, or by releasing the Cinder-Bound from a binding the party can prove is expired — the district's valve-crews keep the seals, and a sharp group can turn a fight into a manumission. Fighting them is possible and expensive; the vent does not want to lose its show.

### If It Comes to Violence

If the finale turns to force, the vault becomes the Vaunt's weapon: he opens flumes, drops the Glazed Run's pits, and lets the geysers and the boiling pool do the work while the Cinder-Bound hold the exits. He is a promoter, not a fighter, and would rather the party win a *good fight* on camera than die dully — which is a lever, if the party realizes the audience is the thing he cannot switch off.

## Ways to Resolve the Conflict

### Take the Arms and Go

Deliver to the bounty-poster, the bond-court, or keep them. Fast and defensible; leaves the audition running and the harvest legal. The arms go on hurting people in their next hands.

### Sign — On Terms

Accept the Vaunt's partnership as leverage rather than surrender. Make dismantling the harvest, releasing the Vault-Claimed, or clearing the arms' liability histories a written condition. The party becomes an arena house with power to change what the arenas do — and a promoter who will test every clause.

### Void the Harvest

Present Proof of the Mortgage and the raised-contestant register to Bishop-of-Bouts Onn. The court suspends the sub-floor clause as resting on an undisclosed lender-built hazard; the Vault-Claimed become wards owed restoration, not property. The Pale Hand becomes a lasting enemy and a discredited one.

### Burn the Show

Publish Proof of the Audition. The broadcast turns: contestants died for a talent search sold as a dare. Rival stables and the bond-court move; the arms' value shifts; Marlo's launch becomes a scandal he must survive rather than a triumph.

### Free the Champion

Break Forfeit's hold on Gorreth before taking the blade — sever the win-lock, make him lose on purpose, or destroy the arm at the summit. Costs a prize; gains an ally who knows the vault, the Vaunt, and every fighter the Pale Hand has claimed.

### Settle the Arms Justly

Return Cairn as evidence to the inquest its owner buried; deny Ovation to any single ambitious hand; destroy or bind Forfeit rather than pass its clock along. None of these is what the bounty-poster asked for, and each is closer to right.

## Restoration and Release

The [[Vault-Claimed]] cannot be freed by winning a fight. Release needs their name from the raised-contestant register, the mortgage clause voided or the Pale Hand's consent, and a stabilizing rite the Pale Hand alone performs cheaply and others perform slowly. A party that destroys a Vault-Claimed guard in the dark has stopped a threat and not freed a person; the difference is the whole moral weight of the branch.

The arms are not restored so much as retired. Forfeit's win-lock can be broken with time, loss, or destruction. Ovation's pull fades when no one is watching, which is rarely in Vorthara. Cairn's roll can be read to the bond-court as testimony before the maul is sealed.

## Ending States

### The Rising House

The party signs on their terms, the harvest is dismantled as a condition, and the Vault-Claimed are released into their care. Vorthara gains a new arena stable that recovers people from institutions instead of feeding them to one. Marlo is a chastened partner testing every clause. This is the most restorative continuation.

### The Court Carves Them

Both Proofs reach Bishop-of-Bouts Onn. The sub-floor clause is voided, the audition is entered as evidence, and the arms are impounded as contested property. The party is paid in standing, the Vault-Claimed become wards, and the Pale Hand and the Vaunt both leave the room smaller.

### Clean Recovery, Dirty Floor

The party delivers the arms and takes the coin. The audition keeps running, the harvest stays legal, and the next team's deaths are already someone's collateral. The arms resurface in new hands with fresh liability histories.

### The Vaunt Wins His Launch

The party runs the vault brilliantly and leaves without touching the harvest or the show. Marlo debuts his stable on their footage whether they signed or not, and the mortgage keeps eating. The party is famous for the wrong thing.

### The Harvest Freed, the Enemy Made

The party voids the mortgage and frees the Vault-Claimed. The Pale Hand marks them, quietly and permanently. A rescue network of former contestants forms around the released dead; so does a Reaper's long grudge.

### Forfeit Passed On

The party takes the victory-eater and keeps it. Someone starts winning, and cannot stop, and cannot set it down. The clock that killed Gorreth is now the party's, and the next adventure is getting free of a prize.

## What the Adventure Changes

- Vorthara gains a precedent on whether a lethal audition can be sold as sport, and whether a sub-floor mortgage can rest on lender-built hazards.
- The Vaunt becomes a rising rival impresario, a scandal-survivor, or the party's own tested partner.
- The Pale Hand becomes an enemy, a discredited lender, or an untouched institution that simply moved its clause to the next vent.
- The three [[The Trophy-Arms|Trophy-Arms]] enter play as impounded evidence, retired hazards, dangerous loot under terms, or the seed of a body-market inquest.
- The [[Vault-Claimed]] enter the known bestiary of Vortharan magical industry as a distinct wrong: the mortgaged dead.
- [[Cinder-Bound]] enter the district's known hazards as bound service that can be paid, wagered, or freed.
