Jiraharin life is deeply **ritualistic and legalistic**, where **every action, from waking to sleeping, is dictated by divine law**. Their culture is built upon **structured traditions, sacred obligations, and strict codes of conduct**, ensuring that they remain **pure and untainted by the corruption of outsiders**.

Their **ritual laws (Ahkam al-Sabil)** govern:

1. **Daily Rituals** – Structured prayers, ablutions, and the sacred structuring of time.
2. **Marriage Laws** – Arranged marriages with strict purity codes, conversion requirements, and legal contracts.
3. **Pilgrimages** – The sacred journeys to lost sites, particularly **Al-Madinat al-Khaliyah** (_The Empty City_), where Al-Muntazar is prophesied to appear.

Each of these aspects forms the **foundation of Jiraharin identity**, reinforcing their belief that they are the **Chosen People of the Path**.

---

## **1. Daily Rituals: Structuring Life Around the Divine Path**

The Jirahar believe that **every action, no matter how small, must be done in accordance with divine law**, ensuring that they remain spiritually aligned with the **will of the One Above All**.

### **The Five Pillars of Daily Ritual**

1. **The Dawn Cleansing (Taharat al-Sabah)**
    
    - Each morning, every Jirahari must **perform a ritual ablution** before speaking their first words of the day.
    - This cleansing represents **the renewal of purity**, washing away **spiritual corruption** from the previous night’s dreams.
    - Without this, **a Jirahari is considered ritually unclean**, and their words for the rest of the day are believed to be tainted.
2. **The Morning Recitations (Tilawat al-Suhuf)**
    
    - After cleansing, the first words spoken must be a **recitation from the Scrolls of the Path**.
    - Each day has **a specific passage assigned**, guiding behavior and **setting the tone for the day’s spiritual obligations**.
3. **The Ritual of Trade (Bayt al-Tijara)**
    
    - Any form of trade, no matter how small, **must be conducted under a structured legal contract**, ensuring divine oversight in all transactions.
    - Even minor exchanges, like purchasing food, involve **formal blessings and witnessed agreements**.
4. **The Sunset Covenant (Ahd al-Maghrib)**
    
    - At sunset, families gather to **renew their daily oaths**, reciting verses about **loyalty to the Path and the coming of Al-Muntazar**.
    - This ensures that **each Jirahari ends the day in divine alignment**, free from the influence of outsiders.
5. **The Night Vigil (Sahirat al-Layl)**
    
    - The most devout Jirahar conduct **a silent meditation before sleeping**, believing that **dreams are messages from the divine**.
    - This practice is most common among **scholars, judges, and warriors**, who are expected to seek wisdom in their visions.

## **2. Marriage Laws: The Sacred Bond of the Path**

Marriage in Jiraharin society is not simply **a union of individuals**, but a **spiritual and legal covenant** that reinforces **the purity of the bloodline** and **the divine order**.

### **Arranged Marriages: Preserving Lineage and Law**

- All marriages must be **approved by the Keepers of the Path**, who ensure that both families meet **ritual purity requirements**.
- Marriages are arranged **through legal contracts**, which define **the duties of each spouse**, financial agreements, and spiritual expectations.
- A **bride-price (Mahr)** is typically paid by the groom’s family, symbolizing **his commitment to upholding the Path**.

### **Purity Codes and the Laws of Lineage**

The Jirahar strictly regulate **who can marry whom**, ensuring that **bloodlines remain untainted by outsider influence**.

- **Only those of Jiraharin blood can marry freely.**
- If an outsider wishes to marry into a Jiraharin family, they **must fully convert** and undergo a **seven-year process of instruction in the Scrolls of the Path**.
- Mixed marriages between **Jirahar and non-believers** are strictly forbidden, as they are seen as a **dilution of the Chosen People**.

### **The Sacred Wedding Ritual (Aqd al-Muqaddas)**

Jiraharin weddings are highly structured, involving:

1. **The Ritual of Witnesses** – Two Keepers of the Scrolls **must oversee and approve the union**, ensuring legal and religious compliance.
2. **The Seven Blessings** – Seven sacred verses are recited, each one **binding the couple not only to each other but to the Path itself**.
3. **The Binding Oath** – The couple must **sign a legal contract**, which is **archived in the Tower Town’s records**, making the marriage **official under divine law**.
4. **The Marriage Feast** – A celebration with **ritual meals**, during which the couple is presented with **a scroll containing the laws of marriage**, ensuring they understand their new obligations.

## **3. Pilgrimages: The Journey to Sacred Lands**

Pilgrimage is a **fundamental obligation** in Jiraharin life, symbolizing both **the physical and spiritual journey** towards the final fulfillment of prophecy. The most important of these journeys is the **pilgrimage to Al-Madinat al-Khaliyah** (_The Empty City_), the prophesied place where **Al-Muntazar will appear**.

### **The Three Great Pilgrimages**

#### **1. The Journey to Al-Madinat al-Khaliyah (Rahlat al-Muntazar)**

- Considered **the holiest pilgrimage**, this journey is only undertaken by the most devout Jirahar.
- It is believed that **one day, Al-Muntazar will appear in the Empty City**, calling the Jirahar to reclaim their divine inheritance.
- Pilgrims travel **on foot, following the route inscribed in the Scrolls of Journeys**, ensuring they **retrace the path of the Great Prophet**.
- Upon reaching the Empty City, they **fast for seven days**, meditating on the coming of the Awaited One.

#### **2. The Pilgrimage of the Founding Stones (Rahlat al-Sakhra)**

- A journey to the **sacred ruins of the first Tower Town**, built by the Great Prophet after the Exodus.
- Pilgrims **collect a stone from the ruins** to carry home, symbolizing their **continued commitment to the Path**.

#### **3. The Ancestral Pilgrimage (Rahlat al-Abaa')**

- Families must, at least once in their lives, **travel to the burial place of their ancestors**, offering prayers to ensure their spirits remain **aligned with the divine order**.
- This pilgrimage reinforces **family bonds** and ensures that **Jiraharin traditions are passed from generation to generation**.

### **Lesser Pilgrimages and Annual Rites**

While the three major pilgrimages are the most sacred, the Jirahar also conduct **smaller annual journeys**, including:

- **The Caravan of Remembrance** – A ritual journey that retraces the **Great Prophet’s escape from the lands of the Giants**.
- **The Night of Waiting** – A yearly vigil held in **every Tower Town**, where Jirahar gather to pray for **the coming of Al-Muntazar**.
- **The Festival of the First Fire** – Celebrating the discovery of black powder, a gift believed to have been **sent by the divine to protect the Chosen People**.

## **Conclusion**

The Jiraharin way of life is **deeply structured, legalistic, and centered on divine obedience**. Their **daily rituals, strict marriage laws, and sacred pilgrimages** reinforce their belief that they are **the only people preserving the true Path**, awaiting the day when **Al-Muntazar will return to restore their rightful place in the world**.

Through **unceasing devotion, meticulous tradition, and unwavering faith**, the Jirahar continue their journey, ensuring that their customs will endure **until the end of days**.
