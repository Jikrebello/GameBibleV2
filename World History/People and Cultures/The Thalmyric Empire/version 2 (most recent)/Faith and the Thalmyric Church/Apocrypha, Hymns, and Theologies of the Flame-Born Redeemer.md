### **I. Apocryphal Miracle-Acts and Mystic Visions**
The Thalmyric Church preserves a rich oral and written tradition of **non-canonical miracle-acts**, preserved in the monasteries and vision-scrolls of isolated ascetics. These stories are not part of the official scriptures, but are revered as revelations of the **Redeemer’s warfare** in the unseen world.

#### **1. The Fire at Dolmiran**
According to an ancient vision, the Redeemer once entered the city of **Dolmiran**, which had fallen under the sway of a sorcerer-king who enslaved his people with illusions. The Redeemer struck his staff to the ground and declared, "Let that which is false burn."

- Fire erupted from the temples and statues, revealing their hollowness.
- The Redeemer freed the people by walking through flame, his body untouched.
- The city was said to remain abandoned, its stones charred but unbroken.

#### **2. The Seven Curses Broken**
A mystic named **Sister Cyrielle of the Ashbound Veil** wrote of a vision in which the Redeemer entered a mountain cave where seven spirits lay in wait, each bound by chains of suffering.

- The Redeemer named each curse: Fear, Pride, Despair, Hatred, Sloth, Deceit, and Cowardice.
- With each naming, a flame appeared in his hands and severed one chain.
- When the last spirit was freed, the Redeemer said, "Go now and burn away shadow."

These spirits are now said to be **Flame-Bearing Saints**, wandering the unseen world to inspire the faithful.

#### **3. The Blaze on the Black Sea**
A sailor’s legend recounts that during a terrible storm on the Black Sea, a boat crewed by faithful pilgrims cried out for deliverance. The Redeemer appeared upon the waves, cloaked in fire.

- He raised his hands, and the sea parted in a spiral of flame.
- The storm calmed, but the ocean glowed for seven days, warming the frost-bound coasts.

### **II. Hymns and Liturgical Chants of the Redeemer’s War**
Thalmyric liturgy includes entire hymn cycles dedicated to the **Redeemer's struggle**, sung especially during the **Night of the Flame Vigil** and **Feast of the Burning Light**.

#### **Hymn: "Swordless, Yet Slaying"**
_"He bore no blade, yet struck the dark, His eyes aflame, his breath a spark. He wept for men, yet stood alone, And broke the Adversary’s throne."_

This hymn is sung by the **Order of the Iron Martyrs** before suicidal missions.

#### **Liturgical Chant: "The Flame That Walks"**
_"Flame that walks, flame that weeps, Wake the ash where sorrow sleeps. Strike the earth, oh fire divine, Burn the sin from soul and spine."_

This chant is intoned during the **Communion of Ember**, often accompanied by low drums and the hissing of incense.

#### **Sermon Fragment: The War Before the Cross**
From the writings of **Hierophant Galmir the Black-Robed**:

> "The Cross was not the beginning of his pain, nor the peak of his power. It was the crown of a long war waged in silence: in alleyways where he cast out shadows, in caves where he shattered idols, in hearts where he lit the first spark."

### **III. Theological Debate: Flame and Mercy**
Within the monasteries and scriptoria of the Thalmyric Church, a quiet but persistent debate exists over how to reconcile the Redeemer’s **fiery justice** with his **boundless mercy**.

#### **Position I: The Flame Is the Mercy**
Championed by the **Monks of Saint Lucirion**, this view holds that:

- The Holy Fire is **both purifier and comforter**.
- The Redeemer’s fire _hurts_ only because the soul resists it.
- To be consumed by flame is to be **freed of impurity** and thus healed.

This position emphasizes **willing submission to suffering** as a path to transformation.

#### **Position II: The Flame Is a Weapon, Love Is the Cure**
Argued by certain **Elenoran ascetics**, this doctrine claims:

- The Redeemer used fire _against the Adversary_, but poured **gentle water upon the people**.
- The Holy Fire is a **sword of justice**, not a balm.
- True healing comes from the Redeemer's **compassionate presence**, not his wrath.

This view is popular among mystics who experience divine visions as **tender warmth** rather than consuming blaze.

#### **Synthesis: The Dual Flame**
A growing consensus among modern theologians speaks of a **Dual Flame**:

- One fire **burns the wicked**, one **illumines the meek**.
- The Redeemer _is_ both sword and salve, fire and water, because he bears the fullness of divine nature.
- This reflects the Redeemer's title in Thalmyric texts: **"He Who Burns and He Who Binds."

---

### **Conclusion**
To the Thalmyric faithful, the Redeemer is not a singular image, but a flame with many tongues:

- A war-walker who defied the Adversary before the Cross.
- A fire-bearer whose miracles scorched shadow from the land.
- A tender comforter whose light calls the broken home.

His apocrypha, his hymns, and his paradox are all flames in the brazier of Thalmyric belief. The faithful do not seek to solve the Redeemer’s mystery—only to follow it into the fire.
