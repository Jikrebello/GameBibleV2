# A Reader's Guide to the World Egg

The World Egg is a human world of households, cities, roads, faiths and inherited conflicts. The supernatural is real, but most lives are governed first by land, work, family, law, worship and political power. The present year is 1360 AR. Older empires and migrations remain visible in the peoples, borders and institutions of that world.

This guide is for a reader encountering the setting for the first time. It leads from the broadest accounts to more specialised articles without requiring prior knowledge of the names.

## A fifteen-minute introduction

Read these four entrances in order for the shortest coherent orientation:

1. [[01 Canon Reference/History/10 Chronology and Control/Historical Spine of the Known World.md|The known world in one historical account]] — the central sequence from the Giant Age to 1360 AR.
2. [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Cultures MOC.md|Peoples and Cultures]] — the living societies produced by that history and the best first article for each one.
3. [[01 Canon Reference/Geography/Geography Atlas.md|Places and Geography]] — the regions, routes and landscapes in which those societies live.
4. [[01 Canon Reference/History/70 Later History and Present Day/Present-Day Courts and Crowns (1360 AR).md|Courts and Crowns in 1360 AR]] — the present rulers, states and political relationships.

For the complete chronological route, begin with [[01 Canon Reference/History/The Ages of the World.md|The Ages of the World]], which leads into the full [[01 Canon Reference/History/World Historical Atlas MOC.md|World Historical Atlas]].

## Before reading about a people

Ancestry, culture, religion, citizenship and political allegiance overlap in the World Egg, but they are not interchangeable. A person can belong to a culture without sharing one uniform ancestry, live under a state without belonging to its majority people, or practise a faith found in several countries. The [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Ethnic Groups Overview.md|Peoples and Ancestry Register]] explains these distinctions, while [[01 Canon Reference/History/Peoples in the World Historical Atlas.md|Peoples through History]] follows their development over time.

Culture overview articles are written as first stops. Each one introduces historical formation, regions, ordinary life, institutions, faith and the present situation before directing the reader to detailed articles.

## How deep to read

Most subjects use four layers. A **guide or map of content** gives the available routes. An **overview** defines the subject and supplies a balanced account. A **focused article** explains one person, place, institution, practice or event. A **historical atlas** follows change across a long period and links back to the focused articles that support it.

Begin with the overview unless a link has brought you to a specific question. Long articles are organised by headings and summarise their main subject in the unheaded opening paragraphs; the page contents panel can take you directly to a later section.

## Reading source labels

The reader displays the source file's status and visibility beneath every title. **Canon** articles control the setting. Received myths and in-world texts preserve what particular communities say and may disagree with one another. Author-only controls explain the decisions behind the reference work. Research and archived drafts remain available for provenance but do not become canon merely because the reader can display them.

## Other ways to find an article

Use full-text search when you know a name or phrase, **Articles A–Z** when you know the title, and **Collections** when you want a filtered catalogue. Every article also displays its source path, linked articles and backlinks.
