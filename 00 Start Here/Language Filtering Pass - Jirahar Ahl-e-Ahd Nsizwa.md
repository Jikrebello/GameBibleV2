# Language Filtering Pass - Jirahar Ahl-e-Ahd Nsizwa

This is the control sheet for the next terminology pass. Do not rename terms ad hoc across the vault. Use this sheet to build one filtered-analog table, then apply it consistently.

## Rule

Keep the cultural coding. Move the surface language one step sideways.

- Do not use major real-world religious, legal, or ethnic terms verbatim when a filtered form can carry the same function.
- Preserve the institution's role before changing its sound.
- Rename in batches by culture, then update links and aliases.
- Keep old terms as aliases only when needed for search or legacy notes.

## First Pass Targets

| Culture | Current Risk | Direction |
|---|---|---|
| Jirahar | Hebrew/Jewish-coded terms too direct in some files. | Keep law, memory, tower-town, exile, prophecy; filter titles and ritual vocabulary. |
| Ahl-e-Ahd | Arabic/Islamic-coded terms too direct in some files. | Keep covenant, contract, seal, desert law, pilgrimage; filter offices, doctrines, and war terms. |
| Nsizwa | Zulu-coded terms too direct in some files. | Keep cattle, warrior houses, ancestor law, island-continent placement; filter weapon, priest, and political titles. |

## Known Terms to Review

- **Ahl-e-Ahd:** Sahifat, jihad/Jahd remnants, caliph/caliphate where too direct, sheikh, emir, sultan-caliph, Malak al-Ahd.
- **Jirahar:** rav, ben, Ta'arifat, Al-Muntazar, tower-town legal titles.
- **Nsizwa:** inkandu, Spirit-Keepers, war-regiment, nkosi, and any direct clan/warrior-office terms.

## Acceptance Criteria

- Each culture gets a short naming rule: phonetics, title style, religious vocabulary, and what real-world terms are forbidden.
- No active note loses clarity about what the institution does.
- In-world texts may preserve old/archaic terms if marked as source language.
- MOCs and templates use the filtered terms after the pass.
