# Sir Gerhold "The Iron-Bear" Brandt, Marschal of Grenzburg

**Role in the Rule of Three:**
He is the Shield. Not a strategist, not a politician—**the man who maintains the wall between order and annihilation**. Every decision is measured in bodies and breaches. His internal war is between tradition, ruthless efficiency, and bitter pragmatism.

---

## Core concept

A career soldier who believes in the **profession of arms** as the only true nobility. He sees the Duchy not as a coalition, but as a **fortress**. His duty is to keep the fortress standing, even if he must brick up the windows himself. He is plagued by the knowledge that his professional soldiers are outnumbered by Grobi, outmaneuvered by the Folk, and undermined by the Bank's mercenaries.

**He survives by enforcing a discipline no one else has the stomach for.**

---

## Public face

- "The Iron-Bear," the unyielding defender, the father of the garrison.
- Speaks in the language of duty, sacrifice, and chain of command. "The wall doesn't care about your problems."
- A figure of immense physical presence and unshakeable, grim reliability. His approval is a medal worn by his soldiers.

---

## Private truth

- He is a **traditionalist watching his world become obsolete**. The future belongs to men like Koss (ruthless contractors) and realities like Liese's (dirty local deals).
- He harbors a deep, personal shame: during a past winter crisis, he ordered a section of the wall sealed, sacrificing the defenders outside to save the inner keep. He sees that choice in every hard decision he makes.
- He is loyal to the *Duke*, not the Duchy's politics. He fears the day Alaric dies and he must serve a schemer.

---

## Goals

### Short-term (this season)

1. Maintain garrison morale and discipline through the winter boredom and terror.
2. Ensure the Blackjacks are effective but *contained*; prevent them from becoming a parallel army.
3. Keep the scout networks active and reporting, despite Folk ambushes.
4. Stockpile and secure arrow fletching, pitch, and lumber for siege repairs.
5. Identify and neutralize any potential internal points of failure in the wall defenses.

### Long-term

1. Reform the levy system from a chaotic mob into a trained reserve.
2. Build a dedicated, Duke-loyal engineering corps to reduce reliance on guilds for wall repairs.
3. Establish a permanent watchtower line in the southern forests (a dream constantly thwarted by the Folk).
4. Die knowing the fortress held.

---

## Resources

- **The Garrison:** 200 professional Marchwardens who are fiercely loyal to *him*.
- **The Wall Itself:** He knows every crack, sally port, and blind spot.
- **The Armory:** Control over the distribution of real military gear (mail, polearms, crossbows).
- **Martial Law Authority:** In a direct siege emergency, his word can override the Landvogt's in the streets.
- **The Veterans:** A cadre of grizzled sergeants who implement his will.

---

## Constraints and vulnerabilities

- **Manpower Ceiling:** He cannot expand the professional garrison without the Duke and Bank's funds.
- **The Blackjack Problem:** Captain Koss's force is better funded, more flexible, and answers to a different master.
- **Intel Dependence:** He is utterly reliant on Scout Sergeant Liese and her networks, which have their own loyalties.
- **Political Blind Spots:** He understands battle, not banking or theology. He is often outmaneuvered in council.
- **The Winter Calculation:** He must constantly make cold math: lose 20 men in a sally to save 100 civilians? He hates that math.

---

## Taboo lines (things he avoids unless cornered)

- **Disbanding or disarming the Garrison** in favor of Bank-funded mercenaries.
- **Ordering the Garrison to act against the Duke's lawful person.**
- **Formalizing a military alliance with a Grobi tribe** (though he'll tolerate trade).
- **Surrendering the inner keep.** He will fight to the last stone of the inner curtain wall.
- **Allowing family lineage to determine officer promotion.**

---

## Relationship with his Subordinates

### With Captain Valdemar Koss (The Bad)
A relationship of **controlled antagonism**. Gerhold needs the Blackjacks' deniable skills and special manpower, but despises their lack of honor and their loyalty to the Bank's ledger.
- **He Uses Koss For:** Dirty pre-emptive strikes, artifact recovery (to fund *his* garrison), and dealing with problems "outside the wall" of military law.
- **He Fears Koss Will:** Successfully lobby to have Blackjacks integrated into the wall defense as equals, erode garrison morale with higher pay, or stage a coup during a crisis.
- **The Tension:** Gerhold represents the state's monopoly on violence. Koss represents the privatization of force. Their clash is inevitable.

### With Sergeant Liese (The Ugly)
A relationship of **grudging, essential dependence**. She is his eyes where his soldiers are blind. He trusts her truth, but not her ultimate loyalty.
- **He Uses Liese For:** True intelligence on Grobi movements, Folk mood, and the realities of the hamlets. She tells him what the scouts *really* saw, not what the Guild Masters paid them to say.
- **He Fears Liese Will:** Withhold critical info to protect her people, or broker a side-deal with the Folk that undermines his operational control.
- **The Tension:** He represents centralized, top-down command. She represents decentralized, bottom-up survival. He needs her network, but cannot officially control it.

---

## Seasonal behavior

### Summer/Autumn (Deployment Season)
- Drills the garrison relentlessly. Rotates troops to remote watchposts to harden them.
- Personally inspects the hamlets' pallisades, offering (gruff) advice.
- Constantly feuds with Koss over jurisdiction beyond the walls.

### Winter (The Test)
- Lives on the wall. Sleeps in the gatehouse.
- His authority is absolute. The "Iron-Bear" persona is fully in effect—any sign of pity or doubt is locked away.
- Holds the line between necessary ruthlessness (flinging the sick into a quarantine pit) and monstrousness.

### Spring (The Reckoning)
- Tallies the dead. Advocates fiercely for widows' and orphans' pensions.
- Purges the garrison of anyone who broke or looted during the siege.
- Begins the slow, sullen process of rebuilding the professional force.

---

## Relationship to the Players (Blackjacks)

He views them as **Koss's dangerous tools**. Useful in the moment, corrosive in the long run.
- **If they follow his orders:** He will be sternly fair, give them legitimate military support, and maybe even offer a curt "well done."
- **If they go rogue:** He will hunt them down with the same efficiency he hunts Grobi raiders.
- **What he might offer them (rarely):** A chance to "earn a real uniform"—a pardon and a place in the Garrison, escaping the Bank's debt. This is his ultimate attempt to recruit talent away from Koss.

---

## His "Ugly" signature move

**He will sacrifice tactical advantage to maintain institutional integrity.** Example: He will refuse to use a potent but unstable alchemical weapon offered by the Bank, because it would make his siege engineers dependent on outside contractors. He would rather lose more men holding the line the "proper" way than win in a way that erodes the Garrison's self-reliance.

---

## What makes him sympathetic

- He bears the weight of every life lost under his command.
- He genuinely believes the rule of law and professional discipline are the only things separating Grenzburg from barbarism.
- He protects his soldiers with a ferocious, paternal intensity.

---

## Failure modes

1.  **The Garrison Splits:** His veterans side with Liese's pragmatism, his junior officers are seduced by Koss's efficiency. He is left commanding a hollow shell.
2.  **The Wall Breaks on His Watch:** The one thing he exists to prevent happens. He either dies on the breach or survives, broken and disgraced.
3.  **He Must Choose Between Duke and Duchy:** A coup or succession crisis forces him to pick. Choosing the "rightful" ruler might destroy the fortress. Choosing stability makes him a kingmaker—and a traitor to his oath.
4.  **He Becomes What He Hates:** In desperation, he adopts Koss's methods completely, turning the Garrison into a mercenary company in all but name.

### **Captain Valdemar Koss, Commander of the Blackjacks**

**Role in the Rule of Three:**
He is the Scalpel. Not a soldier, not a warden—**the asset manager of deniable violence**. He represents the "Bad" within the military structure: efficiency unburdened by tradition, loyalty, or morality. He believes Gerhold's fortress is a sentimental relic; the future is a network of secure contracts and calculated outcomes.

**Core Concept:** A former mercenary captain bought out of his own debt by the Medori Bank and placed in charge of their most valuable assets: the Blackjacks. He sees people as capital—skills, debt balances, and risk profiles. His loyalty is to the ledger that owns him, and to the power it grants.

**Public Face:** Coldly competent, impeccably equipped. Speaks of "results," "liquidation of problems," and "return on investment." Presents himself as the modern, professional alternative to the Marschal's stubbornness.

**Private Truth:** He is terrified of falling back into the debtor's pit. His ruthless efficiency is a suit of armor. He secretly despises the Bank's "Silent Partners" but craves their approval enough to do anything. He believes if he makes himself indispensable enough, he can eventually own his own destiny again.

**Key Motivation:** To prove that the **contract** is stronger than the **oath**. To make the Blackjacks so essential that they become the de facto core of Grenzburg's defense, rendering the traditional Garrison obsolete.

**Relationship with the Marschal:** Sees Gerhold as a bottleneck. Respects his ability but considers his scruples a systemic vulnerability. Constantly lobbies the Duke and Bank for expanded Blackjack authority within the city walls.

**Relationship with the Players:** He is your direct boss. He gives you the worst, most profitable jobs. He will defend you fiercely from external criticism (you're *his* assets), but will liquidate you without a second thought if your risk profile outweighs your value. He offers bonuses, better gear, and vague promises of "debt restructuring" for exceptional results.

### **Sergeant Liese of the Marcher Vanguard**

**Role in the Rule of Three:**
She is the Ground. Not an officer, not a scout—**the living link between the fortress and the land**. She represents the "Ugly" pragmatism of those who live in the mud and snow. Her loyalty is to the people of the Marches, which sometimes aligns with the Duke, sometimes with the Folk, and never perfectly with the Marschal's rigid lines on a map.

**Core Concept:** A third-generation marcher. She joined the Garrison not for glory, but because it was the only way to get the training and authority to protect the scattered hamlets. She commands not by rank, but by earned respect from scouts, hunters, and Folk trackers.

**Public Face:** Taciturn, weather-beaten, always slightly out of uniform. Speaks sparingly in council, but when she does, it's to report hard facts: "The Stone-Tooth are moving north early." "The Ghost-Foot left a warning at Timberfalls."

**Private Truth:** She is walking a knife's edge. She trades information with the Moss-Crown clan. She has a Grobi contact (Grakka Stone-Tooth) she meets in the dead valleys. She withholds this from Gerhold not out of disloyalty, but because she knows he would be forced by his position to act on it, breaking the fragile webs that keep her people alive.

**Key Motivation:** **Sustainability.** Not just of the fortress, but of the human and Folk communities in the Marches. She believes Grenzburg can only survive if it becomes part of the ecosystem, not a foreign tumor fighting it.

**Relationship with the Marschal:** She is his conscience and his frustration. She brings him the truth he needs, and the complications he hates. He trusts her because she's never been wrong, and fears her because she operates in a moral universe he cannot command.

**Relationship with the Players:** She is your best source for what's *really* happening beyond the wall. She'll give you straight advice, warn you of ambushes the official scouts missed, and might ask for favors that help a hamlet but contravene orders. Helping her builds a network of allies in the shadows. Crossing her means getting lost in the deep woods with bad information.
