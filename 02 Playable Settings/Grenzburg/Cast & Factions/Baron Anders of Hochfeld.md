### **Baron Anders of Hochfeld, The Landvogt of Grenzburg**

**Role in the Rule of Three:**
He is the Scale. Not a reformer, not a brute—**the arbiter whose judgments define what "order" means in the Marches.** He represents the "Good" within the Ugly business of justice: the conviction that consistent, recorded law is the bedrock of civilization. His internal war is against corruption, vigilantism, and the creeping feeling that law is just violence with better paperwork.

**Core Concept:** A minor noble from a poor, rocky barony, Anders came to Grenzburg seeing a land without law. He built his power and identity on being the man who says, "This is how it is done." He is a procedural fundamentalist; the ritual of the court matters almost as much as the outcome. He knows the law is flawed, but believes fiercely that a flawed law applied evenly is better than the whim of the strong.

**He survives by making his court the only game in town, even when everyone cheats at it.**

---

## Public Face

- The stern, meticulous, and weary judge. He moves and speaks with deliberate, ponderous gravity.
- Speaks in the language of precedent, charter, and due process. "The law does not care for your reasons, only your actions."
- Holds court in a simple, cold chamber. His authority is vested in the heavy, leather-bound *Book of Judgments* on the bench before him, where he records every verdict in his own hand.

---

## Private Truth

- He is a **secret romantic**. He clings to the law because he has seen what happens without it—his own family lands were stripped by a rival using legal trickery. In Grenzburg, he has a chance to build a system that is *just*, not just clever.
- He is plagued by doubt. Every judgment favoring a guild over a commoner, every necessary pardon for a brute who is useful in winter, chips away at his ideal. He drinks to quiet the voice that asks if he's just Bailiff Hagen with a fancier title.
- He fears the Gravedigger not because of the violence, but because the **people cheer for them**. It means his law is failing to provide the justice they crave.

---

## Goals

### Short-term (This Judicial Year)

1. Secure a conviction against a major guild figure (for something real, not a trumped-up charge) to prove the law applies to all.
2. Codify the "Winter Edicts" (emergency laws) into a clear, limited charter to prevent marshal overreach.
3. Recover at least one significant asset stolen through fraud and return it to its rightful owner.
4. Infiltrate and document Bailiff Hagen's protection network.
5. Identify the Gravedigger, by any means necessary.

### Long-term (The Hochfeld Code)

1. Establish a proper courthouse and archive, separate from the Duke's keep.
2. Train and swear in a corps of bailiffs who answer only to the court, breaking Hagen's monopoly.
3. Draft and promulgate the *Laws of the March*, a simple code every settler can understand.
4. Be remembered as the man who brought **civilization**, not just survival, to the frontier.

---

## Resources

- **The Bench:** The power to fine, imprison, banish, and (in extreme cases) condemn to the Debtors' Corps.
- **The Book of Judgments:** His growing body of precedent. In a land with few written laws, this book *is* the law.
- **The Bailiff Corps:** On paper, they are his enforcers. In reality, they are a nest of vipers he partially controls.
- **The Duke's Mandate:** His authority flows from Alaric's charter. While the Duke can overrule him, doing so undermines the very legal fiction they both rely on.
- **Public Legitimacy:** The common folk still bring their disputes to him *first*, before resorting to knives or the Gravedigger. This trust is his most precious commodity.

---

## Constraints and Vulnerabilities

- **The Enforcement Problem:** His judgments are only as good as the bailiffs who carry them out. Hagen's corruption makes a mockery of his rulings daily.
- **The Folk Exception:** His law stops at the treeline. He has no jurisdiction over the Folk, making disputes between settlers and clans a diplomatic (or martial) issue, not a legal one.
- **The Winter Nullification:** During siege, martial law supersedes his authority. Sir Gerhold can hang a man without trial, and Anders can only record it after the fact.
- **The Bank's Immunity:** Medori Bank agents operate under commercial charters from Sestaren. Prosecuting them is a diplomatic nightmare.
- **The Knowledge Gap:** He is often the last to know about crimes, as Hagen's network controls the flow of information.

---

## Taboo Lines (He will not cross these)

- **Sentencing a person to death without witnessing a confession or irrefutable proof.** He will allow the Marschal to do it, but he will not put the black seal on the parchment himself.
- **Formally recognizing the Gravedigger's actions as "justice."** To do so would be the death of his life's work.
- **Allowing a trial to be decided by public vote or duel.** The law is reason, not passion or strength.
- **Destroying or falsifying an entry in the Book of Judgments.** The record must be inviolate.
- **Using his office to directly enrich his own family or barony.**

---

## Relationship with his Subordinates

### With Bailiff Hagen (The Bad)
A relationship of **toxic symbiosis**. Hagen is a cancer on the court, but he is also its muscle and its ears on the street. He gets results, even if they are foul.
- **He Uses Hagen For:** Enforcing judgments against powerful people who would otherwise laugh at the court. Collecting intelligence on the criminal underworld. Maintaining a bare minimum of street order through fear.
- **He Fears Hagen Will:** Fabricate evidence that leads to a catastrophic miscarriage of justice, utterly destroying the court's credibility. Or, simply stage a coup and replace him with a more pliable judge.
- **The Tension:** Anders represents the law as an ideal. Hagen represents the law as a racket. They are locked in a dance, each using the other to maintain their power, each waiting for a chance to eliminate the other.

### With "The Gravedigger" (The Ugly)
A relationship of **obsessive, furious paradox**. The Gravedigger is his nemesis, a living insult to his authority. Yet, they only kill those who have escaped his justice.
- **He Uses the Gravedigger For:** Nothing. He hunts them. But their existence is a constant, grim pressure gauge showing the failure of his system.
- **He Fears the Gravedigger Will:** Kill someone innocent, or worse, kill someone *important* under his protection, sparking a war he cannot control.
- **The Tension:** He represents order through procedure. The Gravedigger represents order through terror. The people see the Gravedigger as their champion when the law fails. This truth eats at Anders more than any corruption.

**(Theories on the Gravedigger's Identity Anders is pursuing):**
*   **A Folk Sympathizer:** A settler married into the Moss-Crown clan, targeting cruel lumber bosses.
*   **A Disgraced Veteran:** One of Sir Gerhold's former officers, court-martialed unjustly and now "cleaning up" the town.
*   **A Rival Criminal:** Someone challenging Hagen's monopoly by eliminating his clients and making it look like justice.
*   **Clerk Anya:** The Bank's creature, eliminating people whose debts have become a liability or who threaten Bank interests in a way the law cannot touch.

## Seasonal Behavior

### Summer (The Circuit)
- Rides the circuit to the hamlets, holding open-air courts. This is where he feels most like a true justiciar.
- Settles land disputes, logging rights, and mining claims with fresh evidence before him.
- Is most vulnerable to ambush or "accident" here, far from his bailiffs.

### Autumn (The Crackdown)
- Holds court daily in the city, processing the year's backlog. The mood is punitive; he aims to "clean the streets" before winter lockdown.
- Sentences minor offenders to hard labor on the walls. Bargains with major players to ensure winter peace.
- His most politically fraught season.

### Winter (The Hall of Echoes)
- The court becomes a quarantine for internal strife. He adjudicates ration theft, bunkhouse disputes, and petty violence to prevent it from boiling over.
- Works in his chambers, poring over the Book, trying to find patterns, planning his spring moves.
- A time of introspection and simmering frustration.

### Spring (The Reckoning)
- Conducts the trials of those accused of major crimes during the winter (murder, grand theft, treason).
- Issues warrants and authorizes banishments, purging the city of its worst elements before the gates open.
- A season of grim, final judgments.

---

## Relationship to the Players (Blackjacks)

He views them as **walking legal contradictions**. They are both agents of order (working for the Duke) and manifestations of lawlessness (debtors, criminals, deniable assets).
- **If they operate within his framework:** He may hire them as special bailiffs for a dangerous task, granting them temporary legal authority to make an arrest or seize property.
- **If they operate outside it:** He will issue warrants for their arrest the moment they breach the peace. He sees Captain Koss's whole operation as a threat to the rule of law.
- **What he might offer them:** **Judicial Clemency.** He can legally restructure their debt, reduce their sentence, or even issue a pardon in the Duke's name. The price? They must work *for him* to bring down Hagen or find the Gravedigger, operating as legally-sanctioned investigators. He offers them a chance to be true instruments of justice, not just profit.

---

## His "Ugly" Signature Move

**He will uphold a technically legal but morally reprehensible outcome to preserve the authority of the court.** Example: He will rule in favor of Guild Master Strun in a land dispute against a hamlet, even though he knows Strun bribed the witnesses, because the hamlet could not produce a written charter. He sacrifices the people's immediate faith in *fairness* to defend their long-term need for *procedure*. He believes the system, once pure, will correct itself. The people see only a corrupt judge.

---

## What Makes Him Sympathetic

- He is fighting a lonely, often thankless battle to implant an ideal in soil fertilized by violence and greed.
- Every corrupt judgment or failed case causes him genuine, private anguish.
- He believes, to his core, that without him and his book, Grenzburg is just a gang of bandits in a fancy castle.

---

## Failure Modes

1.  **The Gravedigger Wins:** The vigilante kills Hagen or a major guild lord, and the public acclaims them a hero. Anders's court is revealed as a useless pantomime. His authority evaporates.
2.  **He Becomes Hagen:** In despair, he decides to beat the corruption by joining it. He starts taking bribes to fund his own "loyal" bailiff corps, becoming the very tyrant he sought to replace.
3.  **The Bench is Broken:** The Duke, under pressure from the Bank or Church, overrules a foundational judgment. Anders's authority is shown to be a sham, and he resigns or is removed, leaving the law in the hands of pure opportunists.
4.  **He Discovers the Truth:** He finds the Gravedigger, and it is someone he admires, or someone whose cause is just. He is forced to choose between his life's work and his conscience.
