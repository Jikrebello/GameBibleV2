### **The Frontier Industry Lords (Vassals & Guild Masters)**

**Role in the Rule of Three:**
These are the **Ugly** factions of production. They are not ideologues like the Church or abstract like the Bank; they are the brutal, practical engines of extraction that make Grenzburg viable. They sustain the Duchy while simultaneously carving it up for their own benefit. Each industry is a kingdom within a kingdom, ruled by a sovereign of saw, pick, or trap, each battling their own internal civil war between **Tradition, Greed, and Treason.**

**Core Concept:** The frontier exists to be exploited. These lords control the means of that exploitation. They hold the real power on the ground, in the forests, and in the mines. The Duke needs their output to pay his debts and feed his people, but in relying on them, he empowers rival centers of authority that are often lawless, corrupt, and dangerously ambitious.

---

## **1. The Timber-Cartel: Guild Master Holger Strun**

**Concept:** The first lord of the frontier. Lumber is Grenzburg's lifeblood—for fuel, construction, and export. Strun didn't just build a guild; he built a **shadow government** for the woodcutters. He is the state for anyone who lives by the axe.
*   **Public Face:** A bear of a man with sawdust in his beard. Calls himself "First Forester," dresses in fine furs over practical wool. Hosts raucous, meat-heavy feasts for his loyal crews. The image of rough, marcher prosperity.
*   **Private Truth:** He is terrified of running out of trees. The good, accessible timber is gone. Every year, his crews must push deeper into Folk territory, sparking more raids. His secret trade with rival kingdoms is not just for profit—it's to build a foreign nest egg for when Grenzburg's forests (and his power) are exhausted.
*   **Goal:** To monopolize all lumber and charcoal production in the Marches, and to secure a hereditary noble title from the Duke (or a rival kingdom) that grants him formal ownership of vast forest tracts.

**Internal Rule of Three:**
*   **The Good (Traditionalists):** **Old Tomas & The Season-Cutters.** Veterans who follow the old cycles, selectively fell mature hardwoods, leave offering groves for the Folk, and believe the forest can provide forever if respected. They are the guild's skilled backbone but are increasingly marginalized.
*   **The Bad (Exploiters):** **"Clear-Cut" Karl & The Firebrands.** Aggressive, short-term profit crews. They use slave or debtor labor, burn tracts to clear land faster, and actively provoke Folk attacks as an excuse to demand military protection and more logging rights. They produce volume and fear.
*   **The Ugly (The Sovereign):** **Holger Strun.** He plays all sides. He publicly chastises Karl while secretly funding his operations for quick cash. He listens to Old Tomas's wisdom in council while selling the very "protected" ironwood Tomas cherishes to Eclessian shipwrights. He pays off Bailiff Hagen to look the other way and is in bed with the Medori Bank's venture division (Elara) for equipment loans.

**Seasonal Strategy:**
*   **Spring/Summer:** Push new cuttings. Use Karl's crews as shock troops to establish claims. Use Tomas's crews for high-value, skilled harvesting.
*   **Autumn:** A desperate race to float the season's cut downriver before freeze-up. Conflicts with the Folk peak.
*   **Winter:** Sells stockpiled lumber and charcoal at a 300% markup inside the city. Consolidates political power with his wealth.
*   **Political Bargain with the Duke:** Strun provides the essential material for survival and construction. In return, he demands military protection for his camps, lax oversight of his operations, and a veto on any laws regulating forestry.

## **2. The Delvers' Union: Lady Katarina "the Veiled"**

**Concept:** The noble of the deep. Mining is dangerous, but artifacts are the lottery that makes men kings. Katarina doesn't just own mines; she controls access to the underworld and its treasures. Her union is less a guild and more a **licensed cult of delve**.
*   **Public Face:** Always veiled in grey gossamer, purportedly due to "a sensitivity to light" from years underground. Speaks in a measured, whispery tone. Holds court in a stone manor filled with strange mineral specimens. An enigmatic, respected, slightly feared figure.
*   **Private Truth:** The veil hides ritual scarring from an early encounter with a ruin-artifact that showed her visions of power. She is not interested in wealth; she is an **occultech**. She believes the artifacts are pieces of a lost science-magic, and she intends to reassemble the puzzle, no matter the cost. The mine is her laboratory; her workers are her test subjects.
*   **Goal:** To uncover the "Prime Artifact" she believes is buried beneath the mountains, a key to unlocking the power of the old world, making her the most powerful being in the Marches.

**Internal Rule of Three:**
*   **The Good (Engineers):** **Master Miner Borghild & The Shorers.** Practical experts in geology, timbering, and drainage. They want safe, stable, productive mines. They see artifacts as dangerous curiosities to be handled by specialists. They are the reason the mines haven't collapsed.
*   **The Bad (Tomb-Breakers):** **Rollo the Grave-Light & The Phosphor Gang.** Glory-seeking delvers who bypass the safe tunnels to plunge into unmapped ruins. They grab anything shiny, trigger ancient wards, and release curses (shadow-plagues, madness-inducing whispers, stone-melting spirits). They produce the biggest hauls and the biggest disasters.
*   **The Ugly (The Archivist):** **Lady Katarina.** She directs Borghild to dig where she needs tunnels for her research. She secretly funds and eggs on Rollo's gang, using them as canaries to trigger traps and find vaults. She hoards the most dangerous and strange artifacts in a secret vault below her manor, studying them obsessively. She is in a silent war with the Church's Chancery (who want her relics) and the Bank's Silent Partners (who see her as an unlicensed competitor).

**Seasonal Strategy:**
*   **Spring/Summer:** Borghild's season. Opening and shoring up the main adits. Safe, steady ore extraction.
*   **Autumn:** Rollo's season. Last-chance ruin delves before the snows seal the high passes.
*   **Winter:** Katarina's season. Studying the year's finds in her manor-laboratory, making cryptic notes in her private cipher.
*   **Political Bargain with the Duke:** Katarina provides vital ore and the lion's share of the artifact wealth that services the Duchy's debt. In return, she demands absolute autonomy over her mining claims, immunity from Church relic-seizure, and the right to judge and punish crimes within her tunnels herself.

## **3. The Trappers' League: The Council of Elders**

**Concept:** The silent power. Furs and fish are the steady, less glamorous cousins to lumber and ore, but they feed the city and provide its luxury goods. The League isn't ruled by one person, but by a **gerontocratic syndicate** of old marcher families. They are the deep roots of Grenzburg, and their loyalty is to their lineages, not the Duke.
*   **Public Face:** A rotating council of three stern, weathered elders (currently **Gunhild, Egil, and Rolf**). They meet in a smoky longhouse, settle disputes between trappers, and set prices. They represent tradition, stability, and the "old ways."
*   **Private Truth:** The Council is dying. Their children want the quick money of logging or delving. To maintain their wealth and influence, they've made a deal with the devil—or rather, with the Folk and the Bank. They are now the region's premier **smugglers and grey-market brokers**.
*   **Goal:** To preserve their families' wealth and autonomy by controlling the underground trade between the human and Folk economies, making themselves indispensable to both.

**Internal Rule of Three:**
*   **The Good (Stewards):** **The Lineage Trappers.** Families who have trapped the same valleys for generations. They follow strict, sustainable practices, know Folk borders, and trade respectfully at sanctioned meet-ups. They are the source of the League's legitimate reputation and highest-quality goods.
*   **The Bad (Poachers):** **The Wire-Snappers.** Desperate newcomers or debtors who use illegal snares, poison streams, and trespass deep into sacred Folk hunting grounds. They bring in volume and provoke violent retribution that the Lineage Trappers then suffer for.
*   **The Ugly (The Syndicate):** **The Council Elders.** They publicly condemn the Wire-Snappers while secretly buying their illicit, cheap furs. They then use their Folk contacts (like the Moss-Crown Clan) to broker these furs, along with luxury pelts from the Lineage Trappers, in exchange for rare Folk herbs, dyes, and toxins. These they sell at a massive markup to the Medori Bank (for alchemical research) and to southern apothecaries, all while evading the Duke's taxmen. They are balancing a three-way game between tradition, crime, and treason.

**Seasonal Strategy:**
*   **Spring/Summer:** Lineage Trappers work their runs. Council brokers quiet meetings with Folk traders.
*   **Autumn:** The great sorting. Legitimate furs are presented for tax assessment. Illicit goods are moved to hidden caches.
*   **Winter:** The smugglers' season. Goods are moved across the frozen lake or through hidden tunnels. The Council's wealth grows while the city hibernates.
*   **Political Bargain with the Duke:** The League provides a steady supply of protein and luxury goods, and they help pacify the southern forests through their Folk contacts. In return, they demand light taxation, self-policing authority, and that the Duke turns a blind eye to the "minor" problem of poaching.

## **Collective Power & Relationship with the Duchy**

Together, these Industry Lords form the **Vassals' Council**. They are the Duke's most necessary and most troublesome partners.
*   **What They Provide:** The raw materials of survival and wealth. Without them, Grenzburg is a hollow fortress.
*   **What They Demand:** Autonomy, light regulation, military protection for their assets, and a direct voice in governance that often overrules the "soft" city advisors like Leviticus or Caestus.

Their Faustian bargain is fundamental: **The Duke empowers these petty kings to keep his kingdom alive, knowing each one is actively undermining his authority, his laws, and his long-term stability for their own gain.**

## **Relationship with the Players (Blackjacks)**

The Industry Lords see Blackjacks as **specialized contractors for industrial problems**.
*   **Holger Strun (Lumber):** Might hire you to "discourage" a village of settlers from logging in a tract he's claimed, to retrieve a stolen timber-map, or to quietly eliminate a Folk war-party that's targeting his camps without starting an official war.
*   **Lady Katarina (Mining):** Will hire you for esoteric, dangerous tasks: escorting a delicate artifact transport from a ruin to her manor, "containing" a curse that has escaped her mines (e.g., hunting a stone-shadow in the city sewers), or eliminating a rival scholar who is getting too close to her research.
*   **The Trappers' Council:** Likely to hire you for deniable smuggling operations: acting as armed guards for a grey-market caravan, sabotaging a Duke's excise station, or "retiring" a Wire-Snapper who has become a liability and knows too much about their operations.

**The Faustian Bargain:** Working for the Industry Lords makes you complicit in the hollowing out of the Duchy. You strengthen monopolies, enable ecological and spiritual destruction, and empower figures whose loyalty is for sale. Your pay might be in uncut gemstones, untaxed silver, or rare alchemical components, but the cost is the slow death of the very state that gives you shelter.
