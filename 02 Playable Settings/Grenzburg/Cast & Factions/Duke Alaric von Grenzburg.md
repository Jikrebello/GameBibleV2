# Duke Alaric von Grenzmark, Duke of Grenzburg

**Role in the Rule of Three:**  
He is the human hinge-pin. Not heroic, not villainous—**the guy who signs the deals that keep Grenzburg standing**. Every winter he chooses which poison the Duchy drinks: Church influence, Bank dependence, or Folk concessions.

---

## Core concept

A frontier duke who rules more like a **coalition manager** than a sovereign. His real skill is not war or administration—it’s **tradeoffs**. He is constantly deciding which faction gets “just enough” power to help, but not enough to own the Duchy.

**He survives by being useful to everyone and trusted by no one.**

---

## Public face

- “Father of the Marches,” the winter shield, patron of the lumber trade.
    
- Speaks in stability language: duty, survival, sacrifices, “hard winters make hard men.”
    
- Performs legitimacy rituals with the Church, shakes hands with merchants, hosts Folk envoys.
    

---

## Private truth

- He’s exhausted and quietly afraid of being the **last Duke** (annexed by rivals, bankrupted by debt, overthrown by a coup, or replaced by Church authority).
    
- He knows Grenzburg is a **valuable choke point** (timber, ore, lake trade, artifact access), so every powerful party would happily replace him with someone pliable.
    
- He is morally flexible because winter makes purity a luxury.
    

---

## Goals

### Short-term (this year)

1. Survive winter siege without mass starvation or riot
    
2. Keep lumber and ore extraction profitable enough to pay soldiers and debts
    
3. Prevent Folk conflict from becoming a unified insurgency
    
4. Keep the Medori Bank helping without letting them become de facto rulers
    
5. Keep the Church close without letting them become the law
    

### Long-term (5–10 years)

1. Build a second “winter refuge” (fortified satellite) so Grenzburg isn’t the only lifeboat
    
2. Rebalance dependency: reduce Bank leverage; reduce Church veto power
    
3. Convert parts of the Marches into stable tax base (not seasonal chaos)
    
4. Secure a dynastic marriage alliance that deters annexation
    

---

## Resources

- **Legal sovereignty:** courts, charters, pardons, outlawry proclamations
    
- **Force:** the garrison, patrol rights, levy authority (weak in practice outside winter)
    
- **The winter machine:** granaries, ration bureaucracy, labor drafts
    
- **Symbolic capital:** ducal lineage; the idea of “Grenzburg is the shield”
    
- **Gate control:** who enters in autumn; who gets ration tokens in winter
    
- **Licensing power:** lumber rights, mining claims, expedition permits into ruins
    

---

## Constraints and vulnerabilities

- **Manpower bottleneck:** needs warm bodies every winter; cannot screen everyone.
    
- **Credit dependence:** one bad year and the Bank owns the docks, then the Duchy.
    
- **Legitimacy dependence:** if the Church turns the people, he’s done.
    
- **Information dependence:** needs scouts (often Folk or ex-bandits) to track grobi.
    
- **Factional capture risk:** any office-holder can become a rival pole of power.
    
- **Succession fragility:** if heirs are weak/contested, every external actor intervenes.
    

---

## Taboo lines (things he avoids unless cornered)

These are great “pressure moments” for player-driven choices.

- **Letting a foreign kingdom garrison Grenzburg** (looks like annexation)
    
- **Handing relic custody or ducal courts to Church authority**
    
- **Granting the Bank permanent law-enforcement authority in the city**
    
- **A blanket amnesty for outlaws** (destroys what little trust exists)
    
- **Open war against all Folk** (would unify them and collapse labor supply)
    

When he crosses one of these lines, it should be a story earthquake.

---

## Leverage points (what others use to control him)

- **Church:** public blessing or public condemnation; control of “moral narrative”
    
- **Bank:** credit extension, payroll timing, supply contracts, debt calls
    
- **Folk blocs:** road control, scouting intelligence, camp raids, hostage leverage
    
- **External Eclessians:** recognition, marriage alliances, “aid,” sabotage
    
- **Grobi pressure:** each winter failure erodes his political oxygen
    

---

## Seasonal behavior (how he moves across the year)

### Summer (low occupancy, high opportunity)

- Grants permits, quietly sponsors expeditions, negotiates trade and alliances.
    
- Tries to stockpile and repair: wall works, granaries, ship maintenance.
    
- Accepts “people who disappear” because they bring skills and silence.
    

### Autumn (funnel season, high tension)

- Gate policy becomes political warfare.
    
- Uses selective pardons and selective punishments to shape who ends up inside.
    
- Leans hardest on Bank logistics and Church pacification.
    

### Winter (siege governance)

- Delegates heavily: he becomes a **symbol** and crisis arbiter.
    
- Makes ruthless “who eats” decisions by proxy (quartermaster politics).
    
- Holds public rites with Church relics to keep morale from cracking.
    

### Spring (reckoning)

- Trials, banishments, land dispute arbitration.
    
- Must reward allies (Bank, Church, auxiliaries) without conceding the state.
    
- Attempts to reassert monopoly on violence—often fails outside the walls.
    

---

## Relationship to the player (Blackjacks)

He never fully trusts them. He uses them because:

- they are **deniable**
    
- they are **competent**
    
- they are **not native**, so they have fewer local loyalties
    
- their existence threatens local power brokers, which he can exploit
    

### What he offers

- “Letters of marque” style authority: temporary warrants, immunity, access
    
- Information: what’s _really_ happening among advisors
    
- The ability to erase a past (“disappear” officially)
    

### What he demands

- Solve problems that can’t be solved openly:
    
    - break smuggling rings that undermine ration control
        
    - remove agitators without martyring them
        
    - sabotage a rival expedition without implicating the Duchy
        
    - recover stolen documents/ledgers/relics
        
    - retrieve a hostage quietly before it becomes a political crisis
        

---

## How he lies (useful for grounded intrigue)

- He says “for the good of Grenzburg” when he means “to prevent collapse.”
    
- He promises reform “after winter” knowing winter will force new compromises.
    
- He keeps multiple contradictory channels open: Church, Bank, Folk, rivals—then denies it.
    

---

## His “Ugly” signature move

**He trades institutional integrity for immediate survival.**  
Example: he’ll allow Bank collectors to operate inside the city for one winter because it prevents starvation—then spends the next two years trying to claw that power back.

---

## What makes him sympathetic (without turning him into a hero)

- He shoulders blame others avoid.
    
- He understands that justice in winter is often just **crowd control**.
    
- He’s willing to be hated if it prevents mass death.
    

---

## Failure modes (ways he can fall)

Pick one (or let player actions determine which becomes dominant):

1. **Bank capture:** debt crisis → Bank becomes the payroll → Duke becomes ceremonial
    
2. **Theocracy capture:** relic politics → Church courts expand → Duke becomes a vassal
    
3. **Coup from within:** Castellan/Provost/Household turns the garrison
    
4. **Folk unification:** brutal policies unify clans → chronic insurgency
    
5. **External annexation:** rival kingdom “helps,” then never leaves
    
6. **Winter catastrophe:** famine + disease → riot → legitimacy collapse
    

---

## 10 quest premises that naturally emerge from Duke Alaric

1. **The Gate List:** someone is selling entry tokens; find the ledger without exposing the Duke’s own exceptions.
    
2. **The Winter Oath:** a Folk clan offers scouts—if the Duke publicly recognizes their rights. Decide the price and enforce it.
    
3. **The Debt Draft:** the Bank demands 200 laborers “for repayment.” The Duke needs you to make it happen without riots.
    
4. **The Relic Procession:** the Church wants the Duke to kneel publicly. He needs leverage: retrieve proof the Legate is compromised.
    
5. **The Mine Strike:** ore output collapses; it’s either sabotage or “legal dispute.” Fix it quietly before spring payroll fails.
    
6. **The Smuggler’s Ice Road:** contraband is moving across the lake before freeze-up. Shut it down—or steal it for the Duke’s reserve.
    
7. **A Heir Problem:** an heir’s scandal threatens legitimacy. Make it disappear without creating a martyr.
    
8. **The Outlaw Fort:** a deserter band is wintering in a ruin. The Duke can’t spare troops. Clear it with deniable force.
    
9. **Foreign Aid:** a rival Eclessian envoy offers grain if allowed to station “advisors.” Investigate what’s really in the convoy.
    
10. **The Artifact Warrant:** the Duke secretly licenses your team to retrieve an artifact _before_ the Church claims it—then decides whether to hand it to the Bank, the Church, or keep it.

## Canon Ruling: Grenzmark vs Grenzburg

The ducal *family* is **von Grenzmark** (of the March); the *seat and duchy* are **Grenzburg**. Act I's "Duke Alaric von Grenzmark" and this note's "von Grenzburg" are therefore both correct usage: formal style *Alaric von Grenzmark, Duke of Grenzburg*.
