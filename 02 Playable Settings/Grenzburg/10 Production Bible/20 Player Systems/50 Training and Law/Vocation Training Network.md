# Vocation Training Network

## Purpose

This note controls where the player gains vocations, rank permissions, signature techniques, and proficiencies. It reserves institutions and regional routes without prematurely creating trainer personalities or full training quests.

Ordinary vocation skills advance through mastery. A trainer is required when knowledge has to be transmitted, tested, licensed, fitted, witnessed, or morally entrusted.

## Training Layers

| Layer | What it grants | Typical source |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | unlocks a starting vocation after character creation | public school, Blackjack instructor, field veteran |
| Practice | improves ordinary authored skills as mastery rises | use, drills, study, ordinary instruction |
| Rank proof | opens a new skill family or advanced technique | institution, master, practical examination |
| Signature teaching | grants a named rare skill, augment, or method | quest, master, recovered manual, dangerous proof |
| Advanced vocation | opens Paladin, Binder, Bard, or Lancewright | companion personal quest |
| Capstone | completes an advanced vocation or Warlock master spell | late quest with moral, technical, or material proof |

Experience cannot purchase knowledge the character has never encountered. Training also cannot substitute money for moral coherence, meaningful consent, or the resolution of a specific quest.

## Campaign Availability Contract

- The character-creation vocation is ready during the prologue.
- At least one alternate starting-vocation trainer becomes available before chapter 5.
- All five starting vocations can be opened by the end of chapter 6.
- Every starting vocation has at least one route that does not require permanent faction loyalty.
- Loss of one settlement or institution may make training harder, dearer, or later, but cannot permanently erase a starting vocation from a completed campaign.
- No advanced vocation opens before its companion's Act II finale.
- The [[Custom Vocation Workshop]] opens only after two authored vocations reach full mastery.

## Trainers as World Characters

Later trainer dossiers must answer:

- what work the trainer performs when the player is absent;
- who recognizes their authority and who rejects it;
- what they believe their discipline is for;
- what proof they require beyond payment;
- what conduct makes them refuse or alter instruction;
- where their teaching goes if they die, leave, or lose their institution;
- which technique is genuinely theirs rather than a menu reward.

Trainers should not stand idle as class kiosks. They require schedules, institutional ties, seasonal states, and at least one conflict that reveals why their knowledge matters.

## Rank Gates and Failure

A failed training proof changes the route rather than deleting the discipline. It may require recovery, restitution, replacement equipment, a field demonstration, a different teacher, or a later attempt. A sacred or covenant failure can close a specific teaching until the underlying wrong is addressed.

Ordinary rank proof should test the vocation's actual play:

- Fighter protects and controls a line.
- Slayer reaches, isolates, and exits.
- Hunter reads and prepares a hunt.
- Warlock solves a script safely under pressure.
- Medium distinguishes imprint from person and dismisses cleanly.
- Lancewright places and loads a physical mark, then releases or Yields it without uncontrolled discharge.
- Paladin protects a real good at cost.
- Binder establishes a valid relation and honors release.
- Bard sustains a group, reads when support should change, and respects a refusal under pressure.

## Proficiency Training

The five shared proficiencies in [[Proficiencies and Field Skills]] use broader networks than vocations. Locksmiths, scouts, thieves, delvers, couriers, hunters, craftspeople, soldiers, and companions can all teach relevant ranks. A criminal source does not make the knowledge itself evil; the player's use and the teaching price remain morally significant.

## Deferred Named Cast

This constitution intentionally creates no final trainer names. Named masters belong to later waves, when their quest ownership, district schedules, faction ties, possible deaths, and seasonal availability can be designed together.
