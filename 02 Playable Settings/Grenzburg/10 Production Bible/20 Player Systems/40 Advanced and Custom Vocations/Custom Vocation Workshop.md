# Custom Vocation Workshop

## Purpose

The Custom Vocation Workshop is a metagame reward for players who understand at least two authored combat languages. It lets the player build a personal vocation from earned parts without dissolving the authored vocations into unrestricted equipment swapping.

There is no in-world workshop, guild, teacher, lore event, or secret order behind it. It appears in the main menu and at safe vocation-change points once its account gate is met.

## Unlock Gate

The Workshop opens after the player fully masters any two authored vocations.

Once opened:

- the builder is available in the current campaign;
- the builder remains available to future new games on the account;
- account-unlocked parts remain visible across campaigns;
- saved blueprints become account-wide;
- quest-gated advanced parts still require their original account unlock at least once.

The gate teaches before it combines. Reaching an early rank in two vocations is not enough.

## Eligible Parts

A custom vocation may draw from account-unlocked:

- weapon families;
- armour classes;
- core attacks and movement parts;
- active skills;
- augments;
- signature mechanics;
- advanced-vocation parts;
- mastered authored growth profiles.

Unlocking a vocation does not automatically unlock every part within it. Rank, mastery, trainer, quest, and capstone gates remain attached to their rewards.

## Field-Locked Result

A saved custom vocation is one coherent field kit. Once equipped, it has fixed:

- native weapon arrangement;
- armour allowance;
- core movement and defensive interaction;
- active-skill loadout;
- signature resource or mechanic;
- selected growth profile.

The player changes the blueprint only at the Workshop. They do not swap individual weapon and skill families in immediate danger.

## Growth Profiles

Each blueprint chooses one of two growth rules:

1. **Single profile:** use the level-up growth of one fully mastered authored vocation.
2. **Blended profile:** average the growth of two fully mastered authored vocations and round every gain down.

The blend is intentionally less efficient at extremes. It gives a transparent route to a balanced character without quietly granting the best growth of both sources.

## Compatibility Laws

The final part budget and full matrix remain provisional. These laws are already fixed:

- a body cannot perform incompatible two-handed and paired-hand functions at the same time;
- instrument-dependent skills require the relevant instrument or an explicitly compatible replacement;
- one build cannot maintain two independent Arcane Burn pools;
- Warlock and Rune Fist parts share the same Burn state;
- Medium parts never turn shades into souls or persons;
- Paladin sacred parts retain White-Flame coherence and restitution rules;
- Binder parts retain consent, release, and anti-soulbinding rules;
- a movement part can improve a route but cannot become mandatory for the critical path;
- capstone parts remain unavailable until their quests are completed;
- a custom vocation must present a readable combat promise rather than a pile of unrelated highest-value moves.

## Blueprint Record

Each saved blueprint records:

- player-facing name;
- source vocations;
- weapon and armour kit;
- core interaction;
- active skills and augments;
- growth choice;
- signature-resource behavior;
- unresolved compatibility warnings;
- the account unlocks on which it depends.

The game should explain why a part is unavailable or incompatible before the player commits a build.

## Authored-Vocation Protection

Authored vocations remain the clearest onboarding, trainer, quest, companion, reputation, and balance units. They should retain strong internal synergies and signature behaviors. The custom system expands expression after mastery; it does not make the six starters disposable tutorials.

## Narrative Boundary

NPCs respond to visible equipment and acts, not to the metagame term `custom vocation`. A character using war-wands, grave-imprints, and heavy armour may draw all the relevant reactions even though no inhabitant knows the blueprint menu exists.

## Deferred Production Decisions

- total part or complexity budget;
- number of active skills and augments;
- exact incompatible pairs;
- duplicate-resource conversion;
- whether every blueprint must select one signature mechanic;
- presentation of stat-growth previews;
- blueprint sharing outside the local account.

These belong to combat prototypes and interface testing, not lore canon.
