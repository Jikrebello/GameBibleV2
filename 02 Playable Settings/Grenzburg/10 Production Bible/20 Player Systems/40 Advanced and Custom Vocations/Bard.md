# Bard

## Player Fantasy

The Bard is a mobile back-line conductor who makes a capable party faster, safer, harder to disrupt, and more dangerous together. It controls fog, battlefield rhythm, recoverable health, weapon enchantment, hostile cadence, and brief battle compulsion through a keyed double flute.

The vocation is strongest when the player watches allies and enemies rather than repeating one song from safety.

## In the World

`Bard` is the plain player-facing name for trained practitioners of [[Harmonic Shellcraft]]. Sermoran reed companies, naval signal crews, salon schools, travelling ensembles, military bands, and private teachers disagree about rank and repertoire.

[[Ilyana Veshkar]] teaches the player a field form during a personally completed [[The Free Refrain]]. Her instruction is inseparable from a crisis in which every performer may refuse. This does not make Ilyana the founder or owner of Bardic practice.

The first account unlock requires direct player participation. If The Free Refrain resolves autonomously, Ilyana may still save people, but the current-playthrough and account unlock do not occur. A changed spring aftermath cannot restore the missed unlock. After the first personal completion, Bard becomes an earned metagame option at supported vocation-change points in future new games.

## Native Kit

| Element | Bard rule |
|---|---|
| Instrument | brass-and-darkwood keyed double flute |
| Armour | robes or light armour |
| Primary statistics | Stamina, Magick, Magick Defence, Intelligence |
| Signature interaction | sustain refrains, designate Leads, build and spend Resonance |
| Movement advantage | breath-step repositioning and short fog-covered evasions |

The double flute has two independently keyed bores. One maintains the field refrain while the other carries a Lead line, counterphrase, enchantment, or pressure note.

The Bard has no native melee weapon. Ordinary instrument strikes are not a viable combat plan.

## Combat Grammar

1. Read the party's formation, enemy elements, terrain, and immediate danger.
2. Establish one refrain around the Bard.
3. Designate a Lead ally for more exact support.
4. Build Resonance through coordinated attacks, counters, rescues, and successful support.
5. Spend Resonance on an enchantment, counterphrase, compulsion, or crescendo.
6. Reposition before the field collapses or the Bard is isolated.
7. End or retune the refrain before breath and instrument strain become dangerous.

## Refrains and Leads

Opening mastery permits one sustained refrain and one Lead. Veteran mastery permits two compatible layered refrains and two Leads.

Refrains remain centered on the Bard. They do not become permanent buffs carried indefinitely across the map. Leads preserve a bounded portion of the support while an ally briefly leaves the field.

Compatible examples include:

- sheltering fog plus marching breath;
- mending mist plus anti-stagger cadence;
- concealment plus cold resistance;
- movement cadence plus counter-signal readiness.

Contradictory refrains consume extra Stamina or collapse into strain rather than stacking freely.

## Core Skill Families

| Family | Intended function |
|---|---|
| Fog refrains | shelter, concealment, cooling, resistance, and route control |
| Marching refrains | movement, Stamina efficiency, action recovery, and formation support |
| Mending refrains | restore recoverable Health and stabilize active allies |
| Lead lines | concentrate or briefly carry support on one, later two, allies |
| Elemental cadences | grant temporary fire, ice, lightning, or corrosive mineral weapon relations to the group |
| Weakness descants | expose existing elemental or affliction vulnerabilities |
| Counterphrases | interrupt hostile spells, roars, signals, and conducted workings |
| Battle enchantment | fascination, fear, sleep, emotional pressure, or one brief simple command |
| Pressure notes | stagger, repel, break aim, and create self-defence space |
| Crescendos | spend accumulated Resonance on a short decisive party window |

## Recoverable Health

Mending refrains restore only the recoverable white-health portion described in [[Health Recovery Afflictions and Encumbrance]]. They cannot:

- restore lasting Health loss;
- revive a defeated person;
- cure disease or replace destroyed tissue;
- cleanse corruption or repair moral coherence;
- route or recall a soul;
- substitute for camps, medicine, hospitals, or White Flame.

## Resonance

Resonance increases when:

- a Lead ally exploits a marked weakness;
- an ally attacks inside the intended elemental relation;
- a counterphrase interrupts a valid hostile action;
- the Bard supports a successful rescue, carry, or revival;
- allies alternate coordinated attacks within the refrain.

Repeated empty notes, idle performance, and hitting helpless targets do not build Resonance.

High Resonance enables stronger support but increases the consequence of a broken cadence. Silence, lung injury, deafening attacks, instrument damage, and isolation directly threaten the vocation without making it unusable in ordinary bad weather.

## Compulsion Limits

Battle compulsion follows [[Harmonic Shellcraft]]. It never creates consent or continuing loyalty. The interface marks lethal commands as lethal actions and identifies targets that cannot hear, understand, or physically comply.

Using compulsion against civilians, prisoners, companions, or non-hostile people creates authored legal and relationship consequences. A high social statistic cannot turn coercion into permission.

## Mastery Shape

- **Unlock:** one refrain, one Lead, mending mist, pressure note, one elemental cadence, and basic Resonance.
- **Practiced:** broader fogs, four elemental families, weakness reading, cleaner counters, and safer movement.
- **Veteran:** two compatible refrains, two Leads, stronger group enchantment, and bounded battle compulsion.
- **Capstone:** personal completion of [[The Last Common Measure]] grants a master counter-cadence and the Sermoran Field Bard custom blueprint.

## Training and Reputation

Ilyana supplies the first Grenzburg training route. Warlock Hall can explain Shell safety but cannot teach performance, consent, breath, or ensemble judgement by itself. Sermoran travellers, signal crews, instrument makers, and recovered scores support later rank gates.

Bards are welcomed in companies, hospitals, taverns, ships, marches, and civic ceremonies. They are distrusted where compulsion, privateering, propaganda, or ownership of performers has left a wound.

## Custom Vocation Parts

Mastery contributes the keyed double flute, light armour, one selected refrain, Lead support, pressure-note movement, Resonance augments, and unlocked elemental cadences to [[Custom Vocation Workshop]].

Two-refrain layering and the strongest compulsion parts require full Bard mastery. Every custom part retains recoverable-health and consent limits.

## Originalization

The support logic draws design lessons from area healing, boons, counters, sustained performance, group enchantment, and resource conversion. No source-derived skill name is approved for public use. [[Vocation Originalization Register]] controls final naming.

## Design Research

These are author-facing mechanical references, not setting canon:

- [Dragon's Dogma Mage](https://dragonsdogma.fandom.com/wiki/Mage): support casting, elemental boons, recovery, and party utility.
- [Dragon's Dogma Online Priest](https://ddon.wikidot.com/vocations%3Apriest): centered support fields, sustained auras, and party positioning.
- [Dragon's Dogma Online Shield Sage](https://maxhubnet0x01.github.io/ddon-fan-wiki/vocations/shield_sage/): converting successful defensive or coordinated play into a spendable support resource.
- [Pathfinder Bard](https://legacy.aonprd.com/corerulebook/classes/bard.html): maintained performance, group benefits, counter-performance, and limited enchantment.

The Bard adapts those lessons into Harmonic Shellcraft, Resonance, consent limits, a no-melee native kit, and Grenzburg's party structure. Names, presentation, progression, and final combat behavior remain originalization-controlled.
