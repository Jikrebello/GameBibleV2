# Virtue and Sin Tendencies

The tendency system records repeated conduct. It does not declare morality subjective, convert faction approval into righteousness, or remove ordinary dialogue at random.

## Seven Pairs

| Sin | Virtue | Conduct being tracked |
|---|---|---|
| **Lust** | **Chastity** | reducing persons to appetite or possession versus ordering desire toward fidelity, dignity, and the good of another |
| **Gluttony** | **Temperance** | consumption without rightful measure versus disciplined use of food, drink, pleasure, and comfort |
| **Greed** | **Charity** | grasping beyond need and justice versus costly provision for another's genuine good |
| **Sloth** | **Diligence** | refusal of owed effort or relation versus faithful work, attention, and perseverance |
| **Wrath** | **Forgiveness** | vengeance and rage enthroned over justice versus relinquishing hatred without pretending evil did not occur |
| **Envy** | **Kindness** | resenting another's good and seeking its loss versus willing and assisting the good of another |
| **Pride** | **Humility** | treating the self as the final measure versus truthful knowledge of one's dignity, limits, dependence, and fault |

Forgiveness does not forbid judgement. Charity does not require funding evil. Chastity is not fear of embodied affection. Humility is not self-contempt. Diligence does not sanctify exploitation.

## Visible States

Each pair moves through four readable states:

- **Neutral:** no sustained pattern;
- **Leaning:** repeated conduct is becoming recognizable;
- **Established:** companions and institutions can name the habit;
- **Defining:** the habit has become strong enough to govern rare vows, temptations, training, or climactic commitments.

Exact point values and decay rates remain provisional. Threshold changes always receive visible feedback.

## Character Creation

The player may begin with one virtue or sin at **Leaning**, or begin Neutral across all seven pairs. No character begins Established or Defining.

## What Changes Tendencies

Tendencies respond to completed conduct and accepted cost, not selected wording alone. The system weighs:

- what the player actually did;
- what they knew at the time;
- whether they accepted sacrifice or merely chose the profitable appearance of virtue;
- repeated treatment of the weak, enemies, companions, debtors, prisoners, dead, and strangers;
- restitution, confession, reform, and renewed misconduct.

One isolated decision may be memorable without redefining the person.

## Mechanical and Narrative Effects

Tendencies may:

- unlock recognition, vows, temptations, training, or companion conversations;
- change how a reward is framed or accepted;
- alter an NPC's expectation of the player;
- strengthen or weaken relationship interpretations;
- support or obstruct sacred coherence;
- make a rare defining commitment unavailable when it would contradict a Defining habit.

They never randomly remove routine questions, refusals, prices, investigation lines, or practical solutions.

## Repentance and Change

No tendency is permanent merely because it reached a threshold. Change requires contrary conduct sustained over time. Grave acts may also require confession, restitution, surrender of gain, acceptance of judgement, or repair of a relationship before the game treats a claimed change as credible.

For [[Paladin]], physical training remains learned while White-Flame techniques depend upon current coherence. Restoration is a playable moral process, not a purchased respec.

## Moral Boundaries

- Objective evils remain evil when profitable, lawful, traditional, or skillful.
- A faction cannot make cruelty virtuous by approving it.
- An institution's corruption does not make every rebel righteous.
- A virtuous tendency does not guarantee prudence or correct information.
- The interface reports the player's habit, not a complete divine judgement on the soul.
