# Character Creation and Identity

Character creation defines an outsider who already had a life before the Black Road. It supplies recognitions, practical experience, social habits, and a first combat discipline without inventing a secret destiny.

## Fixed Life Situation

Every player character is:

- human;
- an outsider to Grenzburg;
- legally or practically bound by debt;
- entering service with the [[Blackjack Corps]];
- capable enough to have learned one complete starting vocation;
- silent and visually customizable;
- not a secret heir, chosen sovereign, commander, or native expert on Grenzburg.

Appearance and sex do not change combat statistics. Sex controls the relationship eligibility already established by [[Companion Constitution]]. Origin, knowledge, background, conduct, and vocation control recognition and capability.

## Creation Sequence

### 1. Appearance and Sex

The player chooses appearance and male or female sex. The protagonist has no fixed spoken voice or authored personality.

### 2. Origin Culture

Choose one origin from the nine supported knowledge families:

- Eclessian;
- Thalmyric;
- Sestaran;
- Jirahar;
- Ahl-e-Ahd;
- Folk;
- Ättar;
- Tarkhan;
- Nsizwa.

Origin grants that culture's knowledge tag automatically. It establishes childhood context, familiar etiquette, likely institutions, and some reactions. It does not prescribe morality, faction loyalty, class, or politics.

Broad culture tags translate practical familiarity. Specific dialogue should still identify the relevant city, realm, tower, tribe, confession, clan, or social register. A Sestaran tag does not make every Concord city interchangeable; an Ahl-e-Ahd tag does not erase the differences among Covenant peoples.

### 3. Additional Cultural Familiarity

Choose one additional culture tag. It represents sustained contact through family, travel, military service, pilgrimage, trade, captivity, education, exile, or neighborhood life.

This is genuine familiarity rather than blood quantum or racial intuition. It can identify customs and likely misunderstandings but cannot reveal a stranger's private belief.

### 4. Background Knowledge

Choose one background from [[Social Skills and Knowledge]]:

- Wilderness;
- Underworld;
- Military;
- Nobility;
- Scholarly;
- Merchant/Craft;
- Peasant/Labour;
- Mystic/Religious;
- Drifter/Vagabond.

Background knowledge represents practiced life. It supplies dialogue, investigation, preparation, and alternative methods. It is not a second vocation and does not grant an entire profession's combat kit.

### 5. Social Focus

Choose a primary and secondary focus among Charisma, Wisdom, Intelligence, and Luck. Choosing the same statistic twice creates a specialist. Choosing two different statistics creates broader passive growth.

The exact rate of passive growth remains provisional. The identity of the favored statistics is canon.

### 6. Initial Moral Leaning

Choose one starting virtue or sin leaning from [[Virtue and Sin Tendencies]], or begin neutral across all seven pairs. A starting leaning is a habit and reputation of the self, not proof that the character can never change.

### 7. Starting Vocation

Choose [[Fighter]], [[Slayer]], [[Hunter]], [[Warlock]], [[Medium]], or [[Rune Fist]]. The choice grants one coherent native kit for the prologue. It does not prevent learning the other authored vocations.

## What Creation Does Not Do

- It does not write a fixed biography beyond the debtor premise.
- It does not make culture a morality package.
- It does not hide mandatory optimum combinations.
- It does not assign faction loyalty.
- It does not force a personality through random dialogue removal.
- It does not decide romance or sworn-bond commitment.

## Dialogue Recognition

Origin, additional culture, and background may all produce visible options. The option identifies its source before selection. When more than one knowledge source applies, they may support different interpretations rather than stacking into automatic truth.
