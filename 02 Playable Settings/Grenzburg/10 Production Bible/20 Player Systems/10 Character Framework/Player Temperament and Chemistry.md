# Player Temperament and Chemistry

This note controls the player's optional archetypal expression and its use in companion relationships. Temperament describes **how** the player tends to establish worth and approach other people. [[Virtue and Sin Tendencies]] continues to describe moral direction; [[Social Skills and Knowledge]] continues to resolve capability and risk.

## Available Tendencies

The player selects a male or female body during character creation. That selection determines the four temperament tracks available to the protagonist.

| Female player | Internal meaning |
|---|---|
| Tomboy | competence, autonomy, practical action |
| Goth | hidden truth, estrangement, selective intimacy |
| Cheerleader | belonging, morale, social coordination |
| Bohemian | imagination, reinvention, creative freedom |

| Male player | Internal meaning |
|---|---|
| Jock | embodied performance, duty, team standing |
| Nerd | systems mastery, explanation, technical care |
| Rebel | refusal, sovereignty, action outside permission |
| Goofball | play, warmth, adaptability, social release |

These names are author-facing. Ordinary dialogue never displays an archetype icon or tag.

## Optional Creation Questionnaire

Character creation offers an optional eight-question temperament questionnaire. Skipping it begins with an empty ledger.

The questionnaire presents one answer from each available track. The player sees only the answer text.

### 1. The intake line stops moving

**Female responses**

- Take the heavy end of the obstruction and start moving it. `[Tomboy]`
- Watch who benefits from the confusion before acting. `[Goth]`
- Give everyone a task and get the line moving together. `[Cheerleader]`
- Turn the delay into an improvised exchange of food, news, and help. `[Bohemian]`

**Male responses**

- Step forward, set a pace, and ask who can keep it. `[Jock]`
- Find the broken part of the process and correct it. `[Nerd]`
- Open the unused side gate and deal with permission afterward. `[Rebel]`
- Make the official laugh long enough to stop defending the mistake. `[Goofball]`

### 2. A stranger is being mocked

**Female responses**

- Stand beside her and make the mockers address you instead. `[Tomboy]`
- Name the insecurity everyone is pretending not to see. `[Goth]`
- Bring her visibly into your group and make exclusion socially costly. `[Cheerleader]`
- Ask about the strange thing with genuine interest and let it become the evening's subject. `[Bohemian]`

**Male responses**

- Challenge the loudest mocker to say it without an audience. `[Jock]`
- Demonstrate that the supposed mistake is not a mistake at all. `[Nerd]`
- Tell the stranger where the back door is and leave with him. `[Rebel]`
- Turn the joke until the mockers realize they are its subject. `[Goofball]`

### 3. The assigned plan is failing

**Female responses**

- Change position and do the necessary work yourself. `[Tomboy]`
- Ask which truth the plan was designed not to acknowledge. `[Goth]`
- Reassign the team around the people who are still functioning. `[Cheerleader]`
- Abandon the form of the plan and invent another route to its purpose. `[Bohemian]`

**Male responses**

- Hold the line long enough for everyone else to reset. `[Jock]`
- Recalculate from what has actually happened. `[Nerd]`
- Refuse the premise and break the rule causing the failure. `[Rebel]`
- Admit the disaster plainly, release the panic, and improvise from there. `[Goofball]`

### 4. Someone gives you an expensive gift

**Female responses**

- Test whether it is useful and ask what they expect in return. `[Tomboy]`
- Ask why this gift, and why now. `[Goth]`
- Receive it graciously and make the giver's generosity visible. `[Cheerleader]`
- Imagine immediately what strange new thing it makes possible. `[Bohemian]`

**Male responses**

- Accept it as a mark of trust and promise to justify that trust. `[Jock]`
- Ask how it was made and what it can really do. `[Nerd]`
- Refuse until certain it creates no claim over you. `[Rebel]`
- Accept, then give the giver something ridiculous and sincere in return. `[Goofball]`

### 5. Your group wins publicly

**Female responses**

- Praise the person who did the hardest unseen work. `[Tomboy]`
- Notice who has gone quiet during the celebration. `[Goth]`
- Make sure every contributor is named before the crowd disperses. `[Cheerleader]`
- Turn the victory into a story, song, or ritual nobody planned. `[Bohemian]`

**Male responses**

- Put the whole team before the crowd and own the standard together. `[Jock]`
- Explain the overlooked decision that made victory possible. `[Nerd]`
- Give credit to the person the institution would rather omit. `[Rebel]`
- Make the defeated side laugh before pride becomes cruelty. `[Goofball]`

### 6. A friend refuses your help

**Female responses**

- Respect the refusal, remain nearby, and be ready when action is wanted. `[Tomboy]`
- Ask once whether the refusal is privacy or fear. `[Goth]`
- Offer a form of help that does not isolate or embarrass them. `[Cheerleader]`
- Leave a surprising invitation they may accept on their own terms. `[Bohemian]`

**Male responses**

- Tell him the offer stands and keep watch without hovering. `[Jock]`
- Clarify exactly which kind of help he is refusing. `[Nerd]`
- Give him the route out and do not make him ask permission. `[Rebel]`
- Leave food, a bad drawing, and no obligation to discuss either. `[Goofball]`

### 7. An authority gives a lawful but foolish order

**Female responses**

- State the practical failure and refuse the unsafe part. `[Tomboy]`
- Ask what fear or reputation the order is protecting. `[Goth]`
- Build enough agreement that the authority can reverse course without public collapse. `[Cheerleader]`
- Satisfy the real need through an unexpected interpretation. `[Bohemian]`

**Male responses**

- Demand the order be given clearly and accept the public consequence of opposing it. `[Jock]`
- Present the evidence and a workable correction. `[Nerd]`
- Disobey before the order can harm anyone. `[Rebel]`
- Obey so literally that its absurdity becomes impossible to defend. `[Goofball]`

### 8. The night after a frightening victory

**Female responses**

- Check equipment, injuries, exits, and tomorrow's route. `[Tomboy]`
- Sit with the person who cannot join the celebration. `[Goth]`
- Feed the room, retell the brave moments, and restore its sense of itself. `[Cheerleader]`
- Find music, colour, or a view that makes survival feel larger than relief. `[Bohemian]`

**Male responses**

- Take the first watch and let the others sleep. `[Jock]`
- Record what happened before fear distorts the account. `[Nerd]`
- Leave the official camp for a place where nobody can assign meaning to the victory. `[Rebel]`
- Tell the story badly on purpose until everyone can breathe again. `[Goofball]`

## Questionnaire Result

The highest answer count produces a plain-language description:

- **Competence and autonomy:** "You meet uncertainty by becoming useful and difficult to confine."
- **Hidden truth and distance:** "You look beneath what a room agrees to say and choose trust carefully."
- **Belonging and coordination:** "You make groups stronger by helping people recognize one another."
- **Imagination and reinvention:** "You answer confinement by making another way to live."
- **Performance and duty:** "You prove yourself by carrying weight where others can see and rely on you."
- **Mastery and understanding:** "You make danger manageable by learning how its parts relate."
- **Freedom and refusal:** "You preserve yourself by recognizing when permission has become ownership."
- **Play and adaptability:** "You keep people human by making fear and failure survivable."

The player may:

1. accept the result;
2. choose any other available description;
3. begin open-ended.

An accepted or manually chosen result adds three opening signals. A tied quiz presents the tied descriptions without resolving them automatically.

## Rolling Signal Ledger

- The ledger retains the latest twelve authored signals.
- An expressive dialogue choice adds one signal to exactly one track.
- A consequential authored action may add two identical signals.
- Broad combat, equipment, loot, travel, and faction participation never add signals by inference.
- A choice may carry both a temperament tag and a moral tag, but each system evaluates it independently.

### Recognition

- A primary tendency requires at least four of the twelve signals and a lead of at least two over the runner-up.
- A secondary tendency requires at least three signals after a primary has been recognized.
- A tie for first, an insufficient lead, an empty ledger, or deliberately mixed conduct produces `grey`.
- Old signals falling out of the ledger allow sustained new conduct to change the primary or secondary.
- Companions may notice a genuine change in temperament before the lock, but no system banner announces it.

## Act III Lock

Completion of [[Ghor at the Gate]] freezes the current primary, secondary, or grey state.

- Act III dialogue uses the locked state.
- A grey player continues receiving complete neutral writing.
- The lock does not close ordinary dialogue or change quest availability.
- The lock does not alter relationship commitment, approval, virtue, sin, social statistics, or faction standing.

## Chemistry Use

[[Companion Archetype Chemistry Matrix]] is an optional hook bank. The Grey response is always complete, and no companion requires eight temperament variants or a fixed chemistry-scene quota.

- Primary tendency chooses the full scene interpretation where a variant exists.
- Secondary tendency may alter one follow-up, objection, joke, or method.
- Secondary tendency never creates a second full branch.
- Grey uses the authored baseline.
- Temperament may change information presentation, trust framing, route proposals, public posture, or the way a companion asks for help.
- It may not remove a critical solution, decide a companion's moral outcome, or make one temperament the superior partner.

## Coverage Target

Reserve at least twenty-four scored decision scenes across the playable campaign:

| Content family | Minimum scenes |
|---|---:|
| Main campaign | 8 |
| Faction and local stories | 6 |
| Companion campaigns | 8 |
| Company life and systemic world | 2 |

Across those scenes, each available tendency must have at least twelve possible signals in a broad run and at least four on the critical path. Every scored scene also offers at least one unscored or differently framed response.

## Postgame Reset

After [[The First Summer Road]], an Old Market apothecary sells the repeatable **Draught of Second Nature**.

- Drinking it clears the temperament ledger, primary, secondary, and lock.
- It reopens the questionnaire result flow: quiz, direct selection, or grey.
- The new choice is immediately treated as the locked postgame temperament.
- Future temperament reactions behave as though the new profile had always been current.
- Completed quests, memories, approval, relationships, virtues, sins, and world states do not change.
- The draught is a game-facing alchemical respec convenience and establishes no wider canon of reliable personality replacement.
