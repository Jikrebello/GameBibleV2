# Player Mandate and Dual Chain of Command

## Fixed Premise

The protagonist is customizable in ancestry, prior trade, combat training, and reason for entering debt. One fact does not change: the Medori Bank owns the protagonist's enforceable debt-contract, and the [[Crisis Charter of Secondment]] assigns the protagonist's Blackjack unit to Grenzburg for the crisis year.

The player therefore begins with two legitimate masters whose orders regularly conflict:

- **Bank command:** [[Captain Valdemar Koss]] issues collection orders, recovery riders, secrecy clauses, and artifact claims on behalf of the Iron Ledger.
- **Ducal field command:** [[Castellan Pierre Hohenwall]] issues public survival missions under the ducal warrant.
- **Strategic authority:** [[Duke Alaric von Grenzburg|Duke Alaric von Grenzmark]] can redefine priorities but cannot erase the underlying debt without provoking the Bank.

The player is not a secret ducal agent and not an independent adventurer. The contradiction is public, contractual, and politically useful: the Duke gains specialists he cannot afford, while the Bank gains legal access to the frontier.

## Two Controlling Tracks

Both tracks run from **0 to 4**.

| Track | Starting Value | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Ducal Trust | 1 | Confidence that the player serves Grenzburg rather than only the current contract. |
| Bank Leverage | 3 | The Bank's practical ability to compel the player through debt, equipment, clauses, and threatened seizure. |

### Ducal Trust

- **0 - Warrant Revoked:** ducal facilities close; missions continue through Bank or faction channels.
- **1 - Seconded Asset:** lawful access only while following a named order.
- **2 - Proven Troubleshooter:** discretion, audiences, and limited requisition authority.
- **3 - Warden's Confidence:** required for the Duke's Heart-Stone custody and integrative ending.
- **4 - Defender of the March:** the player may unite ducal, Church, Folk, and reformist Bank forces.

### Bank Leverage

- **0 - Contract Broken:** rare emancipation state; Bank rewards and command access end.
- **1 - Negotiable Debt:** the player can refuse riders without immediate seizure.
- **2 - Managed Liability:** Bank pressure remains but cannot dictate the final settlement.
- **3 - Owned Specialist:** starting state; refusal causes penalties, not game over.
- **4 - Foreclosed Asset:** Bank ending pressure; companions, rewards, or discoveries can be claimed as collateral.

## Quest Order Rule

Every main quest presents two briefs:

1. **The ducal objective** states the public survival need.
2. **The Bank rider** identifies property, debtors, evidence, or artifacts the Iron Ledger expects the player to secure.

The player may satisfy both, privilege one, expose either, or exploit a loophole. Refusing the Duke reduces access and legitimacy. Refusing the Bank increases collection pressure, equipment restrictions, and coercion against the player's associates. Neither refusal automatically ends the campaign.

## Canonical Continuation

The intended Duke's Path ends with Ducal Trust at 3 or 4, Bank Leverage at 2 or below, and no outside concession track at 4. The player's service proves that a contract can be subordinated to lawful public duty without pretending the debt never existed.
