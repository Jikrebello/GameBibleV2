# Grenzburg as Vertical Slice

Grenzburg is the first proof that the World Egg setting works as a game world. It compresses the setting's major themes into a frontier duchy: winter, debt, faith, migration, relics, Folk land claims, artifact exploitation, war magic, and buried metaphysical wounds.

## What It Tests

- **Institutional truth:** Church, Bank, Duke, Folk, and outsiders all claim authority to interpret crisis.
- **Moral contrast:** survival bargains can be necessary without becoming morally clean.
- **Material grounding:** food, fuel, walls, roads, labor, and winter matter before metaphysics escalates.
- **Magic as institution:** relics, artifacts, warlock applications, and occult systems change politics.
- **Geography as pressure:** Grenzburg's frontier position creates the story; the plot should not work anywhere else.

## Required Local Anchors

Grenzburg should have:

- a clear map of city, villages, roads, woods, rivers, barrows, mines, and winter approaches,
- named institutions for rationing, debt, worship, law, labor, and defense,
- a war-magic footprint: licensed warlocks, artillery scripts, ruined Shell scars, or Sestaran/Vortharan consultants,
- a relic/artifact economy tied to Bank and Church pressure,
- Folk sacred sites and legal claims,
- Grobi migration routes and internal politics,
- at least one thin space or buried metaphysical wound that explains why the Heart-Stone matters.

## Game Design Rule

Every major quest should pressure at least three layers:

1. material survival,
2. faction legitimacy,
3. metaphysical consequence.

If a quest only solves a combat problem, it is not yet a Grenzburg quest.
