# Sandbox Quest Pattern

This note records the quest-building lessons Grenzburg takes from the blocked adventure library. It is a production pattern, not permission to import those adventures' names, plots, locations, casts, or outcomes.

## When to Use It

Use this pattern for companion act finales, marquee quests, major dungeons, faction climaxes, regional campaigns, and any story expected to support several sessions or several distinct gameplay phases.

A simple rescue, hunt, delivery, defense, or confrontation may remain simple.

## Required Structure

### Central Truth

State what actually happened, who is responsible, what the opposition wants, and which apparent explanations are false. Moral and metaphysical complexity cannot replace causal clarity.

### Immediate Human Hook

Begin with a person, community, loss, danger, or practical need. Explain why the player acts now before asking them to understand the deepest lore.

### Open Preparation

Offer several useful operations, leads, allies, discoveries, or material preparations. Each should provide both information and a concrete advantage. Preparation improves the climax rather than merely filling a completion bar.

### Multiple Approaches

Support at least two credible combinations of social play, investigation, traversal, stealth, combat, preparation, or direct assault. Every player vocation retains a critical route.

### Legible State

Use only the state tracks needed to make pressure understandable. A track must change encounters, available choices, cost, survival, access, or the ending.

### Fail-Forward Play

Failed investigation, rescue, negotiation, or assault changes the next problem. It may cost time, people, evidence, resources, position, trust, or safety. It does not normally remove the route to the conclusion.

### Prepared Finale

Earlier work must visibly affect the final space, opposition, rescue burden, ally presence, environmental options, or ending choices. No preparation asset should exist only as a line of dialogue.

### Persistent Ending

Record several coherent outcomes without selecting a player result as future canon. The place, people, access, services, enemies, and later encounters retain the chosen state.

## Information Standard

Every required fact has at least two sources drawn from observation, testimony, records, material evidence, cultural knowledge, companion expertise, or experimentation. A single failed roll cannot permanently hide the story's necessary truth.

## Scope Control

Complexity must belong to the premise. Do not add a Bank conspiracy, secret patron, contract, soul route, universal mastermind, or moral inversion merely to make a quest appear important.

## Blocked-Adventure Boundary

The conversion library is a craft laboratory. Grenzburg may borrow structural lessons such as open leads, preparation assets, clocks, routes, and fail-forward endings. Direct integration requires a separate canon decision.
