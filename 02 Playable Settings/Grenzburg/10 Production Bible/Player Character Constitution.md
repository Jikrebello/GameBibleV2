# Player Character Constitution

This note controls who the player is, what character creation means, how the game communicates social risk, and how vocations grow. Detailed trees and numerical balance belong to the player-systems wave.

## Fixed Identity

The protagonist is a silent, customizable outsider whose debt has been converted into service in the [[Blackjack Corps]]. They enter the region in a Medori debtor convoy on the northern road and are seconded to Grenzburg as a field specialist during the emergency.

The player is not:

- native to Grenzburg;
- a secret heir, foretold sovereign, or chosen savior;
- commander of the Blackjacks, ducal army, militia, or companions;
- automatically loyal to the Bank or Duke;
- free of debt at the beginning.

The Duke and his officers drive the public war effort. The Bank controls the player's service account, equipment access, and the threat of collection. Bank pressure appears through specific assignments, faction quests, consequences, and personal leverage. It is not a second brief attached to every main quest.

## Silent-Protagonist Rule

The player never receives a fixed spoken voice or authored personality. Dialogue choices communicate intent, knowledge, temperament, and moral position without pretending that the protagonist speaks only in slogans.

Responses should be concise enough to scan and precise enough that the player understands the commitment. Important choices may expose follow-up questions before commitment. Silence, refusal, deception, mercy, threat, cultural recognition, and practical problem-solving should all be viable forms of expression.

## Character Creation Layers

Character creation separates six decisions that must not be collapsed into a single biography package.

### 1. Origin Culture

Origin culture determines childhood context, some recognitions, starting relationships to custom, and occasional reactions from others. It does not prescribe morality or lock the player into one faction.

### 2. Cultural Knowledge

The player selects meaningful familiarity beyond origin. Nine controlled knowledge tags are supported:

- Eclessian;
- Thalmyric;
- Sestaran;
- Jirahar;
- Ahl-e-Ahd;
- Folk;
- Ättar;
- Tarkhan;
- Nsizwa.

A cultural tag recognizes institutions, etiquette, language registers, rituals, history, material culture, and likely misunderstandings. It never functions as racial mind-reading and never proves that an individual conforms to a stereotype.

### 3. Background Knowledge

Background knowledge records learned work and lived experience such as soldiering, criminal practice, trade, scholarship, pilgrimage, hunting, medicine, craft, delving, or court service. It supplies practical options, not a complete class.

### 4. Social Focus

The player chooses an initial strength among the game's social approaches. Exact skill names and formulas will be locked in the player-systems wave, but every social check must identify the governing skill before commitment.

### 5. Moral Tendencies

Character creation may establish an initial leaning among visible virtue and sin tendencies. These are habits, not alignment labels and not random dialogue deletion.

### 6. Starting Vocation

The player selects one of six starting combat vocations. Vocation choice determines the first coherent combat kit, not the permanent limits of the character.

## Social Check Interface

Before a checked response is selected, the interface displays:

- the governing social skill or knowledge tag;
- any visible situational advantage or penalty;
- a qualitative risk band.

The controlled risk bands are **Favored**, **Even**, **Risky**, and **Dire**. Exact target values, random rolls, and Luck adjustments remain hidden. The result must follow from character capability and circumstances rather than feeling arbitrary.

A failed social check changes the negotiation: price, trust, evidence, timing, hostility, or available compromise. It should rarely terminate the whole quest.

## Virtue and Sin Tendencies

Moral tendencies are visible in the character record and move through sustained behavior. Thresholds can unlock recognitions, vows, temptations, companion responses, training, and rare choices. They can also make certain commitments unavailable when the player's established character would render them incoherent.

Rules:

- one isolated choice does not redefine the character;
- ordinary dialogue lines are never randomly removed;
- threshold changes receive clear feedback;
- the system evaluates repeated conduct, not faction loyalty;
- generosity is not always prudence, severity is not always cruelty, and lawfulness is not automatically virtue;
- objective evils remain evil even when a tendency or skill makes them easier to perform.

## Vocation Constitution

Vocations are coherent combat learning profiles. The player may use a pure profile or combine two unlocked profiles into a hybrid. Switching between previously learned pure and hybrid configurations is reversible outside immediate danger.

Unlocked weapon, armor, active-skill, and augment proficiencies may be mixed freely. A vocation accelerates mastery and supplies signature interactions; it does not erase knowledge already earned.

### Starting Vocations

| Vocation | Primary fantasy | Starting combat grammar |
|---|---|---|
| Fighter | Armored line-holder and weapon master. | Sword and shield, guard control, counters, armor, and formation pressure. |
| Slayer | Mobile close-range killer and infiltrator. | Paired blades, grappling line, climbing attacks, evasion, weak-point exploitation, and stealth. |
| Ranger | Frontier hunter and mobile marksman. | Bow, spear or light blade, tracking, traps, ammunition craft, monster reading, and terrain control. |
| Warlock | Scripted elemental battle-mage. | War wands, prepared rune phrases, elemental weaving, counterscripts, and controlled Shell draw. |
| Medium | Commander of temporary combat shades. | Imprints drawn from defeated enemies, short-lived minions, fear, positioning, and residue discipline. |
| Rune Fist | Melee-first rune combatant. | Inscribed gauntlets, stance chains, impact scripts, short wards, elemental strikes, and close-range spell release. |

### Medium Safeguard

Medium shades are temporary behavioral and combat imprints taken from the residue of a defeated form. They are not captured human souls, resurrected people, companions preserved after death, or evidence that personality survives in corpse residue. Human souls route onward according to established cosmology.

### Advanced Vocations Locked in Wave 1

- **Paladin:** a great-weapon sacred fighter learned through Bernard's resolved path. It combines commitment, protective challenge, heavy strikes, and White-Flame discipline. It is not simply a stronger Fighter.
- **Binder:** a bound-summon vocation learned through Zafir's resolved path. It uses lawful containment, inversion-breaking, and controlled manifested servants. It cannot legitimize slavery, soulbinding, or predatory patron ownership.

Druid, Witch, Monk, and further advanced vocations are reserved for later design. Their absence from Wave 1 is not a ruling that they do not exist in the wider world.

## Party and Field Role

The field party is always the player plus up to three active companions. The player selects party composition outside restricted encounters and at safe reorganization points. Inactive companions continue their own routines, react to world changes, and remain available unless their personal state removes them.

The protagonist remains the controlled character. Companions contribute combat behavior, traversal or investigation capabilities, camp and travel conversation, quest interventions, and personal judgement without replacing player agency.

## Debt and Freedom

Debt is a sustained pressure and a long-form player state, not a timer that causes arbitrary failure. It may change through payment, service credit, fraud exposure, sponsorship, political settlement, defiance, or escape. Becoming free must have material and relational consequences; remaining with the Blackjacks after freedom must be a meaningful choice rather than an invisible reset.

No single employer-refusal ends the campaign. Refusal changes access, equipment, debt terms, faction standing, or the danger applied to the player and their associates.
