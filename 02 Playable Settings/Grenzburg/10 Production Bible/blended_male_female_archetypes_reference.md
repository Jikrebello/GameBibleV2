# 1. Scope and method

This document extends eight recurring gender-coded narrative archetypes into ordered blends.

### Female base archetypes

- **Tomboy**
- **Goth girl**
- **Cheerleader**
- **Arty or bohemian girl**

### Male base archetypes

- **Jock**
- **Intellectual nerd**
- **Rebel**
- **Goofball**

No academic literature validates the resulting twenty-four combinations as literal personality categories. This is a narrative-development framework, not a clinical or psychological diagnostic system.

The framework is nevertheless consistent with several ideas from personality, identity and narrative research:

- Personality is better understood as a configuration of traits, motives, adaptations and self-narratives than as a single categorical type.
- People express different personality states across situations while retaining characteristic averages.
- Identity is partly constructed by negotiating cultural expectations and master narratives.
- Person-centred research examines profiles and configurations rather than assuming one trait explains the entire person.
- Narrative characters become more convincing when they contain more than one strategy for securing dignity, regulating threat and imagining a meaningful life.

The combinations are ordered. A Tomboy–Goth is not psychologically identical to a Goth–Tomboy, just as a Jock–Nerd is not the same as a Nerd–Jock.

The primary archetype supplies the character’s default survival and dignity strategy. The secondary modifies that strategy, compensates for its weaknesses and usually points toward the life the character hopes to construct.

# 2. How primary and secondary archetypes work

## The primary archetype

The primary archetype is the character’s default method of securing dignity and regulating danger.

It determines:

- What the character notices first.
- What kind of humiliation is intolerable.
- How the character responds under pressure.
- Which kind of power feels legitimate.
- What the character considers most real.
- Which defence reappears when other strategies collapse.
- What the character believes a respectable woman or man must be capable of doing.

A useful test is:

> **What does the character become when there is no time to curate the self?**

That reaction usually identifies the primary archetype.

## The secondary archetype

The secondary archetype is the character’s modifier, aspiration or compensatory strategy.

It influences:

- Social presentation.
- Preferred methods.
- Personal style.
- Vocation.
- What the character admires in others.
- What emerges in safety or intimacy.
- How the character compensates for the primary type’s limitations.
- What kind of life the character is trying to build once immediate survival or dignity is secured.

The secondary is often most visible around trusted friends, in creative work, during intimacy, in long-term ambition and when imagining an ideal future.

## Why order matters

### Tomboy → Goth

> “I enter the darkness because someone has to act.”

Action is primary. Darkness modifies what she acts against and how she interprets risk.

### Goth → Tomboy

> “I act because the darkness I perceive must be defended, contained or avenged.”

Perception, boundary and hidden truth are primary. Action becomes their enforcement mechanism.

### Jock → Nerd

> “I use intelligence to perform, lead and prevail more effectively.”

### Nerd → Jock

> “I develop physical courage and force so that knowledge can act in the world.”

The order changes the motivational centre, stress response, moral risk and character arc.

# 3. The psychoanalytic lens

The id–ego–superego model is used here as a literary interpretive vocabulary, not as a literal neurological model.

- **Id:** impulse, appetite and immediate desire.
- **Ego:** reality-testing, practical problem-solving and mediation.
- **Superego:** internalized standards, prohibitions and ideals.

In this framework:

- **Id asks:** What does the character want before editing or shaming the desire?
- **Ego asks:** How does the character realistically obtain or defend it?
- **Superego asks:** What kind of person does the character believe they ought to be?

The primary archetype usually governs the ego strategy. The secondary often colours the id, ego-ideal or style through which the primary is expressed.

A compelling hybrid usually has a contradictory superego:

- Tomboy–Cheerleader: “Always be capable” and “Never let the group down.”
- Goth–Cheerleader: “Never conform” and “Never embarrass the community you represent.”
- Jock–Rebel: “Never abandon the team” and “Never let the team own you.”
- Nerd–Goofball: “Be intellectually rigorous” and “Never become rigid or pompous.”

The character cannot obey both commands perfectly. That conflict creates coherent contradiction rather than random inconsistency.

# 4. Female archetype blends

The four female primaries regulate identity differently:

- **Tomboy-primary:** competence and autonomy.
- **Goth-primary:** authenticity, boundaries and hidden truth.
- **Cheerleader-primary:** belonging, visible excellence and social coordination.
- **Bohemian-primary:** creativity, experience and self-invention.

## 4.2 Tomboy-primary blends

The tomboy-primary woman asks first:

> **“What can I do about this?”**

Her secondary changes what she considers worth doing, how she presents competence and what she hopes competence will provide.

### 4.2.1 Tomboy primary, Goth secondary — The Shadow Operative

#### Core formula

> **“I act because I know the world is more dangerous and dishonest than it appears.”**

Her tomboy primary makes her practical, interventionist and operationally capable. Her goth secondary gives that competence a dark lens: suspicion, secrecy, morbidity, occult interest or psychological reserve.

She is not primarily a contemplative goth who happens to fight. She is a doer whose understanding of danger is goth-coded.

#### Outward expression

- Quietly athletic rather than openly competitive.
- Competent in stealth, investigation, survival or combat.
- Drawn to dark but practical clothing.
- Sarcastic rather than theatrical.
- Protective of outsiders but reluctant to admit attachment.
- More comfortable entering danger than attending an ordinary social event.
- Interested in death, trauma, taboo and hidden systems because they have practical consequences.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** autonomy through capability.
- **Secondary:** epistemic control over hidden danger.
- **Secret:** someone who can enter her dark world without demanding softness or normalization.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Physical or operational powerlessness.
2. Deception through trusted appearances.
3. Emotional exposure followed by control.
4. Discovering toughness cannot prevent loss.
5. Becoming similar to the threat she fights.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** intensity, danger, forbidden knowledge and decisive release. Anger feels cleaner than grief.
- **Ego:** preparation, competence, vigilance and action.
- **Superego:** be incorruptible, self-sufficient and impossible to victimize.

#### Light expression

- Brave without applause.
- Protective of stigmatized people.
- Unsentimental about danger.
- Able to act while others remain in denial.
- Loyal with secrets.
- Difficult to intimidate through social disapproval.
- Willing to enter frightening spaces for others.

Her goth secondary prevents pure impulsiveness by adding discernment and respect for trauma.

#### Shadow expression

- Hypervigilant.
- Punitive.
- Emotionally inaccessible.
- Suspicious of kindness.
- Addicted to danger.
- Contemptuous of less hardened people.
- Violates boundaries “for protection.”
- Treats private judgment as sufficient authority.

> **Distortion:** “Because danger is real, trust is foolish.”

#### Stress behaviour

Moderate stress makes her colder, more secretive and more operational. Extreme stress turns grief into mission fixation and replaces interpretation with obstacle removal.

#### Ethics and politics

- Anti-authoritarian.
- Civil-libertarian.
- Suspicious of surveillance and secrecy.
- Protective of outsiders.
- Pragmatic about force.
- Vulnerable to vigilantism.

#### Narrative arc

> **Trust is not the absence of vigilance; it is vigilance made reciprocal.**

She must learn cooperation without naïveté and vulnerability without passivity.

#### Illustrative characters

- Laura Kinney/X-23
- Cassandra Cain
- Shego, in a comic or antagonistic register
- Some versions of Nebula

#### Reversal distinction

- Tomboy → Goth: enters darkness to act.
- Goth → Tomboy: acts to defend or avenge dark truth.

### 4.2.2 Tomboy primary, Cheerleader secondary — The Field Captain

#### Core formula

> **“Competence gives me authority, and authority should make the group effective.”**

The tomboy supplies action-first orientation. The cheerleader secondary supplies coordination, confidence and morale awareness.

#### Outward expression

- Team captain rather than solitary fighter.
- Competitive but morale-conscious.
- Direct privately and charismatic publicly.
- Comfortable in tactical or athletic environments.
- Uses conventional presentation when useful.
- Protective of weaker members.
- Skilled at translating blunt truth into usable communication.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** prove herself through action.
- **Secondary:** legitimate recognition and leadership.
- **Secret:** belonging without surrendering autonomy.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Being treated as ornamental.
2. Losing authority through public failure.
3. Choosing between equality and belonging.
4. Letting the group down.
5. Becoming the controlling authority she resisted.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** compete, win, move and act.
- **Ego:** organize, delegate and convert competence into leadership.
- **Superego:** be strong, fair, composed and endlessly dependable.

#### Light expression

- Leads from the front.
- Gives public credit.
- Makes institutions accessible.
- Persuades where a pure tomboy would confront.
- Understands morale as a practical resource.
- Grounds status in contribution.

#### Shadow expression

- Protection becomes control.
- Leadership becomes micromanagement.
- Sacrifice generates entitlement.
- She defines who counts as a real contributor.
- Standards conceal rivalry.
- Dissent is suppressed.
- Indispensability becomes identity.

> **Distortion:** “If I am not controlling the situation, I am failing everyone.”

#### Stress behaviour

Becomes directive and less consultative. Emotional conflict becomes rules, assignments or takeover.

#### Ethics and politics

- Meritocratic communitarianism.
- Institutional reform.
- Equal-access feminism.
- Duty and civic participation.
- Competence-based hierarchy.

#### Narrative arc

> **Leadership does not require being the strongest person in every room.**

She must delegate, receive care and permit imperfect contribution.

#### Illustrative characters

- Buffy Summers
- Kim Possible
- Suki
- Some versions of Wonder Woman

#### Reversal distinction

- Tomboy → Cheerleader: leadership is justified by competence.
- Cheerleader → Tomboy: competence strengthens social legitimacy.

### 4.2.3 Tomboy primary, Bohemian secondary — The Maker-Adventurer

#### Core formula

> **“Freedom becomes real only when I can build, reach or experience it myself.”**

The tomboy supplies agency. The bohemian determines what the agency serves: art, exploration, invention, travel or unconventional life.

#### Outward expression

- Explorer, craftsperson or practical artist.
- Comfortable with tools and uncertainty.
- Values experience over prestige.
- Improvisational but not helpless.
- Protective of creative people.
- Emotionally more expressive than the pure tomboy.
- Restless in repetitive environments.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** freedom through self-sufficiency.
- **Secondary:** meaning, beauty and lived experience.
- **Secret:** recognition for imagination, not only endurance.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Dependence.
2. Routine.
3. Being useful but unknown.
4. Creative sterility.
5. Sacrificing desire to responsibility.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** movement, novelty, sensuality and creation.
- **Ego:** acquires skills and resources so freedom does not require permission.
- **Superego:** build rather than merely fantasize.

#### Light expression

- Makes dreams tangible.
- Inventive under constraint.
- Respects craft.
- Refuses the split between manual and artistic intelligence.
- Encourages active participation in life.
- Survives outside convention without expecting others to subsidize her.

#### Shadow expression

- Restlessness becomes flight.
- Abandons projects after novelty.
- Romanticizes hardship.
- Treats obligation as threat.
- Refuses help, then resents its absence.
- Accumulates experiences without continuity.

> **Distortion:** “Anything that ties me down will kill the real me.”

#### Stress behaviour

Moves, builds, travels or starts another project. Action may be adaptation or avoidance.

#### Ethics and politics

- Anti-paternalist.
- Environmental or localist.
- Pro-craft.
- Anti-credentialist.
- Progressive but practical.
- Sympathetic to cooperatives and mutual aid.

#### Narrative arc

> **Freedom is not only the ability to leave; it is the ability to finish and sustain what one chooses.**

#### Illustrative characters

- Jo March
- Ellie
- Lara Croft
- Some versions of Korra

#### Reversal distinction

- Tomboy → Bohemian: skill creates creative freedom.
- Bohemian → Tomboy: creativity recruits practical skill.

## 4.3 Goth-primary blends

The goth-primary woman asks first:

> **“What is hidden, repressed, false or forbidden here?”**

### 4.3.1 Goth primary, Tomboy secondary — The Occult Enforcer

#### Core formula

> **“The truth I perceive is worth defending, even when defence requires force.”**

She begins with hidden truth, boundary or estrangement, then acts.

#### Outward expression

- Reserved until a boundary is crossed.
- Skilled physically without defining herself through sport.
- Strategic rather than impulsive.
- Protective of secrets and outsiders.
- Capable of sudden decisiveness.
- Uninterested in proving toughness socially.
- Comfortable acting alone.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** authenticity, truth and psychic sovereignty.
- **Secondary:** competence to defend them.
- **Secret:** trust without total disclosure.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Assimilation or invasion.
2. Betrayal.
3. Inability to defend a boundary.
4. Loss of control.
5. Becoming feared by those she protects.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** forbidden knowledge, intensity and revenge.
- **Ego:** controlled access, observation and selective action.
- **Superego:** power must remain contained until morally justified.

#### Light expression

- Looks beneath appearances before acting.
- Protects people institutions overlook.
- Uses proportionate force.
- Respects consent and boundaries.
- Resists reputational manipulation.
- Combines introspection with courage.

#### Shadow expression

- Becomes judge and executioner.
- Suspicion becomes certainty.
- Hurt becomes universal law.
- Withholds information while demanding loyalty.
- Competence prevents intimacy.
- Punishment becomes justice.

> **Distortion:** “Because I see what others cannot, ordinary accountability does not apply.”

#### Stress behaviour

Withdraws, researches and watches before decisive confrontation.

#### Ethics and politics

- Strong privacy rights.
- Anti-persecution.
- Suspicion of majoritarian morality.
- Selective force.
- Defence of stigmatized people.
- Hostility to institutional gaslighting.

#### Narrative arc

> **Moral certainty requires external challenge, especially when one possesses unusual power.**

#### Illustrative characters

- Wednesday Addams
- Raven
- Nico Minoru
- Some versions of Morrigan

### 4.3.2 Goth primary, Cheerleader secondary — The Midnight Socialite

#### Core formula

> **“I refuse normality, but I understand social power well enough to use it selectively.”**

Her identity remains outsider-coded; her secondary supplies charisma, organization and social fluency.

#### Outward expression

- Darkly dressed but socially capable.
- Popular in alternative spaces.
- Host or organizer for outsiders.
- Cheerful about macabre subjects.
- Able to make unusual people welcome.
- Institutionally useful without normalization.
- Switches between irony and enthusiasm.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** authentic outsider identity.
- **Secondary:** community recognition.
- **Secret:** public value without domestication.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Becoming a mascot.
2. Acceptance only after sanitization.
3. Loss of outsider-group status.
4. Exposure of approval needs.
5. Discovering subculture is another performance economy.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** fascination, intensity and social attention.
- **Ego:** charm and coalition control reception of difference.
- **Superego:** remain authentic while representing outsiders well.

#### Light expression

- Builds hospitable outsider spaces.
- Uses social intelligence without surrendering truth.
- Makes darkness accessible without trivialization.
- Translates marginalized concerns institutionally.
- Allows joy within alienated identity.

#### Shadow expression

- Becomes a subcultural queen bee.
- Popularity masquerades as authenticity.
- Gatekeeps taste and dress.
- Outsiders become audience.
- Exclusion and secrets become weapons.

> **Distortion:** “I am not conformist because I control a different hierarchy.”

#### Stress behaviour

Increases impression management and loyalty tests.

#### Ethics and politics

- Socially progressive.
- Pro-representation.
- Coalition-oriented.
- Anti-censorship.
- Institutionally willing.
- Vulnerable to symbolic politics.

#### Narrative arc

> **Community is not authentic if belonging depends on passing a private test of authenticity.**

#### Illustrative characters

- Abby Sciuto
- Death from The Sandman
- Mavis
- Some versions of Sam Manson

### 4.3.3 Goth primary, Bohemian secondary — The Dark Romantic Artist

#### Core formula

> **“I will translate what is painful, forbidden or incomprehensible into meaning.”**

Goth supplies the subject; bohemianism supplies transformation through art.

#### Outward expression

- Photographer, poet, musician or filmmaker.
- Private about wounds but expressive through art.
- Attracted to ruins and marginal people.
- More fluid than the pure goth.
- Re-enchants dark subjects.
- Suspicious of commercialization.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** truthful confrontation with darkness.
- **Secondary:** transformation into beauty or communion.
- **Secret:** recognition of autobiographical truth without forced confession.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Pain being trivialized.
2. Aesthetic convention.
3. Creative sterility.
4. Commodification.
5. Recovery erasing depth.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** intensity, grief, beauty and dissolution.
- **Ego:** turns overwhelming material into symbols.
- **Superego:** never lie about darkness or create merely for approval.

#### Light expression

- Gives language to unspeakable experience.
- Dignifies grief without permanence.
- Creates outsider solidarity.
- Finds beauty in the discarded.
- Transforms pain into understanding.
- Refuses compulsory optimism.

#### Shadow expression

- Romanticizes deterioration.
- Invests identity in suffering.
- Provokes crisis.
- Uses people as material.
- Confuses obscurity with quality.
- Resents recovery.
- Treats happiness as unserious.

> **Distortion:** “If the pain goes, the truth and art will go with it.”

#### Stress behaviour

Retreats into artistic production, journaling, fantasy or aesthetic curation.

#### Ethics and politics

- Anti-censorship.
- Pro-art funding.
- Restorative or anti-carceral.
- Suspicious of exploitation.
- Attentive to memory and cultural erasure.
- Vulnerable to aesthetic radicalism without strategy.

#### Narrative arc

> **Healing does not falsify art; it changes the material from which new art can be made.**

#### Illustrative characters

- Lydia Deetz
- Marceline
- Jane Lane
- Some versions of Delirium

## 4.4 Cheerleader-primary blends

The cheerleader-primary woman asks:

> **“Where do I stand in the group, and how do I shape what it becomes?”**

### 4.4.1 Cheerleader primary, Tomboy secondary — The Athletic Social Captain

#### Core formula

> **“I belong at the centre because I can perform, contribute and withstand pressure.”**

#### Outward expression

- Polished and physically disciplined.
- Comfortable with ceremony and competition.
- Status-conscious.
- Proud of femininity and strength.
- Makes achievement publicly visible.
- Expands rather than rejects feminine possibility.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** recognized belonging and leadership.
- **Secondary:** real competence.
- **Secret:** respect without diminished femininity.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Public humiliation.
2. Being ornamental.
3. Losing rank.
4. Being too aggressive or insufficiently feminine.
5. Irrelevance after decline.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** applause, movement and victory.
- **Ego:** discipline and presentation secure authority.
- **Superego:** represent the group flawlessly and prove feminine strength.

#### Light expression

- Unites femininity and competence.
- Understands morale and preparation.
- Mentors others.
- Treats visibility as responsibility.
- Mobilizes during crisis.

#### Shadow expression

- Everything becomes ranking.
- Polices appearance and effort.
- Humiliates weaker members.
- Toughness becomes inclusion price.
- Cannot tolerate another woman at the centre.
- Defends institutions that rewarded her.

> **Distortion:** “If I earned my place under these rules, the rules must be fair.”

#### Stress behaviour

Trains harder, tightens discipline and controls presentation.

#### Ethics and politics

- Equal opportunity.
- Meritocracy.
- Institutional feminism.
- Reform rather than rejection.
- Vulnerable to respectability politics.

#### Narrative arc

> **Personal excellence does not prove the institution distributes dignity fairly.**

#### Illustrative characters

- Torrance Shipman
- Brittany Taylor
- Some versions of Princess Peach
- Girly-bruiser superhero and magical-girl types

### 4.4.2 Cheerleader primary, Goth secondary — The Dark Queen

#### Core formula

> **“I understand the social order, but I refuse to appear harmless within it.”**

#### Outward expression

- Popular with dark styling or power.
- Socially commanding but unreadable.
- Fascinated by the occult while central.
- Attractive and threatening.
- Creates mystique and exclusivity.
- Controls reputation and rumour.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** social centrality.
- **Secondary:** uniqueness and immunity from ordinary judgment.
- **Secret:** belonging without predictability or replaceability.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Replacement.
2. Exposure.
3. Ordinariness.
4. Loss of narrative control.
5. Fear without love.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** admiration, intensity and control.
- **Ego:** alliances plus dark signals deter challenge.
- **Superego:** never appear needy, naïve or common.

#### Light expression

- Protects stigmatized people from inside hierarchy.
- Exposes respectable hypocrisy.
- Makes mainstream room for nonconformity.
- Leads in dark situations.
- Refuses compulsory harmlessness.

#### Shadow expression

- Weaponizes secrets.
- Rules through fascination and fear.
- Turns exclusion into theatre.
- Cultivates others’ vulnerability.
- Treats people as court or enemies.
- Uses privilege to perform transgression.

> **Distortion:** “If people cannot fully know me, they cannot replace or control me.”

#### Stress behaviour

Increases surveillance, coldness, indirect leaks and loyalty tests.

#### Ethics and politics

- Socially liberal but hierarchical.
- Strong on representation.
- Weak on democratization.
- Elite transgression is the political shadow.

#### Narrative arc

> **Mystique creates power; reciprocal vulnerability creates loyalty.**

#### Illustrative characters

- Cheryl Blossom
- Lydia Martin
- Jennifer Check
- Dark queen figures in teen horror

### 4.4.3 Cheerleader primary, Bohemian secondary — The Cultural Curator

#### Core formula

> **“I will make belonging beautiful, exciting and culturally meaningful.”**

#### Outward expression

- Performer, fashion leader or event organizer.
- Socially strategic and aesthetically experimental.
- Audience-aware.
- Connects artists with resources.
- More individualistic than standard cheerleader.
- Redesigns rather than rejects social life.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** visible influence.
- **Secondary:** authorship of group style and tone.
- **Secret:** recognition as a creator rather than consumer.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Irrelevance.
2. Derivativeness.
3. Losing the audience.
4. Creativity dismissed as vanity.
5. Dependence on trends.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** beauty, pleasure, novelty and admiration.
- **Ego:** networks and timing turn ideas into events.
- **Superego:** elevate the group and remain aspirational.

#### Light expression

- Makes culture accessible.
- Creates platforms.
- Understands audiences and logistics.
- Turns rituals into genuine celebration.
- Moves institutions emotionally.
- Gives social execution to imagination.

#### Shadow expression

- Creativity becomes branding.
- People become accessories.
- Appropriates outsider aesthetics.
- Attention replaces substance.
- Friendship becomes networking.
- Taste becomes moral superiority.

> **Distortion:** “If I can make people want it, I must have made something valuable.”

#### Stress behaviour

Produces more spectacle, reinvention and public projects.

#### Ethics and politics

- Institutionally progressive.
- Pro-arts.
- Representation-oriented.
- Market-comfortable.
- Vulnerable to commodification.

#### Narrative arc

> **An audience is not a community, and visibility is not authorship.**

#### Illustrative characters

- Cher Horowitz
- Elle Woods
- Sharpay Evans
- Some versions of Midge Maisel

## 4.5 Bohemian-primary blends

The bohemian-primary woman asks:

> **“What kind of life could I create rather than inherit?”**

### 4.5.1 Bohemian primary, Tomboy secondary — The Practical Free Spirit

#### Core formula

> **“I follow possibility, and I acquire whatever skills are needed to keep possibility open.”**

#### Outward expression

- Adventurous artist.
- Resourceful traveller.
- Experimental craftsperson.
- Energetic but not status-oriented.
- Spontaneous yet capable.
- Comfortable changing identity or vocation.
- Toughness appears when necessary.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** experience and creation.
- **Secondary:** competence protecting freedom.
- **Secret:** companions who join without creating hierarchy.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Deadening inherited life.
2. Dependence.
3. Loss of curiosity.
4. Incapacity when opportunity appears.
5. Permanent identity foreclosure.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** novelty, pleasure and discovery.
- **Ego:** improvises and learns quickly.
- **Superego:** fear must not make her conventional.

#### Light expression

- Curiosity becomes exploration.
- Adapts rather than fantasizes.
- Gives permission to experiment.
- Learns from unfamiliar communities.
- Uses capability to protect.
- Survives failure imaginatively.

#### Shadow expression

- Runs when novelty fades.
- Treats danger as self-discovery.
- Ignores planning.
- Leaves consequences to others.
- Caricatures commitment as captivity.
- Turns encounters into identity material.
- Confuses motion with growth.

> **Distortion:** “As long as I am moving, I cannot be trapped.”

#### Stress behaviour

Changes environment instead of staying with the problem.

#### Ethics and politics

- Experiential education.
- Environmentalism.
- Freedom of movement.
- Mutual aid.
- Social experimentation.
- May underestimate structural inequality.

#### Narrative arc

> **A chosen boundary can deepen experience rather than terminate it.**

#### Illustrative characters

- Rapunzel
- Luz Noceda
- Anne Shirley
- Some versions of Amélie

### 4.5.2 Bohemian primary, Goth secondary — The Melancholic Visionary

#### Core formula

> **“I create new worlds from places where ordinary reality feels haunted or incomplete.”**

#### Outward expression

- Dreamy, artistic and eerie.
- Open to supernatural possibility.
- Moves between whimsy and melancholy.
- Fascinated by symbols and forgotten places.
- Playful with darkness.
- More transformative than authenticity-policing.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** creative self-expansion.
- **Secondary:** mystery, loss and depth.
- **Secret:** others entering her imaginative world without pathologizing it.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Imaginative deadness.
2. Literal normality.
3. Meaningless suffering.
4. Loss of beauty in darkness.
5. Being consumed by the material.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** fantasy, beauty, strangeness and immersion.
- **Ego:** stories and symbols make experience tolerable.
- **Superego:** creation must be original and deep.

#### Light expression

- Turns grief into possibility.
- Reveals meaning outside consensus.

## 4.7 Female situational responses

### Immediate danger

- Tomboy-primary acts.
- Goth-primary diagnoses.
- Cheerleader-primary organizes.
- Bohemian-primary reframes and improvises.

### Social rejection

- Tomboy proves usefulness or leaves.
- Goth withdraws and converts rejection into outsider identity.
- Cheerleader repairs or retakes position.
- Bohemian reinvents or finds another community.

### Grief

- Tomboy–Goth: mission.
- Tomboy–Cheerleader: responsibility for everyone.
- Tomboy–Bohemian: movement or creation.
- Goth–Tomboy: withdrawal then confrontation.
- Goth–Cheerleader: public stability and private intensification.
- Goth–Bohemian: art.
- Cheerleader–Tomboy: disciplined group maintenance.
- Cheerleader–Goth: narrative control.
- Cheerleader–Bohemian: ritual or spectacle.
- Bohemian–Tomboy: active new chapter.
- Bohemian–Goth: symbolic processing.
- Bohemian–Cheerleader: communal remembrance.

### Success risks

- Tomboy hybrids: addiction to indispensability.
- Goth hybrids: outsider identity incompatible with happiness.
- Cheerleader hybrids: recognition mistaken for moral correctness.
- Bohemian hybrids: continuity abandoned after establishment.

## 4.8 Female light and shadow by primary

### Tomboy-primary

- **Light:** competence, courage, fairness, intervention and protection.
- **Shadow:** control, impatience, emotional refusal and contempt for dependency.
- **Corrections:** Goth adds discernment; Cheerleader coordination; Bohemian meaning.
- **Corruptions:** Goth paranoia; Cheerleader control; Bohemian flight.

### Goth-primary

- **Light:** authenticity, insight, boundaries and outsider protection.
- **Shadow:** nihilism, secrecy, superiority and romanticized damage.
- **Corrections:** Tomboy action; Cheerleader connection; Bohemian transformation.
- **Corruptions:** Tomboy punishment; Cheerleader hierarchy; Bohemian aestheticized pain.

### Cheerleader-primary

- **Light:** loyalty, coordination, morale and institutional skill.
- **Shadow:** conformity, comparison, exclusion and domination.
- **Corrections:** Tomboy competence; Goth depth; Bohemian creativity.
- **Corruptions:** Tomboy harsh meritocracy; Goth intimidation; Bohemian branding.

### Bohemian-primary

- **Light:** creativity, openness, experiential courage and freedom.
- **Shadow:** irresponsibility, exemption, instability and weak continuity.
- **Corrections:** Tomboy capacity; Goth depth; Cheerleader community.
- **Corruptions:** Tomboy recklessness; Goth dissociation; Cheerleader attention dependence.

## 4.9 Writing female blended characters

- The secondary must solve one primary weakness and create another.
- Show the primary in crisis and secondary in safety.
- Give the two archetypes different fears.
- Build a contradictory superego.
- Integrate rather than delete either type.

### Female compressed matrix

- Tomboy–Goth: *I become capable so darkness cannot surprise or control me.*
- Tomboy–Cheerleader: *I become capable so I can lead and protect the group.*
- Tomboy–Bohemian: *I become capable so I can live and create without permission.*
- Goth–Tomboy: *I defend truths and boundaries normal society refuses to respect.*
- Goth–Cheerleader: *I build belonging for difference without surrendering difference.*
- Goth–Bohemian: *I transform darkness into beauty, meaning and communication.*
- Cheerleader–Tomboy: *Visible femininity and competence can occupy the same centre.*
- Cheerleader–Goth: *I master the social world without appearing ordinary or harmless.*
- Cheerleader–Bohemian: *I turn social life into a creative cultural experience.*
- Bohemian–Tomboy: *I develop competence so freedom remains materially possible.*
- Bohemian–Goth: *I create from mystery and loss without accepting ordinary reality as final.*
- Bohemian–Cheerleader: *I turn imagination into shared experience and community.*

> **Female central principle:** The primary explains how she survives. The secondary explains what kind of life she is trying to build once survival is secured.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** amusement, novelty and attention.
- **Ego:** humour tests ideas and smuggles insight.
- **Superego:** use real understanding to help.

#### Light expression

- Detects overlooked patterns.
- Makes knowledge accessible.
- Asks obvious questions.
- Uses analogy as cognition.
- Reduces intellectual hierarchy.
- Understands emotional systems.

#### Shadow expression

- Performs incompetence.
- Hides insight.
- Refuses discipline.
- Turns correction into jokes.
- Resents lack of recognition while concealing effort.
- Sabotages success to preserve role.

> **Distortion:** “If people never know how capable I am, they cannot demand enough to expose my limits.”

#### Stress behaviour

Becomes chaotic and associative; either humour collapses or hidden intelligence takes command.

#### Ethics and politics

- Informal knowledge-sharing.
- Educational play.
- Anti-credentialism.
- Open-source culture.
- Vulnerable to dilettantism.

#### Narrative arc

> **Being known as capable creates obligations, but also contribution and self-respect.**

#### Illustrative characters

- Sokka
- The Doctor
- Ford Prefect
- Milo Murphy
- Some versions of Jimmy Olsen

### 5.5.3 Goofball primary, Rebel secondary — The Anarchic Trickster

#### Core formula

> **“Authority depends on everyone taking it seriously, so I will attack its seriousness.”**

#### Outward expression

- Class clown targeting authority.
- Satirist.
- Prankster exposing hypocrisy.
- Slacker revealing arbitrary rules.
- Trickster hero.
- Refuses official framing.
- More playful than the Rebel–Goofball.

#### Desire hierarchy

- **Primary:** emotional and imaginative freedom.
- **Secondary:** removal of humiliating authority.
- **Secret:** a cause he can join without losing the right to question it.

#### Fear hierarchy

1. Humiliating control.
2. Fixed roles.
3. Solemn commitment.
4. Becoming object of the joke.
5. Harming people weaker than the target.

#### Id, ego and superego

- **Id:** pleasure, chaos and reversal.
- **Ego:** humour disrupts scripts.
- **Superego:** no authority should be immune to ridicule.

#### Light expression

- Punctures authoritarian mystique.
- Exposes hypocrisy.
- Lets frightened groups laugh.
- Finds nonviolent resistance.
- Resists shame.
- Challenges accepted absurdity.
- Prevents movements becoming pompous.

#### Shadow expression

- Automatic contrarianism.
- Every boundary becomes oppression.
- Pranks target the vulnerable.
- Disruption replaces change.
- Finds hypocrisy to avoid commitment.
- Destroys trust in the name of liberation.

> **Distortion:** “Anything sincere, structured or sacred is secretly trying to control me.”

#### Stress behaviour

Becomes more provocative and escalatory.

#### Ethics and politics

- Anarchic.
- Anti-authoritarian.
- Anti-censorship.
- Socially libertarian.
- Anti-elite.
- Vulnerable to empty anti-politics.

#### Narrative arc

> **Structure is not automatically prison, and causes require responsibility.**

- Bugs Bunny
- Ferris Bueller
- Bart Simpson
- The Mask
- The Dude
- Some versions of Loki

## 5.8 Male light and shadow by primary

### Jock-primary

- **Light:** courage, discipline, reliability, protection and leadership.
- **Shadow:** domination, status anxiety and emotional repression.
- **Corrections:** Nerd strategy; Rebel independence; Goofball warmth.
- **Corruptions:** Nerd technocracy; Rebel entitlement; Goofball bullying humour.

### Nerd-primary

- **Light:** curiosity, precision, foresight and ingenuity.
- **Shadow:** condescension, resentment and treating people as systems.
- **Corrections:** Jock implementation; Rebel resistance; Goofball flexibility.
- **Corruptions:** Jock competence supremacy; Rebel elitist radicalism; Goofball irresponsibility.

### Rebel-primary

- **Light:** autonomy, integrity, courage against authority and chosen loyalty.
- **Shadow:** selfishness, contrarianism, abandonment and domination disguised as freedom.
- **Corrections:** Jock discipline; Nerd strategy; Goofball warmth.
- **Corruptions:** Jock strongman behaviour; Nerd manipulation; Goofball charming irresponsibility.

### Goofball-primary

- **Light:** humour, resilience, accessibility and anti-pretension.
- **Shadow:** avoidance, irresponsibility, self-sabotage and arrested development.
- **Corrections:** Jock dependability; Nerd insight; Rebel resistance.
- **Corruptions:** Jock recklessness; Nerd rationalization; Rebel compulsive disruption.

## 5.9 Writing male blended characters

- The secondary must solve one weakness and create another.
- Show the primary under involuntary pressure.
- Give primary and secondary separate fears.
- Construct a contradictory superego.
- Distinguish virtue from status defence.
- Do not make vulnerability erase masculinity.
- Test the hybrid through failure of the secondary.

### Male compressed matrix

- Jock–Nerd: *I use intelligence to make strength and leadership more effective.*
- Jock–Rebel: *I prove myself without allowing an institution to own me.*
- Jock–Goofball: *I carry responsibility without letting pressure make the group inhuman.*
- Nerd–Jock: *I develop courage so knowledge can act in the world.*
- Nerd–Rebel: *I understand systems deeply enough to know when obedience is surrender.*
- Nerd–Goofball: *I use play to keep intelligence creative and accessible.*
- Rebel–Jock: *I become strong enough that freedom does not depend on permission.*
- Rebel–Nerd: *I master power’s mechanisms so they cannot capture me.*
- Rebel–Goofball: *I turn defiance into charm so freedom remains connected to people.*
- Goofball–Jock: *I preserve play but become formidable when loved ones require it.*
- Goofball–Nerd: *My apparent foolishness contains serious intelligence.*
- Goofball–Rebel: *I use humour to stop authority and shame becoming absolute.*

> **Male central principle:** The primary explains how he protects dignity. The secondary explains how he expands that protection into a fuller identity.

# 6. Unified comparative framework

## Competence-regulation and freedom-regulation

### Competence-regulation systems

- Tomboy
- Jock
- Nerd
- To a degree, Cheerleader

These defend dignity by becoming capable, effective, recognized or indispensable.

### Freedom-regulation systems

- Goth
- Bohemian
- Rebel
- Goofball

These defend dignity by resisting assimilation, constructing alternatives, refusing control or making pressure emotionally nonabsolute.

## What each primary does under pressure

| Primary | First response |
|---|---|
| Tomboy | Act |
| Goth | Diagnose hidden truth or boundary |
| Cheerleader | Organize the social field |
| Bohemian | Reframe and improvise |
| Jock | Perform, lead or restore rank |
| Nerd | Analyse and master |
| Rebel | Resist, exit or reclaim autonomy |
| Goofball | Defuse, reframe or joke |

## What each secondary adds

| Secondary | Added function |
|---|---|
| Tomboy | Practical courage and intervention |
| Goth | Suspicion, depth and boundary sensitivity |
| Cheerleader | Coordination, visibility and legitimacy |
| Bohemian | Creativity, meaning and possibility |
| Jock | Discipline, embodiment and protection |
| Nerd | Analysis, foresight and technical mastery |
| Rebel | Independence, dissent and resistance |
| Goofball | Humour, accessibility and resilience |

## Core writing structure

A useful hybrid contains:

1. Primary virtue.
2. Primary wound.
3. Secondary correction.
4. Secondary temptation.
5. A conflict in which the correction fails.
6. A mature synthesis preserving both types.

### Example: Tomboy–Cheerleader

- Virtue: competence.
- Wound: helplessness.
- Correction: social coordination.
- Temptation: control.
- Failure: the group rejects takeover.
- Synthesis: leadership that delegates.

### Example: Nerd–Rebel

- Virtue: understanding.
- Wound: intellectual powerlessness.
- Correction: resistance to capture.
- Temptation: elitist radicalism.
- Failure: unilateral exposure harms innocents.
- Synthesis: principled dissent with accountability.

## Central conclusion

> **The primary archetype explains how the character survives or protects dignity. The secondary explains the fuller life the character is trying to build.**

The hybrid becomes compelling when the secondary strategy promises growth but also creates a new defence.

The mature hybrid does not abandon either archetype. It learns to use the primary without being imprisoned by it and pursue the secondary without turning it into another avoidance mechanism.
