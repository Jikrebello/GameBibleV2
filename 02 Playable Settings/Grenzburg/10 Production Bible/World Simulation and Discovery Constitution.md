# World Simulation and Discovery Constitution

This note controls the unmarked and systemic layer that makes Grenzburg worth inhabiting between authored quests. It supplements [[Grenzburg Worldspace Location Register]] without turning the game into a global economy simulation.

## Experience Target

Ordinary on-foot exploration should present a meaningful discovery, sign, encounter, or decision about every five to eight minutes on average. The director must preserve quiet travel, distant anticipation, weather, music, and safe return. It may not fill every interval merely because a card is available.

A meaningful opportunity may be:

- a person or group traveling, working, hiding, arguing, or asking for practical help;
- signs of a nearby threat, hunt, camp, ruin, or changed route;
- faction, military, criminal, or settlement activity;
- ecology, predation, scavenging, migration, or creature behavior;
- weather, terrain, damage, or traversal pressure;
- a small discovery, memorial, cache, scene of aftermath, or environmental story;
- a companion observation or identity-specific complication.

## Authored Systemic Decks

Every card is written and reviewed by an author. Runtime selection may combine conditions; it may not generate unsupported lore or dialogue.

Cards may read:

- district, exterior region, road, river, or local landmark;
- campaign act and current season;
- weather and time of day;
- route safety, settlement survival, and recent quest outputs;
- faction presence and patrol control;
- player origin, background, vocation, public reputation, and criminal exposure;
- active companions and autonomous companion assignments;
- one-shot, finite-repeat, or repeatable history.

## Production Targets

- Each exterior region reserves 16-24 authored cards across the campaign.
- Each city district reserves 8-12 authored cards.
- Cross-region road, river, weather, and convoy decks supplement local cards.
- At least half of a deck must be one-shot or finite.
- No more than one third of a deck may repeat freely.
- Repeatable cards require route, target, weather, faction, or encounter variation and a cooldown.

These are provisional production targets. Playtesting may change density without changing the world rules.

Named discoveries and unique aftermath scenes are one-shot. Only credible patrols, hunts, camps, travelers, work crews, bounties, and job families may repeat.

## Runtime Reach

Systemic encounters instantiate only near the player. The game does not continuously resolve every caravan, patrol, predator, and crime elsewhere on the map.

Distant change occurs only through:

- main-story milestones;
- faction, companion, marquee, or local quest outcomes;
- explicit seasonal transitions;
- authored cutoff and autonomous-resolution rules.

This boundary prevents invisible simulation from destroying content while still allowing the world to move without the protagonist.

## Persistence Boundary

Systemic results may alter:

- road safety and temporary closures;
- patrol and camp presence;
- prices, shortages, and local services;
- generic travelers, workers, refugees, prisoners, and hostile populations;
- later card availability and follow-up encounters;
- renewable nests, hunts, bounties, and job sites.

They may not silently:

- kill a named principal character;
- destroy a major settlement;
- decide a faction line;
- resolve a companion's defining moral choice;
- reopen a permanently cleared named dungeon;
- reverse a campaign casualty or political settlement.

Those outcomes require bespoke authored content.

## Discovery Rules

Public roads, settlements, and major terrain remain visible on the starting survey map. Minor paths, ruins, waystones, caves, camps, and secret sites appear through sight, rumor, purchased maps, testimony, companion knowledge, or exploration.

An encounter should normally be legible in the world before its map marker appears. Smoke, tracks, bells, birds, damaged carts, fleeing animals, distant fighting, lights, or changed traffic are preferable to unexplained interface alerts.

## Encounter Consequence

An encounter may finish immediately, begin a short chain, seed a formal quest, transform a later card, or leave a persistent scene. Failure may produce injury, loss, pursuit, a closed route, a hostile return, or a changed witness. It should not normally erase the event without consequence.

## Danger and Scaling

Regional danger bands remain meaningful. Cards select suitable threats; they do not make every wolf, bandit, Grobi patrol, or residue formation scale directly to player level. Early entry into dangerous pockets remains possible, readable, and escapable.

## Postgame Use

After [[The First Summer Road]], encounter decks use postwar seasonal variants controlled by [[Postgame Reconstruction Cycle]]. They may rotate work, weather, ecology, remnants, and renewable jobs without replaying the siege, Drake hunt, dead march, or destroyed dungeon states.
