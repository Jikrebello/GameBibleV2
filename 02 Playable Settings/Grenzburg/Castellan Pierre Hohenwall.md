### “The Wall Is the Law” (Duchy: Good)

**Role in the Rule of Three:**  
He is the Duchy’s “Good” engine: **competence, discipline, predictability**. If Grenzburg survives winter on paper and in steel, it’s because Pierre runs the machine. He is also the person most likely to collide with the Duke’s Ugly compromises and the Bank/Church’s creeping capture.

---

## Core concept

A professional commander-administrator hybrid: part fortress engineer, part quartermaster, part judge. He believes the frontier only stays human through **procedures**—not heroics.

**He is what keeps Grenzburg from becoming a hungry mob with a duke-shaped hat.**

---

## Public face

- The man on the battlements in a storm, the one who knows the mortar is failing before anyone else does.
    
- Speaks in numbers and schedules: “days of grain,” “cordwood per district,” “manning rotations,” “ice thickness.”
    
- Respected by soldiers, feared by anyone trying to cheat the ration system.
    

---

## Private truth

- He thinks the Duke is necessary but dangerously soft on “exceptions.”
    
- He views the Church and Bank as **rival sovereigns** trying to colonize the state.
    
- He has a deep, quiet hatred for romantic adventuring into ruins during summer because it creates winter problems (artifacts, cursed objects, new claimants, new factions).
    

---

## Goals

### Short-term (this winter)

1. Keep the walls intact and manned (including outworks, docks, and granaries)
    
2. Keep the ration system credible (no collapse into black market control)
    
3. Prevent disease cascades in seasonal wards
    
4. Maintain a single chain of command during siege
    

### Long-term (2–5 years)

1. Expand the fortress’ “winter depth”: secondary granaries, inner fallback lines
    
2. Create a professional year-round patrol corps (so the Marches aren’t lawless 8 months)
    
3. Reduce dependence on Bank enforcement and Church “miracle governance”
    
4. Build hardened satellite redoubts near key camps/mines
    

---

## Resources

- **Garrison command:** discipline, patrol assignments, emergency tribunals
    
- **Fortress infrastructure control:** walls, gates, towers, siege stores, repair crews
    
- **Ration bureaucracy:** seals, tokens, distribution points, warehouse audits
    
- **Labor drafts:** winter work gangs, punitive labor as sentence
    
- **Intelligence:** scouts, watch captains, informant networks in taverns and camps
    

He’s one of the few people who can ruin anyone’s life legally without needing the Duke’s personal signature.

---

## Constraints and vulnerabilities

- **He needs legitimacy**: if the Duke publicly undermines him, his machine breaks.
    
- **He needs manpower**: winter surge forces him to tolerate outsiders he despises.
    
- **He needs clean books**: ration fraud is contagious; once people think it’s rigged, order dies.
    
- **He makes enemies**: smugglers, corrupt officials, and anyone profiting from chaos.
    

---

## Taboo lines (what he refuses)

- Letting Bank agents command soldiers or jailers
    
- Letting Church courts supersede fortress law during siege
    
- Letting “licensed adventurers” carry weapons freely inside winter wards
    
- Allowing Folk warbands inside the inner city (outer camps maybe, inner no)
    
- Negotiating with grobi war chiefs (he will negotiate corridors only through proxies)
    

If the Duke forces him across a taboo, Pierre doesn’t whine—he starts planning contingencies.

---

## Leverage points (how others pressure him)

- **The Duke:** can override him with “exception warrants”
    
- **The Bank:** can delay payroll/supplies that keep soldiers loyal
    
- **The Church:** can inflame public sentiment against “harshness”
    
- **Rivals inside the duchy:** can frame him for starvation or “missing grain”
    
- **Soldier politics:** if winter is too hard, mutiny becomes plausible
    

---

## Signature doctrine (how he thinks)

1. **The wall is only as strong as the granary.**
    
2. **Starvation is a political weapon.** Whoever controls distribution controls obedience.
    
3. **Mercy without structure is cruelty later.** (He’ll punish early to prevent riots late.)
    
4. **Winter justice must be fast.** Not “fair” by modern standards—fast.
    

---

## Seasonal behavior

### Summer

- Repairs and audits. Quietly stockpiles. Trains recruits.
    
- Pushes for patrols to suppress banditry before autumn.
    
- Opposes “ruin fever” expeditions unless they bring clear strategic value.
    

### Autumn

- Gate control, weapon peace-bonding, registration, ration-token issuance.
    
- Builds a “known faces” ledger for who belongs where.
    
- Begins black market suppression before it becomes a winter cancer.
    

### Winter

- Runs the city like a ship in storm conditions: schedules, drills, punishments.
    
- Uses labor sentencing aggressively: thieves go to wood gangs, smugglers to wall repair, agitators to latrine duty.
    
- Coordinates counter-siege: night sorties, fire control, reserve rotations.
    

### Spring

- Restores the monopoly on violence: disarms seasonal gangs, clears illegal camps.
    
- Forces restitution trials to prevent feud cycles.
    
- Pushes the Duke to punish corrupt officials (often politically impossible).
    

---

## Relationship to the player (Blackjacks)

He respects competence and hates freelancing.

### How he sees you

- Useful scalpel, but also a destabilizer—because you answer to the Bank and operate outside normal chains.
    
- He assumes you’re corruptible until proven otherwise.
    

### What he offers

- Legit warrants (the kind that actually work at gates and checkpoints)
    
- Access to records: ration lists, entry rolls, patrol reports
    
- Equipment and safehouses—if you play by his rules
    

### What he demands

- Clean outcomes, not chaos:
    
    - shut down a smuggling route without riots
        
    - remove a gang leader without making them a martyr
        
    - retrieve stolen ration seals intact
        
    - expose a corrupt watch captain with proof strong enough to survive politics
        

If you succeed, he becomes your strongest institutional ally. If you embarrass him, he becomes a cold, lawful antagonist.

---

## Internal politics (Duchy Good vs Duchy Ugly vs Duchy Bad)

- **With Duke Alaric (Ugly):** constant friction over “exceptions.” Pierre views exceptions as rot.
    
- **Against the Provost/Master of Coin (Bad):** Pierre is the biggest obstacle to skimming and charter scams.
    
- **With the Church:** Pierre tolerates charity, despises coercive moral politics.
    
- **With the Bank:** he’ll take supplies and payroll—but he refuses to outsource sovereignty.
    

This makes him a natural magnet for coups—both for and against.

---

## Failure modes (how he can break)

1. **Scapegoated for famine:** someone rigs the books, blames him, riots follow
    
2. **Mutiny:** payroll delayed + harsh discipline + cold winter = soldiers turn
    
3. **Assassinated by smugglers/bank rivals:** not a duel—poison, arson, “accident”
    
4. **Forced resignation:** Duke chooses a softer replacement to appease Church/Bank
    
5. **Hardline overreach:** his crackdowns create the very rebellion he fears
    

---

## 10 quest premises that fit Pierre

1. **The Missing Seal:** ration tokens are duplicated. Find the die-maker and recover the stamp.
    
2. **Ice Road:** contraband crosses the lake at night. Track it without spooking the dockmaster (who might be involved).
    
3. **The Payroll Delay:** Bank shipment is “lost.” Pierre suspects sabotage. Recover it before mutiny.
    
4. **The Gate Riot:** a Folk clan tries to force entry. Pierre needs a solution that avoids massacre but keeps control.
    
5. **Warehouse Fire:** arson hits the granary. Prove who ordered it—smugglers, Church zealots, or Bank rivals.
    
6. **The Watch Captain:** a popular captain is skimming and still beloved. Pierre needs proof and a clean arrest.
    
7. **The Outwork:** a southern tower goes silent midwinter. Restore it or evacuate survivors.
    
8. **Disease Quarter:** sickness blooms in the overflow ward. Pierre needs quarantine enforced without sparking rebellion.
    
9. **The Secret Tunnel:** smugglers are using old ruin tunnels under the city. Map and seal them.
    
10. **The Exception List:** Pierre discovers the Duke’s private entry warrants. Does he confront, leak, or cover?
