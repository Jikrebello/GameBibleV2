### **The Seasonal Village Headmen**

**Role in the Rule of Three:**
They are the **Human Fulcrum**. Not powerful lords, not ideological players—**the men who hold the bloody, muddy end of the Duke's policies.** Each village is a microcosm of the Duchy’s existential crisis, and each Headman is a petty king drowning in impossible choices. For the Duke, they are the ultimate "Ugly": helping them is a moral and strategic necessity, but every intervention is a political landmine that triggers conflict with a more powerful faction.

**Core Concept:** These are not mayors; they are **crisis managers of doomed outposts**. Their authority lasts only as long as the season, and their people are the first to die when winter comes or threats descend. Their loyalties are fractured between the distant Duke, the exploitative Guilds, the vengeful Folk, and the desperate need of their own community to survive.

## **1. Lakewatch: Headman Jaromir**

**Concept:** The First Bastion. Lakewatch is not just a fishing village; it is the early-warning horn for Grenzburg. Perched on the northern lakeshore, it takes the full brunt of the first Grobi ice-raids when the lake freezes. Jaromir’s people buy the city time with their lives.
*   **Public Face:** A gnarled, salt-weathered man in his 60s. Speaks little, moves with the resigned economy of an old sailor who has seen every storm. His authority comes from being the oldest survivor.
*   **Private Truth:** He is **quietly revolutionary**. He believes Grenzburg’s strategy of letting the hamlets fall is a fatal, cowardly error. He dreams of a chain of fortified lake-towers that could hold year-round, breaking the Grobi before they reach the city. He hates that his people are considered expendable.
*   **Goal:** To secure enough men and mortar to build a proper watchtower and ice-break, transforming Lakewatch from a sacrifice into a shield.

**Internal Rule of Three (The Village Schism):**
*   **The Good (The Evacuationists):** **Marta, the Net-Mender.** Argues for full, early evacuation. Wants to abandon the village the moment the first snow flies, saving every life. Believes trying to hold is prideful suicide.
*   **The Bad (The Die-Hards):** **Lars, the Ice-Fisher.** A hot-headed young father. Wants to fight for every hut. Argues that if they abandon the village, the Duke will never give them the resources to rebuild. He’s secretly stockpiling weapons and plans to lead a militia.
*   **The Ugly (The Collaborators):** **"Silent Svea."** An older widow who trades fish and information with the Stone-Tooth Grobi tribe across the winter ice. She ensures her family is spared during raids. She believes in survival by any means and sees loyalty to Grenzburg as a death sentence.

**The Duke’s Dilemma with Lakewatch:**
Jaromir pleads for a permanent garrison of 20 soldiers and building supplies.
*   **If the Duke agrees:** He triggers conflict with **Sir Gerhold**, who views it as a catastrophic dispersion of his already thin forces. It also angers **Holger Strun**, who demands those soldiers protect *his* lumber camps first.
*   **If the Duke refuses:** He sacrifices loyal subjects, emboldens the Grobi, and proves Jaromir’s point—that the Duchy abandons its own. It pushes the village towards Svea’s ugly pragmatism or Lars’s doomed defiance.
*   **The Faustian Bargain:** Sending help drains the city and angers powerful allies. Not sending help destroys morale and the first line of defense. Either way, the Grobi still come.

## **2. Highlake: Headman Berti**

**Concept:** The Cursed Dig. Highlake is a mining town whose wealth is its curse. It sits atop a sprawling, pre-human Folk burial complex. Every pickaxe strike is blasphemy, and the "accidents"—collapsing tunnels, poisoned wells, sudden madness—are the mountain’s answer.
*   **Public Face:** A pragmatic, weary foreman in his 40s. He speaks in the measured tones of a man trying to reason with an earthquake. Focuses on practical solutions: better bracing, new water cisterns.
*   **Private Truth:** He knows the truth. The mountain is **alive with wrongness**. He’s seen the carvings in the deep seams. He believes the only way to survive is to negotiate with the Folk, not Lady Katarina’s ruthless delving. He’s secretly mapped the burial chambers to avoid them, angering his own miners.
*   **Goal:** To broker a formal treaty with the local Ghost-Foot Clan: designated "no-dig" zones in exchange for safe mining in others and a share of the ore.

**Internal Rule of Three (The Mountain's Toll):**
*   **The Good (The Engineers):** **Dagny, the Surveyor.** Wants to mine smarter, not deeper. Advocates for using geology to avoid unstable—and sacred—ground. Seeks a technical solution to a spiritual problem.
*   **The Bad (The Grave-Robbers):** **Knud, the Claim-Jumper.** Leads a crew that deliberately breaks into burial niches to loot the "grave-goods" (strange, crystalline artifacts buried with the dead). They cause the most severe "accidents" and provoke the fiercest Folk retaliation.
*   **The Ugly (The Whisperer):** **The figure known as "Echo."** A miner who was lost in a collapse for three days and came back changed. He now claims to hear the mountain’s voice and leaves small Folk-style offerings at tunnel heads. Some think he’s a Folk plant; others think he’s just mad. But the accidents *do* lessen where he works.

**The Duke’s Dilemma with Highlake:**
Berti begs for the Duke to sanction his treaty and send a diplomat to the Ghost-Foot.
*   **If the Duke agrees:** He triggers catastrophic conflict with **Lady Katarina**, who views any concession to the Folk as an intolerable limit on her research and profits. It also angers **Deacon Ignatius**, who would see it as treating with devils.
*   **If the Duke refuses:** The "accidents" continue, killing essential miners and slowing the ore that services the Duchy’s debt to the Bank. It pushes the town towards Knud’s destructive greed or the unstable mysticism of Echo.
*   **The Faustian Bargain:** Making peace with the Folk saves lives and ensures steady ore, but it cripples the artifact economy and enrages the Church. Ignoring the problem kills miners and risks a catastrophic Folk uprising that could bury Highlake entirely.

## **3. Timberfalls: Headman Gunther**

**Concept:** The Arrogant Heir. Timberfalls is the engine of Strun’s lumber empire, and Gunther is his arrogant nephew, placed there to learn the trade. He sees the forest as a conquered enemy, the Folk as pests, and his uncle’s warnings as the prattle of an old man.
*   **Public Face:** A brash, well-fed man in his late 20s. Wears fine clothes inappropriate for the woods. Holds court in the best hut, holding feasts with imported wine. Rules through bluster and his uncle’s name.
*   **Private Truth:** He is **terrified of failure**. This post is his test to inherit the Cartel. His arrogance is a mask over a deep fear of the whispering forest and the eyes he feels watching from the trees. He doubles down on brutality to prove he isn’t scared.
*   **Goal:** To exceed his uncle’s cutting quotas by any means necessary, proving his worth and securing his inheritance.

**Internal Rule of Three (The Forest's Revenge):**
*   **The Good (The Old Growth):** **Birgit, the Camp Cook & Herb-Wife.** A marcher-born woman who remembers the old ways. She tries to mediate, leaves offerings at the forest’s edge, and treats injured Folk she finds. She is the village’s fragile moral center.
*   **The Bad (The Clear-Cutters):** **Gunther’s Boys.** His handpicked enforcers, led by a brute named **Fedor**. They brutalize the workforce, flog any talk of "Folk nonsense," and actively seek out and destroy Folk totems and sacred groves, viewing it as sport.
*   **The Ugly (The Saboteur):** **Someone inside the camp.** Supplies go missing. Axle-trees are cut. A food cache is poisoned (non-lethally, but clearly a warning). Is it a Folk infiltrator? A disgruntled worker paid by a rival guild? Or one of Birgit’s followers taking a dark turn? The uncertainty is paralyzing.

**The Duke’s Dilemma with Timberfalls:**
Gunther’s reckless expansion triggers a major Folk ambush. He demands a company of soldiers for "pacification."
*   **If the Duke agrees:** He becomes an active participant in Holger Strun’s (and Gunther’s) war of aggression against the Folk, uniting the clans against the Duchy and jeopardizing every other settlement. It makes a mockery of any treaty.
*   **If the Duke refuses:** Gunther will appeal directly to his uncle. Strun may enact his own "security measures," which could be even more brutal, or he might threaten to withhold lumber shipments—a catastrophic blow on the eve of winter. It also leaves the loggers, many of whom are just trying to work, undefended.
*   **The Faustian Bargain:** Protecting Timberfalls empowers its worst instincts and starts a wider war. Abandoning it collapses the lumber supply and reveals the Duke as powerless against his own vassal. The "solution" likely requires betraying someone: sacrificing Gunther to the Folk, betraying Birgit to find the saboteur, or confronting Strun directly.

## **Collective Power & The Duke’s Calculus**

Together, the pleas of these three Headmen represent the **Trilemma of the Frontier**. They are a live, ticking political circuit. Solving one problem inevitably shorts out another:
*   Helping **Lakewatch** (military) angers the **Marschal** and the **Lumber Cartel**.
*   Helping **Highlake** (diplomacy) angers the **Delvers’ Union** and the **Church**.
*   Helping **Timberfalls** (aggression) angers the **Folk** and empowers the **Cartel**.

**The Ultimate Ugly Truth:** The Duke cannot save them all. He must constantly decide which village to sacrifice, which alliance to strain, and which fire to let burn, all while knowing that the survivors—and the families of the lost—are watching, and their loyalty is crumbling with every calculated abandonment. The Headmen and their people are the currency with which he buys another year for Grenzburg.
