# The Folk Clans

**Role in the Rule of Three:**
The Folk are the **Wild Card**. They are not a single "Ugly" faction but a spectrum of them, representing the land's ancient claim against the newcomers. They are the ultimate variable in the Duke's calculus: they can be the difference between a hamlet's survival or annihilation, a clear road or an ambushed trail. Their internal divisions mirror and mock the conflicts of the so-called "civilized" men.

**Core Concept:** The Fasgrangar are the indigenous peoples of the deep forests and high valleys. They are not primitive; their culture is ancient, complex, and tied to the spirit of the land in ways the Eclessians cannot comprehend. Their conflict with Grenzburg is not a war of conquest, but a **war of ecology and memory**. Each clan represents a different strategy for surviving the invasion: assimilation, resistance, manipulation, or something in between.

---

## **1. The Moss-Crown Clan: The "Good" (Potential Allies)**
**Led by: Anya Moss-Crown**

*   **Concept:** The diplomats and stewards. The Moss-Crown see the marchers as a flood that cannot be stopped, only directed. They believe in sustainable coexistence through fierce negotiation and clear boundaries.
*   **Leader:** Anya Moss-Crown is not a warrior-queen but a **speaker-for-roots**. Middle-aged, sharp-eyed, with a crown of living moss and braided hawthorn. She speaks the trader's tongue fluently, understands debt, and thinks in generations.
*   **Goal:** To secure a **Great Sanctuary**—a tract of primeval forest, larger than any logging claim, legally recognized by the Duke's charter and placed under their stewardship. No axe, no fire, no settlement, forever.
*   **Offer:** In exchange, they provide unparalleled services: guides through treacherous passes, early warnings of Grobi movements, and trade in rare, potent herbs that can cure winter fevers or heal wounds.
*   **Player Voice:** [[Runa Moss-Crown]] is Anya's subordinate grove warden and the recruitable witness to sanctuary law.
*   **Faustian Bargain with the Duke:** Granting the Sanctuary would be an act of supreme statesmanship... and economic suicide. It would:
    1.  **Cripple the Lumber Cartel**, stripping Holger Strun of his most valuable future tracts, likely causing him to revolt or defect.
    2.  **Enrage the Settlers**, who see any "untouched" land as wasted opportunity, breeding unrest.
    3.  **Empower the Clan**, making them a semi-sovereign power within his borders.
    4.  **Be denounced as heresy** by Deacon Ignatius, who sees all land as meant for Redeemer-tilled fields.

**The Moss-Crown's Own Rule of Three:**
*   **Good (The Wardens):** Those who truly believe in the stewardship ideal. They patrol the Sanctuary borders and teach marcher children about medicinal plants.
*   **Bad (The Bloodprice):** A minority who whisper that Anya is too soft. They demand not just land, but the heads of specific loggers or the right to try marcher criminals in their own courts for crimes on the land.
*   **Ugly (The Grey Traders):** Members who secretly partner with the Trappers' Council, using the clan's knowledge and mobility to run the most lucrative smuggling routes, personally profiting from the very chaos Anya seeks to end.

## **2. The Shattered-Tusk Clan: The "Bad" (Active Enemies)**
**Led by: Jorund Shattered-Tusk**

*   **Concept:** The avengers. The Shattered-Tusk are defined by a historical trauma—the "Red Winter" massacre where a marcher lord broke a treaty and slaughtered a gathering of their families. Their culture is now built around a sacred oath of total vengeance.
*   **Leader:** Jorund is a **walking scar**. He bears ritual burns mirroring the wounds of the fallen. He is a tactical genius of asymmetric warfare, employing terror, poison, and pit-traps. He views any Folk who treat with marchers as race-traitors worthy of the same fate.
*   **Goal:** The complete eradication of Eclessian presence from the Marches. Not just defeat, but the erasure of their works: farms burned to salt, ruins re-sanctified, and the memory of Grenzburg made into a warning tale.
*   **Method:** Total war. No trade, no prisoners, no distinction between soldier and settler. They ally with no one, viewing even the Grobi as temporary vermin to be cleared out after the humans.
*   **Faustian Bargain with the Duke:** The only way to stop them is annihilation, which requires:
    1.  **Empowering the Blackjacks** or hiring foreign mercenaries for a brutal, genocidal campaign in the deep woods, making the Duke a monster in the eyes of other clans and his own more moral subjects.
    2.  **Diverting critical resources** from the Grobi front, risking the main wall to fight a shadow war.
    3.  **Creating martyrs,** ensuring the Shattered-Tusk legend lives on, inspiring future generations of extremists.

## **3. The Ghost-Foot Clan: The "Ugly" (Manipulators)**
**Led by: The Weavers (a council of three: The Spinner, The Dyer, The Loom)**

*   **Concept:** The occult brokers. The Ghost-Foot believe the old powers sleeping in the land and the ruins are the true rulers. They practice a dangerous, syncretic magic that blends Folk spirit-lore with fragments of pre-human artifice.
*   **Leadership:** The Weavers are never all seen. One negotiates, one watches from the trees, one remains unseen in the deep places. They speak in riddles and metaphors of thread, pattern, and price.
*   **Goal:** To maintain their autonomy and accumulate esoteric power by brokering deals between the human and spirit worlds. They are not for or against Grenzburg; they are **using** its conflict.
*   **Offer:** They can provide solutions no one else can: curse a Grobi warlord with waking nightmares, blight the fields of a rival Eclessian lord, or find a hidden path to a specific ruin-chamber.
*   **Faustian Bargain:** Their price is never coin. It is always a **sacrifice of order, legacy, or sanity**. Examples:
    *   *"The laughter of your firstborn for a season."* (The child becomes permanently, silently morose.)
    *   *"A law unmade."* (They might demand the Duke repeal a specific tax, pardon a particular criminal, or remove a stone from the city wall, creating a tangible or symbolic weakness.)
    *   *"A story forgotten."* (They might erase the memory of a founding hero from the city's records, or take the true name of an ancestor, leaving a genealogical and spiritual hole.)
    *   Helping them always creates a deeper, weirder problem than the one solved.

## **4. The Chapel-Folk (The "Redeemed"): The In-Between**
**Led by: Elof of the Chapel-Folk**

*   **Concept:** The torn. Two generations ago, a Folk clan devastated by plague was saved by a wandering preacher of the Redeemer. They converted, adopting the faith and some customs. Now, they are **outcasts to both worlds**.
*   **Leader:** Elof is a man of two faces. He wears a simple Redeemer's wheel around his neck but keeps his hair in the braids of his mother's clan. He speaks both tongues perfectly and is fluent in the scriptures, which he uses to argue for his people's place.
*   **Goal:** **Recognition.** They want the Church to formally recognize them as a pious congregation, not "converted heathens." They want the marchers to see them as neighbors, not suspicious half-breeds. They want the other Folk to stop calling them "Lost-Songs."
*   **Dilemma:** They are the ultimate buffer zone, and both sides abuse this.
    *   **The Church (Bishop Konrad & Ignatius)** uses them as propaganda tools ("See? The heathens can be saved!") but denies them a proper priest of their own blood and covets the sacred grove they still tend.
    *   **The Marchers** distrust them, seeing them as Folk first. They are often the first accused of theft or sabotage.
    *   **The Other Folk Clans** despise them as traitors who sold their ancestors' ways for false comfort. The Shattered-Tusk specifically targets them.
*   **What They Offer the Duke:** They are **invaluable intermediaries**. They can explain Folk motives to the council, predict Shattered-Tusk movements, and identify which Ghost-Foot promises are literal and which are poetic traps. They know the land as Folk, but think in terms the Duke can understand.
*   **Faustian Bargain with the Duke:** To champion them is to:
    1.  **Enrage the Church's Zealots** (Ignatius), who believe true conversion requires complete cultural eradication, not synthesis.
    2.  **Alienate the Traditionalist Folk** (Moss-Crown and others), who see any support for the Chapel-Folk as validating cultural destruction.
    3.  **Create a Permanent, Vulnerable Dependent.** Protecting them is a constant drain, as they are attacked from all sides. Abandoning them is a moral and strategic catastrophe, proving no one can trust the Duke's promises of integration.

**The Chapel-Folk's Internal Rule of Three:**
*   **Good (The True Believers):** Those who genuinely love the Redeemer and seek to live peacefully, hoping their virtue will win acceptance.
*   **Bad (The Resentful):** Those who feel cheated. They blame the Church for their pariah status and may secretly aid the Shattered-Tusk, or plot to reclaim their Folk identity through violence.
*   **Ugly (The Opportunists):** Those who play both sides. They sell information to the Trappers' Council, act as scouts for the Bank's delve teams, and offer to "pray away" the curses the Ghost-Foot create—for a fee. They have no loyalty, only a desperate will to profit from their own liminality.

## **5. The River-Clans: The Road-Brokers**

**Led by: Speaker Bran of the River-Clans**

The River-Clans live along the downstream crossings and seasonal fisheries. They favor bounded treaties, hostage exchange, and paid scouting because movement is their survival. Bran offers warning of Grobi crossings and neutral meeting ground, but every road concession establishes a Folk jurisdiction the Duke will struggle to retract.

They are not interchangeable with Moss-Crown. Moss-Crown bargains for permanent sanctuary; River-Clans bargain for movement, toll, and recognition along living waterways.

---

## **The Collective Folk Dynamic & The Duke's Hell**

The Folk are not a problem the Duke can solve with a single treaty or war. They are a **condition of the landscape**. His strategy must be a balancing act:
*   **Appease the Moss-Crown** to secure routes and intelligence, but **anger Strun and the settlers**.
*   **Crush the Shattered-Tusk** to protect his people, but **empower the Bank's mercenaries** and **create martyrs**.
*   **Use the Ghost-Foot** to solve an immediate crisis, but **incur a terrifying, long-term debt**.
*   **Champion the Chapel-Folk** to gain perfect intermediaries, but **inflame religious and cultural tensions** on all sides.

Every decision with one clan reverberates through the others, turning the forest itself into a web of obligations, betrayals, and escalating vengeance. The Folk, in their fractured totality, ensure that the frontier can never truly be tamed—only temporarily, and at great cost, bargained with.
