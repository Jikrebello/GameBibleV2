# The Grobi

This is the provisional Grenzburg campaign register for Grobi actors. [[Grobi Overview]] controls species, continuity, lifecycle, and moral agency. [[Factions Overview]] controls their game role: Grobi are enemies and negotiable regional actors, not a joinable faction line.

## Why They Move North

The deep southern winter closes forage, freezes routes, and concentrates Grobi hunger. Many bands move north toward Grenzburg's grain, animals, timber, shelter, and people. Climate and metabolism create pressure; they do not erase moral agency. Intelligent Grobi can choose raid, massacre, restraint, trade, betrayal, captivity, or cooperation and remain responsible for those choices.

## Ghor and the Bloody-Sun Host

Ghor is a powerful true-born Ogre who turns seasonal movement into organized conquest. He promises food, transformation, status, and a walled human storehouse to bands willing to submit to his war leadership.

His coalition has real military structure, scouts, routes, siege aims, supply predation, and internal fractures. It is not controlled by Pale-Skins, Hollow-Kings, the Heart-Stone, the Left Hand, the Medori Bank, or the Deep Muster. Ghor chooses the siege because Grenzburg is valuable and because victory would establish his authority over rivals.

The organized host is defeated at the end of [[ACT II WINTER'S TEETH]]. Ghor's death, capture, displacement, or survival beyond the broken siege remains to be locked in Wave 5 because each creates a different spring frontier.

## Herd-Matron Uru

Uru leads a large train of dependents and guards rather than the main conquering host. Her people still require food and may raid, coerce, or abandon outsiders when pressed. Their vulnerability does not make them harmless.

The player may negotiate a corridor, exchange prisoners, supply or redirect movement, expose a deception, refuse tribute, or leave the train to Ghor's recruitment. Every arrangement must name who is protected, who pays, what Uru restrains, and what happens when stores fail.

## The Stone-Tooth Actors

**Grakka Stone-Tooth** leads a predatory band that values continuing access over total destruction. Trade and intelligence can make him useful, but his preferred peace treats weaker neighbors as managed food and tribute.

**Trader-Captain Skiv** moves prisoners, tools, medicine, food, and route knowledge. His information is reliable only while reliability remains profitable.

[[Skrit Stone-Tooth]] is a possible broker, scout, enemy, informant, or temporary quest actor. He is not a member of the eight-companion roster and receives no redemption merely for cooperating when paid or threatened.

## Other Bands

The campaign needs smaller actors that do not collapse into the three named leaders:

- starving splinters with little discipline;
- raiders who reject Ghor but still prey on settlements;
- captive or coerced bands inside the host;
- traders and scavengers following the war;
- dependents seeking a pass north or west;
- revenge bands formed after winter losses.

No individual actor speaks for all Grobi.

## Campaign Use

### Act I

Teach the difference between ordinary migration pressure, independent raiding, and Ghor's military consolidation. Grobi appear first through tracks, attacks, prisoners, trade, scouts, and threatened communities rather than a single exposition scene.

### Act II

Ghor's siege is a military and survival crisis. Negotiation with one band can weaken or redirect another but cannot persuade the whole host to disappear. The player serves as a shock specialist while armies and civilian institutions conduct the wider defense.

### Act III and Summer

The broken coalition leaves captives, corpses, dependents, deserters, revenge leaders, empty routes, disputed prisoners, and bands seeking new territory. These outcomes belong to spring reclamation and summer world state. Grobi do not cause the Deep Muster.

## Moral and Writing Rules

- Hunger explains pressure without absolving chosen cruelty.
- Protecting noncombatants does not require pretending their guards are innocent.
- Killing committed attackers can be just without turning all Grobi into vermin.
- A bargain must identify the predation it permits or prevents.
- No Grobi agreement becomes proof that all cultures are morally equivalent.
- Pale-Skins have no role in Grenzburg's active campaign.
