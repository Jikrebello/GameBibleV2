# Grenzburg Campaign Spine

This is the story authority for Grenzburg's critical path. Individual quest notes control their playable detail; [[Grenzburg Chapter and Window Matrix]] controls order and gates; [[Main Campaign State Matrix]] controls persistent outputs.

## Player Premise

A silent, customizable Medori debtor survives a sabotaged convoy and enters Grenzburg as a Blackjack specialist under ducal field warrant. The player never becomes army commander or supreme faction leader. They are the mobile outsider sent where ordinary authority, fixed formations, or local politics cannot solve the immediate problem.

The Duke drives the public campaign. Bank pressure appears selectively through Blackjack service and personal debt, not as a private rider attached to every quest.

## Campaign Shape

| Movement | Dominant crisis | Play identity | Fixed culmination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prologue | human road violence and first Grobi pressure | rescue, survival, onboarding | player reaches Grenzburg and receives a warrant |
| Act I: The Closing Roads | bandits, deserters, and specific hostile Folk war-bands | frontier tour, road dungeons, settlements, evacuation | Fort Tannbruck falls |
| Act II: Winter's Teeth | Ghor's Grobi invasion and the displaced Emerald Drake | city siege, urban investigation, monster hunt, shock operations | Drake and Ghor die; organized siege breaks |
| Act III: The Unending Muster | Qianglong residue armies and the General Below | reclamation, anchor campaign, dead march, mega-dungeon | Gerhold is destroyed; General Below is stopped |
| Summer | political, personal, and material consequence | memorial, settlement, reconstruction, open postgame | persistent summer play opens |

Earlier consequences continue across acts without erasing each act's enemy palette. Act I's Folk disputes remain political in winter and spring. Grobi survivors remain regional actors after Ghor. Winter casualties become the material stakes of the Muster.

## Independent Causes

- Closing roads arise from climate, weak infrastructure, predation, neglected settlements, and contested land.
- Hostile Folk action belongs to named war-bands and Jorund's total-war followers, never all Folk.
- Ghor turns hunger and migration into organized conquest. No hidden power controls him.
- Logging, refugee fuel-cutting, and Grobi war fires jointly displace the Emerald Drake. It is a non-sapient animal.
- The Unending Muster begins recurring through thaw, damaged ancient works, and accumulated war dead.
- Gerhold neither awakens the Muster nor causes its first attacks. He knowingly accepts its command later.
- The General Below is a sovereign Abyssal true dragon using the Muster's refusal of discharge as a Material foothold.

No Bank, Heart-Stone, Pale-Skin, Drake, Grobi chief, or universal mastermind causes all three acts.

## Open-World Rhythm

The main story advances time only at named gates. Between them the player can pursue companion quests, faction lines, authored local stories, dungeons, hunts, trainers, and jobs. Main chapters open content in clusters rather than requiring immediate continuation.

The minimum critical path targets roughly 40 hours. A broad playthrough exceeds 100 hours through content with its own places, characters, rewards, and world states rather than through repeated filler.

## Fixed Results

- the protagonist enters service as a Blackjack debtor and ducal specialist;
- Fort Tannbruck falls at the end of Act I;
- the Emerald Drake dies in the mandatory winter hunt;
- Ghor dies and the organized siege breaks;
- the Cold-Iron anchor is destroyed and the Muster becomes known;
- Gerhold accepts command, is severed, and is killed or executed;
- the General Below is stopped and the deepest altar is destroyed;
- summer becomes playable.

## Variable Results

- named and grouped survivors, casualties, stores, services, and settlement condition;
- land relationships and the fate of hostile Folk war-bands;
- Uru, Grakka, Skiv, Grobi dependents, prisoners, and post-siege routes;
- city districts, walls, pumps, camps, and defenses;
- Emerald law-gem custody and its Act III intervention;
- number of destroyed Muster anchors, approach strength, and Deep Muster closure state;
- faction settlements, debt, Blackjack legal form, companions, romance, sworn bond, and reconstruction priority.

No variable player outcome becomes future-setting canon in this pass.

## Moral and Ontological Rules

- Human grievance may explain violence but does not excuse murder, kidnapping, slavery, civilian attack, or predation.
- Specific Folk and Grobi groups retain distinct responsibility; neither people becomes a moral collective.
- The Emerald Drake cannot speak, bargain, consent, or reveal prophecy.
- Residue may copy voice, posture, and memory without containing a routed soul.
- The Medium's imprints and Muster formations are not captured people.
- Gerhold's prior virtue makes his fall tragic, not reversible.
- Duty and sacrifice are real goods; eternal ownership of service is the General Below's lie.

## Retired Material

The Heart-Stone theft, Pale-Skin main conspiracy, Leviticus betrayal, mandatory Bank riders, Dragon's Tomb finale, and canonical Duke's Peace remain archived. The Heart-Stone barrow may support a later optional side adventure and has no critical-path function.
