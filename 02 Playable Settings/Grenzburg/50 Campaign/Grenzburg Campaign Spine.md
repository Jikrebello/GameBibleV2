# Grenzburg Campaign Spine

This is the sole authority for Grenzburg's critical-path story. It fixes the beginning, act functions, independent crises, irreversible turns, finale, and chapter budget. It does not yet pretend that every chapter is a finished quest.

## Campaign Premise

A convoy of Medori debtors reaches Grenzburg as the roads begin closing for autumn. The player survives the northern road, enters the Blackjack Corps under secondment, and becomes one of the Duke's expendable specialists. What begins as road work, bandit suppression, Grobi pressure, and settlement relief grows into a winter siege. The city's survival then exposes a second danger: an ancient Qianglong muster complex has begun repeating orders beneath the thawing frontier. Sir Gerhold Brandt, convinced Grenzburg cannot survive another winter with living soldiers, chooses to seize that dead army. His command completes the opening through which the General Below attempts to materialize.

## Causal Separation

The campaign's principal pressures converge but do not share a mastermind.

| Pressure | Independent cause | How it intersects |
|---|---|---|
| Closing roads | Southern climate, autumn migration, weak infrastructure, bandit opportunity, and political neglect. | Produces refugees, shortages, exposed forts, and the player's early work. |
| Grobi siege | Hunger, winter movement, Ghor's ambition, Grobi fractures, and Grenzburg's stores and walls. | Forces the city inward and kills enough people to make spring recovery harder. |
| Emerald Drake | A territorial animal displaced or provoked within the changing Southwood ecology; exact local cause is reserved for the hunt block. | Attacks supply and military movement during winter, requiring an independent monster hunt. |
| Urban faction struggle | Existing disputes over law, debt, land, faith, crime, and emergency power. | Determines who can supply, shelter, inform, or exploit the war effort. |
| Deep Muster | A Qianglong death-and-war complex independently resumes a damaged command cycle beneath the thawing frontier. | Reclamation opens access and Gerhold deliberately turns a contained recurrence into an army. |
| General Below | A unique Abyssal wound embodying eternal conscription and refusal to release service at death. | Uses the completed Muster and Gerhold's command as conditions for materialization. |

Pale-Skins, the Heart-Stone, the Medori Bank, and Ghor do not cause the Deep Muster.

## Worldspace Spine

[[Grenzburg Regional Geography]] fixes the campaign's physical logic. The prologue descends through [[Grenz Lowlands]] and [[The Funnel]]; autumn opens the city and near frontier before the fixed fall of [[Fort Tannbruck]]; winter alternates between the eight city districts, the [[Emerald Drake Range]], and the Upper Grenz and Tuskway fronts; spring reclaims those roads before converging through [[Frostfenn March]] and [[Deep Muster Approaches]].

The Fenn Road, Upper Grenz road, and Tuskway are three distinct Act III march corridors. This makes the final crisis regional without treating the Muster as the cause of earlier dangers.

## Story Shape

### Prologue: The Black Road

The opening is understandable without a lore primer. A debtor convoy encounters grounded violence and a collapsing road situation. Combat, traversal, rescue, looting, party commands, social checks, debt pressure, and the first companion relationships are taught because surviving requires them.

The prologue ends at Grenzburg's northern gate. The player has seen the human cost of the closing roads before hearing the Duke, Church, Bank, Folk, or Grobi explain what the crisis means.

### Act I: The Closing Roads

Act I opens Grenzburg and the near frontier in deliberate layers. Bandits, deserters, predators, Grobi scouts, ordinary residue, practical dungeons, village disputes, and failing roads dominate. Factions are introduced through work and places rather than a council roll call.

The player repeatedly returns to [[Fort Tannbruck]], the southern shield controlling the upper-road convergence. The fort's fall is unavoidable because the regional position cannot be held once the roads close and Ghor commits his coalition. Player action determines who escapes, what supplies are denied to the enemy, which warnings reach Grenzburg, what civilians survive, and how costly the winter defense becomes.

Act I ends with the surviving column reaching Grenzburg as winter closes behind it.

### Act II: Winter's Teeth

Act II balances three equal forms of main-story play:

- urban political and criminal missions inside Grenzburg;
- the mandatory hunt and slaying of the Emerald Drake;
- military shock operations that break Ghor's siege structure.

The Drake is dangerous court-fauna, not a speaking dragon or hidden conspirator. Its death solves an immediate military and civilian threat while creating ecological consequences that can be explored later.

The player serves as a mobile specialist in raids, rescues, sabotage, wall actions, and decisive assaults. They do not command the siege. Act II culminates in the defeat of Ghor's organized host. Prior work determines casualties, Grobi splintering, prisoners, refugee survival, faction legitimacy, and the condition of the city.

### Act III: The Unending Muster

Spring reopens a damaged world. The player helps reclaim holdings, bury the dead, restore roads, settle land, and confront mundane aftermath. Reports then reveal coordinated corpse formations, empty watch posts, and old Qianglong commands repeating beneath reclaimed ground.

The Deep Muster was waking independently. Gerhold does not cause its first signs. He chooses to take control after concluding that living Grenzburg can never field enough soldiers. Once he accepts the Muster's authority and orders the dead into service, he knowingly treats corpse residue and command patterns as a permanent replacement garrison. His choice gives the complex the living command it lacked and accelerates the General Below's materialization.

Gerhold is confronted as a boss. He may die in battle or be defeated and executed. There is no redemptive ending after he accepts the Muster. Earlier relationship or service can change the confrontation's words, allies, evidence, and cost, but not erase his choice.

The dead march toward Grenzburg while the player and surviving powers hold evacuation and defense operations. The final descent enters [[The Deep Muster]], breaks its command architecture, and confronts [[The General Below]]. A completed campaign always stops the Deep Dragon. Political power, debt freedom, casualties, companion futures, and settlement survival remain variable.

### Summer Aftermath

Summer is playable. [[Accounts of the Living]] establishes immediate survival, debt, authority, and companion outcomes. [[The First Summer Road]] returns the player to the changed region and opens the postgame. No political settlement is designated as setting canon in Wave 1.

## Onboarding Discipline

The campaign introduces setting complexity in this order:

1. debtors, road danger, bandits, weather, and rescue;
2. Grenzburg's walls, basic authority, Grobi scouts, and faction services;
3. Folk land relationships, warlock craft, residue, and older ruins through practical problems;
4. the Emerald Drake as a major but comprehensible monster;
5. Qianglong history, patterned corpse armies, and the metaphysics of the Muster;
6. the General Below as the earned final cosmological escalation.

No required early conversation lists nine cultures, explains every plane, or names all faction leaders.

## Faction and Companion Intersections

Faction lines run parallel to the critical path. Their completion supplies methods, allies, spaces, services, and summer claims. No faction is required to stop the Deep Dragon.

Companion personal quests unlock interventions, advanced vocations, relationship scenes, and ending states. No companion is a mandatory key. The main campaign must provide a baseline solution even when every personal arc is unresolved.

## Fixed and Variable Results

### Fixed

- the player reaches Grenzburg as a debtor;
- Fort Tannbruck falls;
- the Emerald Drake is slain during the winter campaign;
- Ghor's organized siege is defeated;
- Gerhold accepts the Unending Muster and receives no redemption;
- the General Below is stopped in a completed campaign;
- summer becomes playable.

### Variable

- convoy and fort survivors;
- settlement survival and reconstruction;
- food, fuel, disease, and military losses;
- Grobi splintering, captives, and migration outcomes;
- faction leadership and political authority;
- player debt and Blackjack status;
- companion loyalty, romance or bond, vocation teaching, and destination;
- how the Deep Muster is broken and what evidence or power survives;
- Grenzburg's political settlement.

## Prohibited Shortcuts

- no universal mastermind;
- no Pale-Skin main-story reveal;
- no Heart-Stone theft or required heart-gem counterweapon;
- no Bank rider on every main mission;
- no secret prophecy that makes the player uniquely able to win;
- no trapped-soul explanation for residue armies or Medium shades;
- no redeeming Gerhold after his deliberate acceptance of the Muster;
- no canonical political ending before the endings wave.
