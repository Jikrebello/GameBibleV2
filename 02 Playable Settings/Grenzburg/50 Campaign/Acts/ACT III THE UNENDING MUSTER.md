# Act III: The Unending Muster

Act III begins with the most ordinary promise in the game: return home, reopen the road, bury the dead, and make damaged land livable. That work exposes an old Qianglong military complex whose command patterns have resumed. The act escalates from reclamation to residue war, Gerhold's deliberate fall, a regional dead march, and the Deep Dragon.

## Intended Feel

Spring is mud, floodwater, broken roofs, swollen graves, abandoned fields, returning birds, scavengers, arguments over land, and the shock of seeing what winter did. It should initially feel wider and more hopeful than Act II. Communities return with tools and seed. Soldiers search for the missing. Factions convert wartime credit into claims.

The horror grows through order rather than chaos. Corpses are found standing in ranks. Empty armor turns toward old commands. Patrol routes repeat across centuries. Muster marks appear on doors. Bells and horns answer from buried spaces. The dead do not plead because their souls are not imprisoned; what remains is being organized as material.

## The Deep Muster Truth

[[The Deep Muster]] is a Qianglong death-and-war complex built to preserve military service beyond ordinary defeat. Its residue armies are corpse patterns, equipment memory, and command architecture. Human souls have already routed onward.

The complex begins waking before Gerhold acts. Thaw, damaged seals, old infrastructure, and the accumulated availability of war dead permit its commands to recur. The precise site-local incident that first makes the recurrence visible remains a campaign-blocking decision; its location and causes are fixed. It cannot be a secret action by Gerhold, Ghor, the Drake, Bank, Heart-Stone, or Pale-Skins.

[[The General Below]] is not a Qianglong general whose soul became a dragon. It is an Abyssal wound that embodies eternal conscription: the refusal to release service even at death. The complex gives that refusal architecture, bodies, hierarchy, and a path into the Material Realm.

## Worldspace Use

Spring begins by reopening [[Upper Grenz]], fallen [[Fort Tannbruck]], damaged Longlake roads, and abandoned holdings. Evidence then converges on [[Frostfenn March]]. The dead march divides along the Fenn Road, Upper Grenz command road, and Tuskway before the player secures [[Deep Muster Approaches]] and enters [[The Deep Muster]].

## Gerhold's Fall

Gerhold observes a terrible strategic fact: Grenzburg survived winter with too few living defenders and will remain exposed. He concludes that a garrison that cannot hunger, desert, age, or demand pay is the only permanent answer.

He investigates and prepares without causing the original recurrence. When confronted with the choice, he knowingly accepts the Muster's command authority and orders the dead into Grenzburg's service. He may claim necessity, loyalty, or sacrifice, but he chooses to turn the dead and their remains into instruments and invites a power founded upon refusal.

Once he accepts, Gerhold has no redemptive ending. The player may kill him during the boss confrontation or defeat, capture, and see him executed. Earlier friendship can produce grief, hesitation, evidence, or tactical advantage. It cannot make continued command morally or metaphysically safe.

## Main Chapters

### 16. The Open Thaw

The player returns to the exterior through a reclamation operation: clear a route, escort returnees, recover the missing, bury bodies, and restore a practical service. Coordinated residue appears inside a grounded task rather than through a lore briefing.

### 17. The Land Reclaimed

Multiple communities and factions claim damaged places. The player solves one major restoration conflict while tracking repeated orders, marks, and movements. Mundane land questions remain important even as the Muster becomes visible.

### 18. The Unending Muster

The party enters one or more outer Qianglong works, proves that the events belong to a linked command complex, and identifies the dangers of assuming control. This chapter opens final companion quests, faction settlements, seal recovery, and defense preparation.

### 19. Gerhold's Choice

The player reaches Gerhold before or during his acceptance of the dead command but cannot talk him back after the irreversible act. The chapter culminates in a boss encounter built around living military doctrine distorted through tireless residue formations. Gerhold dies or is defeated and executed.

His fall completes a command relationship the Deep Muster can exploit. The dead begin moving toward populated territory in force.

### 20. The Dead March

The region becomes a war space. The player conducts decisive operations while armies and communities act around them: hold an evacuation road, break a command relay, rescue a settlement, defend a gate, destroy a bridge at the correct moment, or secure entry to the Deep Muster.

Prior settlement, faction, companion, and material states determine what can be saved and who holds each front. The chapter ends with the final descent open and the General Below nearing complete materialization.

### 21. The Deep Muster

The final mega-dungeon must pay off traversal, party composition, vocations, factions, companions, Qianglong history, residue ontology, warlock systems, and the player's accumulated preparations. Its spaces progress from military infrastructure to impossible command architecture and finally the wound beneath it.

Victory requires stopping the General Below and breaking the conditions that let the Muster endlessly reconstruct command. Combat is necessary but bodily damage alone is insufficient. Multiple valid methods may combine physical destruction, White-Flame release, warlock counterscripts, severed relays, rejected ranks, destroyed command records, and collapsing architecture. No one vocation, companion, or faction is mandatory.

## Mega-Dungeon Requirements

Wave 5 and the dungeon pass must provide:

- multiple entrances earned through world play;
- a return route and changing fallback spaces;
- military, funerary, industrial, command, and Abyssal layers with distinct gameplay;
- optional objectives that weaken later phases or save people outside;
- faction and companion interventions without mandatory keys;
- legible residue behavior and no trapped-soul twist;
- at least one large traversal spectacle and one large party combat set piece;
- a Gerhold legacy payoff inside the dungeon even after his death;
- a final encounter whose mechanics express eternal conscription;
- different permanent closure methods with different losses.

## Parallel Spring Content

Spring contains:

- settlement return and rebuilding;
- grave recovery and lawful burial;
- land, inheritance, and sanctuary disputes;
- surviving Grobi bands and displaced dependents;
- war-crime investigations and prisoner outcomes;
- third and fourth companion quests;
- final faction fractures and settlements;
- reopened dungeons and thaw-only ruins;
- hunts against predators drawn to battlefields;
- simple reconstruction jobs as well as major moral stories.

Not every spring quest foreshadows the Deep Muster. The region remains a world rather than one giant clue board.

## Act III Output

The finale records:

- the method and cost of stopping the General Below;
- condition of the Deep Muster and surviving Qianglong knowledge;
- Grenzburg, settlement, road, and faction survival;
- debt and Blackjack status;
- Gerhold's death or execution;
- companion loyalty, relationship, and destination;
- political claims eligible for summer settlement;
- remaining Grobi, ecological, criminal, and supernatural pressures.

## Prohibited Act III Moves

- no Gerhold redemption after accepting the Muster;
- no claim that residue soldiers contain trapped souls;
- no secret player bloodline or prophecy required to stop the dragon;
- no Heart-Stone or Drake heart-gem as the mandatory solution;
- no Pale-Skin authorship;
- no Bank ownership of the entire crisis;
- no ending where the General Below remains active after a completed campaign;
- no fixed political winner.
