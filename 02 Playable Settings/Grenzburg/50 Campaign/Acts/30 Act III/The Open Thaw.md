# The Open Thaw

## Chapter Function

This chapter changes the game's enemy language from living armies to organized residue. It begins as a missing-worker rescue in a known industrial site and proves, step by step, that recent and ancient dead are obeying one campaign architecture. The player destroys the first of five regional Muster anchors.

## Trigger and Commitment

Three days after Ghor's rout, a spring melt opens Highlake's lower haulage route. The **Ninth Shift**, twenty-six miners and two surveyors, fails to return from [[Cold-Iron Deeps]]. Highlake's normal rescue crew enters and sends back one impossible message: the missing workers are continuing their shift under the names of soldiers dead for centuries.

Accepting the descent begins a closed mine expedition. It does not close other Early Spring content.

## Beginning: The Missing Shift

At Highlake, the player must distinguish panic from evidence:

- the lift log shows the Ninth Shift descended alive after the thaw;
- ore carts return carrying recent blood, ancient armor flakes, and sorted human teeth;
- a wounded rescuer repeats an obsolete dismissal formula while trying to report for duty;
- Ghost-Foot observers have heard drilling continue during hours when every living miner fled.

The party can enter by the regulated incline, the damaged rope lift, or the Ghost-Foot cliff passage. Every route reaches the same lower break; the choice changes who can be rescued first and which hazards remain active.

## Middle: Work Without Release

The mine is divided into three vertical problems:

1. **The living upper works:** collapse, meltwater, frightened workers, and ordinary mine hazards.
2. **The joined galleries:** recent corpse residue repeats mining actions while Qianglong War-Caste recurrence imposes files, shifts, and punishment.
3. **The Muster Shaft:** a concealed lower work draws iron, discarded armor, and bodily residue into crab-like replacement plates.

The residue uses voices and gestures from the dead, including people lost in the winter campaign. These are patterns and remains, not trapped persons. Burial rites may quiet individual residue; destroying the anchor is required to stop the organized recurrence.

The player locates surviving miners in three groups: barricaded surveyors, workers compelled by threat and mechanical routing rather than mind control, and injured people trapped beyond a moving armor line.

## End: Cold-Iron Muster Shaft

The anchor is an industrial command engine built across an older seam, not the whole Deep Muster and not a Shell gate. It can be destroyed by:

- collapsing its iron throat after evacuating the lower gallery;
- counter-scripting the sorting cadence, then breaking its exposed command plates;
- flooding and freezing the replacement channels;
- using the selected Emerald law-gem aid to restrain movement while the party dismantles it.

Each method changes salvage, mine stability, and survivor risk. The anchor must end `destroyed`; merely damaging it pauses the chapter and produces another organized sortie until the party finishes the work.

When it breaks, every active formation turns south in perfect unison before collapsing. Recovered route plates name four other relay sites and a command destination beneath the Frostfenn.

## Encounters and Opposition

- mine rescue under spring flood and lift failure;
- first disciplined recent residue formation;
- Qianglong equipment and rank engine encounter;
- moving replacement-armor line;
- multi-objective anchor destruction with workers still below;
- escape through a physically changed mine.

## Information Paths

Mine logs establish the present sequence, Ghost-Foot marks establish the newly opened lower path, Qianglong tablets identify the recurrence, and physical routing plates reveal the regional network. Ashira, Church experts, warlocks, or local burial knowledge can explain residue, but the environment itself proves that no living worker is speaking through the dead.

## Choices and State Changes

- `cold_iron_anchor` becomes destroyed.
- `muster_known` becomes true.
- `mine_company_survivors` records each rescue group, named foremen, and the mine's recovery crew.
- `muster_anchor_count` becomes at least one.

The player also determines whether Cold-Iron Deeps reopens under strict limits, closes for the crisis, or is physically crippled by the destruction method.

## Fail-Forward Results

- A failed entry costs a rescue window and moves survivors deeper, but leaves two routes open.
- Misreading residue as people may expose the party to ambush; later evidence corrects the error without ridiculing compassion.
- Failed evacuation causes named casualties and reduces Highlake support.
- An unstable destruction method damages the mine and triggers an escape sequence, but still severs the anchor.
- Party defeat returns the player to an upper rescue station while the lower formation gains armor and one survivor group worsens.

## Companion Intersections

Ashira is the clearest residue specialist; Ilyana and Tavio read the engine; Dismas reads the hostile approach while Ylva establishes the casualty route and relief change; Bernard protects the remains without mistaking them for souls; Elara and Zafir identify alternate mechanisms. No companion is mandatory.

## Faction Advantages

Marcher support improves evacuation, Folk progress supplies the cliff route, Church progress improves remains recovery, Warlock Hall contacts improve counter-script options, and Blackjack progress brings industrial rescue stores.

## Cutoff and Unlocks

The chapter reveals the [[Muster Anchor Matrix]], opens [[The Land Reclaimed]], and marks the first controlled public knowledge of the Unending Muster. The Deep Muster itself remains unmapped.
