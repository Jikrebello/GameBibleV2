# Act I: The Closing Roads

Act I turns an unknown setting into a place the player can understand and care about. Its work is practical: learn Grenzburg, open roads, meet people through jobs, enter the frontier, and discover that the southern defense cannot hold. It ends in a military loss whose human and material shape belongs to the player.

## Intended Feel

Autumn begins busy rather than apocalyptic. Markets are full, carts arrive, leaves turn, migrant camps grow, and every institution still believes it can arrange the winter to its advantage. The pressure becomes visible through closed roads, missing travelers, exhausted animals, bad stores, deserters, Grobi scouts, frightened villages, and arguments over who receives protection.

The player should spend enough time in ordinary inns, workshops, farms, chapels, barracks, woods, and ruins that later destruction has memory. Humor, local rivalry, trade, courtship, petty crime, and competence belong beside danger.

## Introduction Discipline

Act I introduces the world in layers:

1. **The Black Road:** debtors, road violence, rescue, weather, and first companions.
2. **Grenzburg Gate:** the Duke's emergency, Blackjack service, basic city geography, and one representative face from each immediate institution.
3. **First Road Work:** bandits, Grobi signs, local authority, and one comprehensible ruin.
4. **Fort Tannbruck:** the military frontier, supply reality, and the difference between Ghor's war coalition and other Grobi movement.
5. **Open Autumn:** Folk kindreds, warlock craft, residue, deeper dungeons, and faction lines through player-chosen work.

No council scene introduces the complete cast. Important people become memorable by giving or obstructing concrete work.

## Main Chapters

### 3. The Closing Roads

The first ducal assignment sends the player to a road problem that appears ordinary and becomes the frontier in miniature. The chapter must contain travelers worth rescuing, a clear hostile force, a route or traversal decision, a social aftermath, and one dungeon or occupied site small enough to teach exploration without announcing the game's deepest lore.

### 4. The Southbound Muster

The player joins a movement toward [[Fort Tannbruck]], meets marcher troops and southern civilians, and learns that the fort is a working community rather than a battle map. This chapter opens the fort as a hub and establishes the player as a specialist sent where regular formations are too slow or visible.

### 5. Fort Tannbruck

A grounded crisis inside or near the fort exposes structural weakness: supply corruption, a failed bridge, deserter collusion, captured scouts, a compromised outwork, or another practical failure to be selected in Wave 4. Solving it earns trust and improves the eventual withdrawal but does not make the fort permanently defensible.

### 6. Roads Worth Saving

This is Act I's broadest sandbox chapter. Multiple routes and communities require aid. The player can pursue faction lines, recruit remaining companions, explore ordinary and supernatural dungeons, train vocations, hunt threats, and choose where limited relief goes. Main progression requires a bounded set of frontier objectives, not completion of every settlement quest.

### 7. The Last Convoy

The player knowingly begins the late-autumn operation. Evacuation, stores, wounded soldiers, prisoners, road intelligence, and defensible chokepoints all compete for time and carrying capacity. Earlier local work determines who trusts the player, what routes remain, and which optional rescues are possible.

### 8. Tannbruck Falls

Ghor commits sufficient force to make the position untenable as winter closes the roads. The operation is not about winning an impossible defense. It is about holding long enough, destroying what cannot be carried, recovering the isolated, breaking a pursuit, and getting a coherent column to Grenzburg.

The fort always falls. Player success determines:

- defenders and civilians saved;
- stores, animals, weapons, and records recovered;
- bridges, engines, and supplies denied to Ghor;
- Grobi losses and intelligence;
- the condition of the retreat;
- winter morale and faction credit;
- which local communities remain exposed.

## Open-World Content Mix

Act I should contain the game's widest range of low- and middle-intensity content:

- bandit and deserter work;
- Grobi scouts, raiders, traders, captives, and migrating dependents in distinct contexts;
- missing-person rescues;
- local predators and one early marquee monster;
- abandoned farms, chapels, road posts, mines, and Atherian ruins;
- faction admission quests;
- first companion quests;
- vocation trainers and craft chains;
- village disputes and Folk land conflicts;
- simple hunts, bounties, escorts, and clearings where gameplay carries the quest.

Supernatural encounters remain bounded. Residue is introduced correctly as pattern without trapped souls. Warlock and Folk magic are shown through use before receiving deeper explanation.

## Grobi Rule

Ghor's forces are organized enemies preparing war. Other Grobi bands may flee him, trade, raid independently, carry dependents, seek corridors, or exploit the confusion. Act I must teach that distinction without requiring the player to excuse murder or treat all Grobi action as morally neutral.

## Faction Parallelism

Each deep faction line can begin in Act I, but no faction owns the main story. Faction service may improve roads, supplies, intelligence, local trust, or extraction. It cannot prevent Tannbruck's fall. Its reward is a better result and a different winter, not cancellation of the campaign turn.

## Act I Output

At winter transition, the game records:

- convoy and Tannbruck survivors;
- state of exposed settlements and routes;
- food, fuel, animals, arms, and garrison losses;
- known Grobi coalition information;
- recruited companions and first personal stages;
- faction admission and early influence;
- open, transformed, failed, or resolved autumn quests;
- player debt and standing.

## Prohibited Act I Moves

- no Heart-Stone main objective;
- no Pale-Skin clue trail;
- no cosmic mastermind reveal;
- no mandatory Bank rider on every assignment;
- no early parade of every culture and metaphysical term;
- no preventable victory at Tannbruck;
- no claim that all Grobi share Ghor's plan;
- no quest whose only purpose is reading background lore.
