# A Splendid Little Expedition

## Premise

A partial wall fall exposes a sealed upper gallery at [[Ropehouse Ruin]]. Elara sees the perfect first expedition for her new Grenzburg life: close enough to stage, dangerous enough to matter, and untouched enough to name.

She produces ribbons for the packs, a dramatic route title, and a list headed **Persons of Distinction**. The list includes fighters, a sketcher, and a photographer-equivalent survey artist. It omits the rope splicer, shoring carpenter, local grave watcher, and recovery hand who make entry possible.

## Hook and Party

Elara pitches the outing at [[Blackjack Yard]]:

> “One clean gallery. One afternoon. No dynasties, no curses, and absolutely no committee.”

[[Neda Volk]] looks at the cracked support sketch and asks who is carrying the second beam.

The normal field party remains available. The player can accept Elara's proposed crew, broaden it, or let present workers define roles. Every choice reaches the ruin; it changes safety, information, and who has authority to stop.

## Beginning: Build the Company

The player secures four functions:

- **access:** a route witness or custodian;
- **support:** a shorer and rope team;
- **record:** somebody who can document finds;
- **recovery:** a hand responsible for people and named property.

Faction standing offers candidates but never a complete crew. Background and culture hooks alter trust:

- Peasant/Labour recognizes that “helping hands” means unpaid risk;
- Merchant/Craft can negotiate explicit shares and replacement costs;
- Folk knowledge establishes that a route witness can close a chamber;
- Scholarly knowledge improves recording without outranking custody;
- Wilderness supplies an exterior descent.

If the player follows Elara's first list, the missing roles become live complications rather than a quest failure.

### Scene business

Elara is genuinely excellent here. She notices a nervous porter, gives them a task they can do, and makes the preparation feel like a feast-day departure. When a carpenter corrects her load estimate, she smiles, repeats the correction as though it were hers, and keeps speaking.

The player may interrupt publicly, correct her privately, or ask the carpenter to give the briefing. Elara's reaction changes chemistry, not automatic approval.

## Middle: Ropehouse Outer Works

The expedition crosses:

1. a leaning rain stair;
2. a rope loft occupied by ruin vermin;
3. an exposed winch bridge;
4. the newly opened gallery.

All vocations can use the public route. Climbing, tracking, magic, and brute-force methods create shortcuts or safer recoveries.

Inside, the company finds a late Atherian freight gallery later reused by local households. Its impressive bronze direction plate sits above less glamorous evidence: repair marks, meal hooks, children's height cuts, and a sealed side niche under current local protection.

Elara immediately understands the plate. The custodian understands the room.

## Crisis: The Gallery Takes Weight

Removing the plate, crossing the central shelf, or reaching the rear survey point begins a progressive failure:

- the winch bridge drops;
- dust and vermin split the crew;
- one support shifts;
- water enters through a cracked roof channel.

The objectives are:

- retrieve two separated workers;
- preserve or deliberately sacrifice the bronze plate;
- brace the exit;
- leave the protected niche closed unless its custodian consents.

With a full mixed crew, all four are achievable. Without one function, the party must perform that work under pressure. No vocation is mandatory: tools, alternate anchors, debris ramps, coordinated carrying, and Elara's Faultfinder all provide solutions.

Elara wants to carry the plate because it is “the reason anyone will remember the day.” The separated rope splicer shouts that the beam has thirty breaths.

## End: Who Tells It

Back at the Yard, Elara begins a polished account. The player can:

- let every crew member give one part;
- have Neda record labor and custody before the story;
- let Elara narrate but require accurate names and refusals;
- permit the old version, in which “the company” means whoever appears in the exciting paragraph.

If contradicted, Elara does not deliver a moral thesis. She folds the speech, hands it to the carpenter, and says:

> “You hate this sort of thing.”
>
> “Yes.”
>
> “Good. They will know it is true.”

## Fail-Forward

- A lost plate still leaves route measurements and a safer future entrance.
- An injured worker survives and later refuses Elara's Act I crew unless repaired.
- An opened niche yields no treasure, only a current burial violation and loss of custodian support.
- A failed retreat leaves the party recovered by Neda's team; the gallery closes and Elara loses the right to name the expedition.
- If the crew scatters, their separate accounts still locate Seven Mounds.

## Archetype Chemistry

- Cheerleader/Jock players can amplify Elara's morale and must decide whether acclaim is shared.
- Tomboy/Rebel players can challenge ceremony but risk dismissing why recognition matters.
- Goth/Nerd players notice the backstage evidence and may become condescending about pleasure.
- Bohemian/Goofball players improvise roles and can either broaden participation or make safety somebody else's burden.
- Grey receives a fully authored practical exchange about who signs the stop order.
