# A Blade Freely Given

## Quest Function

This is Bernard's friendship episode. It contains no villain, theft, emergency, or hidden test. The player and Bernard spend an afternoon maintaining his visibly inherited field kit and deciding what honest personal ownership can look like.

The activity is one-on-one but does not invoke the combat-duo rules. Ordinary party management resumes when the pair leave the Great Forge.

## Hook

After [[Little Hands, Long Road]], the right shoulder plate has shifted, two scale rows are cracked, and the inherited greatsword's tang has begun to work loose.

Bernard intends to return the sword for reissue rather than spend company labor on a fourth major repair. He describes this as obvious practicality, then admits he cannot imagine choosing a weapon and saying it is his.

## Beginning: Marks Under the Scale

At the Great Forge the pair strip the armour onto a public work table. Beneath later repairs are:

- a Kedravan Long Watch issue mark;
- two former Blackjack inventory stamps;
- an Eclessian marcher rivet pattern;
- initials from previous wearers, none of whom are secretly famous.

The player may preserve, clean around, or visibly catalogue the marks. Removing corrosion is maintenance; polishing away provenance to invent a noble history is not.

Every vocation can complete the practical activity through ordinary tools. Craft and Military knowledge add observations, while Fighter, Paladin, and Warlock training offer distinct handling comments without gating progress.

## Middle: A Weapon Returned

Bernard places the old grey greatsword on the common rack with a complete condition tag. It remains Blackjack property and may later appear in another debtor's hands. Returning it is neither self-punishment nor proof that he deserves nothing.

The forge has one plain, newly completed greatsword suited to Bernard's height and Long Watch guard. The player can lawfully secure it through:

- ordinary coin;
- registered salvage credit;
- a Great Forge labor exchange completed during the scene.

No wealth threshold can close the gift. Every route establishes a clean chain of ownership.

The player explicitly gives the sword to Bernard without reserve, repayment term, romance implication, or future claim. Bernard asks once:

> "Given, then? Not issued. Not kept until you ask for it back."

On confirmation he accepts it as personal property.

## Handling and Sparring

Bernard tests the balance against a padded post and then offers a friendly measured exchange. The player may spar, hold targets, compare guard positions, or simply watch.

The sequence teaches **Given Steel**: Bernard's fitted weapon improves the stability of Long-Watch Guard and his first protective counter after an interposition. Exact values remain provisional.

The blade remains unnamed. Bernard says a name should describe the life it has actually served, not the hope attached on purchase.

## Un-repaying

Within a few days he starts settling up, quietly, without ever calling it that.

Extra unasked labour on the player's kit. A repair nobody requested. Something useful appearing in the player's pack that Bernard did not mention putting there — a whetstone, a spare strap, a wrapped portion — offered as coincidence if raised.

The player catches him at it. One short scene: he has to **un-repay**, which he finds physically difficult, and which he cannot make funny for more than one sentence. He is not ashamed of generosity; he is being asked to accept that the gift is finished and nothing is outstanding, and his whole architecture says that cannot be true.

The player may accept a smaller honest exchange, refuse everything, or let him keep paying and never say so. Receiving stays hard after the ceremony; the friendship episode is not frictionless.

## The Child's Portion

During the walk back Bernard discusses Mika's hidden food. This becomes the second pre-commitment relationship scene.

He distinguishes:

- protecting a child from humiliation;
- restoring an honest food count;
- building a real household remedy;
- recognizing his own urge without turning Mika into a lesson about him.

The player may express romantic interest, sworn-bond interest, ordinary friendship, a boundary, or no interest. Friendship access and moral resolution remain open on every response.

## Outcome and Persistence

- `bernard_friendship_episode`: complete, declined, or missed;
- `bernard_owned_sword_state`: gifted, declined, or missed;
- `bernard_child_portion_scene`: romance-open, bond-open, friendship, closed, or missed;
- `bernard_long_watch_kit_provenance`: documented or unavailable.

A resolved Bernard names the sword **Open Hand** after [[The Open Hospice]]. Compromised, broken, departed, and missed-finale outcomes leave it unnamed without revoking his ownership.
