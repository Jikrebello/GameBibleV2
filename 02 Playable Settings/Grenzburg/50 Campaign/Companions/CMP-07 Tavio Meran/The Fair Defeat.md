# The Fair Defeat

## Quest Function

Tavio's Act II finale is an investigation and honest live bout in which Lio controls his own participation. The player serves as Tavio's second, not the author of the result.

Personal completion unlocks [[Lancewright]] for the current campaign and account.

## Hook

Lio files a public challenge — nine numbered terms, posted at the Great Forge and copied to the Crucible register, quoted whole in [[The Red Brass Papers]] and read on screen. Item four gives each second an unoverrulable halt. Item seven says the recorded result remains what happens. Item nine says he is not asking for an apology and will not accept one in place of any term above.

He filed it before speaking to Tavio. The terms were never a conversation.

The Red Brass Bench offers a private settlement in reply: accept sole blame, preserve the branch, receive a path back to supervised exhibition. **Folded inside the instrument is a letter in Maro Vezan's hand** — no threat, no denial, no legalese, and the most dangerous document in the campaign. It is quoted whole in the same file. It reads as love, because it is.

At the same time, someone alters Lio's brace fittings, and two men are buying impossible betting positions.

## Preparation

| Asset | Concrete advantage |
|---|---|
| Lio's intact brace | permits his full chosen rules and movement |
| Filed bout rules | prevents private substitution |
| Neutral officials | preserves the result through disruption |
| Red Brass witness | links sabotage to the branch |
| Bookmaker ledger | identifies the attack and fixed odds |

Two assets allow the event. Three make Lio, officials, evidence, bout, and culprit achievable. Four or five preserve one objective after sabotage or public disorder.

## Beginning: The Second's Work

Accepting Tavio's request at the Great Forge forms the player-Tavio duo. Lio has his own second and remains outside the party system.

The player helps file:

- permitted marks;
- gauntlet capacity;
- brace warnings;
- arena boundaries;
- medical halt;
- second's halt;
- result recording;
- refusal and withdrawal rights.

Tavio may object to any rule, but Lio is never pressured to broaden the bout to preserve spectacle.

### The Filing Mirror

Lio files his brace warnings as information — item five, in writing, in front of the examiner: *pain, brace noise, and equipment warnings are treated as information and not as forfeiture.* He does it flatly, the way he does everything, and it takes ninety seconds.

**And the question is on the table.** Tavio has a hand that trembles at maximum load, four years concealed, and the filing sheet in front of him has a line for exactly this.

- **He files it.** The examiner records the limitation; the bout accommodates it; the crowd is told nothing and the officials know. This is the resolved read and it costs him the last private advantage he owns: *limits are part of mastery*, applied to himself, at the worst possible moment to discover he believes it.
- **He conceals it.** The Bench's architecture in his own body, live, in a bout with two halt authorities and an examiner watching — and if the tremor surfaces mid-exchange, it surfaces as a hidden capacity problem inside a certified housing, which is the exact sentence Hella used about the Fifth Ring.

The player, as second, may know or not know depending on Act I–II attentiveness. **A second who knows and stays silent gets one line of reckoning later**, from Hella, after the result, in private.

*(Tremor beat 2 of 4.)*

### Pause Point: Rules Filed

The player may pause before the investigation and public event.

## Middle: Sabotage Before Applause

The duo investigates three linked threats:

1. a replaced brace ring;
2. a bookmaker message ordering a mid-bout interruption;
3. pressure on neutral officials to accept Red Brass custody.

Approaches include:

- lawful inspection;
- underworld surveillance;
- technical bait fitting;
- rooftop pursuit;
- public disclosure;
- following the sabotage to Maro or Seraf.

Maro and Seraf adapt to their prior remote states. An active figure may direct the operation through local agents; if both have lost control, a Red Brass quartermaster protects the branch. Neither man enters Grenzburg.

## The Popular Version

Mid-quest, a Bench-fed broadsheet circulates: **one cheat, one ring, one disgraced champion, and an arena that was betrayed along with everybody else.** It is well written. It is not even mostly false.

It sells. Vendors work the queues outside the Great Forge with it; forge families buy it; two of Tavio's own drill crew buy it and are visibly relieved by it, because one villain is a much easier thing to love a sport through than a corrupt institution.

Tavio watches people he likes purchase a version of events in which he is the whole problem — and it is the version Maro's letter is asking him to sign.

### Hella, before the reckoning

She does not comfort him and she does not argue with the broadsheet's facts.

> **Hella:** You want to carry it alone because it is the last thing you are still the best in the world at.
>
> *(She lets that sit.)*
>
> **Hella:** A martyr buys the arena's innocence at a discount. If you sign that, six men keep signing blanks, and in four years I am proving another ring out of another boy's hand.

She says it once and goes back to her bench. This is the only place in the campaign where the sole-blame option is named as a performance, and nobody repeats it.

## Set Piece: Protect the Measure

Before the bout, hired disruptors attack officials or try to ignite the proof frame. The duo must protect:

- the filed rules;
- Lio's brace;
- independent witnesses;
- the public evacuation route.

The bout may proceed under full rules, altered safe rules, postponement, or Lio's refusal.

Refusal is a valid outcome. Tavio must accept it without declaring himself denied redemption.

## The Bout

If it proceeds, the player acts as Tavio's second:

- call visible warnings;
- permit or refuse equipment changes;
- protect the ring from outside interference;
- call a justified halt;
- prevent hired violence from entering the contest;
- never control Tavio's attacks directly.

Lio can:

- win;
- lose;
- draw;
- withdraw because of pain or brace warning;
- refuse to forgive after any result.

Tavio may not throw the bout. A deliberate loss steals Lio's fair contest as surely as the hidden ring did.

## The Result

The bout has no neutral outcome, and the quest must author each one.

### Fair loss — the richest branch

The crowd does the cruellest available thing, and it does it within a day: it **retroactively voids his career.**

*He was never good without the ring.* Nine years of work, six of them clean, reinterpreted in an afternoon by people who were there. An honest defeat at thirty to a man fighting with a hip brace becomes proof that every honest win was hollow — and **Lio's own item seven now protects the reading that damns him**, because the record remains what happens and Tavio may not improve it in speech or in writing.

He agreed to that term. He wanted that term. It is the term he broke four years ago, and now it is load-bearing against him.

The scene is Tavio doing nothing: not correcting a bout bill, not giving an interview, not accepting the sympathetic reframe an officer offers him in good faith. His finest moment in the campaign is a man declining to fix a record.

### Fair win

Differently barbed, and not a reprieve. Beating the man he maimed wins him nothing he can hold: the cheer feels obscene from inside it, the Bench's broadsheet cites it approvingly by evening, and **Lio confirms the record stands and means it** — asks the recorder to read it back, checks his brace, and goes. The absence of anger is worse than anger, and Tavio has no idea what to do with a result he cannot apologize for.

### Draw

Lio objects — not to the outcome, but to its wording, and spends twenty minutes with a clerk getting the phrasing exact while the crowd disperses around them. Tavio waits. The public reading is anticlimax, which the arena hates and which is nobody's fault.

### Withdrawal

Lio's brace warns, or the pain crosses his own filed threshold, and he stops. Item five made that information rather than forfeiture, and he wrote item five himself, so he honours it in front of a crowd that will call it something else. Half the room believes he was losing; the other half believes Tavio was let off. Nobody is satisfied and the record is accurate.

### Refusal

Lio may refuse the bout entirely, before or after the sabotage investigation. It is authored, valid, and final: he still gives his testimony against the Bench, because that was never the same request.

**Tavio may not narrate himself denied redemption**, and no dialogue option treats Lio as an obstacle to it.

## Reckoning

After the result, Tavio chooses whether to:

- expose the Bench and accept an arena ban;
- accept sole blame to preserve the institution;
- minimize his knowledge;
- attack Lio's credibility;
- treat an honest loss or win as sufficient restitution.

The strongest choice exposes both personal and institutional culpability without making Lio responsible for absolution.

**The betting is not a conspiracy.** The impossible positions belong to Maro and Seraf personally — two men who know the branch is dying and are hedging its collapse with their own exit money. One ledger line establishes it. It is cynical, human, and small, and it is the cheapest way to show that the institution Maro is asking Tavio to die for is already being liquidated by the man asking.

### The confession owed to Lio

Placed at the weigh-in or after the result, branch-dependent, and spoken to Lio rather than to the player:

> **Tavio:** You were better that season. I knew it in the third exchange.

Four years of *I carried the risk* collapses into one sentence about being outclassed and unable to bear it. **Lio's response is his own**, and he is entitled to find it useless — a fact about Tavio's interior life, offered late, changing nothing about a hip.

Maro and Seraf may be exposed, referred for arrest abroad, stripped of local agents, or left at large. Later content uses state-based substitutes and never requires either man to enter Grenzburg.

## Fail-Forward

- Brace sabotage succeeds: Lio may alter rules, postpone, testify, or withdraw.
- Officials flee: Hella and public records preserve a reduced event.
- Evidence burns: betting shifts and physical tool marks remain.
- Every result advances the campaign. No result advances it *normally* — see The Result below.
- Party defeat during disruption: the bout proceeds later under heavier supervision.
- Missed deadline: the winter relationship opportunity and Selective Yield close; a spring changed examination preserves access to Lancewright without replaying the bout.
