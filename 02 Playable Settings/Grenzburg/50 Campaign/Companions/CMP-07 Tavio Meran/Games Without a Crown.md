# Games Without a Crown

## Quest Function

This universal friendship episode gives Tavio a complete, uninterrupted experience of competition without title, fraud, villain, or crisis.

It is one-on-one in presentation, but not a combat duo quest.

## Invitation

Tavio asks the player to help run a small fair for debtors, forge families, and displaced households. He has no patron, no championship purse, and no authority beyond what participants freely grant.

The player chooses three activities:

- bridge-ring toss;
- marked-barrel relay;
- safe spear-vault demonstration;
- blind spice tasting;
- cookware percussion;
- absurd-prize judging;
- children's chalk arena;
- pack-and-carry team race.

## Rules

Every game has:

- a declared measure;
- an honest handicap;
- a participant-controlled stop;
- a non-humiliating way to withdraw;
- a prize whose joke never depends on poverty, injury, culture, or debt.

The player may compete, officiate, cook, keep score, or invent one final prize.

## Steffen

One competitor is nineteen, from the crane yards, and better than he has any business being.

He wins the safe spear-vault demonstration. When Tavio praises him — publicly, precisely, naming the thing he did well — the boy lights up like a lamp and is unbearable about it for the rest of the afternoon, and everybody forgives him because he is nineteen and it is a fair.

He gives his name as **Steffen**. Nothing else about him surfaces here: no pits, no gear, no shadow. The player should simply like him. ([[Steffen Krall]].)

## The Day

The episode moves through Old Market, Bridgehold, and the Blackjack Yard. Small choices alter dialogue and ambient fair scenes:

- let a child change a rule that excludes younger players;
- catch Tavio quietly correcting a score against himself;
- decide whether a champion should compete under a handicap or serve as teacher;
- choose a winner, shared victory, or comic award;
- accept a loss without demanding a rematch.

There is no attack, theft, sabotage, hidden moral test, or interrupted climax.

## Closing Scene

After cleanup, Tavio admits this is the first crowd since Vorthara whose enjoyment did not depend on him being unbeatable.

The player may answer as:

- a competitor;
- an honest second;
- a friend who does not need a performance;
- someone who simply enjoyed the day.

Winning, losing, or tying changes dialogue only.

## Rewards

- `tavio_friendship_episode`;
- **Declared Handicap**, a small social advantage when proposing fair contests;
- an absurd keepsake chosen during the fair;
- friendship scene access independent of romance or bond.
