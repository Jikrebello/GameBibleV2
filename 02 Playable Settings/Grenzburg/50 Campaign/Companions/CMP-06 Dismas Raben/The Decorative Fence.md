# The Decorative Fence

## Quest Function

Dismas's introduction. **It is a caper, it works completely, and it contains no lesson.**

The player must enjoy being his accomplice before anything in this campaign is allowed to cost them — the same sequencing the roster uses for Elara's first expedition and Bernard's Yard breakfast. The rockfall is not mentioned. His freeze is not staged. Nobody refuses him and nothing goes wrong.

The only shadow in the piece is a neighbour who watches from a window and says nothing, and no narration, flag, or line draws attention to it.

## Premise

[[The Lask Household]] — Anke, Torvald, nine-year-old Bekka, and Anke's brother Girel — hold a stall licence and a back room in the Old Market. A seizure ruling lands the day after tomorrow: technically valid, materially predatory, obtained by a creditor who bought the debt at a discount specifically to take the stock.

There is no appeal in the time available and no illegality to expose. The writ is good. That is the problem.

Dismas proposes the obvious thing, in a tone that makes it sound like a picnic:

> **Dismas:** They cannot seize what is not there. It is astonishing how much of the law is a question of furniture.

## Party and Staging

The normal party remains available. Dismas is required but never the only route.

`Writ Clock 3-0` advances through delay, a lost roof route, or a public disturbance. At 0 the bailiff's men arrive early and the operation becomes an argument in a doorway with the stock half-loaded — which is playable, louder, and worse.

**No forgery.** The quest forbids it and Dismas says why, once, without moralizing: paper that can be checked is a trap you build for the person holding it.

### Pause Point: Route Walked

Once the route is scouted the quest may pause. The Lasks continue trading; the clock does not advance.

## Beginning: Read the Hinge

The party walks the ground with Dismas, who narrates Grenzburg like a man reading a page:

- the market's north gate is locked and its hinge is on the outside;
- the Quarry Steps laundry loft has a landlord who does not live in the city;
- the bailiff's men will come up the wide lane because the wide lane is where the cart fits;
- and the **iron fence** along the stall's back yard is four feet of very good ironwork bolted to nothing, put up by a guild that wanted to look prosperous in a year when it was not.

This is the introduction's teaching: he does not defeat obstacles, he notices which obstacles were never obstacles. Unlocks **Read the Hinge**.

## Middle: The Move

The stock has to be somewhere else by dawn, and the family has to be somewhere the bailiff will not think to look for a week.

Four functions, and the player assigns them:

- **the cover** — a legitimate reason for a cart to be in that lane at that hour (a Yard delivery, a Low Lantern haulage slot, a Customs re-inspection nobody requested);
- **the route** — laundry loft, the market roofline, the drain lane, or straight down the wide lane wearing the correct expression;
- **the swap** — Dismas's preferred method, and the one his banter scene is built on: **leave every document untouched and change what the cart is carrying.** The writ describes goods. It does not describe boxes.
- **the room** — where four people sleep for a week; each option costs something small and real, and the Low Lantern option costs the most later.

Every vocation completes every function. Specialist movement changes speed and elegance, never access.

## Scene Business

The best material in the quest is in its margins and none of it is a test:

- **The bored guard.** He is not corrupt, not cruel, and not stupid; he is nineteen and eleven hours into a twelve-hour post, and Dismas gets him talking about his sister's wedding for exactly as long as the cart needs.
- **Bekka.** Nine years old, stashed in the cart with the crockery, and she laughs at completely the wrong moment. Everyone freezes. Nothing happens. Dismas, afterward, tells her it was the best-timed laugh he has ever heard, and means it.
- **The card trick.** He teaches her one, badly, and loses a queen doing it. He looks for it for about four seconds and then stops looking, which the player may or may not notice. (See [[Dismas Companion Campaign]] on the queen.)
- **Torvald** repairs the cart's bad wheel mid-operation without being asked, and does not speak to anyone.
- **Girel** wants to fight the bailiff's men and is talked out of it by his sister, not by Dismas.

## End: Breakfast

They come out through the decorative fence, which lifts off its bolts in under a minute, and the last of the stock is in a bonded room by first light.

Dismas takes the player up to an unused watch platform above the Quarry Steps — laundry loft, two roofs, a ladder that is not supposed to be there — and produces breakfast, which he has been carrying the whole night.

> **Player:** Is this allowed?
>
> **Dismas:** No. That is why nobody has spoiled it with a railing.

The view is worth it. They eat. He is happy, unguarded, and very funny, and this is the scene the rest of the campaign is measured against. Writes `dismas_roof_scene` — the first pre-commitment relationship beat.

## The Window

As the cart pulled away, a neighbour two doors down watched from an upper window with a lamp behind her.

She did not shout, did not send for anyone, and is not a villain. She saw four Blackjacks and a debtor family remove goods under a valid writ at three in the morning, and she has her own licence, her own inspection due, and her own opinion about people who bring officials onto a street.

**One flagged look. No dialogue. No music. No journal entry.** [[The First Mile]] collects.

- `lask_seizure_state`: stock-saved, stock-partial, or seized;

- `dismas_direction`: movement only if the player pushes him toward abandoning the second half of the job.

## Fail-Forward

- A blown route means the wide lane, in daylight, with a story — harder, funnier, and more publicly witnessed.
- A lost cover means a bribe, a favour owed to Mara Senn, or a genuine Customs re-inspection that costs a day of trading.
- If the bailiff arrives early, the family keeps its persons and loses a third of the stock; nothing is unrecoverable and nobody is hurt.
- **The Lasks are never hurt in this quest, in any branch.** Their cost arrives in winter, at their door, and this piece must not foreshadow it.

## Archetype Chemistry

- Rebel players will love him instantly and should be allowed to, without a corrective.
- Cheerleader/Jock players may want the caper made public or lawful, and he will explain why the paper route ends with the Lasks in a registry.
- Goth/Nerd players notice the window.
- Goofball players and Dismas will make the whole night into a bit, and Bekka will join in.
- Grey receives the complete practical exchange about writs, bonded rooms, and what a bailiff's cart cannot fit down.
