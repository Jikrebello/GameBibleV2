# Dismas Banter and Reactivity

## Voice Key

- Gives authority nicknames and practical warnings.
- Tells good stories with himself missing from the brave part.
- Sometimes uses charm as the first stage of departure; in ordinary work he is often concise or silent.
- Says difficult truths sideways.
- Notices exits before faces and later remembers both.
- Becomes absent under containment.
- Must be allowed to enjoy refusal before its shadow is judged.

## Calibration Scenes

### Joy — The forbidden view

Dismas leads the player through a laundry loft, across two roofs, and onto an unused watch platform.

**Player:** “Is this allowed?”

**Dismas:** “No. That is why nobody has spoiled it with a railing.”

He has brought breakfast.

### Competence — A lawful gate

Dismas watches the guard stamp three travel sheets.

**Dismas:** “Lie to the paper if you like. The cart already knows.”

He swaps the visible cargo cover, leaves every document untouched, and walks through while the player is still considering forgery.

### Embarrassment — He returns

Dismas arrives at the yard after announcing he was leaving Grenzburg.

**Brina:** “Back?”

**Dismas:** “No. Passing through slowly.”

His bedroll is already under his old bunk.

### Anger — Reprisal

A smuggler boasts that a village can absorb the punishment after the clients escape.

Dismas pins the route map to the table with a knife.

**Dismas:** “Then it isn't an escape. You sold them the first mile.”

He stays to plan the return route.

### Care — An unsigned exit

A debtor finds a key, dry boots, and a map beneath their bunk.

Dismas denies involvement until the player points out the missing queen from his card deck was used as the route marker.

**Dismas:** “Waste of a good queen.”

### Shame — The rockfall

The sound of shifting stone stops him.

Later:

**Dismas:** “Three voices. Two at the front. One pretending the wheel wasn't frightening him.”

The player waits.

**Dismas:** “I counted because counting was easier than going back.”

He does not call the confession courage.

### Apology — A route repaired

Dismas returns to a family whose flight drew retaliation and gives them the names, supplies, and testimony needed to move safely.

**Dismas:** “I gave you a door and called the room behind it your problem.”

He accepts whatever task they assign without bargaining for forgiveness.

### Quiet — The second night

Dismas takes the first watch. At dawn he does not leave.

The player wakes during the second night and finds him there again.

**Dismas:** “Do not make this a habit.”

He means the ceremony, not the return.

### Containment — The kind trap

Brina has painted his name on the route board and given him the bunk by the gate as a permanence rather than a habit.

**Brina:** “It's yours. Nobody else takes it.”

**Dismas:** “Right. Good. That is — yes.”

He moves his bedroll three times that week, each time somewhere worse, and cannot say why to anyone including himself. On the eighth night it is back under the old bunk and he acts as though it never left.

### The joke at the wrong moment

Anke Lask is explaining what the licence review will cost her family.

**Dismas:** “I have seen worse queues. There was a toll house in Korrheim where a man—”

*(She waits.)*

*(He hears himself.)*

**Dismas:** “...What day is the hearing.”

The recovery is the character. The joke arriving at all is the shadow.

### Peace — Nothing is wrong

Spring. The network's fee book balances. Two guides have stopped speaking to each other over eleven miles of road.

**Dismas:** “I could go and look at the fen.”

**Player:** “Is something wrong with the fen?”

**Dismas:** “No.”

*(A while.)*

**Dismas:** “That is rather my difficulty.”

## Company-Life Voice Samples

Dismas studies a swollen cellar door.

**Dismas:** “Not locked. Offended. Shoulder it below the hinge.”

Halfway down a steep track:

**Dismas:** “I promised shorter. Comfort was never mentioned.”

He returns a kettle somebody accused him of stealing.

**Dismas:** “I borrowed it from the future. The future objected.”

## Foil Arc — Ilyana

### Autumn

Ilyana turns an illicit passage into a communal event. Dismas objects to visibility; she objects to freedom experienced only through fear and silence.

### Winter

They create an escape and a shelter. Each tries to assign the maintenance to the other. They return together after both initially leave.

### Spring

They build a route and ritual that continue without either founder. Ilyana leaves room in the song; Dismas arrives when promised.

## Faction Reactivity

- Respects Liese's bounded authority and challenges checkpoints, reprisals, and rank without consequence.
- Supports a free Blackjack company and opposes Koss's ownership.
- Values sanctuary without coerced confession.
- Respects particular Folk passage authority while rejecting civilian murder.
- Favors Low Lantern protection and forces it to account for clients after the exciting escape.

## Temperament Use

- Grey trust is established by whether the player leaves him a route back.
- Rebel similarity must not become endless mutual validation.
- Cheerleader contrast is about leverage and capture, not a simple popular-girl/outlaw script.
- Jock authority may earn respect when it accepts consequence.
- These are optional hooks. Grey dialogue is authored first and loses no substance.

## Grounded Chatter Rules

- Before `dismas_romance_interest_signaled`, there is no flirting, sexual teasing, jealousy, suggestive danger-talk, or romantic interpretation of private routes.
- At Normal frequency, use no more than two ambient exchanges during roughly twenty minutes of uninterrupted travel. High draws on extra contextual lines rather than repeated jokes.
- Nicknames require a concrete abuse, pompous display, or established familiarity. Dismas remains professionally civil when antagonism would endanger a client or colleague.
- A good lawful process is allowed to work. He may dislike it, be wrong about it, and correct himself without a quip restoring his status.
- Deaths, funerals, confessions, stealth, major failures, and solemn discoveries impose the global silence lock.

## Failure Modes

Reject any Dismas line that:

- makes him a permanently gloomy guilt container;
- treats every lawful institution as equally corrupt;
- makes escape intrinsically mature;
- converts charisma into constant sarcasm;
- resolves his story by placing him obediently beneath a good commander;
- **says the door was open.** The exits are shown, never named. No character remarks that he could have left;
- **explains Sella Etten,** speaks for her, or interprets her silence. Her instruction is her whole answer;
- **makes the lawful route secretly futile.** When [[Mette Harrow]] is right, she is right on the merits;
- **treats the count as therapy.** He finishes it once. Nobody praises him for it and nobody calls it healing.
