# The First Mile

## Quest Function

The bill for [[The Decorative Fence]], delivered to the wrong door.

This is Dismas's Act I operation and the first thing in his campaign with no exit in it. Its title is his own line: *“Then it isn't an escape. You sold them the first mile.”*

## Premise

Three weeks after the seizure was dodged, the Watch is on the Lasks' street.

Nobody was hurt and nothing was stolen. What happened is smaller and permanent: the neighbour who watched from the window mentioned it, twice, to people who mention things. A creditor with a valid writ and no goods filed a complaint. The Watch opened an inquiry into an Old Market stall licence, and the licence is now **pending review** — which means the stall is shut, in autumn, three weeks before the winter trade.

And **Girel Lask** has been stopped four times in nine days, because he is twenty-two, sleeps in a back room, and matches a description that describes half the ward.

## What Dismas Wants To Do

Move them again. He says so within a minute of hearing, and he has a plan already, and it is a good plan.

The quest lets him start building it. The player can help — a room in Lantern Ward, a licence bought in another name, a cart out to Longlake before the frost. It is faster, cleaner, and entirely within his competence.

**Then Anke Lask says no.**

Not gratefully, not tearfully, and not as a lesson. She wants the stall. She has traded on that pitch for eleven years, her mother traded on it, and the licence is the only thing the family owns that cannot be carried. Moving is what people do when they have already lost.

## Mette

[[Mette Harrow]] is at the market when the argument happens, waiting on a load, and she has opinions about tolls.

She wants the ruling behind the seizure contested **properly**: petitioned, heard, decided, and written down where the next carter can point at it. She is not naive and not a bootlicker. Her cousin's wagon burned in a reprisal three years ago after somebody else's clever escape, in a village she can name, and she can tell you what the second winter looked like.

> **Mette:** Your way, they move. My way, the next one doesn't have to.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> **Mette:** I have watched your way. I know what the second winter looks like.

**She wins the argument on screen.** The quest is required to let her: no scene reveals the lawful route as secretly futile, no clerk betrays the process to vindicate his instincts, and no officer proves him right by accident. She is correct. He is wrong. Everything downstream of the campaign follows from him staying to find that out.

## The Operation

Three tracks, any order, none of them thrilling:

### The Review

Get the licence review heard, and get it heard well.

- witnesses from the Old Market — the fishmonger who has been beside them for nine years, the cooper who owes them nothing, the guild clerk who will confirm eleven years of paid dues;
- the writ's provenance: the debt was bought at a discount, and while that is lawful, the discount is *evidence of intent* and a magistrate is allowed to notice;
- the queue itself, which is four hours long and where half the useful conversations happen.

Dismas has to sit in the Hall of Petitions. Actually sit there. The player may leave; he cannot, and the quest notices whether he does.

### Girel

He is going to be charged with something eventually if the stops continue. Options: a standing surety in a named person's hand, a carrying job with an alibi in it, a word from Brina, or a word from Liese — or Dismas can teach him to disappear, which works and makes him a fugitive at twenty-two.

**The surety costs Dismas something real:** his own name on a Watch form, in a registry, attached to a debtor with a purchased service contract. It is the first document he has voluntarily entered in eleven years.

### The Neighbour

She can be threatened, charmed, avoided, or spoken to.

She is not sorry and does not think she did anything. She has her own inspection due and a view about people who bring officials onto a street, and she is not entirely wrong about that either. The best available outcome is not reconciliation; it is that she stops talking, which is worth exactly as much as it sounds.

### Pause Point: Review Date Set

Once the hearing is scheduled the quest may pause. The stall stays shut.

## End: The Task

However the review goes — licence restored, restored with conditions, or lost for the season — the Lasks do the thing the banter file has always said they would.

They assign him something.

Not thanks and not forgiveness: **a job.** Standing surety visits at the monthly review. A carry of stock they cannot move themselves. Being the name on a form for a family that cannot use its own. It runs through winter and into spring, it is boring, it cannot be delegated, and the exit stays open the entire time.

He accepts it without bargaining, and the scene is his banter apology, staged at last:

> **Dismas:** I gave you a door and called the room behind it your problem.

He does not ask whether they forgive him. He asks what day.

## Outputs

- `dismas_lask_repair_begun`: true — Act I preparation;
- `dismas_lask_task_kept`: initialized; resolves through ambient follow-through across winter and spring — Act II preparation;
- `lask_licence_state`: restored, conditional, or lost-for-the-season;
- `girel_lask_state`: sureted, employed, charged, or vanished;
- `toll_precedent_state`: filed, pending, or refused — Mette carries a copy if filed;
- `dismas_direction`: **returning** if he stays through the review and takes the task; **held** if the player or Brina has to insist; **gone** if he funds it and leaves.

## Fail-Forward

- A lost review means a season shut, not a family destroyed; the precedent can still be filed in spring.
- A charged Girel is recoverable through testimony, at cost, and remembers who stood up.
- If Dismas skips the hearing, it proceeds worse and the task is still offered — more coldly.
- **No outcome makes the lawful route fail on the merits.** If it loses, it loses on time, money, or a clerk's error, and Mette says so.

## Archetype Chemistry

- Rebel players will hate the queue and should; the quest is asking them to sit in it anyway.
- Nerd/Cheerleader players are unusually strong here and Dismas notices, with some irritation.
- Jock players may want to solve Girel by intimidation, which works for a week.
- Goofball players get the card trick performed at an actual bailiff, which the bailiff enjoys.
- Grey receives the complete practical exchange about discounted debt, licence review, and surety.
