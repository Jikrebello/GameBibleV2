# Companion Dialogue and Scene Standard

This note controls companion voice, banter, response writing, and emotional scene construction. It exists to prevent technically correct dialogue from sounding like characters politely presenting their design documents to one another.

## First Rule

Characters enter a scene wanting something immediate.

They may want the kettle, the dry blanket, the last word, a witness to leave, a route chosen, a joke to land, a question avoided, a buckle repaired, or somebody to stay. Theme emerges from how they pursue that want. Theme is not the thing they announce.

## Prohibited Shortcuts

Do not write:

- two characters accurately summarizing one another's wounds;
- balanced four-line exchanges that end in mutual insight;
- therapy vocabulary where ordinary speech, faith, craft, insult, silence, or gesture would carry the meaning;
- dialogue whose only purpose is restating a central lie or need;
- universal eloquence under fear, shame, exhaustion, or anger;
- constant snark as proof of intimacy;
- a joke immediately explained by the person who made it;
- an argument that resolves because both parties state the correct lesson;
- interchangeable "you care more than you admit" observations;
- exposition both speakers already know unless one is using it to accuse, tease, test, conceal, or persuade.

## Scene Construction

Every substantial companion scene records:

1. **surface business:** what is physically or socially happening;
2. **private want:** what the companion hopes to obtain or avoid;
3. **player leverage:** what the player can notice, challenge, grant, or refuse;
4. **turn:** the interruption, discovery, joke, error, silence, or action that changes the exchange;
5. **residue:** what remains unresolved afterward.

At least one line or action must be impossible to transfer to another companion without sounding wrong.

## Human Texture

Use:

- interruptions, unfinished answers, corrections, and delayed replies;
- mismatched line length;
- attention to tools, clothes, weather, food, pain, smell, crowds, and work;
- private names, repeated jokes, petty preferences, and remembered embarrassments;
- mistakes about other people that may take more than one scene to correct;
- silence that has a specific action inside it;
- affection expressed through the companion's care language;
- moments where a companion is funny without performing comedy;
- moments where a companion is wrong without becoming monstrous;
- moments where a companion is right for reasons nobody else in the party could supply.

## Calibration Set

Each companion response bible requires eight short calibration scenes:

1. **Joy:** enjoying something with no immediate threat.
2. **Competence:** solving a concrete problem in their own manner.
3. **Embarrassment:** losing social control without turning immediately solemn.
4. **Anger:** revealing what they protect and how they become unfair.
5. **Care:** helping without explaining the virtue of helping.
6. **Shame:** attempting concealment, deflection, or repair.
7. **Apology:** making a specific repair rather than requesting absolution.
8. **Quiet:** sharing space without converting silence into exposition.

These are voice tests, not mandatory final game scenes.

## Ambient Balance

Across a companion's ambient and reactive material:

| Material | Target share |
|---|---:|
| appetite, humor, observation, taste, and ordinary life | 35% |
| competence, tactics, craft, and world interpretation | 25% |
| relationships, factions, and current events | 20% |
| original wrong, fear, and direct arc pressure | no more than 20% |

Individual quests may concentrate arc pressure. The overall companion must still feel alive when the personal plot is not being discussed.

## Banter

Banter is not a portable ethics seminar.

- Begin from shared surroundings, work, rumor, food, equipment, weather, or another character's visible behavior.
- Let pairings develop recurring bits and wrong assumptions.
- Some exchanges end because the road changes, somebody refuses, or neither has the language.
- Foil pairs receive an autumn, winter, and spring progression.
- Non-foil pairings still require a concrete bond object: a task, hobby, wager, irritation, ritual, or person they both care about.
- Other companions never intrude on the emotional climax of a duo quest.

## Player Temperament

Archetype variants alter the shape of an exchange, not its moral grade.

- Write the grey scene first.
- Add primary variants only where the player would genuinely frame the moment differently.
- Let the companion misread the player sometimes.
- A secondary tendency modifies one beat at most.
- Do not make every archetype say its philosophy.
- Do not label archetype dialogue in the player-facing text.

## Voice Calibration

### Elara

Bright imperatives, names used deliberately, public praise, elegant euphemism, quick wagers, and an instinct to arrange bodies in a room. Under humiliation she becomes ceremonially polite and begins speaking for other people.

### Ylva

Compressed practical language, weather and distance as measures, care disguised as instruction, and humor so dry it can pass unnoticed. Under fear she grows quieter and takes on work nobody assigned.

### Ashira

Exact observation, patient pauses, private questions asked without flinching, and dark amusement that never begs to be admired. Under threat she becomes intimate rather than loud and may use another person's secret as a scalpel.

### Ilyana

Sensory metaphor, rhythm changes, playful invention, and questions that invite a person to imagine another self. Under pressure she turns conversation into a performance and may crowd out the answer she requested.

### Bernard

Earthy stories, absurd specificity, hospitable teasing, prayer language, and jokes that make room rather than claim it. When the joke stops, his sentences become very plain.

### Dismas

Sideways honesty, nicknames for authority, abrupt practical warnings, and stories that leave out the moment he mattered. Under containment he becomes charming, then absent.

### Zafir

Definitions, examples, careful distinctions, sudden technical enthusiasm, and dry corrections offered as gifts. Under shame he overexplains until the human subject disappears.

### Tavio

Kinetic language, inclusive challenges, public encouragement, bodily metaphors, and the assumption that effort deserves witnesses. Under shame he becomes formal, stops touching people, and calls everyone by title.

## Blind Voice Test

For each companion:

1. select twelve representative lines across at least four emotional modes;
2. remove names and dialogue labels;
3. require an informed reader to identify at least ten correctly;
4. revise any line identifiable only because it mentions the companion's vocation, homeland, or original wrong.

The test evaluates voice, not catchphrases.

## Production Status

- `emotional_rewrite_status: bible-locked` marks an approved character or voice foundation.
- `emotional_rewrite_status: pending-paired-pass` marks a campaign authority whose structure remains valid while its scene writing awaits the named pair pass.
- `emotional_rewrite_status: preserve`, `reframe`, or `replace` records a formal piece's current audit disposition.
- `emotional_rewrite_status: cleared` is reserved for a paired pass that has completed the required emotional rewrite.
- `dialogue_status: calibrated` means the character bible and calibration set pass this standard.
- `dialogue_status: pending-rewrite` means the scene or quest retains structural value but its dialogue is not implementation-ready.
- `dialogue_status: approved` is reserved for a later scene-level pass.
