# Companion Dialogue and Scene Standard

This note controls companion voice, scene construction, banter, restraint, and relationship presentation under [[Companion Grounded Writing Standard]].

## First Rule

Characters enter a scene wanting something immediate: a route chosen, a buckle repaired, a witness gone, a meal finished, a rule obeyed, an accusation withdrawn, a joke to land, or permission not to answer. Theme emerges through conduct.

## Adult Speech

- People adapt to rank, culture, hospitality, danger, age, and familiarity.
- Civil speech is not blandness. It allows conviction without permanent antagonism.
- Not everyone is equally witty, emotionally articulate, sexually forward, or comfortable naming a feeling.
- A person may be wrong, silent, unfair, embarrassed, distracted, or unable to finish.
- Rudeness requires an object. The scene must establish what caused it and what it costs.
- An apology names a specific act and repair; it does not request absolution.

## Prohibited Shortcuts

Do not write:

- constant snark as proof of personality;
- balanced four-line exchanges ending in mutual insight;
- companions diagnosing one another's wounds;
- therapy language where faith, craft, insult, duty, silence, or gesture belongs;
- universal eloquence during fear, shame, exhaustion, or anger;
- a joke immediately explained or used to close grief;
- flirtation before a player interest signal;
- sexualized comments used to make a companion seem modern, free, dangerous, or charming;
- a companion commenting on every player decision;
- exposition both speakers know unless it is being used to accuse, test, hide, persuade, or correct.

## Scene Record

Every substantial scene identifies:

1. surface business;
2. immediate private want;
3. player leverage;
4. social context and manners;
5. turn;
6. residue that remains unresolved;
7. whether silence is stronger than another line.

At least one action or line must be impossible to transfer to another companion without sounding false.

## Dialogue Balance

Across ambient and reactive material:

| Material | Target |
|---|---:|
| work, place, tactics, craft, and current events | 50% |
| appetite, humor, ordinary irritation, and local relationships | 25% |
| trust, factional opinion, friendship, and attraction | 15% |
| original wrong and direct backstory | no more than 10% |

Personal quests may concentrate history. Recruitment and ordinary travel may not.

## Banter

Banter begins from visible business: weather, food, equipment, a road, a person, a task, an injury, a mistake, or something both characters witnessed.

- **Low:** no ambient pair exchanges; retain essential, tactical, quest, and player-prompted lines.
- **Normal:** no more than two ambient exchanges in roughly twenty minutes of uninterrupted travel.
- **High:** no more than four contextual exchanges in the same period.
- All settings use the same information, approval, and quest access.
- Repetition suppression is global across settings.
- Silence locks override frequency after deaths, funerals, confessions, major failures, stealth entry, solemn discoveries, and explicit requests for quiet.
- No pair exists to insult one another at camp.

## Relationships

The player must select an explicit interest line before romantic subtext activates. Friendly warmth, admiration, and trust are not retroactively treated as flirting.

After interest:

- attraction develops privately and intermittently;
- the companion may decline without punishment;
- commitment remains an authored choice;
- other companions do not provide a chorus about the relationship;
- mature intimacy fades to black.

Sworn fellowship has no romantic imagery, jealousy, sexual tension, or fade-to-black presentation.

## Calibration

Each companion requires grounded examples of:

- useful work;
- ordinary pleasure;
- social manners with a stranger;
- disagreement without contempt;
- anger with a concrete object;
- care without explanation;
- embarrassment without immediate confession;
- quiet shared space.

No more than two of these scenes may directly mention the original wrong.

## Voice Test

Select twelve lines across work, pleasure, disagreement, care, and quiet. Remove names and labels. An informed reader should identify at least ten through priorities and rhythm, not homeland, vocation, catchphrases, sexuality, or past wrongdoing.

## Production Status

- `dialogue_status: calibrated` means the bible passes ordinary-life voice tests.
- `dialogue_status: pending-rewrite` means useful source remains but is not implementation-ready.
- `dialogue_status: approved` requires the grounded company, respect, interest, and silence tests.
- `grounded_rewrite_status: approved` certifies the social foundation, not final performance capture.
