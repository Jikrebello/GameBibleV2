# What the Ice Takes

## Quest Function

Ylva's Act II finale is a player-and-Ylva evacuation across failing Longlake ice under Grobi pressure. It tests informed risk, distributed command, and whether abandoning cargo becomes a shared civic decision or another private debt she uses to justify self-erasure.

Other companions leave at the staging point. Signal crews, boat hands, shelter workers, and evacuees are temporary NPCs outside the companion limit.

## Trigger

A Grobi push closes the west-bank road while fire and wall damage threaten the nearest city gate. West Reed Ferry holds:

- nineteen civilians;
- six wounded soldiers and laborers;
- three ferry hands;
- two sleds of flour and dried fish;
- one chest of field medicine;
- bridge rope and repair timber.

The lake has frozen unevenly. A direct crossing can move everyone before the Grobi arrive, but loaded sleds may break the weakest sheet.

Ylva proposes moving each group herself along separate lanes.

## Beginning: Sound the Ice

At Longlake Ward Shore, the duo receives incomplete reports from three sounding teams. Preparation determines how much is already known.

The player and Ylva establish:

- one thick northern arc suitable for people and light litters;
- one shorter central lane with rotten ice near an old current;
- a southern reed line protected from arrows but too narrow for full sleds;
- two shore stations able to signal load changes.

Every route remains usable by all vocations. Shared ice poles, ropes, planks, swimming, carrying, and ordinary climbing provide the baseline.

## Crossing One: The Wounded

The wounded group cannot move at one pace.

The player decides whether to:

- use litters on the northern arc;
- divide ambulatory and carried patients;
- send the fastest people first to prepare shelter;
- keep everyone together at greater time cost.

Ylva wants to take the heaviest litter and the lead line. A shared approach gives one role to a ferry hand or the player and tells the receiving shore exactly what risk they are accepting.

Grobi archers test the ice from distance. Smoke, cover, counterfire, fast movement, or breaking their angle all work.

## Crossing Two: Food and Medicine

The second group brings both people and stores. The medicine chest can travel with a person. The food sleds cannot safely use the same sheet at full load.

Options include:

- unload food into hand-carried bundles;
- abandon one sled and preserve the other;
- raft sealed sacks beneath a line;
- draw Grobi pressure toward the stronger northern arc;
- send food by the longer reed lane;
- sacrifice all freight to maximize evacuation speed.

With three preparation assets and strong play, every person, medicine, and essential food supply can survive. Fewer assets force real material tradeoffs.

## Distant Choice

One receiving team reports a new crack and asks whether it should remain in place or withdraw.

Ylva initially attempts to order them to stay while withholding the full reading. The player may insist that the team receive:

- the measured depth;
- the expected load;
- the alternative route;
- the consequence of withdrawal;
- and authority to choose.

The team's informed decision varies with trust and preparation. They may hold, relocate, or create a third receiving point. Respecting that choice moves Ylva toward shared even when it costs time or stores.

## The Break

During the final movement, Grobi skirmishers push onto the ferry approach and a loaded sled opens the central crack.

The duo must:

- keep civilians moving;
- prevent pursuit from reaching the line;
- recover anyone in the water;
- decide whether to cut loose the food sled;
- reach Icehouse Steps before the current widens the break.

Ylva anchors herself as the last line holder. Her harness begins pulling beneath the ice.

The player can:

- take the second anchor;
- order the receiving team to share load;
- cut the sled and release the strain;
- use a prepared extraction line;
- accept losing remaining cargo;
- encourage Ylva to stay alone.

No one solution requires a particular vocation. Combat, traversal, rescue, and environmental interactions overlap without a single-roll death.

## Liability

At Icehouse Steps, a ducal clerk and Medori factor ask who authorized the abandoned stores.

Possible filings:

- named collective emergency authority;
- ducal siege loss;
- settlement relief loss;
- Blackjack company decision;
- Ylva's sole surety;
- falsified claim against a dead or absent person.

The strongest moral result uses a truthful shared filing. The player need not favor Medori, Duke, Church, Folk, or underworld to achieve it.

Ylva must agree not to sign alone. The player cannot secretly fix this after she leaves.

## End

Ylva assesses the receiving shelters and sees other teams continue the work without her. If Trusted and personally accompanied, she opens relationship commitment at the next safe hub.

The scene states plainly that the player did not "save" her from being competent. They required her to include herself in the rescue plan.

## Fail-Forward

- **A route fails:** the group returns to stable ice and uses a slower lane.
- **A person falls through:** immediate rescue begins; failure causes injury and resource loss, not off-screen death.
- **Grobi seize the ferry:** the final group crosses under greater ranged pressure.
- **Food is lost:** winter prices and shelter strain worsen, but the campaign continues.
- **Medicine is lost:** casualty recovery takes longer and one nonfatal injury becomes lasting.
- **Ylva signs alone:** the evacuation succeeds while her debt and managed direction worsen.
- **Duo defeat:** receiving teams pull survivors from the line according to readiness; no unseen death occurs.

## Rewards

- **Rescue Scout Doctrine:** Ylva prioritizes carried, wounded, and interaction-locked allies more effectively.
- relationship commitment becomes available if other requirements are met.
- Act II direction and readiness states.
- access to [[A Hearth Chosen]] or [[The Sister Watch]], never both.
