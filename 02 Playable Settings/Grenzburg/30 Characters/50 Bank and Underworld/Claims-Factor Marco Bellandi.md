# Claims-Factor Marco Bellandi

## Core Concept

Marco Bellandi is a forty-four-year-old Fiorenzian claims-factor assigned beneath [[Ledger-Master Lucia Medori]]. His narrow portfolio contains the eight purchased sureties, restitution bonds, civil liabilities, and service claims attached to Grenzburg's principal Blackjack specialists.

He is the person a companion must face when an account changes. He is not the person who decides whether they have become better.

## Appearance and Manner

Bellandi dresses as a travelling commercial officer rather than a court banker: dark green wool, weatherproof boots, plain gloves, and one Medori brass seal worn inside the coat.

He is dry, attentive, and exact. He does not imitate sympathy he does not feel. He offers water before a difficult meeting, leaves enough silence for an answer, and writes down the answer that was given rather than the one that would make the file easier.

He dislikes theatrical threats, vague mercy, and Koss standing behind a debtor while they sign.

## Conviction and Blind Spot

Bellandi believes enforceable obligation is one of the structures that lets strangers cooperate without trust.

He therefore insists that a claim should be:

- finite;
- legible;
- evidenced;
- reviewable;
- protected from informal additions;
- closed when its stated conditions are met.

His blind spot is fundamental. He believes those safeguards can make ownership of a person's service legitimate. He can recognize cruelty, fraud, and bad administration without recognizing the full wrong of the architecture he serves.

## Authority

Bellandi may:

- reject unsupported charges;
- separate a purchased claim from Koss's operating account;
- hold restitution or recovered property in escrow;
- receive remote instructions, testimony, and refusals;
- recommend restructuring or satisfaction to Lucia;
- execute ordinary releases within his delegated thresholds;
- prepare the companion settlements used by [[Accounts of the Living]].

He cannot:

- abolish Medori policy;
- release every debtor by preference;
- forgive crimes or private injuries;
- force Lucia to accept a collective settlement;
- countermand Brina's company governance outside the account;
- decide a companion's approval, relationship, or moral outcome.

## Dramatic Function

Bellandi gives all eight companions one consistent local legal interface without turning him into a universal antagonist.

- With **Elara**, he administers remote household instructions and Vardek's restoration offer without becoming either side's advocate.
- With **Ashira**, he distinguishes civil liability from the Bank's appetite for a useful forbidden method.
- With later companions, he will replace visiting former employers, victims, colleagues, and rivals as the person who can legally alter the file.

He should appear briefly and specifically. A scene belongs to Bellandi only when a claim, charge, escrow, release, or settlement actually changes.

## Relationship to Grenzburg Figures

- **Lucia:** his superior. He respects her competence and does not mistake limited reform for liberation.
- **Brina:** an adversarial collaborator. She knows the lived cost behind his categories and uses his own record standards against Koss.
- **Koss:** a recurring source of unsupported additions, coerced signatures, and conveniently missing equipment evidence.
- **The companions:** eight different people, never a matched set. Bellandi refuses to discuss one file as precedent for another in front of its subject.

## Outcome Range

Bellandi may remain a Medori claims-factor, serve a regulated Bank, transfer the portfolio into a free-company settlement, execute ducal absorption, or lose authority under a hostile faction outcome.

His procedural fairness is a real good inside a corrupt ownership system. It is not moral neutrality and does not make Medori ownership just.

## Voice

Bellandi speaks in short factual sentences and asks one question at a time.

- “That charge is not in the instrument.”
- “The refusal is authenticated. It is still a refusal.”
- “I can close the account. I cannot tell you what the closure means.”
- “Captain Koss may submit his loss. He may not submit your body as its explanation.”

He never delivers the moral lesson of a companion quest.
