### “The Ledger Never Lies” (Duchy: Bad)
**Role in the Rule of Three:**  
He is the Duchy’s “Bad” engine: **extraction, patronage, and legalized predation**. Enzo turns every crisis into revenue and every concession into a debt owed to him personally. He is the reason the Duke can pay the garrison and fund the walls—while also being the reason the Duchy is perpetually one bad winter away from bankruptcy or revolt.

---
## Core concept
A career administrator from a minor knightly house, risen through ruthless competence in revenue collection. He sees the frontier not as a shield to hold, but as a **claim to mine**. His philosophy: survival requires coin, coin requires pressure, and pressure creates opportunity.  
**He is the man who makes the Duchy profitable—by making sure someone always pays more than they should.**

---
## Public face
- The polished official at charter signings, smiling as he hands over mining rights or lumber quotas.
   
- Speaks in reassuring mercantile language: “fair shares,” “mutual benefit,” “investment in our common future.”
   
- Presents himself as the practical voice keeping the Duke’s treasury afloat, patron of guilds and prosperous vassals.

---
## Private truth
- He believes the Marches are a temporary windfall—timber, ore, artifacts—and the smart man extracts while the extracting is good.
   
- He keeps private ledgers separate from the ducal ones; his real wealth is in favors, hidden shares, and off-books claims.
   
- He views the Duke as a useful figurehead and the Castellan as a dangerous idealist who doesn’t understand that **order without profit is unsustainable**.
   
- Deep down, he knows his methods breed resentment that could one day burn the city down—but that’s a problem for after he’s secured his own exit.

---
## Goals
### Short-term (this year)
1. Maximize autumn charter and toll revenue before lockdown
   
2. Secure winter “emergency fees” (entry permits, ration surcharges, labor exemptions)
   
3. Expand personal stake in high-yield operations (a hidden share in a rich mine, a cut of artifact sales)
   
4. Neutralize threats to his networks (rival smugglers, honest auditors, overzealous Church investigators)
   
5. Keep the Bank cooperative without letting them audit his private books

### Long-term (5–10 years)
1. Accumulate enough liquid wealth to buy a comfortable estate in a safer, richer duchy
   
2. Build a patronage network strong enough to survive a change of duke (or orchestrate one)
   
3. Corner the artifact black market through proxies
   
4. Ensure his family inherits key bailiff posts or guild monopolies
   
---
## Resources
- **Revenue monopoly:** collects ducal taxes, tolls, fines, charter fees, market duties
   
- **Charter authority:** grants/revokes lumber rights, mining claims, expedition licenses, trading privileges
   
- **Patronage web:** network of indebted vassals, guild masters, camp overseers, and dock officials
   
- **Information asymmetry:** knows who owes what, who smuggles what, who can be blackmailed
   
- **Private muscle:** hired enforcers (often ex-Blackjacks or deserters) operating under “official” cover
   
- **Access to seals and warrants:** can legitimize almost any transaction—for a price

---
## Constraints and vulnerabilities
- **He needs the Duke’s signature:** without it, his charters are contestable.
   
- **He needs plausible deniability:** open corruption invites the Castellan’s tribunals or Church condemnation.
   
- **He is hated by the commons:** miners, lumberjacks, and small vassals see him as the face of every unfair levy.
   
- **His ledgers are a liability:** if the wrong person sees the private ones, he’s finished.
   
- **Bank rivalry:** the Medori see him as a competitor in the extraction game.

---
## Taboo lines (what he avoids unless the payoff is enormous)
- Directly stealing from the winter granaries (starvation riots would destroy the revenue base)
   
- Openly defying a ducal command in public (he prefers to reinterpret or delay)
   
- Alienating the entire guild bloc (he needs their cooperation to keep production flowing)
   
- Crossing the Castellan into open confrontation (Pierre commands the garrison)
   
- Dealing directly with grobi (too risky, too little legitimacy)

When he does cross a line, he does it through layers of proxies.

---
## Leverage points (how others pressure him)
- **The Duke:** can demand audits or replace him (though finding a competent successor is hard)
   
- **The Castellan:** controls the garrison and can enforce “honest” collections
   
- **The Church:** can declare certain fees usurious or morally corrosive
   
- **The Bank:** can outbid him for charters or expose his private dealings
   
- **Disgruntled vassals/miners:** strikes or sabotage can crater revenue
   
- **Black market rivals:** undercut his smuggling cuts

---
## Seasonal behavior
### Summer  
- Auction season: sells new charters to prospectors and expedition leaders.
   
- Travels the camps, inspecting (and assessing) operations, building debts.
   
- Quietly funds ruin delves that promise high-return artifacts.

### Autumn  
- Peak extraction: entry tolls, emergency taxes, “winter preparation levies.”
   
- Sells exceptions to gate policy and ration priority.
   
- Buys up distressed claims from failing camps.

### Winter  
- Operates from warm offices: collects “protection” from warehouse holders, runs high-stakes gambling on ration futures.
   
- Trades favors for fuel, food, or influence inside the locked city.
   
- Uses scarcity to force indebted vassals into deeper obligations.

### Spring  
- Debt collection season: calls in loans, seizes failed claims, auctions reclaimed land.
   
- Positions allies for the next summer’s charters.
   
- Covers tracks from winter excesses before the Castellan can investigate.

## Relationship to the player (Blackjacks)
He sees you as both threat and opportunity—competent outsiders who can be bought or intimidated.

### How he sees you
- Useful for dirty jobs that protect his revenue streams.
   
- Potential rivals (you work for the Bank, his biggest competitor).
   
- People who understand debt as a tool (he respects that).

### What he offers
- Lucrative side contracts: recover a stolen ledger, intimidate a striking miner boss, escort “his” artifact shipment.
   
- Insider information on charters and claims ripe for exploitation.
   
- Protection from Castellan scrutiny (for a cut).

### What he demands
- Loyalty when interests align: sabotage a rival’s mine, frame a honest bailiff, deliver a message the Duke must never see.
   
- A share of any artifact haul you bring back.

If you cross him, he doesn’t rage—he quietly adds your name to a ledger.

---
## Internal politics (Duchy Bad vs Duchy Good vs Duchy Ugly)
- **With Duke Alaric (Ugly):** symbiotic but tense—Enzo funds the Duke’s compromises, but constantly pushes for more latitude.
   
- **Against Castellan Pierre (Good):** mortal enemies—Pierre wants clean books, Enzo wants creative ones.
   
- **With the Bank:** wary partnership—shared interest in extraction, but they’re bigger predators.
   
- **With the Church:** polite distance—he pays tithes generously to avoid scrutiny.

---
## What makes him human (not cartoon evil)
- He genuinely believes the Duchy would collapse without his revenue.
   
- He protects his own people (family, retainers, loyal vassals) fiercely.
   
- He remembers winters when he was hungry; profit is security.

---
## Failure modes (how he can fall)
1. **Audit exposure:** private ledgers discovered → ducal trial or Castellan arrest
   
2. **Revolt from below:** miners or lumberjacks rise up → Enzo becomes the face of every grievance
   
3. **Bank squeeze:** Medori outmaneuver him and buy his debts
   
4. **Assassination:** too many enemies, one finally pays the right killer
   
5. **Duke’s scapegoat:** when revenue fails, Enzo is sacrificed to appease the Castellan or Church

## 10 quest premises that naturally emerge from Enzo
1. **The Forged Charter:** someone is operating a mine under Enzo’s seal—but he never signed it. Recover the plate before the Castellan notices.
   
2. **The Striking Camp:** lumberjacks refuse quotas. Enzo needs production restored—by negotiation, intimidation, or replacement labor.
   
3. **The Hidden Share:** a rich ore vein was reported low-yield. Enzo suspects the warden is cheating him. Prove it and claim his cut.
   
4. **The Winter Toll:** a Folk clan wants to bring herds inside the outer wards—for a fee. Enzo wants the deal, but the Castellan will object.
   
5. **The Rival Bidder:** a Bank-backed consortium is undercutting Enzo’s charter auctions. Sabotage their bid without tracing it back.
   
6. **The Smuggler’s Ledger:** a captured smuggler has records that could expose Enzo’s private trades. Retrieve or destroy it.
   
7. **The Debt Collector:** a vassal knight defaulted and fled to a ruin. Bring him back—alive for testimony, dead for silence.
   
8. **The Artifact License:** Enzo will grant your team exclusive rights to a promising ruin—if you cut him in and keep the Church out.
   
9. **The Grain Shortfall:** warehouse audit shows missing stocks. Enzo needs a scapegoat before the Castellan digs deeper.
   
10. **The Succession Play:** rumors of a ducal heir in debt. Enzo wants leverage—find the proof and deliver it quietly.
