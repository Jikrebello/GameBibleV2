# The White-Sled Rescue

## Event

In the winter of 1359 AR, a mixed Medori freight and passenger convoy attempted Snowblind Rise during a tightening weather window. A wind shift erased the marked road, and a shelf collapse trapped the lead sleds above an exposed descent.

[[Ylva Winter-Track]], then serving as a contracted route scout, judged that waiting for formal cargo authorization would kill people. She ordered the freight cut loose and used it as rescue material:

- two cargo sleds became litters and windbreak frames;
- sailcloth and packing felt became shelters;
- lamp oil sustained warming and signal fires;
- cordage secured the surviving wagons;
- valuable timber was burned to keep the exposed passengers alive.

Every trapped passenger and crew member reached the lower shelter alive. Several suffered frost injury, but none died.

## The Claim

Medori's regional office treated the destroyed freight, converted vehicles, missed delivery, and recovery work as a single material-loss claim. The convoy crew had credible grounds to contest:

- the dispatch window;
- inadequate weather reserve;
- shared emergency authority;
- and the value Medori assigned to property already unrecoverable after the collapse.

Before the crew could organize a joint answer, Ylva signed the full surety. She believed a sole admission would prevent the drivers, guards, and passengers from being drawn into litigation or debt service.

The signature let Medori convert the claim into personal service and sell Ylva's labour into the [[Blackjack Corps]].

## Moral Control

The rescue was right. Human life took precedence over replaceable freight.

The claim was exploitative. Medori used emergency loss and a hurried signature to secure far more labour than the rescue reasonably owed.

Ylva's mistake was not spending the cargo. It was deciding that no one else could share the truth, liability, resistance, or cost. She protected the crew from a choice they had a right to make.

No hidden saboteur, supernatural force, corrupt survivor, or homeland enemy caused the event. No person from the convoy appears in Grenzburg. The event persists through Ylva's contract, her memories, and the habits it reinforced.

## Present Consequences

- Ylva treats every unclaimed burden as hers.
- She hides injury until it changes the route.
- She records expendable stores precisely but avoids discussing her own limits.
- Koss cites the surety when assigning her extra watches.
- Brina regards the signature as a model of how emergency debt isolates useful people.
- Ylva initially describes the event as proof that taking everything on herself worked.
