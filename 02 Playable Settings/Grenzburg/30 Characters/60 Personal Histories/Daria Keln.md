# Daria Keln

## Core Concept

Daria Keln is Lord Marek Vardek's reserve expedition factor, records clerk, and conditional field antagonist. She assumes control of the Seven Mounds operation only when [[Vasko Dren]] has already been captured or killed.

She exists to preserve consequence rather than plot armour. Removing Vasko changes the later expedition's tactics, evidence, and personality; it does not make Vardek abandon a profitable operation already in motion.

## Background

Daria began as a literate estate clerk who converted labor advances, inherited fines, food charges, and travel costs into apparently voluntary hiring records. She never entered the graves herself. She knew why workers could not leave and learned to describe coercion as settled account.

Vardek promoted her because she could recover a damaged operation without sharing Vasko's vanity. She is less capable with shoring and demolition but better at schedules, false manifests, replacement crews, and moving blame into documents.

## Presentation

Daria is in her late thirties, compact, plainly dressed, and almost aggressively unmemorable. She carries a ledger case, a short sword, waxed signal cards, a hand crossbow, and keys distributed among hired overseers.

She fights defensively while workers, doors, and records separate pursuers from her exit. She does not use supernatural protection or hidden patronage.

## Conditional Quest Role

- In [[The Sixth Field Book]], Daria is named in correspondence as Vardek's reserve field factor but does not expose herself to capture.
- If Vasko remains free, she controls the Seven Mounds perimeter and document carts while he controls the excavation.
- If Vasko is captured or dead, she becomes the principal human opponent in [[A House Fed by Bones]] and favors sealed exits, hostage leverage, and burning the worker roll over a demolition spectacle.
- If she escapes, she may organize record substitution in [[Sixty-Three Claims]] or the loyalist clearance inside [[No Lady of Velescar]].
- Capture or death is permanent. A generic Vardek household captain may fill a combat position later, but never recreates Daria's knowledge.

## Moral Boundary

Daria was not born to rank and received fewer protections than Elara. That difference matters when assigning privilege; it does not excuse her deliberate conversion of family vulnerability into labor control.

She may surrender when her records, exits, and guards are lost. She may testify to save herself, but testimony does not turn her into the workers' representative.
