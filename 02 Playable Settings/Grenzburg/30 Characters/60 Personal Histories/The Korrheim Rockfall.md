# The Korrheim Rockfall

The controlling account of [[Dismas Raben|Dismas Raben's]] conviction, his purchased service, and the thing he has never let himself finish.

## The Plan

In the autumn of 1355 AR, Dismas's band prepared a rockfall above the Stonefall Cut on the Korrheim revenue road. A wagon of seized tolls was expected within a two-day window: escorted, insured, and — by the band's reasoning and by the reasoning of most people who used that road — stolen already.

The preparation was competent. Dismas set it himself: three anchors, one release, a spotter's line of sight from the upper track. He had done it twice before without a death.

## The Trigger

He fired it before confirming the target.

He has never claimed a mistake about what he heard. He heard wheels and an escort's pace, he was cold and had been waiting two days, and he pulled it because it was the sound he had decided to expect.

The coach beneath the stone was a civilian post-coach: a driver and three passengers on a shared fare.

## The Fixed Record

**These facts do not vary by playthrough, preparation, direction, or approval. No player action discovers a kinder version, and none earns the arithmetic comfort of a worse one.**

- **Two people died.** The driver, **Hedric Vane**, aged fifty-one, of Korrheim town, killed by the fall itself. And a passenger, **Anke Bekk**, aged nineteen, travelling to a place her family did not tell the court, who did not die at once.
- **One survived.** [[Sella Etten]], a passenger, pinned but alive, dug out by the soldiers whose approach made Dismas run.
- **One walked away.** The fourth passenger was thrown clear, gave a statement, and vanished from the record entirely — untraceable, unnamed, and not a mystery the campaign solves.
- **The counterfactual is unavailable.** Whether staying would have saved Anke Bekk cannot be established. The stone shifted twice; the soldiers arrived within the hour; the record supports both readings and settles neither. **No document, testimony, examination, or expert in the campaign resolves it, and none may be written that does.**

He heard people alive under the stone. He counted three voices. He left.

## The Conviction

Dismas was taken four months later on an unrelated road charge and convicted of armed robbery and road destruction. **The full human cost was never established** — the coach was recorded as damaged, the deaths were attributed to the fall as an incident rather than to a person, and the prosecution had a confession to the robbery and no appetite for the harder proof.

He did not correct them.

Offered long imprisonment or purchased service, he took the service. Medori acquired it eighteen months later in a portfolio sale and moved him to the Grenzburg march.

## The Gap

This is the campaign's spine: **the legal record is smaller than the truth, and only Dismas can close the difference.**

Nobody is looking for him. No survivor is coming. No warrant exists for the deaths. The count he never made is the one act of accountability in the roster that the world will never compel — see [[The Name on the Charge]], where he commissions it himself, against three clean chances to stop.

## Remote Apparatus

No survivor, band member, road rival, or Korrheim official enters the base-game worldspace.

- **[[Sella Etten]]** filed a civil claim years ago against *persons unknown*. It sits in a Korrheim registry and can be retrieved through [[Claims-Factor Marco Bellandi]]. Her response to Dismas's own filing is a fixed instruction, not a scene.
- **The absent cofounder** — the band's other planner, never caught — surfaces once, by letter, offering to absorb the blame conveniently or warning Dismas off the inquiry. He never arrives. Accepting his offer is the fled ending wearing a gift's face.
- Hedric Vane's and Anke Bekk's families exist in the Korrheim registry as estate entries. They are addresses, not characters, and the resolved settlement reaches them as money.

## Present Consequences

- He counts under stress, and cannot stop at two.
- He sleeps by exits and chooses ground by its lines of retreat.
- He tells accurate stories with himself removed from the crucial sentence.
- Koss cites the purchased service when assigning route work.
- The missing queen from his card deck has marked seven Grenzburg routes he never signed.
- He has never said the number of voices out loud to anyone in Grenzburg.
