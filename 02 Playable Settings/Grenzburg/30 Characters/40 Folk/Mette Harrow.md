# Mette Harrow

## Core Concept

A Lowlands carter who wants the predatory toll ruling contested **lawfully** — process, magistrate, precedent, a decision written down where the next carter can point at it.

She is the person who has priced Dismas Raben's kind of freedom and can show the receipt.

## Why

Her cousin's wagon burned in a reprisal three years ago, in a village she can name, after somebody else's clever escape. The escape worked. The people who made it are still telling the story. The village paid for it over a winter and her cousin has not driven since.

She is not naive, not a bootlicker, and not against breaking rules — she has run unlicensed loads herself when the alternative was hunger. Her position is narrower and harder to argue with: **an exit that helps one cart and costs a street is not a route, it is a bill somebody else pays.**

## Function

She appears in [[The First Mile]] and **wins the argument on screen.**

Dismas's reflex is another extraction — move the Lasks again, better this time. Mette wants the licence review contested in the Hall of Petitions with witnesses and a filed precedent, which is slow, boring, humiliating for everyone involved, and works.

The quest must let her be right. No scene reveals that the lawful route was secretly futile; no officer betrays the process to vindicate his instincts. She is correct, he is wrong, and the campaign's whole shape follows from him staying to find that out.

## Voice

Four sentences maximum in any exchange. She talks in loads, distances, and what a thing costs to do twice.

> **Mette:** Your way, they move. My way, the next one doesn't have to.
>
> *(beat)*
>
> **Mette:** I have watched your way. I know what the second winter looks like.

She does not moralize, does not lecture him about responsibility, and does not stay to see whether he agrees. She has a cart to load.

## Later

If the licence review is contested and won, she carries the precedent — literally, a copy folded in her coat — and cites it at two other checkpoints during spring. If Dismas built the guide network, she uses it, pays for it, and complains about the fees.

She never becomes a follower and never joins anything.
