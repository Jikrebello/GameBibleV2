# The Lask Household

Debtor family, Old Market stall-holders, and the people who inherit the aftermath of [[Dismas Raben|Dismas's]] best night.

They are his local faces — the [[Dismas Rewrite Spec]]'s staging of the apology scene that has been sitting finished in his banter file since the character was written.

## The Household

- **Anke Lask**, thirty-eight, holds the stall licence and does the talking. Practical, tired, not remotely sentimental about being rescued.
- **Torvald Lask**, her husband, forty-one, mends kettles and does not talk to officials on principle.
- **Bekka**, nine. Wants the card trick. Gets the card trick. This is the household's only unguarded warmth and it is aimed at Dismas from the first scene to the last.
- **Girel**, Anke's brother, twenty-two, sleeps in the stall's back room and is the one the Watch keeps stopping.

## What Happens To Them

**Autumn — [[The Decorative Fence]].** A technically valid, materially predatory seizure ruling is about to take their goods. Dismas gets the family and the stock out before the writ lands, without forging a document or hurting anyone, and it *works completely*. It is the best night he has had in years and the player has a wonderful time.

A neighbour watches them go, from a window, and says nothing.

**Winter — [[The First Mile]].** The reprisal arrives at their door and not his: a Watch inquiry, the stall licence pulled pending review, Girel stopped three times in a week, and a neighbour who has decided the Lasks are the reason officials are on the street. Nobody was hurt. Everything got worse.

**The assigned task.** When Dismas comes back to repair it, they do not want another extraction. They assign him something ongoing and unglamorous — standing surety visits at the licence review, a monthly carry of stock they cannot move themselves, and being the name on a form for a family that cannot use its own. He accepts it without bargaining, which is the whole point of the scene.

Whether he keeps the appointments through spring is `reprisal_family_state`, and it is visible in ambient play rather than a quest log.

## Voice Rules

**Never eloquent about power.** Their anger is small-scale, specific, and about consequences a person can hold: the licence, the neighbour, Girel getting stopped, Bekka's place in the school line.

- **Anke** talks in what things cost and how long they take. She never once says the word *freedom*.
- **Torvald** says almost nothing and repairs things while other people argue.
- **Girel** is furious and nineteen kinds of unfair about it, including toward Dismas, including when he is right.
- **Bekka** is delighted by him and does not understand any of it, and her delight is the thing that makes the scenes hurt.

No Lask ever analyses Dismas, diagnoses his flight, or explains the aftermath to him. They tell him what happened and what they need, and the rest is his problem.

## Summer

On a resolved path, the licence is restored, the stall is open, Girel has a carrying job with the guide network, and Bekka has learned the card trick badly enough to perform it at people.

They are established, unremarkable, and still there. Nobody thanks him at length.
