### **Magister Leviticus of Sestaren, The Kämmerer of Grenzburg**

**Role in the Rule of Three:**
He is the Architect. Not a warrior, not a priest—**the civil servant trying to build a state out of a fortified robbery.** He represents the "Good" within the Ugly world of finance: the belief that order, law, and balanced books are the only foundation for lasting survival. His internal war is against corruption, chaos, and the cold logic of the ledger.

**Core Concept:** An outsider from the sophisticated Sestaren Concord, where money is a science. He was sent to this frontier backwater as a promising bureaucrat, only to find a place where the "treasury" is a locked chest in a damp cellar and taxes are paid in squirrel pelts. He is appalled, fascinated, and driven by a reformer's zeal to drag Grenzburg into the light of proper statecraft.

**He survives by making himself the indispensable calculator in a world of brutes and believers.**

---

## Public Face

- The dry, precise, slightly impatient foreign expert. Always has a wax tablet and stylus to hand.
- Speaks in the language of efficiency, equity, and long-term yield. "Sentiment is a luxury. Sustainability is a necessity."
- Presents himself as the Duke's dispassionate tool, a human abacus. His power is subtle, woven into every contract, quota, and ration ticket.

---

## Private Truth

- He is a **closet idealist**. He truly believes that if he can just get the systems right—fair tax codes, transparent ledgers, a stable currency—he can save Grenzburg from itself and prove the superiority of rational governance over feudal madness.
- He is secretly horrified by the human cost of his numbers. The "efficiency" of letting a hamlet starve to preserve city rations haunts his sleep.
- He fears two things equally: failure (being recalled to Sestaren in disgrace) and success (becoming so vital that the Duke makes him a permanent prisoner of this freezing, violent outpost).

---

## Goals

### Short-term (This Fiscal Year)

1. Complete the first-ever accurate census of winter-resident population.
2. Implement a standardized "Duke's Measure" for grain and lumber to combat guild fraud.
3. Recover at least 30% of the embezzled funds he knows are being stolen.
4. Keep the Medori Bank's interest payments from consuming more than 50% of annual ore revenue.
5. Formalize the artifact tax (the "Tithe of Ruin") before the Church claims it all as holy property.

### Long-term (The Leviticus Reforms)

1. Establish a Grenzburg Mint and issue a local coinage to break dependence on foreign specie.
2. Create a public granary system independent of the guilds' price-fixing.
3. Draft a *Codex Grenzburgensis*—a unified set of commercial and property laws.
4. Be remembered not as a chamberlain, but as the **founder of Grenzburg's civil service**.

---

## Resources

- **The Ledger:** His true weapon. He knows where every barrel of salt fish, every iron ingot, and every silver mark is *supposed* to be.
- **Licensing Authority:** Controls the permits for trade, mining claims, and market stalls.
- **The Assessment Rolls:** The lists of who owns what, and who owes what. More feared than the bailiff's club.
- **Sestaren Methods:** Double-entry bookkeeping, archival indexing, contract law. To Grenzburg, this is arcane magic.
- **The Duke's Ear (on matters of coin):** Alaric may not like his answers, but he cannot ignore his numbers.

---

## Constraints and Vulnerabilities

- **The Violence Gap:** He has no soldiers. His authority rests entirely on the Duke's willingness to enforce his pronouncements.
- **Cultural Alienation:** The marchers see him as a fussy foreigner who doesn't understand "how things are done."
- **The Bank's Shadow:** Clerk Vesta is a constant, silent reminder that his treasury is not truly his.
- **Information Lag:** It takes months for reports from the hamlets to reach him, by which time they are often obsolete or falsified.
- **The Corruption Quagmire:** He is trying to drain a swamp while standing in it. Every under-clerk and bailiff expects their "bit."

---

## Taboo Lines (He will resign before crossing these)

- **Forgiving the Duke's personal debts** to the Bank or a rival lord (it would destroy sovereign credit).
- **Formalizing serfdom or debt-slavery** to balance the books.
- **Allowing the Church to audit the secular treasury.**
- **Falsifying a public ledger entry.** The books must be *true*, even if the truth is catastrophic.
- **Using treasury funds for private vengeance or gain.**

---

## Relationship with his Subordinates

### With Treasurer Baldric (The Bad)
A relationship of **cold war**. Leviticus knows Baldric is stealing. Baldric knows Leviticus knows. But Baldric is well-connected to the guilds and has dirt on half the minor nobility.
- **He Uses Baldric For:** Access to the informal, "grey" economy. Baldric knows where the real money flows. Leviticus tolerates him to monitor the corruption he cannot yet root out.
- **He Fears Baldric Will:** Trigger a liquidity crisis at a critical moment (e.g., just before the winter payroll) to force Leviticus's hand or discredit him.
- **The Tension:** Leviticus represents the rule of law. Baldric represents the rule of the bribe. They are locked in a silent duel for the soul of Grenzburg's economy.

### With Clerk Vesta (The Ugly)
A relationship of **respectful mutual surveillance**. She is the most competent person in the treasury. He admires her skill and despises her master.
- **He Uses Vesta For:** Her impeccable, unforgeable records. She is the one person who can reliably catch Baldric's manipulations. He also uses her as a direct, if glacial, channel to the Medori Factor.
- **He Fears Vesta Will:** Invoke a "debt clause" at the Bank's command, legally seizing a key asset without his or the Duke's consent.
- **The Tension:** He is trying to build a state treasury. She is ensuring the state remains a profitable subsidiary of the Bank. They need each other to function, but their end goals are diametrically opposed.

## Seasonal Behavior

### Summer (The Audit)
- Travels to hamlets and camps for on-site assessments. Hates it. Gets muddy.
- Negotiates annual contracts with guilds and the Bank, armed with fresh data.
- Attempts infrastructure projects (repairing the tax warehouse).

### Autumn (The Squeeze)
- His most powerful and hated season. Oversees the collection of the "Harvest Tithe."
- Decides who gets tax relief and who gets the bailiffs. Makes enemies by the dozen.
- Becomes the most visible and despised man in Grenzburg.

### Winter (The Accountant)
- Turns inward. Analyzes the year's data, plans reforms, builds his case for spring.
- Manages the intricate dance of the winter ration economy with icy precision.
- Receives the forged neutral-escrow order and a threat to terminate imported grain credit if the Heart-Stone remains in ducal custody.

### Spring (The Reckoning)
- Presents the Duke with the brutal truth of the previous year's finances.
- Issues writs for back taxes and begins prosecutions for major fraud (carefully chosen).
- Lobbies fiercely for his reform agenda while political capital is fresh.

## Relationship to the Players (Seconded Blackjacks)

He views them as volatile Bank assets carrying a valid ducal warrant. Their divided authority frightens him and gives him the only investigators capable of checking both sets of books.
- **If they are discreet:** He may hire them for deniable audits—to physically verify the contents of a remote mine or lumber cache a guild master claims is empty.
- **If they are destructive:** He sees them as a liability that destabilizes the economic predictability he craves.
- **What he might offer them:** **Debit Notes.** Officiously stamped pieces of parchment that can be used to pay certain taxes or fees. To a Blackjack, it's a way to legally launder treasure or offset their own debts. To Leviticus, it's a way to draw them into the formal economy and gather data on their activities.

---

## His "Ugly" Signature Move

**He will use economic shock therapy to force reform.** Example: He will deliberately withhold the Duke's copper-coin payment to the lumberjacks, forcing a switch to a silver-based payroll. This breaks the guild's stranglehold on credit (as they deal in copper) and causes short-term panic, but in his mind, it modernizes the labor market. He sacrifices immediate stability for systemic change.

## Canonical Winter Betrayal

The Silent Partners coerce Leviticus through the forged escrow interpretation recorded in the [[Crisis Charter of Secondment]]. He authorizes the Heart-Stone's transfer through Katarina's service tunnel rather than falsify the books or accept immediate grain foreclosure. Pale-Skins seize the transfer. In spring, the player can prove the order fraudulent and redeem Leviticus through public testimony; this is the canonical Duke's Path result.

---

## What Makes Him Sympathetic

- He is fighting a lonely, Sisyphean battle against entropy and greed.
- He genuinely wants to build a system that is fair and functional for the common marcher, not just the powerful.
- He carries the immense, invisible weight of knowing that if his numbers are wrong, people die.

---

## Failure Modes

1.  **The Ledger Eats Him:** Baldric's embezzlement is revealed to be so vast that Leviticus's oversight is deemed criminal negligence. He is made the scapegoat.
2.  **The Bank Assimilates Him:** Anya is promoted over him. The treasury becomes a mere branch office of the Medori Bank, and he becomes a glorified clerk in his own department.
3.  **The Reforms Breed Revolt:** His new tax code or currency sparks a guild-led uprising. The Duke sacrifices him to placate the mob.
4.  **He Becomes the Corruptor:** In exhaustion, he decides to work *with* Baldric, rationalizing that controlled corruption is better than chaotic corruption. He becomes the very thing he was sent to fix.
