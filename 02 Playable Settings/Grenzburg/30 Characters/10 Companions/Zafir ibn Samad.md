# Zafir ibn Samad

## Companion Contract

An Iqashari jurist who designed fixed-term true-name restraints on living debtors, called them humane, and was later bound by an inversion written by his legally enslaved copyist. Zafir ibn Samad is an outsider debtor and one of Grenzburg's eight full companions. He can join the field party of the player plus three companions and remains optional to critical-path completion.

## Debt and Culpability

Zafir enforced living-person restraint with term limits and petitions, arguing that bounded ownership was mercy. His copyist Samira bint Nahal learned the notation, inverted his cord, and escaped. His law house paid claims, voided affected instruments, and called his professional surety. Medori bought the liability and court-appointed custody of the inverted cord.

His contracts placed persons under command. Limits and petitions reduced some harms but did not make ownership just. Samira's inversion is culpable coercion of him without retroactively making his system humane.

## Person Before Problem

An elegant disputant who enjoys exact language, good tea, and being corrected by someone who has done the reading. He is generous with legal technique and slow to recognize how a beautiful procedure can preserve an ugly power.

## Combat and World Use

He begins as a constrained ring-and-cord specialist. Quest three unlocks Binder: directing non-conscious bound forms and freely covenanted beings. Human souls and coerced persons are prohibited without exception.

Zafir ibn Samad contributes alternate methods, observations, and late interventions. He is never a mandatory key for traversal, investigation, or the main ending.

## Recruitment

Recruitment opens in **Early Autumn**. A declined invitation remains repairable until the end of Open Autumn. Recruitment establishes the debt and immediate field role without unloading the entire foreign history in one conversation.

## Outcome Range

**Resolved:** He cuts every living-person instrument he can reach, teaches ethical Binder practice, and returns to Iqashar to face clients and help void surviving restraints.

**Compromised:** He prohibits permanent ownership but preserves fixed emergency restraint under courts. His reform remains vulnerable to the same logic he once defended.

**Broken or departed:** He regains mastery of Samira's inversion and uses it to command her or others. After warning and repair he may leave to build a new law house around supposedly perfect terms.

Death is possible only through an explicit, late, telegraphed sacrifice after the player is shown a viable alternative. It is never an unseen punishment for low approval.

## Approval and Confrontation

Zafir opens through precise promises, free consent, lawful protection that serves persons, and admitting when form conceals domination. He strains at enslavement, coercive shortcuts, anti-Covenant caricature, or treating all law as fraud.

Approval is shown as **Guarded**, **Open**, **Trusted**, or **Strained**, plus named unresolved conflicts. Permanent departure requires a warned confrontation and one clear repair opportunity.

## Relationship

The relationship theme is **freedom without ownership**. A female player may pursue romance; a same-sex player may pursue a sworn bond. Commitment becomes available only after quest three, is explicit, and does not follow automatically from approval. Romance grants **Unowned Accord**, allowing one freely chosen coordinated action to ignore ordinary command pressure while both partners resist control. Sworn bond grants **Witnessed Freedom**, letting either oath-partner break one control effect on the other and extend that release to a freely allied bound form.

Romance and sworn bond have distinct scenes and promises but equivalent power. A playthrough may contain one committed romance and one committed sworn bond.

## Principal Foil

[[Elara Velescar]] challenges Zafir ibn Samad's central habit without becoming a constant antagonist. Their disagreements change after personal quests and include warmth, shared competence, and occasions when each is plainly right.
