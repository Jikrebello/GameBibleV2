# Dismas Raben

## Immediate Read

Dismas knows which fence is decorative, which guard is bored, and which road law was written because one rich man lost a wagon twenty years ago.

He is lean, weathered, quick to grin at the wrong official, and very good at making departure feel like courage. He opens cages, bypasses checkpoints, and gives frightened people the route nobody in authority was willing to name.

The trouble begins after the gate is open and somebody must decide who returns for the people still coming through.

## Character Thesis

Dismas establishes worth through refusal. He wants to remain unowned and to act where institutions have made permission morally useless.

At his best, he recognizes fraudulent authority before respectable people do and risks himself to create an exit for somebody with none. At his worst, he treats every obligation as a future chain, every structure as corruption in waiting, and every aftermath as the concern of people who chose to stay.

His resolved arc does not domesticate him into obedient marcher service. He learns to make a chosen commitment, retain the right to criticize it, and return tomorrow after the exhilaration of refusal has passed.

## Creed

Dismas is a Rebel with a Goofball's tongue — the Lovable Rogue, defiance made charming, whose named conflict is **seriousness versus evasion** and whose shadow is **charming nihilism.**

The blend's payload is his precise failure surface: **the joke deployed at the moment gravity was owed.** He is never funnier than in the four seconds before he leaves, and the charm is not a mask over the flight — it *is* the flight, beginning early. His broken outcome is not "renewed fugitive"; it is a very likeable man who has decided nothing is worth staying for and can make that sound like wisdom.

## Shape

**The door stays open, and he keeps not leaving.**

Every act ends with a clean exit visibly, genuinely available and untaken. Nothing in the campaign contains him — no oath, no rank, no cell, no good commander — because a contained Rebel proves nothing. His resolved ending is **uncontained return:** commitment as repetition, chosen again on a morning when nobody could have made him.

The gesture-spine is **counting.**

- He counted voices at the coach, and ran. ([[The Korrheim Rockfall]].)
- He counts chimneys above a hamlet that exists because of him, alone, every year. ([[No Railing]].)
- He makes the count of the dead, out loud, once. ([[The Name on the Charge]].)
- He counts the network's living in spring. ([[Tuesday]].)

The campaign is one man learning to finish a count.

## Identity

- **Age:** thirty-four in 1360 AR.
- **Origin:** Korrheim road country.
- **Former work:** scout, guide, smuggler, and highway robber.
- **Current standing:** purchased-service debtor and Blackjack route specialist.
- **Public style:** irreverent pathfinder, charming dissenter, and man with an exit.
- **Private contradiction:** he despises ownership while repeatedly leaving other people to carry the cost of his freedom.
- **Present ambition:** to establish a free guide network that protects movement without becoming another toll authority or personality cult.

## Appearance

Dismas is rangy rather than broad, with dark brown hair cut by whoever has the knife, grey eyes, weathered skin, and an old break across the bridge of his nose.

He wears:

- a patched road coat with several concealed inner pockets;
- light armour chosen for quiet climbing and long travel;
- gloves with two fingertips removed;
- a narrow scarf used for dust, disguise, splinting, and card tricks;
- route chalk, compact traps, wire, climbing hooks, and a folding spyglass;
- no visible badge unless circumstances make a badge useful.

His final vocation package remains reserved for the Ilyana-Dismas paired retrofit. His current Hunter designation records field function, not a locked combat solution.

## Korrheim Roads

Dismas learned roadcraft where tolls, confessional boundaries, military patrols, smugglers, pilgrims, and displaced families all claimed different versions of lawful passage.

He began as a scout and guide. He became useful to people avoiding predatory tolls and arbitrary seizures. He also discovered that righteous language improved a robbery's recruitment and his own opinion of himself.

He was not a folk hero unjustly accused. Some people he helped were vulnerable. Some targets were corrupt. He still chose armed robbery.

## The Rockfall

The controlling account is [[The Korrheim Rockfall]]. In brief: he triggered a prepared fall before confirming the target, and the coach beneath the stone was a civilian post-coach.

**Two people died** — the driver Hedric Vane, and a nineteen-year-old passenger named Anke Bekk, who did not die at once. **[[Sella Etten]] survived**, dug out by the soldiers whose approach made him run. He heard people alive under the stone. He counted three voices. He left.

He was convicted of armed robbery and road destruction because the full human cost was never established, and he did not correct the record. He chose purchased service over imprisonment; Medori acquired it.

**Whether staying would have saved Anke Bekk cannot be established, in any playthrough.** The stone shifted twice, the soldiers came within the hour, and the record supports both readings and settles neither. He does not get the counterfactual, and neither does the player.

No survivor, band member, road rival, or Korrheim official physically arrives in Grenzburg. Nobody is looking for him and no warrant exists for the deaths. The truth reaches the record only if he pays a Fiorenzian claims-factor to go and find it — see [[The Name on the Charge]].

## Culpability

Dismas's controlling excuse is:

> “A road cannot own the person who survives it.”

Survival did not require robbery. Flight did not require abandoning people he had buried.

His trauma is a consequence, not a supernatural haunting or an acquittal. His debt contract is coercive, but being coerced now does not make every former refusal virtuous.

## Appetite

Dismas loves:

- a locked gate with an architectural mistake;
- forbidden views, false walls, rooflines, and roads officials call impossible;
- cards played for information rather than money;
- embarrassing pomp punctured by one accurate sentence;
- a horse that dislikes uniforms;
- maps annotated by travelers instead of surveyors;
- helping a person leave before the person in charge notices;
- returning anonymously to see whether an escape route became a life.

He dislikes checkpoints designed as humiliation, hereditary entitlement to movement, uniforms worn as personality, heroic last stands ordered by people with horses, and revolutionaries who cannot cook.

## Humor and Embarrassment

Dismas's humor is sideways and conspiratorial. He gives authority nicknames and tells stories that always omit the moment he was brave.

He is embarrassed by:

- being thanked publicly;
- returning after announcing he would not;
- caring whether a group kept a place for him;
- losing a route contest to someone who followed the rules;
- being unable to leave because somebody trusts him;
- discovering that a lawful process worked without humiliating anyone.

Embarrassment makes him inspect a window.

## Care

Dismas shows care by:

- revealing the unguarded exit;
- falsifying the right record for the right vulnerable person;
- taking the pursuit away from somebody slower;
- leaving tools, money, or information without a signature;
- refusing an order that makes another person disposable;
- returning when nobody can compel him.

His care becomes irresponsible when he gives freedom without supplies, warning, consent, or any plan for the second night.

## Fear, Shadow, and Preserving Change

Dismas fears ownership and the moment a useful role becomes an identity other people may enforce.

His shadow appears when he:

- leaves before a promise can be requested;
- treats maintenance as obedience;
- provokes authority without protecting those who cannot escape;
- assumes anybody who remains inside an institution has surrendered;
- turns guilt into another reason to disappear;
- makes being uncatchable the only proof that he is free.

His resolved self remains an outlaw-minded critic and independent road specialist. He learns that a chosen duty may be renewed rather than imposed, and that staying for the aftermath can be an act of sovereignty.

## Voice

Dismas uses sideways honesty, practical warnings, nicknames for authority, and abrupt changes of subject when a room approaches ownership.

He says:

- “That gate is lawful. The hinge is less devout.”
- “I didn't say it was safe. I said the guards don't like it.”
- “Captain Polished-Buckle has discovered weather.”
- “I'll be there tomorrow. Do not make a ceremony of it.”

Under containment he becomes charming, then absent. Under real shame he tells an accurate story with himself missing from the crucial sentence.

His voice obeys [[Companion Dialogue and Scene Standard]].

## Present-Day Grenzburg

### Low Lanterns

[[Mara Senn]] values his route knowledge and distrusts his romance with unstructured escape. She requires named handoffs, supplies, and return signals before calling a smuggling route protection.

### Marcher Authority

[[Sergeant Liese of the Marcher Vanguard|Liese]] respects useful dissent stated before the operation fails. Dismas expects every uniform to become ownership; Liese expects every criticism to include responsibility for the alternative.

### Blackjack Yard

[[Quartermaster Brina Kest|Brina Kest]] refuses to let him disappear from rosters while preserving the distinction between attendance and ownership. Dismas quietly fixes the yard's worst exit routes and complains whenever anyone records that he did.

### Road People

Carters, ferrymen, refugees, peddlers, and checkpoint laborers become his real Grenzburg constituency. They are not grateful scenery. Some want reliable law more than another clever smuggler — and one of them, [[Mette Harrow]], has priced his kind of freedom and can name the village that paid for it.

### The Lasks

[[The Lask Household]] are the family he gets out of a predatory seizure in autumn, and the family the reprisal lands on in winter. Their nine-year-old wants the card trick. They assign him something ongoing and unglamorous, and whether he keeps the appointments is visible all spring.

## Company Routine

At camp Dismas:

- chooses the sleeping place with the clearest exit;
- improves other people's knots while denying it;
- deals cards from a deck with one missing queen;
- leaves during formal speeches and returns with useful information;
- marks unofficial routes on the underside of common maps;
- cooks surprisingly well when nobody calls it duty;
- occasionally sits through the entire watch after another person falls asleep.

He may be recruited and dismissed normally. He reacts badly to being ordered into the party “for his own good” and warmly to being asked because his particular judgment is wanted.

## Combat and Field Use

Dismas is a [[Hunter]] on the **Quarry-Wise** doctrine, and the name is the inversion: Ylva hunts, and Dismas is the escaped quarry who learned the hunter's habits from the wrong side. Prey-minded, exit-first, counter-tracking. He does not close on a target; he collapses a pursuer's options.

Full package in [[Dismas Quarry-Wise Package]]. His field functions:

- reconnaissance;
- reversible traps;
- pursuit disruption;
- weak-route identification;
- counter-scouting;
- rapid withdrawal.

He remains doctrine-distinct from [[Ylva Winter-Track|Ylva's]] Rescue Scout: she moves toward the endangered, he removes the pursuer's choices. No critical route depends on his package.

## Approval and Moral Limits

Dismas approves of:

- refusing predatory authority;
- creating exits for captives and civilians;
- returning for consequences;
- breaking rules with a named beneficiary and a viable aftermath;
- exposing hypocrisy;
- commitments entered and renewed freely.

He opposes:

- slavery and coercive ownership;
- collective punishment;
- orders sacrificing civilians for institutional convenience;
- romantic martyrdom imposed by leaders;
- prisons used to make poverty invisible;
- rebellions that leave ordinary people with the reprisals.

## Companion Campaign Direction

[[Dismas Companion Campaign]] is now the controlling authority for all eleven authored pieces. The old four-quest outline is archived.

The rebuilt line must move through:

1. a thrilling act of justified rule-breaking that makes his company desirable;
2. an escape whose practical aftermath he initially avoids;
3. the rockfall truth recovered without a past survivor arriving to prosecute him;
4. a friendship episode built around illicit pleasure, skill, and trust;
5. an urban winter operation where resistance requires maintenance;
6. public accountability he chooses rather than a courtroom plot that corners him;
7. distinct romance and sworn-bond episodes organized around return;
8. a free guide network that may continue without becoming his throne;
9. a final road he answers because nobody owns him enough to force the answer.

## Relationships

Dismas is heterosexual. A female player may pursue romance; a male player may form the exclusive sworn bond.

Romance concerns two people defining commitment as a route repeatedly chosen, not a cage or rescue. Sworn friendship requires a positive shared object or cause that remains after the authority they opposed has fallen.

Friendship alone can produce the strongest moral resolution.

## Principal Foil

[[Ilyana Veshkar]] gives freedom colour, music, and future possibility. Dismas gives it nerve and an unlocked door.

Their conflict is never simply art versus practicality. It is whether either will:

- keep the books;
- repair the route;
- return after the crowd leaves;
- accept that repetition may be chosen.

## Faction Outlook

- **Ducal/Marcher:** respects Liese and bounded service while challenging emergency powers, checkpoints, and rank without responsibility.
- **Blackjacks/Medori:** supports Brina's path toward a free company and opposes Koss's ownership of useful people.
- **Church:** values sanctuary that does not demand confession; opposes coercive jurisdiction.
- **Folk:** respects particular passage claims and armed resistance while rejecting civilian murder and total-war romanticism.
- **Underworld:** naturally favors Low Lantern protection but must learn that smugglers can own clients through routes as effectively as a magistrate can.

## Seasonal Movement

- **Autumn:** makes refusal attractive and discovers who pays for his exits.
- **Winter:** becomes indispensable inside a besieged city where leaving is not a complete political program.
- **Spring:** builds or abandons durable roads for living people during the dead march.
- **Summer:** becomes a free road protector, useful drifter, supervised scout, renewed fugitive, or explicit sacrifice according to play.

He cannot die autonomously.
