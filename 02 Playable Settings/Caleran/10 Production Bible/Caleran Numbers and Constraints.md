# Caleran Numbers and Constraints

## Population

- **Permanent population:** approximately 350,000.
- **Ordinary daily population:** 365,000-385,000 after sailors, petitioners, seasonal laborers, students, and pilgrims.
- **Major pilgrimage peak:** approximately 450,000.
- **Households:** roughly 72,000 permanent households, with large variation between noble compounds, monasteries, tenements, barracks, and hostel dormitories.

Pilgrimage surges add about 100,000 people, concentrated in Martyrs' Steps, Ember Forum, Caravan Ward, Outer Crown, Synod Close, and the Quays.

## Water

Seven historic aqueduct lines enter the metropolitan basin. Four operate at full capacity, two function seasonally or by district, and one is dry but structurally accessible. Aurin river intakes, roof cisterns, wells, and monastery reservoirs provide redundancy but cannot replace the aqueducts for long.

- A loss of one full line causes district rationing within two days.
- Two simultaneous losses close bathhouses and mills and raise public heat.
- Three threaten hospitals and pilgrim camps within a week.
- The Keepers of the Aurin maintain valves, settling pools, pressure towers, and contamination watches.

## Food and Fuel

- Approximately 60 percent of staple grain arrives by river or sea.
- About 25 percent comes from the metropolitan agricultural belt and return villages.
- The final 15 percent comes from Church estates, military reserves, and rotating civic granaries.
- Fish, oil, wine, salt, fruit, and preserved foods move primarily through Aurin Quays.
- Charcoal and firewood arrive from managed uplands; lamp oil and wax are politically sensitive pilgrimage commodities.

The city maintains six weeks of ordinary grain reserve. A pilgrimage peak reduces that margin below one month unless shipping remains uninterrupted.

## Movement

- Adjacent districts: 15-35 minutes in ordinary conditions.
- Across the inner city: 60-100 minutes.
- Quays to Outer Crown by freight cart: two to three hours.
- Peak feast congestion can double travel time near Ember Forum and Martyrs' Steps.
- Curfew, procession closures, fire, flood, or public disorder can make bridges and gates more important than physical distance.

## Security and Institutions

- The imperial prefect commands roughly 2,400 professional watch and gate personnel.
- District watches and guild patrols add approximately 3,000 part-time or locally funded personnel.
- The Church maintains its own guards around sacred sites, archives, hospitals, and relic movements.
- The city garrison numbers about 5,500 regulars, with more in nearby forts and naval facilities.
- Licensed delver companies number around 160; fewer than thirty can safely operate below the public underworks.

## Delving and Antiquities

Most excavations produce little of value after labor, shoring, assessment, and claimant shares. About one licensed dig in twenty reaches a sealed room; fewer than one in two hundred finds an intact supernatural or operational system. Illegal digs are more dangerous because they avoid survey, ventilation, burial review, and district notification.

## Design Constraints

- Caleran cannot feed itself without the Aurin and maritime trade.
- Its sacred districts cannot absorb peak pilgrims without the Outer Crown and Caravan Ward.
- The undercity cannot be completely mapped because old collapses, rerouted water, sealed law, and occupied structures constantly alter access.
- City authorities can suppress a neighborhood but cannot govern the whole city through force for long.
