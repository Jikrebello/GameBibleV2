# Caleran Authority Register

No single office governs every part of Caleran. Jurisdiction depends on the person, place, act, object, and historical charter involved.

## Controlling Authorities

| Authority | Leader | Governs | Cannot safely claim |
|---|---|---|---|
| Patriarchate and Holy Synod | [[The Patriarchate of Veridun|Patriarch Sophron]] | doctrine, clergy, sacraments, relic authentication, consecrated burial | ordinary taxation, civic law, imperial arms |
| Imperial Prefecture | [[Helena Kallistrate]] | crown property, security, state archives, fortifications, treason, military antiquities | doctrine, city privileges without hearing |
| Council of the Old City | [[Theon Vardas]] | streets, markets, civic courts, fire response, district representation | imperial succession, Church discipline |
| Court of Five Lamps | [[Kyrillos of the Five Lamps]] | relic trials, disputed miracles, sacred custody | automatic ownership of every old object |
| District councils | varied | local watch, wells, festivals, neighborhood mediation | acts beyond district charter |
| Guild and customary courts | varied | craft discipline, foreign-quarter custom, maritime practice | capital crimes or unconsenting outsiders |

## Fixed Rulings

- Veridun remains the imperial capital; Caleran is the sacred and historical capital.
- The Patriarch's title remains “of Veridun” although his seat is at Caleran.
- Crown claims cover imperial arms, strategic systems, state archives, mints, and defenses.
- Church claims cover authenticated relics, martyr sites, consecrated burials, and liturgical objects.
- Landholders own ordinary material unless a superior category is established.
- Licensed finders receive a declared share after stabilization, registration, and claimant review.
- Occupation-era contracts are not presumed valid or void merely because of origin; coercive name custody and enslavement provisions are void.
- Routed souls cannot ordinarily be summoned by civic or private rite.

## The Ordinary Path of a Dispute

Most disputes begin with a district magistrate, guild officer, parish priest, or watch captain. Matters involving competing jurisdictions go to a joint hearing. Public emergencies permit temporary seizure or closure, but permanent custody requires review.

The system is slow because the city contains genuine overlapping rights, not because every official secretly wants paperwork. Bribery and obstruction exist, but so do conscientious judges trying to avoid destroying a living district with a historically elegant ruling.

## Pressure Points

- Sophron and Helena cooperate on public safety but dispute secret excavations and military custody.
- The civic council resents Church processions and Crown closures that disrupt markets without compensation.
- Relic judges fear both false sanctity and political pressure to authenticate useful objects.
- Glass Court communities invoke lawful occupation-era protections against old-house restitution claims.
- Finders argue that institutions claim every valuable discovery while assigning every danger to delvers.
