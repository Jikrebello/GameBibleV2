# Caleran Intrigue Toolkit

The toolkit measures changed relationships and permissions. It is system-neutral and should not replace roleplay with arithmetic.

## Faction Influence, 0-4

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | unknown, irrelevant, or actively excluded |
| 1 | recognized; routine audiences and small work |
| 2 | useful; protected access and meaningful favors |
| 3 | trusted; consulted on policy and internal disputes |
| 4 | defining ally or threat; able to change leadership or doctrine |

Influence rises through service, truth, sacrifice, leverage, or shared interest. It falls through betrayal, public humiliation, conflicting loyalties, and demands that expose the faction's dependents.

## District Heat, 0-4

| Score | Meaning |
|---:|---|
| 0 | ordinary anonymity |
| 1 | noticed by local networks |
| 2 | watched; rumors and prices respond |
| 3 | organized resistance, protection, or pursuit |
| 4 | district crisis centered on the party |

Heat can be favorable or hostile. A beloved party and a hated party are both unable to move unnoticed.

## Institutional Access

- **Closed:** no lawful entry or hearing.
- **Petition:** access through sponsor, public procedure, or narrow warrant.
- **Working:** routine professional access while the purpose remains valid.
- **Trusted:** unsupervised or confidential access with personal responsibility.

Access belongs to a purpose and relationship, not a universal key. Trusted access to an archive does not grant access to its relic vault.

## Contacts

Record four facts:

- **Capability:** what the contact can actually do.
- **Obligation:** what relationship or debt supports the help.
- **Vulnerability:** what threatens the contact.
- **Loyalty:** person, community, institution, principle, or self.

A contact asked to betray their primary loyalty either refuses or changes permanently.

## Social Conflict

Every serious social conflict identifies:

1. what each side wants changed;
2. what each can concede;
3. what evidence or leverage matters;
4. which audience can enforce the outcome;
5. who bears the cost if agreement fails.

Success produces access, policy, testimony, withdrawal, public support, or a concession. It does not inflict conversational damage.

## Pursuits and Heists

Run pursuits through districts and chokepoints, changing which faction controls the next obstacle. Run heists through **access, observation, preparation, breach, extraction, and consequence**. The best heist reward is often evidence or custody rather than coin.

## Failure

Fail forward by changing the board: evidence becomes public, a rival gains custody, heat rises, a contact is exposed, a district closes, or the party must accept a worse sponsor. Failure should not erase discovered truth.
