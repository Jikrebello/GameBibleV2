# Entombed with the Court — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** A Sestaran curator hires the party to document an Ansharan satellite court before treasure hunters strip it. A rival expedition backed by a hidden Regalis patron pursues the same site. Both crews become trapped inside a funerary court that is trying to appoint them as its new household.

**Beginning:** At the Lantern Exchange, the party buys clues, meets the rival Gilded Step, and hears a blinded survivor describe the court's progressive recognition marks. Both crews then race to steal or bargain for the Processional Face that can call the hidden complex back into the Material Realm.

**Middle:** The Face reveals the court beneath the desert. The entrance seals, the crews are marked as guests and servants, and their rivalry becomes a dangerous negotiation. They must break four court offices while deciding whether to free the wax-bound household or preserve the archives.

**End:** [[Satrap Varesh]] awakens, the court begins folding back into its paradise-garden, and the survivors have one hour to escape with whatever people, evidence, or relics they chose to save. Modern Sanguine and Regalis claimants then force a final decision about ownership and truth.

**Tier:** W. Competent regional adventurers can survive through preparation and alliances; direct combat with every awakened court office should be ruinous.

**Expected length:** 5-7 substantial sessions: exchange and investigation, relic heist, desert race, two or three sessions in the court, timed escape, and claimant reckoning.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; curator Livia Serrat; survivor Hadran Pell; the rival [[The Gilded Step|Gilded Step]]; Regalis intermediary Lord Cassian Veyr; Sanguine memory-keeper Mother Serai; and the dead household of [[Satrap Varesh]].
- **What:** recover the Processional Face, reach Varesh's satellite court, survive its recognition protocol, and decide what happens to its household, archives, and blood relics.
- **Where:** the Lantern Exchange on the northern Glass road, the collector Oren Tal's villa-vault, the buried Road of Cypress, and a folded Ansharan paradise-court beneath the [[Great Glass Desert]].
- **Why the party acts:** Livia offers pay and salvage rights; one party member seeks an Ansharan truth; the crew needs the prestige of a documented first entry; or Hadran personally asks them to free the companions he left behind.
- **Why the danger exists:** Varesh refused the destruction of Anshara and built a court that could survive without living subjects. Its protocol cannot distinguish an explorer from a petitioner. Anyone who crosses the threshold is assigned a place, and anyone who remains long enough becomes household property.
- **How it escalates:** the Processional Face wakes the whole complex, both crews compete for four office-seals, throne-wax begins rewriting marked intruders, and breaking the final seal starts the court's return to its folded prison.

## Canon and Moral Ruling

Anshara was not a lost golden age. Its beauty rested on Giant supremacy, human servitude, primordial vampirism, and the claim that the ruler's will defined the proper place of every soul beneath him.

The wax-bound servants are victims even when they defend the court. Their memory is valuable, but preserving a complete record does not justify leaving them enslaved. The Regalis claim is an attempt to inherit the Sovereign's hierarchy. The Sanguine claim preserves truths that mortals erased, but also restores rites that sustain vampiric power.

The strongest resolution frees the household, preserves selected testimony, and denies every modern claimant possession of Varesh's blood engine.

## The Central Truth

Varesh governed a remote Ansharan plateau and its desert approaches on behalf of the Court of Courts. He was not one of Anshara's supreme rulers. He was a satellite satrap whose wealth came from water control, caravan tribute, and the breeding of human bloodlines for service.

When Anshara began to consume itself, Varesh did not flee. He sealed his palace-tomb inside a cultivated fold of the Faewild: a paradise-garden stripped of freedom and reduced to perfect repetition. He divided his authority among four offices so the court could continue even while he slept:

1. **The First Radiance** governs appearance, audience, and acceptable identity.
2. **The Keeper of Measures** governs tribute, proportion, and the accounting of bodies.
3. **The Garden Marshal** governs water, beasts, and violence beyond the throne room.
4. **The Closed Hand** governs punishment, secrecy, and the sealing of disobedient servants.

Each office carries an anchor-seal. Together they sustain Varesh and the folded court. The Processional Face is not merely a key: it supplies the missing face of the audience, causing the palace to recognize that petitioners have arrived.

Varesh's human household was embalmed alive in **throne-wax**, a blood-and-resin compound that preserves memory while subordinating will. Some have endured in fragments for millennia. The modern vampire bloodlines descend from gifts and punishments first refined in courts like this one.

## Modern Expedition Conflict

The expedition is financed through the **Meridian Antiquities Venture**, a Sestaran partnership with two concealed agendas.

- **Livia Serrat**, curator and public expedition lead, hires the party. She wants a documented recovery, a defensible museum collection, and proof that Sestaran scholarship can reach the site before private looters.
- **Lord Cassian Veyr**, a cultivated Regalis intermediary, secretly finances the Gilded Step. He wants Varesh's blood genealogy and throne-seal, which he believes can establish an inherited right over lesser vampires.
- **Mother Serai**, a Sanguine memory-keeper, does not fund either crew. She warns that every Ansharan object is part of a murdered people and demands custody of the blood archives. She tells the truth about the court's origin and lies about how harmless Sanguine custody would be.

The venture did not deliberately hire two identical teams. Its principals funded different recoveries through the same pool while hiding their ultimate beneficiaries.

## Villain Structure

- **Ancient primary antagonist — [[Satrap Varesh]]:** a Giant vampire-sovereign who regards hierarchy as mercy and wants a living court again.
- **Active system — the Court Protocol:** an intelligent ritual architecture that recognizes entrants, assigns rank, and enforces precedence without hatred or discretion.
- **Modern antagonist — Lord Cassian Veyr:** a Regalis intermediary who wants Varesh's throne-seal and blood genealogy. He operates through proxies and arrives only after the tomb opens.
- **Rival pressure — [[The Gilded Step]]:** a competent crew whose members do not all know their patron's nature and can become allies, casualties, or recurring enemies.
- **Compromised claimant — Mother Serai:** she opposes Varesh's awakening and can free some memories, but will preserve predatory blood rites if given control.

## Adventure States

- **Court Recognition (0-4):** how completely the court has assigned the party to its household. At 4, marked characters begin becoming [[Wax-Bound Courtiers]].
- **Household Freed (0-4):** how many groups of servants have had their memories separated from Varesh's commands and their souls released or stabilized.
- **Rival Trust (0-4):** whether the Gilded Step shares routes, rescues casualties, and rejects Cassian's final order.
- **Evidence Secured (0-4):** how much testimony and archaeology survives without preserving the blood engine intact.

## Act I — Faces at the Lantern Exchange

### Beat 1: The Auction

The Lantern Exchange is a temporary market assembled when caravans from the deep desert arrive. Scholars, collectors, smugglers, Sanguine agents, and Covenant factors bid under canvas lanterns while glass storms move across the horizon.

Livia gives the party a limited purse and four priorities. They cannot afford all of them:

| Lot | Immediate use | Truth it contains |
|---|---|---|
| **Cypress-road rubbing** | Shortens the desert search | Shows the court was approached in strict rank order |
| **Throne-wax reliquary** | Reveals how to soften wax without destroying memory | Contains a servant's whispered name |
| **Jade measure weight** | Disarms proportional floor traps | Proves humans were counted as household property |
| **Broken garden lens** | Reveals folded-space seams | Shows the paradise-garden repeating the same hour |

The Gilded Step bids against the party but does not sabotage the sale. Nadia approaches afterward as **Nadia Pellin**, an independent Ansharan translator who claims Livia hired her separately. She is helpful, competent, and false.

Mother Serai publicly challenges the sale of Ansharan funerary goods. She names Varesh and warns: **“His court does not kill trespassers. It promotes them.”** Most bidders take this as vampire theatre.

### Beat 2: The Survivor Behind the Veil

Livia takes the party to Hadran Pell, sole survivor of the first expedition. He keeps his eyes covered and lives in rooms without mirrors. He describes three marks that appeared on walls only after he looked directly at them:

1. **The Open Palm:** Guest.
2. **The Lowered Eye:** Servant.
3. **The Bound Reed:** Retainer.

He blinded himself when his companions began calling him by a court title. He heard them welcome a fourth mark, **the Closed Mouth**, before throne-wax covered their faces.

Hadran gives the party a route to the Road of Cypress and explains that the court remains absent without the Processional Face. He asks them to speak the names of his companions inside, because remembered personal names interrupt court titles.

### Beat 3: The Missing Face

The Processional Face was believed to be displayed in Oren Tal's private museum. Auction evidence proves the display is a replica. The real Face is hidden in his villa-vault because Tal discovered that servants who wore it began addressing empty rooms.

Nadia steals a route sketch or minor personal object, but the theft must remain discoverable. A careful party can expose her now. If confronted with respect, she admits she works for another expedition but offers a truthful temporary bargain. If humiliated or threatened, she feeds the Gilded Step a false account that makes later cooperation harder.

### Act I Output

The party understands the recognition threat, has selected useful clues, knows the Face is real, and has met three competing interpretations of the site: scholarship, vampire inheritance, and ancestral memory.

## Act II — The Race for the Processional Face

### Beat 4: Oren Tal's Villa-Vault

Tal's villa is holding a masked garden feast while creditors and collectors inspect his public rooms. The party can obtain the Face in several ways:

- **Bargain:** clear another dangerous Ansharan object from Tal's collection and promise him first publication rights.
- **Expose:** prove the replica has already harmed servants and force Livia to confront him publicly.
- **Steal:** enter through the dry cistern, bypass household guards, and extract the Face from a throne-wax case.
- **Compete:** discover the Gilded Step inside and negotiate a shared recovery or race them through the vault.

The villa contains no random death maze. Its danger comes from Tal's guards, awakened collection pieces, and the Face projecting court roles onto feast guests. Violence can kill innocent servants and turn the authorities against both crews.

### Beat 5: Fifty Miles of Glass

Whoever secures the Face gains a lead but not exclusive access. Both crews know the Road of Cypress. Run the journey as three pressures rather than a sequence of wandering monsters:

1. **Glass storm:** shelter together or lose mounts and water.
2. **False garden:** a folded mirage offers shade, food, and voices from the court; entering raises Court Recognition.
3. **Collapsed causeway:** one crew can cross quickly by destroying the route or preserve it for the people behind them.

Helping rivals raises Rival Trust. Abandoning them gains time but lets Cassian's surviving agents enter later with no loyalty to anyone.

### Beat 6: Calling the Court

At the buried processional gate, the Face must be worn while four petitioners stand in the proper order. The party can use its own members, cooperate with the Gilded Step, or place statues and gear as false petitioners if it understood the auction clues.

The desert opens like a curtain around a cypress-lined road. Beyond it stands Varesh's palace beneath a fixed amber sunset. As soon as living entrants cross the audience threshold, the gate closes and the Open Palm appears behind them.

The Face fuses to the first wearer's skin until the First Radiance seal is broken. This person becomes the court's **Herald** and receives visions pointing toward the throne.

### Act II Output

One or both crews enter the folded court. Everyone inside has at least one recognition mark, the way out has vanished, and the person wearing the Face is becoming part of the court's ceremony.

## Act III — The Court Appoints Its Household

The court is not a conventional tomb corridor. It is a palace repeating the last audience Varesh never received. Servants perform incomplete tasks, fountains run with red mineral water, and every door opens more readily for someone who accepts the rank assigned to them.

The four office precincts may be approached in any order. Breaking an anchor-seal weakens Varesh and removes one support from the recognition protocol. Each precinct offers a quick destructive solution and a slower solution that frees part of the household.

### Recognition Protocol

Characters gain marks by accepting court titles, failing precedence trials, resting in assigned quarters, or drawing directly on Varesh's blood relics.

- **1 — Guest:** doors open and courtiers offer hospitality. Refusing food is safe; insulting servants wakes guards.
- **2 — Servant:** the court assigns a task. Completing it grants access but makes the title harder to resist.
- **3 — Retainer:** throne-wax appears beneath the skin. The character can command lower courtiers but hears Varesh's voice.
- **4 — Household Dead:** the character becomes a Wax-Bound Courtier unless allies speak their personal name, break an office-seal, or use softened throne-wax to restore memory.

Marks should create temptations and access, not function only as punishment.

### Precinct 1: Hall of First Radiance

Mirrors, cosmetics, masks, and waxed petitioners preserve the identity Varesh considered suitable for each servant. **Namarra, First Radiance** wears a hundred human faces and refuses to acknowledge anyone outside their appointed role.

- **Fast solution:** shatter the face-mirror and kill Namarra's wax body.
- **Freeing solution:** recover three servants' names, make Namarra see the people beneath their roles, and separate the Processional Face from the Herald.
- **Rival intersection:** Nadia's false identity becomes physically fixed. Helping her recover her name greatly raises Rival Trust.
- **Reward:** the Radiance seal and the ability to remove one false appearance or imposed identity.

### Precinct 2: House of Measures

Every servant, ration, heartbeat, and drop of water is represented by moving jade weights. **Ilyon, Keeper of Measures** insists that no one is enslaved because every life receives an exact place and provision.

- **Fast solution:** overload the scales, causing stored tribute and bound courtiers to collapse together.
- **Freeing solution:** use the named reliquary and household records to remove people from the property columns before breaking the central balance.
- **Rival intersection:** Olek's brother appears in a vision as a debt-weight. Cassian offers by whisper to free him if Olek secures the throne-seal.
- **Reward:** the Measure seal, treasure inventories, and proof that Ansharan bloodlines were engineered by court office.

### Precinct 3: Paradise of the Garden Marshal

The court's garden repeats one sunset. Cypress trees grow from buried servants, hunting cats patrol dry canals, and a wax-bound court beast guards the irrigation heart. **Sarkesh, Garden Marshal** treats the entire garden as Varesh's domesticated body.

- **Fast solution:** rupture the irrigation heart, turning the garden to salt and killing everything rooted in it.
- **Freeing solution:** redirect its water through four neglected servant plots, waking memories long enough to release the buried people.
- **Rival intersection:** Yara can use rune magic to force the channels, but doing so exposes her to primordial blood and advances her recognition.
- **Reward:** the Garden seal, preserved seeds, and a route to the outer wall used during the escape.

### Precinct 4: The Closed Hand

This prison-archive contains people Varesh could neither trust nor allow to die. Hadran's companions are here, along with witnesses to Anshara's collapse. **Kheled the Closed Hand** has no face and speaks in the voices of prisoners.

- **Fast solution:** burn the archive and deny Varesh its stored dissent.
- **Freeing solution:** open cells one at a time, distinguish memory from bait, and let prisoners speak names the court prohibited.
- **Rival intersection:** Vasco discovers Cassian's true orders: take the throne-seal, destroy the genealogy copies, and leave all witnesses inside.
- **Reward:** the Closed Hand seal, testimony about the Court That Drank Itself, and the exit procession.

### Dynamic Rivalry

After two seals fall, both crews understand that neither can safely escape alone. Use Rival Trust to determine behavior:

- **0:** the Gilded Step takes a seal, abandons a casualty, and races for Varesh.
- **1:** it proposes a temporary division of precincts but withholds Cassian's orders.
- **2:** members exchange tools and rescue one another while still claiming separate salvage.
- **3:** Vasco reveals the Regalis patron and asks the party to help break the contract holding Olek's brother.
- **4:** the crews enter the throne room as one expedition and reject Cassian's extraction order.

### Act III Output

The party has broken between two and four office-seals, decided how many servants are worth the time to free, and learned that the modern Regalis claim depends on Varesh's throne-seal and genealogy.

## Act IV — The Last Audience

### Beat 7: Satrap Varesh Wakes

Varesh sits beneath a canopy of petrified vines. He is too large, too still, and too complete to resemble a modern vampire. The broken offices wake him because the court can no longer govern in his place.

He offers each marked explorer a perfected court role based on what they did inside. These offers grant real power and erase personal obligation. His argument is simple: every mortal society already ranks people, but his court makes rank honest, beautiful, and eternal.

Varesh can be defeated through a combination of:

- breaking all four office-seals;
- returning names to the household so his commands no longer define them;
- forcing him to drink corrupted throne-wax from his collapsing court;
- using the Processional Face to declare the audience ended;
- direct battle against his weakened body.

Killing Varesh without breaking the offices allows the court to rebuild him through a marked Herald. Breaking the offices without confronting him begins the fold but leaves him hunting the escape route.

### Beat 8: One Hour Before the Garden Closes

Varesh's defeat cracks the Processional Face and begins the court's return to its folded paradise. Run the escape as a choice among people, evidence, and treasure rather than repeated movement checks.

The survivors can carry only four major burdens unless they prepared pack routes or have high Rival Trust:

- Hadran's surviving companions and other coherent courtiers;
- the blood genealogy;
- the testimony archive;
- the throne-seal;
- the softened throne-wax formula;
- portable treasure sufficient to pay both expeditions;
- wounded members of either crew.

The Garden route is fastest if preserved. The Closed Hand procession is safest if recovered. The main audience road remains open longest but is defended by every Wax-Bound Courtier the party did not free.

Failure does not require an instant total party death. Anyone left behind becomes part of the closed court and can appear in a later rescue. The Processional Face is destroyed, so reopening the site requires a new and dangerous method.

### Beat 9: Claimants at the Glass Road

Outside, Livia, Mother Serai, and Cassian's agents converge on the survivors.

- Livia asks for a public record and accepts that dangerous relics may need destruction.
- Serai asks for the testimony and softened wax, promising to preserve every recovered name.
- Cassian demands the throne-seal under the venture's hidden salvage clause and orders the Gilded Step to enforce his claim.

If Rival Trust is 3 or higher, Vasco reveals Cassian's coercion and stands down. At 4, the Gilded Step helps destroy or hide the throne-seal. At 0-1, surviving rivals fight or steal for it unless the party can expose that Cassian planned to abandon them.

## Ending States

### Names Returned

Varesh is destroyed, the throne-seal and blood engine are broken, Household Freed is 3+, and enough testimony survives to establish what happened. The freed dead are routed or allowed a final act of witness. Livia publishes a limited account that exposes Ansharan slavery without printing usable blood rites. This is the strongest continuation.

### The Sanguine Archive

Mother Serai receives the household memories and blood records. She preserves names mortal historians would have lost and releases some trapped souls. The Sanguine also recover rites that strengthen their authority over vampire society. The past is remembered, but not disarmed.

### Regalis Restoration

Cassian obtains the throne-seal and genealogy. He cannot recreate Varesh, but can compel deference from vampires whose lines appear beneath the satrap's household. Regalis agents begin calling him **Heir of the Cypress Court**, and the expedition becomes the opening move in a vampire succession struggle.

### The Open Museum

Livia or the party removes the collection without adequately separating relics from household memory. The exhibition makes the expedition famous. Visitors begin dreaming of assigned rooms, museum attendants develop wax beneath their skin, and the court slowly reconstructs itself around public attention.

### Court Without a Satrap

Varesh falls, but one marked explorer accepts the throne or retains all four office-seals. The court closes around a new ruler. That character can preserve the trapped household temporarily, but the architecture steadily teaches them to treat people as offices rather than souls.

### Entombed

The court folds before the crews escape. The desert site becomes ordinary glass again. Hadran hears new voices in his dreams, each using the court title of a missing explorer.

## What the Adventure Changes

- The party proves that Ansharan satellite courts can survive in folded paradise-gardens.
- Modern vampire bloodlines gain, lose, or contest physical evidence of their Giant origins.
- The Sanguine and Regalis become active archaeological powers rather than background lore.
- Surviving Gilded Step members become trusted peers, bitter rivals, or coerced agents of Cassian.
- Throne-wax becomes a known method of preserving memory by enslaving identity.
- Testimony from the household can revise the public history of Anshara without making its blood rites common knowledge.
