# The Court That Drank Itself — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** A nine-person Qahuri-Jirahar expedition vanishes after finding an Ansharan ruin eight days beyond [[Qasr Ilyun]]. The party follows its lantern trail into [[Bitter Crown Basin]] and discovers the [[Palace of the Last Audience]], a death-palace that treats every intruder as a presumptuous petitioner before a court that murdered and consumed itself.

**Beginning:** [[Way-Keeper Tavira bath Selan]] hires the party after a pack camel returns with one extinguished expedition lantern and bloodless equipment. At the empty camp, the party reconstructs how the [[Six-Lantern Expedition]] found three entrances and learns that two monumental gates are lethal classifications, while the hidden servants' door is the true route.

**Middle:** Inside, the party reads the palace's court logic, survives deliberately deceptive passages, and rescues explorers scattered among waiting halls, tribute machinery, a crushing throne procession, and a rank labyrinth. A counterfeit sovereign and richly furnished false crypt offer a convincing victory designed to send capable intruders away.

**End:** The servant route beyond the false audience descends to the palace's blood distillery and [[The Five Memory Cups]]. Each vessel destroyed strips power from [[Azravan the Last Cup]], the primordial Vampire Giant who drank Anshara's final assembled court. Permanent victory requires destroying all five Cups and Azravan's body before escaping the collapsing palace.

**Tier:** 3, expert expedition, level 10-14 analog.

**Expected length:** 4-6 substantial sessions: Qasr Ilyun and the empty camp, buried forecourt and petition circuit, false audience, then the Last Cup works and escape.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player party; Tavira; the nine members of the Six-Lantern Expedition; Azravan; Wax-Bound Courtiers; Many-Handed Tribute Wardens; and the stolen memory voices inside the Five Cups.
- **What:** find the missing expedition, understand a palace built to kill entitled intruders, distinguish the false ending from the true court, destroy Azravan's continuity machinery, and return with survivors and evidence.
- **Where:** Qasr Ilyun, the abandoned Black Salt Track, Bitter Crown Basin, and four descending bands of the Palace of the Last Audience.
- **Why the party acts:** Tavira pays for a rescue; one of the missing is a colleague or relative; the breach court needs a capable neutral team; or the party seeks a lost traveler who joined the expedition under another name.
- **Why the palace exists:** Anshara built continuity palaces so hierarchy could survive catastrophe. Azravan turned this one into a trap, memory distillery, and proof that he alone embodied the dead court.
- **How it escalates:** accepted rank, blood tribute, theft, violence, and delay increase Court Attention; survivors deteriorate or are relocated; the false audience attempts to dismiss the party; and breaking the Cups wakes Azravan fully.

## Canon Boundaries

The palace, basin, Six-Lantern mission, named cast, Azravan, Five Cups, and starting disappearance are local canon. No rescue result, destroyed vessel, recovered record, or fate for Azravan is assumed elsewhere.

This adventure is independent from [[Entombed with the Court — Full Adventure]]. Both reveal Ansharan court horror, but no person, clue, relic, office-seal, patron, or outcome crosses between them. A group can play either first or only one of them.

The Palace of the Last Audience was a supreme continuity site, not Anshara's capital. It proves how one last audience died and corroborates the wider Drinking Wars. It does not reveal the Court of Courts or settle the last sovereign's fate.

## Moral and Metaphysical Ruling

Anshara's beauty, learning, and administrative power rested upon Giant supremacy, human slavery, bloodline engineering, and the claim that rank determined personhood. The palace's mechanisms are coherent because oppression can be systematic. Coherence does not make them fair or morally neutral.

Azravan chose to summon and consume the Last Court. Some of those he killed were themselves predatory nobles and blood-priests; others were guards, servants, officials, and captives. Their guilt or innocence did not give him ownership of their lives.

The Memory Cups preserve blood-borne impressions: knowledge, sensation, trained response, and partial voices. They do not contain whole souls. Recovering testimony can honor victims and expose Anshara without preserving functional blood rites or extending soul-routing machinery into the adventure.

Azravan cannot be redeemed. He understands what he did and calls it good. Bargains with him may save a person or open a route temporarily, but feeding him, accepting his succession, or carrying his continuity into the world is always an evil outcome.

## The Central Truth

During [[The Fall of Anshara]], the Grand Chamberlain controlled a palace intended to receive the imperial court during invasion, rebellion, or disaster. Its official routes classified every arrival as conqueror, tributary, petitioner, officer, or servant. Its hidden labor routes allowed mortal attendants to keep the spectacle functioning without enduring the hazards built for rivals.

Azravan invited the surviving high court to a final audience. He claimed the sovereign would return if the court maintained perfect continuity. Once the assembly entered, he sealed the palace, separated groups by rank, and used its tribute architecture to drain them.

He distilled selected memories into five Giant vessels:

- Crown for rule and precedence;
- Spear for war and punishment;
- Veil for secrecy and misdirection;
- Garden for cultivated life and bodily preservation;
- Ash for death, record, and reconstruction.

The remainder he drank. He then rebuilt the audience route as an adversarial tomb. Lesser intruders feed the palace. Skilled intruders are tested, deceived by a false sovereign, and sent away carrying marked treasure that spreads his reputation. Exceptional intruders reach the Cups, where their blood or accepted titles can provide Azravan a living heir.

The Six-Lantern Expedition entered through the servants' route and penetrated farther than most. After Qasim died at the Gate of Conquest, Bashir offered his blood at the Gate of Tribute to open a safer route and prevent another death. The palace recognized him as a tributary officer, raised Court Attention, and began separating the expedition according to its specialties.

## The Missing Expedition

See [[Six-Lantern Expedition]] for all nine members. Six begin recoverable and three are dead before the party arrives.

### Survivor Deterioration

Do not kill a recoverable explorer because of one missed check elsewhere. Each has three stages: **stable**, **endangered**, and **lost**.

- advancing Court Attention to 2 endangers one unfound survivor;
- advancing it to 3 endangers two more or relocates one already endangered survivor deeper;
- at Attention 4, each major delay or retreat can lose one endangered survivor unless the party established a protection, disabled the relevant mechanism, or gave that person an escape route;
- a direct hazard failure involving a survivor can endanger them immediately;
- found survivors remain vulnerable but do not worsen off-screen without a clearly announced palace action.

When a survivor becomes endangered, show it: a voice over the wall, fresh blood in a channel, a moving title mark, or a Warden changing route. The party must have a meaningful chance to respond.

## Adventure States

### Court Attention: 0 to 4

Begin at **0** in Bitter Crown Basin.

- **0 - Dust:** only touched mechanisms wake.
- **1 - Petitioners:** mosaics watch and rank-gates begin sorting entrants.
- **2 - Household Notice:** Courtiers mobilize; Azravan speaks through official surfaces.
- **3 - Grand Audience:** the Veil Cup shifts routes, guardians coordinate, and a survivor is moved deeper.
- **4 - Court Assembled:** Azravan projects commands throughout the palace and prepares a living heir.

Raise Attention for accepting a formal title, giving blood or valuable tribute, stealing marked regalia, destroying helpless Courtiers without necessity, or retreating for a full rest after the palace has recognized the group. Loud combat alone does not automatically raise it.

### Survivor Roster

Track Bashir, Shimara, Rahil, Mazen, Tiratha, and Korin separately. Record location, condition, imposed mark, and whether they have a return route. Qasim, Lethara, and Halim are dead and provide evidence that makes later danger more legible.

### Memory Cups: 5 to 0

Begin at **5**. Each neutralized Cup removes one palace power and one protection from Azravan. Record whether useful testimony was separated before destruction. See [[The Five Memory Cups]].

### Return Route: 0 to 3

Begin at **0**.

- gain 1 by marking and securing the Dust Door and basin approach;
- gain 1 by disabling the Throne Procession or opening its maintenance bypass;
- gain 1 by opening the blood-distillery drain route or fixing the Veil Cup's lower passages in one configuration.

At 3, survivors can evacuate along a known route during collapse. At 2, the party must abandon bulky evidence or treasure. At 1, each group needs a guide and faces one collapsing hazard. At 0, escape becomes a desperate reverse traversal and scattered survivors are likely to be separated.

## Running Lethal but Legible Hazards

Every lethal feature below has **signs**, **purpose**, and **responses**. Present the signs without requiring a roll when characters inspect the relevant surface, remains, airflow, or geometry. Checks provide interpretation, speed, or extra options.

Testing with poles, weights, light, thrown objects, rope, mirrors, expendable gear, or controlled magic should reveal behavior. A failed test costs time, equipment, position, or Attention before it costs a life. Death becomes possible when someone commits bodily after the danger is legible or chooses to rescue another person from an active mechanism.

## Act I - The Empty Lanterns

### Beat 1: The Camel at Qasr Ilyun

On day twenty-nine, a Six-Lantern pack camel reaches Qasr Ilyun alone. Its harness is intact. One brass lantern hangs cold from the saddle, and its remaining tools have been wiped clean of blood without being washed of dust. The animal refuses to face the Black Salt Track.

Tavira assembles the party at the road-company court. She presents:

- the expedition's expected twenty-four-day itinerary;
- its complete nine-name roster;
- a route log ending on day sixteen at the Bitter Wells;
- a scrap of Shimara's writing: **"The high door is for those the court wishes to notice."**

The breach court supplies ordinary closure cloths and evidence boxes but has no jurisdictional instrument that controls the ruin. Tavira prevents a second treasure expedition from following for twelve days, giving the rescue party a head start rather than a deadline imposed by magic.

### Beat 2: The Black Salt Track

The eight-day journey should establish the cost of reaching the site without becoming a separate survival campaign. Use three decisions:

- carry extra water or heavier rescue and demolition gear;
- repair the Bitter Wells seep or rely on carried stores for the return;
- follow Giant processional markers or Shimara's smaller servant cairns.

Repairing the seep creates a safe return cache. Following servant cairns reaches the camp without crossing midday glass. Neither is required, but both improve the final escape.

### Beat 3: The Abandoned Camp

The camp is orderly and unnervingly complete. Bedrolls for nine, three days of ready food, and the expedition's spare water remain. Six lanterns once marked the approach; one returned with the camel, and five remain in a line toward Crown Ridge.

Evidence available without a check:

- the expedition entered carrying one day's water and rescue rope, expecting to return quickly;
- no one was attacked in camp;
- Shimara copied three entrance symbols: spear, bowl, and unmarked hand;
- a bloodied bandage was burned rather than discarded, showing fear that the ruin responded to blood;
- tracks of all nine reach the ridge, while only the camel returned.

Securing the camp, relighting the lantern line, and marking the ridge grants **Return Route 1**.

### Beat 4: Three Doors Under the Crown

Wind has exposed three possible approaches.

#### Gate of Conquest

An immense relief shows enemies entering upright and emerging as kneeling courtiers.

**Signs:** Qasim Darun's body lies just inside with both arms braced against inward-moving walls; scrape lines converge toward a central blood gutter; loose pebbles roll inward when weight crosses the third paving band.

**Purpose:** disarm and crush armed rivals while displaying their remains as conquered petitioners.

**Responses:** withdraw before the third band; brace the walls at Qasim's marked sockets; drain the counterweight through a servant grate; enter without weapons through a narrow inspection channel. Recovering Qasim's body gives his family closure and reveals a maintenance token.

Entering carelessly activates the crush in stages. A first failure traps equipment or a limb; continued commitment or failed rescue under pressure can kill.

#### Gate of Tribute

A gilded bowl stands before a door framed by images of envoys pouring treasure and blood.

**Signs:** old coins inside are fused to the bowl; blood channels run from the rim into wrist-height marking needles; every depicted envoy leaves wearing a collar not shown on entry.

**Purpose:** classify donors as tributaries and establish palace authority over their bodies and goods.

**Responses:** offer an identified valueless object and withdraw before the marking cycle; interrupt the needles; open the receiver's service panel with Qasim's token; ignore the gate and follow its drain to the Dust Door.

Giving blood or valuable treasure opens the route but raises Attention and places a court mark. It is a usable dangerous entrance, not a required sacrifice.

#### Servants' Dust Door

Below the formal paving, a dry drain terminates at an undecorated stone panel.

**Signs:** handholds are polished by repeated mortal use; lamp soot points inward and outward; the threshold is worn at human rather than Giant stride; Shimara scratched an unadorned hand beside it.

**Purpose:** allow servants to maintain the palace without entering ceremonial hazards.

**Responses:** clear the drain, lift the counterweighted panel, or use Qasim's maintenance token. This is the safest true entrance.

### Act I Output

The party understands that visible grandeur marks danger and labor routes reveal survival. It has entered the palace, can recover Qasim, and may have established the first section of its return.

## Act II - Petition Without Welcome

### The Litany Gallery

The Dust Door opens behind a mosaic corridor depicting five ranks approaching an empty throne. A formal Ansharan litany runs above the images. Shimara has added corrections in charcoal at human eye level.

The official reading praises the crown, armed hand, hidden counsel, cultivated garden, and honored dead. The servant grammar changes each command:

- walk below the crown's gaze;
- keep the bearing hand empty;
- trust no door that reflects you whole;
- water follows the lowest root;
- ash remembers the road beneath honor.

These are clues to the Cups and major routes, not a complete answer key. Copying the corrections gives later characters a reliable way to distinguish servant inscriptions from court propaganda.

At the far end, five rank-gates open and close according to carried items, clothing, blood marks, and marching order. The undecorated lamp recess can be widened into a side passage. Choosing a titled gate raises Attention to 1 but can reach specific survivors faster.

### The Annihilation Mouth

A black oval set inside a smiling Giant face appears to be a short passage beyond the rank-gates.

**Signs:** dust and airflow stop at its surface; Lethara bath Orin's severed test line lies fused at the threshold; one side of her copper mirror is missing without heat deformation; the mosaic warning shows a mouth with no figure emerging.

**Purpose:** erase unauthorized messengers, evidence, and offerings the court refuses even to classify.

**Responses:** do not enter; use reflected light to confirm that no interior surface exists; follow the mouth's cooling channel to a maintenance bypass; disable its black-glass frame from behind. Nothing necessary lies inside it.

Crossing bodily destroys ordinary material at the boundary. This is the adventure's clearest absolute hazard and therefore its most explicitly warned.

Lethara's body remains outside the threshold. The surface took her tethered mirror and severed her outstretched arm when she tried to recover it; her companions pulled her clear, but could not stop the blood loss. Her notes identify the safest rank-gate.

### Waiting Hall: Bashir al-Rasin

Bashir sits at the lowest of seven waiting benches while a throne-wax attendant opens a vein at each bell. He remains lucid but weak. The hall interprets his original blood offering as proof that he is responsible for every petitioner.

**Signs:** lower benches hold older, less drained remains; the bell rope runs to a measuring basin; the attendant pauses whenever Bashir states someone else's personal name.

The party can break the basin, interrupt the bell, name the expedition members in turn, or remove Bashir through the servant meal hatch. Accepting the title **Bearer of Petitioners** transfers the drain to a rescuer and raises Attention; it is fast but dangerous.

Rescued Bashir maps the liquid channels and later grants an advantage against the Garden or Ash Cup. If left after Attention 2, he becomes endangered and is moved toward the distillery.

### Tribute Court: Mazen Harad

A [[Many-Handed Tribute Wardens|Many-Handed Tribute Warden]] stands above sorted expedition equipment. Mazen has driven his rune instrument into the floor to ground its blood-glass sight. Four stone hands pin him; withdrawing the instrument wakes the remaining arms.

**Signs:** the Warden moves arms in a seven-bell sequence; joints on its undecorated rear are unarmored; each arm attends a different category of object; Mazen's instrument hums before the blood-glass lenses turn.

**Purpose:** receive gifts, confiscate undeclared property, and physically sort petitioners by court role.

**Responses:** interrupt the bell order; overload it with contradictory rank signs; attack rear joints from the servant ledge; replace Mazen's ground with ash and wire; surrender expendable gear into separate receiving hands to divide its actions.

A brute-force victory is possible but risks crushing Mazen or destroying the instrument. Failure marks equipment as tribute and moves the party into separate holding niches rather than ending the adventure.

Mazen can later burn out one Memory Cup with his instrument. Doing so makes the device unusable for the rest of the adventure.

### The Throne Procession: Rahil Dun

A Giant stone throne moves around a rectangular processional circuit, filling each corridor from wall to wall. Counterweights reverse it at four audience stations. Halim Sahr lies beneath one rail. Rahil survives in a maintenance recess whose inner release broke behind him.

**Signs:** Halim's chisel marks number the intervals between passes; warm air precedes the throne by two breaths; crushed debris lies only on the raised central path; servant gutters pass beneath the rail at each station.

**Purpose:** display the sovereign through the palace while crushing anyone occupying a rank or route not cleared for procession.

**Responses:** follow Halim's timing marks; crawl through servant gutters; jam a station counterweight; board the rear maintenance platform; reverse the throne from the Tribute Warden's gesture ring.

A first timing failure costs position or equipment. Remaining on the central path after the warning air and visible movement can kill. Opening Rahil's recess or disabling the mechanism grants **Return Route 2**.

Rahil can guide unmarked survivors back to the Dust Door. Halim's chisel and remains can be recovered if the throne is stopped.

### Wax-Bound Petitioners

Several [[Wax-Bound Courtiers]] repeat audience preparation in side halls. They dress intruders, confiscate names, and escort anyone wearing a mark. Their personal names remain inscribed on hidden laundry tallies.

The party can bypass them, release individual memories, use their procession to reach the false audience, or destroy them. Needless destruction raises Attention because the palace reads it as an armed challenge. Defending oneself does not.

### Act II Output

The party has encountered the palace's most direct lethal logic, can rescue Bashir, Mazen, and Rahil, and knows that the grand route is designed to be seen rather than survived. The Throne Procession leads toward the Rank Labyrinth and apparent royal crypt.

## Act III - The False Audience

### The Rank Labyrinth: Shimara bath Selan

Five mirrored corridors repeatedly return explorers to the same audience marker while changing their apparent rank. Shimara hides in an inscription cavity and scratches corrections beneath each false honorific.

**Signs:** reflections show court clothing the viewer is not wearing; foot dust accumulates against apparently open doors; Shimara's corrections use servant verb forms; only the lowest corridor reflects feet accurately.

**Purpose:** exhaust skilled intruders, assign them a flattering identity, and direct them toward the false sovereign under a title they may accept.

**Responses:** travel by the reflection of feet rather than faces; cover mirrors; follow servant grammar; leave carried regalia behind; cross in an order that contradicts the assigned ranks. Failed navigation loops the party, raises Attention after repeated acceptance of new titles, or separates a member into a known adjacent passage.

Shimara is stable at Attention 0-2. At 3, the Veil Cup begins writing over her corrections and she becomes endangered. Rescued, she identifies the false crypt's inscription as later work and can separate testimony from the Crown or Veil Cup.

### Hall of Empty Names: Tiratha bath Emon

Tiratha shelters behind an overturned embalming table with two incoherent Wax-Bound Petitioners. The hall offers every entrant a title plaque while scraping personal names from carried records.

**Signs:** blank spaces are cleaner than surrounding stone; title plaques fit over mouths rather than doors; Tiratha has written names on skin because ink disappears from paper; Courtiers pause when addressed personally.

**Purpose:** remove identities that compete with court office before the grand audience.

**Responses:** speak and repeat personal names; carry records inside undecorated containers; break the scraping stylus; pass through the mortuary drain. Taking a plaque grants immediate passage and a powerful court mark.

Tiratha can stabilize Bashir and other survivors, release a coherent Courtier through personal witness, and help preserve non-operational testimony from a Cup.

### The Counterfeit Sovereign

The apparent throne room contains a Giant figure in imperial armor beneath a crown of black glass. It rises when approached and calls itself Anshara's final sovereign. It is a hollow wax-and-bronze effigy animated by the Crown and Spear Cups.

**Signs:** its crown is fixed backward for anyone seated on the throne; it repeats proclamations but cannot answer personal questions; footfalls sound hollow; every attack movement follows the same bell sequence as the Tribute Warden; the throne faces away from the processional road an emperor would enter upon.

**Purpose:** give dangerous intruders an enemy they can defeat and a story they can believe.

**Responses:** destroy the effigy; silence its bells; expose the hollow back through the servant stair; refuse to address it as sovereign; sever Crown or Spear signals from a maintenance panel. Defeating it opens the ceremonial crypt.

The fight should be severe but conventional compared with the palace's traps. Its accessibility is itself part of the deception.

### The False Crypt

Behind the effigy lies a richly furnished Giant crypt containing a crown, weapons, blood-glass jewelry, scroll cylinders, and a sarcophagus filled with royal ash. The treasure is real enough to satisfy experienced delvers.

**Signs of falsehood:** the ash contains several blood types and ground throne-wax; the epitaph avoids a personal sovereign name; servant drains continue downward despite the apparent end; regalia carries receiver marks showing it was presented, not buried; Shimara dates the inscription to after the Last Audience began.

The party may accept the victory and leave. Portable regalia remains valuable but carries court marks that let Azravan observe dreams, call lesser artifacts, and build a reputation through the supposed discovery of his sovereign tomb. This creates **The False Audience** ending.

Searching beyond the obvious treasure reveals a laboratory where blood was separated by office, a narrow servant stair, and a dry channel descending beneath the sarcophagus.

### The False Treasure Test

Removing marked treasure without recording it raises Attention. The first item taken causes a delayed sign rather than instant punishment: it grows warm, shows the bearer in Ansharan court dress, or speaks a stolen title. The party can return it, strip its receiver mark, document custody, or knowingly carry the risk.

No treasure is a disguised monster solely to punish curiosity. The danger is that the goods extend recognition and help Azravan construct an heir outside the palace.

### Act III Output

The party can leave with a plausible victory or recognize the false ending. It may rescue Shimara and Tiratha, has access to the lower servant stair, and knows the palace was built to preserve something beneath the official crypt.

## Act IV - The Last Cup

### The Blood Distillery: Korin bar Atara

The servant stair reaches a vertical works of stone cisterns, red glass pipes, separating basins, and maintenance bridges. Korin holds a narrow junction against Wax-Bound attendants that keep trying to carry him toward an empty decanting frame.

**Signs:** pipes carry warmth but no heartbeat; labels name offices rather than people; maintenance bridges avoid every decorated platform; one drain slopes steadily toward an exterior fault; Cup pulses travel through the system in a visible five-beat order.

**Purpose:** separate blood-memory by function and feed the Five Cups while discarding personal identity.

**Responses:** open the exterior drain; reverse selected channels; cool the red glass; use Bashir's water map; follow Korin's route to the Cup galleries. Opening and marking the drain grants **Return Route 3**.

Korin becomes endangered at Attention 3 and lost only if the party ignores a clearly announced decanting cycle at Attention 4. Rescued, he can escort survivors to the secured return or help hold the lower junction.

### The Cup Galleries

The five galleries branch from the distillery and may be tackled in any order. Each Cup is Giant-sized, but its support and channels can be reached by human maintenance routes.

#### Crown Cup - Hall of Echoing Titles

Hundreds of voices proclaim contradictory precedence from a suspended gold-red vessel.

**Signs:** each command begins only after someone answers a title; servant alcoves are acoustically dead; voices disagree when personal names are introduced.

**Responses:** refuse titles in silence; divide the composite with named testimony; shatter suspension chains from the dead alcove; use [[White Flame Doctrine|White Flame]] to separate voices before breaking the Cup.

**Result:** Azravan loses court-wide sight and command; his brow-seal becomes vulnerable.

#### Spear Cup - Arsenal of Kneeling Hands

The vessel stands inside a forest of disassembled Warden arms that assemble when weapons are raised.

**Signs:** empty-handed servant tracks remain untouched; arm joints turn toward drawn weapons before bodies; Mazen's instrument resonates with the command pulse.

**Responses:** enter unarmed and dismantle the support; redirect gesture rings; ground the command through Mazen's instrument; collapse the arm racks onto the Cup.

**Result:** Wardens cease coordinating and Azravan's armor-like skin loses its force-turning property.

#### Veil Cup - Chamber Seen Twice

The Cup appears in two identical rooms, each visible through the other. Only one holds matter; the second is a route-fold sustained by stolen spy and courtier memories.

**Signs:** breath fog appears in only one reflection; dust falls upward in the false room; servant writing uses mirrored grammar to label the decoy; carried marked regalia appears in both while personal objects do not.

**Responses:** follow breath and dust; cover the connecting blood-glass; separate contradictory memory voices; break the true support from the maintenance crawl.

**Result:** hidden routes stop changing, the false audience cannot re-form, and Azravan's true position remains visible.

#### Garden Cup - The Preserved Orchard

The vessel irrigates a lightless court garden whose roots grow through preserved bodies. The greenery is real and sustained by stolen vitality.

**Signs:** leaves warm near blood channels; roots avoid name tablets but enter title plaques; cuts in the plants close in rhythm with distant Giant breathing; the lowest irrigation channel leads back to the distillery drain.

**Responses:** drain the irrigation; name and separate the preserved bodies; salt the feeder roots; ignite the Cup only after opening the exterior vent; use Bashir's channel map to starve it without burning the whole gallery.

**Result:** Azravan cannot heal at range and his wounds stop closing rapidly.

#### Ash Cup - Crypt of the Unfinished Body

The Ash Cup rests beneath the Dry Throne beside partial Giant bodies assembled from bone dust, resin, and blood-glass.

**Signs:** each body lacks exactly the damage Azravan most recently suffered; ash flows toward the Cup against gravity; funerary voices recite procedures rather than personal memories; a servant chute can drop the vessel into a deep furnace fault.

**Responses:** separate the continuity pattern with White Flame; reverse the ash flow; destroy every partial body and then the vessel; drop it through the chute; sever it during the final battle when Azravan attempts reconstruction.

**Result:** Azravan cannot rebuild after bodily destruction.

### Timing the Cups

Destroying Cups raises no automatic Court Attention; Azravan already knows the attack once the first vessel is harmed. Instead, he changes tactics:

- at 5 Cups, he remains at the Dry Throne and speaks through the palace;
- at 4, he sends remaining Wardens and offers a captive exchange;
- at 3, he wakes physically and begins moving between lower galleries;
- at 2, he abandons court spectacle and hunts the party directly;
- at 1, he tries to carry or feed the last Cup and escape;
- at 0, he is fully vulnerable but mobile, enraged, and still a primordial Vampire Giant.

The party can confront him early, retreat through known passages, and return to Cups. At 4-5 active Cups, destroying his body is temporary and should be shown as such before the party spends an entire battle on an impossible victory.

### The Dry Throne

Azravan sits behind an empty Giant throne rather than upon it. His dry body unfolds to full height when blood reaches the floor or the third Cup falls. The throne is empty because he needs others to believe he protects succession rather than covets it.

His attacks and defenses depend upon remaining Cups. Across all phases he uses reach, physical force, blood drain, memory theft, and the architecture itself. He targets whoever accepted the highest title, then healers and anyone carrying names separated from a Cup.

Azravan offers exact, corrupt terms:

- one living captive for safe passage to the surface;
- one Cup left intact in exchange for every surviving explorer;
- recognition as regent in exchange for shutting down the palace;
- an accepted heir who may leave carrying his continuity;
- selected truth about Anshara in exchange for fresh blood.

He honors the immediate exchange because fulfilled audience strengthens his claim. The result remains evil and never becomes the strongest ending.

### Destroying the Last Cup and Body

Permanent victory requires both conditions:

1. Memory Cups reach 0;
2. Azravan's physical body is destroyed after the Ash Cup can no longer reconstruct it.

Possible contributions include:

- White Flame exposing Pride's blood-lien and preventing a final transfer;
- Mazen's warlock instrument grounding a Cup or Azravan's blood command;
- collapsing the Dry Throne into the furnace fault;
- physical destruction after Spear and Garden protections fall;
- memory separation denying Crown and Veil authority;
- turning the distillery drain into a destructive pressure release.

No one discipline is required. The party can win through preparation, combined tools, or catastrophic collapse.

### Escape from the Palace

Azravan's final death ends the authority holding the palace in ceremonial order. Floors settle, blood-glass bursts, Wax-Bound Courtiers stop or experience brief personal awareness, and the Throne Procession loses its braking sequence.

Resolve escape through Return Route:

- **3:** survivors follow the marked lower drain, procession bypass, and Dust Door; the party chooses what evidence and treasure it can carry.
- **2:** one major route fails; abandon bulky treasure, operational artifacts, or a cache of records to keep everyone together.
- **1:** the party must lead groups through one active collapse hazard and cannot preserve all wounded people, evidence, and valuables without an extraordinary solution.
- **0:** the route fragments. Characters choose whom to guide, what to leave, and whether to risk a reverse traversal for separated survivors.

Failure during escape causes separation, injury, lost evidence, or a rescue continuation before death. A character dies only after remaining inside a visibly collapsing section or taking a known mortal risk for someone else.

## Fail-Forward Results

- **The camp is misread:** the lantern line and Shimara's scrap still lead to Crown Ridge, but the party reaches it after losing time or supplies.
- **A false gate is entered:** the mechanism exposes its function in stages, costs equipment or injury, and reveals the Dust Door through its maintenance system.
- **The Litany is mistranslated:** servant wear, survivor corrections, and later repeated geometry preserve the practical clues.
- **A survivor is missed:** Court Attention announces their relocation; another survivor or maintenance record reveals the new destination.
- **The Tribute Warden wins:** it sorts and confines rather than executing everyone, creating an escape from adjacent holding niches.
- **The party accepts the false crypt:** it can leave and later notice marked regalia, recurring titles, and evidence that the palace remains active. Returning remains possible.
- **A Cup is carried rather than destroyed:** its palace power travels with it, Azravan speaks through it, and the party can still destroy it before reaching Qasr Ilyun.
- **Azravan's body falls too early:** ash and blood visibly begin reconstruction, proving the need to destroy the Cups without erasing the party's damage or discoveries.
- **The party withdraws:** survivors left behind become court captives rather than automatically dead. A second expedition faces higher Attention and altered routes.

## Ending States

### The Last Cup Broken

All five Cups and Azravan's body are destroyed. Every recovered survivor and selected non-operational testimony returns to Qasr Ilyun. The palace collapses or becomes inert. The evidence proves the Last Audience massacre and Drinking Wars without identifying Anshara's final sovereign.

### Palace Burned

The party destroys Azravan through uncontrolled fire, White Flame, warlock overload, or structural collapse. The Cups perish with him, but survivors, names, or historical records remain inside. The threat ends at a real human cost.

### The False Audience

The party defeats the counterfeit sovereign, accepts the ceremonial crypt, and leaves with marked regalia. Azravan remains beneath the palace. His objects spread dreams, false history, and invitations to a court the world now believes was discovered and defeated.

### The Court Walks

Azravan escapes through an intact Cup, accepted heir, reconstructed body, or corrupt bargain. The palace may fall behind him. A primordial Vampire Giant now enters the present world carrying enough stolen memory to attract modern bloodlines and ambitious rulers.

### Audience Unfinished

The party withdraws before waking or destroying Azravan. Rescued people and recovered evidence still matter. Remaining captives endure inside, and the palace becomes a known sealed threat or the object of a later return.

No ending is canonical.

## What the Adventure Changes

- six missing explorers return, die, remain captive, or carry marks from the Last Audience;
- Qasr Ilyun closes the Black Salt Track, conceals it, or attracts further expeditions;
- Tavira gains a truthful record, a list of unresolved dead, or a reason to organize a second rescue;
- Anshara's fall becomes better evidenced without its capital or emperor becoming known;
- dangerous blood rites are destroyed, hidden, or carried into modern hands;
- Azravan remains buried, is permanently destroyed, or becomes a present-age threat.

## GM Quick Reference

**Immediate objective:** follow the returned lantern, find the Six-Lantern Expedition, and extract the living.

**Hidden truth:** Azravan is a surviving Vampire Giant who drank the Last Court and distilled its memories into five vessels.

**Dungeon rule:** grandeur attracts danger; servant routes reveal survival.

**Primary states:** Court Attention, six-person Survivor Roster, Memory Cups 5-0, and Return Route 0-3.

**False ending:** counterfeit sovereign, ceremonial crypt, and genuinely valuable marked regalia.

**Permanent victory:** destroy every Memory Cup and Azravan's body.

**Do not introduce:** rival expeditions, Regalis patrons, Sanguine claims, office-seal collection, folded paradise-gardens, legal-contract solutions, or required continuity from another module.
