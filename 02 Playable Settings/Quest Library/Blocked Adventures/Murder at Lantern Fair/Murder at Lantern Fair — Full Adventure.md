# Murder at Lantern Fair — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** A mixed traveling fair is framed for two murders by the warehouse partnership that stands to profit from its expulsion. The party has three days to identify the killers, prevent a riot, and preserve evidence strong enough to survive public scrutiny.

**Beginning:** The party enjoys Lantern Week, meets the fair-company, and helps prevent a mundane accident on the lantern mast. The next morning, Halmenth's public weighmaster is found dead with a performer's missing rigging knife in his chest. A bonded warehouse was robbed during the same hours.

**Middle:** The party compares the body, robbery, company inventory, and witness routes. When Sister Alena Naris announces that she will mediate, the killers manufacture a pickpocketing panic and murder her inside the fair. Halmenth quarantines the company and prepares to tear down its charter poles.

**End:** A former Chainhouse tallyman becomes the intended third victim and bait for an ambush against the investigators. Saving him or reading the planted evidence leads to Warehouse Nine, where the party must secure proof and stop a simultaneous raid on the fair's portable credit chest.

**Tier:** R to W. The opening supports complete beginners; the warehouse finale assumes the party has built allies, reduced the riot clock, or advanced through its first progression step.

**Expected length:** 4-6 substantial sessions: Lantern Week, first investigation, second murder and quarantine, false lead, warehouse operation, and public reckoning.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; [[Mara Tide-Knot]] and her fair-company; performer Osric Sealsson; treasurer Tessa Marin; Bailiff Klara Wess; Sister Alena Naris; warehouse proprietors [[Hester Vallin]] and Gero Vallin; and former tallyman Niko Pell.
- **What:** determine who killed Gerhard Lenz and Alena Naris, protect the fair from collective punishment, stop the theft of its credit chest, and present an evidence chain that identifies the Vallins.
- **Where:** Halmenth on the River Celion, the Lantern Meadow outside its river gate, Lenz Bonded House, Saint Naris chapel, Rook House, and Warehouse Nine above the Chainhouse undercroft.
- **Why the party acts:** Mara hires them after seeing their competence; Klara needs investigators not tied to Halmenth's merchant factions; the party knows one of the performers; or it is present when the lantern mast fails.
- **Why the murders happen:** the fair charter permits direct trade and Sestaran settlement, bypassing the Chainhouse's storage charges, short loans, and control of dock labor. Lenz opposed an exclusive warehouse concession. Alena learned enough to make the frame collapse.
- **How it escalates:** each murder increases public fear, the Vallins convert that fear into formal quarantine, and the final riot gives them cover to seize the chest and destroy the fair's records.

## Canon and Moral Ruling

There is no hidden monster, Idol, patron, or supernatural compulsion behind these crimes. Hester and Gero Vallin knowingly murder innocent people, exploit their workers, and aim public anger at outsiders because it serves their interests.

Halmenth's townspeople are not uniformly hateful, and the Turning Lantern Company is not uniformly virtuous. The fair uses staged wonders, protects trade secrets, tolerates gambling, and moves on before every grievance is settled. None of that makes its members guilty of murder.

The case has an objective answer. Familiarity, ethnicity, itinerancy, and profit are not evidence. Physical sequence, opportunity, motive, testimony, and possession of stolen goods are evidence.

## Lantern Week in Halmenth

Lantern Week falls in Emberrise, when Halmenth relights its harbor and river beacons, blesses the first full sailing convoys, and settles winter trade. Children carry lantern jars through the streets; Saltborn crews hang keel-lamps from poles; Sestaran factors open temporary exchange tables; and highland and steppe traders arrive with pelts, honey, herbs, and carved winter work.

The Turning Lantern Company's charter grants it seven days on Lantern Meadow. Company merchants may sell directly, settle letters through their own chest, and rent temporary stalls without placing all goods in Halmenth bonded storage.

This arrangement is popular with small merchants and hated by the Chainhouse. Hester and Gero Vallin have spent years absorbing rival warehouses. A permanent exclusive concession is one council vote away. Lenz's opposition has stalled it.

## The Portable Credit Chest

The chest is not a box overflowing with coin. It contains:

- authenticated Sestaran letters of credit;
- fair-company partnership rolls;
- merchant deposits and settlement tallies;
- dies and seals used to verify paper;
- a modest reserve of silver for cash settlement.

Possession of the chest lets traveling merchants trade without Chainhouse loans. Its documents are worth more than its specie. Stolen intact, it can be used briefly to redeem deposits elsewhere. Burned, it ruins dozens of small traders who cannot prove their balances.

Mara keeps the chest secret from casual workers and initially from the party. Treasurer Tessa Marin maintains it beneath the lamp-repair wagon and moves it every night according to a schedule written only in her memory-chain.

## The Chainhouse Plan

Hester designed the frame; Gero controls labor, wagons, and violence.

1. Steal Osric Sealsson's forked rigging knife during the opening show.
2. Kill Lenz in a Chainhouse cart, stage his body near the fair route, and use his keys to rob Lenz Bonded House.
3. Spread accounts of drunken fair-workers and dangerous foreign tricks.
4. Kill Alena inside the fair after she announces mediation, making the company appear incapable of containing its own people.
5. Secure quarantine and arrange a public assault on the charter poles.
6. Kill former tallyman Niko Pell and use a forged note to lure the investigators into an ambush at Rook House.
7. During the riot, enter the fair through the lamp-oil delivery lane, seize the credit chest, and burn the records wagon.

The plan changes in response to the party. Hester is willing to abandon the murder frame if she can instead make the investigators look like hired fair-company killers.

## Opposition Structure

- **Principal architect: [[Hester Vallin]].** Publicly conciliatory, privately precise, and willing to let Gero appear the obvious brute.
- **Operational commander: Gero Vallin.** Directs carts, coerced porters, warehouse guards, and the final chest raid.
- **Institutional engine: [[The Chainhouse]].** Legitimate storage power shielding an organized criminal operation.
- **Public danger:** frightened townspeople, aggrieved merchants, opportunistic looters, and watch officers acting on incomplete evidence.
- **Compromised ally: [[Mara Tide-Knot]].** Innocent of murder but initially conceals the chest and some of the company's staged attractions.

## Investigation Rules

### No Essential Clue Is Gated

Every scene contains at least one **automatic clue** available to anyone who examines the place or speaks to the right person. Skill, magic, leverage, or excellent reasoning provides additional context, earlier access, or stronger evidence. Failure costs time or trust, not the entire mystery.

### Evidence Packets

Track **Evidence (0-6)** by conclusion, not by every object collected.

1. Osric's knife was stolen and he could not have killed Lenz.
2. Lenz was killed in a Chainhouse freight cart and moved.
3. Lenz's keys enabled a planned bonded-house robbery.
4. Alena's murder used Chainhouse materials and a manufactured crowd diversion.
5. Hester forged the Niko-to-Rook lead and prepared the investigator ambush.
6. Warehouse Nine contains the stolen goods, disguises, chest plan, and Vallin orders.

Three packets convince Klara to delay arrests. Four justify a search of Warehouse Nine. Five sustain public charges. Six breaks the Chainhouse beyond plausible denial.

## Adventure States

- **Evidence (0-6):** conclusions the party can demonstrate to neutral listeners.
- **Town Temper (0-4):** 0 is anxious peace; 4 is an active attack on the fair.
- **Company Trust (0-3):** determines access to wagons, schedules, the chest, and company testimony.
- **Chest Security (0-3):** whether the chest is hidden, movable, guarded, and backed up by duplicate settlement rolls.

## Act I — Lanterns Before Blood

### Beat 1: The Company Arrives

The Turning Lantern Company enters Halmenth in procession. Saltborn rope-workers raise keel-lamps, Sestaran sellers unfold painted stalls, Folk performers walk the high lines, and local apprentices join the parade for the price of wearing a paper crown.

Give the party time to participate:

- throw a weighted ring over a moving mast;
- walk a low practice rope with Elof;
- identify counterfeit trade tokens at Tessa's game;
- hear Osric explain how his forked rigging knives catch ropes without cutting them;
- watch Mara negotiate stall boundaries with Hester Vallin.

This establishes faces, tools, schedules, and ordinary reasons to care before anyone dies.

### Beat 2: The Falling Lantern Mast

During the evening lighting, the main lantern mast leans over the crowd. Its retaining pin has been replaced with soft metal. This was a Chainhouse attempt to embarrass the fair, not cause mass death, but a child has climbed the lower rigging.

The party can hold the mast, clear the crowd, climb for the child, or secure the lines. Their response demonstrates competence to Mara and Klara.

Automatic clues show that the failed pin came from a cargo crate rather than the fair's fittings. This clue is not yet enough to implicate the Chainhouse, and the killers proceed with the murder regardless.

### Beat 3: Gerhard Lenz Is Found

After the final show, Lenz drinks one cup with council merchants and walks toward home. At dawn, his body is found beneath the river-wall lanterns. Osric's forked rigging knife is lodged beneath his ribs.

Within the same hour, Lenz Bonded House reports one dead night watchman, missing portable collateral, and use of Lenz's master keys.

Mara asks the party to investigate for the company. Klara grants them a temporary witness badge because they handled the mast safely and are not known members of either merchant faction.

### Act I Output

The party knows the fair, has a formal but fragile mandate, and faces two linked crime scenes before the town has settled on a single story.

## Act II — The First Evidence Web

The party can examine these scenes in any order.

### Scene A: Lenz's Body and Route

**Automatic clue:** there is too little blood beneath the body for Lenz to have died there.

**Additional clues:**

- blue fish-oil grease coats the knife handle; Osric maintains his tools with pale pine rosin;
- red cargo-hemp lies beneath Lenz's cuff;
- one wheel rut near the wall has a repeating notch from an iron-capped freight wheel;
- Lenz's boots carry flour dust although his route from the fair crosses no mill or grain store;
- a witness heard a covered cart stop after the river lamps were dimmed.

The Chainhouse uses blue fish oil, red freight nets, and a notched left wheel on Cart Twelve. These facts can be learned from any dockworker, a daylight visit, or comparison with fair equipment.

### Scene B: Lenz Bonded House

**Automatic clue:** the master door and vault were opened with keys, while display cases were smashed afterward to imitate a rushed burglary.

**Additional clues:**

- only compact goods with high collateral value were selected;
- the dead watchman was struck with a square cargo maul before being stabbed;
- the intruders used a side passage known to licensed hauling companies;
- a torn Chainhouse loading chit is trapped beneath a crate skid;
- one intruder left blood after catching a hand in the vault gate.

Agnes Lenz confirms her husband opposed Hester's exclusive concession and had recently accused an unnamed warehouse of false weights.

### Scene C: The Turning Lantern Company

**Automatic clue:** Osric performed continuously between the knife's last confirmed presence and Lenz's disappearance, in view of dozens of spectators.

**Additional clues:**

- his knife case was forced with a square warehouse nail;
- Elof saw someone in a Chainhouse tally coat behind the rigging wagons;
- Tessa's settlement rolls show several merchants moved deposits away from the Chainhouse that day;
- Mara admits Hester asked to purchase the company's entire charter position before the fair opened;
- the mast pin and knife theft occurred during the same backstage access window.

Company Trust rises if the party distinguishes embarrassment from guilt and falls if it searches private wagons by force without explaining why.

### Scene D: Chainhouse Public Offices

Hester welcomes the party, offers records, and condemns the murder. The public books are clean because the incriminating work happens beneath Warehouse Nine.

**Automatic clue:** Cart Twelve is absent despite the office roll saying every freight cart remained parked overnight.

Hester claims it is under repair. Gero says it carried late fish oil. Their answers cannot both be true.

Further inquiry reveals Lenz scheduled a public weight inspection for the morning after his death. Hester frames this as routine harassment by a business rival.

### Pressure Event: Rumor Becomes Instruction

After the party completes two scenes, anonymous broadsheets appear claiming Osric killed Lenz after an old tavern dispute. The dispute is real; the alleged witness is not.

The broadsheet uses the same cheap blue-black ink as Chainhouse loading chits. Treating the rumor seriously enough to check it can produce evidence. Repeating it publicly without context raises Town Temper.

### Act II Output

The party can establish that Lenz was moved, Osric was framed, and the robbery required commercial knowledge. Before it can finish connecting those facts, Alena's intervention triggers the second murder.

## Act III — Murder Inside the Fair

### Beat 4: Alena Announces Mediation

At Saint Naris chapel, Alena states that a murdered man deserves truth and a traveling company deserves judgement based on deeds. She plans to inspect the fair, speak with Osric, and meet a confidential dock witness that afternoon.

Niko Pell slipped an unsigned message into the chapel poor-box: **“Ask who opens Nine when the river gate is shut.”** Alena records the line in her pocket notebook.

Hester's chapel informant reports the announcement. The Vallins decide Alena must die before meeting Niko.

### Beat 5: The Manufactured Panic

During the busiest evening, Chainhouse pickpockets cut several purses and immediately shout that fair thieves are working the crowd. Arguments erupt at three gates. Watch officers and company guards are pulled away from the paper-theatre lane.

The party can pursue thieves, calm a gate, protect company wagons, or recognize coordinated shouts occurring too close together. Capturing any pickpocket reveals a Chainhouse meal token and instructions to raise the alarm even if nothing valuable is taken.

### Beat 6: Alena's Body

Alena is found behind the paper-theatre scrim. She was strangled with treated lamp wick and cut after death to resemble the forked-knife wounds on Lenz.

**Automatic clue:** the small amount and direction of blood proves the cuts were made after death.

**Additional clues:**

- the wick is dyed with Chainhouse blue fish oil; fair lamps use beeswax and flax;
- square-nailed cargo boots crossed behind the theatre during the panic;
- Alena's notebook contains Niko's message and “H.V. asked Lenz to withdraw the vote”;
- three stolen paper masks are found beside dock cloaks, not performer costumes;
- the rear meadow gate was opened with a copied Chainhouse delivery token.

The Vallins wanted the body found quickly. Its purpose is not concealment but escalation.

### Beat 7: Quarantine

Klara closes Lantern Meadow until the murderer is identified. If Evidence is 3 or higher, she privately tells the party she believes the fair is being framed but lacks authority for a warehouse search. Below 3, she arrests Osric and places Mara under watch.

Mara now reveals the credit chest. She understands that the murders may be aimed at the company's trading infrastructure, not merely its reputation. Company Trust becomes 3 if the party protected the company without demanding blind loyalty.

### Pressure Event: Elof at the Charter Pole

A group of townspeople catches Elof outside the quarantine while he tries to deliver medicine to a local friend. They tie him to a charter pole and begin pulling it down, not realizing the crossbeam will crush him.

The party can expose the immediate danger, bring Klara, appeal to fairgoers who watched Elof perform, or fight without lethal force. Saving him calmly reduces Town Temper. Killing townspeople may rescue him but drives the fair toward open battle.

### Act III Output

The party has a second connected murder, a named warehouse number, and knowledge of the killers' ultimate target. The Vallins now know the investigators are close and move against Niko Pell.

## Act IV — The Witness and the False Lead

### Beat 8: Niko's Missed Meeting

Niko asks to meet beneath the old river hoist at dawn. When the party arrives, it finds signs of struggle and a trail deliberately dressed to resemble Folk caravan marks.

Niko has been taken to an unused Chainhouse weighing shed. If the party follows the physical route rather than the planted symbols, it reaches him before he dies. If delayed, he is found dead but still carries useful evidence.

**Automatic clue:** the supposed Folk trail was made with one repeated boot stamp rather than several travelers.

Niko can provide or leave:

- the location of Warehouse Nine's night door;
- a duplicate of the notched Cart Twelve maintenance tag;
- knowledge that Hester keeps a second seal cabinet below her counting room;
- the name of Armand Rook, who rejected a Chainhouse purchase offer;
- proof that Gero uses coerced porters to move untallied cargo after the river gate closes.

### Beat 9: The Forged Rook Note

The weighing shed contains an employment note apparently written by Armand Rook, inviting Niko to discuss Chainhouse crimes at Rook House. The ink is fresh, the fold predates the writing, and the paper was cut from a Chainhouse delivery roll.

Following the note is still useful. Rook House has been occupied by Vallin agents preparing to impersonate the party during an attack. Armand, his family, and servants are alive but bound upstairs so they can later provide manipulated testimony.

One agent wears a rough copy of the most visually distinctive player character's coat. Another rehearses orders using a description supplied by Hester's observers.

The party can:

- enter openly and trigger the false performance;
- approach through the river garden;
- alert Klara and surround the house;
- turn the disguises against the attackers;
- rescue the witnesses before confronting the agents.

Armand's study contains Hester's purchase offer, council minutes on the concession, and a map showing that his warehouse and the fair lane both connect to Warehouse Nine's drainage culvert.

### Fail-Forward Result

If the Vallin agents defeat or evade the party, they stage enough evidence to raise Town Temper to 4. Klara still notices contradictions if Evidence is 4 or higher, buying the party one final chance to reach Warehouse Nine before arrests begin.

### Act IV Output

The party has direct testimony or equivalent physical evidence tying the Vallins to the frame, a route into Warehouse Nine, and warning that the chest raid will occur during the charter-pole riot.

## Act V — Warehouse Nine

### Two Simultaneous Threats

At sunset, Gero's hired crowd moves toward Lantern Meadow while Hester orders the undercroft cleared. The party cannot personally cover every objective.

Allies handle one threat based on prior play:

- Klara and the watch can hold the town gate if given Evidence 4+.
- Turning Lantern guards protect the chest if Company Trust is 3.
- Armand's crews block the drainage culvert if rescued.
- Calmed townspeople refuse the paid riot if Town Temper is 1 or lower.
- Otherwise, the party must choose between entering the warehouse immediately and defending the fair first.

### Warehouse Zone 1: Public Floor

The legitimate warehouse is full of ordinary goods and frightened employees. Gero tells workers the fair has sent killers to destroy their livelihoods.

Showing specific evidence, naming coerced porters, or offering amnesty to unarmed workers prevents the public floor from becoming a needless battle. Burning or looting the goods destroys innocent merchants' property and weakens the eventual case.

### Warehouse Zone 2: Night Door and Cart Bay

Cart Twelve stands washed but not clean. Its boards retain Lenz's blood, flour dust, red hemp, and blue fish oil. Nearby are Osric's forced knife case, spare paper masks, and the missing bonded-house collateral.

This zone establishes Evidence packet 6 even if the deeper conspirators escape.

### Warehouse Zone 3: Seal Room

Hester's second cabinet contains copied delivery tokens, false council notices, Lenz's master keys, Alena's missing correspondence, and dies prepared to redeem the Turning Lantern credit letters.

Hester tries to burn the cabinet if cornered. Saving people from the fire and preserving only part of the evidence remains a success; the party need not choose documents over trapped workers.

### Warehouse Zone 4: Chainhouse Undercroft

The hidden undercroft contains stolen collateral, debtor leverage, assault equipment, and a wall plan of Lantern Meadow. Routes mark the lamp-oil lane, record wagon, and expected location of the credit chest.

Gero holds the final line with committed guards. Hester bargains, offering the party the chest's silver reserve and public credit for solving the murders if it lets the Vallins leave with the letters.

### Final Outcomes in the Warehouse

- **Capture both Vallins:** strongest legal result, especially with preserved evidence.
- **Hester escapes:** the Chainhouse still falls, but she retains forged identities and can recur as a commercial criminal.
- **Gero escapes with the chest:** the party must pursue through the drainage culvert while the fair burns above.
- **Destroy the undercroft:** ends the operation but may erase claims needed to return stolen property.
- **Accept Hester's bargain:** saves the fair publicly while transferring losses to merchants whose names the party may never know.

## Act VI — The Lantern Reckoning

Klara convenes the hearing at dawn beneath the surviving charter poles. The party presents conclusions, witnesses, and physical sequence. It does not need to narrate every clue, but must answer five questions:

1. Why could Osric not have killed Lenz?
2. Where was Lenz actually killed?
3. How did the killers enter the bonded house?
4. Why was Alena murdered inside the fair?
5. What did the Vallins stand to gain from expulsion and riot?

Evidence determines what can be proved. Town Temper and the party's conduct determine whether people are willing to hear it.

## Ending States

### The Lanterns Hold

Evidence is 5+, the chest remains secure, and Town Temper ends at 2 or lower. The Vallins are convicted or publicly exposed, stolen property is returned, and the Turning Lantern charter survives. Halmenth creates an independent weighing office and bars any single warehouse partnership from controlling fair settlement. This is the strongest continuation.

### Truth After the Fire

The party proves the murders but cannot prevent the riot from damaging Lantern Meadow. The company is exonerated and compensated from Chainhouse assets, yet refuses to return next year. Halmenth learns the truth after losing the relationship the truth should have protected.

### The Road Company's Escape

The party saves the people and chest but lacks sufficient proof against the Vallins. Mara pulls down her own charter poles and leaves before arrests. The Turning Lantern Company survives as a recurring traveling ally, while Halmenth officially records the murders as unresolved.

### The Quiet Settlement

Town leaders accept that the Vallins are guilty but suppress the full case to protect Halmenth's credit and bonded goods. The company receives money and safe passage in exchange for surrendering its charter. The murderers lose some power without facing public judgement, and the party becomes party to the compromise.

### Chainhouse Victory

The credit chest is taken or destroyed, evidence disappears, and the fair is expelled. Osric, Mara, or another company member is convicted. The Vallins gain their exclusive concession and use stolen settlement records to ruin small merchants throughout the coast.

### Hester's New Name

The Chainhouse falls but Hester escapes with selected credit letters and seal dies. Months later, a respectable settlement house opens in another Saltborn-coast port under a different proprietor whose handwriting bends the same way.

## What the Adventure Changes

- Halmenth either reforms or consolidates its warehouse economy.
- The Turning Lantern Company becomes a trusted recurring location, a wounded road community, or a fugitive fair.
- The party gains a reputation for evidence-based investigation and public crisis work.
- Small Saltborn, Folk, and Sestaran traders retain or lose access to direct fair settlement.
- Bailiff Klara Wess becomes an ally, compromised official, or casualty of merchant politics.
- The Vallins are convicted, privately protected, or transformed into recurring commercial antagonists.

## Encounter and Evidence Register

- Falling lantern mast: rescue and environmental tutorial.
- Lenz route and bonded house: paired forensic and robbery scenes.
- Manufactured pickpocketing panic: pursuit, crowd reading, or de-escalation.
- Alena's murder: second physical clue web.
- Elof at the charter pole: nonlethal public crisis.
- Niko's weighing shed: timed witness rescue with fail-forward evidence.
- Rook House: hostage rescue and attempted investigator frame.
- Warehouse Nine: social entry, evidence preservation, fire, and human opposition.

No new bestiary entry is required. Any animal, illusion, or supernatural rumor presented during Lantern Week is part of the show rather than the murder plot.
