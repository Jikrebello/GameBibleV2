# The Reed Mother

## Classification

The Reed Mother is a unique local manifestation of the **Gut-Maw** form described in [[Gluttony Masks and Feast-Beasts]]. Its dossier adds no new reusable Shadowlands family. It is a dependent Mask of Gluttony, not a full Idol, Menhir, natural plant, saint, patron, or human soul.

## Origin

During Merewick's Three Wet Years, future cult families murdered a lost grain carrier, removed his name from the event, and served his grain at a communal meal from [[The First Bowl]]. Their conscious decision to treat a person as the hidden ingredient in communal survival created a feeding architecture.

A Gluttony Mask formed around the Bowl, the body buried at [[Hollow Acre]], and the repeated feast. Later sacrifices strengthened a Mother-Root beneath the field and produced the present manifestation.

## Continuity and Agency

The Reed Mother is an `idol-pattern` with cunning and limited moral agency. It recognizes routes, assigned roles, names, and repeated behavior. It can imitate voices, defend anchors, choose the easiest victim, and offer another season of abundance.

It does not possess subjective human memory or a valid reciprocal claim. Voices within it are feeding records and imitations. Destroying it harms no trapped soul. The human Keepers remain fully responsible for choosing to maintain it.

## Feeding Architecture

The Mask consumes:

- the remaining vitality of a named sacrifice;
- future fertility stored in seed, fish-spawn, soil, and bodies;
- nourishment withheld from households excluded from the inner feast;
- the repeated social claim that murder is the price of belonging.

It concentrates those goods into immediate harvest, full nets, preserved food, and reduced illness around childbirth. Nothing is created from nothing. Every successful year makes Merewick's untreated future weaker.

## Material Intrusion

The Reed Mother follows the ordinary Idol ladder:

1. feast dreams and unusual appetite;
2. richer food that satisfies for less time;
3. the First Bowl, Mother-Root, and Feast Daughter custom;
4. a dominion overlay across Hollow Acre during the final procession;
5. a mobile Reed Child if the living seed must escape.

The sovereign Idol never enters Merewick. Claims that the Devourer lives beneath the field are cult teaching or witness error.

## Signs

- food from green-glazed bowls smells richer but hunger returns early;
- reed roots grow toward household doors rather than water;
- fish traps fill while spawning beds become empty;
- seed stores lose viability without visible rot;
- imitation voices assign listeners as guest, keeper, vessel, or course;
- bowls and knives arrange themselves around sleeping people;
- the same young person disappears after being publicly honored.

## Body and Lair

The primary body grows from the Mother-Root at Hollow Acre: a broad mass of reed, peat, inward-folding leaves, fibrous stomach chambers, and human-made serving ware absorbed into its structure. Its dry peat island, root cellar, hidden stores, and reed maze sustain the overlay.

The **Reed Child** is a compact escape body grown from the seed carried by [[Reeve Gerwin Holt]]. It seeks the First Bowl, stored food, deep fen water, or a new communal table.

## Threat Pattern

The Reed Mother controls space rather than hunting openly:

- closes reed walls and footpaths;
- restrains people in assigned places;
- swallows packs, food, and carried evidence;
- repeats loved voices from consuming routes;
- turns hidden stores into barriers or bait;
- seeds minor Feastlings from discarded portions;
- forces choices between pursuit, rescue, and preventing food loss.

It retreats through active serving routes rather than fighting to physical extinction when an anchor remains.

## Weakness and Permanent Defeat

Physical destruction of the field body is temporary unless all recurrence conditions end. Permanent local defeat requires:

1. rescue or recovery of the named victim;
2. destruction and cleansing of the First Bowl;
3. destruction of the Mother-Root and every viable fragment;
4. extraction or destruction of Gerwin's living seed;
5. ending the sacrificial procession and distributing food to excluded households.

White Flame exposes false identity and separates the seed from a human bearer. Fire destroys roots but can also burn captives and winter stores. Drainage, broad cutting, salt, lime, and ordinary labor can dismantle the physical body. No single attack replaces the whole defeat grammar.

## Materials and Remains

Mother-Root fiber retains flavor without nourishment and is dangerous to transport. Green glaze fragments reveal every meal served through the Bowl. Seed knots can demonstrate recurrence but remain viable until burned, salted, and separated from stored food.

The most valuable remains are evidence: victim tokens, distribution marks, root growth rings corresponding to sacrifice years, and Gerwin's investiture seed scar.

## Faction and Religious Response

The [[The First Bowl Keepers|First Bowl Keepers]] worship the Mask as a necessary village mother. [[The Reedbound Houses]] fear it without understanding its full ontology. [[The Open Lanterns]] oppose the disappearances through mutual aid and records.

Old-Church and Freeflame teaching both condemn the sacrifice. Church investigators classify the Reed Mother as a false local power. They may disagree over rites and jurisdiction without disagreeing that people cannot be made into food for communal prosperity.

## Regional Names

Cult records use **Reed Mother**, **Good Root**, and **First Hostess**. Frightened children say **the Bowl-Wife**. Grave-watchers who encounter evidence may use **feast-root** or **field maw**.

## Story Uses

- a surviving seed enters a district relief kitchen;
- a recovered Bowl fragment makes ordinary food increasingly unsatisfying;
- former Keepers claim a failed harvest proves the Mask was necessary;
- another village copies the public feast without knowing what its songs once opened;
- victim names reveal households whose prosperity was deliberately excluded from the public story.
