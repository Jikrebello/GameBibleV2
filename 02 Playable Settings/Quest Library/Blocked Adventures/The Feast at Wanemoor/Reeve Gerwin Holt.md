# Reeve Gerwin Holt

Gerwin Holt is Merewick's reeve, First Keeper, and principal human antagonist of [[The Feast at Wanemoor — Full Adventure]]. His household descends from the leaders who concealed Odo Renn's murder during the Three Wet Years.

## Public Office

Gerwin is an effective village administrator. He organizes flood labor, knows every channel, hosts travelers, settles small land disputes, and has kept district demands modest. These real competencies help him hide the murders and make neighbors fear that exposing him will destroy every useful institution at once.

He presents warmth after other villagers show reserve. He offers food, lodging, a guided search, and concern for [[Brother Matthias Veit]]. His prepared story is that Matthias took the poor-box silver and fled by punt.

## Belief and Choice

Gerwin believes a reeve must be willing to do what distant authorities will not. He knows the Mask consumes future fertility and that every successful feast deepens the next need. He continues because the rite preserves his office, gives him control over distribution, and lets him define murder as sacrifice rather than admit his inheritance is evil.

Hunger and isolation explain the first generation's fear. Gerwin has decades of evidence and chooses repetition anyway.

## The Living Seed

Gerwin swallowed a Reed Mother root-seed during investiture. It is an attached Idol anchor, not his soul, identity, or excuse. He remains a human being with full moral agency.

If he dies or is critically wounded while the seed remains active, it emerges as the Reed Child. If captured alive, the seed can be exposed and removed through surgery, White Flame, a boundary script, or genuine renunciation while [[The First Bowl]] breaks.

## In Confrontation

Gerwin uses authority, followers, hidden paths, sleep draughts, sickles, reed smoke, and prepared escape boats. At Hollow Acre he makes rescuers choose between him, the current victim, stores, and the Mother-Root.

He offers one final season of food if the party names another victim or leaves before first light. The offer is materially effective and morally corrupt.

Gerwin can surrender as a man, but surrender does not neutralize the seed automatically. Genuine repentance requires naming victims, releasing captives, helping expose the Bowl, accepting removal, and submitting to judgement. Fear after defeat is not the same as repentance.
