# The Reedbound Houses

The Reedbound Houses are eight Merewick households whose adult members know that outsiders, dissenters, and some Feast Daughters disappear into the final procession. They do not all know the Reed Mother's nature, the First Bowl's origin, or the full victim count.

## What They Know

Most Reedbound adults believe:

- the feast requires one hidden death during bad years;
- the victim's household must not be publicly named;
- refusing selection transfers the danger to another family;
- the First Bowl Keepers control food and can punish disobedience;
- outside authorities would close Merewick or execute everyone if told.

These beliefs combine truth, cult teaching, and self-protective exaggeration.

## What They Do

Reedbound households watch visitors, repeat departure stories, supply procession labor, hide cult movement, and pressure selected families. Some have guarded paths or restrained captives. Others have done no direct violence but kept silent for years.

Elsa Fenner's family attempts to substitute a visitor because they expect her to die otherwise. Their love is genuine and their planned kidnapping is wrong.

## Breaking the Bind

Reedbound adults are most likely to defect when:

- a selected person is protected without collective reprisal;
- hidden stores prove that immediate starvation was partly engineered;
- Gerwin's knowledge of worsening fertility becomes public;
- an outside relief route remains open;
- specific testimony is distinguished from collective accusation.

At high Village Trust, several houses refuse the longest-night curfew and help hold stores or routes. At low Trust, they may continue obeying even after the Keepers' story fails.

## Responsibility

Membership is not one crime. Courts and survivors must distinguish murder, abduction, restraint, concealment, threatened complicity, warning, sabotage, and ignorance. Nuance in judgement does not make the sacrificial system morally neutral.
