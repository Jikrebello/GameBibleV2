# The Feast at Wanemoor — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** Sister Hanne Veit asks the party to find her brother, diocesan almoner [[Brother Matthias Veit]]. His last letter reported that [[Merewick]] had a full poor-box and abundant feast stores during a failed district harvest, while several named households remained hungry. He has not returned from the fen village.

**Beginning:** Reach Merewick on the morning of [[The Reed-Crown Feast]]. An escaped child's blood-kite tests whether the party distinguishes strange animal husbandry from evil. [[Reeve Gerwin Holt]] welcomes them, claims Matthias stole chapel silver, and invites them to the feast and Holt House.

**Middle:** Investigate the chapel, poor-box, ferry, smokehouses, Holt House, local testimony, and festival. The village's hospitality is real, but its stories have been rehearsed. Elsa Fenner is crowned the Feast Daughter, while evidence shows Matthias remains alive at [[Hollow Acre]].

**Turn:** [[The Reedbound Houses|Elsa's family]] tries to lure or abduct a visitor so the outsider can replace her if Matthias is rescued. Exposing the attempt reveals [[The First Bowl Keepers]], who move openly or secretly toward Hollow Acre as the village enters its longest night.

**End:** Cross the abandoned farm and reed maze, rescue the chosen victim, break [[The First Bowl]], destroy the Mother-Root and Gerwin's living seed, and prevent [[The Reed Mother]] from rebuilding. Then preserve food and secure relief before winter turns liberation into another death sentence.

**Tier:** compact folk-horror adventure, level 3 analog.

**Expected length:** 3-5 substantial sessions: approach and village investigation, feast and abduction, Hollow Acre and maze, then the ritual and aftermath. A fifth session can focus on testimony, relief, and judgement.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player party; Hanne and Matthias Veit; Gerwin Holt; [[Elsa Fenner]]; Merewick's innocent majority; the First Bowl Keepers; compromised Reedbound households; the Open Lanterns; and the Reed Mother.
- **What:** determine what happened to Matthias, distinguish local custom from predation, survive the cult's response, stop the sacrifice, end every anchor of the Mask, and keep Merewick alive afterward.
- **Where:** a raised-path and shallow-boat village in the eastern Wanemoor fens, its chapel, common stores, Holt House, feast ground, abandoned dye farm, reed maze, and Hollow Acre.
- **Why the party acts:** Hanne asks for personal help; the district bailiff issues a missing-person warrant; Matthias is known to one party member; or the party carries relief goods and discovers the official assigned to receive them has vanished.
- **Why the crisis exists:** during famine, Merewick's leaders murdered a stranger, accepted the resulting abundance as proof, and built a yearly rite around repeating the choice. Their custom now feeds a Gluttony Mask that makes each future harvest more dependent upon another death.
- **How it escalates:** investigation increases cult alarm; the feast clock advances regardless of solved clues; ordinary villagers withdraw or assist according to trust; and destroying the Mask without securing food lowers Merewick's chance of surviving winter.

## Canon Boundaries

Merewick, its ordinary life, Matthias's disappearance, Gerwin Holt, Elsa Fenner, the three village factions, Hollow Acre, the Reed-Crown Feast, the First Bowl, and the Reed Mother are local canon.

No outcome is canon. Public references should describe Merewick as an isolated fen village with an annual harvest feast and unresolved reports of missing visitors. They should not state whether Matthias survives, Gerwin falls, the Reed Mother is destroyed, or the village remains inhabited.

This adventure is independent from [[The Dead Press Upward — Full Adventure]]. Both occur in [[Wanemoor District]], but neither assumes the other's cast, chronology, Seal outcome, ghoul activity, town condition, or winter result. If used together, the Feast normally falls about three weeks before Souls-month, but a table may reverse or separate them without changing canon.

The Reed Mother belongs to Gluttony's ecology but has no tie to [[The Long Table]]. The Long Table did not found, finance, supervise, or discover Merewick's cult. Similar vice can arise wherever people build the same feeding architecture.

## Moral and Ontological Ruling

The Reed Mother is a local Gut-Maw Mask. It can recognize patterns, defend its anchors, imitate familiar voices, and offer more abundance. It does not possess a human soul, a valid claim to worship, or a reciprocal place-law. It is not a misunderstood Menhir.

The Mask genuinely increases the current catch and harvest. It does so by consuming a victim's vitality and drawing down future seed, fish-spawn, soil, and maternal health. It creates dependency rather than nourishment. The immediate good is stolen and temporary.

Human culpability remains individual:

- the First Bowl Keepers know a person is murdered and deliberately preserve the rite;
- Reedbound adults know that Feast Daughters and outsiders disappear, but many do not know the Mask's nature;
- Open Lanterns resist through food-sharing, records, and attempts to contact the district;
- most villagers believe they are attending an eccentric but legitimate harvest observance;
- children and uninformed beneficiaries are not guilty because food reached them.

Fear, family love, and hunger explain why people participate. They do not make kidnapping or substituted sacrifice permissible. Ending the rite is right. Failing to feed the liberated village afterward is negligence, not proof that the rite was necessary.

The Eclessian Church and Freeflame are not secretly responsible for the cult. Weak visitation, poor roads, and local concealment allowed it to grow. Both confessions possess teachings that condemn murder and can support relief, even while their institutions argue over who failed first.

## The Central Truth

### The Three Wet Years

From 1326 to 1328 AR, repeated floods ruined Merewick's rye strips, scattered fish-spawn, and drowned winter stores. The raised road failed. District relief arrived once and was insufficient. Several households left; others began eating seed grain.

The village's reeve household sheltered a lost grain carrier named **Odo Renn**. During a fight over a concealed sack, three future First Bowl families killed him. They hid the body beneath the abandoned dye field and served the recovered grain at a communal meal from a green-glazed bowl. They removed Odo's name from the story and called the meal proof that Merewick had saved itself.

The next growing season was unexpectedly strong. Mortal guilt, a shared feast built upon an excluded dead stranger, and the conscious decision to repeat the act created a Material feeding architecture. A local Gluttony Mask formed around the bowl, the burial root, and the village's new story of survival.

### The First Bowl Cycle

In 1329, village leaders offered another outsider at the autumn feast. The fields rose early and nets filled. Over the following three decades the rite acquired songs, games, a Reed Crown, a procession, and a private final table at Hollow Acre.

Each year the Mask transferred present abundance from three sources:

1. the victim's remaining vitality;
2. fertility stored in Merewick's future seed, soil, spawning beds, and bodies;
3. nourishment withheld from households excluded from the inner feast.

The result is real food followed by deeper fragility. The cult interprets every worsening baseline as evidence that the next sacrifice must be larger or more willing.

### Brother Matthias

Matthias reached Merewick six weeks before the adventure to distribute diocesan aid and inspect the village's own poor relief. He found a full poor-box, hidden feast stores, seven hungry households, and records showing young people supposedly leaving without taking clothes or wages.

He sent one letter with a peat barge. Gerwin intercepted his second message and confined him at Hollow Acre. The Keepers have bled him in small amounts to strengthen the Mother-Root but keep him alive for the final offering.

Gerwin tells visitors that Matthias drank, stole the poor-box, and fled north by punt. The silver remains in Holt House so that it can be displayed if the party accepts a partial search.

### The Living Seed

Gerwin swallowed a root-seed during his investiture as Keeper. It does not own his soul or compel his crimes. He chose the rite and continues choosing it. The seed lets the Reed Mother rebuild if its field body and Mother-Root are destroyed.

If Gerwin dies or suffers critical injury while the seed remains active, it tears free as the **Reed Child**, a compact mobile body of root, gut, and cutting leaf. If Gerwin is captured alive, the seed can be exposed and removed through surgery, a warlock boundary script, White Flame, or his genuine renunciation while the First Bowl is broken.

## Merewick in Numbers

- **Population:** about 180 people in 43 households, including 37 children.
- **First Bowl Keepers:** 24 adults who know the sacrifice and Mask.
- **Reedbound Houses:** 31 adults in eight households who know people disappear into the rite but possess uneven knowledge.
- **Open Lanterns:** 13 active resisters and mutual-aid keepers; most ordinary villagers belong to no faction.
- **Ordinary defenders:** 26 adults can use bows, eel spears, axes, or hunting dogs if convinced to act.
- **Cult stores:** 130 person-months of grain, smoked fish, beans, roots, and oil, much of it hidden at Hollow Acre.
- **Public stores:** about 70 person-months after the feast is prepared.
- **Recorded disappearances:** 32 likely victims since 1329, though only 19 names are recoverable during the adventure.

These numbers define moral and logistical stakes. The chosen game system should treat most villagers and cultists as groups, fronts, or background action rather than placing dozens of individuals in one combat.

## Adventure States

### Feast Clock: 5 to 0

Begin at **5** when the party reaches Merewick.

- **5 - Morning arrival:** preparations and ordinary work continue.
- **4 - Guests received:** Gerwin offers hospitality and cult testimony aligns.
- **3 - Festival games:** public investigation remains easy but watched.
- **2 - Reed Crown:** Elsa is selected and the feast table opens.
- **1 - Longest night:** ordinary villagers return home while Keepers mobilize.
- **0 - First light:** the Hollow Acre sacrifice begins.

Substantial delay, long recovery, public indecision, or following a false route advances the Clock. The Clock reaching 0 begins the ritual; it does not end the adventure. The party can interrupt a sacrifice already underway.

### Cult Alarm: 0 to 4

Begin at **0**.

- **0 - Welcoming:** Keepers rely on one rehearsed story.
- **1 - Watching:** a guide follows the party and evidence starts moving.
- **2 - Redirecting:** Reedbound households apply social pressure and Gerwin changes lodging plans.
- **3 - Hunting:** cult crews attempt abduction, isolate allies, and close the ferry.
- **4 - Procession:** the Keepers abandon secrecy, move captives, and occupy routes to Hollow Acre.

Careful comparison, private alliance, and preserving cover keep Alarm low. Threatening uninformed villagers, open burglary, failed infiltration, or letting a Keeper escape raises it. High Alarm makes the cult dangerous sooner but also exposes membership.

### Village Trust: 0 to 4

Begin at **1**.

- **0 - Strangers feared:** ordinary households hide from both cult and party.
- **1 - Polite distance:** private conversation is possible but no one takes public risk.
- **2 - Names shared:** witnesses identify inconsistencies and provide shelter.
- **3 - Lanterns lit:** noncult villagers refuse the longest-night curfew and protect captives.
- **4 - Village rising:** ordinary defenders hold paths, stores, and prisoners while the party enters Hollow Acre.

Protecting the blood-kite, helping with real work, sharing food, honoring legitimate rites, treating witnesses individually, and rescuing villagers raise Trust. Mockery, collective accusations, theft, abandoning a named person, or burning homes lowers it.

### Winter Margin: 0 to 3

Begin at **1**. See [[Merewick Winter Margin]]. Gain one, to a maximum of 3, for each of the following:

- preserve the public granary and smokehouses;
- recover the hidden cult stores without burning, tainting, or leaving them under armed control;
- send a relief signal and keep the Reedway or ferry route open.

Major fire, deliberate spoilage, flooded stores, or a failed evacuation can reduce Margin. Ending the Mask does not automatically destroy existing food, but Mother-fed seed cannot safely be planted next spring.

## Act I - The Raised Road

### Hanne's Request

Hanne shows the party Matthias's single surviving letter. It names seven hungry households, states that Merewick's poor-box contains more silver than expected, and asks why no relief was distributed. The final line says he will inspect the feast stores before leaving.

The district bailiff authorizes a missing-person search and provides a sealed introduction, not a tax collection order. The party carries basic medicines and two sacks of salt as legitimate aid.

### The Flooded Reedway

The road is partly submerged. Travelers can continue over raised stretches, hire a shallow punt, or follow Jost Rell's marker poles. Signs of isolation remain practical:

- a washed-out culvert;
- abandoned peat carts;
- one marsh-jaw slide near a deep crossing;
- last season's road-repair stakes still bundled beside the work;
- smoke from Merewick despite reports of fuel shortage.

A marsh-jaw or sudden washout can create one opening hazard. It is an animal, not an emissary of the Reed Mother.

### Pip the Blood-Kite

Near the village, a hand-sized fen animal darts from reeds toward the party. Its long mouthparts and leathery wings make it look predatory. A child named **Otto Lenz** follows, shouting for Pip.

Blood-kites are Material animals kept in hutches. Merewick feeds them pig blood and uses controlled secretions in old bloodletting practice. Pip is frightened and tries to reach its wicker transport box. Calm handling, a net, food, or letting Otto approach resolves the scene.

Killing Pip is not an unforgivable act if the party reasonably believes someone is threatened, but careless cruelty reduces Trust. The scene establishes that unfamiliarity is not proof of supernatural evil.

### First View of Merewick

Merewick appears poor but unusually well provisioned. Roofs sag, the chapel needs repair, and the Reedway is failing, yet smokehouses are full and the feast ground carries more food than several ordinary households see in a month.

Gerwin Holt meets the party publicly. He expresses concern for Matthias, then gives the prepared account:

- Matthias received the poor-box funds;
- he drank at Holt House;
- he left before dawn with the silver;
- Jost ferried him north;
- the village assumed he returned to Wanenbruck by another road.

Only the first statement is partly true. Gerwin invites the party to eat, attend the feast, and stay at Holt House while he assembles testimony.

## Act II - The Reed-Crown Feast

Act II is open. Present at least four investigation sites and two festival events. Every site yields both ordinary village texture and one useful discrepancy.

### The Reed Chapel

The old-Church chapel contains a plain altar, a Redeemer image darkened by lamp smoke, a neglected poor-box, and a side table used by Freeflame households for shared psalm books under local toleration.

The building is underkept, not desecrated. Marta Lenz maintains it without a resident priest. Inside the poor-box are:

- Matthias's folded list of seven hungry households;
- a wax impression from his almoner's seal;
- more silver than Gerwin claims Matthias took;
- several coins given by people recorded as too poor to contribute;
- a reed fiber matching the holding cage at Hollow Acre.

Marta shares her disappearance list at Trust 2 or after the party proves Matthias could not have taken the box.

### The Ferry and North Channel

Jost Rell initially repeats that Matthias left north. His punt log, pole marks, and the channel's flood line show no loaded departure that morning. At Trust 2 he admits he was ordered to leave his boat tied at Hollow Acre two nights after Matthias disappeared.

Keeping Jost safe preserves the fastest route for relief and escape. At Alarm 2, Keepers cut his ferry rope. At Alarm 3, they attempt to detain him.

### The Smokehouses

Merewick's fish and pork stores are genuinely full. The household distribution marks are not. First Bowl families receive excess while the seven names from Matthias's list have missed allocations.

The oldest smokehouse floor hides a passage to a cult store ledger. It records food by **guest**, **root**, and **house**, showing that each past disappearance corresponds to a sudden increase in allotments.

Securing rather than burning the smokehouses protects Winter Margin.

### Holt House

Gerwin's large raised house contains guest rooms, feast records, the chapel silver he says Matthias stole, and a family chronicle that begins with the Three Wet Years. Investigation can reveal:

- Matthias's torn outer cloak beneath peat sacks;
- his second letter burned incompletely in the stove;
- a route sketch circling Holt House, the chapel, smokehouse, ferry, and Hollow Acre;
- green glaze flakes where the First Bowl rested;
- a locked cupboard of Reed Crowns from young people said to have left Merewick.

Gerwin moves evidence as Alarm rises. Nothing essential exists in only one location.

### Festival Events

The festival is a real communal good corrupted at its center. Let players enjoy it without making every song a clue.

- **Raised-Way Race:** pairs cross narrow wet planks carrying one basket. It reveals who knows the hidden field paths and earns local respect.
- **Eel-Pot Draw:** contestants identify full traps by weight and water movement. One old pot contains Matthias's brass cloak clasp, discarded in the channel.
- **Reed-Torch Leap:** adults jump a low smoking bundle as a promise to bring stored food through winter. The cult later uses the same fire to light the Hollow Acre path.
- **Seed-Tale Contest:** three young people tell household histories. Elsa's rehearsed story omits every person who left after being Reed-Crowned.
- **Common Table:** most food nourishes normally, but dishes served from green-glazed copies smell richer while leaving guests hungry sooner.

### The Reed Crown

Gerwin names [[Elsa Fenner]] the Feast Daughter. Publicly, she will carry first seed to the chapel at dawn and spend a year learning household duties. Reedbound adults understand that she may disappear if no outsider is available.

Elsa attempts to learn whether the party can rescue her without killing her parents. At Trust 2 she warns one character not to sleep alone. At Trust 3 she reveals that her parents intend to bring a visitor to the abandoned dye farm.

## Act III - The Longest Night

### The Fenner Attempt

Rudolf and Anke Fenner try to substitute an outsider for Elsa. If the party accepts Elsa's invitation, she leads them toward a reed shelter while her parents and three relatives wait with sleep draught, nets, sickles, and two trained blood-kites.

If the party refuses or remains together, the family enters their lodging after the longest-night lamps are extinguished. They target one person and retreat rather than fighting to the death.

When one Fenner is seriously wounded, the others attempt to protect and carry that relative. Captured family members admit:

- Matthias is alive at Hollow Acre;
- Elsa becomes the victim if no stranger is delivered;
- Gerwin says the whole village will starve if the feast fails;
- the Keepers gather at the old dye farm before first light.

Their family love is real. Their attempted kidnapping remains culpable. Mercy, restraint, trial, escape, or death are all possible; automatic absolution is not.

### If the Cult Moves Early

At Alarm 3-4, the First Bowl Keepers abandon the quiet abduction. Four crews move through Merewick to:

1. seize Matthias's evidence from the chapel;
2. take Elsa or another fallback victim;
3. close the ferry and Reedway;
4. protect the stores and procession route.

Most villagers flee, hide, or gather at the chapel. They do not become cult soldiers. At Trust 3+, Open Lanterns organize them to hold one or two objectives while the party chooses the rest.

### Hollow Acre Farm

The abandoned dye farm stands at the edge of [[Hollow Acre]]. Its house, barn, root cellar, vats, and collapsed loft are the cult's working headquarters.

Threats include:

- weak floors and flooded vats;
- a natural rot-heap fed on dye waste and slaughter refuse;
- trained blood-kites in reed cages;
- strangler vines cultivated across the back approach;
- Keepers guarding ledgers and prisoners;
- family relics used to make the farm appear memorial rather than occupied.

The root cellar contains Matthias or a captured party member until the procession begins. It also holds the hidden winter stores and nineteen recoverable victim tokens.

Burning the farm can break cult defenses but risks the captives and lowers Winter Margin. Careful entry, freeing prisoners, and securing the root cellar preserve options.

### The Reed Maze

Beyond the farm, cut reeds and flooded ditches form a shifting field maze. It was first built to move animals without trampling crop beds and later altered for the procession.

Use three navigation principles rather than arbitrary checks:

- water flows away from Hollow Acre's raised center;
- procession knots face inward while evacuation cuts face the ferry;
- the Reed Mother's imitation voices always come from a route that consumes time or isolates someone.

At low Alarm, the party can follow the procession. At high Alarm, Keepers attack from cross-cuts and withdraw. Feastlings prepare the inner paths by arranging bowls, cutting packs into serving portions, and fastening names to reed tables.

## Act IV - The First Bowl

### The Hollow Acre Rite

The center is a dry peat island containing the Mother-Root, a stone table, the First Bowl, and an old drainage ring. Gerwin, Widow Alke, and surviving Keepers bind the current victim and begin four stages:

1. remove the victim's name from the village list;
2. serve one mouthful from the First Bowl to each Keeper;
3. bleed the victim into the Mother-Root;
4. kill the victim at first light and distribute the first seeded mud.

Interrupting any stage prevents immediate sacrifice. Permanent defeat still requires every anchor to be addressed.

### The Reed Mother in Battle

The Mother-Root opens into a broad body of reed, mud, fibrous stomach chambers, and inward-folding leaves. It blocks routes, swallows supplies, restrains people as assigned courses, and imitates voices recorded during prior feasts.

It cannot create a valid bargain. Its only offer is another cycle: name someone else, preserve the stores, and let Merewick live one more season. Acceptance knowingly continues the murder architecture.

The Mask's current body can be cut, burned, drained, exposed by White Flame, or collapsed into the peat channel. Destroying the body alone leaves the First Bowl and living seed able to rebuild it.

### Gerwin and the Reed Child

Gerwin uses human authority, prepared routes, concealed blades, and the crowd before relying on the Mask. He tries to make the party choose between pursuing him and saving the bound victim.

If Gerwin dies or is critically incapacitated while carrying the seed, the Reed Child emerges one round or one decisive beat later. It is fast, mobile, and intent on reaching the First Bowl or deep fen water.

If captured alive, Gerwin can be restrained while the seed is extracted. White Flame reveals the foreign pattern; surgery removes the physical root; a warlock boundary script prevents transfer; genuine renunciation while the Bowl breaks causes the seed to detach. Gerwin remains answerable for murder regardless of method.

### Permanent Local Defeat

The Reed Mother cannot recur at Merewick only when the party completes all four acts:

1. rescue or recover the named sacrifice;
2. break and cleanse the First Bowl;
3. destroy the Mother-Root and gather viable fragments;
4. destroy or extract Gerwin's living seed while ending the sacrificial procession.

Food must then reach the households deliberately excluded from the feast. This repairs the mortal architecture that defined some people as guests and others as courses. One symbolic shared meal is not enough without actual distribution and relief.

## Fail-Forward Rulings

- **Pip is killed:** Trust falls, but Otto's father still explains blood-kite husbandry and may later recognize trained cult animals.
- **The party rejects the feast:** investigation continues while the Clock advances; Elsa or Marta brings a warning after dark.
- **Holt House evidence is moved:** the poor-box, smokehouse ledger, Jost, and Hollow Acre preserve the same truths.
- **The Fenner abduction succeeds:** the captured character wakes in the farm root cellar beside Matthias. Another player may temporarily direct Marta or Jost during the rescue.
- **Elsa refuses help:** her Crown and memorized route still identify the procession; she remains recoverable at Hollow Acre.
- **Matthias dies:** his body, hidden list, and field notes prove the murders and relief problem. The current sacrifice shifts to Elsa or a captive.
- **Gerwin escapes:** he carries the living seed and The Mother Walks becomes a pursuit ending.
- **The First Bowl is removed intact:** the Mask can project appetite and rebuild around a later feast wherever the Bowl is served.
- **Hollow Acre burns:** cult opposition and Mother-Root weaken, but captives and stores face immediate danger and Winter Margin falls.
- **The Clock reaches 0:** the victim is wounded and the Mask fully manifests. Rescue and permanent defeat remain possible.
- **Trust remains 0:** ordinary villagers do not fight for the cult, but they also do not help hold stores, roads, or prisoners.

## Ending States

### The Table Broken

The sacrifice is stopped, all three anchors are destroyed or separated, the named victims are recorded, and the First Bowl Keepers lose control. With Winter Margin 2-3, Merewick survives without another offering. This is the morally strongest ending.

### The Root Burned

The Mother-Root and visible Mask are destroyed, but the Bowl, living seed, or sacrificial custom survives. The harvest effect ends for now. A recurrence remains possible in Merewick or wherever the surviving anchor is carried.

### The Village Freed

The cult is exposed and Matthias or Elsa rescued, but winter relief fails. Merewick enters hunger, rationing, and partial evacuation. The suffering is real; it does not vindicate the murders.

### The Old Plenty

The party accepts a substituted victim, conceals the cult, or lets Gerwin complete the rite in exchange for food and order. Merewick receives one more season of abundance while its future fertility worsens and the next demand grows.

### The Mother Walks

Gerwin or the Reed Child escapes with a living seed or the First Bowl. Merewick may be liberated, but a mobile Gluttony anchor moves into the fen roads, refugee stream, or district relief network.

### The Fen Takes Merewick

The rite, stores, ferry, and village defenses all fail. Survivors evacuate toward Wanenbruck or scattered reed hamlets while Hollow Acre remains dangerous. The adventure becomes a refugee, pursuit, and reclamation story.

No ending is selected as future canon.

## Judgement After the Feast

Specific acts determine responsibility:

- Gerwin and senior Keepers planned and performed murders.
- Kidnappers and guards answer for the people they restrained, injured, or delivered.
- Reedbound adults who concealed knowledge but harmed no one still owe testimony and restitution.
- coerced participants who warned victims or sabotaged the rite should not be treated as its architects.
- uninformed households and children are not collectively guilty.

Secular authorities judge murder, abduction, concealment, and property crimes. Church investigators determine the nature of the false rite. Neither process should replace immediate food, shelter, and care.

## Session Rhythm

1. Hanne's request, flooded Reedway, blood-kite, and arrival.
2. Open village investigation and Reed-Crown Feast.
3. Fenner abduction, cult exposure, and Hollow Acre farm.
4. Reed maze, sacrifice, Reed Mother, and living seed.
5. Stores, relief, testimony, judgement, and winter consequences.

For three sessions, combine arrival with the feast and run Hollow Acre plus the maze as one long final night. Longer play should deepen village relationships and aftermath rather than add unrelated supernatural factions.
