# The First Bowl

The First Bowl is the green-glazed serving vessel used when Merewick's famine leaders shared grain taken from the murdered carrier Odo Renn. They deliberately excluded his name from the meal. The Bowl became the social and physical invitation around which [[The Reed Mother]] formed.

## Function

The Bowl assigns four feast roles:

- **Keeper:** receives abundance and preserves the story;
- **Guest:** shares the public meal without knowing its cost;
- **Vessel:** carries seed, blood, or future fertility;
- **Course:** the person excluded from the community by being consumed for it.

Serving from the Bowl during the final rite opens the Mask's strongest local overlay. Green-glazed copies used at the public table spread appetite and prepare witnesses but cannot recreate the Reed Mother without the original.

## Signs

- glaze remains wet in dry storage;
- food smells stronger than its ingredients;
- diners unconsciously wait for one person to be served last;
- names spoken over the rim become difficult to repeat inside Hollow Acre;
- fractures fill with root fiber instead of widening;
- the Bowl grows warm when an outsider accepts formal hospitality.

## Moving the Bowl

The vessel can be carried safely only when empty, wrapped separately from food, and kept outside any shared table. First Bowl Keepers use a reed sling and never let one person carry both Bowl and victim token.

Removing it intact extends the feeding architecture. A meal served elsewhere can establish a new suggestion or sign stage, especially if someone is deliberately excluded.

## Breaking and Cleansing

Permanent destruction requires:

1. name Odo Renn and the later recoverable victims;
2. empty the vessel without another person receiving its assigned course;
3. break the ceramic body away from food, stores, and fertile soil;
4. burn or lime the root-filled fractures;
5. distribute an actual meal to the households excluded by the Keepers.

White Flame can expose the assigned roles and prevent the Bowl repairing itself. Physical tools can break it. Neither replaces truth or food distribution.

## Evidence

Each repaired fracture corresponds to one sacrifice year. Under strong light, the glaze shows handprints from Keepers who served the final table. Fragments are therefore both dangerous anchors and evidence against particular leaders.
