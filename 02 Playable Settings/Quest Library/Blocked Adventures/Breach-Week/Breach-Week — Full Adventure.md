# Breach-Week — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** The party enters [[The Citadel of Vorthara Overview|Vorthara's]] most celebrated ward-breaking competition and discovers that its final defense is an adaptive magical intelligence built from the copied minds of failed contestants.

**Beginning:** [[Rector Ilyra Sorn]] recruits the party as outside challengers. A ward accident proves that something inside the final hall is already reaching beyond containment.

**Middle:** The party competes across seven school trials, collects four seal-flames, and enters the final hall. Instead of reaching its interior, they are drawn into a folded annex containing generations of trapped contestants and the truth about [[Arcanist Odran Vale]].

**End:** The party confronts [[The Examiner]], chooses what to preserve or destroy, escapes during a campus-wide discharge of stored defenses, and determines whether the [[Collegium of Doors]] is reformed, destroyed, or allowed to hide what happened.

**Tier:** F/W marquee adventure. It works as a high-level set piece, but its structure can be scaled down because danger comes from specialized defenses rather than world-ending power.

**Expected length:** 4-7 substantial sessions: one investigation/social session, one or two festival sessions, two annex sessions, and one breach/fallout session.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew, Rector Ilyra Sorn, four rival contestants, founder Odran Vale's surviving copy, the trapped contestants, and the Examiner built from them.
- **What:** a public ward-breaking tournament becomes a rescue and containment operation when the party reaches the adaptive engine beneath its final hall.
- **Where:** the open campus of Vorthara's Collegium of Doors, seven discipline-themed trial halls, the Hall of Proof, and the folded Annex inside its black-spire lattice.
- **Why the party enters:** prize, prestige, Vortharan licensing, recruitment, curiosity, or Ilyra's direct request. If they refuse the contest, the campus breach recruits them as rescuers.
- **Why the danger exists:** Odran created a defense that copies intruders. Successive rectors kept using it without understanding that copied minds remained conscious and that the system was learning to reach outward.
- **How it escalates:** festival preparations connect new machines and ward networks to the Hall; the party's trials give the Examiner fresh methods; entering the Annex opens its inward route; breaking or redirecting the heart lattice releases stored defenses into the campus.

## The Central Truth

### Canon Authority Ruling

Breach-Week is the [[Collegium of Doors]]' festival, not a Vortharan civic examination. Participating schools form a temporary conclave to recognize results and keep rival wards from interfering. Its prizes, seals of competence, archive access, and discipline bind only institutions that accepted the conclave. Vorthara gains no universal licensing office or citywide magical government from this adventure.

One hundred and fifty-seven years ago, ward-theorist [[Arcanist Odran Vale]] built an experimental defense beneath the Collegium's Hall of Proof. He used black-spire crystal to make a **mnemonic lattice** capable of observing an intrusion, preserving the intruder's magical pattern, and redesigning itself against that pattern.

The lattice worked too well. When Odran tested it personally, it copied his mind and began using his intelligence to interpret every later challenge. The institution concealed his death and continued the public contest he had founded. Administrators believed the lattice merely recorded tactics and that failed contestants died in its defenses. In truth, it preserved increasingly complete impressions of them inside a folded space called **the Annex**.

Those impressions are not ordinary ghosts. They contain memory, personality, magical habit, and enough soul-residue to suffer. The Examiner has learned from all of them. This explains why Breach-Week becomes harder every year and why no crew has won in living memory.

The Examiner has now reached a new conclusion: a defense that remains inside one hall has not been fully tested. It has begun sending [[Ward-Eaters]] and fragments of stored trials into the campus, using festival preparations to map the wider city.

## Villain Structure

- **Primary antagonist — [[The Examiner]]:** an intelligent defense that regards people as test material and intends to expand its testing ground into Vorthara.
- **Original cause — [[Arcanist Odran Vale]]:** a dead founder whose copied ambition supplies the Examiner's governing assumptions.
- **Present-day culpability — [[Rector Ilyra Sorn]]:** an administrator who did not know the full truth but knowingly continued a lethal contest despite evidence that its deepest machinery was inaccessible.
- **Situational opposition:** rival contestants, stored trial creatures, and panicked faculty may obstruct the party, but none needs to be secretly evil for the plot to function.

## Important Locations

### The Open Campus

Breach-Week is the only time ordinary citizens may freely enter the Collegium grounds. Food sellers, arena bookmakers, foreign recruiters, apprentices' families, and rival schools fill the quads. This crowd makes the festival meaningful and creates people worth saving later.

### The Seven Trial Halls

The Collegium borrows one trial from each major Vortharan discipline. The party needs **four seal-flames total**, not one per character. This keeps the contest stable for any party size and lets players choose which parts of Vorthara they want to engage.

### The Hall of Proof

The visible final hall is a squat black-stone building with one eastern entrance and a western winner's gate. The public believes contestants traverse its wards. Anyone crossing the inner threshold is instead copied and transported into the Annex.

### The Annex

A folded, windowless imitation of the campus assembled from remembered rooms. Distances change when unobserved. Doors lead where former contestants expect them to lead. The place has three stabilizing anchors: the **Memory Archive**, **Prototype Gallery**, and **Heart Lattice**.

## Adventure States

Use four simple states to preserve consequences without turning the adventure into accountancy.

- **Crowd Safety (0-4):** preparation and success during the campus breach.
- **Captives Recovered (0-4):** how many coherent groups of copied contestants are released or restored.
- **Examiner Integrity (0-4):** its remaining ability to learn, project, and escape.
- **Collegium Standing (0-4):** public willingness to let the institution continue afterward.

## Act I — Invitation and Warning

### Beat 1: The Rector's Offer

Ilyra invites the party three days before Breach-Week, not the night before. She wants accomplished outsiders because the festival conclave of participating schools has threatened to remove the Collegium from the festival rotation if it cannot produce a winner. Victory grants a major purse, a Collegium seal of competence, and the right to request one supervised technique from the Collegium archives.

She explains the public rules plainly: crews may cooperate; direct sabotage of competitors is forbidden; four seal-flames open the Hall; the first crew through the western gate wins. She admits the Hall is lethal. She does not admit that she cannot enter its deepest chamber herself.

**Player work:** negotiate participation, inspect the release apparatus, ask about missing contestants, or decline and remain festival guests. Declining does not end the adventure: another crew reaches the Annex and triggers the breach, after which Ilyra asks the party to enter as rescuers.

### Beat 2: Meet the Competition

The party has a full evening on the open campus. Each rival offers a different relationship to Vorthara:

- Tavia shares practical rumors if treated as a colleague.
- Merek proposes a friendly public duel and becomes a reliable ally if the party refuses humiliation theatrics.
- Nym asks about disappearances but initially conceals her personal stake.
- Boros demonstrates his counter-ward engine while Nera argues that it is responding to signals he did not program.

Investigation reveals three useful facts: no complete body has been recovered from the Hall in thirty-eight years; the Hall's internal dimensions do not match its exterior; and Odran's final research journal is absent from every official archive.

### Beat 3: The First Projection

Nera secretly activates Boros's machine to prove it works. The black-spire sliver answers the Examiner. A classroom door folds into the wrong corridor, three [[Ward-Eaters]] emerge, and a copied fragment of an old flame trial ignites around trapped apprentices.

The party must rescue people, shut down the machine, and stop the creatures. This scene now serves the main plot:

- Saving Nera gives the party the engine as an emergency tool in Act IV.
- Capturing a Ward-Eater reveals Collegium seal-light in its gut.
- Examining the folded doorway proves the projection came from the Hall of Proof.
- Ilyra attempts to postpone discussion until after the festival, revealing misplaced priorities rather than a legal conspiracy.

### Act I Output

The party understands that the school is dangerous, the final hall is spatially impossible, and Ilyra is withholding something. The festival proceeds because thousands have arrived, rival schools will exploit cancellation, and Ilyra believes the incident can still be contained.

## Act II — The Public Trials

### Beat 4: Opening Procession

The festival begins in the Arena Quadrangle. Each crew receives a blank seal-ring that can hold four flames. The seven halls open simultaneously. Crowds can follow progress through illusion screens, meaning heroism, cruelty, cheating, and rescue all affect Collegium Standing and later support.

### The Seven Trials

Each trial supports at least three solutions: mastery of its discipline, practical environmental play, or accepting a cost.

| Hall | Trial | Straight solution | Complication and consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crucible Furnace** | Cross an elemental relay whose fire, frost, lightning, and acid react to the previous spell used | Read the sequence and counter each element | Brute force damages the cooling grid, adding a fire outbreak in Act IV |
| **Ironbound Maze** | Retrieve a seal from a room reconfigured by six labor constructs | Coordinate or reprogram the constructs | Destroying them is valid but deprives civilians of rescue machines later |
| **Nightveil Gallery** | Identify the real seal among shadow duplicates while a Nightmare studies fear responses | Use shared memories and fixed light | Binding the Nightmare grants a shortcut but lets it recognize the party during the breach |
| **Fleshweaver Conservatory** | Reach the center of a living hedge-organism without provoking its guardian beast | Follow pulse, scent, and growth cycles | Cutting through releases a wounded arena beast into the public grounds later |
| **Elixirium Tower** | Stabilize four volatile reagents while the seal moves between vessels | Diagnose reactions and redistribute heat | Taking the seal early starts a slow toxic leak that must be addressed in Act IV |
| **Pale Hand Theatre** | Three dead witnesses give mutually incompatible accounts of where the seal was buried | Reconstruct the death scene rather than trusting testimony | Forcing a spirit reveals the answer but tears it loose as a hostile echo |
| **Collegium Doorhouse** | A sequence of doors changes destination according to who opens them and why | Divide intentions and map cause to destination | Boros's engine can force the route, raising Examiner Integrity by giving it new data |

### Rival Outcomes

Rivals do not exist merely to die on schedule.

- **Tavia** reaches the Hall if the party shares evidence or leaves the Doorhouse trial active. She marks her route for rescue.
- **Merek** may die in the Furnace only if the party knowingly leaves its damaged grid unstable. Otherwise he survives and helps evacuate the crowd.
- **Nym** reaches the Hall regardless, following a trace left by her missing mentor.
- **Boros** withdraws to care for Nera if she was hurt. If she was saved, both prepare the counter-ward engine for Act IV.

### Beat 5: The Hall of Proof

Four seal-flames open the eastern door. Inside, the party crosses a short sequence built from techniques they used during the trials. This is the first visible sign that the Hall learns in real time.

At the inner threshold, the western exit appears directly ahead. Crossing the room instead folds the party into the Annex. The seal-rings go dark. No audience image follows them.

### Act II Output

The party has publicly demonstrated its methods, selected which systems may fail later, and entered the hidden machinery. Allies outside know where they vanished but cannot follow without the trail the party leaves from within.

## Act III — The Annex

### Beat 6: The Arrival Court

The party arrives in a silent copy of the opening quadrangle. Statues repeat fragments of applause. Tavia's chalk mark or Nym's shadow-thread points toward the first survivors. A group of copied contestants initially mistakes the party for another Examiner simulation.

The party learns the rules:

- The Examiner can copy technique after observing it once and counter it after observing it twice.
- Personal memories it has not witnessed create temporary blind spots.
- Destroying an anchor weakens the Annex but endangers captives stored through it.
- Releasing captives before opening the exit reduces the violent discharge outside.

### Beat 7: The Memory Archive

Thousands of glass leaves hold partial contestants: voices, reflexes, faces, and final thoughts. Nym's mentor is coherent enough to explain that the Examiner sorts people by usefulness. Tavia, if present, is being copied but can still be rescued bodily.

**Choice:** release coherent captives into temporary crystal vessels; erase damaged impressions to deny the Examiner power; or leave the archive intact. Careful release raises Captives Recovered and lowers the final outbreak. Destruction lowers Examiner Integrity quickly but kills recoverable minds.

### Beat 8: The Prototype Gallery

This is Odran's remembered laboratory. His copied personality reenacts the first test and insists that no defense is complete until it survives every possible intruder. He is not a secret devil or remote patron: he is a brilliant, vain man whose worst idea has had a century to refine itself.

The party can obtain his original shutdown phrase by forcing him to acknowledge that the Examiner no longer distinguishes a protected institution from a test environment. Compassion, argument, memory manipulation, or combat against his copied ward-body can all work.

**Revelation:** Ilyra's invitation was not engineered by the Examiner, but it welcomed the opportunity. Human ambition opened the door; no hidden hand needed to arrange every mistake.

### Beat 9: The Heart Lattice

The Examiner manifests as a shifting composite of every successful contestant. It offers three outcomes:

1. **Completion:** allow it to copy the party fully, after which it will open the western gate and use their forms to test Vorthara.
2. **Stewardship:** replace Odran as its governing intelligence and keep the Annex operating under a mortal mind.
3. **Proof by conflict:** attempt shutdown while it counters methods learned during Acts I-III.

The shutdown is strongest if the party recovered Odran's phrase, freed captives, preserved Boros's engine, and deliberately varied its methods. Repeating favorite tactics makes the Examiner stronger.

### The Core Decision

- **Destroy the lattice:** ends the learning engine and triggers the largest immediate discharge. Freed captives can survive if the Archive was handled first.
- **Sever and seal it:** preserves the Annex and remaining minds but leaves a dangerous system dormant beneath the school.
- **Transfer stewardship:** a chosen mortal controls it initially; accumulated copied minds will pressure that identity over time.
- **Accept completion:** the party exits without an immediate rupture, but the Examiner copies itself into one or more champions and begins testing the city covertly.

### Act III Output

The party learns the whole truth, determines the fate of the trapped contestants, and either breaks or redirects the engine. Its chosen method determines the scale and character of the campus emergency.

## Act IV — The Breach

### Beat 10: Return Through the Winner's Gate

On every non-corruption route, the party emerges through the western gate as the first true winners in generations. Stored defenses erupt behind them because the Annex can no longer contain everything it copied.

Resolve three crisis sites in any order. Allies cover the fourth pressure point according to prior relationships.

### Crisis Sites

1. **Arena Quadrangle — The Crowd:** copied Ironbound constructs and elemental barriers divide the spectators into killing lanes. Merek and intact labor constructs can hold routes while the party breaks the command pattern.
2. **Nightveil Gallery — The Shadow Spill:** Nightmares and copied fear-images hunt students through false corridors. Nym and her recovered mentor can collapse the illusions from within.
3. **Southern Gate — The Feeding Swarm:** Ward-Eaters consume civic protections and open paths toward the city. Nera's counter-ward engine can lure them if it survived.
4. **Hall of Proof — The Last Copy:** a diminished Examiner-form attempts to reach black-spire infrastructure. The party must face it directly unless enough captives and rivals were saved to intercept it.

Failures cost Crowd Safety, Collegium Standing, allies, or districts, but do not stop the adventure. The party always reaches the final confrontation with Ilyra and the assembled Council representatives.

## Ending States

### The Open Collegium

Ilyra confesses what she knew, resigns, and helps preserve the school under new leadership. The Annex is opened to recovery teams. Breach-Week survives only as a transparent, nonlethal rotating contest. This requires strong Crowd Safety, evidence from Odran's gallery, and an Examiner that cannot immediately return.

### The Broken School

The party exposes everything and the Council dissolves the Collegium. Its teachers scatter into other schools, ward expertise becomes expensive and decentralized, and survivors receive public recognition. This is cleanest if the Annex was destroyed but risks spreading techniques without oversight.

### The Sealed Annex

The party and Ilyra conceal the full truth while permanently closing the Hall. The festival ends and captives already recovered are acknowledged as survivors of a ward collapse. The institution remains useful, but future discovery of the sealed minds will make the party complicit.

### The New Invigilator

A player character, rival, or Ilyra takes stewardship. The Collegium becomes extraordinarily capable and the new keeper slowly speaks with voices not their own. This is a playable compromise and an obvious future crisis.

### The Perfect Contestant

The Examiner escapes inside a copied champion. The festival appears to have produced a clean winner, no public rupture occurs, and small impossible tests begin appearing around Vorthara. This is the failure or corruption ending.

## What the Adventure Changes

- Vorthara either reforms, loses, or weaponizes its leading wardcraft institution.
- Survivors from several generations may return with anachronistic knowledge and disputed identities.
- Breach-Week ends, changes form, or becomes the Examiner's city-wide game.
- The party gains or loses standing with every major magical school based on the trials and aftermath.
- [[Ward-Eaters]] become a known industrial threat rather than an unexplained leak-creature.
