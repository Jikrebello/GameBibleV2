# The Bright-Fire Carnival — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** A traveling carnival arrives at the logging town of [[Ferren Hollow]] for the [[Folk Daily Life and the Thirteen Moons|Bright-Fire]] fair. It is a genuine delight — and it is a [[Sylvorn]]-court instrument, staffed by the [[The Cloven]] of an offering grove that the [[The Frontier Industry Lords|Timber-Cartel]]'s crews burned. They have come to collect a debt.

**Beginning:** Three days of real festival. The attractions delight by day; the chill is a little wrong; the performers are a little too graceful. The party enters as fairgoers and becomes the only people asking why the carnival feels like a countdown.

**Middle:** The party learns two truths in sequence. First, the carnival's fey are collecting a lawful, proportionate [[The Grove-Debt|grove-debt]] against the people who burned the grove — measured, targeted, and stoppable by payment. Second, a colder thing has gotten inside the collection: [[The Rime Rider]], a [[The Atlas of the Left Hand|Left-Hand]] factor who buys aggrieved land-debts, has inflated the invoice and means to take the whole town instead of the guilty few.

**End:** On the third night, after the Bright-Fire bonfires and the fireworks, the attractions turn. Each daytime delight becomes its lethal shadow. The party can fight through the gauntlet, remove the Rider, pay the original debt, force the guilty to pay it, or some combination — but force ends the Rider without ending the debt, and paying the debt ends the Cloven without ending the Rider.

**Tier:** W→3. A marquee side-quest with a moral knot discovered late — the aggressor was wronged first, the punishment was fair before it was hijacked, and the clean solution (pay what is owed) is the one the guilty will not allow until it is their own warmth on the invoice.

**Expected length:** 3-4 substantial sessions: the three festival days as investigation, the turn after the fireworks, the attraction gauntlet, and the reckoning over who pays.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; [[Sisrin the Alder-Maid]], the grove's surviving law running the carnival; [[The Rime Rider]], the cold factor corrupting her collection; the [[The Cloven]] carnival-staff and their corrupted [[The Rimebound]] forms; "Clear-Cut" Karl and the [[The Frontier Industry Lords|Timber-Cartel]]; Old Tomas of the Season-Cutters; and the fairgoers of [[Ferren Hollow]].
- **What:** understand the [[The Grove-Debt|grove-debt]], stop the massacre the Rider has planned for the third night, and decide who actually pays what is owed.
- **Where:** Ferren Hollow, a Sylvan Marches logging town on the Folk border, during its Bright-Fire fair; the carnival on the Two-Fires green; and the burned offering grove — the Stump-Ring — in the wood beyond.
- **Why the party acts:** they are at the fair; a child's laughter or a neighbor's years go missing in front of them; a Cloven performer tells them the truth expecting them to fail to stop it; or a Season-Cutter begs them to make the cartel pay before the whole town does.
- **Why the carnival exists:** the Cloven filed a proportionate collection for a grove burned without offering. The festival is the lawful venue and the lure. It would have ended with the guilty frightened and the debt paid.
- **How it escalates:** the Rider bought the grievance, inflated its coin, and set the third-night bonfires as the moment a targeted scare becomes a town-wide harvest — and every hour the cartel refuses to pay makes the Rider's version look more like justice.

## Moral and Metaphysical Ruling

The grove-debt is real and the Cloven's original claim is just. An offering grove was burned to hit a quota, without the payment the Folk and the Season-Cutters have always made, and under [[Sylvorn]]'s law the land remembers and collects. A proportionate collection against the people who did it is not malice; it is the frontier's oldest arithmetic.

The corruption is the crime. The Rime Rider did not suffer the loss and is not owed the debt. It bought the grievance as an instrument and inflated it because grief, taken by force from a whole town, yields more than a settled invoice. Turning a fair punishment into a massacre is the Rider's wrong, not the grove's.

The town is not innocent and not collectively guilty. Most of Ferren Hollow neither burned the grove nor stopped those who did; some profited quietly, some warned against it, one crew actually did it. A massacre erases that difference. A just collection depends on it.

The Cloven are people of a kind, bound to a law rather than choosing freely. They can be reasoned with in the grammar of the debt, not the grammar of mercy. The Rimebound — Cloven whom the Rider's cold has overtaken — have lost even that, and freeing them means breaking the Rider's hold, not defeating a monster.

The horror of the piece is that the cleanest solution is a receipt. Pay what is owed and the fair ends as a fair. The reason it becomes a slaughter is that the guilty would rather risk the town than admit the bill — and a colder thing is counting on exactly that.

## The Central Truth

Last spring, to make a quota Holger Strun had quietly promised a southern buyer, "Clear-Cut" Karl's Firebrands burned a tract the Season-Cutters had always left standing: an offering grove on the Folk border, one of the groves Old Tomas's crews leave uncut and pay into each Bright-Fire. Karl burned it because burning is faster than felling and because a Folk reprisal would let him demand more guards and more logging rights — the same play he always runs.

The grove did not simply die. Under [[Sylvorn]]'s law a grove that old is a node of territorial memory, and its law survived its trees. It gathered itself into [[Sisrin the Alder-Maid]] — the grove wearing a face — and filed a collection in the only venue the frontier and the Faewild share: a festival. Sisrin took over a real traveling carnival, staffed it with the grove's [[The Cloven]], and brought it to Ferren Hollow for Bright-Fire. Her intended collection was proportionate and targeted: frighten the town, take specific years and names and warmth from the crews who did the burning, and leave with the debt paid. Cruel by human measure, but bounded.

Then the debt was noticed by something that trades in them. [[The Rime Rider]] is a factor of the [[The Atlas of the Left Hand|Left Hand]]'s cold reaches — the architecture that buys legitimate debts and collects them predatorily — wearing the mask of the year's winter half, when territorial-memory debts fall due hardest. The Rider approached Sisrin as an ally of the wronged and offered to *guarantee* the collection. What it actually did was buy a share of the grievance and inflate its coin: reclassify the debt from "the crews who burned the grove" to "the town that ate the profit," and set the third-night bonfires as the moment the whole of Ferren Hollow pays.

Sisrin knows the collection has been corrupted and cannot easily undo it, because she took the Rider's guarantee in the grove's name and Faewild law holds her to it. She wants the guilty to pay the original debt before the third night, which would satisfy her claim and strip the Rider of the pretext it is riding. The Timber-Cartel refuses. Karl will not admit the burning; Holger Strun will not pay for a grove he can still deny knowing about. Every hour they refuse, the Rider's version looks more like justice, and the fireworks come closer.

## The Cloven, the Cartel, and the Left Hand

The [[The Cloven]] are [[Sylvorn]]'s forest-fey — fauns, thorn-maids, grove-spirits, beautiful hazards that arise where the land-law runs hot. They do not choose cruelty; they collect in the coin their law recognizes: names, laughter, years, warmth. Under Sisrin they are a lawful collection crew wearing a carnival. Under the Rider's cold, some become [[The Rimebound]], collectors with the proportion burned out of them.

The [[The Frontier Industry Lords|Timber-Cartel]] is canon Grenzburg: Holger Strun's shadow-government of the axe, split between Old Tomas's sustainable Season-Cutters, "Clear-Cut" Karl's provocateur Firebrands, and Strun himself, who funds Karl in secret and chastises him in public. The burned grove is the exact wrong the cartel's structure is built to produce and deny.

The [[The Atlas of the Left Hand|The Left Hand]] is the setting's predatory architecture — the meta-faction that buys legitimate obligations and collects them for ruin. The Rime Rider is one of its cold instruments. It is not the wronged party and not the law; it is the thing that turns a just debt into a catastrophe because catastrophe pays better.

## Adventure States

- **Fair Warmth (3 → 0):** the festival's remaining safe days, counting down over the three Bright-Fire nights. At 0, the attractions turn.
- **The Invoice (targeted / inflated):** whether the collection is still aimed at the guilty crews or has been widened to the whole town. The Rider raises it; paying installments or exposing the Rider can lower it.
- **Cartel Admission (0-3):** how close Karl, then Strun, are to admitting the burning and paying — moved by evidence, pressure, Folk testimony, and the Season-Cutters.
- **Cloven Trust (-2 to +2):** whether the carnival's fey resist the party, deal with them in debt-grammar, or help them strip the Rider from the collection.
- **Rider's Hold (0-3):** how deep the cold has sunk into the Cloven. High Hold means more Rimebound and a harder unbinding at the climax.

## The Ways Through

Most groups will braid these; none of them alone is clean.

### Pay the Debt

Settle the original grove-debt in its own coin — years, names, warmth, or the Folk-recognized offering — from those who owe it. This satisfies Sisrin's claim and removes the Rider's pretext. The obstacle is not the fey; it is getting the guilty to pay.

### Force the Guilty

Make Karl, and then Strun, admit the burning and pay before the third night. Evidence, Folk testimony, Old Tomas's confession, and public pressure at the fair all raise Cartel Admission. This is the Tier-W spine: the massacre is stoppable, and the people who can stop it won't.

### Strip the Rider

Expose or defeat the Rime Rider to collapse the inflated invoice back to its targeted form. Force removes the Rider but leaves the real debt unpaid — Sisrin will still collect from the guilty, and if they still refuse, the fair still ends badly, just smaller.

### Break the Bargain

Find the terms of the guarantee Sisrin took from the Rider and void it under Faewild law — a thin-space filing, a Folk grove-court, or the Rider's own overreach used against it. This frees Sisrin to call off the corrupted collection, and is the only path that saves both the town and the grove's claim.

### Survive the Turn

If the third night comes with the debt unpaid and the Rider intact, the party runs the attraction gauntlet to save as many as they can, then confronts the Rider and Sisrin in the ruin of the fair. The worst-shaped ending, and the one the module is built to make avoidable.

## Act I — Three Days of Bright-Fire

### The Fair by Day

The carnival stands on the Two-Fires green outside [[Ferren Hollow]], between the town and the wood. The two Bright-Fire bonfires are lit; cattle are driven between them; courting couples walk the lanes; the Folk moon-count's new tally-sticks are cut nearby, and borderland priests fret about the Greenveil overlap as they do every year.

The attractions are real and good:

- **The Laughing House:** a mirror-and-corridor funhouse that leaves you lighter than you went in.
- **The Sweetheart Float:** a slow boat-ride through a dim, flower-hung tunnel, for couples.
- **The Wonder Menagerie:** impossible, gentle beasts in willow cages.
- **The Fortune Tent:** a Cloven seer who tells true fortunes in exchange for small, unnoticed things.
- **The Ring-Toss and the Bright Wheel:** games whose prizes are a little too dear to the winner.
- **The Fireworks Rig:** the great third-night display everyone is waiting for, built high over the green.

### The Wrongness Under the Fun

Over three days, attentive players collect the dissonances:

- performers whose grace and chill do not fit a frontier fair;
- winners who cannot quite remember a name, a face, a year, after taking a prize;
- a barker, **Wren Sallow**, who will tell the plain truth to anyone who asks the right question, and is unbothered because no one ever does anything;
- the fair's warmth failing at the edges as the third night nears — frost where there should be spring mud;
- the wood beyond the green, where the burned Stump-Ring still smells of last spring's fire.

Each day lowers **Fair Warmth**. The party can spend the days investigating, and what they learn determines whether the third night is a reckoning or a rescue.

### Act I Output

The party knows the carnival is fey, that its warmth is running out, and that something in the wood was burned. Whether they yet know the debt is *just*, and that a colder thing has hold of it, depends on how they spent the days.

## Act II — The Debt and the Corruption

### The Grove-Debt

Investigation in the wood, at the Stump-Ring, and among the Season-Cutters reveals the [[The Grove-Debt]]: an offering grove burned by Karl's Firebrands, the payment never made, the land's memory collecting. Sisrin, approached in the debt's own grammar rather than as a monster, will confirm it plainly — she is not hiding the claim; she is proud of it.

This is the hinge of the adventure. Once the party understands the debt is proportionate and the collector is the wronged party, "kill the fey" stops being a clean answer.

### The Rider's Hand

A second layer surfaces at the Fortune Tent, in the frost at the fair's edges, and in Wren Sallow's franker moments: the collection has been corrupted. [[The Rime Rider]] has inflated the invoice from "the crews who burned the grove" to "the town that ate the profit," and set the third-night fires as the harvest. Sisrin took the Rider's guarantee and is bound by it; she wants the guilty to pay before the third night precisely because that would strip the Rider of its pretext.

- The party can find the **terms of the guarantee** — a thin-space bargain with a voidable overreach.
- They can find the **Rider's true nature** — a Left-Hand factor, not the wronged party, collecting for yield.
- They can find that **Sisrin regrets it**, which makes her a possible ally against the thing she invited in.

### The Guilty Who Won't Pay

Running parallel is the human obstacle. Old Tomas confesses that he knew and could not stop Karl. Karl denies everything and works the fair's "trouble" into a demand for more guards. Holger Strun, if reached, treats the debt as a negotiation to be lost slowly, and Mayor Ports has buried the rumor to protect the season's trade.

Raising **Cartel Admission** — evidence of the burning, Folk testimony, Tomas's confession, public pressure on the green — is how the party turns the one clean solution from impossible into merely very hard.

### Act II Output

The party understands the whole shape: a just debt, a guilty party that won't pay, and a predator inflating the bill toward massacre. They have chosen to pay, to force payment, to strip the Rider, to void the bargain, or to prepare to survive the turn.

## Act III — The Third Night

### After the Fireworks

The great fireworks fire over the Two-Fires green, the whole town gathered to watch — exactly as the lure intended. If the debt is unpaid and the Rider intact when the last shell bursts, **Fair Warmth** hits 0 and the attractions turn.

### The Gauntlet of Turned Attractions

Each daytime delight becomes its lethal shadow, kept from the original as a gauntlet of set-pieces the party already walked through by day:

- **The Laughing House** becomes a maze that takes a year for every wrong turning and will not let the lightened leave.
- **The Sweetheart Float** runs cold and deep; the flowers are frost, and the tunnel drowns couples in the warmth they came to share.
- **The Wonder Menagerie** opens its willow cages, and the gentle beasts are [[The Rimebound]] now.
- **The Fortune Tent** collects the fortunes it told, calling in every small thing taken all week at once.
- **The Bright Wheel** turns into a thing that takes the warmth of everyone strapped to it.

The gauntlet is survivable and, crucially, *interruptible*: paying the debt or breaking the Rider's hold can stop attractions mid-turn. The party is choosing, room by room, between fighting the horror and ending its cause.

### The Rider and the Alder-Maid

At the center, by the dying bonfires, the party reaches [[The Rime Rider]] and [[Sisrin the Alder-Maid]]. The confrontation resolves along whichever throughline the party built:

- **Pay or force payment now:** if the guilty pay the original debt at the fires — even under duress, even at the last shell — Sisrin's claim is satisfied and she must, by her own law, call the collection off. The Rider loses its pretext and must either withdraw or attack openly as what it is.
- **Void the bargain:** if the party has the guarantee's terms and a venue to file — Folk grove-court, thin-space rule, or the Rider's own overreach — Sisrin is freed to turn on the Rider beside them.
- **Strip the Rider by force:** defeating the Rider collapses the invoice to its targeted form; Sisrin will still collect from the guilty, but the town is spared, and the party has traded a clean ending for a smaller ugly one.

### If It Comes to Violence

A pure fight is the module's worst shape and remains playable: the Rider fights with cold, the Rimebound with the proportion burned out of them, and the turned attractions as terrain. But the fireworks-lit green is full of people, and the party's real problem is never killing the Rider — it is that killing the Rider does not pay what Ferren Hollow still owes.

## Ways to Resolve the Conflict

### The Debt Paid Justly

The guilty pay the grove-debt in its own coin and the offering groves are pledged restored. Sisrin calls off the collection; the Rider, robbed of pretext and yield, withdraws or is destroyed as an open aggressor. The fair ends as a fair, a year late. The strongest ending.

### The Cartel Broken Open

Pressure forces Karl's confession and Strun's payment publicly, on the green, in front of the Folk and the Church-date fairgoers both. The cartel's provoke-and-profit machine is exposed; the debt is paid; the frontier's timber politics shift. Costs the party the cartel's goodwill for good.

### The Rider Cast Out, the Debt Remaining

The party strips the Rider by force or filing but never makes the guilty pay. The town survives the third night; Sisrin collects proportionately from Karl's crews and leaves. Just enough justice to be bitter, and an offering grove still to re-seed.

### The Grove Re-Seeded

Beyond stopping the massacre, the party undertakes the Folk rite to re-seat the grove — a seed-burial oath, an offering restored, the Season-Cutters vouching. Sylvorn's law is answered rather than merely paid, and Ferren Hollow gains a real Folk-border peace instead of a settled invoice.

### Survived, Not Solved

The party runs the gauntlet and saves who they can, but the debt goes unpaid and the Rider escapes with a share of the town's warmth. Ferren Hollow is a smaller, colder place; the Rider has a new instrument and a taste for the Marches; the grove is still owed.

### The Rider Wins the Turn

The clean paths all failed and the fight came too late. The third night takes the town or most of it. The Cloven, appalled at what their collection became, may turn on the Rider too late to matter — a tragedy the module exists to make optional.

## Restoration and Release

The [[The Rimebound]] are not freed by being killed. Release means breaking the Rider's hold — voiding the guarantee, paying the debt, or destroying the Rider — after which a Cloven can return to Sisrin's measured law or leave the collection entirely. Cutting one down in the gauntlet stops a threat and frees no one.

The grove-debt is not "cured"; it is paid or re-seated. Payment in the court's coin ends the claim. Re-seating the grove — the fuller answer — needs a Folk seed-burial rite, a restored offering, and the guilty party's participation, and it turns a stopped massacre into a healed border.

## Ending States Summary

- **Best:** debt paid, Rider cast out, grove pledged re-seeded, cartel's game exposed.
- **Good:** Rider destroyed and town saved, but the debt lingers and an offering grove waits.
- **Costly:** town saved by force, cartel goodwill lost, Folk border still raw.
- **Tragic:** the turn came, the town paid in warmth, the Cloven grieve what they were made into.

## What the Adventure Changes

- Ferren Hollow learns the difference between the fey who came to collect and the thing that came to feast — or does not, and pays for it.
- The [[The Frontier Industry Lords|Timber-Cartel]]'s provoke-and-profit machine is exposed, reformed, or vindicated, depending on whether the burning is ever admitted.
- [[Sisrin the Alder-Maid]] becomes a satisfied claimant who leaves, a grove-court ally on the Folk border, or a bound instrument the party could not free from her own bargain.
- [[The Rime Rider]] becomes a destroyed aggressor, a withdrawn factor nursing a grudge, or a cold power loose in the Marches with a new debt to buy.
- The [[The Cloven]] and [[The Rimebound]] enter the known bestiary of the Sylvan Marches as, respectively, the land's lawful collectors and what they become when a colder hand inflates the bill.
- The offering-grove custom is re-established, quietly abandoned, or turned into a Folk-border flashpoint.
