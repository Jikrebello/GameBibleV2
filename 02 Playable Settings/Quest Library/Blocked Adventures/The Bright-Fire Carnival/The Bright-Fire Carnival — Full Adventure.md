# The Bright-Fire Carnival — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** A traveling carnival arrives at the logging town of [[Ferren Hollow]] for the [[Folk Daily Life and the Thirteen Moons|Bright-Fire]] fair. It is a genuine delight - and it is a [[Sylvorn]]-court instrument, staffed by the [[The Cloven]] of an offering grove that the [[The Frontier Industry Lords|Timber-Cartel]]'s crews burned. They have come to force recognition of the wrong and exact a dangerous ecological price.

**Beginning:** Three days of real festival. The attractions delight by day; the chill is a little wrong; the performers are a little too graceful. The party enters as fairgoers and becomes the only people asking why the carnival feels like a countdown.

**Middle:** The party learns two truths in sequence. First, Karl's crews committed a real wrong and Sisrin's collection is targeted at the people responsible, but her chosen coin of years, names, and warmth is not made just by [[Sylvorn]]'s law. Second, a colder thing has gotten inside the collection: [[The Rime Rider]], an ancient winter-hunt predator that fastens itself to aggrieved land, has widened the claim and means to take the whole town instead of the guilty few.

**End:** On the third night, after the Bright-Fire bonfires and the fireworks, the attractions turn. Each daytime delight becomes its lethal shadow. The party can fight through the gauntlet, remove the Rider, compel confession and concrete repair, persuade Sisrin to accept non-personal restitution, or combine those paths. Stopping the Rider does not heal the grove, and appeasing the grove does not by itself stop the Rider.

**Tier:** W→3. A marquee side-quest with a moral knot discovered late: the apparent aggressor was wronged first, but a real grievance does not sanctify a cruel remedy. The clean solution requires exposing the guilty, repairing the land, persuading Sisrin to accept a defensible settlement, and stopping the predator that profits from refusing all distinctions.

**Expected length:** 3-4 substantial sessions: the three festival days as investigation, the turn after the fireworks, the attraction gauntlet, and the reckoning over guilt and repair.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; [[Sisrin the Alder-Maid]], the grove's surviving law running the carnival; [[The Rime Rider]], the cold factor corrupting her collection; the [[The Cloven]] carnival-staff and their corrupted [[The Rimebound]] forms; "Clear-Cut" Karl and the [[The Frontier Industry Lords|Timber-Cartel]]; Old Tomas of the Season-Cutters; and the fairgoers of [[Ferren Hollow]].
- **What:** understand the [[The Grove-Debt|grove-response]], stop the massacre the Rider has planned for the third night, and decide how the guilty answer without sacrificing other people or treating Menhir law as justice.
- **Where:** Ferren Hollow, a Sylvan Marches logging town on the Folk border, during its Bright-Fire fair; the carnival on the Two-Fires green; and the burned offering grove — the Stump-Ring — in the wood beyond.
- **Why the party acts:** they are at the fair; a child's laughter or a neighbor's years go missing in front of them; a Cloven performer tells them the truth expecting them to fail to stop it; or a Season-Cutter begs them to make the cartel pay before the whole town does.
- **Why the carnival exists:** Sisrin chose a customary shared festival as the venue for a bounded collection against the grove's actual destroyers. The carnival is both summons and lure.
- **How it escalates:** the Rider attached itself to the grievance, widened its coin, and set the third-night bonfires as the moment a targeted threat becomes a town-wide harvest. Every hour the cartel refuses admission gives the Rider more leverage.

## Moral and Metaphysical Ruling

The burning is a real wrong and the grove-debt is a real metaphysical pressure. Karl destroyed an offering grove to hit a quota, endangered the border, and concealed the act. Under [[Sylvorn]]'s law the land remembers and seeks equivalence. That response proves an injury occurred; it does not prove that taking years, names, or warmth is a just punishment.

The Rime Rider's intervention is a separate and greater crime. It did not suffer the loss. It attached itself to the grievance because grief taken by force from a whole town yields more than any bounded settlement. Turning the grove's response into a massacre is chosen predation.

The town is neither innocent nor collectively guilty. Most of Ferren Hollow neither burned the grove nor stopped those who did; some profited quietly, some warned against it, and one crew actually did it. Moral judgement preserves those differences. The Rider erases them, while Sisrin initially mistakes targeted ecological equivalence for justice.

The Cloven are court persons with full agency, though court compulsion narrows the choices available to them. They can understand argument, guilt, restraint, and mercy; they are accountable for the choices they retain. The Rimebound - Cloven whom the Rider's cold has overtaken - suffer a coercive condition, and freeing them means breaking the Rider's hold rather than merely destroying their bodies.

The cleanest solution is not a receipt but a four-part reckoning: confession, material restitution, ecological repair, and the Rider's defeat or expulsion. The guilty would rather risk the town than admit what they did, Sisrin must be moved beyond her first cruel equation, and a colder thing is counting on both refusals.

## The Central Truth

Last spring, to make a quota Holger Strun had quietly promised a southern buyer, "Clear-Cut" Karl's Firebrands burned a tract the Season-Cutters had always left standing: an offering grove on the Folk border, one of the groves Old Tomas's crews leave uncut and pay into each Bright-Fire. Karl burned it because burning is faster than felling and because a Folk reprisal would let him demand more guards and more logging rights — the same play he always runs.

The grove did not simply die. Under [[Sylvorn]]'s law a grove that old is a node of territorial memory, and its law survived its trees. It gathered itself into [[Sisrin the Alder-Maid]] - the grove wearing a face - and announced a collection in the shared venue of a festival. Sisrin took over a real traveling carnival, staffed it with the grove's [[The Cloven]], and brought it to Ferren Hollow for Bright-Fire. Her intended collection targeted the actual crews rather than the town: frighten them, take specific years, names, and warmth, and leave when the grove recognized equivalence. It was bounded, but morally cruel. Sisrin can be persuaded to accept confession, labor, goods, restored land, and renewed offering instead.

Then the grievance was noticed by something that feeds through them. [[The Rime Rider]] is an old Faewild winter-hunter exiled from seasonal retinues, wearing the mask of the year's winter half, when territorial-memory claims press hardest. The Rider approached Sisrin as an ally of the wronged and offered to *guarantee* the collection. What it actually did was fasten itself to the grievance and widen its coin: reclassify the target from "the crews who burned the grove" to "the town that ate the profit," and set the third-night bonfires as the moment the whole of Ferren Hollow is harvested.

Sisrin knows the collection has been corrupted and cannot easily undo it, because she took the Rider's guarantee in the grove's name and Faewild law holds her retinue within its mechanism. She wants the guilty to answer before the third night, which would satisfy the grove and strip the Rider of its pretext. The Timber-Cartel refuses. Karl will not admit the burning; Holger Strun will not repair a grove he can still deny knowing about. Every hour they refuse strengthens the Rider, and the fireworks come closer.

## The Cloven, the Cartel, and the Rime Rider

The [[The Cloven]] are [[Sylvorn]]'s forest-fey - fauns, thorn-maids, grove-spirits, and beautiful hazards that arise where the land-law runs hot. They possess agency, although their place-law makes some choices easier to imagine than others. Under Sisrin they are a court retinue wearing a carnival. Under the Rider's cold, some become [[The Rimebound]], courtiers whose ordinary restraints have been overtaken.

The [[The Frontier Industry Lords|Timber-Cartel]] is canon Grenzburg: Holger Strun's shadow-government of the axe, split between Old Tomas's sustainable Season-Cutters, "Clear-Cut" Karl's provocateur Firebrands, and Strun himself, who funds Karl in secret and chastises him in public. The burned grove is the exact wrong the cartel's structure is built to produce and deny.

The Rime Rider is an exiled winter-hunter that survives by attaching itself to genuine grievance. It is neither the wronged party nor a moral authority; it turns a bounded ecological response into indiscriminate predation because stolen warmth lets it endure beyond its proper season.

## Adventure States

- **Fair Warmth (3 → 0):** the festival's remaining safe days, counting down over the three Bright-Fire nights. At 0, the attractions turn.
- **The Claim (targeted / widened):** whether the collection is still aimed at the guilty crews or has been widened to the whole town. The Rider raises it; concrete repair or exposing the Rider can lower it.
- **Cartel Admission (0-3):** how close Karl, then Strun, are to admitting the burning and funding restitution - moved by evidence, pressure, Folk testimony, and the Season-Cutters.
- **Cloven Trust (-2 to +2):** whether the carnival's fey resist the party, deal with them in debt-grammar, or help them strip the Rider from the collection.
- **Rider's Hold (0-3):** how deep the cold has sunk into the Cloven. High Hold means more Rimebound and a harder unbinding at the climax.

## The Ways Through

Most groups will braid these; none of them alone is clean.

### Repair the Breach

Compel the offenders to confess, compensate those endangered, restore the land, and renew the offering custom. Persuade Sisrin that this concrete repair provides the equivalence the grove requires without taking years, names, or warmth. This satisfies the grove and removes the Rider's pretext.

### Force the Guilty

Make Karl, and then Strun, admit the burning and commit resources before the third night. Evidence, Folk testimony, Old Tomas's confession, and public pressure at the fair all raise Cartel Admission. This is the Tier-W spine: the massacre is stoppable, and the people who can help stop it refuse because admission costs them power.

### Strip the Rider

Expose or defeat the Rime Rider to collapse the town-wide harvest back to Sisrin's targeted claim. Force removes the Rider but leaves the grove damaged and Sisrin's demand unresolved. If she and the guilty both refuse defensible repair, the fair still ends in a smaller confrontation.

### Break the Bargain

Find the terms of the guarantee Sisrin took from the Rider and void it through a thin-space precedent, a Folk grove-court, or the Rider's own overreach. This frees Sisrin's retinue from the mechanism. It stops the compulsory harvest but does not settle the human wrong or decide whether Sisrin accepts repair.

### Survive the Turn

If the third night comes with the breach unrepaired and the Rider intact, the party runs the attraction gauntlet to save as many as they can, then confronts the Rider and Sisrin in the ruin of the fair. This is the worst-shaped ending, and the one the module is built to make avoidable.

## Act I — Three Days of Bright-Fire

### The Fair by Day

The carnival stands on the Two-Fires green outside [[Ferren Hollow]], between the town and the wood. The two Bright-Fire bonfires are lit; cattle are driven between them; courting couples walk the lanes; the Folk moon-count's new tally-sticks are cut nearby, and borderland priests fret about the Greenveil overlap as they do every year.

The attractions are real and good:

- **The Laughing House:** a mirror-and-corridor funhouse that leaves you lighter than you went in.
- **The Sweetheart Float:** a slow boat-ride through a dim, flower-hung tunnel, for couples.
- **The Wonder Menagerie:** impossible, gentle beasts in willow cages.
- **The Fortune Tent:** a Cloven seer who tells true fortunes in exchange for small, unnoticed things.
- **The Ring-Toss and the Bright Wheel:** games whose prizes are a little too dear to the winner.
- **The Fireworks Rig:** the great third-night display everyone is waiting for, built high over the green.

### The Wrongness Under the Fun

Over three days, attentive players collect the dissonances:

- performers whose grace and chill do not fit a frontier fair;
- winners who cannot quite remember a name, a face, a year, after taking a prize;
- a barker, **Wren Sallow**, who will tell the plain truth to anyone who asks the right question, and is unbothered because no one ever does anything;
- the fair's warmth failing at the edges as the third night nears — frost where there should be spring mud;
- the wood beyond the green, where the burned Stump-Ring still smells of last spring's fire.

Each day lowers **Fair Warmth**. The party can spend the days investigating, and what they learn determines whether the third night is a reckoning or a rescue.

### Act I Output

The party knows the carnival is fey, that its warmth is running out, and that something in the wood was burned. Whether they yet understand the genuine guilt, Sisrin's morally flawed remedy, and the colder thing riding both depends on how they spent the days.

## Act II — The Debt and the Corruption

### The Grove-Debt

Investigation in the wood, at the Stump-Ring, and among the Season-Cutters reveals the [[The Grove-Debt]]: an offering grove burned by Karl's Firebrands, the wrong concealed, and the land's memory pressing for equivalence. Sisrin, approached as a wronged court person rather than dismissed as a monster, confirms it plainly. She is not hiding her claim and is proud of its exactness.

This is the hinge of the adventure. Once the party understands that the grove was wronged, "kill the fey" stops being a clean answer. Once they understand that years, names, and warmth are not just payment merely because the land accepts them, appeasement also stops being a clean answer.

### The Rider's Hand

A second layer surfaces at the Fortune Tent, in the frost at the fair's edges, and in Wren Sallow's franker moments: the collection has been widened. [[The Rime Rider]] has shifted the target from "the crews who burned the grove" to "the town that ate the profit," and set the third-night fires as the harvest. Sisrin took the Rider's guarantee and her retinue is bound by it; she wants the guilty to answer before the third night precisely because recognized repair would strip the Rider of its pretext.

- The party can find the **terms of the guarantee** — a thin-space bargain with a voidable overreach.
- They can find the **Rider's true nature** — a winter-hunt predator, not the wronged party, collecting for yield.
- They can find that **Sisrin regrets it**, which makes her a possible ally against the thing she invited in.

### The Guilty Who Will Not Admit It

Running parallel is the human obstacle. Old Tomas confesses that he knew and could not stop Karl. Karl denies everything and works the fair's "trouble" into a demand for more guards. Holger Strun, if reached, treats the debt as a negotiation to be lost slowly, and Mayor Ports has buried the rumor to protect the season's trade.

Raising **Cartel Admission** - evidence of the burning, Folk testimony, Tomas's confession, and public pressure on the green - makes a restorative solution possible. The party must still secure actual repair and bring Sisrin to accept it.

### Act II Output

The party understands the whole shape: a real wrong, an amoral land-pressure, a culpable court claimant, guilty humans who refuse repair, and a predator widening the crisis toward massacre. They have chosen to pursue repair, force admission, strip the Rider, void the guarantee, or prepare to survive the turn.

## Act III — The Third Night

### After the Fireworks

The great fireworks fire over the Two-Fires green, the whole town gathered to watch — exactly as the lure intended. If the debt is unpaid and the Rider intact when the last shell bursts, **Fair Warmth** hits 0 and the attractions turn.

### The Gauntlet of Turned Attractions

Each daytime delight becomes its lethal shadow, kept from the original as a gauntlet of set-pieces the party already walked through by day:

- **The Laughing House** becomes a maze that takes a year for every wrong turning and will not let the lightened leave.
- **The Sweetheart Float** runs cold and deep; the flowers are frost, and the tunnel drowns couples in the warmth they came to share.
- **The Wonder Menagerie** opens its willow cages, and the gentle beasts are [[The Rimebound]] now.
- **The Fortune Tent** collects the fortunes it told, calling in every small thing taken all week at once.
- **The Bright Wheel** turns into a thing that takes the warmth of everyone strapped to it.

The gauntlet is survivable and, crucially, *interruptible*: completing a recognized repair or breaking the Rider's hold can stop attractions mid-turn. The party is choosing, room by room, between fighting the horror and dismantling its causes.

### The Rider and the Alder-Maid

At the center, by the dying bonfires, the party reaches [[The Rime Rider]] and [[Sisrin the Alder-Maid]]. The confrontation resolves along whichever throughline the party built:

- **Complete the repair now:** if the guilty confess and commit restitution at the fires, and Sisrin accepts that work as sufficient equivalence, the grove is satisfied and she calls the collection off. The Rider loses its pretext and must withdraw or attack openly as what it is.
- **Void the bargain:** if the party has the guarantee's terms and a venue to file — Folk grove-court, thin-space rule, or the Rider's own overreach — Sisrin is freed to turn on the Rider beside them.
- **Strip the Rider by force:** defeating the Rider collapses the town-wide harvest to Sisrin's targeted claim. The town is spared, but the party may still need to restrain Sisrin, compel the guilty, or negotiate a defensible repair.

### If It Comes to Violence

A pure fight is the module's worst shape and remains playable: the Rider fights with cold, the Rimebound with their restraints burned out, and the turned attractions as terrain. But the fireworks-lit green is full of people, and killing the Rider alone neither repairs the grove nor resolves Sisrin's claim.

## Ways to Resolve the Conflict

### The Wrong Repaired

The guilty confess, fund restitution, restore the offering grove, and undertake bounded stewardship. Sisrin accepts this work rather than taking years, names, or warmth. She calls off the collection; the Rider, robbed of pretext and yield, withdraws or is destroyed as an open aggressor. This is the most restorative branch.

### The Cartel Broken Open

Pressure forces Karl's confession and Strun's restitution publicly, on the green, in front of both Folk and Church-date fairgoers. The cartel's provoke-and-profit machine is exposed; resources are committed to repair; frontier timber politics shift. This costs the party the cartel's goodwill for good.

### The Rider Cast Out, the Debt Remaining

The party strips the Rider by force or precedent but never makes the guilty repair the grove. The town survives the third night. Sisrin may take her cruel personal coin from Karl's crews, be restrained by the party, or withdraw with an unresolved grievance. The offering grove still waits to be re-seeded.

### The Grove Re-Seeded

Beyond stopping the massacre, the party undertakes the Folk rite to re-seat the grove - a seed-burial oath, an offering restored, long-term stewardship, and the Season-Cutters vouching. Sylvorn's law recognizes closure while human restitution answers the actual wrong. Ferren Hollow gains a real Folk-border peace instead of a mere appeasement.

### Survived, Not Solved

The party runs the gauntlet and saves who they can, but the breach remains unrepaired and the Rider escapes with a share of the town's warmth. Ferren Hollow is a smaller, colder place; the Rider has a new instrument and a taste for the Marches; the burned grove remains an open wound.

### The Rider Wins the Turn

The clean paths all failed and the fight came too late. The third night takes the town or most of it. The Cloven, appalled at what their collection became, may turn on the Rider too late to matter — a tragedy the module exists to make optional.

## Restoration and Release

The [[The Rimebound]] are not freed by being killed. Release means breaking the Rider's hold - voiding the guarantee, completing recognized repair, or destroying the Rider - after which a Cloven can return to Sisrin's retinue or leave the collection entirely. Cutting one down in the gauntlet stops a threat and frees no one.

The grove-debt is not cured by force alone. It ends when the territorial memory recognizes repair or when the place-law is permanently broken at greater ecological cost. Re-seating the grove needs a Folk seed-burial rite, a restored offering, concrete restitution, and the guilty party's participation. It turns a stopped massacre into a healed border without conceding that personal sacrifice was a righteous price.

## Ending States Summary

- **Best:** wrong confessed, Rider cast out, grove re-seeded, restitution secured, cartel's game exposed.
- **Good:** Rider destroyed and town saved, but the land-pressure lingers and an offering grove waits.
- **Costly:** town saved by force, cartel goodwill lost, Folk border still raw.
- **Tragic:** the turn came, the town paid in warmth, the Cloven grieve what they were made into.

## What the Adventure Changes

- Ferren Hollow learns the difference between a court claimant with a real grievance, a Menhir law that cannot judge justice, and the predator that came to feast - or fails to learn it and suffers the cost.
- The [[The Frontier Industry Lords|Timber-Cartel]]'s provoke-and-profit machine is exposed, reformed, or vindicated, depending on whether the burning is ever admitted.
- [[Sisrin the Alder-Maid]] becomes a satisfied claimant who leaves, a grove-court ally on the Folk border, or a bound instrument the party could not free from her own bargain.
- [[The Rime Rider]] becomes a destroyed aggressor, a driven-off predator nursing a grudge, or a cold power loose in the Marches with a new debt to buy.
- The [[The Cloven]] and [[The Rimebound]] enter the known bestiary of the Sylvan Marches as court persons shaped by land-law and the coerced forms they take when a colder hand overtakes their restraints.
- The offering-grove custom is re-established, quietly abandoned, or turned into a Folk-border flashpoint.
