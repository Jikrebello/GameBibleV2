# Sisrin the Alder-Maid

Sisrin the Alder-Maid is the surviving law of a burned offering grove, gathered into a face - a [[The Cloven|Cloven]] mistress of [[01 Canon Reference/Cosmology/30 Menhir/20 Third Circle/Sylvorn.md|Sylvorn]]'s land-court and the ringmistress of the carnival in [[The Bright-Fire Carnival]]. She is not the adventure's principal villain, but she is a full moral agent. The grove was genuinely wronged; her chosen remedy must still be judged on its own merits.

## What She Is

When "Clear-Cut" Karl's Firebrands burned her grove to hit a quota, the trees died but the grove's law did not. Under Sylvorn, a grove that old is a node of territorial memory, and its law persisted after its body — condensing, over a season, into Sisrin: the alder-stand wearing a woman's shape, cold at the edges, beautiful the way a hazard is beautiful. She remembers every footfall, oath, and fire the grove ever knew, because she is the part of it that remembers.

## The Collection

Sisrin announced her collection in a venue the frontier and the Faewild both recognize: a festival. She took over a real traveling carnival, staffed it with the grove's Cloven, and brought it to [[Ferren Hollow]] for Bright-Fire. Her intended claim was targeted rather than indiscriminate - frighten the town, take years, names, and warmth from the crews who did the burning, and leave when the [[The Grove-Debt|grove-response]] was satisfied. The proposal is bounded and truthful, but still cruel. Ecological equivalence does not make human identity and life-hours fair payment.

She is proud of the claim and states it plainly to anyone who addresses her in the grammar of the grove rather than dismissing her as a monster. She is more honest than the cartel and less predatory than the Rider. Her central error is believing that exact correspondence is the same thing as justice.

## The Mistake

She took a guarantee from [[The Rime Rider]]. It came to her as an ally of the wronged, offering to *ensure* the collection succeeded, and she accepted in the grove's name. What the Rider actually did was fasten itself to the grievance and inflate its coin — reclassifying the debt from the guilty crews to the whole town, and setting the third-night bonfires as a harvest. Faewild law binds Sisrin to the bargain she struck. She cannot simply call it off.

This is her tragedy and the party's opening: she wants the original offenders to answer before the third night, because a recognized repair would satisfy the grove and strip the Rider of the pretext it is riding. The party can meet that need without accepting her preferred personal coin.

## Character

Sisrin is composed, exact, and capable of both restraint and cruelty. She grieves the grove the way the grove would, seasonally and without forgetting. She despises Karl for the burning and the Rider for what it made her collection into, and she is ashamed, in a way she will not show, of having invited the second in to punish the first. Unlike Sylvorn, she possesses full moral agency and can learn to distinguish accountability from predatory payment.

## Negotiation

Sisrin can be worked with by anyone who takes the debt seriously:

- she will first demand the personal coin recognized by the grove, but can accept public admission, labor, seed, timber, compensation, oath, and restored offering as sufficient repair;
- she will help void the Rider's guarantee if shown a venue — a Folk grove-court, a thin-space filing, the Rider's own overreach;
- she will prize the re-seating of the grove above mere payment, and reward a party that pledges it;
- she will not ignore the crews' guilt, but can be persuaded that punishment need not consume their years, names, or warmth;
- if she refuses every defensible repair, the party may oppose her without denying that the grove was wronged.

She refuses to pretend the burning was harmless, and she refuses to let the town's comfort erase the grove's destruction. That refusal is sound; the equation she initially builds from it is not.

## Continuation

Sisrin can become a reconciled claimant who withdraws after concrete repair, a standing grove-court ally on the Folk border, a re-seated grove's renewed law if the party heals rather than merely settles, an embittered adversary if the wrong is denied, or - if the third night comes - a collector horrified at what her claim became, turning on the Rider too late.
