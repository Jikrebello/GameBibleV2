# Sisrin the Alder-Maid

Sisrin the Alder-Maid is the surviving law of a burned offering grove, gathered into a face — a [[The Cloven|Cloven]] mistress of [[Sylvorn]]'s land-court and the ringmistress of the carnival in [[The Bright-Fire Carnival]]. She is not the villain of the piece. She is the wronged party, running a lawful collection that a colder thing has stolen out from under her.

## What She Is

When "Clear-Cut" Karl's Firebrands burned her grove to hit a quota, the trees died but the grove's law did not. Under Sylvorn, a grove that old is a node of territorial memory, and its law persisted after its body — condensing, over a season, into Sisrin: the alder-stand wearing a woman's shape, cold at the edges, beautiful the way a hazard is beautiful. She remembers every footfall, oath, and fire the grove ever knew, because she is the part of it that remembers.

## The Collection

Sisrin filed a collection the only way the frontier and the Faewild both recognize: at a festival. She took over a real traveling carnival, staffed it with the grove's Cloven, and brought it to [[Ferren Hollow]] for Bright-Fire. Her intended claim was proportionate and targeted — frighten the town, take specific years, names, and warmth from the crews who did the burning, and leave with the [[The Grove-Debt|debt]] paid. Cruel by human measure; bounded by her law.

She is proud of the claim and will state it plainly to anyone who addresses her in the grammar of the debt rather than the grammar of monsters. She considers herself the most honest party at the fair, and she is not wrong.

## The Mistake

She took a guarantee from [[The Rime Rider]]. It came to her as an ally of the wronged, offering to *ensure* the collection succeeded, and she accepted in the grove's name. What the Rider actually did was fasten itself to the grievance and inflate its coin — reclassifying the debt from the guilty crews to the whole town, and setting the third-night bonfires as a harvest. Faewild law binds Sisrin to the bargain she struck. She cannot simply call it off.

This is her tragedy and the party's opening: she wants the guilty to pay the *original* debt before the third night, because payment would satisfy her claim and strip the Rider of the pretext it is riding.

## Character

Sisrin is composed, exact, and without human mercy, but not without justice — the distinction matters and she insists on it. She grieves the grove the way the grove would, seasonally and without forgetting. She despises Karl for the burning and the Rider for what it made her collection into, and she is ashamed, in a way she will not show, of having invited the second in to punish the first.

## Negotiation

Sisrin can be worked with by anyone who takes the debt seriously:

- she will accept payment of the original grove-debt from the guilty, in its coin, and consider her claim closed;
- she will help void the Rider's guarantee if shown a venue — a Folk grove-court, a thin-space filing, the Rider's own overreach;
- she will prize the re-seating of the grove above mere payment, and reward a party that pledges it;
- she will not spare the crews who burned the grove simply because sparing them is kind.

She refuses to pretend the debt is not owed, and she refuses to let the town's comfort outweigh the grove's death.

## Continuation

Sisrin can become a satisfied claimant who withdraws with the grove-debt paid, a standing grove-court ally on the Folk border, a re-seated grove's renewed law if the party heals rather than settles, or — if the third night comes — a collector horrified at what her claim became, turning on the Rider too late.
