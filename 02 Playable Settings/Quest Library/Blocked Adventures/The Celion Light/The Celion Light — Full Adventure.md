# The Celion Light — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** During Lantern Week, [[Celion Light Station]] goes dark and Halmenth's inspection cutter fails to return. An eight-vessel feast convoy is four hours from the outer reef. A Qazmari privateer landing party has taken the station and means to cast a false channel light, ground the convoy, and carry away its cargo and survivors.

**Beginning:** Halmenth raises the dark-beacon alarm. [[Captain Ivana Whale-Mark]] is the only local captain with a boat shallow and fast enough to reach Saint Naris's Tooth before the convoy. The party sails through false channel marks, concealed skiffs, and reef divers trying to disable their rudder.

**Middle:** The party lands on the occupied islet and retakes the four levels of the station. In the signal gallery they find [[Petar Reef-Braid]], the Saltborn pilot who sold the raiders their opening and is now being forced to finish a massacre he did not intend. At the top, [[Chief Keeper Milena Shore-Wake]] is holding the lantern chamber against the attackers.

**End:** The party descends into the flood caves beneath the station, rescues the twelve sailors of *Naris's Gull* and the captured keeper household, and confronts [[Captain Nadira bint Safwan]] before her boats launch from [[Black Fig Cove]]. The convoy must then be guided, warned, or rescued whether the sun-glass survives or not.

**Tier:** W coastal. The dangers are trained human opposition, a hostile reef, fire and glare inside a working lighthouse, rising water, hostages, and time.

**Expected length:** 2-3 substantial sessions: Halmenth and the crossing, the tower assault, then the flood caves and convoy reckoning.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; Captain Ivana Whale-Mark; Chief Keeper Milena Shore-Wake; compromised pilot Petar Reef-Braid; Captain Nadira bint Safwan; Luka Reef-Braid; the keeper household; and the twelve captured sailors of *Naris's Gull*.
- **What:** retake the station, save its people, prevent a false light from wrecking the feast convoy, and stop the Black-Cord boats from taking grounded ships and captives.
- **Where:** Halmenth, the reef passage six miles west of the Celion mouth, Saint Naris's Tooth, Celion Light Station, and Black Fig Cove beneath its western cliff.
- **Why the party acts:** the harbor bell calls every able defender; people aboard the convoy are known in Halmenth; Ivana needs fighters who can board a held station; or the party is promised a coast-defence share for saving the ships.
- **Why the crisis exists:** Petar sold practical intelligence to what he believed was a smuggling and naval-theft venture. Nadira used it to seize the lighthouse and prepare a civilian wrecking operation.
- **How it escalates:** the convoy closes upon the reef, the landing party finishes its false alignment, Nadira prepares her skiffs, and every delay gives the raiders more time to move or threaten captives.

## Moral and Metaphysical Ruling

The adventure's evil is ordinary and objective. Nadira intends to wreck civilian ships in darkness, let people drown, and enslave or ransom the survivors. Profit, reprisals, and professional pride explain the act without excusing it. Her company is a particular Qazmari force; it does not stand for every Qazmari sailor, household, believer, or ruler.

Petar is a guilty man under coercion, not an innocent captive and not Nadira's equal. He freely sold schedules, maintenance weaknesses, and reef marks because he wanted money and believed Halmenth had discarded his family after his brother died in service. He did not agree to mass killing. His refusal came late, but it came, and Nadira answered it by taking his nephew. Saving Luka explains how Petar can help stop the raid. It does not decide whether he should be forgiven, punished, or trusted afterward.

[[The Celion Sun-Glass]] is Atherian optical engineering. It magnifies a maintained flame, casts it through shaped glass and mirrored shutters, and can burn, blind, or show a false bearing when deliberately misaligned. It is not alive, does not route souls, and has no connection to patrons, the Undertow, idols, or the Left Hand.

Saint Naris matters because generations of Saltborn sailors have prayed, worked, married, and mourned around the beacons maintained in the saint's memory. The chapel is spiritually important because people use it faithfully, not because a secret metaphysical machine sits beneath it.

## The Central Truth

Captain Nadira bint Safwan commands the *Veiled Heron* and the [[The Black-Cord Company|Black-Cord Company]], a Qazmari privateer band accustomed to seizing disputed cargoes and fighting Ättar or Thalmyric crews along the western strait. A Qazmari maritime house gave her broad leave to take Thalmyric naval stores during the current round of unofficial reprisals. That leave neither ordered nor excuses what she has chosen at Halmenth.

Petar Reef-Braid approached one of Nadira's intermediaries months ago. His elder brother died when a coast-defence cutter foundered, and the delayed service payment left the family boat under levy and Luka's apprenticeship in jeopardy. Petar sold the Celion station's watch changes, supply day, tide door, and emergency channel marks. He told himself a smuggler would use the information to pass one cargo or steal naval stores without paying Halmenth's share.

Nadira saw a larger prize. Lantern Week brings the season's first concentrated convoy through one predictable channel. She landed twenty-eight people at [[Black Fig Cove]], surprised the keeper household, captured the inspection cutter *Naris's Gull*, and cut the station's ordinary signal. Chief Keeper Milena Shore-Wake and her youngest lamp-tender, Ana Salt-Braid, reached the lantern chamber and barred its iron door. One keeper died at the tide stair. Two more were taken into the caves with Luka and the cutter crew.

Petar tried to sabotage the alignment when Nadira told him the true plan. She put Luka among the cave prisoners and placed a signal-master over Petar in the gallery. Petar can finish the false bearing, delay it, or reverse it if given an opening. Milena keeps the main lamp dark from above, forcing the raiders to smoke her out before they can cast the false channel at full strength.

The *Veiled Heron* waits beyond the reef with its sailing crew. Nadira remains ashore because the operation depends upon exact timing and because she no longer trusts Petar. Once the convoy grounds, Black-Cord skiffs will leave Black Fig Cove, board the damaged vessels, and tow prizes or captives to the privateer ship before Kedrava can gather a response.

## The Raid in Numbers

The Black-Cord landing party contains twenty-eight people, separate from the sailing crew aboard the *Veiled Heron*.

- **Eight station guards** hold the tide door, stores, keeper hall, and exterior paths.
- **Six signal saboteurs and deck-fighters** occupy the gallery and upper stairs.
- **Eight cave guards and boat crews** hold the prisoners and ready four narrow skiffs.
- **Four reef divers** maintain false buoys, watch the approaches, and attack rudders.
- **Nadira and her signal-master** move between the caves and tower as the clock changes.

The approaching convoy contains eight vessels and roughly 160 people. It is a mixed group of fishing craft, two pilgrim ferries, three cargo carriers, and a coastal packet. None is a warship. The inspection cutter *Naris's Gull* has twelve sailors, all alive at the start and imprisoned in the flood caves.

## Adventure States

### Convoy Approach: 4 to 0

- **4 - The alarm:** the convoy is beyond the outer haze. Halmenth has about four hours.
- **3 - The departure:** the party leaves Halmenth. The convoy cannot yet see the station.
- **2 - The landfall:** the convoy's lookouts begin searching for the Celion light.
- **1 - The false-bearing window:** unless stopped, the raiders force the lamp and cast the wrong channel.
- **0 - The reef:** the leading ships commit. A true signal guides them; a false signal grounds them; darkness forces desperate seamanship and immediate rescue.

Advance the clock at the default transitions above, after any long rest, after a full retreat to Halmenth, or when the party spends substantial time on a failed approach. An exceptionally swift or clever solution can preserve a step.

### Raider Readiness: 0 to 3

- **0 - Routine watch:** the landing party believes Halmenth has not understood the seizure.
- **1 - Reinforced:** the tide door and gallery receive additional guards.
- **2 - Accelerated:** Petar is forced to complete the alignment; Nadira moves the prisoners deeper into the caves.
- **3 - Launching:** skiffs leave Black Fig Cove, captives become shields, and the *Veiled Heron* closes toward the reef.

Loud fighting, escaped sentries, visible approach, or a failed bluff raises Readiness. Silencing the false buoy team, cutting the cave signal line, or feeding Nadira false information can lower or hold it.

### Beacon State

- **Dark:** Milena is holding the lamp dark; ships receive no safe bearing.
- **False:** the outer shutters cast a plausible but lethal channel toward the Teeth.
- **True:** the lamp and shutters show the proper Celion passage.
- **Destroyed:** the optic cannot guide anyone; the party must improvise a living signal or reach the convoy.

### Lives at Risk

Track four groups plainly: Milena and Ana in the lantern chamber; Petar in the gallery; Luka, two keepers, and twelve cutter sailors in the caves; and the 160 people aboard the convoy. A group can be safe, threatened, wounded, captured, or lost. Do not turn rescue into an abstract score.

## Act I - The Dark Beacon

### The Missing Sweeps

At dusk during Lantern Week, Celion Light misses its first three sweeps. Halmenth's harbor bell sounds the dark-beacon pattern: three strokes, a pause, and three again. The inspection cutter *Naris's Gull* leaves immediately and does not return. No answering lamp appears from Saint Naris's Tooth.

The harbor knows what this means without a lecture. The feast convoy is coming from the west. Without Celion Light it must either risk the reef in darkness, scatter into open water, or turn back toward weather it has already outrun.

Useful facts can be gathered quickly:

- the light was supplied two days ago and should not lack fuel;
- Petar Reef-Braid missed his harbor watch and has not been seen since morning;
- an unfamiliar lateen-rigged ship was reported west of the reef before the haze closed;
- *Naris's Gull* carried twelve and draws too much water to use the Tooth's western cleft;
- only a shallow Saltborn pilot boat can cross the inner reef without the true light.

### Captain Ivana

[[Captain Ivana Whale-Mark]] is already stripping unnecessary weight from the *Little Wake* when the party reaches her. She does not need persuasion to sail. She needs people who can climb, fight in narrow rooms, and obey a pilot when the reef is louder than conversation.

She gives the practical brief:

1. get a signal onto the Tooth;
2. find the cutter if it still floats;
3. do not chase the privateer ship into open water while the convoy remains blind.

The party may receive a coast-defence warrant, promise of a salvage share, or simple public thanks, but no document causes the adventure. Ships full of people are approaching a dark reef.

### Leaving Late

If the party misses Ivana's departure or loses access to the *Little Wake*, a fishing family provides a slower open boat. Advance Convoy Approach once and begin Act II with wet supplies and Raider Readiness 1. The adventure continues.

### Act I Output

The party knows the light was deliberately interrupted, Petar is missing, and a western ship may be involved. They leave Halmenth aboard a craft able to reach Saint Naris's Tooth.

## Act II - The Reef Run

### The False Outer Mark

The first danger is a channel buoy burning where no Celion mark should be. It is a Black-Cord decoy anchored above a spur of reef. Following it grounds the *Little Wake*; recognizing or testing it reveals tool marks, Qazmari cordage, and a weighted line running toward two hidden divers.

The party can cut the buoy, capture a diver, leave it as a false sign that their boat was fooled, or use its line to find the raiders' seaward route. A noisy confrontation raises Raider Readiness. Capturing a diver reveals that a landing party holds both the lighthouse and a cave on its western face.

### The Rudder Attack

Near the Tooth, two reef divers try to foul or cut Ivana's steering oar while a concealed skiff looses missiles from behind a rock shelf. This is a moving maritime problem, not an underwater monster fight. The party can protect the rudder, board the skiff, force it onto the reef, or let Ivana draw it away while swimmers reach the islet.

If the *Little Wake* is disabled, it drifts into a lee pool rather than sinking immediately. Ivana remains able to help with the finale if the party protects the hull or later recovers a Black-Cord skiff.

### Choosing the Landing

The station offers four entries:

- **The Tide Door:** the eastern service quay and fastest route, watched by station guards behind keeper barricades turned outward.
- **The Windward Climb:** an exposed ascent to the keeper-hall level; difficult in spray but lightly watched.
- **The Maintenance Ladder:** an iron ladder and narrow outer walk leading toward the signal gallery; visible from the tower but able to bypass the lower floors.
- **Black Fig Cove:** the hidden western cave used by the raiders. It reaches the prisoners and skiffs first, but only at favorable tide or by a dangerous swim.

Ivana can put the party near any entry but cannot hold the *Little Wake* safely under missile fire forever.

### Act II Output

The party reaches Saint Naris's Tooth, understands that trained privateers hold it, and chooses whether to strike the tower, infiltrate above, or enter through the raiders' own cave anchorage.

## Act III - The Tower

The four levels are a practical working station. Every room should reveal how the keepers live and how the raiders are misusing their work.

### Level One - Tide Door and Stores

The tide door opens from the eastern quay into wet stores, a repair bench, fuel vault, and the lower stair. Black-Cord guards have turned keeper tables into cover and hung a bell-line to the gallery. One wounded keeper, Daro Gull-Mark, is hidden behind a false cask wall after escaping the first sweep.

Useful discoveries:

- the keeper household was surprised from the western caves, not the quay;
- the raiders have taken signal oil but left food and ordinary valuables;
- a concealed stair behind the repair bench descends toward the flood caves;
- Petar's own channel annotations are pinned beside Qazmari orders.

Silencing the bell keeps Readiness from rising. Freeing Daro identifies Milena's emergency destruction lever in the lantern chamber.

### Level Two - Keeper Hall and Naris Chapel

This floor contains the household kitchen, bunks, chart room, and a low chapel to Saint Naris. The raiders use the hall as a ready room. They have moved icons and mourning books aside to keep them out of lamp oil, not staged a theatrical desecration. Their crime does not need invented sacrilege to be evil.

The chart room shows the true and false channels. A party that studies it can later signal the convoy without Petar. The unreturned-ships register names people now aboard the approaching vessels, making the stakes local rather than numerical.

The chapel has an exterior storm shutter opening onto the windward climb. A captured raider or recovered order gives Nadira's name and the phrase "Black Fig," but not yet the cove's location.

### Level Three - Signal Gallery and Counterweights

Petar is here under the eye of Nadira's signal-master and several saboteurs. The gallery controls the outer mirror shutters, rotation brake, fog bell, and emergency color screens. Petar has delayed the alignment by introducing small errors that appear plausible to non-pilots.

He admits enough immediately to save time: the false bearing points ships toward the Teeth; Luka is alive in the caves; Milena still controls the lamp above. He does not volunteer that he sold the schedules until confronted with his annotations or Luka confirms it.

Possible resolutions:

- defeat or deceive the signal crew and free Petar;
- make Petar complete a harmless alignment while feeding the raiders a false readiness report;
- have Petar reverse the shutters and show the true channel once Milena relights the lamp;
- bypass him and use the chart-room marks, accepting a harder alignment;
- leave him bound while the party secures the lantern chamber, preserving the rescue without deciding his judgement.

If Readiness reaches 3 here, Nadira orders the signal-master to ignite smoke below the lantern door and launches the first cave skiffs.

### Level Four - Lantern Chamber

Milena and the young lamp-tender Ana Salt-Braid are barricaded behind the iron lantern door. They have survived on the chamber's water jar and emergency bread while Milena works the manual brake that keeps the raiders' outer mirrors from settling.

The final tower confrontation happens on narrow iron walks around heat, glass, turning gears, and open drops. Saboteurs try to force the door with smoke and oil. Careless attacks can crack the lens, ignite the gallery, or blind everyone facing the beam.

Once relieved, Milena offers three immediate choices:

- **restore the true light,** which needs the gallery secured and either Petar or the chart-room alignment;
- **keep the lamp dark,** preventing a false signal while the party warns the convoy another way;
- **break the optic,** denying it to the raiders permanently but forcing an improvised signal.

Milena will destroy the lens if the raiders are about to take it. She will not do so merely to preserve pride or punish Petar.

### Act III Output

The party controls or has denied the beacon, knows Petar's part in the seizure, and has rescued Milena and Ana. The remaining captives and Nadira are below, while the convoy continues toward the reef.

## Act IV - The Flood Caves

### The Keeper Stair

The concealed stair from the lower stores descends through old Atherian masonry into natural sea caves. At high tide its bottom steps are submerged; at low tide they open onto slick ledges above Black Fig Cove. Raiders use a hauled rope and shuttered lamp to signal the *Veiled Heron*.

Cutting the signal line delays Nadira's escape and prevents the offshore ship from knowing whether the false light succeeded.

### The Prison Ledge

Luka, two captured keepers, and the twelve sailors of *Naris's Gull* are held on a dry ledge behind a cargo net and two guards. They have been forced to repair skiffs, sort boarding lines, and prepare salvage tackle. Luka has loosened one ringbolt but cannot free the group before the rising tide covers the lower path.

Freeing the prisoners provides:

- twelve exhausted but capable sailors who can crew recovered skiffs;
- Luka's account of Petar's initial bargain and later refusal;
- the inspection cutter, moored deeper in the cove with a damaged mast but sound hull;
- knowledge of Nadira's escape route and the location of her signal horn.

If Readiness is 3, Nadira divides the prisoners among launching boats. The rescue becomes a moving fight across ledges and skiffs rather than an execution scene.

### Black Fig Cove

[[Black Fig Cove]] is a narrow western cleft open to the sea through a low arch. Four Black-Cord skiffs wait here with boarding hooks, weighted nets, and empty irons. The *Veiled Heron* is visible beyond the reef whenever the haze parts.

The cove can be flooded by releasing an old storm gate, blocked by sinking a skiff beneath the arch, or used to launch the freed cutter crew toward the convoy. Every choice changes the final problem:

- blocking it traps Nadira but removes the fastest rescue boats;
- flooding it scatters raiders and prisoners alike;
- preserving it gives the party boats but leaves Nadira an escape route.

### Captain Nadira

Nadira holds the inner landing with the cave crews unless earlier events draw her into the tower. She fights to preserve an exit, not to die in a lost lighthouse. When the beacon changes or the prisoners are freed, she tries to reach a skiff, signal the *Veiled Heron*, and carry at least one valuable hostage or the station charts with her.

She may bargain for passage by releasing captives or revealing the convoy's exact approach. She keeps bargains that secure an immediate exchange because professional reputation matters to her, but she does not repent and will exploit any wording or delay. Accepting a useful surrender is not the same as trusting her.

Nadira can be captured, killed, stranded, or allowed to escape. The convoy's safety remains the primary objective; chasing her into open water while ships enter the reef is the mistake she hopes the party will make.

### Saving the Convoy

At Convoy Approach 0, resolve the ships according to the Beacon State:

- **True:** the lead pilot sees the proper Celion passage. The convoy clears the reef unless raider skiffs remain able to attack.
- **Dark:** the ships shorten sail and begin to scatter. Ivana, Petar, the freed cutter, signal rockets, bells, or multiple shore fires can guide them, but at least one vessel risks grounding.
- **False:** the lead vessels commit toward the Teeth. The party must reverse or extinguish the light, race out in skiffs, or turn the lead ship before the convoy follows.
- **Destroyed:** no fixed signal is possible. A line of living lamps on the cliff, burning a recovered skiff in the true channel, the *Little Wake*'s mast light, or direct pilotage can still save the ships.

A partial grounding changes the climax into rescue. People in cold water, broken hulls, raider boats, and a falling tide matter more than whether Nadira remains within sword reach.

### Act IV Output

The prisoners are rescued, lost, or carried away; Nadira is stopped or escapes; and the feast convoy clears, grounds upon, or is taken beyond the reef. Petar's fate remains a separate decision after the immediate danger.

## Adversary Tactics

### Black-Cord Deck-Fighters

- fight in pairs with short spears, hooked blades, and bucklers suited to decks and stairs;
- pull opponents off balance rather than forming heavy infantry lines;
- fall back through prepared narrow points instead of holding rooms to the death;
- surrender when isolated if they believe Nadira cannot see them do it.

### Reef Divers

- attack rudders, anchor lines, and swimmers rather than trading blows underwater;
- surface behind cover, use weighted knives, and retreat through reef gaps;
- carry no supernatural breathing aid; exhaustion and cold apply to them too.

### Signal Saboteurs

- use smoke, lamp oil, shutter brakes, mirrors, and the station's height as weapons;
- try to crack or seize the optic if they cannot hold the gallery;
- threaten Petar and Luka as leverage but do not possess magical control over either.

### Nadira

- keeps mobile between cave ledges and boats;
- uses hostages to buy movement, not as disposable scenery;
- orders disciplined withdrawal once the convoy is clearly safe;
- signals the *Veiled Heron* and escapes if the party abandons the reef to pursue her.

## Fail-Forward Results

- **Delayed departure:** use a slower fishing boat, advance the convoy, and begin with alerted raiders.
- **The *Little Wake* disabled:** it reaches a lee pool; the party swims, boards a skiff, or climbs while Ivana repairs enough to return later.
- **Tower assault repelled:** the party can enter through Black Fig Cove, negotiate for captives, or wait for a tide route; the false light activates sooner.
- **Petar killed or unavailable:** the chart room and Milena together can align the beacon, but it takes time.
- **Milena lost:** Petar or Ana can work the lamp with the recovered station book; destroying it remains possible.
- **Sun-glass destroyed:** living lights, bells, boats, and direct pilotage replace it.
- **Convoy partly grounded:** the adventure becomes a reef rescue and boarding defence rather than an automatic failure.
- **Nadira escapes:** the people and ships can still be saved; she becomes a named western-strait enemy rather than invalidating the victory.

## Petar's Judgement

Stopping the raid determines whether people live. It does not automatically settle Petar's guilt.

- **Confession and restitution:** Petar admits the sale publicly, gives up his pilot share, helps recover the grounded ships, and submits to Halmenth's court. Saving the convoy weighs in his favor but does not erase the betrayal.
- **Public trial:** the party delivers Petar and evidence without promising an outcome. Luka may testify to both the initial secrecy and Petar's later resistance.
- **Concealed guilt:** the party lets Halmenth believe Petar was only a captive. This preserves a skilled pilot and a family but gives him a secret others can later weaponize.
- **Death:** Petar may die stopping the false light or saving Luka. Sacrifice proves what he chose at the end; it does not make the original sale harmless.
- **Escape:** Petar flees before judgement, possibly with Luka or aboard a recovered skiff. He remains useful, guilty, and afraid of both coasts.

## Ending States

### Clear Light

The true Celion beam guides all eight ships through. The captives are freed and Black Fig Cove is denied to the raiders. Nadira may still escape, but the operation fails completely and Halmenth sees the station working when it matters most.

### Broken Light and Living Signal

The sun-glass is destroyed or unusable. Ivana, the keepers, the freed cutter crew, and the party build a line of fires, bells, boats, or signal rockets that brings the convoy home. The station is crippled, but the people who gave it meaning replace it for one night.

### Reef Rescue

One or more ships ground before the false bearing is stopped. The party prevents a mass loss of life, fights off boarding skiffs, and gets survivors ashore. Halmenth mourns and rebuilds; the result is costly without being a failed adventure.

### Black Channel

The false light holds long enough for multiple ships to ground, and Nadira's boats take cargo or captives to the *Veiled Heron*. Survivors remain to rescue and the station can still be retaken. The result opens a pursuit, ransom, or reprisal adventure rather than ending the campaign.

No ending is the authorial continuation. The station's seizure and the people involved are local canon; player outcomes remain open.

## What the Adventure Changes

- Celion Light Station remains active, is rebuilt, or stands blind until a replacement optic is made.
- Halmenth strengthens sensible coast watches or lashes out indiscriminately against western-strait sailors.
- Captain Nadira becomes a prisoner, a corpse, a disgraced captain, or a recurring privateer enemy.
- Petar becomes a confessing offender, a concealed asset, a dead rescuer, or a fugitive.
- Ivana and Milena become reliable Saltborn allies whose authority comes from competent service rather than court rank.
- The Black-Cord Company is broken, depleted, or emboldened by prizes taken through the Black Channel.

## GM Quick Reference

**Immediate objective:** prevent the false light and save the convoy.

**Hidden truth:** Petar knowingly sold the access information but resisted when he learned Nadira intended mass civilian wrecking.

**Primary locations:** Halmenth harbor, the reef approach, Celion Light Station's four levels, the keeper stair, and Black Fig Cove.

**Primary clock:** Convoy Approach 4 to 0.

**Primary pressure:** every loud or slow move lets the raiders reinforce, relocate prisoners, or launch boats.

**Opposition:** human deck-fighters, reef divers, signal saboteurs, and Nadira. No creature dossier or hidden supernatural faction is required.

**Best practical result:** captives freed, true or improvised signal established, convoy safe, and Nadira denied both prizes and hostages.
