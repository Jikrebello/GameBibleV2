# Captain Nadira bint Safwan

Captain Nadira bint Safwan commands the *Veiled Heron* and the [[The Black-Cord Company|Black-Cord Company]]. She is the principal antagonist of [[The Celion Light — Full Adventure]]: a disciplined Qazmari privateer who chooses to turn a limited reprisal commission into the deliberate wrecking of a civilian convoy.

## Background

Nadira comes from a minor Qazmari maritime house whose fortunes depend upon strait tolls, armed escort, and prizes taken from enemies. She earned command by bringing crews and cargo home through Ättar attacks and by taking two Thalmyric naval store ships without losing a boarding party. Her adopted ship-kin matter more to her than court favor, although she uses the Caliph-Wasi's rivalry with Thalmyria whenever it broadens her room to act.

Her privateering leave permits attacks upon Thalmyric naval stores during a season of undeclared reprisals. It does not authorize wrecking pilgrims, enslaving civilians, or attacking Halmenth's beacon. Nadira knows the distinction and plans to return with enough wealth that her sponsor will prefer to praise the result rather than inspect the method.

## The Celion Operation

Petar Reef-Braid's information gave Nadira a rare opportunity: a predictable convoy, an undermanned station, a concealed cave, and a channel that could be made lethal by moving light rather than fighting a fleet. She landed twenty-eight people at [[Black Fig Cove]], seized the inspection cutter, and forced Petar to complete the alignment.

The operation is not an invasion. Nadira wants a fast harvest of ships, cargo, ransom-worthy passengers, and captives before Kedrava can assemble coastal forces. She remains ashore because she distrusts Petar and because the first grounded ship will decide whether the raid becomes a triumph or a stranded landing party.

## Character

Nadira is controlled, observant, and proud of solving maritime problems with preparation. She does not shout when a quiet order will do, and she does not mistake cruelty for discipline. This makes her decision at Celion worse, not better: she understands exactly what the false light will do.

She distinguishes her own ship-kin from everyone outside them. A Black-Cord sailor is owed rescue, fair shares, and an honored burial. A foreign crew is a prize if law and opportunity can be made to agree. Her failure is not lack of moral vocabulary but the boundary she draws around whom it protects.

## Levers

- **The convoy over the captain:** if the party chooses saving ships over pursuing her, Nadira can escape but loses the prize she came for.
- **The landing party:** proof that the *Veiled Heron* is withdrawing or that Black-Cord escape is cut off can force a surrender.
- **Professional reputation:** she keeps immediate exchanges of named captives because no future negotiation survives a captain known to murder after payment.
- **Her commission:** exposing its narrow terms can deny her shelter at home, though it does not stop the present raid by itself.
- **Petar's errors:** she knows he has delayed the alignment and will abandon or kill him once the false light is secure.

## In Conflict

Nadira fights from cave ledges, boats, and narrow approaches. She uses a hooked boarding blade, short bow, smoke, and prepared retreat routes. She tries to separate a pursuer from the convoy crisis, signal the *Veiled Heron*, and leave with a hostage or station chart if the main prize is lost.

She does not fight to the death for an empty cove. If captured cleanly, she surrenders as a captain and expects confinement, exchange, or execution according to what Halmenth proves. If cornered while a boat remains, she takes the boat.

## Possible Continuations

Nadira can become a Qazmari prisoner whose trial strains western-strait relations, a disgraced captain denied harbor at home, a recurring privateer with a personal defeat to answer, or a dead raider whose sponsor insists she exceeded every order.
