# Magister Edrik Morn

Magister Edrik Morn is the principal antagonist of [[The Dead Press Upward — Full Adventure]]. He is a former diocesan antiquarian and death-law scholar who deliberately broke [[The Seal of the Shut Mouth]] in an attempt to inherit the Boatman's office. He now calls himself **the Second Boatman**.

## Before Wanemoor

Edrik trained in burial law, disputed relic custody, plague registers, and the authentication of old Church property. He was clever, patient, and useful in cases where a shrine's legend had become tangled with land title. His access to real funerary records convinced him that institutions preserved only fragments of an older practical science.

His error hardened into doctrine: if burial rites helped the soul road, then the Boatman must be a technician rather than a servant under divine authority. Edrik began treating names, corpses, consecration, and grief as components through which a sufficiently informed mortal could reproduce the office.

The diocese revoked his seal after he opened a sealed ossuary without witnesses and attempted to make a corpse answer questions through its residue. He retained copies of surveys, rites, and legal marks.

## The Last Lamp Experiment

Edrik learned that Bruch Mine had encountered protected burial ground beneath [[Abbey of the Last Lamp|the Abbey of the Last Lamp]]. He sold [[Helene Bruch]] a false survey declaring the relevant galleries collapsed, then used the mine's access after work hours.

He found the Seal, misunderstood its annual closing rite as an investiture, and cut the central name-stone. The released force crushed and burned away his lower body. Grave-script and residue kept his remaining body alive long enough for him to fix himself into a pale iron bier-chair.

The chair moves on four jointed pallbearer legs and hangs from chains in the Seal chamber. Tubes feed Edrik water and bitter medicine. He is not an ancient undead lord. He is a dying human being using necromancy to refuse the judgement whose office he claims.

## Public Face

Edrik speaks through residue corpses with quiet ceremonial authority. He calls living people "passengers," graves "waiting rooms," and the Seal chamber "the second quay." He presents the rising as a demonstration that Wanemoor's abandoned dead have accepted his jurisdiction.

When speaking in person, pain and exhaustion break through his composure. He then makes the bier-chair speak for him or directs dead mouths to repeat his claim.

## Private Truth

Edrik cannot perceive souls. He sees residue, physical remains, ritual marks, and damage along a threshold. Every experiment has confirmed that something essential leaves his reach at death.

He hides this from followers and from himself. His final project is not merely power; it is an attempt to make the world answer before his own failing body forces him to face the truth.

## Goals

- direct enough residue to prove that Wanemoor must recognize his office;
- widen the Maw-Road into a permanent controlled threshold;
- recover every name-tablet so no contradictory burial authority remains;
- outlast or subjugate [[The Wane-Warren]];
- prevent anyone from distinguishing moved remains from routed souls.

## Resources

- the bier-chair and grave-script apparatus;
- six corpse-masks through which he observes and directs dead;
- knowledge of the abbey routes and closing rite;
- residue dead marked as pallbearers, guards, and messengers;
- the ability to contaminate the Seal's channels;
- Helene's false survey and enough records to implicate the mine council.

## Vulnerabilities

- destroying or covering corpse-masks blinds his remote control;
- speaking the communal clause for the unnamed breaks his claim that lost names create ownerless dead;
- restoring any Seal channel weakens his grave-script;
- his living body remains dependent on the chair;
- he cannot answer a direct request to identify one soul he has actually judged;
- he fears release from the machine more than physical pain.

## Encounter Behavior

Edrik watches the party through dead eyes and adapts only to tactics he actually observes. Each disabled mask removes one prepared counter or directed group from the finale. He uses corpses to divide the party, foul the Seal, and attack the rite-keeper rather than standing still to exchange attacks.

When defeated, he may attempt to drag the chair into the Maw-Road, demand recognition in exchange for closing instructions, or finally ask whether the Boatman will receive him. None of these requires forgiveness or survival.
