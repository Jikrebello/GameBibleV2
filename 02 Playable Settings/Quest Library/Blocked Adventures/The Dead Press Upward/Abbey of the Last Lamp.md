# Abbey of the Last Lamp

The Abbey of the Last Lamp is a dissolved Brauvalian burial house on the ridge east of [[Wanenbruck]]. Its ruined upper buildings, Grey Sweat pauper galleries, and [[The Seal of the Shut Mouth|ancient Seal]] form the principal delve in [[The Dead Press Upward — Full Adventure]].

## Foundation

The abbey was founded in 1028 AR after early Brauvalian clergy discovered a ghoul passage beneath an older funerary quarry. The brothers closed the passage, built a cemetery above it, and made an annual office of naming, lamp vigil, and physical inspection part of the Seal's maintenance.

The house never became rich. It buried strangers, road dead, mine laborers, plague victims, and people whose parishes could not pay. Its title, **the Last Lamp**, referred to the light left burning after a funeral household departed.

## Grey Sweat Galleries

During [[The Grey Sweat]], the abbey received hundreds of dead from Wanenbruck and surrounding hamlets. New wall niches and communal galleries were cut quickly but recorded carefully. Known names were pressed into clay tablets. Fifty-three unidentified dead were represented by blank plaques and commended together rather than treated as unowned.

The plague overwhelmed the house. Later debts, the Reformation, and declining numbers ended its independent office. The abbey was formally dissolved, but burial custody passed to Wanenbruck's sextons. Its ground never became ordinary leasehold.

## Present Condition

The surface church has lost its roof, the bell loft leans, and timber crews have taken reusable outbuildings. The cloister path, keeper's room, undercroft stair, and cemetery wall remain. Locals tend the lower graves but avoid the upper ridge after dark.

Bruch Mine approached from the east. Its adit crossed warning stones and entered the plague galleries through a mortared wall. [[Magister Edrik Morn]] used that route to break the Seal. [[The Wane-Warren]] then entered from below through the reopened Maw-Road.

## The Four Entrances

- **Abbey gate:** broad, visible, and easy to defend; passes through the cloister.
- **Cemetery sink:** reaches the upper plague galleries through unstable graves.
- **East mine adit:** suitable for tools, stretchers, and collapse work; contains the clearest evidence of the trespass.
- **Ruined cloister roof:** reaches the keeper stair and Edrik's first corpse-mask without crossing the main undercroft.

Every route has a purpose. The site should not contain corridors that exist only to spend encounter time.

## Major Zones

### Cloister of the Last Lamp

Residue shades repeat the abbey's final funeral procession. Following its direction reveals the order in which lamp, water, ash, and names approached the Seal.

### East Adit

The mine breach contains trapped workers, cut grave markers, false survey marks, and the remains of the two workers recorded as deserters.

### Grey Sweat Gallery

The burial register and clay name-tablets are stored among opened niches. Ghouls attack here to take bodies and destroy the records that make a complete rite possible.

### Wane-Warren Cut

Raw feeding tunnels intersect the human galleries. Closing this cut limits the number of ghouls able to reach the Seal chamber.

### Hall of Unnamed Marks

The 53 blank plaques establish that loss of a name does not make a person ownerless. Its communal clause directly refutes Edrik's doctrine.

### Seal Chamber

The lowest human chamber crosses the Maw-Road. Edrik's grave-script, the broken Seal, and the Wane-Warren's ascent converge here.

## Hazards

- unstable burial walls and mine shoring;
- narrow corpse chutes used by ghouls;
- grave-script that lets Edrik see through dead masks;
- residue processions triggered by reversed or interrupted rites;
- disease from opened plague galleries and ghoul contamination;
- flooding in the lowest quarry channels after heavy rain.

## What the Site Is Not

The abbey is not a secret Left Hand complex, a Bank vault, or a machine built to trap souls. Its ward joined honest ritual, named burial, maintained stonework, and a bounded sacred office. The crisis exists because people neglected and deliberately violated those duties.
