# Magister Othmar Veyl

Othmar Veyl was Hellerbruck's resident magus, an authority on reflective court-fauna and the murdered scholar whose estate drives [[The Drake's Demand — Full Adventure]].

## Work

Othmar studied gold-court molts, memory retained in drake law-gems, reflective Faewild instruments, and the relationship between repeated sensation and territorial behavior. His most important finding was that Dame Sabine's trophy scales and recent molts belonged to the same drake.

He also demonstrated that exceptional age can organize a drake's accumulated law-gem memory into language and stable selfhood. This work underlies the elder-threshold ruling in [[Drakes Overview]].

## Fault

Othmar confined court-fauna inside the [[House of Veyl]], exposed them to controlled glare, and treated distress as useful data. He stopped several experiments and began arranging transfers only after recognizing persistent memory in his subjects. His change came late and did not erase the suffering he caused.

The menagerie is dangerous because he believed that being correct about a threat excused keeping too much danger under personal control.

## Murder

Othmar learned that Sovarath had returned and was assembling a mortal court. He hid the [[The Meridian Mirror|Meridian Mirror]], copied Sabine's final journal, and attempted to warn [[Baroness Margarethe Heller]].

The [[The Bright-Tail Host|Bright-Tail Host]] intercepted his message. Kezra Seven-Rings led the search of his lower tunnels, and loyalist Giltlings killed Othmar after he sealed the Mirror away. The same tunnelling destabilized the founder's tower.

## Surviving Legacy

Othmar leaves no perfect weapon. He leaves evidence and procedures:

- molt sequences proving Sovarath's identity and elder state;
- two fragments of the First Heat-Name;
- the three alignments of the Meridian Mirror;
- a method for closing the Noon Window;
- maps of the high quarry and servant fissures;
- records exposing both the danger outside and the cruelty inside his house.

Using his knowledge does not require defending his conduct. Compensation to his servants and responsible release of the menagerie are part of settling the estate.
