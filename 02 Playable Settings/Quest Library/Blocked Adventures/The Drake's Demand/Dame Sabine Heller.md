# Dame Sabine Heller

Dame Sabine Heller was the marcher captain and bridge founder for whom [[Hellerbruck]] is named. In 1117 AR, she led the hunt against the gold drake now called [[Sovarath-of-the-Open-Noon]].

## The Hunt

The young Sovarath had killed quarry workers, livestock, and river travelers while extending its territory down the Weiss ridge. Sabine's decision to hunt it was a legitimate defense of nearby settlements. She used a consecrated boar-spear, a sun-notched shield, mirror cloths, and quarry charges to drive the animal back into its nesting court.

Sabine pierced the chest and survived the cave fall that buried Sovarath. Blood, stillness, and the destruction of the visible nest convinced her that it had died. She removed the [[The Meridian Mirror|Meridian Mirror]], several scales, and a gilded court crest as trophies.

## The Founding

Sabine established a bridge fort and market beneath the cleared ridge. She divided public obligations among twelve households and trades so that no single noble family could abandon the crossing. The later town converted this arrangement into the Twelve Charters.

Her error benefited the town, but it was not a deliberate fraud. She possessed neither the later doctrine of drake law-gems nor a reliable way to reach the lower court beneath the collapse.

## Final Doubt

In old age, Sabine found warm scale-dust inside a sealed trophy chest and heard a slow pulse beneath the founder crypt during high summer. Her private journal ordered her heirs to inspect the ridge and return nonessential court objects if the animal survived.

She died during a winter fever before the inspection. Her heir sealed the journal with her arms, interpreting it as fear and failing to act. Public legend therefore remained simpler than Sabine's own final position.

## Legacy

Sabine is neither a spotless saint nor the architect of a conscious lie. The town may honor her defense and civic charter while correcting the history of the nest. Her spear and shield can help kill Sovarath because they carry the physical and ritual memory of the first wound, not because inheritance makes the Heller family morally pure.
