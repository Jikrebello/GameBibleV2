# The Drake's Demand — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** The party reaches [[Hellerbruck]] as the last tower of its founder's castle collapses into the market quarter. The wreckage exposes dead [[Giltlings]], a newly opened tunnel system, and the murder of local magus [[Magister Othmar Veyl]]. The investigation eventually reveals that the gold drake Dame Sabine Heller supposedly killed at the town's founding survived. Centuries of accumulated heart-gem memory have made [[Sovarath-of-the-Open-Noon]] a speaking, morally responsible elder, and it has returned to reclaim its nesting court through terror.

**Beginning:** Rescue townspeople, investigate the fallen tower, follow the Giltlings into the old quarry passages, and discover why Othmar died before he could warn anyone.

**Middle:** Secure the dangerous [[House of Veyl]] without plundering its lawful estate, recover Othmar's research, live in Hellerbruck through a month of smaller troubles, and attend the auction at which Sovarath issues its impossible demand.

**Turn:** Hellerbruck has ten days to surrender the [[The Meridian Mirror|Meridian Mirror]], Dame Sabine's regalia, every public bell, the gilded fittings of its shrines, and twelve living attendants. The party must prepare the town and weaken Sovarath by recovering the founder's arms, closing the Noon Window at the [[Abbey of the Vigilant Bell]], mastering the Mirror, learning the drake's first heat-name, and breaking its alliances.

**End:** Enter [[The Open-Noon Lair]] by the tribute road, old quarry, or abbey servant route. Every preparation changes the final confrontation. Killing Sovarath ends the tyranny only if its law-gem and old nesting court are protected from immediate exploitation.

**Tier:** town campaign, level 1-7 analog.

**Expected length:** 8-12 substantial sessions: two opening sessions, two estate sessions, one or two Hellerbruck interludes, three or more preparation sessions, and a one- or two-session finale.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player party; Baroness Margarethe Heller; the households of Hellerbruck; Othmar Veyl's surviving evidence; Sovarath; the divided Giltlings; and four principal lieutenants who serve for very different reasons.
- **What:** protect Hellerbruck, solve Othmar's murder, learn what returned from the old nesting court, reject or endure the tribute, assemble concrete advantages, and stop Sovarath from making the town into a mortal Sun-Court household.
- **Where:** central Brauvalis on the River Weiss, within Hellerbruck, its founder's ruins, the Veyl estate, Dame Sabine's crypt, the abandoned abbey above the old quarry, and the sun-court caverns beneath the limestone ridge.
- **Why the party acts:** they survive the tower collapse and become the only mobile group trusted by several town factions; Margarethe formally commissions them after their first rescue; or they arrive with Othmar's last undelivered message and are caught in the disaster it was meant to prevent.
- **Why the crisis exists:** Dame Sabine wounded Sovarath and honestly believed it dead. She founded Hellerbruck over its nesting ground and removed the Meridian Mirror. Sovarath recovered, crossed the elder threshold, remembered the loss as a personal grievance, and chose to answer it by reducing the town to tribute and servitude.
- **How the party can prevail:** Othmar left observations rather than a single killing weapon. Founder arms, a heat-name, the Mirror, a closed court threshold, defectors, rescued captives, and a town willing to resist strip away Sovarath's advantages until a lethal confrontation becomes possible.

## Canon Boundaries

Hellerbruck, its founding history, Sovarath, Othmar's murder, the Giltlings, the named cast, the ten-day demand, the preparation sites, and the Open-Noon Lair are local canon.

No outcome is canon. Public references should describe Dame Sabine as the town's drake-slaying founder and the upper quarry as disputed old ground without asserting whether Sovarath dies, escapes, rules, or remains dormant. This adventure is independent of [[The Dead Press Upward — Full Adventure]], [[The Feast at Wanemoor]], and other Brauvalian campaigns.

Sovarath is a gold drake, not a true dragon. Its rare elder sapience does not make ordinary drakes speaking peoples, patrons, or lesser Abyssal dragons. See [[Drakes Overview]] and [[01 Canon Reference/Creatures/20 Faewild and Menhir/10 Drakes/Gold Drakes.md|Gold Drakes]].

## Moral Ruling

The nesting ground was taken. Sabine's mistake and Hellerbruck's benefit from it must not be concealed once discovered. Restitution can include preserving surviving court-stone, returning nonessential trophies, and placing the high nest beyond future quarrying.

That truth does not give Sovarath ownership of people born centuries later. Its demand for living attendants, destruction of farms, murder of Othmar, and threat to burn the town are chosen acts. Age has given it the ability to understand refusal, innocence, proportionality, and death. It chooses domination anyway.

The Giltlings are also morally responsible once they can understand the choices before them. Fear explains service without cleansing it. Nirr's defectors can make restitution and withdraw. Kezra's loyalists continue kidnapping and killing after alternatives are offered.

The strongest ending kills Sovarath, saves the selected attendants, tells the founding truth, and protects the nesting court. Slaying a tyrant does not entitle the victors to strip its corpse and destabilize the land for immediate profit.

## The Central Truth

### The First Battle

In 1117 AR, Dame Sabine Heller led retainers against a gold drake that had begun hunting carts and quarry crews along the upper Weiss. The creature was then a large but nonsapient territorial animal. Sabine drove it into its nesting court and wounded it through the chest with a consecrated boar-spear. A cave fall buried the body beneath sunstone and lime.

Sabine recovered the Meridian Mirror, several scales, and a gilded crest from the outer court. She read the stillness, blood, and collapsed passages as death. The first Hellerbruck charter described the ridge as land freed from a devouring beast. The town grew around a bridge, quarry, brewery yards, and bell foundry.

Near the end of her life, Sabine found warm scale-dust in a sealed trophy chest and heard a slow pulse beneath the crypt during high summer. She recorded her doubts privately. Her heirs treated them as an old warrior's fear and sealed the journal with her arms.

### Sovarath's Return

Sovarath survived in a lower court chamber and escaped through fissures beyond the ridge. It recovered over centuries in remote sun-cliffs, returning only in summer to circle the lost ground. Its law-gem retained sensations of injury, metal, Mirror-light, and the town's bells. Ordered memory eventually became reflection, language, and self-awareness: the rare elder threshold.

Sapience did not make Sovarath wise. It built a personal court out of remembered instinct. Hellerbruck's bells became stolen voice; Sabine's regalia became stolen rank; the inhabitants became descendants of trespassers who should kneel. Sovarath gathered court-fauna and Giltlings, learned mortal speech from captives, and began planning a return grand enough to prove that the nesting law had never ended.

### Othmar's Discovery

Othmar Veyl spent twenty years comparing drake molts, reflective behavior, and surviving Menhir court instruments. He kept creatures in cramped enclosures and exposed them to controlled glare. Some of that work was cruel, and his estate remains dangerous because he believed observation justified too much captivity.

Three years ago, he proved that the founding scales and recent molts came from the same drake. He also identified repeated patterns in the molts: memory was accumulating within the law-gem. Othmar hid the Meridian Mirror from the public trophy room, began testing how to interrupt its alignment, and wrote to Margarethe.

The Bright-Tail Host intercepted the courier. Kezra's faction entered through the exposed quarry passages, killed Othmar, and searched for the Mirror. Their tunnelling destabilized the founder's last tower. The campaign begins when that damage finally brings it down.

## Hellerbruck in Play

Hellerbruck has about 2,400 permanent residents and perhaps 300 additional seasonal workers, bargemen, and apprentices during the autumn auction month. It is large enough to contain different interests and small enough that every public loss is personal.

Its old-Church parish and protected Freeflame congregation disagree over images and authority but both reject human tribute. The twelve charter households are not simply noble families: they include the Heller house, two shrine custodians, the bridge wardens, bell founders, brewers, potters, quarry households, bargemen, healers, and two old landholding families. Sovarath deliberately selects one person from each to make the town's institutions serve it bodily.

Use the month before the auction to establish five recurring places:

- **Fallen Tower Market:** rescue site, public notice ground, and later muster square.
- **The Twelve-Bell Foundry:** employer of forty households and the physical target of the demand.
- **Weiss Bridge and Brewery Quay:** food, river traffic, gossip, and evacuation route.
- **Saint Ardel's parish and the Free Hearth:** rival confessional centers that share care of the injured.
- **The High Quarry:** source of limestone, pottery clay, and the passages into the old court.

## Campaign States

### Tribute Clock: 10 to 0

The clock activates when Sovarath makes the demand at the auction.

- **10-8:** messengers carry the terms; refugees begin leaving upper farms.
- **7-6:** selected charter households receive hot gold marks at their doors.
- **5:** Sovarath burns an empty public structure as proof unless the party prevents the strike.
- **4-3:** Giltlings and court-fauna attempt to seize bells, regalia, or one selected attendant.
- **2:** the tribute road opens in full glare and Sovarath's lieutenants gather.
- **1:** every bell in town rings once without being touched.
- **0:** Sovarath attacks Hellerbruck or receives the tribute, depending on the town's decision.

A substantial expedition normally costs one or two days including travel and recovery. Failed routing, retreat, or public disorder may cost another. Partial tribute can add one or two days but raises Sun-Court Reach.

### Town Resolve: 0 to 4

Begin at **2** after the opening rescue.

- **0 - Breaking:** flight, hoarding, and private surrender leave no coordinated defense.
- **1 - Frightened:** institutions function separately and protect only their own.
- **2 - Holding:** watches, shelters, and ordinary food distribution continue.
- **3 - United:** confessional and guild leaders share defenses and evacuation plans.
- **4 - Defiant:** Hellerbruck can resist the first direct assault and support the lair expedition.

Truthful leadership, visible rescue, protecting the selected households, fair treatment of defectors, and solving ordinary crises raise Resolve. Concealing Sabine's journal after it matters, looting Othmar's estate, abandoning districts, or surrendering people lowers it.

### Sun-Court Reach: 0 to 4

Begin at **1** during the opening and rise to **2** when Sovarath speaks through the Mirror.

- **0 - Severed:** no stable passage remains between Sovarath's court and Hellerbruck.
- **1 - Traces:** heat marks, Giltling paths, and isolated court-fauna.
- **2 - Open Glare:** Sovarath can project voice and sight through prepared reflections.
- **3 - Procession:** court creatures cross through the Noon Window and marked doors.
- **4 - Mortal Court:** Sovarath can impose its territorial law across much of the town during daylight.

Closing the Noon Window, splitting the Bright-Tail Host, grounding the Mirror, and breaking lieutenants reduce Reach. Surrendering bells or gilded fittings, accepting court titles, and aligning the Mirror for Sovarath increase it.

### Preparation Assets

Record assets as yes, partial, or lost:

- **Founder's Arms:** pierce one layer of territorial protection and provide the killing instrument Sabine used.
- **First Heat-Name:** interrupts Sovarath's command or fear effect once per confrontation phase.
- **Meridian Mirror Mastery:** redirects a glare attack, reveals the law-gem's position, or closes a reflected route.
- **Noon Window Closed:** prevents court reinforcements and removes Sovarath's long-range projection.
- **Mara Rescued:** turns Lukas and reveals the Window's rhythm.
- **Lieutenants Neutralized:** each absent lieutenant removes a finale complication.
- **Giltling Route:** Nirr reveals the servant fissure and a safe withdrawal path.

## Act I - The Fallen Tower

### Opening Disaster

The party is crossing Fallen Tower Market when the old structure groans, sheds gold-lit dust, and collapses. Run the opening as rescue under movement rather than a single fight:

- brace a pottery arcade before families are crushed;
- stop a panicked horse team on the bridge approach;
- enter a dust pocket to recover trapped children and a bell founder;
- drive off two heat-maddened court scavengers following the collapse;
- notice that part of the tower fell inward toward a tunnel rather than outward from age.

Success saves people and immediately establishes trust. Failure costs named townspeople, damages the market, or reduces Town Resolve, but Margarethe still needs the only capable witnesses.

### What the Rubble Reveals

The lower chamber contains three dead Giltlings, one survivor too injured to flee, bronze chisels sized for small hands, and strips of Othmar's missing letter. One corpse has a mirror burn across its face; another was killed by falling stone rather than combat.

Concrete clues lead in three directions:

1. **Old quarry tunnel:** small tracks and dragged paper lead beneath the ridge.
2. **House of Veyl:** a charred Giltling lies at Othmar's side entrance, killed by a defensive mirror.
3. **Bridge post:** Lukas Auen logged unusual night lights but later altered the entry.

The party may approach these in any order. Entering the House early is possible but more dangerous before the tower evidence reveals its warning conventions.

### The Founder's Undercroft

The tunnels beneath the tower contain collapsed castle stores, quarry cuts, and a Bright-Tail staging chamber. Hazards include unstable lime, reflected sunlight from stolen hand-mirrors, and a trapped menagerie animal being used as a guard.

At the staging chamber, the party encounters Kezra's loyalists and Nirr's frightened breakaway. Nirr can be separated through proof that Sovarath allowed the tower to crush its own servants. A deal requires the defectors to return stolen pages, identify Othmar's route, and cease attacks. They do not yet confess their role in the murder.

If the party kills or drives off everyone, Othmar's coded route remains on a stolen page and can be reconstructed at the estate. If Nirr defects, the party gains an early guide and the possibility of the Giltling Route asset.

### Othmar's Death

Othmar's body is found in a lower observation tunnel rather than beneath the tower. He was stabbed by Giltling tools after sealing the Meridian Mirror behind a counterweighted wall. His final message names no dragon, but says:

> The founder did not kill what she buried. It remembers in the heart now. Do not let it see itself in noon-glass.

The body, broken sending tube, and Kezra's orders establish murder. If the evidence is lost, Othmar's house servant and anatomical records establish the same conclusion later.

### Act I Output

The party knows that Othmar was murdered, the tower was undermined by small intelligent intruders, the high quarry connects to something older, and Othmar feared a remembered creature associated with reflected sunlight. Margarethe commissions them to secure the estate and recover evidence without treating it as loot.

## Act II - The House of Veyl

### Estate Mandate

Othmar's niece **Elsbet Veyl**, a physician in Korrheim, instructs Margarethe to sell the estate and use part of the proceeds to compensate Othmar's servants. The party may use consumables necessary to survive, but books, instruments, specimens, and valuables must be inventoried for the auction.

Looting is not a mechanical trap. It changes public trust, who can bid later, and whether Elsbet releases Othmar's private papers. Returning dangerous objects to public display can also be irresponsible; the party must distinguish custody from enrichment.

### Estate Zones

#### 1. Receiving Hall and Mirror Wards

Othmar designed the entrance to expose court influence. Mirrors turn toward heat, spoken lies produce a bright rim, and one defensive panel has already killed a Giltling. The party can learn the estate's visual language before deeper hazards.

#### 2. The Sun-Court Menagerie

Cramped enclosures hold lesser gold-court fauna: glare moths, brass-backed burrowers, and a wounded heat-cat. Neglect since Othmar's death is making them desperate. Feeding, releasing, sedating, or killing them are all possible, but careless release creates later town attacks.

Records show that Othmar caused suffering to measure court responses. They also show that he stopped several experiments and began arranging transfers once the creatures exhibited persistent memory. His moral change was incomplete but real.

#### 3. Molt Archive

Scales from the founding trophy and scales collected in the last three years share fracture lines and law-patterns. A rotating frame demonstrates how older molts preserve ordered responses. This is the first hard proof that Sovarath survived and crossed the elder threshold.

#### 4. Servant Wing

Surviving steward **Ada Rinn** has barricaded herself with two apprentices. She can explain Othmar's routines, identify missing keys, and direct the party to a false wine ledger that contains his field notes. If the party delayed entering, escaped fauna threaten them but do not automatically kill them.

#### 5. Noon Chamber

This room projects sunlight through an array of metal leaves. Helka Brand has left a delayed alchemical fire intended to destroy it. Disarming the fire preserves the first heat-name fragment. Failure burns the chamber but leaves molten letter-shapes that Ilyra can later interpret.

#### 6. The Hidden Sun-Cave

The cave lies beyond a cistern and requires either Othmar's counterweight notes, Nirr's route, or following warm airflow. It contains the Meridian Mirror, Sabine's copied journal fragments, the final molt comparison, and Othmar's plan for closing the Noon Window.

### The Meridian Mirror Decision

The Mirror cannot be safely left aligned. The party can:

- **master it:** learn its three positions and retain it as a dangerous asset;
- **ground it:** lock it face-down in iron, denying projection but requiring later work to use it;
- **destroy it:** end Sovarath's reflected access at the cost of losing the finale advantage;
- **hide it:** preserve uncertainty, but Helka continues searching and can steal it later.

The object is town history and court machinery, not casual treasure.

### A Month in Hellerbruck

The estate auction is set thirty days later so Elsbet's agent and regional buyers can arrive. Present two to four interludes according to campaign length.

#### Quarry Under Water

Ordinary bad shoring opens a spring and traps nine workers. The disaster is not Sovarath's plot. Rescue preserves livelihoods and reveals an old service stair toward the abbey.

#### The Heat-Cat at the Hop Fields

An escaped menagerie animal is burning crops as it seeks a warm den. Careful handling can relocate it; killing it is defensible if people remain in danger. The incident determines whether townspeople trust the party's distinction between animals and Sovarath.

#### Twelve-Bell Night Raid

Kezra attempts to steal bell moulds and kidnap an apprentice able to identify the charter bells. Captured orders name Helka but not Sovarath.

#### The Veyl Compensation Hearing

Othmar's servants, injured quarry workers, and Elsbet's agent dispute the estate proceeds. No hidden villain controls the hearing. A fair settlement raises Resolve and determines who supports the party at auction.

### The Auction and Demand

Hold the auction in the covered bell market. Helka bids aggressively for the molt archive and Meridian Mirror. If the party destroyed or concealed the Mirror, she instead seeks its plates, plans, and Sabine's journal.

When Helka loses, every polished surface turns toward the high quarry. Heat shimmer gathers into the outline of a great gold drake. Sovarath speaks in its own voice rather than through an interpreter. It names itself, recounts Sabine's assault, and gives the town ten days.

The demand is deliberately impossible without dismantling Hellerbruck's public identity and surrendering innocent people. Sovarath scorches the auction roof without killing anyone, proving that it can strike while preserving victims for later service.

### Act II Output

The party has lived long enough in Hellerbruck to know what its bells, shrines, trades, and households mean. Sovarath is identified, its motive is intelligible, and its chosen remedy is plainly tyrannical. The ten-day campaign begins.

## Act III - Ten Days of Noon

Run Act III as an open preparation board. At any point the party may attempt the lair, prepare Hellerbruck for an attack, or surrender part of the tribute. Each major site provides information and at least one asset.

### Preparation Site A: Crypt of Dame Sabine

The crypt lies beneath a small founder chapel, not a palace. Margarethe opens it once shown Othmar's evidence. If she refuses, town leaders can compel a public hearing or the party can enter through the old quarry stair at political cost.

The crypt's residue is not Sabine's imprisoned soul. Her companion **Gernot Vale**, two former guards, and generations of repeated founder-pageantry have left identities clinging to the regalia. They react violently to anyone they read as a thief.

The party can settle the crypt by naming the dead, reading Sabine's final doubt aloud, returning Gernot's omitted name to the memorial, or enduring the guardians long enough to take the arms. Destroying the residue is possible but loses testimony and lowers Resolve if done carelessly.

Rewards and truths:

- Sabine's consecrated boar-spear and sun-notched shield grant the Founder's Arms asset.
- Her sealed journal proves she believed Sovarath dead at the founding.
- The last entry admits doubt and instructs her heirs to investigate rather than lie.
- A rubbing of the original nesting boundary shows that the surviving upper court can be preserved without abandoning Hellerbruck.

### Preparation Site B: Abbey of the Vigilant Bell

The abandoned abbey stands above the quarry road. It once watched a natural Sun-Court threshold and rang warning rather than worship. Sovarath's lieutenants have reopened its Noon Window and converted the cloister into a tribute station.

Approaches:

- the public bell road, fastest and most visible;
- the quarry service stair found during the flood rescue;
- a cliff walk used by Lukas;
- Nirr's ash flue, too small for heavy equipment but ideal for a scout.

Key areas:

1. **Outer Bell Yard:** Giltling loyalists sort seized metal and mark charter households.
2. **Pilgrim Hall:** Lukas maintains false patrol maps while trying to divert attacks from homes.
3. **Herb Cloister:** Mara is held among heat-sensitive plants and can signal through watering channels.
4. **Brass Refectory:** Helka prepares glare salts and court-feeding compounds.
5. **Roof Procession:** Gorun Many-Wing hunts anyone crossing open stone.
6. **Noon Chapel:** Ilyra coils around the Window and recites the titles of a court that no longer exists.

Closing the Window requires changing its noon alignment, breaking three bronze leaves, restoring the warning bell, or using the Meridian Mirror to reflect the passage back upon itself. Mara knows its rhythm; Othmar's notes show its structure; Ilyra knows its ceremonial command.

The party need not fight every lieutenant. Rescue can turn Lukas. Gorun can be driven away after the Window breaks. Ilyra can be persuaded that Sovarath has replaced court order with appetite wearing titles. Helka knowingly remains and only retreats to preserve herself.

### Preparation Site C: The First Heat-Name

Sovarath's first heat-name is not a true name that grants ownership. It is the ordered sensory pattern by which its young law-gem recognized itself before language. Voicing or reproducing the pattern forces the elder to distinguish current choice from inherited territorial reflex.

The complete pattern requires any two of three sources:

- Othmar's preserved molt sequence;
- Ilyra's memory of Sovarath's juvenile court-call;
- Sabine's shield, whose sun-notches record the rhythm she used to draw it from cover.

The name does not redeem or command Sovarath. It interrupts one court-wide effect and strips away its claim that instinct makes its crimes inevitable.

### Preparation Site D: Hellerbruck Holds

Town preparation is a full operation, not background bookkeeping. The party can:

- move the twelve selected people into defensible or concealed shelters;
- organize river evacuation for children and the infirm;
- place bells as warning instruments rather than leaving them in towers;
- reconcile parish and Free Hearth watch schedules;
- expose Sabine's journal and frame restitution without accepting slavery;
- identify reflective roofs and windows Sovarath can use;
- prepare the foundry square as a fallback defense.

At Resolve 3 or 4, Hellerbruck survives an early assault without requiring the party to abandon the lair. At Resolve 0 or 1, Sovarath can force a return by attacking civilians unless evacuation is already underway.

### Partial Tribute

The town may surrender metal, fittings, bells, regalia, or even volunteer attendants to buy time. Material surrender is not equal to human surrender.

- Returning the Meridian Mirror without mastering or destroying it adds two days and raises Reach by 1.
- Surrendering shrine fittings or regalia adds one day and may lower Resolve unless publicly justified.
- Surrendering bells adds two days but prevents coordinated town alarms.
- Sending living attendants adds three days, raises Reach to at least 3, and makes their rescue part of the finale.

Sovarath accepts each payment accurately. It does not honor the spirit of restraint: every concession proves to it that the court is taking shape.

## Act IV - The Open-Noon Lair

### Approaches

#### Tribute Road

The open quarry road leads directly to the audience shelf. It permits wagons, allies, and heavy equipment but exposes the party to heat, watchers, and formal challenge. A false tribute can bring the group close before Sovarath attacks.

#### Old Quarry

The lower quarry passes through flood channels, abandoned cut faces, and the cavity where Sabine's cave fall occurred. It bypasses the audience shelf but risks collapse and law-gem heat. Quarry maps or worker aid make it reliable.

#### Abbey Servant Route

The route behind the closed Noon Window enters the lair's maintenance ledges. It is the safest approach if Mara or Ilyra provides the final sequence. If the Window remains open, it becomes the strongest reinforcement route instead.

#### Giltling Fissure

Nirr's path reaches the captive galleries and an emergency vent. It grants the best rescue and withdrawal route but cannot carry large allies or founder regalia without widening, noise, and delay.

### Lair Zones

1. **The Tribute Shelf:** bells, fittings, and prisoners are displayed according to Sovarath's invented ranks.
2. **Molting Arcade:** old scales replay fragments of heat and movement; Othmar's method can read them safely.
3. **Attendants' Gallery:** Mara, seized townspeople, and surrendered attendants are held for court training.
4. **Mirror Well:** the Meridian Mirror's original socket amplifies glare and projection.
5. **Upper Nest Memorial:** the surviving ancient court-stone and old clutch hollows prove the nesting claim.
6. **Open-Noon Chamber:** Sovarath's main territory, where its law-gem aligns with the ridge.
7. **Cooling Fissure:** escape route and possible environmental weapon, but collapsing it threatens captives and the protected nest.

### Sovarath's Court Powers

Use the elder drake as a multi-phase regional antagonist rather than a larger ordinary drake.

- **Territorial Glare:** exposed creatures struggle to hide, coordinate, or lie. Meridian Mirror Mastery can redirect it.
- **Bell Command:** surrendered bells carry commands across the lair. Town Resolve or recovered bells deny this power.
- **Remembered Wound:** Sovarath changes tactics after suffering the same kind of attack twice. The First Heat-Name resets this adaptation once.
- **Court Procession:** remaining Giltlings, Gorun, Helka, or other fauna enter according to unresolved alliances. Neutralized lieutenants remove these phases.
- **Law-Gem Shelter:** the nest-stone turns a killing blow into heat and displacement. Founder's Arms pierce the shelter.
- **Open Passage:** an intact Noon Window permits retreat, reinforcement, or projection into Hellerbruck.

### The Final Offer

Before committing fully, Sovarath offers a boundary settlement:

- Hellerbruck remains inhabited;
- the upper quarry and bells belong to the court;
- twelve attendants serve for twelve years and are replaced thereafter;
- no new stone is cut above the old boundary;
- Sovarath judges disputes touching metal, glare, and the ridge.

The boundary protection is materially possible. The human servitude and imposed rule make the settlement tyrannical. Sovarath will accept a version without attendants only if already defeated and driven away; it will not choose such restraint while dominant.

### Slaying the Tyrant

When reduced to physical vulnerability, Sovarath attempts to align its law-gem with the Meridian socket. The party must choose between a quick destructive kill and controlled separation.

Controlled victory can use the founder's spear to pin the chest law, the Mirror to reveal its pulse, White Flame to stabilize the surrounding court, physical wedges to separate nest-stone, or a carefully prepared warlock grounding script. No single character tradition is mandatory.

After death, the law-gem remains hot and territorially active. Immediate extraction for sale or weaponry destabilizes the ridge, ignites stored metal, and risks turning the victors into the next exploiters. Safe options include:

- entombing the gem beneath the upper nest under joint town and ecclesial witness;
- seating it within the grounded Meridian socket until specialists arrive;
- dividing no material until the captive rescue and land settlement are complete;
- preserving the old court as protected ground while retaining Hellerbruck's inhabited lower ridge.

## Fail-Forward Rulings

- **Nirr dies or refuses:** Othmar's tunnel diagrams reveal the servant fissure after additional time and one dangerous collapse.
- **The house burns:** Ada Rinn, melted molt impressions, and Ilyra together can reconstruct the main truths.
- **Helka wins the auction:** she carries the Mirror or records to the abbey, making that site harder but preserving the campaign.
- **Sabine's arms remain sealed:** ordinary weapons can kill Sovarath after every other protection is removed; the final phase is longer and more dangerous.
- **Ilyra is killed:** the first heat-name can be completed from the shield and molt archive.
- **The Noon Window remains open:** reinforcements arrive and Sovarath can threaten Hellerbruck during the finale.
- **The Mirror is destroyed:** Reach falls by 1, but the party loses reflected-route and glare advantages.
- **The clock reaches 0:** Sovarath attacks or accepts tribute. The campaign becomes defense, rescue, and pursuit rather than ending.
- **The party retreats from the lair:** captives are redistributed and one unresolved lieutenant gains authority. Routes and damage remain, allowing a second attempt.

## Ending States

### The Tyrant Slain

Sovarath dies, the living tribute is rescued, and Hellerbruck survives. If the founding truth is published and the nesting court protected, the town can retain its lower settlement without preserving Sabine's error as a heroic lie. This is the morally strongest ending.

### The Drake Driven Off

The party breaks Sovarath's court and forces it from the ridge without killing it. Hellerbruck survives and can protect the nest, but Sovarath remains sapient, embittered, and capable of rebuilding elsewhere.

### The Hard Boundary

The upper nesting ground is restored and Sovarath is contained beyond it through force, a closed Window, and continued watch. No attendants are surrendered. The immediate peace is better than submission but leaves an unrepentant killer beside the town.

### The Sun-Tithe

Hellerbruck surrenders its bells, treasures, and recurring attendants. Sovarath protects the road and suppresses some predators, but the town becomes a tributary court whose order rests on hostage service and fear.

### The Court of Noon

Hellerbruck burns, evacuates, or submits completely. Sovarath occupies the ridge and repopulates the town with mortal servants, Giltlings, and court-fauna. Survivors create a resistance, refugee, or later reconquest campaign.

## Session Rhythm

1. Tower collapse and rescue.
2. Undercroft, divided Giltlings, and Othmar's body.
3. House of Veyl entrance and menagerie.
4. Molt archive, servants, and hidden sun-cave.
5. Hellerbruck interludes and relationships.
6. Auction and Sovarath's demand.
7. Crypt of Dame Sabine.
8. Abbey approach, Mara, and Lukas.
9. Noon Window and remaining lieutenants.
10. Town defense or additional preparation.
11. Open-Noon Lair approach and captive rescue.
12. Sovarath, law-gem decision, and aftermath.

Groups can combine Sessions 3-4, 5-6, or 8-9 for an eight-session campaign. Longer campaigns should add town relationships and preparation consequences rather than unrelated dungeons.
