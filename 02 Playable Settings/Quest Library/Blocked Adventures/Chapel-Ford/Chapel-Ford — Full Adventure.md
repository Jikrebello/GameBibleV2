# Chapel-Ford — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** A converted Folk village has found prosperity and spiritual recognition through an apparition claiming to speak for Saint Elsin. The miracles are a [The Weeping Courts](The%20Weeping%20Courts.md) counterfeit, but the relief, investment, and some late-stage healings are real.

**Beginning:** The party arrives during the anniversary of the apparition's first appearance. [[Edda Bent-Oak]] asks them to find her missing granddaughter Miri without provoking the pilgrims or outside Church authorities.

**Middle:** Investigation of the village reveals how the miracles are staged, why ordinary people defend them, and how the House of the Kind Lantern moves written grief to an abandoned toll-house.

**End:** The party enters the toll-house, rescues Miri from being remade as a living witness, confronts [[Marta Veyr]] and a maturing [[Borrowed Saints|Borrowed Saint]], then decides how truth reaches Chapel-Ford and what replaces the false shrine.

**Tier:** W. The visible task is a missing-person investigation; the moral knot is discovered through the village rather than announced at the beginning.

**Expected length:** 3-5 substantial sessions: arrival and disappearance, village investigation, toll-house expedition, and reckoning.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; Bent-Oak elder Edda; her granddaughter Miri; local priest Father Tomas Wren; benefactor and Weeper Marta Veyr; her practical partner Pellan Drost; and the apparition they created.
- **What:** find Miri, prove how the apparition works, stop its attempt to create a living saint, and decide what happens to the village's shrine and benefactor economy.
- **Where:** Chapel-Ford on the southern Eclessian frontier, its inn and blended chapel, the Kind Lantern trading house, the riverside apparition shrine, and the ruined toll-house in the marsh downstream.
- **Why the party acts:** Edda hires them discreetly; Father Tomas requests outside help; the party escorts pilgrims into town; or the apparition addresses one of them publicly and demonstrates knowledge it should not possess.
- **Why the danger exists:** Marta exploited the Chapel-Folk's longing for recognition by manufacturing messages from Saint Elsin. Years of devotion and comparison with recognized holy places gave the counterfeit enough Shadowlands substance to act independently.
- **How it escalates:** the Borrowed Saint lures Miri to the toll-house, uses her grief and Bent-Oak lineage to build a permanent body, and prepares a public return that would make Chapel-Ford dependent on it for identity, healing, and prosperity.

## Moral and Spiritual Ruling

The apparition is not an alternative form of sainthood. It is an Idol-fed counterfeit that turns neglected grief into envy and dependency. Its destruction is a genuine good.

The villagers are not fools and their improved lives are not fraudulent. The Kind Lantern repaired the bridge, bought seed after two bad winters, funded dowries and funerals, and attracted honest pilgrimage trade. Destroying the spiritual machinery does not require burning those goods or humiliating everyone who accepted help.

The best resolution separates **the lie that must end** from **the community that should survive it**.

## The Central Truth

Six years ago, Marta Veyr and Pellan Drost opened the **House of the Kind Lantern** as a frontier trading house. Marta was already a trained Weeper in [[The Weeping Courts]]. She recognized Chapel-Ford as ideal ground: converted Folk who sincerely followed the Redeemer but remained unrecognized by old clans and treated as embarrassing dependents by much of the Church.

The Kind Lantern offered every household practical help in exchange for stories, petitions, memorial letters, and descriptions of dreams. Marta used those details to stage an apparition of **Saint Elsin at the Ford**. Hidden speakers, prepared witnesses, weeping glass, and carefully timed charity made the early appearances convincing.

Devotion then gave the image substance. Villagers began comparing their muddy shrine with the great recognized sites that had ignored them. That envy fed the Rival. The projected Elsin became a [[Borrowed Saints|Borrowed Saint]] capable of minor healing, independent speech, and entering dreams.

Marta still directs the public performances, but she no longer controls the thing completely. It wants Miri Bent-Oak because her lineage descends from Weylan Oath-Bent and gives her symbolic authority over Chapel-Folk identity. If it overwrites Miri's memories and returns her as its living witness, the counterfeit will no longer need Marta's apparatus.

## Villain Structure

- **Primary supernatural antagonist — the Ford Apparition:** a Borrowed Saint that wants exclusive spiritual and cultural possession of Chapel-Ford.
- **Human architect — [[Marta Veyr]]:** a Weeping Courts operative who built the fraud, funded real improvements, and continued after the apparition became independently dangerous.
- **Practical accomplice — Pellan Drost:** Kind Lantern factor, smuggler, and organizer of guards and couriers. He cares about profit and survival rather than theology.
- **Institutional pressure:** visiting pilgrims, outside clergy, unconverted Folk kin, and frightened villagers may obstruct the investigation without belonging to the conspiracy.

## Chapel-Ford as a Living Place

Chapel-Ford contains roughly enough households for everyone to recognize one another but not enough for one family to control every trade. Chapel-Folk marry at fire and altar, keep Church feast days alongside inherited seasonal customs, and occupy an uneasy middle ground between Eclessian settlement and Folk clan territory.

### The Bent Cup Inn

The adventure's social hub. Pilgrims, wagoners, village families, hired guards, and unconverted kin share its common room. Harek knows who arrived when, which rooms the Kind Lantern pays for, and which pilgrims left without their luggage.

### Chapel of the Two Fires

Father Tomas's small stone-and-timber chapel. One flame burns at the altar and one in an older central hearth. Official visitors pretend the second is merely practical. The apparition has begun asking worshippers to visit the riverside instead of attending ordinary services.

### House of the Kind Lantern

Trading floor, warehouse, guest rooms, and charity kitchen. Its prices are fair. Its books show real spending on the village alongside unexplained shipments of blank glass, silvered cloth, lamp oil, and sealed memorial letters.

### Bent-Oak Hall

Edda's household and the village's meeting place. Its walls preserve genealogies connecting the village to Weylan Oath-Bent. Miri's missing cloak and a wet trail toward the ford provide the first physical lead.

### The Ford Shrine

A simple riverside arch built where the apparition first appeared. Weeping glass hidden among ordinary votive plaques records voices and emotional residue. At dusk, reflections answer a heartbeat later than the people casting them.

### The Old Toll-House

A ruined fortified crossing two miles downstream in reed marsh. The bridge is gone, but its stone house, cellar, culvert, and pre-Church undercroft remain. Marta's cell uses it as workshop, archive, refuge, and the apparition's hidden body-site.

## Adventure States

- **Village Trust (0-4):** whether residents share information and accept the party's account.
- **Material Security (0-4):** how much of the Kind Lantern's useful work survives the revelation.
- **Apparition Strength (0-4):** fed by public performances, destroyed evidence, and humiliation between groups.
- **Miri's Self (0-4):** how much of her memory and agency remains when the party reaches her.

## Act I — The First Weeping

### Beat 1: Arrival During the Feast

The party reaches Chapel-Ford on the eve of the apparition's sixth anniversary. Choose the entry hook that fits the campaign:

- escort a pilgrim wagon through frontier roads;
- deliver a letter to Father Tomas;
- seek food, shelter, or work at the Bent Cup;
- answer Edda's earlier request for neutral outsiders;
- follow rumors of a frontier apparition rejected by official relic courts.

The village is busy and visibly better kept than neighboring settlements. Fresh bridge timbers, full storehouses, and new roofs carry the Kind Lantern's mark. Nobody needs to explain these achievements in abstract terms; the party sees them.

### Beat 2: The Apparition

At sunset, villagers gather at the ford. A pale image resembling traditional depictions of Saint Elsin appears over the water. It names private griefs, blesses the Chapel-Folk as the saint's forgotten children, and contrasts their neglected shrine with wealthy holy cities.

It then addresses Miri: **"Your mother waits where the old road drowned."** Miri flees the gathering. The apparition briefly turns toward a player character and repeats a grief learned from another person's description, providing the first imperfect clue.

### Beat 3: The Missing Girl

Miri is gone by midnight. Her boots lead toward the river and disappear where an old ferry path branches downstream. Edda asks the party to investigate quietly until morning. She fears three outcomes:

- pilgrims panicking or forming a search mob;
- the Church sending an inquisitorial wagon;
- unconverted kin accusing Chapel-Ford of sacrificing its own children to a foreign saint.

Father Tomas supports the search. Marta publicly offers supplies and privately redirects attention toward the northern woods.

### Act I Output

The party has an urgent missing-person case, has witnessed the false apparition, and understands that the village's prosperity and dignity are tied to belief in it.

## Act II — The Village Behind the Miracle

The party may investigate locations in any order. Each produces one piece of a three-part proof:

1. **Knowledge proof:** the apparition's secrets come from submitted letters and interviews.
2. **Material proof:** the Kind Lantern supplies hidden equipment to the old toll-house.
3. **Spiritual proof:** weeping glass and Shadowlands residue show that the image now exists beyond stagecraft.

### Bent-Oak Hall: Miri's Motive

Miri's hidden letters describe dreams of her mother at the old toll-house. Edda reveals that Miri's mother died after being rejected by both an Eclessian hospice and her unconverted birth clan. This grief gives the apparition leverage, not moral authority.

**Benefit:** personal memories recovered here increase Miri's Self during the final rescue.

### Chapel of the Two Fires: The First Fraud

Father Tomas retains his original apparition notes. One prophecy was delivered before the event it predicted, but the event was a Kind Lantern grain donation Marta had already arranged. He also identifies phrases supposedly spoken by Elsin that imitate Chapel-Folk idiom rather than the saint's surviving sermons.

**Choice:** reassure Tomas and gain his help, threaten exposure and make him defensive, or demand he announce doubt immediately, which raises Apparition Strength through public panic and comparison.

### Kind Lantern: Good Works and Hidden Rooms

The public books are honest about most spending. The village really received what Marta promised. A concealed correspondence room contains copied memorial letters, reports on household grief, performance schedules, and shipment marks for the toll-house.

Pellan confronts intruders with hired guards. He bargains readily: he will reveal the marsh route if allowed to leave with portable coin and the courier ledger. Letting him take the ledger protects local informants but conceals the link to the broader Weeping Courts.

### Bent Cup: Witnesses

Harek remembers sealed boxes moved after midnight and pilgrims whose testimony was rehearsed in private rooms. Sivra Ash-Reed produces a letter Miri's mother wrote before death. The apparition quoted it word for word despite the letter never leaving Sivra's possession; a Kind Lantern scribe copied it while helping Sivra petition the Church.

### Ford Shrine: The Thing Looking Back

Removing a votive plaque exposes weeping glass threaded through the arch. Breaking it weakens the apparition but releases grief-doubles into the crowd. Replacing it with ordinary glass allows the party to follow its residue downstream without alerting Marta.

### Dynamic Pressure

After two investigations, Marta announces that the apparition has promised Miri will return blessed at dawn. This gives the party one night before the cell completes the living-witness ritual. Villagers become less willing to search the toll-house because doing so now appears to oppose Miri's promised return.

### Act II Output

The party can prove that the miracle began as fraud, has become supernaturally real, and is centered at the old toll-house. Their treatment of beneficiaries determines whether villagers later hear the truth or defend the shrine against them.

## Act III — The Old Toll-House

### Approaches

- **Broken road:** fastest and watched by Pellan's guards.
- **Reed channel:** quiet but passes through residue-fed water and grief-doubles.
- **Dry culvert:** discovered through old bridge records at Bent-Oak Hall; enters the cellar but can flood if the apparition senses intruders.

### Zone 1: The Abandoned Crossing

The upper ruins house six to ten guards, not a random bandit ecology. Some are paid wagon-men; two are committed Court Postulants; others surrender if shown that Pellan intends to abandon them. Signal lamps warn the cell below unless disabled.

### Zone 2: Workshop of Miracles

Props, voice pipes, silvered cloth, prepared tears, hidden viewing slits, and copied letters document the early fraud. The room also contains practical stores intended for Chapel-Ford's next winter. Fire or careless fighting can destroy both proof and supplies.

### Zone 3: The Weeping Cellar

Glass vessels hold condensed grief drawn from the ford shrine. Some contain coherent final memories that can be returned to families; others have curdled into hostile doubles. A four-braided flame mark on one shipping case can foreshadow [[The Fane of the Braided Flame]] without making that campaign responsible for this one.

### Zone 4: The Drowned Road

An older undercroft overlaps the Shadowlands. It resembles a road beneath black water, lined by doorways showing the holy recognition each viewer envies. Following those visions feeds the Borrowed Saint. Choosing present companions and remembered ordinary kindnesses preserves direction.

### Zone 5: The Living Witness

Miri stands within a frame of weeping glass while the Ford Apparition copies her face and Bent-Oak memories. Marta conducts the rite. Pellan is preparing to flee through a rear channel with the courier ledger.

Marta argues that Chapel-Ford finally has food, dignity, and a saint who answers. She knows the Rival feeds through the shrine and regards that price as smaller than abandonment. She is wrong, but her record of material care makes the argument credible.

### Defeating the Borrowed Saint

The apparition has four supports:

1. **Miri's grief:** countered with memories and living relationships recovered in the village.
2. **Marta's performance:** ended by persuasion, incapacity, confession, or death.
3. **Weeping glass:** destroyed, purified with White Flame, or disconnected from the ford.
4. **Comparative devotion:** weakened if the party treated villagers' faith with dignity instead of mocking them.

Breaking supports reduces Apparition Strength and returns Miri's agency. Simply attacking the visible image while supports remain causes it to re-form through Miri.

### Marta's Outcomes

- **Confession:** possible if confronted with the apparition consuming Miri and proof that it no longer serves Marta's stated purpose.
- **Capture:** preserves testimony and the Kind Lantern's assets but risks a rescue attempt by the Weeping Courts.
- **Death:** ends her direction of the rite but may make her a martyr if the village lacks evidence.
- **Escape:** she carries the courier ledger and becomes a recurring Court operative.

### Act III Output

Miri lives, dies, or returns altered; the apparition is destroyed, sealed, or preserved; and the party controls some combination of evidence, winter stores, correspondence, and Marta herself.

## Act IV — What the Village Is Told

The party returns before the promised dawn appearance. Villagers and pilgrims have gathered at the ford. The final confrontation is social unless the apparition survived, in which case it manifests publicly using Miri or Marta as its witness.

The party must explain three truths separately:

- the apparition was built from stolen grief;
- some later supernatural effects were real but came from a predatory source;
- the village's worth, faith, and right to survive do not depend on that source.

Evidence without compassion convinces authorities and loses the village. Compassion without evidence allows the Court to call the party persecutors. Both are needed for the most restorative branch.

## Ending States

### The Honest Chapel

The apparition is destroyed, Miri is restored, and Marta confesses or leaves conclusive evidence. Edda, Tomas, Sivra, and Harek convert the Kind Lantern's remaining stock into a village storehouse and pilgrim hostel. Chapel-Ford loses spectacle but keeps most material security. The Chapel of Two Fires openly acknowledges both the community's faith in the Redeemer and its inherited customs without claiming false sainthood.

This is the most restorative continuation, requiring Village Trust 3+, Material Security 2+, and a destroyed or powerless apparition.

### Ash and Salt

The party summons Church authorities or destroys the site without protecting people and supplies. The counterfeit is ended decisively, but the shrine, trading house, records, and much of the village economy are burned. Chapel-Ford survives poorer, angrier, and more vulnerable to the next recruiter promising recognition.

### The Quiet Burial

The cell and apparition are destroyed, but Edda and Tomas conceal the scale of the fraud to prevent outside punishment. Miri is said to have survived an attempted abduction. The village recovers slowly, while incomplete truth leaves rumors that the saint was real and silenced.

### The Pilgrim Throne

The party accepts Marta's argument, preserves the apparition, or redirects it under a new keeper. Pilgrimage and healing expand. Chapel-Ford becomes wealthier and increasingly hostile toward every holy place receiving recognition it lacks. The Rival gains a permanent frontier court.

### The Broken Ford

Miri is lost, evidence is destroyed, and the party publicly denounces the village without proving the cell. Chapel-Folk split between shrine defenders, Church loyalists, and families returning to unconverted kin. Violence at the ford ends the pilgrimage but not the Weeping Courts' story about persecution.

## What the Adventure Changes

- Chapel-Ford becomes a recurring safe settlement, damaged community, or hostile pilgrimage center.
- The party gains relationships among Chapel-Folk, frontier clergy, Folk kin, and the Weeping Courts.
- Marta becomes a prisoner, witness, corpse, or recurring antagonist.
- Miri may become a bridge keeper, spiritual witness against counterfeit apparitions, or vessel of the Ford Lady.
- Evidence from the courier ledger can expose other false shrines or point toward [[The Fane of the Braided Flame]].

## Encounter and Creature Register

- [[Borrowed Saints]] — the Ford Apparition and its grief-doubles.
- Human Kind Lantern guards and Weeping Courts Postulants.
- Residue-fed river hazards rather than unrelated giant animals.
- Weeping-glass memory vessels as rescue, hazard, and evidence.
- No creature in the toll-house exists only to fill a room.
