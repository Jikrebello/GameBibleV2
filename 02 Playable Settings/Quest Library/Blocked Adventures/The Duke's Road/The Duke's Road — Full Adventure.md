# The Duke's Road — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** Duke Alaric has sixty workable days to open a western relief corridor from Grenzburg toward Dornwatch. The road is already half built when the party reaches [[Fort Alderwatch]]. Workers have been murdered, the company is losing confidence, and the remaining route crosses land whose people have valid rights and good reasons to distrust a permanent ducal road.

**Beginning:** [[Road-Captain Hanne Eber]] asks the party to inspect a butchered work crew before panic empties the camps. The evidence points to thorn-bodied hunters rather than the [[Alder-Crossing Clan]] blamed by some foremen. The killers belong to [[The Red Garland]], but their purpose and patron remain hidden.

**Middle:** The party operates freely across [[Alderway Vale]]: rescuing workers, repairing the camp's own failures, clearing ordinary dangers, finding stone and timber, dealing with bandits, meeting the Alder-Crossing moot, and deciding which road line receives the company's finite labor. Each success improves progress, the fort, local standing, or the resources available for a harder route.

**Turn:** Rune-collared [[01 Canon Reference/Creatures/20 Faewild and Menhir/10 Drakes/Ruby Drakes.md|Ruby Drakes]] attack Fort Alderwatch. Their collar plates, signal bearings, and a captured Garland token reveal that rogue siege-warlock [[Magister Caspar Rauk]] has been using the vale as a proving ground. He activates [[The Cinder Seed]] beneath the fort and retreats to [[Cinder Crown]].

**End:** The party crosses the northern mountain approaches, breaches Rauk's redoubt, and stops or survives the Seed. The road that emerges depends on what was built, whose help was earned, and what remains after the assault. No ending is the authorial continuation.

**Tier:** frontier mini-campaign, level 5-7 analog.

**Expected length:** 4-6 substantial sessions: opening investigation, two or more open-operation sessions, the drake assault, and the Cinder Crown finale.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player party; Hanne Eber; [[Master Surveyor Oskar Pell]]; the mixed road company; the Alder-Crossing moot; the Red Garland; Caspar Rauk; coerced ruby drakes; and ordinary people trying to survive the project.
- **What:** keep the road company alive, learn who is targeting it, settle or force a route, defend the fort, stop the Cinder Seed, and decide what kind of road can remain.
- **Where:** Alderway Vale north of Wolfsmere, Fort Alderwatch, Still-Hearth Glen, worker camps and resource sites, the abandoned treaty hearth, and Cinder Crown.
- **Why the party acts:** Hanne hires proven troubleshooters; Pierre Hohenwall sends them as independent field agents; a missing worker is known to them; or the party needs westward passage and finds the only road camp under attack.
- **Why the road exists:** Grenzburg's river road is a single vulnerable supply line. A western relief route toward Dornwatch would reduce famine and military isolation without deciding the main Grenzburg campaign.
- **Why the crisis exists:** the project combines real need, careless camp practice, old frontier dangers, disputed land, and deliberate attacks. Rauk exploits those pressures because he wants to destroy the Duke's work publicly and replace law with his own armed protection.
- **How it escalates:** time and casualties reduce what the company can build; hostile choices reduce local help; Rauk studies the response; the drakes expose his hand; and the Cinder Seed turns a road deadline into a rescue and assault deadline.

## Canon Boundaries

The starting situation is local canon: Alderway Vale, the unfinished project, Fort Alderwatch, the Alder-Crossing claim, the named cast, Rauk's plot, the Red Garland, the collared drakes, and the Cinder Seed all exist.

The adventure is independent from Grenzburg's critical path. It assumes no faction ending, companion outcome, political settlement, road completion, or main-campaign state. It may be scheduled around the seasonal crisis only by deliberately reconciling its sixty-day calendar with the game's milestone windows.

No road result is canon. Later references should describe the **unfinished Alderway project** unless a campaign continuity deliberately selects an ending.

## Moral Ruling

Grenzburg's survival objective is legitimate. A second road can save civilians, move grain, and prevent one broken bridge from isolating the duchy. That need does not make every line just.

The Alder-Crossing claim is also legitimate. Its people maintain crossings and refuge obligations, and Still-Hearth Glen is a real sanctuary rather than empty land made valuable only by a survey. The morally strongest route costs more because justice often requires labor, material, and restraint rather than good intentions alone.

The clan's valid rights do not excuse the Red Garland. The Guests murder uninvolved workers, mutilate bodies, and help a warlock terrorize the vale. Rauk's pride, dismissal, and technical brilliance do not excuse coercing drakes or treating human communities as proof targets.

Oskar Pell is culpable for trying to erase a sanctuary through administrative convenience. He is not secretly responsible for every danger. Hanne's road company contains self-interest, courage, negligence, and genuine public service without becoming wholly virtuous or wholly corrupt.

## The Central Truth

The old Atherian trace through Alderway once linked a signal post at Cinder Crown to the eastern marches. It fell out of regular use after river shifts, forest growth, and the collapse of centralized maintenance. Alder-Crossing households kept parts of it open as seasonal footpaths while allowing the central shelf to return to woodland.

Duke Alaric commissioned a modern relief road after repeated winter shortages showed how easily the River Grenz route could be cut. Pierre Hohenwall selected Hanne Eber to command the field company. Oskar Pell surveyed three possible lines but presented the old central trace as the only affordable one. Work began from both ends and now stands roughly half complete.

Caspar Rauk learned of the project through former arsenal contacts. He had already occupied Cinder Crown, where he was testing long-distance heat scripts and pain-driven animal control. A public road would expose his redoubt. More importantly, its builders provided a political experiment: if he could let ducal authority nearly succeed and then destroy it in one stroke, caravans might accept him as the only power capable of protecting the corridor.

Rauk found the Red Garland at an abandoned treaty camp and paid it with heat, metal, and victims. The Guests began murdering isolated workers and feeding him patrol intelligence. He fitted ruby drakes with rune collars keyed to beacons around Fort Alderwatch. Beneath the fort's unfinished gate, infiltrators laid the receiver roots of the Cinder Seed.

Rauk did not foul the camp well, awaken the old residue field, breed the spiders, recruit every bandit, or command the weather. He studies those problems and sometimes worsens their consequences. The distinction lets careful players discover a bounded conspiracy rather than assuming that every misfortune has one magical author.

## The Road Company in Numbers

- **Field population:** about 170 at Fort Alderwatch and 30-50 in active satellite camps.
- **Useful fighting strength:** 28 guards and scouts, plus up to 40 workers able to hold prepared positions.
- **Road already usable:** the eastern and western approaches together cover roughly half the planned corridor, but no continuous wagon route exists.
- **Workable season:** 60 days when the party arrives.
- **Ruby drakes:** three adults certainly collared; one or two additional drakes may be at Cinder Crown according to desired difficulty.
- **Rauk's mortal force:** about twelve deserters, apprentices, and hired climbers.
- **Red Garland:** thirteen Blood-Briar Guests including the Garland King.

These numbers define stakes. The selected game system should represent most workers and enemies as fronts, hazards, squads, or background action rather than requiring dozens of individual turns.

## Campaign States

### Days Remaining: 60 to 0

Begin at **60**. Mark ordinary travel and work honestly. A substantial operation usually costs 2-5 days including recovery and movement. A serious failure, storm, or replacement of lost equipment costs more. Clever preparation can combine operations but does not create free construction time.

At 30 days, cold rain and early frost begin. At 15, upper work slows and the North Ridge becomes dangerous. At 0, winter closes regular construction. The party may still assault Cinder Crown or evacuate the fort, but Road Progress no longer increases without extraordinary resources.

### Road Progress: 0 to 8

Begin at **3**, representing the half-built approaches and established camps.

- **4:** one continuous pack route crosses the vale.
- **6:** carts can pass with temporary repairs and local guidance.
- **8:** the selected road is complete enough to maintain through its first winter.

Successful operations provide work resources rather than automatic points. At the end of each work interval, Hanne converts secured labor, stone, timber, access, and safe days into Progress. The Shared Way requires two additional work resources beyond the Straight Cut. The North Ridge requires three and cannot reach 8 after Day 15 without exceptional success.

### Fort Condition: 3 to 0

Begin at **3**.

- **3 - Sound:** full stores, connected camps, and effective signal mast.
- **2 - Strained:** sickness, losses, or damage remove one support during later operations.
- **1 - Failing:** satellite camps withdraw and the drake assault begins with a serious breach.
- **0 - Evacuating:** the fort cannot house the company. The finale continues from a field camp or refugee column.

Cleaning the water, rescuing workers, reinforcing the stockade, and protecting supplies preserve or restore Condition. Ignored crises, heavy casualties, and fire reduce it.

### Vale Standing: -2 to +2

Begin at **0**.

- **+2 - Shared cause:** Alder-Crossing guides, refuge halls, and witnesses actively support the project.
- **+1 - Working trust:** the clan provides information and ordinary passage.
- **0 - Guarded:** formal contact but no reliable local aid.
- **-1 - Withholding:** paths close, tolls rise, and some households harass isolated work without joining the Red Garland.
- **-2 - Hostile road:** organized obstruction, false trails, and evacuation of local support. The clan still does not ally with Rauk.

Respecting sanctuary, distinguishing the Garland from the clan, keeping bargains, and repairing harm improve Standing. Trespass, collective punishment, seized food, and breaking the glen reduce it.

### Cinder Clock: 5 to 0

This state is inactive until the drake assault. Rauk then lights the relay and the Clock begins at **5**. Significant delay, a failed intervention, or a retreat from Cinder Crown advances it. See [[The Cinder Seed]] for signs and mitigation.

The clock reaching 0 causes Cinderfall. Evacuation, grounding work, and fort preparation determine casualties and damage. It does not end the adventure.

## The Three Road Lines

| Route | Material cost | Political cost | Operational risks | Completion requirement |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Straight Cut** | Lowest | Violates Still-Hearth, creates an occupied strip, and drives Standing toward hostility | Place-bound disorientation, resistance, and a road that needs permanent guards | Road Progress 8; can finish with no clan agreement |
| **Shared Way** | Two extra work resources | Requires witnessed rights, maintenance terms, and limits on settlement | Two bridges, flood retaining wall, and coordination with local keepers | Progress 8, Standing +1 or better, successful moot |
| **North Ridge** | Three extra work resources | Avoids sanctuary but gives the Duke a military line above local settlements | Steep grades, quarry work, exposure, and late-season closure | Progress 8 before winter; ridge survey and stone source secured |

The party can change routes before Progress 6. Earlier work is not erased, but one point of route-specific preparation is lost. At Progress 6 or higher, changing line prevents completion this season unless extraordinary outside labor arrives.

## Act I - The Half-Built Road

### Arrival at Alderwatch

The party reaches the fort during a stopped work bell. No crews have left since noon. Hanne explains that six workers from Cut Camp Three missed the morning signal. A search party found one body at the tree line and refused to enter the briar beyond it.

Hanne gives four immediate priorities:

1. find the missing workers;
2. determine whether the killing came from a clan, creature, or infiltrator;
3. bring back evidence before frightened guards retaliate against nearby households;
4. return before dark, when another work camp will be exposed.

Oskar wants the party to inspect the straight-line stakes near Still-Hearth at the same time. This is useful but not urgent, and his attempt to bundle the tasks reveals his priorities.

### Cut Camp Three

The abandoned camp shows a short, violent attack:

- tiny triangular punctures in exposed skin and work clothes;
- thorn splinters carrying a sleep-and-panic poison;
- one body with both ears removed;
- small tracks that become root marks under dense scrub;
- a red flower braided around an Alder-Crossing path token stolen from a guide;
- a survey copy missing the northern ridge and river-bend annotations.

The six workers have different fates. Two are dead at the camp. Two are bound alive in a thorn hollow as bait. One fled toward the spider wood. One was carried to the abandoned treaty hearth for the Garland's next welcome game.

The party can track the attackers, rescue the bait, stabilize the poisoned, or follow the escaped worker. It cannot do everything before dark without splitting up or accepting risk.

### First Contact with the Garland

A pair of Blood-Briar Guests imitate a missing worker and offer directions from behind a curtain of brush. If challenged, they fire splinter arrows and retreat. If followed carefully, they lead toward the treaty camp but collapse a thorn tunnel behind them.

A captured Guest knows:

- its host is the Red Garland;
- a "hot-crowned lord" pays for ears and maps;
- the Garland has no authority from Alder-Crossing;
- a red metal token carries Rauk's heat-signal, though his name remains unknown.

Failure to capture or pursue them does not hide the distinction. Reed-Captain Jannik arrives at dusk with the body of one of his scouts bearing the same wounds and demands that Hanne stop blaming the clan.

### The Fort Argument

Back at Alderwatch, a guard faction wants to seize nearby Alder-Crossing fishers. Hanne prevents immediate violence if the party returns with any evidence. Without it, she still allows one night for Jannik to bring the Crossing-Speaker rather than authorizing a sweep.

The party's account sets initial Standing:

- clear public distinction between clan and Garland: +1;
- uncertainty without accusation: remain at 0;
- collective blame, arrests, or burning nearby scrub indiscriminately: -1.

### Act I Output

The road company knows the murders are not ordinary banditry. The party has evidence of a predatory thorn host, a partial clue to an outside patron, and several open crises that must be handled while construction continues.

## Act II - Sixty Days in Alderway

Act II is an open board. Present three or four operations at a time. After each substantial mission, advance Days Remaining and let Hanne report what changed elsewhere. The party should complete enough operations to understand the vale, choose a route, and prepare the fort, but it need not solve every problem.

### Operation: Bitter Water

Fort sickness is caused by a latrine trench, lime runoff, and a tannery pit draining toward the shallow well. Quartermaster Matthes minimized the first report. There is no curse and no enemy poison.

The party can identify the source, move the sick, reopen an older spring pipe, enforce cleaner camp practice, or bring water from an Alder-Crossing channel. Local help is fastest but requires ordinary courtesy and compensation.

- **Success:** preserve Fort Condition and gain one safe work interval.
- **Failure or neglect:** Fort Condition -1; the company still works at reduced strength.
- **Rauk's interference:** after the problem is found, a saboteur tries to break the replacement pump. Capturing the saboteur yields a Cinder Crown climbing token but does not change the mundane cause.

### Operation: The Webbed Cut

The worker who escaped Cut Camp Three has stumbled into an old quarry wood occupied by giant cold-forest spiders. Four members of a later search crew are trapped there as well. The spiders are natural predators drawn by vibration and livestock.

Rescue can use smoke, quiet climbing, decoy animals, or a direct fight. The abandoned quarry contains sound bridge stone and an old Atherian drainage plan.

- **Success:** rescue up to five workers and gain one stone resource.
- **Partial:** workers survive but stone access remains dangerous.
- **Failure:** survivors cut themselves free days later with injuries and a map scrap; Fort Condition -1, but the quarry location remains known.

### Operation: Deserters at Birch Narrows

Six former road guards and local bandits have seized a narrow bridge and begun robbing payroll wagons. Their leader, **Rolf Senn**, deserted after Hanne punished his unit for beating a Folk guide. He tells recruits that the Duke has already lost the vale.

The group can be tracked, negotiated into surrender, divided, driven out, or killed. They have no pact with Rauk, though one unknowingly sells schedules to a Garland voice at night.

- **Success:** recover pay, tools, and one work resource; some deserters may return under sentence.
- **Failure:** the next supply costs 3 extra days and Fort Condition risks falling.
- **Evidence:** watching the night exchange reveals a Garland trail toward the treaty hearth.

### Operation: The Old Muster Field

Road grading exposes an Atherian muster trench. Residue dead repeat the final actions of a forgotten winter retreat: carrying stones, searching packs, and closing ranks around a collapsed standard. They are not trapped souls and do not answer Rauk.

The party can clear them by force, recover names from corroded tags, reroute work briefly, or bring a sexton from a Chapel-Folk hamlet. Proper release takes longer but preserves remains and yields old road measurements.

- **Quick clearance:** one safe work interval, but no extra knowledge.
- **Named release:** one safe interval and an Atherian plan showing the Water Cut into Cinder Crown.
- **Neglect:** workers refuse the section and Progress cannot rise above 5 on the central line.

### Operation: The Stone and Timber Surveys

Two resources can make the costly routes possible:

- **Greybank Quarry** supplies retaining stone for the Shared Way, but its face floods unless the old channel is reopened.
- **Northstand Pine** supplies bridge timber, but careless felling would destabilize a winter refuge slope.

The party may provide labor, solve hazards, negotiate access, or choose extraction that creates later damage. Each responsibly secured site grants one work resource. Seizing either site reduces Vale Standing and adds a maintenance problem to the ending.

### Operation: The Alder-Crossing Moot

The moot gathers at an open ferry hall, not Still-Hearth itself. Crossing-Speaker Marta asks five practical questions:

1. which line does the Duke intend to build;
2. who controls tolls and armed patrols;
3. whether refuge traffic remains free;
4. who repairs damage to fish channels and winter paths;
5. whether road passage becomes a claim to settlement land.

The clan will support the Shared Way if the project recognizes sanctuary bounds, free refuge passage, shared bridge maintenance, compensation for damaged fisheries, and no permanent settlement strip beyond the measured road. It does not demand sovereignty over all travelers or a right to close Grenzburg's relief line at whim.

A successful agreement raises Standing to at least +1, grants guides, and supplies one river-work resource. A failed moot does not block construction. The clan withdraws labor, imposes local tolls, hides side paths, and may obstruct a Straight Cut. The party can reopen talks after concrete restitution.

### Operation: The Red Garland's Hearth

The abandoned treaty camp lies beneath a ring of dead hawthorn. The Garland stages hospitality: warm food that becomes beetles when swallowed, voices of missing workers, and a place set for the party at the old table. The final captive from Cut Camp Three is bound under the hearth roots.

The host can be fought, lured across running water, stripped of its Rauk token, or weakened by finding and properly receiving the murdered guide whose betrayed hospitality created the site. Parley can recover the captive or learn Rauk's title, but the Garland demands another traveler in exchange and will continue killing.

- **Host broken:** worker rescued, murders stop, and Vale Standing +1 if the original guide is honored.
- **Host driven off:** the road becomes safer, but surviving Guests may appear during the drake assault.
- **Bargain accepted:** immediate safety for one camp at the price of future victims; the bargain becomes an active moral and practical threat.
- **Failure:** the captive is left as a marked survivor and returns with Rauk's ultimatum, preserving the next clue at personal cost.

### Choosing the Road

Oskar presents the final estimate after at least two operations or when 40 days remain. The party may endorse a route, force Hanne to delay, ask the moot for a compact, or secure resources before deciding.

Hanne accepts any route she believes can reach continuous wagon passage. She favors the Shared Way if its extra materials and local support are real. She rejects a declaration that the expensive road is morally preferable unless someone also solves how to build it.

Record the chosen line, resources committed, and any rights accepted or violated. The route shapes work scenes and the ending but does not change Rauk's decision to attack.

### Act II Output

The company has a selected line and measurable progress. The party has seen which dangers are ordinary, which arise from the road itself, and which carry the pattern of an outside warlock. When Road Progress reaches 6, when the Garland is broken, or when 25 days remain, Rauk launches the drake assault.

## Act III - Fire Over Alderwatch

### The First Dive

At dusk, the fort's forge bell rings without a hand on it. A ruby drake dives through the signal ropes, another strikes the animal pen, and a third circles toward the powder shed. Their necks carry black-red collar frames. Heat beacons planted around the fort force them into repeated attack lines.

Give the party simultaneous fronts:

- **Forge Yard:** workers trapped by burning roof beams; saving them preserves repair labor.
- **River Tower:** signal crew and pump controls threatened; holding it protects evacuation and firefighting.
- **Animal Pen and West Gate:** panicked teams may break the gate and destroy the remaining wagons.
- **Powder Shed:** saboteurs try to turn quarry charges into a secondary blast.

The party can fight drakes, disable beacons, direct crews, rescue people, or protect stores. Prepared wet hides, clean water, reinforced roofs, and good Standing each remove a complication or provide help.

### Freeing the Drakes

Examining a fallen or grounded drake shows the collar's function: pain for turning away, relief along a marked bearing, and panic heat when the fort beacon flares. Breaking the control gem ends the bearing compulsion. Cooling or grounding the collar before removal prevents a discharge.

A freed drake usually flees toward the ridge or attacks the nearest collar beacon. Gentle handling cannot make it a tame ally during the battle. A party that saves rather than merely kills drakes gains a safer route through the Cinder Crown nesting yard.

### Rauk's Ultimatum

When the attack turns or the fort is badly damaged, the signal mast projects Rauk's heat-script across a prepared iron plate. He names himself lord of Cinder Crown and demands that the road company withdraw from Alderway, leave its tools and stores, and accept his protection if it returns.

He then activates the Cinder Seed. The gate foundation begins to warm and the Cinder Clock starts at 5.

Collar bearings, the Garland token, the old muster plan, a captured saboteur, or simple observation of the signal identifies Cinder Crown. No single clue is required.

### After the Assault

Hanne can hold the fort, begin evacuation, or send workers to ground the Seed while the party assaults the redoubt. Her available actions depend on Fort Condition:

- **3:** she can defend, evacuate vulnerable people, and assign a grounding crew.
- **2:** choose two.
- **1:** choose one.
- **0:** evacuation is already underway; the party must carry any technical aid it wants.

If Hanne is dead or incapacitated, Matthes can evacuate and Oskar can direct one grounding attempt, but neither can perform all of her coordination.

### Act III Output

Rauk's role is proven, the road company is committed or evacuating, and the Cinder Clock is active. The party has several routes to Cinder Crown and must decide how much time to spend stabilizing the fort before climbing.

## Act IV - The Cinder Crown

### Crossing the Approaches

Each known approach rewards a different earlier success:

- the **Quarry Stair** is obvious and fast; recovered road guards or captured deserters can draw off its defenders;
- the **Ash Goats' Path** comes from Alder-Crossing guides and avoids one alarm;
- the **Old Signal Shaft** appears on the Atherian muster plan and reaches the script gallery;
- the **Water Cut** requires the old drainage plan and enters below the furnace.

An unknown route can still be found by scouting, climbing, or forcing the gate at the cost of time and an advanced Clock.

### Lower Works

Rauk's mortal followers defend the quarry bridge and store caverns. Some are committed apprentices; others are deserters promised land after the Duke's failure. Evidence here establishes Rauk's intent to charge caravans for armed protection and proves he planned the Seed before the road reached the sanctuary dispute.

Prisoners include a missing engineer and one Alder-Crossing scout. Freeing them gives either technical help against the relay or a safer path to the drake yard. A loud assault lets Rauk vent the lower furnace, costing time but not sealing the route.

### Drake Yard

One or two collared ruby drakes remain chained near heated nesting shelves. Their eggs or young may be present. Rauk's handlers release them if alarmed.

The party can fight, cool the yard, break the master collar key, open the cliff exit, or use knowledge from the fort assault to free the animals. Released drakes flee unless their nests are threatened. Destroying eggs for convenience prevents the drakes from becoming allies against Rauk and may trigger a desperate attack.

### Script Gallery

The gallery contains the remote Seed relay, route maps, Red Garland bargain-token, and banks of heated mirror plates. Three meaningful interventions are possible:

- **sever transmission:** stops the Clock but leaves the Seed roots hot and dangerous;
- **reverse bearing:** sends the stored pulse into Cinder Crown's furnace, weakening Rauk and threatening everyone inside;
- **copy and shut down:** preserves evidence and instruments but requires time or Rauk's master key.

Destroying the room without understanding it causes a partial discharge at the fort. This can still be the right choice when the clock is nearly empty.

### Crown Furnace

Rauk fights among shutters, chain platforms, furnace vents, and the old Atherian beacon. He tries to keep opponents in marked heat lanes while climbing toward the master relay. At intervals, force a choice between harming him, protecting an ally, cooling a runaway script, or preventing a clock advance.

Rauk's offers reveal his character:

- he will stop the Seed if Hanne recognizes his protection claim;
- he will share road income with the party if they abandon the Duke;
- he will release surviving drakes only if allowed to carry his notebooks and law-gems away;
- he will expose Oskar's concealed estimates as if another man's guilt makes his own rule just.

The terms are real enough to tempt desperate people and corrupt enough to reject. Accepting them creates Rauk's armed road lordship as an ending complication rather than a hidden good path.

### Resolving the Seed

Rauk's death does not automatically disarm the Cinder Seed. The party must use the master key, shut the gallery, communicate grounding instructions to Alderwatch, or accept a controlled discharge. If the fort was evacuated, preserving life may matter more than preserving the wall.

At Cinder Clock 0, resolve damage according to preparation:

- an evacuated and partly grounded fort burns and loses its gate, but suffers few deaths;
- an unprepared sound fort suffers a devastating breach and mass injuries;
- a failing occupied fort is largely destroyed;
- a reversed pulse splits damage between fort and redoubt;
- a fully shut relay leaves hot roots that require days of careful excavation.

### Act IV Output

Rauk is captured, killed, escaped, or installed by a corrupt bargain. The Seed is stopped, softened, or discharged. The surviving company and clan then decide whether the work already done can become a road.

## Fail-Forward Results

- **The murdered crew is not recovered:** a wounded survivor reaches the fort with a Garland token and a false accusation that must be untangled.
- **No Guest is captured:** Jannik's dead scout and later bargain signs still distinguish the Red Garland from the clan.
- **The water crisis is mishandled:** sickness reduces Fort Condition and work rate; the party may obtain water from the clan or withdraw satellite camps.
- **Workers die in an operation:** their tools, marks, or last actions leave the clue needed to continue. Casualties alter morale and ending cost rather than locking the route.
- **The Alder-Crossing moot fails:** construction continues under tolls, obstruction, and lower Standing. Restitution can reopen talks.
- **The Red Garland survives:** it joins the drake assault, attacks evacuation routes, or escapes into a later road story. Rauk is still traceable through collars and signals.
- **The drake assault is lost:** Fort Condition falls to 0 and the company evacuates, but Rauk's ultimatum and Seed still lead to Cinder Crown.
- **The party retreats from Cinder Crown:** advance the Cinder Clock, remove one prepared approach, and let the fort evacuation proceed. The party can assault again or pursue Rauk after Cinderfall.
- **Rauk escapes:** his redoubt and Seed can still be disabled. He becomes a mobile siege-warlock enemy rather than invalidating the campaign.

## Ending States

### The Shared Way

Road Progress reaches 8 on the river-bend line, Vale Standing is at least +1, and the Alder-Crossing compact survives. The road protects Still-Hearth Glen, guarantees refuge passage, and divides maintenance between ducal crews and local crossing keepers. It is slower and more expensive than promised, but resilient because the people who know the water have reason to preserve it.

### The Duke's Cut

The Straight Cut reaches 8 through Still-Hearth Glen. Grenzburg gains immediate supply value and a direct military corridor. The sanctuary is damaged or occupied, Alder-Crossing cooperation collapses, and permanent guards become necessary. The road works materially while creating a lasting Folk conflict.

### The Ridge Hold

The North Ridge reaches a usable state through exceptional labor and early completion. It becomes a defensible high road and fortified corridor. Its grades, exposure, and maintenance costs limit civilian traffic, but it avoids the sanctuary and gives Grenzburg a strong western approach.

### The Vale Remains Closed

Rauk is stopped, but Road Progress remains below a sustainable threshold or the company abandons the project. Existing sections serve local travel and patrols without forming a relief corridor. Grenzburg remains dependent on its old supply line, and another expedition may return with different terms.

### Cinderfall

The Seed destroys Fort Alderwatch or the central works. Survivors form a refugee column, retrieve trapped crews, and pursue Rauk or the Red Garland if either escaped. Some road sections remain, but this season's project fails. Cinderfall begins a recovery campaign rather than erasing the party's rescues or discoveries.

No ending is canonical. Rauk may be dead, imprisoned, fugitive, or briefly recognized through a corrupt bargain; the road's continuity must be selected by any campaign that uses the aftermath.

## What the Adventure Changes

- Grenzburg gains a relief road, a military ridge, a contested cut, or only unfinished works.
- Alder-Crossing becomes a partner, wary toll-holder, dispossessed neighbor, or renewed opponent.
- Fort Alderwatch remains a work fort, becomes a permanent post, burns, or is abandoned.
- Oskar Pell reforms his practice, loses office, doubles down, or becomes indispensable to a compromised road.
- Hanne Eber returns as a respected road-captain, a wounded commander, an evacuation leader, or one of the project dead.
- Rauk's collar and Seed designs are destroyed, captured, copied, or carried into another war.
- Surviving ruby drakes return to the ridge with altered behavior around bells, heat, and people.

## GM Quick Reference

**Immediate objective:** investigate the worker murders and keep the road company functioning.

**Hidden truth:** Caspar Rauk is using the road as a proving ground and has planted a delayed siege script beneath Fort Alderwatch.

**Primary distinction:** the Alder-Crossing land claim is valid; the Red Garland's murders are not clan resistance.

**Open middle:** water, rescue, bandits, residue, resources, moot, Garland hearth, and route work may be handled in several orders.

**Primary campaign states:** Days Remaining, Road Progress, Fort Condition, Vale Standing, then Cinder Clock.

**Best practical result:** stop Rauk and the Seed, preserve the company, and pay the real material cost of a Shared Way that protects sanctuary and passage rights.

**Do not assume:** that every hazard serves Rauk, that the Folk have one government, that drakes are tameable siege beasts, or that finishing any road is automatically a just victory.
