# Collateral Beasts

Collateral Beasts are people held in animal or monstrous working forms under Vortharan debt instruments. The term is administrative and dehumanizing, but remains common in recovery schedules and arena brokerage.

They are not a species. The person retains identity, memory, moral responsibility, and the right to restoration.

## Common Forms

- **Hound forms:** tracking, pursuit, scent verification, and capture.
- **Great-ape forms:** lifting, climbing, intimidation, and armed collection.
- **Bear forms:** heavy recovery, breach work, and crowd control.
- **Draft forms:** transport, mill labor, and road service.
- **Arena forms:** licensed combat bodies with reinforced tissue or grafted attacks.

## Encounter Role

Collateral Beasts can be opponents, captives, witnesses, allies, or mixed groups whose immediate loyalty depends on promised debt credit and access to reversion.

They should never be presented as mindless animals merely because speech is unavailable.

## Signs

- collars or brass tags bearing a personal name and form index;
- trained gestures for writing, counting, or surrender;
- human tactical behavior inconsistent with the body;
- careful protection of receipts, clothes, tools, or personal objects;
- scars or body features that do not fit natural anatomy;
- reaction to institutional commands rather than animal handling;
- frustration around speech-dependent choices.

## Behavior and Tactics

- use animal capabilities with human planning;
- protect the bailiff or register if restoration depends on them;
- avoid lethal risk when service terms do not require it;
- target transformed fugitives for capture rather than feeding;
- retreat or negotiate when offered credible restoration;
- disagree internally over whether to retain useful modifications.

## Release and Restoration

Breaking a collar does not restore the person. Safe release needs the original body index or a skilled reconstruction. The [[Reversion Crook]] is one method, not the only possible one.

Subjects may choose to preserve changes. Restoration should return agency, not impose a presumed “correct” body without consent.

## Moral and Legal Status

Vortharan institutions describe these forms as alternatives to prison and as maintained collateral. Critics argue that a debtor choosing between bodily reassignment and punishments controlled by the same creditor does not provide meaningful consent.

Violence committed in an assigned form remains morally relevant, but coercion, control of reversion, and deployment orders affect culpability.

## Quest Uses

- a collection hound tries to file testimony against its own bailiff;
- an arena debtor wants freedom but not physical reversion;
- a convoy carries body indices separately from the people they restore;
- a town hunts a “bear” that is attempting to reach its children;
- former collateral agents form a rescue and recovery company;
- a school sells a debt portfolio without transferring the reversion keys.
