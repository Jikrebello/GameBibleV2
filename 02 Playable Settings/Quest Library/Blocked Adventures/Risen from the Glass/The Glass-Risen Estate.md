# The Glass-Risen Estate

The Glass-Risen Estate is a minor Ishkatar funerary complex on the northern margin of the [[Great Glass Desert]]. It was buried before the Exodus and exposed in the present day when a violent glass-storm scoured an ancient basin clean.

Its Ishkatar name is not yet translated with confidence. Present-day expeditions name it for the manner of its discovery rather than honor its master.

## Original Purpose

The estate belonged to [[Sethrak the Final Receiver]], a human priest of Avarix who administered property confiscated by the Naga Giant Veshkara-of-the-Nine-Coils. It served simultaneously as:

- a tomb;
- a temple to [[Greed|Avarix]];
- an archive of seized households;
- a transfer mechanism intended to preserve Sethrak's office after death;
- and a prison for servants sacrificed as perpetual property.

The complex stands above a dry tributary of the Serpent's Spine. It was provincial rather than royal and must not be confused with Nagtara or the Golden Pyramid.

## Physical Plan

- **Scoured Basin:** temporary surface access, expedition camps, and the advancing Glass.
- **Receiving Court:** assigns every entrant a legal and ritual role.
- **Long Causeway:** houses the Processional Coil and connects the public court to the tomb.
- **Inventory Court:** weighs, labels, and recovers marked property.
- **Ceremonial Tomb:** a false burial containing Tombwright Hessa and the receiver seal.
- **House of Retainers:** an illusion-covered burial gallery for sacrificed servants.
- **Archive of Confiscations:** records households, property, birth names, and the cult's dissolution.
- **True Counting House:** Sethrak's hidden burial chamber and the center of the estate rite.

## Present-Day Access

The storm leaves the estate accessible for four days. From the fifth day onward, moving glass fills the basin and breaks unsupported passages. The complex may remain intact beneath the desert even if an expedition fails to close it.

## Metaphysical Behavior

The estate classifies people as claimant, witness, kin, adversary, servant, or property. Its surviving systems enforce those categories through seals, title plaques, [[Estate Dead]], and [[Gilded Wardens]].

Secret possession is particularly dangerous. A grave object removed without inventory can attach Sethrak's claim to its bearer. Openly recorded custody limits this effect because the estate cannot pretend concealment proves acceptance.

## Canon Boundary

The estate proves that Ishkatar used funerary ownership magic and that Avarix could preserve an appetite through title and residue. It does not explain the creation of the Great Glass Desert, establish that all Ishkatar tombs work identically, or make Covenant courts the universal heirs of Giant-Age ruins.
