# Risen from the Glass — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** A glass-storm has stripped a forgotten [[The Serpent Kingdom of Ishkatar|Ishkatar]] tomb from the desert floor. Three present-day claimants race toward it while its ancient receiver wakes and begins classifying every entrant as part of the estate.

**Beginning:** The party joins a [[The Qahirid Suzerainty and Iqashar|Qahuri]] breach-assessor, a Jirahar Road Company, or an independent recovery expedition. Its route determines who reaches the tomb first, what supplies survive, and whether the outer court records the party as witnesses or thieves.

**Middle:** The party crosses the exposed forecourt, survives the tomb's moving processional defenses, discovers a false burial, and learns that the apparent royal household was made from murdered servants and confiscated families. Rivals enter behind it and force choices about alliance, evidence, and treasure.

**End:** In the true counting house, [[Sethrak the Final Receiver]] demands that a living claimant either acknowledge his eternal ownership or surrender a person as the estate's new representative. The party can destroy him, close the estate through its own records, transfer custody, or carry his claim back into the living world.

**Tier:** R-W. The adventure supports a level 1-3 analog when [[Gilded Wardens]] are dangerous machines with readable behavior, [[Estate Dead]] can be released without combat, and Sethrak loses powers as the party dismantles his legal and ritual foundations.

**Expected length:** 4-6 substantial sessions: expedition and race, forecourt, false tomb, retainers and archive, final chamber, and aftermath.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; [[Breach-Assessor Samir Veyad]]; Jirahar road-keeper Miriam bat-Oren; Road Company captain Eder Ben-Tal; salvage captain Daro Vessan and the [[Brass Jackals]]; guide Nadim al-Sahr; the Estate Dead; and Sethrak.
- **What:** enter the exposed funerary estate, recover its history and human names, prevent its ancient ownership rite from attaching to the living, and decide who receives its records and wealth.
- **Where:** the northern margin of the [[Great Glass Desert]], two days beyond the Qasr Ilyun breach-court-town of Qasr Ilyun, in a processional tomb built above a dry branch of the Serpent's Spine.
- **Why the party acts:** Samir needs an armed neutral crew to serve notice and protect witnesses; the Jirahar pay for recovery of slave-name tablets; a patron finances salvage; or a storm strands the party with the competing expeditions.
- **Why the tomb woke:** the glass-storm exposed the outer receiving court and sunlight struck a gold tally mirror for the first time since Ishkatar fell. The mirror resumed the estate's final inventory and awakened Sethrak's residue.
- **How it escalates:** the tomb first assigns roles, then enforces them, then sends its wardens to recover removed property. Each unrecorded theft strengthens Sethrak and each restored name breaks part of his household.

## Moral and Metaphysical Ruling

Sethrak's estate is objectively evil. It was built from enslavement, confiscation, murder, and worship of [[Greed|Avarix]]. Age does not turn possession into justice, and beautiful funerary art does not make the people depicted as servants less human.

The Covenant court can identify and sever some of the tomb's surviving obligations because contract magic responds to office, witness, seal, and proof. This does not prove Covenant doctrine universally correct. Samir's best work succeeds because it recognizes personhood and names coercion instead of sanctifying it.

The Jirahar have the strongest living tradition connected to the captives' names and Exodus-era memory. That gives them a serious claim to the tablets and remains, not automatic ownership of every object in the tomb.

Gold taken from slaves and conquered households is not sacred property of the priest who hoarded it. It can be used for restitution, preservation, rescue costs, or public works. Secret looting remains dangerous because the tomb's metaphysics attach ownership through concealed possession.

## The Central Truth

Sethrak was not a Pharaoh-Serpent. He was a human priest and provincial receiver who served the Naga Giant **Veshkara-of-the-Nine-Coils** during Ishkatar's decline. His office absorbed estates seized for debt, rebellion, sacrilege, or simple royal appetite.

He taught that ownership was the only immortality available to lesser beings. A person died; a title persisted. A household scattered; an inventory remained. If Sethrak could become the final legal owner of every person and object placed in his tomb, then his office would have no end.

His funerary complex was therefore built as a working estate:

- the outer court identifies entrants and assigns standing;
- the inventory hall marks carried objects;
- the false tomb attracts ordinary thieves and binds them as replacement guards;
- the retainers' house preserves sacrificed servants as named property;
- the archive proves how every possession was acquired;
- the true counting house transfers ownership to Sethrak after death.

When Ishkatar collapsed, the last tombwrights sabotaged the rite. They hid the receiver's seal in the false tomb, kept a forbidden list of the servants' birth names, and left the cult's dissolution tablet in the confiscation archive. Those three proofs can close the estate.

Sethrak's human soul routed toward judgement and does not remain in the tomb. What wakes is a new residue intelligence shaped by his embalmed body, office, repeated appetite, and Avarix's claim. It remembers his life and is responsible for its own choices; defeating it does not injure or erase Sethrak's soul.

## Present-Day Claims

### The Breach-Court of Qasr Ilyun

The storm uncovered a structure inside territory patrolled by the Covenant. [[Breach-Assessor Samir Veyad]] is empowered to identify hazards, protect living claimants, and issue temporary custody orders. He wants the estate closed and its goods inventoried before they enter black markets.

He does not command the party outside the court charter. He needs its protection and discoveries more than it needs his permission.

### The Caravan of the Bent Reed

A Jirahar Road Company reaches Qasr Ilyun carrying a copied Exodus itinerary. Its oldest margin note identifies this region as **the place where the Receiver buried six households without descendants**, meaning he killed or renamed every recorded heir.

Road-keeper **Miriam bat-Oren** wants the name tablets, remains, and any testimony concerning the vanished households. She is willing to leave most portable wealth under neutral custody if the dead are named publicly.

### The Brass Jackals

The [[Brass Jackals]] are licensed salvagers led by **Daro Vessan**. They have ropes, powder, laborers, two rune-cutters, and a buyer waiting in Qasr Ilyun. Daro genuinely intends to bring everyone home, but believes buried gold belongs to whoever can carry it out.

They begin as rivals, not automatic villains. Greed, casualties, and Sethrak's influence can turn them against the party.

## Expedition Clock

The tomb remains safely accessible for four days. At dawn on Day 5, the Glass begins moving again.

- **Day 1:** clean access; the causeway and forecourt are visible.
- **Day 2:** heat haze and glass dust make surface work difficult; the second expedition arrives.
- **Day 3:** the tomb starts recovering loose objects from camps; the last expedition enters.
- **Day 4:** tremors crack the exposed complex; evacuation loads become decisive.
- **Day 5:** the storm buries or collapses every route not deliberately secured.

The clock should produce choices, not punish curiosity. Major delays change rival positions and evacuation, while essential clues remain available by more than one route.

## Adventure States

- **Estate Hold (0-4):** how strongly the tomb recognizes a living claimant. It rises through secret theft, accepting titles, and wearing estate seals. At 4, Sethrak can act through the claimant beyond the tomb.
- **Names Restored (0-4):** birth-name list, retainers, confiscated households, and public memorial. Each point weakens Estate Dead control.
- **Closure Proof (0-3):** receiver's seal, cult dissolution tablet, and complete confiscation register. All three permit formal closure.
- **Inventory Integrity (0-4):** whether removed goods and records remain identified. It falls through breakage, hidden loot, and rival theft.
- **Rival Trust (-2 to +2):** whether the Jackals sabotage, compete, cooperate, or entrust lives to the party.

## Act I — What the Storm Uncovered

### Beat 1: Glass on the Wind

At Qasr Ilyun, a caravan arrives carrying sketches of a black stone pylon rising from a newly scoured basin. By sunset, every inn knows that gold flashed behind it.

Samir reads a notice declaring the site temporarily hazardous and asks the party to witness service at the tomb. Miriam produces the itinerary note and asks that no inscribed dead be sold. Daro offers transport and a share large enough to be tempting.

The party chooses an expedition arrangement:

- **Court caravan:** slower, supplied, and able to carry the full closure kit.
- **Jirahar Road Company:** follows an older camp road, finds the memorial approach, and learns one servant name before entry.
- **Jackal sprint:** reaches the forecourt first but crosses with fewer water reserves and no formal notice.
- **Independent route:** uses Nadim's glass gullies, risking equipment to arrive between the others.

Arrival order changes the outer court. The first person to cross its threshold becomes **First Claimant** unless Samir serves notice or a Jirahar witness speaks a recovered name before entry.

### Beat 2: The Scoured Basin

The exposed estate resembles a low black processional complex rather than a great royal pyramid. Serpent pillars flank a road of fused green glass. Half the structure remains under dunes, and the storm has cut open servant galleries along one side.

Visible signs establish the tomb's logic:

- every carved figure carries a label describing office or property rather than a personal name;
- gold leaf remains bright while human faces have been deliberately worn smooth;
- chains are carved as loops returning to a central receiving hand;
- tracks from an earlier scout enter the forecourt and return as a single dragging line;
- a dead desert fox lies beside food it cannot lift its jaws from.

Nadim identifies a safe camp shelf and warns that the basin's edge has already begun to refill.

### Beat 3: Serving Notice

The receiving gate opens when a living voice states why it has come. Any answer works, but the tomb classifies it:

- “to take” creates a claimant;
- “to study” creates a witness;
- “to recover the dead” creates kin;
- “to close the place” creates an adversary;
- a lie creates an unregistered asset.

Samir can serve a **Notice of Vacant Estate**, preventing the gate from treating the entire expedition as thieves. The act requires someone to carry the notice physically into the Petition Hall while the gate begins to close.

If Samir is absent or the notice is lost, the party can gain witness standing by placing no treasure in its packs, declaring personal names, and recording the gate's answer.

### Act I Output

The party understands that words have consequences, knows who is entering behind it, and has chosen whether speed, authority, memory, or independence defines its approach.

## Act II — The Processional Machine

### Zone 1: Petition Hall

Stone petitioners kneel in five rows beneath empty title plaques. A central balance accepts one object from each entrant and stamps it with a role mark. The hall is not asking for money; it is creating jurisdiction over whatever is surrendered.

The party can:

- give a valueless but personally identified object and retain ownership;
- submit Samir's notice as the shared expedition object;
- refuse and open a difficult maintenance route;
- place stolen or anonymous treasure on the scale, increasing Estate Hold.

The first tablet fragment here identifies **Sethrak, Final Receiver of Houses Without Heirs**.

### Zone 2: The Long Causeway

A seven-hundred-pace covered road connects the receiving court to the inner estate. Halfway across, the **Processional Coil**, a massive gilded serpent-sledge, begins moving toward the party.

This replaces the original rolling-pyramid death trap with a readable set piece. The Coil moves in a fixed circuit and gathers anyone marked as property. The party can:

- reach wall niches labeled witness or kin;
- jam its runner channels with expedition gear;
- reverse its direction from a tally station;
- lure it into a collapsed section;
- board it and ride into the maintenance court;
- let it collect a marked object, then follow it to the hidden inventory route.

Failure separates characters, destroys supplies, or adds a property mark. It does not simply kill the first person struck.

### Zone 3: Inventory Court

[[Gilded Wardens]] weigh grave offerings, confiscated household goods, and the party's marked equipment. They attack people who conceal marked objects, but ignore declared witnesses until obstructed.

The court contains four useful discoveries:

- an **inventory clasp** that keeps documented objects from attaching themselves to a bearer;
- a map showing a ceremonial tomb and a smaller counting house behind it;
- six storage niches emptied during Ishkatar's collapse;
- a transport ledger listing living servants among furniture and grain.

The party can destroy the wardens, alter the inventory, or earn escorted passage by logging its own equipment. Samir's inventory seals are useful here and rarely elsewhere.

### Zone 4: The Rival Entry

The next expedition catches up. If the party arrived with Samir, Miriam and the Road Company enter. If it arrived with Miriam, the Jackals appear. If it sprinted with Daro, Samir reaches the court with a smaller escort.

The newcomers force a concrete decision:

- share the safe path and preserve Rival Trust;
- conceal it and gain time;
- divide objectives;
- demand that the rival remain as surface support;
- close the gate behind the party, leaving no help for evacuation.

### Act II Output

The party has crossed a coherent defensive system, learned that the tomb inventories people, and discovered that the obvious burial chamber is not the true destination.

## Act III — The Tomb Sethrak Wanted Found

### Zone 5: Hall of Five Mouths

Five serpent heads represent **Taking, Naming, Weighing, Sealing, and Keeping**. Pressing them in the order depicted in Sethrak's triumph friezes opens the ceremonial tomb but also confirms his doctrine.

The hidden tombwright correction is scratched beneath kneeling human figures: **Name, Witness, Weigh, Return, Close**. Using that sequence opens the same route, disables one warden group, and reveals that “Taking” and “Keeping” were later alterations.

Every clue needed for either sequence appears in the Petition Hall, Inventory Court, and friezes. Trial and error triggers glass dust, closing partitions, and warden attention rather than arbitrary death.

### Zone 6: The Ceremonial Burial

A gold sarcophagus rests beneath an immense image of Sethrak receiving tribute from smiling households. The body inside is **Tombwright Hessa**, the architect who built the false chamber and was murdered so the route would remain secret.

Hessa is Estate Dead but retains fragments of self. She attacks anyone opening the sarcophagus as a thief and speaks to anyone who uses her name, found beneath the lid or in the transport ledger.

If released, she reveals:

- the true counting house lies behind the retainers' court;
- she hid Sethrak's receiver seal inside the false death mask;
- the cult had already been dissolved when Sethrak ordered his burial completed;
- the servants were given property names so their birth names could not oppose the rite.

The **receiver seal** is Closure Proof 1. Wearing it grants command over lesser wardens but adds two Estate Hold immediately.

### Zone 7: Treasury of Easy Hands

The room beyond the false tomb displays portable gold, jeweled vessels, weapons, and preserved luxuries. It is deliberately easy to reach.

Every object taken without the inventory clasp becomes adhesive only after leaving the room. The thief is then marked as replacement custodian while wardens emerge from the walls. The danger is not a disguised monster; it is treasure behaving according to the estate's ownership rule.

Careful inventory, leaving the goods, or placing them into shared expedition custody avoids the trap. A Jackal under pressure may steal here. How the party responds determines whether Daro becomes ally, rival, or carrier of Sethrak's claim.

### Zone 8: The Crushed Chapel

A side chapel to Avarix has partly collapsed under glass. Its surviving relief shows Sethrak offering not coins but **the ability of others to own anything at all**. Here the party learns that the estate rite is Idol worship, not merely old law.

Destroying the gold tally mirror ends the tomb's ability to mark new objects but wakes every dormant Gilded Warden. Turning it away from the sun slows the system until the next dawn. Consecration, White Flame, or erasing Sethrak's receiving title permanently blinds it.

### Act III Output

The party possesses the receiver seal, understands the false tomb, and knows that possession itself is feeding Sethrak.

## Act IV — The People in the Inventory

### Zone 9: House of Retainers

The room appears to be a shaded garden court filled with music, fruit, silk, and honored attendants. It is a funerary illusion maintained by the servants' longing for the lives they were denied.

The physical room contains plain burial shelves, broken work tools, and twenty-four human remains. Twelve [[Estate Dead]] move through the illusion under property names such as Cup-Bearer Three, South-Door Girl, and Keeper of Sandals.

The party can fight them, but released servants become the adventure's most important witnesses. Their birth names are recovered through:

- Hessa's hidden list;
- matching family marks to Miriam's itinerary;
- asking about memories rather than duties;
- returning personal objects from the inventory;
- breaking the title plaques fixed above each burial shelf.

Every six servants released adds one Names Restored. Released dead do not become friendly ghosts who linger indefinitely. They speak a final memory, refuse Sethrak's summons, and pass out of the estate's machinery.

### Zone 10: Archive of Confiscations

The archive records six entire households seized during Ishkatar's last generation. Its tablets prove that debt, rebellion, hidden grain, forbidden worship, and the Receiver's personal desire were all treated as equal grounds for confiscation.

The archive is threatened by three pressures:

- one section is collapsing into a glass fissure;
- the Jackals know buyers will pay more for intact royal tablets than unnamed bones;
- Sethrak sends wardens to remove any record that describes the cult as defunct.

The party cannot carry everything before Day 5. It must prioritize:

- the complete household and birth-name register;
- the cult dissolution tablet, Closure Proof 2;
- the full confiscation register, Closure Proof 3;
- art and engineering records;
- portable gold and gems;
- the bodies and memorial objects of the retainers.

Copies, rubbings, delegation, and securing an evacuation route can preserve more than raw carrying capacity permits.

### Zone 11: The Unclaimed Scout

The dragging track from the forecourt belongs to **Faris Dun**, a Qasr Ilyun glass scout who entered alone on the day of exposure. The tomb cannot classify him because he carried no goods, spoke no purpose, and has no written kin in its archive.

He is alive in a wall niche, dehydrated and repeatedly forgetting his own reason for entry. Rescuing him proves the tomb is still taking living people. His memory of the gold mirror provides a fallback clue if the party missed the chapel.

Sethrak offers to release Faris in exchange for one entrant accepting the title of estate representative. The bargain is exact and predatory.

### Zone 12: The Seal Door

The true counting house opens through any of four methods:

- receiver seal plus the old property sequence;
- all three Closure Proofs and a declaration of extinction;
- six recovered servant names spoken by living witnesses;
- physical breach through a warden foundry, costing time and Inventory Integrity.

The chosen route changes Sethrak's final strength and opening demand.

### Act IV Output

The party has found living stakes, identified the people beneath the funerary fiction, and chosen what part of the past it can carry forward.

## Act V — The Final Receiver

### Zone 13: True Counting House

The final chamber is smaller than the false tomb. Sethrak sits upright behind a stone desk, his embalmed body wrapped in strips of old account cloth. Gold wire connects his fingers to title plaques throughout the estate. Behind him coils a serpent-shaped Gilded Warden made from funerary masks.

He addresses characters by the roles the tomb assigned, never by name. His first demand depends on Estate Hold:

- **0-1:** acknowledge that the estate exists and leave its goods.
- **2:** appoint a living representative to administer it.
- **3:** surrender the First Claimant as compensation for disturbance.
- **4:** accept Sethrak as beneficial owner of everything the claimant carries, earns, or inherits.

He is courteous because he believes the conclusion was settled before the party was born.

### Sethrak's Argument

Sethrak claims that every civilization rests on ownership, every inheritance on the wishes of the dead, and every court on force. He asks why the Covenant's sealed estates are righteous if his is monstrous.

The answer is not that all ownership is evil. His estate is monstrous because it made persons into objects, removed names to prevent appeal, fabricated consent, and continued after the authority that created it ceased to exist.

Samir can articulate this distinction, but the party must supply the proof or make the choice.

## Ways to Defeat Sethrak

### Close the Estate

Present all three Closure Proofs, identify living witnesses, and declare the cult extinct and Sethrak without principal or heir. Samir can authenticate the finding. Without him, Hessa's mark, Miriam's itinerary, and the gate's own witness classification can form a less stable closure.

Each Names Restored removes one category of servant from Sethrak's command. High Inventory Integrity prevents him from claiming the party concealed estate goods. At full proof, the gold wires break and his body becomes an ordinary dangerous residue that can be destroyed.

### Break the Receiving Machine

Destroy the tally mirror, title plaques, gold wires, and receiver seal. This ends the ritual by force. Wardens become uncontrolled, parts of the tomb collapse, and some historical records are lost, but no court ruling is required.

### Defeat the Receiver Directly

Sethrak can be fought from the outset. He uses the serpent warden, animated objects, property marks, and Estate Dead still under title. Every unresolved system makes the confrontation harder.

Defeating his body without breaking the receiver seal moves his claim into whoever carries the seal or the largest unrecorded share of treasure.

### Transfer Custody

Samir can accept the estate into temporary Covenant receivership. Sethrak loses control because a living court supersedes his office. This rescues the party and preserves the site, but gives the Covenant state every artifact and creates a dangerous precedent for claiming Giant-Age ruins.

### Name the Dead as Heirs

If Names Restored reaches 4, the party can use the confiscation register to name the murdered households as the only legitimate heirs. The estate consumes itself attempting to return everything to people Sethrak ensured had no legal descendants. The retainers reject his office and drag his residue from the body.

### Accept the Office

A living character may take the receiver seal and order Sethrak to stand down. The tomb obeys its new representative. The character leaves with Estate Hold 4 and a title that attempts to convert every later acquisition into estate property.

## Act VI — What Leaves the Desert

### Evacuation

The exposed route cannot carry every burden safely on Day 4. The party must allocate people and transport among:

- Faris Dun and any injured expedition members;
- recovered remains and memorial objects;
- household and birth-name tablets;
- Closure Proofs and Sethrak's body or seal;
- historical art and engineering records;
- gold, jewels, and ritual objects;
- rival casualties and abandoned equipment.

The Jackals carry more than anyone else if Rival Trust is positive. The Jirahar Road Company protects names and remains first. Samir prioritizes dangerous seals and complete evidence. No faction has enough capacity to preserve everything alone.

### The Returning Glass

If the party leaves before Day 5, the storm becomes a pursuit across the causeway and basin. Gilded Wardens seek marked goods rather than simply killing escapees. Dropping unrecorded treasure can buy time; dropping name tablets cannot.

If the party remains into Day 5, it must choose between sealing the tomb under Glass, holding a protected route with rune-cutters, or sheltering inside until another storm cycle exposes an exit. Being entombed is a campaign complication, not an unavoidable ending.

## Adjudication at Qasr Ilyun

Samir convenes a public breach hearing only if he, a Closure Proof, or a living witness returns. Miriam demands that every recoverable name be read before property is divided. Daro demands shares for labor, risk, and dead Jackals.

The strongest settlement is:

- human remains and personal memorial objects go to Jirahar-led reburial under open witness;
- name and confiscation records are copied for the tower towns, court, and public archive;
- dangerous Avarix relics are sealed or destroyed;
- ordinary grave wealth funds expedition shares, compensation, desert rescue, and restitution to identifiable communities;
- the site is marked as an Ishkatar crime-estate, not celebrated as a lost golden age.

This resolution requires negotiation, but the adventure does not turn its climax into a hearing. The tomb has already forced the meaningful choices.

## Ending States

### The Estate Is Closed and the Names Returned

Closure Proof is 3, Names Restored is 3+, and no living claimant leaves at Estate Hold 4. Sethrak's office ends, the servants pass beyond his control, and the household records reach the Jirahar network. This is the most restorative continuation.

### The Heirs of No One

Names Restored reaches 4 and the dead households inherit the estate. Most wealth is committed to reburial, memorial, and restitution. The ruling becomes a famous challenge to both Covenant confiscation law and treasure-hunter custom.

### Covenant Receivership

Samir transfers the tomb into living court custody. The dead are released and Sethrak is contained, but the breach-court gains control of Ishkatar seals capable of recognizing ownership metaphysically. Honest jurists call this necessary quarantine; ambitious officials call it precedent.

### The Jackals' Share

Daro or the party extracts substantial unrecorded treasure. The tomb is defeated, but its property marks survive on the gold. Over following months, caches slide toward one another, buyers dream of Sethrak's counting house, and one bearer begins signing receipts as Final Receiver.

### The Tomb of the Bent Reed

Miriam takes primary custody, seals the gold, and turns the basin into a Jirahar memorial site. The names are protected, but the Covenant accuses the Road Company of removing artifacts under a religious claim it never submitted to court.

### Buried Under Glass

The party escapes without closing the estate. The storm covers it, trapping Sethrak and any abandoned people or evidence. The site will rise again after another glass-storm, and next time someone may arrive without warning or an assessor.

### The Receiver Walks

A living person accepts the seal or leaves at Estate Hold 4. Sethrak cannot leave in his embalmed body, but he now acts through a legal representative who accumulates wealth, agents, and title in the living world.

## What the Adventure Changes

- A minor Ishkatar estate becomes the first fully documented example of funerary ownership magic.
- The Jirahar recover names and testimony from households erased before the Exodus.
- Qasr Ilyun establishes a ruling on whether Giant-Age slave estates can possess anything in the present.
- The Brass Jackals become trusted ruin specialists, cursed rivals, or casualties whose survivors blame the party.
- Estate Dead and Gilded Wardens enter the known bestiary of desert expeditions.
- Avarix's influence is shown through possession, classification, and appetite rather than generic golden monsters.
- The cause of the Great Glass Desert remains unresolved. The storm exposed the tomb; the tomb did not create the Glass.

## Encounter and Creature Register

- Glass-storm, heat, sharp dunes, and collapsing exposure: environmental pressure with visible preparation.
- Processional Coil: a unique [[Gilded Wardens|Gilded Warden]] transport construct.
- Inventory wardens and serpent-mask guardian: [[Gilded Wardens]].
- Tombwright Hessa and the sacrificed household: [[Estate Dead]].
- Adhesive grave goods and title plaques: estate mechanisms, not unrelated creatures.
