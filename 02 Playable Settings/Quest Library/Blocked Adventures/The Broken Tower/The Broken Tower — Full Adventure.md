# The Broken Tower — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** An earthquake has cracked open the [[The Kestrel Tower]], a long-sealed Atherian-Eclessian frontier fort near Dornwatch. A [[The Medori Bank|Bank]] surveyor went in to assess the exposed ruin and did not come out. The party is hired to bring him back. It is a level-1 delver's job, and a clean tutorial in the trade.

**Beginning:** At Dornwatch, two wounded road-keepers report that an aftershock trapped a small survey party inside the tower. A Bank factor posts the recovery job because one of the missing is his surveyor. The five-floor vertical delve contains unstable masonry, [[Harridans]], and old residue before any human conflict appears.

**Middle:** The party climbs floor by floor — clearing rattle-bones and barrow-shades with iron and silver, driving off the harridan flock, and reading the tower's three layers of dead — until they reach the surveyor's captor: [[Torvald Ghost-Foot]], a Folk exile who is holding the man not out of malice but as the only leverage he has against a Bank that wronged him.

**End:** The finale is a standoff, not a boss fight. The party can storm the top floor, talk Torvald down, or broker his grievance while getting the exhausted survey party safely through a tower that is still collapsing. The Bank's interest in the site matters afterward; it does not replace the rescue.

**Tier:** R starter. Teaches careful advance, resource choice (iron for bones, silver for shades), rescue under environmental pressure, and when to fight or talk.

**Expected length:** 2-3 sessions: the job in Dornwatch, the five-floor climb, and the standoff at the top.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; the Bank factor who posts the job; [[Torvald Ghost-Foot]], the exile holding the tower; Ivo Kesler, the captured Bank surveyor; and the tower's Tier One dead.
- **What:** find the missing survey party, bring every survivor out alive, and decide what to do about Torvald's grievance once it comes to light.
- **Where:** Dornwatch, a small march town on the Grenzburg approaches inside the Grenzburg marches, and the earthquake-split Kestrel Tower a half-day out.
- **Why the party acts:** honest pay for a recovery job; the rumor of an exposed ruin's treasure; or a first commission that needs no guild, no warrant, and no theology.
- **Why the danger exists:** the quake broke a sealed fort, animating its long-settled residue and opening it to a harridan flock and a desperate exile with nowhere else to make his case.
- **How it escalates:** the climb wakes more residue the deeper it goes, the harridans harry any noisy party, and the standoff at the top turns on whether the crew arrives believing they are rescuing a victim from a monster.

## Moral and Metaphysical Ruling

The tower's dead are not people and not a crisis. They are residue — the shed echoes of three garrisons' worth of death, animating because the quake broke the rites and years that had drained them. Putting them down is sexton-work: iron for the rattle-bones, silver for the shades, and a consecration after if a priest is passing. No soul is in danger. This is the load-bearing lesson of the delver trade, and the tower teaches it gently.

Torvald Ghost-Foot is not residue and not a monster. He is a wronged man doing a wrong thing. Taking a hostage is indefensible; the grievance behind it is real, documented, and owed. The adventure should let the party discover, mid-climb, that the "master of the tower" they were sent to stop has a truer claim than the contract that sent them.

The Bank's conduct is shabby but secondary. Its factor understates the danger because one missing employee matters more to him than the local guides. The institution may later claim survey rights, but it did not cause the quake, the residue, the harridans, or Torvald's choice to take a hostage.

The strongest resolution brings every survivor out, frees the surveyor without pretending Torvald's grievance is nothing, and records the tower as unsafe until a proper stabilization crew arrives.

## The Central Truth

The Kestrel Tower was an Atherian watch-fort later held by successive Eclessian marcher garrisons that changed hands three times across two centuries of frontier war, and buried its dead in place each time. When the last war moved on, the fort was sealed, its charter filed, and its residue left to drain into quiet. It nearly had.

Two seasons ago the ground shifted. An earthquake split the tower, cracked its sealed floors open, and — as quakes do to old death-soaked stone — stirred the residue back to motion. A flock of [[Harridans]] moved into the broken lower floors within a month, drawn to the old death the way they always are.

Torvald Ghost-Foot came later, and for a reason. He is an exile of the [[The Folk Clans|Ghost-Foot]] clan, cast out after a Bank survey condemned his family's holding on a technicality of frontier title — a survey he says was wrong, and the record, if anyone read it, agrees. He appealed and was told the matter was closed; his clan, embarrassed, put him out. He came to the Kestrel Tower because it was empty, defensible, and *his* by the only law he had left: he cleared the top three floors himself and lived there.

When Ivo Kesler and two local guides became trapped above the collapsed stair, Torvald helped them reach the top floor. He then recognized the Bank seal on Kesler's satchel and refused to let him leave. Kesler is bound but unharmed; the guides are free, injured, and furious at both men. Torvald wants the Bank to admit the condemnation was wrong and restore his family's title. His grievance is genuine. Turning a rescue into a hostage-taking is still his wrong.

The party is hired to find the whole survey party. The factor speaks only about Kesler until the road-keepers name the two local guides who went in with him.

## The Tower, the Bank, and the Trade

The [[The Kestrel Tower]] is a textbook delving site: an Atherian-Eclessian frontier fort with three layers of dead, cracked open by a quake, holding Tier One residue, a vermin flock, a squatter, and somebody's lost strongbox. It is exactly the kind of place the trade runs on.

The [[The Medori Bank]] is canon Grenzburg: the frontier's lender, surveyor, and charter-holder, whose paperwork decides who owns what on ground the Duke can barely reach. Here it is not a villain so much as an institution behaving as institutions do — cheap with its foot soldiers, quiet about its windfalls, and legally correct while being wrong.

The **delver trade** is the tutorial's practical frame. The party learns its tools (iron, silver, torches, rope, patience), its bestiary (rattle-bones, barrow-shades, harridans), and its first rule: living people come out before relics or claims are counted.

## Adventure States

- **Delve Depth (floors 1-5):** how far the party has climbed; each floor up wakes more residue and brings them nearer the standoff.
- **Noise (0-3):** how loudly the party has delved. High Noise brings the harridan flock down on them and warns Torvald they are coming.
- **Torvald's Nerve (0-3):** whether the exile is ready to talk, to bargain, or to do something desperate to his hostage. Kindness, evidence, and Kesler's safety lower the danger; a stormed floor raises it.
- **What the Party Knows (0-3):** the missing guides, Torvald's grievance, and Kesler's agreement that the old survey was wrong. Each fact changes the finale.

## The Delve — Floor by Floor

The Kestrel Tower rises in five floors. The party climbs; each floor is a small cluster of chambers to clear before the stair up. The residue is native Tier One and the tutorial is in the doing.

### Floor One — The Cracked Gate

The quake split the gate-floor open. Grave-rats and corpse-beetles work the rubble; a single rattle-bones or two lurch up from a collapsed guard-niche. The lesson is the first proverb: **iron for bones.** A torch, a mace, and a careful advance clear it. A lost coin-purse in the rubble is the trade's first small reward and the first taste of salvage.

### Floor Two — The Roost

The harridan flock nests in the broken second floor, where the wall is open to the sky. This is the delve's big vermin fight: a flapping, shrieking swarm that punishes a loud party and can be avoided or driven off by a clever one. **Noise** learned here is the tutorial's second lesson — the tower is listening. Beyond the roost, a barred store-room holds the fort's old armory: iron and, crucially, a little silver.

### Floor Three — The Cold Floor

The middle floor is where the fort buried its officers, and where the residue is coldest. Barrow-shades stand in the dark, slow and bound to their stones. Iron passes through them; this is the second proverb learned the hard way: **silver for shades.** The silver from the armory above is the answer, and a party that skipped it must improvise or retreat. Names on the old interment-stones — three garrisons, three owners — quietly tell the tower's history to anyone who reads.

### Floor Four — Torvald's Line

The fourth floor is lived-in: a barred door, a cook-fire's smoke, snares and alarms rigged from fort debris. This is Torvald's outer line, and the first place the party can *talk* instead of fight. A parley here — or a captured snare-note, or Kesler's voice through the floor — can reveal that the "master of the tower" is one desperate man, not a warlord. **What the Party Knows** can jump here if they listen instead of breach.

### Floor Five — The Top

The stair opens onto the broken top floor, open to the wind, where Torvald keeps Ivo Kesler and makes his stand. This is the standoff, and how it resolves depends entirely on what the party learned climbing to it.

## The Standoff at the Top

The finale is deliberately not a boss fight, though it can become one. Torvald has the surveyor, a real grievance, and nothing left to lose; the party has force, leverage, and — if they used the climb well — the truth.

Ways it can go:

- **Storm it.** The party rushes the top floor. Torvald is a single desperate man and losable, but the surveyor is in the middle of it, and a stormed standoff is the outcome most likely to get Kesler hurt. The tutorial's warning about kicking every door.
- **Talk him down.** If the party arrives understanding the grievance — from the interment-stones, the snare-note, or Kesler's own words — they can offer Torvald something better than a hostage: witnesses, a written record, a route to a hearing. His **Nerve** falls; he releases Kesler.
- **Broker the grievance.** Kesler, freed or trusted, will testify that the old survey was wrong. The party can carry that back and force the correction — the most restorative branch, and one the Bank would rather buy quietly than let go public.
- **Carry out the record.** Kesler's testimony and Torvald's surviving papers create a real path to reopening the old survey once everyone is safely outside.

## Ways to Resolve the Conflict

### The Clean Rescue

Bring Kesler and both guides out alive. The party is paid the posted rate; Torvald's grievance remains unheard. The rescue succeeds even if the injustice remains for another day.

### The Corrected Record

Free Kesler and carry his testimony that the survey was wrong. The Bank corrects the old condemnation — cheaper than the scandal — and Torvald's family title is restored. He surrenders, exiled no longer. The best ending, earned by listening on the way up.

### The Unsafe Site

The party records the cracked foundations, active residue, and harridan access. Dornwatch bars casual salvage until the tower is stabilized. This prevents another disappearance and leaves later ownership to a separate dispute.

### The Stormed Top

The party takes the top floor by force. Torvald falls or flees; Kesler may or may not come through it whole. The job is technically done, the grievance dies with the standoff, and the veterans back in Dornwatch have a quiet word about kicking doors.

## Restoration and Aftermath

The tower's residue does not "restore"; it is put down and the disturbed burials are reconsecrated. The Kestrel Tower remains an unsafe ruin until masons shore its cracked core.

Torvald is not redeemed by being defeated. He is redeemed, if at all, by being *heard* — the correction of a wrong survey, not the storming of a top floor. The adventure's small moral is that the man the contract called a monster had the better paperwork all along.

## Ending States Summary

- **Best:** every survivor freed, the survey corrected, Torvald restored, and the tower closed until stabilized.
- **Good:** every survivor rescued even if the grievance goes only half-heard.
- **Default:** Kesler rescued while one or both local guides are lost or overlooked; the party learns to ask who an employer has omitted.
- **Rough:** the top floor stormed, the surveyor endangered, the grievance buried with its holder.

## What the Adventure Changes

- The party makes its first name in the delver trade and learns its tools, bestiary, and law.
- The [[The Kestrel Tower]] is stabilized, sealed, or left as a known dangerous ruin for later work.
- [[Torvald Ghost-Foot]] becomes a restored clansman, a fugitive, or a cautionary name in Dornwatch.
- The [[The Medori Bank]] is revealed as powerful and selective about whose lives it counts, without becoming the cause of every danger in the tower.
- [[Harridans]] enter the party's known bestiary as ordinary, handleable vermin — the trade's bread.
