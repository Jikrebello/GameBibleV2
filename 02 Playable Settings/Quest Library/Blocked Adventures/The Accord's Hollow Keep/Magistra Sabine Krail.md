# Magistra Sabine Krail

Sabine Krail is a dismissed investigator of the Order of the Lantern who specializes in false relics, battlefield apparitions, and memory attached to material objects. She engineered the present crisis at [[Hollow Keep]] to extract and reproduce [[The Hollow Muster]].

## Former Office

Sabine was effective at distinguishing saintly intercession from grief-made phenomena. She was dismissed after using family testimony and personal possessions to strengthen an apparition deliberately, arguing that a controlled false saint could expose uncontrolled ones.

Her superiors condemned the experiment as spiritual abuse. Sabine concluded that the Church feared any method it could not monopolize.

## Objective

Sabine wants a portable system able to:

- copy a sentry's routine memory;
- leave an obedient replacement at the post;
- confine the living original away from witnesses;
- carry commands through a name register;
- and place the copied identity into a prepared body or thin space.

She presents this as a way to spare soldiers. Her actual method requires nonconsensual copying, captivity, and repeated exposure to death memory.

## Manipulation of Othmar

Sabine told Othmar the truth about Hannes's hidden death and the existence of an echo. She never promised in writing that the whole man remained, but carefully encouraged Othmar to hear that promise.

She supplied the excavation route, pattern objects, forged-order method, and Grey Pike contact. Othmar supplied command authority, family memories, and someone else to blame if the keep fell.

## Presence and Tactics

Sabine prepares rooms before fighting. She uses:

- a Hollow Sentry double wearing her face;
- copied voices issuing orders from wrong corridors;
- name tabs that redirect echoes toward specific targets;
- frightened living soldiers as cover;
- invisibility, misdirection, or setting-appropriate relic craft rather than battlefield dominance.

She bargains readily and retreats if she can preserve the register or a copied identity. Her willingness to die depends on whether escape would leave her research alive.

## Moral Position

Sabine is not merely a reformer condemned by a conservative Church. Her critique of institutional secrecy contains truth, but she knowingly reduces persons to reproducible functions. She believes consent is an obstacle imposed by people too sentimental to use discovery efficiently.

## Continuation

If she escapes, Sabine seeks archives, barracks, bridgehouses, and prisons where routine identities are easier to copy than intimate lives. Her perfect target is an institution that values the uniform more than the person inside it.
