# Accord Wardens

Accord Wardens are mixed-confession soldiers, clerks, guides, and beacon-keepers assigned to roads and watchhouses whose exclusive occupation would threaten [[The Korrheim Accord]]. They are a practical border institution rather than a knightly order.

## Purpose

Wardens keep crossings open, verify troop movements, escort displaced clergy and households, maintain shared rescue stores, and report armed confessional breaches before local retaliation becomes a campaign.

Their presence does not erase territorial rule. They exist where exercising that rule without witnesses would be unusually dangerous.

## Composition

A watch normally includes:

- a commander acceptable to both local signatories;
- soldiers drawn from more than one rite or polity;
- a clerk keeping duty and passage registers;
- signalers trained on both beacon networks;
- rotating auxiliaries supplied by neighboring rulers.

Wardens swear to the peace schedule and assigned road, not to theological neutrality. Individual members retain their confession.

## Strengths

- knowledge of crossings, secret passages, and relief protocols;
- credibility as witnesses when both sides remain represented;
- training in separating armed groups before investigation;
- shared stores and signals;
- familiarity with forged orders and partisan provocations.

## Weaknesses

- accused of divided loyalty by every hardliner;
- command becomes uncertain when one confession loses personnel;
- dependent on signatories that may prefer convenient reports;
- records can become tools of surveillance or confessional sorting;
- small detachments cannot withstand either neighboring army.

## Hollow Keep Detachment

The Hollow Keep wardens are led by Warden-Captain Rohe until [[Othmar Vale]] kills him during the breach. Corporal Mara Eltz becomes senior surviving warden and preserves six people inside the defender passages while five others remain trapped in [[The Hollow Muster]].

## Story Use

Accord Wardens support escort stories, border investigations, mixed garrisons, forged-order crises, succession disputes, and campaigns where keeping peace requires courage without pretending the underlying beliefs are meaningless.
