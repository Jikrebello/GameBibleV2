# Lord Lucan Harrowfen

Lucan Harrowfen is the young principal claimant to a small Sylvan Marches lordship. He was educated outside the frontier and returned after his father's death with more ideas about good rule than experience of what Harrowfen can afford.

## Public Position

Lucan needs Waybrand, the Rain Cup, and the Stag Seal to receive militia, township, and Ranger recognition at Last-Fire. He hires the player crew because Harrowfen household soldiers carry his grandfather's authority and provoke the Old Wood immediately.

He initially describes the recovery as an ancestral trial. This is partly ignorance and partly cowardice. He has read Othmar's public testament and heard rumors of a broken compact, but has avoided asking Eydis Grey-Withy for the full account.

## Character

- Earnest, observant, and uncomfortable with public humiliation.
- Better at listening than deciding when every option costs someone.
- Wants reconciliation but instinctively protects the estate that gives him power.
- Respects Church teaching and initially assumes Folk pact-law is superstition with political consequences.
- Capable of genuine repentance once forced to connect inherited wealth to a specific wrong.

## What He Stands to Lose

Restoring Coldwater destroys the estate's largest sawmill. Reopening the Hart Road weakens toll income and limits patrol control. Joint witness over the Alder Grove creates a permanent Folk claim inside Harrowfen.

These costs do not excuse Othmar's actions. They make Lucan's choice meaningful. A version of the adventure where restitution is free does not test him.

## Resolve Track

- **0:** wants the heirlooms and treats restitution as a later political question.
- **1:** accepts that Othmar broke a real compact but hopes symbolic payment will settle it.
- **2:** agrees to concrete terms and accompanies the party to Hartrest Lodge.
- **3:** renews the compact without demanding the relics in return and presents the confession publicly.

Lucan gains Resolve when shown evidence alongside a workable material response. Shame without a path forward makes him defensive; concealment leaves him weak when the final cost appears.

## Continuation

Under the strongest ending, Lucan becomes a constrained but legitimate frontier lord. He remains vulnerable to poor harvests, Sabine's criticism, Church suspicion, and Folk demands that the compact apply in cases he did not anticipate. His success is the beginning of difficult rule, not proof that one good promise solved the Marches.
