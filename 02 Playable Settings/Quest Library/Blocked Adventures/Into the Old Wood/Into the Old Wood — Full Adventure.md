# Into the Old Wood — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** A young Marcher heir hires the party to recover three family heirlooms from the Old Wood. He believes this is an inheritance trial. The wood remembers it as collection of a debt.

**Beginning:** At the Last Lantern inn, [[Lord Lucan Harrowfen]] offers the job and presents his grandfather's misleading account. Folk witness Eydis Grey-Withy names the three breaches behind the forfeit. Lucan's cousin announces that [[The Briar Lances]] will compete for the same relics.

**Middle:** The party enters a Faewild-thin forest and follows an oath-cairn to three sites: the barred Hart Road, the drowned mill, and the ruined Hartrest Lodge. Each heirloom can be taken through force or redeemed through practical restitution.

**End:** At the lodge, the party confronts an [[Oathwood Wardens|Oathwood Warden]] formed around the murdered witness Alda Grey-Withy. Lucan must either renew the compact, reject it, or lose his inheritance to his cousin or the wood.

**Tier:** R starter. The adventure teaches exploration, environmental reading, parley, limited combat, resource choices, and consequences without requiring knowledge of the wider setting.

**Expected length:** 3-5 substantial sessions: inn and boundary, two open forest sites, lodge finale, and inheritance moot.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; heir Lucan Harrowfen; his cousin Sabine Harrowfen; Eydis Grey-Withy; ranger Ansel Reed; the Briar Lances; and the memory of Alda Grey-Withy.
- **What:** recover Waybrand, the Rain Cup, and the Stag Seal before the Harrowfen moot, then decide whether their return proves ownership or restitution.
- **Where:** Harrowfen in the [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Eclessia/60 Realms and Peoples/Sylvan Marches/The Sylvan Marches Overview.md|Sylvan Marches]], the Last Lantern, the Old Hart Road, Coldwater Mill, and Hartrest Lodge inside the Old Wood.
- **Why the party acts:** Lucan offers honest pay and standing in the Marches; the Rangers need neutral outsiders; Eydis asks the party to make the truth unavoidable; or the crew needs a first commission that does not require military service.
- **Why the danger exists:** Othmar Harrowfen swore a witnessed compact for his survival and later broke every term. The Old Wood is a [[Thin Spaces|Faewild thin space]] whose territorial memory still enforces the forfeit.
- **How it escalates:** the competing crews disturb the sites, the wood begins assigning them the old participants' roles, and Lucan learns that receiving the relics will bind him to restitution his estate can barely afford.

## Moral and Metaphysical Ruling

The Old Wood is not morally infallible and Sylvorn is not the Lord. Faewild land-law preserves function, territory, and witnessed exchange; it can enforce a true promise without understanding mercy, repentance, or human salvation.

Othmar's wrongdoing is nevertheless real. He accepted sanctuary, swore terms, profited from breaking them, and killed a protected witness. Repairing those wrongs does not require worship of the Menhir. A Church character can distinguish honoring truth and returning stolen use from offering devotion to the power enforcing it.

The guardians may be fought when they threaten innocent life. The strongest resolution uses force to protect people while refusing to treat violence as proof that the old debt was false.

## The Harrowfen Compact

Sixty-two years ago, Othmar Harrowfen and six retainers became lost in an early Long Dark storm. Alda Grey-Withy brought them to a Folk hearth beneath the Old Wood's hollow oak. Othmar accepted salt, fire, and three nights of protection.

In return, he swore before Alda, his own sword, and the listening roots that if he survived to hold Harrowfen:

1. **The Old Hart Road would remain open** to settler, Folk, animal migration, and winter flight.
2. **Coldwater Run would remain unbarred**, with no toll placed on drinking water or spawning fish.
3. **The Alder Witness Grove would remain standing**, and any Grey-Withy entering Hartrest Lodge under salt would leave unharmed.

Othmar kept the compact for nine years. Then he fortified the road, dammed Coldwater Run for a profitable sawmill, and cut the grove for Hartrest Lodge. When Alda came to demand witness, Othmar accepted her salt and had his huntsmen kill her after supper. Her body was hidden beneath the lodge hearth.

At Othmar's funeral, roots broke through the chapel floor and took three objects from his bier:

- **Waybrand**, the sword on which he swore the road open.
- **The Rain Cup**, used to share Coldwater with his rescuers.
- **The Stag Seal**, with which he later ordered the road barred and the grove cut.

His public testament says only that the heirlooms were “returned to the wood until a worthy heir should call them home.” His private confession, divided among the three sites, states what happened.

## Why the Inheritance Matters

Harrowfen is a frontier lordship held together by recognition rather than a strong central court. The militia swears before Waybrand, township elders drink from the Rain Cup, and the Rangers recognize orders bearing the Stag Seal. A claimant missing any one can possess the manor but cannot reliably command the lordship.

Lucan has until the Last-Fire moot to present all three. His cousin Sabine argues that Harrowfen needs a decisive ruler, not a penitent one. She hires the Briar Lances to recover any heirloom first. Possessing one lets her block Lucan's acclamation and force a divided settlement.

## Villain and Opposition Structure

- **Original wrongdoer, now dead: Othmar Harrowfen.** His choices created every supernatural danger in the adventure.
- **Final guardian: Alda's Oathwood Warden.** It protects the compact and trapped memory but increasingly treats living people as replacements for the original oath-breakers.
- **Political rival: Sabine Harrowfen.** She genuinely believes renewed concessions will impoverish the estate and expose it to Folk control.
- **Field rivals: [[The Briar Lances]].** They are paid to recover one relic, not slaughter beginners in the forest.
- **The Old Wood:** locally lawful, dangerous, and incapable of deciding whether restitution has become repentance.

## Adventure States

- **Wood's Hostility (0-4):** rises through broken hospitality, needless killing, fire, and forced removal of heirlooms.
- **Debt Understood (0-3):** one point for recovering the truth preserved at each heirloom site.
- **Rival Trust (0-3):** determines whether the Briar Lances compete cleanly, abandon one another, or stand against Sabine's worst order.
- **Lucan's Resolve (0-3):** rises when the party confronts him with evidence and makes him participate in restitution.

## Act I — The Last Lantern

### Beat 1: A Roadside Warning

On the road to Harrowfen, the party finds a Briar Lance pack pony tangled in thorn roots. Its rider is missing, and its baggage contains axes, climbing rope, a charcoal map, and salt that has never been opened.

The party can free the animal, track the rider to the Last Lantern, or take the supplies. The scene teaches three things without controlling the plot:

- another crew has entered the region;
- the forest reacts to tools associated with cutting;
- unopened salt will matter at thresholds.

The missing rider, Toma Pike, reached the inn on foot and falsely claims the party stole the pony. Corin Ash accepts visible evidence and does not force a fight.

### Beat 2: The Competing Offers

Lucan meets the party at the Last Lantern, a fortified coaching inn built where the maintained road ends. He offers half payment immediately and the remainder for all three heirlooms. He presents Othmar's public testament and describes the expedition as a test of courage.

Sabine arrives with the Briar Lances and announces her competing claim. She does not challenge Lucan to a tavern brawl. She states plainly that whichever claimant controls the symbols at Last-Fire controls Harrowfen.

Eydis Grey-Withy then corrects both accounts before the common room. The relics were not hidden. They were forfeited. She names the three compact terms but withholds Alda's murder because her clan promised to speak it only at the lodge or before an heir willing to hear.

Lucan still asks the party to proceed. He cannot command Eydis or the Old Wood, and his household guard carries Othmar's authority too strongly to pass the boundary safely.

### Beat 3: The Oath-Cairn

Ansel guides both crews to the boundary and leaves them at a root-cairn. Three inscriptions describe the sites:

- **Where iron stopped the antler, the road remembers the blade.**
- **Where wheel drank winter, the cup waits beneath still water.**
- **Where salt was answered with blood, the seal keeps an empty house.**

Each entrant must place salt, state a true name, and say whether they enter as guest, hunter, claimant, or witness.

- **Guest:** receives safer rest but may not take food or shelter without asking.
- **Hunter:** may kill animals threatening the crew but cannot claim relics by right.
- **Claimant:** can touch heirlooms but inherits marks from Othmar's role.
- **Witness:** sees hidden memories but is repeatedly drawn toward Alda's death.

Lying at the cairn raises Wood's Hostility. Choosing different roles gives the party complementary ways to solve later sites.

### Act I Output

The party has accepted a voluntary commission, knows the relics represent a forfeit, understands the basic etiquette of the thin space, and can choose the order of the first two sites.

## Act II — Three Things the Wood Remembers

### Site 1: The Barred Hart Road

Othmar built a timber gate and iron toll-chain across an animal migration trail and Folk winter road. The gatehouse has collapsed into a bear den. Waybrand lies beneath the chain, held in place by roots. A mother bear and two half-grown cubs occupy the ruins.

**Signs:** clawed toll boards, deer tracks turning away from the old road, iron links grown into living roots, and the smell of a recent kill.

#### Taking Waybrand

The party can drive off or kill the bears, cut the roots, and pull the sword free. Doing so is quick. The toll-chain reattaches behind them, animal paths begin closing, and Wood's Hostility rises.

#### Redeeming Waybrand

The party must dismantle the gate, remove its buried iron footings, and reopen enough trail for a waiting deer herd to cross. The bear can be distracted with carcass meat, calmed from a distance, or allowed to move her cubs after the work stops threatening the den.

Waybrand then rises from the soil cleanly. Its pommel contains the first portion of Othmar's confession: he closed the road to collect timber tolls and prevent Folk kin from reaching displaced graves.

#### Rival Intersection

Toma Pike tries to smoke out the bears. Corin stops him if Rival Trust is at least 1. The party can rescue a Briar Lance caught beneath the falling gate, trade tools, or let the rivals absorb the danger.

### Site 2: Coldwater Mill

The abandoned sawmill still dams Coldwater Run. Its wheel turns despite the stagnant pool. The Rain Cup rests in the flooded wheel-house, watched by the **Reed-Daughter**, a Cloven water-being formed where the stream's Faewild law presses through.

The Reed-Daughter is dangerous but intelligible. She wants the water moving, spawning beds reachable, and the body of a mill child removed from the jammed sluice. She does not care who owns Harrowfen.

**Signs:** fish striking the dam without fleeing people, reeds growing through floorboards, wet footprints leading into walls, and the wheel turning against the current.

#### Taking the Rain Cup

The party can descend through the flooded wheel-house, fight or distract the Reed-Daughter, and take the cup. The dam remains. Coldwater follows the relic in a sudden flood, endangering rivals and downstream cottages. Wood's Hostility rises.

#### Redeeming the Rain Cup

The party opens the old flood channel, removes the child's remains, and breaks the central sluice. This drains the pool gradually and restores the stream. It also destroys the mill that supplies much of Harrowfen's sawn timber.

The Rain Cup floats to shore. The child's belt contains the second confession fragment: Othmar knew the dam drowned a shared fishery and ordered work to continue after two laborers died.

#### Rival Intersection

Sabine ordered the mill preserved. The Briar Lances attempt a controlled theft of the cup. The party can show them the trapped body, bargain for time, race underwater, or fight across failing walkways. Saving a rival from the flood raises Rival Trust even if the cup remains contested.

### Returning to the Boundary Camp

After the first two relics, the wood opens a path back to the cairn. Lucan and Sabine wait outside with Eydis and Ansel.

The party must decide what to reveal. Each confession shown to Lucan raises his Resolve if the party also explains the material cost of restitution. Concealing the evidence keeps him cooperative now but weakens the final compact.

Eydis asks Lucan to accompany the party to Hartrest Lodge. He may refuse unless Resolve is at least 1. The party can bring him as a vulnerable noncombatant, carry his written pledge, or proceed without him. Only his personal presence permits the most restorative branch.

### Act II Output

The party controls zero to two heirlooms, understands at least part of Othmar's wrong, and has determined whether the rival crew sees it as enemy, competition, or fellow expedition.

## Act III — Hartrest Lodge

### The Approach

Hartrest Lodge stands inside the stumps of the Alder Witness Grove. It was built from the trees Othmar swore to preserve. No birds nest in its roof. Smoke rises from the chimney although rain has rotted the interior.

The main door opens only when a visitor offers salt. Anyone who enters without doing so appears inside the old supper repeatedly until another person invites them across the threshold.

### Zone 1: The Empty Supper

The dining room reenacts Alda's final meal through impressions rather than ghosts. Each seat shows one moment: salt accepted, Othmar's fear of losing the mill, the order whispered to his huntsmen, Alda realizing the hearth will not protect her, and the body dragged below.

Characters can recover the complete truth by sitting through the memory without changing places. Interfering early causes the lodge to assign them roles from the murder. A Witness can speak inside the scene and ask Alda one question.

### Zone 2: Huntsmen in the Walls

Othmar's dead huntsmen are preserved as wooden outlines within the lodge timbers. They emerge when someone draws Waybrand or presents Lucan as lord. They obey the last order: prevent Alda from leaving.

The party can defeat them, name their individual refusal and fear from the supper memories, or open every door and window so the command no longer has a contained house to govern.

### Zone 3: The Root Hearth

Beneath the hearth lies Alda's cairn and the Stag Seal. Roots, lodge beams, antler, and her remaining memory have formed an [[Oathwood Wardens|Oathwood Warden]]. It first attacks anyone carrying a forced heirloom and anyone bearing Othmar's blood.

The Warden is not Alda restored. It remembers her demand and pain but has lost much of her ability to distinguish descendant from murderer. If allowed to complete its law without interpretation, it will root Lucan into the hearth as Othmar's replacement.

### Final Confrontation

The scene has four active sides:

1. The party, carrying the methods and evidence chosen at earlier sites.
2. Lucan, if brought, who must decide whether restitution is worth his inheritance.
3. The Briar Lances, seeking the Stag Seal for Sabine.
4. The Oathwood Warden, enforcing the compact through repetition.

The party can combine the following solutions:

- speak Alda's personal name and recount her life beyond the compact;
- lay voluntarily redeemed heirlooms at the cairn;
- have Lucan renew all three terms without claiming that his blood entitles him to forgiveness;
- expose Othmar's confession to the rival crew;
- open the lodge and return its cut timbers to the grove;
- fight the Warden long enough to separate Alda's memory-knot from its body;
- destroy the Warden and seize the Stag Seal.

If the first two heirlooms were taken by force, the Warden gains their memories as weapons: Waybrand seals exits and the Rain Cup floods the root cellar. If they were redeemed, each can disable one of those effects.

### The Rival Choice

At Rival Trust 0, the Briar Lances wait for combat and seize the Seal. At 1, Corin offers a temporary truce. At 2, the crew refuses to attack Lucan during the rite. At 3, it turns on Sabine's personal enforcer when ordered to burn the lodge with everyone inside.

Sabine herself arrives only if the party invited both claimants to witness the truth. She can still become lord, but must publicly state whether she accepts the same compact. She is not allowed a consequence-free position outside the moral test.

### Act III Output

The Stag Seal is seized, granted, retained by the wood, or destroyed. Alda's memory is freed, lost, or further bound. Lucan and Sabine have each been forced to state what kind of rule they believe Harrowfen requires.

## Act IV — Last-Fire at Harrowfen

The adventure ends at the acclamation moot. Militia households, township elders, Rangers, Church witnesses, mill families, and Folk speakers gather around three empty stands for the heirlooms.

The party presents objects and testimony. Possessing all three proves practical control, but not necessarily legitimacy. The audience responds to visible consequences:

- Is the Hart Road actually open?
- Is Coldwater moving, and what replaces the mill's livelihood?
- Was Alda named, and are the lodge timbers being returned?
- Did either claimant risk anything personally?
- Did the party treat rivals and guardians as people or obstacles?

The strongest result needs a material plan, not only a good speech. Lucan can replace the sawmill with smaller winter-cut yards outside the watershed, pay laborers to maintain the shared road, and place the Alder Grove under joint Ranger and Grey-Withy witness.

## Ending States

### The Compact Renewed

All three breaches are repaired, Alda's memory is released, Lucan personally renews the terms, and the party presents at least two confession fragments. The wood grants the heirlooms rather than losing them. Lucan becomes lord with narrower powers and stronger local legitimacy. This is the most restorative continuation.

### The Inheritance Taken

The party seizes all three relics and destroys the Warden. Lucan is acclaimed under Marcher custom, but the Old Wood no longer recognizes Harrowfen hearths as safe. Roads shift, wells sour, Folk trade ends, and the new lord begins his rule with a frontier he can command only by force.

### Sabine's March

The Briar Lances secure one or more heirlooms and Sabine accepts enough of the compact to win Ranger and township support. She becomes a harder, more competent lord than Lucan would initially have been. If she refuses restitution, she inherits Othmar's conflict with better soldiers and fewer excuses.

### The Grey-Withy Custody

The party returns the heirlooms to Eydis rather than either claimant. Harrowfen's lordship fails to consolidate, but the road and water become shared commons. Neighboring frontier lords move to divide the territory, creating a political crisis after a morally serious local victory.

### Alda Rooted

The Warden survives with the Stag Seal and Alda's memory remains trapped. The Old Wood closes around Hartrest Lodge. Neither claimant can be acclaimed. The Warden begins taking travelers who resemble Othmar's retainers, showing that preserved justice has become destructive repetition.

### The New Oath-Bearer

A player character accepts the compact in place of either heir. The wood grants temporary custody of the relics and recognizes that character as Harrowfen's guarantor. This creates real authority in the Marches and personal responsibility for every future breach.

## What the Adventure Changes

- Harrowfen gains a ruler, divides between claimants, or loses coherent lordship.
- The party earns a reputation as witnesses whose choices matter on the Folk frontier.
- The Old Hart Road opens or becomes a hostile thin-space route.
- Coldwater Mill survives, collapses, or is replaced by a less destructive economy.
- The Grey-Withy gain proof that settler institutions can or cannot honor witnessed compacts.
- The Briar Lances become allies, recurring rivals, or Sabine's hardened household company.
- Oathwood Wardens become a known consequence of land-law preserving human injustice too literally.

## Encounter and Creature Register

- Ordinary bears and cubs at the Hart Road: an ecological obstacle with readable behavior.
- The Reed-Daughter at Coldwater: a local Cloven manifestation of moving-water law, best handled through parley or restoration.
- Wooden huntsmen at Hartrest Lodge: residue bodies animated by the last household command.
- [[Oathwood Wardens]]: the final pact guardian and reusable creature family.
- [[The Briar Lances]]: reactive human competition across all three acts.
