# Fraternity of the First Shelter

The Fraternity of the First Shelter was a small frontier brotherhood founded in 1012 AR by Chapel-Folk households and Eclessian marcher soldiers after the Folk Wars. It maintained a road refuge west of modern [[Birchcross]] and protected travelers, abducted persons, fugitives from unlawful violence, and surrendered enemies awaiting judgement.

## Founding

The fraternity arose from a concrete failure. A mixed refugee group reached an abandoned Atherian relay during an early winter storm. Marcher soldiers initially barred the door because several refugees belonged to families recently at war with them. Two soldiers and three Chapel-Folk broke the order, opened the shelter, and kept everyone alive until thaw.

Their descendants and surviving companions restored the relay as [[First Shelter Watch-Barrow]]. The fraternity never became a universal order, state institution, or representative of all Chapel-Folk.

## Membership

Members included:

- Chapel-Folk road wardens and household readers;
- former marcher soldiers serving fixed local terms;
- millers, smiths, ferrymen, and healers maintaining refuge stores;
- boundary witnesses who could distinguish emergency shelter from a permanent land claim.

The title **watch-brother** applied to any sworn member regardless of sex or ancestry. It described shared duty rather than monastic life.

## The First-Shelter Writ

[[The First-Shelter Writ]] placed immediate rescue and shelter before the settlement of property, rank, compensation, or border. It did not cancel later judgement. A rescued thief still faced theft; a surrendered killer still faced murder; a refugee received warmth before anyone argued over title.

The distinction between immediate mercy and later accountability became the fraternity's most durable teaching.

## Decline

The road shifted toward Grenzburg and Lakewatch. Better-used refuge halls opened elsewhere, the old imperial spur failed, and later rulers preferred garrisons they controlled directly. The fraternity's last watch-brothers joined Birchcross households during the late twelfth century.

Its refuge became a memorial and then a sealed barrow. No modern claimant possesses continuous command authority through the fraternity.

## Present Legacy

Birchcross preserves the open-door emblem, six founder names, one shelter token, and the Writ's order of duties. Politicians sometimes quote the fraternity to demand unrestricted passage or permanent occupation. The surviving text supports neither claim: emergency shelter does not erase local boundaries.
