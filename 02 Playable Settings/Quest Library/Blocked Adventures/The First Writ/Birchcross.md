# Birchcross

Birchcross is a Chapel-Folk road hamlet one day's travel west of Grenzburg on the road toward Lakewatch. Its thirty-four households contain about 150 people. The settlement stands where a mill leat, a birch ridge path, and an overgrown Atherian road spur meet.

## Settlement Shape

Birchcross is built around three small clusters rather than one square:

- **The chapel yard:** timber chapel, common bakehouse, meeting tree, burial plot, and reader's house.
- **The road cross:** smithy, stable, two guest rooms, toll shelter, and winter notice board.
- **Mettler Mill:** watermill, grain sheds, drying floor, cart yard, and six nearby households on the leat.

The clusters stand within shouting distance in dry weather. Floodwater and autumn mud can isolate the mill from the chapel after dark.

## People and Work

The hamlet grinds grain from nearby clearings, cuts birch and alder, repairs carts, keeps pack animals, smokes river fish, and maintains a refuge store for travelers caught between Grenzburg and Lakewatch. Most households combine several trades because no single local market supports a narrow profession year-round.

Birchcross is not wealthy. Its mill and road position make it materially important to neighboring hamlets, especially when the southern roads begin to close.

## Chapel-Folk Life

Birchcross receives the Redeemer's sacraments when an itinerant priest reaches it. Daily worship, scripture reading, burial prayers, and relief work normally depend upon a household reader. Folk kinship, fosterage, the Thirteen Moons, shared winter labor, and walked boundaries remain ordinary parts of life.

The settlement rejects sacrifice and worship offered to local courts. It still observes inherited warnings around old roads, graves, and weather because prudence does not require treating every danger as a god.

## Authority

No single office governs every matter.

- A **road-speaker** coordinates emergencies, travelers, repair labor, and communication with Grenzburg.
- Household elders and kin witnesses handle compensation and local disputes.
- The chapel reader manages worship, poor relief, records, and refuge stores.
- Serious crimes pass to a larger marcher court after the community secures the accused.

[[Reader Signe Bent-Oak]] currently performs both chapel reading and emergency road-speaking because the previous road-speaker died two winters ago.

## First Shelter Memory

Birchcross households preserve tools, names, and the open-door emblem of the [[Fraternity of the First Shelter]]. The fraternity's former refuge, [[First Shelter Watch-Barrow]], lies a half-day west along the abandoned imperial spur.

Most residents know the barrow as an old refuge and grave. Few know that its hidden crypt still opens or that an imperial stair continues beneath it.

## Adventure Boundary

The raid that begins [[The First Writ — Full Adventure]] is local canon. Public references do not select whether [[Klara Mettler]] survives, the stolen stores return, the deserters are captured, or the barrow is opened.

## Story Uses

- a winter closure traps more travelers than the refuge store can feed;
- mill water is diverted by a new Lakewatch road embankment;
- an old fraternity descendant claims authority the original Writ never granted;
- a recovered imperial route suggests that a vanished shelter lies beneath a modern farm;
- Grenzburg demands a permanent garrison in exchange for road protection.
