# The First Writ — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** Eleven deserters raid Mettler Mill outside [[Birchcross]], kill a road groom, steal food, and abduct [[Klara Mettler]]. Their leader, [[Sergeant Volker Rass]], demands twenty sacks of meal, three pack mules, and smithing tools at moonrise. He intends to take the goods, kill Klara, and escape north.

**Beginning:** Stabilize the wounded, reconstruct Klara's resistance, and follow the redundant trail she leaves toward [[First Shelter Watch-Barrow]]. [[Reader Signe Bent-Oak]] provides local knowledge and asks the party to place Klara's life before stores or vengeance.

**Middle:** Deal with a returning deserter patrol, choose one of three approaches to the occupied barrow, clear or bypass its sentries and refuge hall, and cross a visibly dangerous Atherian blade passage.

**End:** Confront Rass in the Oath Hall while Klara actively attempts escape. After the rescue, attentive characters may use [[The First-Shelter Writ]] to open the hidden fraternity crypt and discover the sealed [[Birchcross Imperial Stair]].

**Format:** one long session or two substantial sessions; level 1 analog; rescue and compact delve.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** a party of local volunteers or passing travelers; Klara; Signe; the people of Birchcross; Rass; and the ten other Nine-Mile Deserters.
- **What:** find Klara before the ransom exchange becomes an execution and northward escape.
- **Where:** Mettler Mill, the old Lakewatch road, birch woods, a reused Atherian watch-barrow, its hidden crypt, and the first landing of an imperial stair.
- **Why the party acts:** someone has been carried off and no force capable of reaching the barrow before moonrise is nearer than Grenzburg. Signe asks for help; kinship, conscience, friendship, local duty, or simple refusal to tolerate murder supplies the rest.
- **Why the villains act:** their pay was late and their levy conditions were poor. They chose to murder a groom, rob a hamlet, ransom a witness, and kill her afterward rather than seek lawful release, desert without predation, or accept the consequences of leaving.
- **How it escalates:** time moves the band from concealment to ransom collection and flight, while noise and escaped sentries harden the barrow independently of time.

## Canon Boundaries

Birchcross, Klara, Signe, Rass, the eleven deserters, the First Shelter fraternity, the Watch-Barrow, the First-Shelter Writ, the imperial landing, and the adventure's starting raid are local canon.

No outcome is canon. Public references do not state whether Klara survives, Rass is captured, the supplies are recovered, the crypt is opened, or the imperial warden awakens.

This is a standalone adventure. It does not assume the protagonist is a Blackjack, does not invoke the [[Crisis Charter of Secondment]], changes no Grenzburg campaign track, and requires no main-campaign character. A table may use Blackjacks or ducal agents as its party, but the controlling text does not.

## Moral and Ontological Rulings

### The Deserters

Late wages and brutal muster conditions are real failures of frontier government. They do not compel kidnapping or murder. All eleven deserters know Rass intends to kill Klara after the exchange and continue to guard, collect, and fight for that plan. They may be subdued and tried, but none defects, redeems the band, or rescues Klara for the party.

### The Dead in the Crypt

Any moving fraternity remains are [[Corpse Residue]]. Their souls routed centuries ago. The bones repeat a shield formation impressed by burial, old grave-script, and the disturbance of their chamber. Respectful entry does not wake them; grave theft or forced access does.

### The Imperial Warden

The road warden is an [[Atherian Ward and Trial Engines|Atherian Road Warden]], a non-conscious civic engine. It classifies open and closed roads according to obsolete route markers. Its apparent judgement is procedure rather than personhood.

### The Stair

The [[Birchcross Imperial Stair]] is buried infrastructure. It is not a fifteenth permanent Shell anchor, Way-Gate, soul-route, patron threshold, or living road. The sealed lower shutter exists to reveal depth without adding an unplanned second adventure.

## Historical Background

### The Atherian Relay

Late imperial engineers built a signal and refuge relay above a north-running service stair. Its entrance hall received travelers, its load passage restricted armored groups, and its lower route connected minor road stations no longer visible on modern maps.

### The Fraternity of the First Shelter

In 1012 AR, four years after the Folk Wars formally ended, Chapel-Folk survivors and Eclessian marcher soldiers restored the upper relay as a refuge hall. They formed the [[Fraternity of the First Shelter]] and bound themselves to protect carried-off persons, fugitives from unlawful violence, winter travelers, and surrendered enemies awaiting judgement.

Their founding rule became [[The First-Shelter Writ]]: life and shelter precede claims over property, rank, or border. The fraternity died out as roads moved and its last households joined Birchcross. Its public hall was sealed; its crypt and the older stair passed into story.

### Rass Finds the Barrow

Rass saw the barrow during levy patrols and knew its upper rooms were dry. After deserting at the ninth mile marker, his band killed their quartermaster, stole two horses, and turned west. They raided Mettler Mill for durable food and tools. Klara recognized three of them from muster day and struck one with a mill hook, so Rass took her as leverage and as a witness he intended to remove.

## Birchcross Starting State

- **Population:** about 150 people in thirty-four households.
- **Immediate defenders:** seven able adults remain after the muster, but two are wounded and the others must guard children, stores, and the road.
- **Distance to Grenzburg:** one hard day's travel; help sent for now cannot reach the barrow before moonrise.
- **Mill losses:** four meal sacks and one cart horse already taken; the demanded stores represent much of the hamlet's winter reserve.
- **Known dead:** Hagen Lutz, a road groom who tried to bar the mill gate.
- **Known wounded:** Marten Mettler, Klara's father, and two neighboring workers.

## The Nine-Mile Deserters in Numbers

All eleven are adult human former levy soldiers.

| Position | Number | Function |
|---|---:|---|
| Rass and the Oath Hall guard | 3 | Rass, Dieter Salk, and one bowman hold Klara and the inner exit |
| Refuge hall | 3 | Barricade crew and reserve; two leave for the ransom site when the Clock reaches 2 |
| Threshold sentries | 2 | Watch the road door and signal vent |
| Returning patrol | 3 | Scout the trail, check the ransom stone, and approach from behind if ignored |

No deserter joins the party. A captured deserter may answer under pressure, but does so to save himself rather than Klara.

## Adventure States

### Ransom Clock: 4 to 0

Begin at **4** after the mill is stabilized. Advance for a major delay, a full rest, becoming lost after ignoring all trail evidence, withdrawing from the barrow, or allowing an enemy plan to complete.

- **4 - Afternoon:** Klara is bruised but mobile. The patrol is on the road and all ransom goods remain in Birchcross.
- **3 - Late afternoon:** Klara's hands are tied separately. The patrol checks the exchange stone. Rass studies the Oath Hall inscription.
- **2 - Dusk:** Two hall deserters leave to collect the ransom. Klara is moved against the inner dais. Sending the goods may prevent immediate harm but does not change Rass's plan to kill her.
- **1 - Moonrise:** Rass prepares withdrawal. The ransom collectors return if unopposed; otherwise he knows something failed.
- **0 - Broken bargain:** Rass orders Klara killed and tries to reach the north spur. She receives one active rescue beat before the killing attempt can resolve.

The Clock never kills Klara between scenes.

### Klara's Rescue Beat at 0

Choose the action that follows established play:

- she slips the loosened wrist and drops beneath the first strike;
- she overturns the brazier between herself and Rass;
- she drives a hidden tally shard into Dieter's hand;
- she shouts the safe edge of the blade passage;
- she pulls a hanging signal cord, distracting the guard and marking her position.

The party must still act. Her beat creates a fair opening rather than automatic safety.

### Barrow Alarm: 0 to 3

Begin at **0**. Raise Alarm when a deserter escapes inward, the exterior signal is sounded, the blade trap fires, the party retreats after entry, or the party makes an unmistakably loud approach.

- **0 - Unaware:** sentries are separated; hall defenders are eating or resting.
- **1 - Listening:** bows cover the main threshold and the patrol quickens its return.
- **2 - Barricaded:** the refuge tables form cover and Klara moves to the Oath Hall dais.
- **3 - Hostage posture:** Rass keeps a weapon at Klara, Dieter watches the inner route, and one defender prepares the north-spur withdrawal.

Alarm changes preparation, not enemy numbers.

### Ransom Goods

Record one state rather than another track:

- **Protected:** the goods remain at Birchcross.
- **Surrendered:** the collectors take them toward the barrow.
- **Recovered:** the party intercepts or retakes them.
- **Lost:** survivors carry them onto the north spur.

Sending the goods may hold the Clock at 1 for one scene because Rass waits to count them. It never purchases Klara's release.

## Act I - The Mill Raid

### Opening Image

Mettler Mill still turns because nobody has reached the sluice. Flour hangs in the yard like pale smoke. Hagen Lutz lies beside the gate. Marten Mettler is pinned beneath a broken grain frame, and a strip torn from Klara's apron is knotted around the mill hook she used against a raider.

The party's first decisions are practical:

1. stop the sluice or the damaged wheel crushes more of the structure;
2. free and stabilize Marten;
3. check the road before hooves and evening traffic erase tracks;
4. calm armed relatives who want to ride separately into the woods.

No choice erases the others. Delay advances the Clock only if the party abandons urgent work rather than choosing an order.

### What Happened

The deserters entered as hungry levy stragglers and asked for meal. When Halla recognized stolen tack, Rass drew a weapon. Hagen barred the gate and was killed. Klara struck one deserter, cut a pack strap, and shouted names she recognized. Rass took her because the band needed leverage and could not leave an identifying witness.

### Guaranteed Evidence

The party learns the direction of travel without a roll. Investigation determines detail and advantage.

- **Flour cord:** Klara cut the mill's waxed flour cord into short lengths and dropped one at each fork.
- **Horse:** the stolen cart horse has an uneven left rear shoe, leaving a distinctive deep crescent.
- **Tally sticks:** Klara snapped marked mill tallies at two crossings; their numbers count the raiders she could see.
- **Blood:** the deserter struck by the hook drips intermittently along the first mile.
- **Witnesses:** Halla counted eleven; Marten heard Rass mention the old shelter; Signe knows where that name points.

### The Demand

A demand is tied inside the stolen horse's empty stall. It names twenty meal sacks, three mules, smithing tools, the old mile stone at moonrise, and death if anyone follows. The wording says Klara will be released. Dieter's inner order later proves that promise false.

### Signe's Request

Signe arrives with a chapel medical chest and the last public token of the First Shelter fraternity: a plain iron disk showing an open door above crossed roads.

She explains only what the party needs:

- the Watch-Barrow is a half-day west by the direct woodland trail;
- it was a refuge before it was a grave;
- Klara's life comes before grain, mules, vengeance, or whatever lies below;
- the token identifies the party as rescuers if later kin challenge their entry.

The token has no magical power and grants no universal authority.

## Act II - The Flour Trail

### Route Choices

- **Direct road:** fastest and easiest to follow; most visible to the patrol.
- **Mill leat:** follows cold water and alder cover; slower, but reaches the spring culvert approach.
- **Old signal ridge:** broken ground above the road; loses one trail clue but reveals the Watch-Barrow's vent and returning patrol.

### Trail Discoveries

Use at least two regardless of route:

- a flour cord tied around the correct birch rather than dropped;
- blood and a bloody field dressing from the wounded deserter;
- the stolen pack strap cut nearly through, forcing the band to redistribute weight;
- a tally half marked with eleven strokes, then three separated strokes for the patrol;
- an old open-door fraternity mark recut by Signe's ancestors;
- fresh boot nails mixed with one Green March-pattern levy shoe.

Failed tracking costs time or position. It never removes the trail. A lost party meets the ransom messenger or follows the distinctive stolen horse.

### The Returning Patrol

Three deserters move between the ransom stone and barrow. They are alert at Clock 2 or Alarm 1.

The party may:

- ambush them from ridge or trees;
- hide and follow them to the threshold;
- create a false signal that the ransom is early;
- capture one and learn the barrow deployment;
- bypass them, accepting that they may return during the refuge-hall fight.

If ignored, the patrol reaches the barrow after the party enters and attacks from behind during the next substantial confrontation. If delayed or misdirected, it arrives after Klara's rescue and becomes a retreat complication.

### The Ransom Collectors

At Clock 2, two refuge-hall deserters leave for the old mile stone. The party can intercept them, use their approach as a disguise, follow them back, or let them take the goods. Their absence weakens the hall but advances Rass's withdrawal when they fail to return.

## Act III - The Occupied Shelter

## Three Approaches

### The Road Threshold

The original public entrance is a stone door beneath an open-door relief. It is the fastest route. Two sentries occupy a shallow revetment and can signal the refuge hall by pulling a bronze wire.

Clues include boot scuffs behind the right parapet, food smoke escaping the lintel, and a wire shining where bark has been stripped from its anchor.

### The Signal Vent

The ridge route reaches a roof vent once used for signal smoke. Its grate has one old hinge and one new rope repair. Opening it quietly allows a descent onto the refuge-hall gallery. Failure drops debris, raises Alarm, or separates the first entrant; it does not collapse the route.

### The Spring Culvert

Following the mill leat reaches a waist-deep spring channel. A narrow culvert enters beside the blade passage. It bypasses sentries and most of the refuge hall but soaks equipment, restricts retreat, and risks emerging between Klara and unneutralized defenders.

## Area 1 - The Threshold Hall

The small receiving chamber contains weathered benches, a dry cistern, and the fraternity's open-door mark. The deserters use it for sentry relief and have hung a cup-and-wire alarm across the inner door.

What it teaches:

- an entrance can be observed before crossing;
- alarms can be cut, held, bypassed, or deliberately triggered;
- captured opponents contain information but no guaranteed loyalty.

## Area 2 - The Refuge Hall

The broad hall contains an extinguished central hearth, sleeping ledges, three heavy tables, and two wall carvings.

- One carving shows armored and unarmored travelers entering the same open door during winter.
- The other preserves the First-Shelter Writ in Chapel-Folk and Eclessian hands around an older Atherian route diagram.

At Alarm 0, three deserters rest here. At Alarm 2, they overturn tables, cover the inner passage, and hang Klara's bloodied apron where approaching rescuers can see it.

Useful features:

- old blankets and rope can restrain captured deserters;
- the hearth flue reaches the signal vent;
- a water channel can extinguish fire or make the stone floor slick;
- a refuge register names the fraternity and explains why no bodies lie in the public hall;
- Dieter's discarded order states that Klara dies after payment.

The order provides proof that surrendering goods cannot solve the rescue.

## Area 3 - The Load Passage

The short bent corridor contains an Atherian load-triggered blade arm restored by the fraternity as a last defense. The deserters found the safe curb by testing sacks and removed the later wooden warning board.

### Legible Warnings

Provide at least two before anyone steps on the plate:

- a crescent gouge crosses the wall and ceiling at shoulder height;
- cut flour sacking lies on the central floor stone;
- narrow boot prints continue along a raised edge curb while armored prints stop;
- a bronze counterweight chain is visible through a wall slit;
- dried blood marks the far joint where the wounded deserter misjudged the curb.

### Solutions

- move along the raised inspection curb;
- pin or hold the counterweight chain;
- wedge the blade arm before loading the plate;
- trigger it from cover, then jam the return;
- enter through the spring culvert beyond the plate;
- dismantle the accessible floor pivot.

Triggering the blade injures the exposed person and raises Alarm. It does not seal the route or require a specific skill.

## Act IV - The Oath Hall

### Layout

The final public chamber contains:

- a low stone dais and weathered statue of an open-handed watch-brother;
- Klara bound beside the old signal cord;
- Rass and two guards;
- a north service door opening onto a collapsed surface spur;
- the acoustic plate and riddle that conceal the fraternity crypt;
- hanging lamps, old shield pegs, the signal cord, and loose refuge benches usable during the confrontation.

### Rass's Position

Rass begins with negotiation if he has time and advantage. He demands the party withdraw, bring the ransom, or exchange one rescuer for Klara. None of his promises are sincere once the band can leave safely.

Evidence of his intention includes Dieter's order, the prepared grave behind the north door, Klara's testimony, and two sets of bindings rather than travel restraints.

Rass may talk to gain position, but he does not become morally persuaded out of his plan. He can be deceived, overpowered, separated from Klara, or forced to surrender after losing control.

### Klara's Agency

Klara adapts to the party's approach:

- quiet entry lets her loosen a wrist and move when signaled;
- open negotiation lets her contradict Rass with memorized names and deployment;
- a fight lets her pull the signal cord, overturn a lamp, kick a loose bench, or seize Dieter's dropped knife;
- at Clock 0 she takes the Rescue Beat before the killing attempt resolves.

She will not recklessly attack if doing so places another captive at immediate risk.

### The North Service Door

The door opens onto a rubble-filled stair and a concealed surface cut. It is usable as a narrow escape by one or two people, not the whole band with goods. If Rass escapes through it, flour from Klara's clothes and the wounded deserter's blood preserve a pursuit trail.

### After the Fight

Rescuing Klara completes the adventure's required objective. The party may leave immediately, pursue Rass, recover ransom goods, secure prisoners, or investigate the riddle. No secret discovery is necessary for victory.

## Act V - The Imperial Stair

## The Oath Clue

The refuge-hall carving gives the First-Shelter Writ's order of duties. The statue bears a paraphrased riddle: a word cannot be kept until it is first given.

Speaking the fraternity's shelter promise in the cadence marked by the carving vibrates an old acoustic plate and releases a counterweight behind the dais. This is a mechanical voice key, not contract magic and not a judgement of sincerity. Careful manipulation of the speaking tube or lockwork can open it without recitation.

## The Hidden Crypt

The crypt contains six sealed wall graves, the fraternity's final register, an open-door standard, and the older Atherian threshold descending beneath them.

Respectful investigation is safe. Corpse residue activates only when someone:

- removes grave goods or human remains;
- breaks a grave seal;
- uses a grave slab as leverage against the bronze shutter;
- damages the open-door standard that anchors the burial formation.

When active, six rattle-bones form a shield arc between intruders and the stair. They repeat a defensive habit, do not speak, and do not recognize descendants or good intentions. Replacing stolen material, withdrawing beyond the door, breaking the grave-script, or physically disabling the formation ends the danger.

## The First Landing

Beyond the crypt lies the [[Birchcross Imperial Stair]] landing:

- a route mosaic shows Birchcross's relay, the north spur, two vanished refuge stations, and a deeper road descending south-west;
- three rotatable road stones mark **branch**, **bridge**, and **refuge** as open or closed;
- a brass route tablet rests in a wall clamp;
- a bronze civic mask conceals the dormant road warden;
- a sealed lower shutter has no functioning local power source.

### Warden Warnings

Before activation:

- the civic mask turns a fraction when the tablet is touched;
- route stones rotate toward **open** as weight enters the landing;
- an Atherian instruction mosaic shows a traveler closing three roads before removing a route plate;
- warmth spreads from the clamp toward the mask.

### Safe Choices

- copy the mosaic and leave the tablet in place;
- rotate all three markers to **closed** before removing the tablet;
- cover the warden's receiving lens and disengage its clamp;
- replace the tablet immediately when the mask begins to move;
- reseal the crypt and report the stair to Birchcross.

### If the Warden Activates

The engine raises stone barriers, marks intruders as route obstruction, and animates maintenance arms to return the tablet. It does not pursue beyond the crypt while all route markers are closed. The party can escape, reset it, disable it, or fight it.

The lower bronze shutter remains closed. Forcing it opens only a handspan, releases cold dry air, and fully activates the warden. Nothing beyond the shutter is required or defined by this adventure.

## Fail-Forward Results

- **The party loses the trail:** the ransom messenger or collectors reveal the mile stone and Watch-Barrow; advance the Clock.
- **The patrol escapes:** raise Alarm and return it during the refuge hall or withdrawal.
- **The threshold alarm sounds:** defenders barricade rather than making Klara disappear.
- **The blade fires:** apply injury, noise, and Alarm; leave all routes usable.
- **The ransom is surrendered:** the band gains goods and waits one scene to count them; Klara remains recoverable.
- **Negotiation fails:** Rass begins the execution attempt in view of the party; Klara takes her Rescue Beat.
- **The party retreats:** Rass moves Klara, abandons unnecessary gear, and begins the north-spur withdrawal.
- **Rass escapes:** preserve a visible pursuit trail and let the rescue stand if Klara is already free.
- **The crypt is disturbed:** residue threatens the optional area but never recaptures Klara or erases the rescue.
- **The warden awakens:** the lower stair remains containable by closing route markers or resealing the crypt.

## Ending States

### First Writ Kept

Klara lives, the deserter band is stopped, the ransom goods are protected or recovered, and the Watch-Barrow is left intact. The imperial stair may be documented or resealed. This is the strongest resolution, but not future canon.

### Life Before Stores

Klara is saved while the goods or some deserters escape. Birchcross enters winter poorer, but no one can honestly claim that surrendering a person was the proper price of food.

### Shelter Bloodied

The rescue succeeds, but graves are violated, residue destroyed without reburial, or the road warden remains active. Birchcross survives the raid and inherits a damaged sacred and historical site.

### The North Spur

Rass or several deserters escape. Klara may still be safe. Their trail, stolen goods, and knowledge of the imperial route create a pursuit rather than invalidating the completed rescue.

### First Writ Broken

Klara is killed or carried away after a failed visible intervention. Her evidence and trail remain. The adventure continues as recovery, pursuit, and judgement rather than pretending the loss did not happen.

## Return to Birchcross

Signe distinguishes responsibility:

- captured deserters face named charges for desertion, Hagen's murder, robbery, kidnapping, and intended murder;
- human remains and grave goods return to the crypt;
- recovered stores are counted for winter rather than distributed as adventuring loot;
- information from the imperial stair is recorded without publicly claiming that it has been opened;
- Klara decides when and how she gives testimony after immediate care.

The local moot can hold prisoners overnight, but murder and organized desertion eventually require a larger marcher court. This legal consequence is epilogue, not the adventure's central activity.

## Session Rhythm

### One Long Session

1. Mill aftermath and Signe's request.
2. Trail and patrol.
3. Barrow approaches, hall, and blade passage.
4. Oath Hall rescue.
5. Optional crypt and stair, then return.

### Two Sessions

- **Session 1:** Birchcross, trail, patrol, and approach ending at first barrow entry.
- **Session 2:** occupied halls, rescue, optional stair, consequences.
