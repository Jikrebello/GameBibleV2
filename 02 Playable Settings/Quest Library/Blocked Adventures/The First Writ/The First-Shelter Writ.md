# The First-Shelter Writ

The First-Shelter Writ is the founding rule of the [[Fraternity of the First Shelter]]. It orders a rescuer to protect life and provide immediate shelter before resolving claims over property, rank, compensation, or border.

## Controlling Principle

The Writ's order is:

1. recover the carried-off when recovery remains possible;
2. shelter the pursued from immediate unlawful violence;
3. disarm and hold the surrendered without summary killing;
4. stabilize food, warmth, and wounds;
5. only then determine property, guilt, compensation, and jurisdiction.

The Writ does not forgive crime, transfer land, abolish debt, or create permanent sanctuary. It prevents later questions from becoming excuses to abandon a person in immediate danger.

## Form

The surviving Birchcross text appears in two forms:

- a compact Chapel-Folk and Eclessian inscription in [[First Shelter Watch-Barrow]];
- an iron shelter token showing an open door above crossed roads, kept by [[Reader Signe Bent-Oak]].

The token identifies inherited purpose. It is not a relic, office seal, or universal warrant.

## Oath and Hidden Door

The fraternity spoke a short shelter promise in the rhythm of the Writ's five duties. The Oath Hall's acoustic lock answers to that cadence. The door responds because an engineered plate vibrates and releases a counterweight, not because the words create contract magic or judge the speaker.

The lock can be opened by careful mechanical work without speaking any oath.

## Misuse

The Writ is commonly distorted in three ways:

- conquerors cite emergency passage as permanent road ownership;
- criminals claim shelter means immunity from later judgement;
- officials delay rescue until jurisdiction and payment are settled.

All three contradict the surviving text.

## Game Use

In [[The First Writ — Full Adventure]], the Writ:

- clarifies that [[Klara Mettler|Klara's]] life precedes the ransom stores;
- provides the clue to the hidden crypt;
- supports humane custody without making the deserters innocent;
- gives the adventure its title without turning the rescue into legal procedure.
