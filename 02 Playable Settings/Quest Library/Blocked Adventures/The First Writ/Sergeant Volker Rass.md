# Sergeant Volker Rass

Volker Rass is the leader of [[The Nine-Mile Deserters]] and principal antagonist of [[The First Writ — Full Adventure]]. He turns a legitimate grievance over late wages into chosen murder, robbery, kidnapping, and an intended execution.

## Prior Service

Rass served nine years in temporary marcher formations without earning a permanent garrison post. He became skilled at patrol routes, field discipline, intimidation, and identifying defensible ruins.

During the first autumn muster of 1360 AR, his levy column went unpaid for seven weeks while officers continued drawing supplies. Rass organized complaints, then used the resulting confusion to murder the column quartermaster, steal two horses, and leave at the ninth mile marker with ten willing followers.

They could have deserted without attacking Birchcross. They chose predation because Rass promised that witnesses, stores, and fear would make escape easier.

## The Birchcross Plan

Rass selected Mettler Mill because it held durable meal and stood outside the chapel cluster. The raid became kidnapping when [[Klara Mettler]] recognized members of the band.

His plan is to:

1. hold Klara at [[First Shelter Watch-Barrow]];
2. demand twenty meal sacks, three mules, and smithing tools;
3. collect and count the goods at moonrise;
4. kill Klara and any other identifying captive;
5. use the abandoned north spur to leave Grenzburg's near roads.

He does not intend to honor the release promise.

## Knowledge and Error

Rass saw the Watch-Barrow during patrol duty. He knows its main entrance, signal vent, load passage, and north service cut.

He correctly suspects a hidden route lies beneath the Oath Hall. He wrongly assumes it remains a usable northern escape. The [[Birchcross Imperial Stair]] ends at a sealed bronze shutter within the scope of the adventure.

## Methods

Rass uses:

- patrol discipline and returning sentries;
- simple wire alarms and table barricades;
- archers covering narrow approaches;
- Klara as visible leverage;
- false promises that payment buys release;
- a prepared north-spur escape for himself and one companion.

He avoids fair combat when terrain, a hostage, or a retreat route offers advantage.

## In Confrontation

Rass talks to reposition defenders, verify the ransom, or lure someone across the blade passage. Evidence can expose his lie but does not produce remorse.

He may surrender after disarmament, severe injury, isolation from Klara, and loss of every exit. Surrender is self-preservation. He does not order his followers to save Klara or confess out of conscience.

At Ransom Clock 0, he orders the execution and attempts the north service cut. Klara receives her active rescue beat before the attack resolves.

## Responsibility

Rass is responsible for:

- desertion after the quartermaster's murder;
- conspiracy and leadership of the Birchcross raid;
- Hagen Lutz's murder through command responsibility and participation;
- kidnapping, ransom, theft, and intended witness murder;
- occupation and damage of a burial refuge.

Late wages may establish failures elsewhere. They do not reduce these acts to misunderstanding.
