# The Drakesfall — Full Adventure

## Adventure at a Glance

**Premise:** In a remote highland vale of the Kingdom of Vlastrava, above Kalzaret, the Faewild courts' [[Drakes Overview|drakes]] have come to die since before men, leaving a valley floored with heart-gems slowly returning to the Faewild. An ascetic hermitage, the [[The Drakesfall Vale|Cyrian Skete]], has kept the vale quiet for centuries. Now a [[The Vharo Concession|Vorthara-backed concession]] has come to harvest the gems, killed most of the wardens, and cracked a great old gold drake open fresh — and the vale's untended law is coming apart.

**Beginning:** In the frontier town of Kalzaret, the party hears that something is wrong up the passes — villages reporting nights that will not end, herds walking in circles, and armed men on the old warden road. A dying warden, or the child heir he is trying to get to safety, brings the truth down the mountain.

**Middle:** The party climbs to the vale and crosses the bone-field: heart-gem hazards, stacked seasons, leaking moon-law, and [[The Gemfallen]] — drake-dead animating without a keeper. They find the Skete sacked, the wardens dead but for two, and the concession dug in around a half-harvested gold drake.

**End:** The party can drive off or destroy [[Corvane Sisk]]'s crew, but the harder problem is the field itself and the law that says the harvest is legal. Stopping the desecration means quieting the [[The Unquiet Field|Unquiet Field]] before it takes a village, protecting [[Kyra Vael]] — the last warden, aged twelve — and answering a legal grant that Vorthara and the [[The Concord of Weights|weights-houses]] will defend to the last clause.

**Tier:** W. A journey and a moral knot: the crime is plain, the paper is lawful, the prize is a fortune, and the only people with standing to keep the vale are a dying monk and a child.

**Expected length:** 3-5 substantial sessions: Kalzaret and the road, the bone-field crossing, the sacked Skete, the confrontation, and the reckoning over the field and the grant.

## Who, What, Where, Why, and How

- **Who:** the player crew; [[Kyra Vael]], the twelve-year-old last warden; Elder Sabbas, the dying keeper; [[Corvane Sisk]], the Vorthara harvest-master; his concession crew; Governor Konstantin Vharo, who sold the grant; and the Thalmyric Church court that could void it.
- **What:** stop the harvest, quiet the [[The Unquiet Field]] before its leaking law takes a village, get Kyra to safety or into her vocation, and decide what happens to a legal grant over a holy site.
- **Where:** the frontier town of [[Kalzaret, Bastion of the Returning Road|Kalzaret]], the warden road up the passes, and the [[The Drakesfall Vale|Drakesfall Vale]] with its bone-field and sacked Skete.
- **Why the party acts:** villages down the valley are suffering the vale's leaking law; a dying warden asks it; the Church wants a quiet inquiry into a military governor's grant; or the party is hired by the concession itself and discovers what the job actually is.
- **Why the crisis exists:** the concession killed the wardens who kept the field quiet and harvested a great drake fresh, and untended drake-law does not simply wait — it destabilizes, spills, and animates.
- **How it escalates:** each day unharvested gems bleed more law into the nights, the Gemfallen range further down the passes, Sisk digs deeper toward the richest gems, and Vharo's paper makes every lawful objection slow.

## Moral and Metaphysical Ruling

The vale is holy in the drake sense, not the Church sense, and the difference is the whole political problem. The Drakesfall is a Faewild law-place: a valley where the courts' beasts return their crystallized law to the Faewild slowly, safely, over centuries, if it is tended. It is not a shrine of the Redeemer, which is why the Patriarchate has always eyed the Skete with suspicion — and why nobody in the imperial hierarchy is quite sure the vale is protected by any law they are bound to enforce.

The harvest is a desecration and a hazard, not merely a theft. Pulling heart-gems out of the field in bulk, fast, and killing the keepers who slowed the process, does not just rob a site; it breaks the slow return that kept the accumulated law from spilling. The leaking moon-law taking nearby villages is a direct, foreseeable consequence. Sisk's crew is not only greedy; it is dangerous the way breaking a dam is dangerous.

The grant is legal and that is not a defense. Governor Vharo sold rights he arguably held under Vlastrava's land-for-service law, and a Concord-of-Weights lot of drake gems is a real fortune lawfully claimable. That the paper is valid says something true about Vlastrava's frontier law, not something exculpatory about emptying a holy site and getting its keepers killed.

The wardens are neither pagans to be purged nor saints to be canonized. They are an ascetic order tending something genuinely dangerous, half-heretical by the Patriarchate's lights and entirely necessary. Kyra Vael is a child with a vocation she did not choose and is now the last to hold. The adventure should not resolve her into either an orphan to be rescued from her strange duty or a mascot to be handed the keys; her keeping is real work, and whether it survives is a real question.

The strongest resolution quiets the field, stops the harvest, and finds the vale a standing in imperial law it never had — without pretending the gems are worthless or the child is fine.

## The Central Truth

The Drakesfall has been tended since before the Empire, since before Atheria, by a line of keepers the Church later folded into a tolerated hermitage: the Cyrian Skete, named for the ascetic who first built cells above the vale and taught that the drake-dead must be kept quiet, not cleansed. The wardens' whole vocation is slowness — walking the field, singing the old keeping-offices, letting each heart-gem return its law to the Faewild at the pace the vale can bear. Done right, the Drakesfall is the safest holy place in Vlastrava. Done wrong, it is a dam with a crack.

Two things broke it. First, a great old gold drake — one of Father Sun's herd, generations in the making — came to the vale to die, as the greatest of the courts' beasts do. Its heart-gem alone was worth more than the Skete had ever tended at once. Second, a Vorthara acquisitions house heard of it. Through a factor, it approached Governor Konstantin Vharo, the military governor of the relevant Vlastravan district, and bought a harvesting concession over "unclaimed mineral rights in vacant highland crown-land" — a description that is legally defensible and morally a lie, because the land is neither vacant nor mineral.

Harvest-Master Corvane Sisk brought a crew up the warden road with the great drake's fresh death as the prize and the concession as the shield. The wardens stood in the road. Sisk's crew killed most of them and dug in. Only Elder Sabbas and the child oblate Kyra Vael got clear, and Sabbas is dying of a wound.

Now the field is untended for the first time in living memory, and it is coming apart. The great gold drake's half-harvested heart is bleeding sun-law into the days; a silver drake dead a century back is bleeding moon-law into the nights; the seasons in the vale are stacking wrong; the game walks in circles; and the Gemfallen — drake-dead that the keeping-offices used to settle — are walking out of the bone-field and down the passes toward imperial villages. Sisk sees the instability as a reason to dig faster before the vale becomes unworkable. He has it exactly backwards.

## The Vale, Vorthara, and the Weights

The [[The Drakesfall Vale]] is a Faewild law-place kept by mortal wardens — the drake canon's holiest site, and the most dangerous, because what it holds is crystallized law with nowhere safe to go quickly. It is the adventure's board and its bomb.

The [[The Vharo Concession]] is the antagonist structure: a Vorthara acquisitions house working through Harvest-Master Sisk, under a grant Governor Vharo sold, backed by the promise of a [[The Concord of Weights]]-grade payout. It is not a warband; it is a business with a legal department, and its worst weapon is its paper.

The [[The Concord of Weights]] and Vorthara stand behind it all: heart-gems in bulk are exactly the kind of prize the weights-houses standardize and the Citadel of Vorthara files "under acquisitions." They will not appear in the vale, but their appetite is why the grant exists and why voiding it is hard.

## Adventure States

- **Field Strain (0-4):** how far the Unquiet Field has destabilized. Rises each day the harvest continues and each gem pulled in bulk. At 4, the leaking law takes a village — a night that does not end, a herd or a household walking in circles into the passes.
- **Gemfallen Range (vale / passes / villages):** how far the drake-dead have walked from the bone-field. The further, the more ordinary people are in danger.
- **Kyra's Footing (0-3):** whether the child is a terrified survivor, a guide, or a warden able to sing the keeping-offices again. Sabbas's teaching, the party's trust, and recovered office-books raise it.
- **The Paper (0-3):** how much the party can bring against Vharo's grant — the true description of the land, the military governor's knowledge, the deaths on the road, and Deacon-Advocate Sunn's interest.

## Act I — Kalzaret and the Road

### The Word from the Passes

The party is in [[Kalzaret, Bastion of the Returning Road|Kalzaret]], the Bastion of the Flame, where the reconquest stages and the passes begin. The trouble arrives as frontier trouble does: teamsters who will not take the upper road after dark, a village deputation the garrison is ignoring, and a Phoenix chaplain worried about "nights that don't turn."

The party can learn, before they climb:

- villages up-valley report nights that will not end and herds walking in circles;
- armed men with a Vorthara look have been going up the old warden road with picks and wagons;
- the road leads to the Drakesfall, which everyone in Kalzaret knows to leave alone and no one can quite say why;
- a Church deacon-advocate has been asking questions about a military governor's grant.

### The Warden Off the Mountain

The hook lands in person: Elder Sabbas comes down the road, dying of a wound, with the child Kyra Vael, trying to reach the Church or anyone who will listen. He tells the party what the garrison will not hear — that the wardens are dead, the vale is being harvested, and the field is coming apart. He asks for two things that are already in tension: quiet the field, and keep the child safe.

### Why the Party Climbs

- the villages' suffering is real and worsening, and the garrison is slow;
- Sabbas asks it, and is out of time to ask anyone else;
- Deacon-Advocate Sunn will quietly sponsor a party that can bring back evidence against Vharo's grant;
- or the party was hired in Kalzaret by the concession's own agent and now knows what the job is.

### Act I Output

The party knows the vale is being harvested, the field is failing, and the paper is legal. They climb the warden road with a dying guide, a frightened child, or both.

## Act II — The Bone-Field

The Drakesfall is a journey and a crossing, kept from the original as the field of the dead around the holy heart. It is beautiful, wrong, and instructive.

### The Crossing

The bone-field is centuries of drake-dead settling their law into the vale — normally a slow, safe return, now destabilizing. The crossing teaches the vale's rules by walking them:

- **Stacked seasons:** patches of vale where two seasons hold at once — frost and bloom in one meadow — because the field's timekeeping is slipping. Passable, disorienting, occasionally dangerous.
- **The circling game:** deer and goats walking perfect circles, birds wheeling one way; a sign of law running without a keeper, and a warning of what happens to people who linger too long.
- **Leaking moon-law:** by night, a century-dead silver drake's moon-law bleeds up — unnatural moonlight, stillness the [[Silver Drakes|silver court]] is known for, and a pull toward sleep or wandering that a careful party resists and a careless one does not.
- **The sun-scald:** near the great gold drake's half-harvested body, the days run hot and bright with leaked sun-law, per [[Gold Drakes|the gold court]] — heat-shimmer, glare, hoarding-madness in anyone who pockets a loose gem.

### The Gemfallen

Out of the bone-field walk [[The Gemfallen]]: drake-dead that the wardens' keeping-offices used to settle, now animate without a keeper. They are not the drakes and not evil; they are law walking, drawn toward the imperial villages the way any unfinished thing drifts downhill. They can be put down, but they re-form while the field is unquiet — the real answer is quieting the field, not clearing the bone-yard.

### The Skete

Above the field stand the cells of the Cyrian Skete, sacked: the keeping-office books scattered, the wardens dead but for Sabbas and Kyra, the chapel-cave where the oldest offices are kept broken open. Here the party learns the vocation they are being asked to save — slowness, tending, the offices that let the field return its law safely — and finds the office-books that can raise Kyra's Footing and, later, quiet the field.

### Act II Output

The party has crossed the field, understood the destabilization, met the Gemfallen, and reached the concession's dig with the knowledge that the harvest and the instability are the same problem.

## Act III — The Dig

### Sisk's Camp

At the vale's heart, around the half-harvested gold drake, [[Corvane Sisk]] has dug in: a Vorthara camp of pick-crews, warded wagons, hired blades, and a copy of the concession grant nailed to a post like a charm. Sisk is not a berserker; he is a professional emptying a site he believes is about to become unworkable, and he means to be gone with the richest gems before it does.

The party can:

- **assault the camp** — winnable, but the crew fights with Vorthara gear and the drake's leaked sun-law as terrain, and a pitched fight raises Field Strain as gems are cracked in the chaos;
- **talk Sisk out** — he can be moved by proof that his own digging is what is failing the vale, by the threat of Church liability, or by a buy-out that beats his cut without the risk;
- **turn his crew** — the pick-crews are hired labor who did not sign up to break a holy place and be blamed for a village of sleepwalkers; evidence of the leaking law can split them from Sisk;
- **race the field** — ignore the camp and quiet the field first, which strips the harvest of its urgency and its safety and forces Sisk to deal.

### The Great Drake's Heart

The gold drake's half-harvested heart-gem is the richest prize and the worst hazard: pulled the rest of the way in bulk, it spikes Field Strain badly; left and re-seated, it is the keystone of quieting the vale. What the party does with it is the physical center of the finale, and Sisk wants it more than anything.

### Act III Output

The concession is stopped, bought, split, or outrun. The party now faces the two problems force cannot solve: the failing field, and the lawful grant.

## Act IV — Quieting the Field and Answering the Paper

### The Keeping

Quieting the [[The Unquiet Field]] is the true climax. It needs the office-books, a keeper to sing the offices, and the great drake's heart re-seated to slow the return. The party's options turn on Kyra:

- if **Kyra's Footing** is high, she can lead the keeping herself — a child finding her vocation is the strongest image the adventure offers;
- if not, the party can stand in for the missing wardens under Sabbas's failing guidance, buying the field time rather than truly settling it;
- either way, re-seating the gold drake's heart instead of harvesting it is what turns Field Strain back down and calls the Gemfallen home.

Done, the nights turn again, the game scatters, and the villages are released.

### The Paper

The grant remains. Force did not void it, and a quieted vale is still, on paper, Vharo's to sell. Answering it is the adventure's Tier-W tail:

- **the true description** — proof that the "vacant mineral crown-land" is the Drakesfall, tended and inhabited, voids the grant's premise;
- **the military governor's knowledge** — proof that Vharo knew what he was selling turns a bad grant into an abuse of office, which is what Deacon-Advocate Sunn wants;
- **the deaths on the road** — the killed wardens make the concession liable regardless of the grant;
- **a standing for the vale** — the strongest outcome gets the Drakesfall recognized in imperial or Church law as a kept site, so the next concession cannot simply buy it again.

## Ways to Resolve the Conflict

### Quiet and Keep

Drive off or buy out the concession, re-seat the great drake's heart, and quiet the field — with Kyra leading the keeping if she can. The vale survives, the villages are freed, and the Skete has a future, however thin. This is the most restorative branch.

### Void the Grant

Bring the true description, Vharo's knowledge, and the road-deaths to Deacon-Advocate Sunn. The Church court voids the concession and disciplines the military governor — and, in the same motion, tries to claim the vale for the Patriarchate. The party trades one owner for another and must decide whether Church custody is safe for a Faewild site.

### The Vale Recognized

Beyond stopping this harvest, the party wins the Drakesfall a standing in law — a kept-site recognition that survives Vharo, Sisk, and the next buyer. The hardest ending to reach and the only one that protects the vale past this season.

### The Field Let Go

If the wardens cannot be replaced and the field cannot be held, the party can help the vale return its law all at once, safely, and let the Drakesfall end — a grief, not a victory, but better than a dam breaking on a village. Sabbas can barely say it; sometimes it is the true answer.

### The Harvest Completed

The party fails or is bought, and the concession finishes. The gems go to the weights-houses, the field breaks, and a village or two pays in nights that did not turn. Vorthara files it under acquisitions.

## Restoration and Aftermath

The vale is not "cleared"; it is kept or let go. Quieting the field is tending, not conquest — offices sung, law slowed, the great heart re-seated. The Gemfallen return to the bone-field when the field is quiet; they are not defeated so much as called home.

Kyra is not fixed by rescue. Her best outcome is a vocation she can hold with help — new oblates found, the Skete re-peopled, Sabbas's offices written down before he dies. Her worst is being the last warden of a vale that ended on her watch, at twelve.

## Ending States Summary

- **Best:** field quieted, concession stopped, grant voided or the vale recognized in law, Kyra keeping with help.
- **Good:** the villages freed and the harvest stopped, even if the grant survives and the vale's future is thin.
- **Costly:** the Church voids the grant and takes the vale — one owner for another, and a Faewild site in Patriarchate hands.
- **Grief:** the field let go on purpose, the Drakesfall ended safely rather than broken.
- **Worst:** the harvest completes, the field breaks, and the villages pay.

## What the Adventure Changes

- The Kingdom of Vlastrava gains a precedent on whether a military governor's emergency land-grants can sell a holy or inhabited site — a real limit on frontier military rule, or proof there is none.
- The [[The Vharo Concession]] is stopped, bought, exposed, or successful; Governor Vharo is disciplined, protected, or promoted.
- The Thalmyric Church decides whether a Faewild vale is heresy to purge, property to claim, or a kept site to protect — a live question for the Patriarchate.
- The [[The Drakesfall Vale|Cyrian Skete]] is re-peopled, taken into Church custody, or ended with its last warden.
- The [[The Gemfallen]] and the [[The Unquiet Field|law-field]] enter the known hazards of the drake canon: what a drake-death-place does when its keepers are killed.
- [[Kyra Vael]] becomes a keeping warden, a Church ward, or the child who outlived the vale.
