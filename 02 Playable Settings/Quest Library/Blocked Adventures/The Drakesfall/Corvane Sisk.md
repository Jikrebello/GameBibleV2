# Corvane Sisk

Corvane Sisk is the Vorthara harvest-master of the [[The Vharo Concession]] and the on-site antagonist of [[The Drakesfall]]. He is not a warlord or a fanatic. He is a professional emptying a valuable site under a legal grant, who killed the people in his way and is now digging faster because he can feel the ground going wrong beneath him — without letting himself understand that his digging is why.

## The Business

Sisk works for a Vorthara acquisitions house — the kind that files drake heart-gems "under acquisitions" and sells them on to the [[The Concord of Weights|weights-houses]] in standardized lots. When the house heard a great old gold drake had come to the Drakesfall to die, it bought a harvesting concession from Strategos Konstantin Vharo and sent Sisk to work it. He came up the warden road with pick-crews, warded wagons, hired blades, and a copy of the grant he treats as armor.

He has done this before, at soured mines and war-scars and forgotten undercrofts. He has never worked a site that was still being *kept*, and he mistook the wardens for squatters with a superstition rather than keepers holding a dam shut.

## What He Did

The wardens stood in the road and would not move. Sisk's crew killed most of them and dug in. He tells himself they chose it — that they could have taken a payment and stepped aside, that the site was crown-land and lawfully his to work, that a handful of monks are not worth a fortune in gems. Each of these is a sentence he has practiced.

## The Thing He Won't See

The vale is destabilizing, and Sisk reads the instability exactly backwards. The stacked seasons, the circling game, the leaking law — he sees these as signs the site is about to become unworkable, and concludes he must dig *faster* to get the richest gems out before it fails. In truth his bulk-harvesting is what broke the slow return the wardens maintained, and every gem he pulls makes the field worse. Some part of him suspects this. He does not let it surface, because surfacing it would make him responsible for the villages down the passes.

## Character

Sisk is competent, unsentimental, and reasonable-sounding — a man who has made a career of treating holy places as inventory and has the paperwork to feel fine about it. He is not cruel for its own sake and does not want a fight he can avoid; he wants to be gone with the gold drake's heart before anything he cannot explain catches up with him. That practicality is also his lever: shown that the instability is his doing and his liability, he can be moved.

## Weaknesses and Levers

- **The instability is his fault and his risk.** Proof that his digging is failing the vale — and that a village of sleepwalkers is a liability with his name on it — rattles the professional in him.
- **His crew are hired, not committed.** The pick-crews did not sign up to break a holy site and be blamed for the leaking law; evidence can split them from him.
- **A better deal beats the risk.** Sisk is running a cost-benefit, not a crusade; a buy-out that beats his cut without the danger can end the standoff.
- **The paper is his shield, not his cause.** Take the grant away — void it, or make it a Church-court liability — and Sisk has no reason to stay and every reason to leave.

## In a Fight

Sisk fights like a man protecting an investment: from behind his warded wagons, using hired blades and the gold drake's leaked sun-law as terrain, and ready to cut his losses the moment the gems are more risk than reward. A pitched fight around the dig is dangerous to everyone, because cracked gems in the chaos spike the field's strain.

## Continuation

Sisk can become a professional who cut his losses and left, a defendant in a Church-court liability case, a Vorthara factor who quietly reports the vale as "unworkable" and moves to the next site, or — if the party fails — the man who finished the harvest and filed it under acquisitions.
