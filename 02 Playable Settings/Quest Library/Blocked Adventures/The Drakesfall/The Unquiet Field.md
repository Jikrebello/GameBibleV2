# The Unquiet Field

The Unquiet Field is what the [[The Drakesfall Vale|Drakesfall]] becomes when its keepers are killed and its heart-gems are torn out in bulk: a Faewild law-place destabilizing because the slow, tended return of its accumulated law has been broken. It is the environmental engine of [[The Drakesfall]] and its true climax — the problem that force cannot solve, because you cannot stab a season back into place.

## The Principle

Every drake's heart-gem is crystallized Faewild law that wants to return to the Faewild. In a tended death-place like the Drakesfall, that return is slow and safe: the wardens' keeping-offices set the pace, and the vale releases only as much law as it can bear at once. Kill the keepers and tear the gems out fast, and the law returns anyway — but all at once, unpaced, spilling into the vale and out of it. That spill is the Unquiet Field.

The law does not become evil. It becomes *unpaced* — running without the keeping that made it safe.

## How It Shows

The leaked law takes the color and character of its court:

- **Stacked seasons:** the field's timekeeping slips, and two seasons hold at once in one meadow — frost and bloom together — because succession-law is running without a keeper.
- **The circling game:** deer, goats, and birds move in perfect circles and wheels; territorial and migration law looping with nothing to point it forward. People who linger risk joining the circle.
- **Leaking moon-law:** by night, a century-dead [[Silver Drakes|silver drake]]'s law bleeds up — unnatural moonlight, the silver court's uncanny stillness, and a pull toward sleep and wandering. This is what reaches the villages first.
- **Sun-scald:** near the half-harvested [[Gold Drakes|gold drake]], the days run hot and bright with leaked sun-law — glare, heat-shimmer, and the gold court's hoarding-madness in anyone who pockets a loose gem.

## Field Strain

The field's instability is tracked as **Field Strain (0-4)**:

- **0-1:** tended or nearly so; the vale is quiet, hazards local and mild.
- **2:** the sack's baseline; stacked seasons and circling game inside the vale.
- **3:** leaking law reaches the passes; the [[The Gemfallen]] range beyond the bone-field; the first villages report bad nights.
- **4:** the leak takes a village — a night that does not turn, a household or a herd walking in circles up into the passes. The catastrophe the whole adventure races.

Strain **rises** each day the harvest continues, each gem pulled in bulk, and in any pitched fight around the dig where gems are cracked. It **falls** only through keeping.

## The Keeping — How to Quiet It

Quieting the field is tending, not conquest. It needs three things:

- **the office-books** — the keeping-offices, recovered from the sacked chapel-cave of the Cyrian Skete;
- **a keeper to sing them** — ideally [[Kyra Vael]] at high Footing; failing that, the party standing in under Elder Sabbas's failing guidance, which buys time rather than truly settling the field;
- **the great gem re-seated** — returning the gold drake's half-harvested heart to its slow return, instead of harvesting it, which is what actually turns Strain back down and calls the Gemfallen home.

A stood-in keeping holds the field where it is. A true keeping — a real keeper singing the greater offices with the great heart re-seated — lowers Strain toward quiet and releases the villages.

## Letting the Field Go

If the wardens cannot be replaced and the field cannot be held, there is a last office: to release the vale's law all at once but *safely* — a controlled return that ends the Drakesfall rather than letting it break on a village. It is grief, not victory, and Sabbas can barely bring himself to teach it. Sometimes it is the true answer.

## Story Use

The Unquiet Field is portable to any tended Faewild law-place whose keepers are removed: a drake death-vale, a law-tree grove, a thin-space shrine. Its lesson is the drake canon's quiet warning — the holy sites are kept, not merely guarded, and killing the keepers is how you break the thing you meant to rob.
