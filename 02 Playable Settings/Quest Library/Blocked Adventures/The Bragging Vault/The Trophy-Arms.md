# The Trophy-Arms

The Trophy-Arms are three famous pit-champion weapons — **Forfeit**, **Ovation**, and **Cairn** — stolen and planted by [[Corsin Marlo]] at the end of the [[The Ashplume Vault|Ashplume]] gauntlet. Each is a genuine prize and a genuine liability. Each has a documented history of bouts won, wielders ruined, and owners who reported them "stolen" partly out of relief. They are the objective of [[The Bragging Vault]] and the reason the recovery job is never as clean as it sounds.

These are Crafters' Union and Fleshweaver arena work, not relics of legend. They were made, sold, insured, and re-sold by people who knew exactly what they did. Their danger is a market's, not a curse's.

## Why They Were "Stolen"

Every one of the three has an ugly liability history its registered owner would rather bury than defend. When Marlo's fences lifted them, the owners posted recovery bounties instead of admitting what the arms had done in their keeping. Returning an arm to its "rightful owner" may mean handing a documented weapon back to the person it should testify against.

## Forfeit — The Victory-Eater

A black arena sabre that consumes what it defeats. Forfeit feeds on outcomes: every bout won with it makes its wielder faster and stronger, and every fallen opponent is *taken*, not merely killed.

- **The gift:** escalating prowess with each victory; a fighter who cannot seem to lose.
- **The cost:** Forfeit does not permit its wielder to disarm after a win. The unbroken streak is a trap — the blade holds the hand that keeps winning.
- **The clock:** once it has run long enough, Forfeit begins consuming the wielder as it consumes opponents, hollowing them into a vessel for the next streak.
- **In the vault:** the champion **Gorreth** holds it at the summit of Branch C. He has not lost in months and can no longer set it down. Winning Forfeit means either breaking its hold first or inheriting its clock.
- **Retiring it:** the win-lock breaks with a deliberate loss, with time apart from combat, or with destruction. Passing it on hands the clock to a friend.

## Ovation — The Crowd-Binder

A ceremonial trident-glaive that gathers attention toward its bearer. Ovation made a mediocre fighter a sensation and then a demagogue; crowds lean toward whoever carries it, and the carrier learns to lean on the crowd.

- **The gift:** command of a room; spectators, and sometimes their loyalty, bend toward the bearer.
- **The cost:** the pull is indiscriminate. It draws the attention of things that should be watching something else, and it rewards performance over judgment until the bearer cannot tell applause from counsel.
- **In the vault:** displayed on a plinth inside the sunk glass bubble in Branch B, guarded by a crab too careful to break the glass.
- **Retiring it:** the pull fades when no one is watching — which, in Vorthara, is almost never. Denying Ovation to any single ambitious hand is usually wiser than choosing whose hand.

## Cairn — The Memory-Maul

A war-maul that remembers every champion who died holding it. Cairn is a roll-call of the dead, and its wielder carries them: their skill, their reflexes, and their endings.

- **The gift:** the accumulated craft of every champion on the roll; the maul fights with a lineage's worth of practice.
- **The cost:** the dead come with the skill. The wielder carries their fears and their last moments, and Cairn keeps naming the newly killed onto its own roll.
- **The evidence:** Cairn remembers being used to kill a servant its owner called a "training accident." Read to the arena bond-court, its roll is testimony the buried inquest never got.
- **In the vault:** kept in the conjured dark of Branch A by a [[Vault-Claimed]] champion the maul remembers, which is why the guard fights so well.
- **Retiring it:** the roll can be read as testimony, then the maul sealed. It is the one arm that is arguably more valuable as evidence than as a weapon.

## The Three Costs of Fame

The trio is a single idea in three grips: what victory eats (Forfeit), what crowds demand (Ovation), and what the dead remember (Cairn). Together they are the price of the arena reputation the Vaunt is selling — which is the joke and the warning of [[The Bragging Vault]]. The party is offered fame as a signing fee, and the fame is these three things wearing prize-ribbons.

## Just Recipients

Each arm points to a different right answer, and none of them is simply "whoever hired you":

- **Cairn** belongs with the inquest its roll can reopen, as evidence before it is sealed.
- **Ovation** belongs with no single ambitious hand; it is safest denied, bound, or held in trust.
- **Forfeit** should be broken or bound rather than passed along; its clock does not care whose wrist it starts.

## Story Use

The Trophy-Arms can become impounded contested property, retired hazards, dangerous player equipment under strict terms, testimony that topples a registered owner, or the seed of a Vortharan inquest into what the arms market has been quietly insuring.
