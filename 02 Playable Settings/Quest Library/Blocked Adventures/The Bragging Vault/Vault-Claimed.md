# Vault-Claimed

The Vault-Claimed are people who died running a mortgaged Vortharan venture and were raised, under a [[The Pale Hand]] sub-floor clause, as guards and set-pieces of the very place that killed them. The term is a collections word, not a species. It appears on Pale Hand registers beside a name, a form, and a debt. In [[The Bragging Vault]] they are the guards, dark-vault champions, and menagerie fixtures the party fights — every one of them a contestant from a team that went into [[The Ashplume Vault|Ashplume]] before them and did not come out.

They are undead by the Pale Hand's ordinary craft, raised through Black Mud rite. What makes them their own entry is not the necromancy — Vorthara is full of that — but the *instrument*: they are dead who became collateral by dying below a floor someone else had mortgaged.

## Origin

The Pale Hand lends against ventures and writes a standard clause granting title to any body that dies below the mortgaged level. When a contestant falls in a vault built on such a loan, the lender's factor does not merely collect a debt — she collects a person. Under-factor [[Odalie Vench]] raises the fallen and assigns them a role that repays the note: a guard on a branch, a champion in the dark, a beast on a tier. The labor cost of the dungeon is folded into the mortgage, and the failed applicants staff the audition that will produce the next ones.

## They Are Still People

The person did not consent to this and rarely read the clause that caught them. Memory, skill, and self survive in fragments — enough that a Vault-Claimed champion fights with its living craft, protects the things it protected in life, and can sometimes be reached by its own name. Their present silhouette — guard, trophy, set-piece — does not lower their claim to release. Defeating one a second time stops a threat; it does not free a person.

## Signs

- a brass collar or plinth-tag bearing a personal name and a debt-form;
- fighting skill that belongs to a trained champion, not a raised corpse;
- protectiveness toward a specific arm, gate, or plinth they were set to keep;
- a moment of arrest when addressed by name or shown their own contestant record;
- Black-Mud residue at the joints and the faint cold the Pale Hand's work always carries;
- placement that makes industrial sense — always *below* the mortgaged floor, never above it.

## Behavior and Tactics

- keep their assigned post with a champion's discipline, not an animal's hunger;
- use the vault's hazards as the living contestants were meant to — geysers, dark, glass, glaze;
- fight to incapacitate intruders the show wants captured, and to kill those the mortgage would rather claim;
- falter when their name, their team, or their death is spoken back to them;
- do not pursue beyond the floor they are bound to, because the clause, not malice, holds them there.

## Release and Restoration

Winning the fight does not free them. Release needs the person's name from the raised-contestant register, the sub-floor clause voided or the Pale Hand's consent, and a stabilizing rite the Pale Hand performs cheaply and others perform slowly. A party that destroys a Vault-Claimed guard in the dark has ended a threat and freed no one — the difference is the whole moral weight of the branch.

Voiding the clause at the arena bond-court turns the Vault-Claimed from property into wards owed restoration. It also makes a lasting enemy of the Pale Hand, whose entire lending model rests on the clause.

## Moral and Legal Status

Vorthara treats a sub-floor claim as clean because it is enforceable. Critics — and the party, if they look — see an instrument built to catch desperate ventures at the exact moment a person dies, and to turn that death into labor for the lender. Violence a Vault-Claimed commits under compulsion is morally relevant but heavily mitigated; the culpability sits with the clause, the factor, and the builder who mortgaged the floor.

## Quest Uses

- a raised guard tries to hand the party its own contestant record before it must fight them;
- a family hires the party to find a team that "vanished" in a vault, and the team is the vault's guards;
- a bond-court needs one lucid Vault-Claimed witness to void a sub-floor clause;
- a freed contestant leads the party to the next mortgaged vent in the district;
- a Pale Hand factor sells a debt portfolio without transferring the release-rites, stranding the claimed.
