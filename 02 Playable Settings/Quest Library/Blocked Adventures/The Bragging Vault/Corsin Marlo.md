# Corsin Marlo

Corsin Marlo, called **the Vaunt**, is a Vorthara arena impresario and the builder of the [[The Ashplume Vault|Ashplume Vault]]. He is a promoter, not a fighter — his craft is discovering champions, dressing their bouts as stories, and selling the spectacle to the factions and patrons who bankroll the pits. The gauntlet in [[The Bragging Vault]] is his boldest production: a lethal talent audition disguised as a heist target, broadcast across the city, staged to launch a new stable he does not yet have.

## The Promoter

Marlo never held a champion's contract as a fighter. He rose by taste and nerve — knowing which unknown had a story in them, which bout to frame as a grudge and which as a coronation, which patron would pay for which kind of ending. For a decade his stable was a fixture of the arena calendar.

Then it left him. Some fighters went to rivals, some to the Fleshweavers, one to the grave. A promoter without fighters is a voice without a stage, and Marlo is not a man who can be quiet.

## The Production

Rather than rebuild a stable slowly, Marlo engineered a headline and a roster in one stroke. Through fences and a lapsed champion-broker's license he acquired three famous [[The Trophy-Arms|arms]] — [[The Trophy-Arms|Forfeit, Ovation, and Cairn]] — chosen because their liability histories were so ugly their owners were relieved to report them stolen. He planted them at the end of the Ashplume gauntlet and published a boast in the old style, daring the city to come and take them.

He does not care whether the arms are recovered. He cares who recovers them, and how. Every run is scried and sold. Every contestant is being scouted. The dare is the audition, and the prize for winning is that the Vaunt asks you to sign.

## The Thing He Underpriced

To build cheaply, Marlo took a development loan from the [[The Pale Hand]] secured against the vent — and the standard sub-floor clause grants the lender title to anyone who dies below the mortgaged level. Teams have run the gauntlet and not come out. Their bodies did not vanish; under-factor [[Odalie Vench]] raised them into the vault's own guards. Marlo's audition is now staffed by its failed applicants, and he is behind on the loan.

He tells himself the harvest is Vench's business and the deaths are the risk anyone accepts by entering. He knows better, and the moment Vench files her claim in front of his patrons, everyone will know he knew.

## Character

Marlo is charming, specific, generous on camera, and allergic to a dull moment. He is not cruel; he is worse in a Vortharan way — he treats other people's danger as material and their deaths as a cost of production. His offer to the party is genuinely good. That is what makes it dangerous.

He fights only through the vault: flumes, pits, geysers, the [[Cinder-Bound]] toll. He would far rather the party win a great fight he can broadcast than die in a way that embarrasses the show.

## What He Offers

- a founding partnership in a rising arena stable with patrons already watching;
- the [[The Trophy-Arms|Trophy-Arms]] as a signing fee;
- fame manufactured in real time from the broadcast;
- knowledge of the vault, the arms, and every fighter the Pale Hand has claimed;
- a promoter's protection in the arena economy — for as long as the party is useful.

## Levers

- He is behind on the Pale Hand loan and cannot afford the harvest to become his launch story.
- He cannot switch off the audience mid-finale, and the audience is the one thing he will not sacrifice.
- Proof of the Audition — the scrying rigs, the scout's grading tally, the broker's-license paper — turns his own broadcast into evidence.
- He wants the party's signature badly enough to renegotiate almost anything to get it, including dismantling the harvest.

## Continuation

Marlo can become a rising rival impresario, a scandal-survivor rebuilding smaller, a tested partner in the party's own stable, or — if exposed and abandoned by the Pale Hand — a ruined showman looking for one more production.
