# The Celion Light

**Source:** *The Secrets of Skyhorn Lighthouse* | **Tier:** W coastal | **Region:** Halmenth and western Kedrava | **Band:** level 5 analog

This note records the source adventure and the translation ruling. The controlling World Egg adventure is [[The Celion Light — Full Adventure]].

## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

The eelfolk beneath Skyhorn Lighthouse discover a hidden **Bowl of Commanding Water Elementals**. Because a summoned elemental can turn upon its summoner, they capture sailors and compel those prisoners to perform the summoning. The resulting elemental attacks are blamed on a sea monster, halting shipping and leaving the merchant ship *Jade Lion* missing near the lighthouse.

The eelfolk seize the flooded caves beneath the tower and besiege lighthouse keeper **Lucien Skyhorn** in the lantern room. He keeps them out but cannot free the captives below or reopen the harbor.

### Beginning

The party learns that ships have stopped sailing because of the supposed sea monster and that the *Jade Lion* has vanished near Skyhorn Lighthouse. Most captains refuse to leave port. **Captain Sheila Coppertree** agrees to carry the party to the lighthouse, giving the adventure a short harbor investigation and negotiation before departure.

A water elemental attacks during the crossing. Surviving it proves the danger is real and establishes that something near the lighthouse is directing the attacks.

### Middle

The party reaches Skyhorn Lighthouse and finds it occupied by eelfolk. They fight or bypass the attackers through the lower tower, climb toward the beacon, and reach Lucien, who has barricaded himself in the lantern room.

Lucien explains that the true operation lies in flooded caves beneath the lighthouse. The missing sailors are alive there, but they are being forced to summon the elementals that attack shipping.

### End

The party descends through the lighthouse's concealed lower route into the caves, fights the remaining eelfolk in partially flooded chambers, rescues the captive crews, and secures or destroys the bowl. With the summoning operation ended and Lucien freed, ships can safely return to the harbor.

## What the Source Does Well

- An immediately legible public problem: the beacon and shipping have failed.
- A willing but memorable captain who gets the party to the isolated site.
- A dangerous crossing that proves the harbor rumors are not merely fear.
- A compact vertical assault with a trapped keeper at its top.
- A second movement into flooded caves, where the missing sailors and real cause are found.
- A clean rescue objective whose urgency does not require a complicated conspiracy.

## What the Translation Replaces

- Eelfolk become a culturally specific Qazmari privateer landing party.
- Repeated water-elemental attacks become false buoys, reef divers, concealed skiffs, and the weaponized beacon.
- The commanding bowl becomes [[The Celion Sun-Glass]], fixed Atherian optical engineering rather than a portable supernatural prize.
- Captive summoners become an inspection-cutter crew held as labor and hostages in the flood caves.
- Lucien Skyhorn becomes [[Chief Keeper Milena Shore-Wake]].
- Sheila Coppertree becomes [[Captain Ivana Whale-Mark]].

The conversion does **not** add wreckers, harridans, drowned walkers, patrons, the Undertow, soul-routing, or Left Hand machinery. The source works because one hostile force has taken one practical installation for one comprehensible purpose.

## Exact World Egg Placement

[[Celion Light Station]] stands on **Saint Naris's Tooth**, a basalt reef-islet six miles west of Halmenth and the mouth of the Celion. Its lamp guides traffic through the only reliable channel past the outer reefs. During Lantern Week, an eight-vessel feast convoy carrying roughly 160 sailors, traders, pilgrims, and passengers is approaching from the western sea-road.

The Qazmari [[The Black-Cord Company|Black-Cord Company]] has seized the station. Its captain, [[Captain Nadira bint Safwan]], plans to show the convoy a false channel, ground the ships, and take their cargo and survivors for ransom or slavery. Her ship, the *Veiled Heron*, waits beyond the reef while the landing party operates from the tower and [[Black Fig Cove]].

The raiders gained their opening through [[Petar Reef-Braid]], a Saltborn pilot who sold them beacon schedules and reef information for money and from anger over his brother's uncompensated death in coastal service. Petar believed he was enabling a smuggling run and the seizure of naval stores. When he learned that Nadira meant to wreck a civilian convoy, he tried to withdraw. The company now holds his nephew Luka and forces Petar to align the false light.

[[Chief Keeper Milena Shore-Wake]] remains barricaded in the lantern chamber. [[Captain Ivana Whale-Mark]] is the one local captain willing and able to carry a small crew through the reef in time.

## Native Adventure Spine

- **Premise:** retake Celion Light Station before the Lantern Week convoy enters the false channel.
- **Beginning:** learn why the station is dark, secure Ivana's boat, and survive the raiders' false approach marks.
- **Middle:** breach the station, uncover Petar's responsibility, rescue Milena, and take control of the sun-glass.
- **End:** descend into the flood caves, rescue the inspection-cutter crew, stop Nadira's boats, and save or recover the convoy.
- **Antagonist:** Captain Nadira and a twenty-eight-person Black-Cord landing party acting for profit, prestige, and retaliation.
- **Moral conflict:** Petar's later coercion does not erase his willing betrayal; stopping the massacre does not settle what justice afterward requires.
- **Consequences:** the convoy survives, grounds, or is taken; Nadira is captured, killed, or escapes; Petar confesses, is exposed, is concealed, dies, or flees; Halmenth answers the raid with measured defence or indiscriminate retaliation.

## Canon Ruling

The Qazmari are not collectively responsible for the raid. Nadira and the Black-Cord Company are responsible because they knowingly choose piracy, mass drowning, captive labor, and slavery. Their ordinary motives do not make their acts morally ambiguous.

The Celion sun-glass is dangerous machinery. It is neither a soul nor an idol and has no connection to patrons, the Undertow, soul-routing, or the Left Hand. Saint Naris's importance belongs to the keepers, families, and sailors who depend upon the station, not to a hidden supernatural mechanism in its lens.

This adventure and [[Murder at Lantern Fair — Full Adventure]] use the same city and annual festival independently. Neither assumes the other's cast, clock, or outcome.
