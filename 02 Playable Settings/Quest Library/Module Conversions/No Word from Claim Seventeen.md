# No Word from Claim Seventeen

**Source:** *No Response from Deepmar* | **Tier:** W | **Region:** Shattered Isles and Emerald Expanse route | **Band:** level 4 analog

## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

Deepmar is a remote Chelish island containing valuable crystals used as magical foci. Slave and paid labor proved too costly or unwilling, so Cheliax converted the operation into a penal colony where prisoners could reduce their sentences by exceeding mining quotas.

Miners at Dig Site Caina breached an existing tunnel connected to a derro settlement. The derros observed the colony, learned its routines, and attacked at night. They killed some colonists, captured most, magically controlled Captain Elgin Ashferth, and carefully cleaned the colony so it appeared everyone had simply stopped working and walked away.

The derro leader Gravvik wants to understand why surface people can endure sunlight. He exposes captives to cytillesh fungus, alchemy, surgery, and transformation in hopes of creating surface-capable servants or discovering a cure for derro light sensitivity.

### Beginning: The Silent Penal Colony

1. The party learns that Deepmar has been silent for nearly a month and that a preliminary scouting party also disappeared. It may accept a large reward, help Captain Ashferth's worried daughter, or investigate in exchange for a pardon.
2. A ship leaves the party at Deepmar and promises to return in one week.
3. The gates are locked, the palisade faces inward, and the settlement appears abandoned. Tools, meals, cards, and books remain as though their owners vanished between actions.
4. A deranged girallon has entered the empty mess hall and threatens the only surface survivor, a cook who escaped the underground complex with most of her memory gone.
5. Investigation reveals a few missed blood traces, interrupted routines, the captain's mine notes, worker records, and maps of dig sites named after layers of Hell.

### Middle: Island Survey and Dig Site Caina

1. The party explores Deepmar's wilderness and inactive mines, following maps and unusual entries in the captain's notes.
2. Island encounters include mutilated animal herds, dangerous wildlife, trapped mine shafts, and a malfunctioning clay golem that now attacks almost anything near its assigned claim.
3. The escaped prisoner Blue-Eyes Tammerak uses traps against the party because he assumes any newcomers intend to return him to the colony. He knows mining teams stopped traveling about a month ago and strange things move after dark.
4. Other dig sites provide danger and uncertainty, but only Caina contains worked access into the deeper tunnels.
5. At Caina, abandoned tools, a concealed tunnel, poisoned crystal traps, and derro scouts confirm that the colonists were taken underground.

### End: Derro Lair and Gravvik's Laboratory

1. The party descends through derro patrol routes containing repeating crossbows, traps, a rock troll, constructs, captive workers, and fungus-based experiments.
2. Some miners have died and been animated by sentient cytillesh growth. Others remain alive in prison pits but are close to permanent madness.
3. The derros breed cytillipedes, giant centipedes carrying luminous memory-damaging fungus, as mounts and guards.
4. The blacksmith Jerno has been turned into an alchemical flesh golem. A gug jailer tortures prisoners in the holding pits. Numerous derros occupy the central feast cavern.
5. Gravvik's laboratory contains failed transformations, volatile concoctions, an animated surgical table, the dying prisoner Klangin, and the mind-controlled Captain Ashferth.
6. The party confronts Gravvik and may subdue rather than kill the captain and Klangin. Rescued prisoners remain traumatized and their memories are damaged.
7. If the derros are defeated, the adventure assumes the Deepmar crystal operation can eventually resume. Survivors recover slowly, the party receives its reward or pardon, and Gravvik may return if he escaped.

## Conversion Diagnosis

### Preserve

- Arrival by ship with a fixed return window and no immediate retreat.
- A fenced work camp whose ordinary routines stopped at the same moment.
- The uncanny tidiness of an abduction scene cleaned by the abductors.
- One survivor with partial memory rather than a complete exposition.
- A surface investigation that narrows several claims to one breached gallery.
- A suspicious runaway who understands the island but distrusts relief crews.
- Worked stone beneath a modern mine as the decisive warning.
- Captives in several conditions, making rescue more complex than opening one cell.
- A threat experimenting with memory, bodies, and adaptation.
- A controlled foreman who can be rescued or killed depending on the party's method.

### Replace

- The original island encounters are varied but often disconnected from the disappearance. Claim Seventeen needs one ecology generated by mining, escaped camp animals, ancient maintenance systems, and the spreading mnemonic hazard.
- The derros' sunlight obsession belongs to their species rather than this setting. The translated antagonist should express Nythalum's body-transcendence and machine-memory legacy.
- The meticulous cleanup is the strongest image in the module and should be a direct clue to the buried system's purpose, not merely monster secrecy.
- Essential findings should not depend on selecting the correct mine after several unrelated dangers. Every investigation route must converge on Gallery Seventeen.
- Passage-debtors are not prisoners in name, but neither are they disposable wage workers. Their contracts, bodies, and freedom must remain part of the resolution.
- The final laboratory should explain what happened in a causal sequence and provide several ways to disable it.
- Reopening the mine after defeating the occupants would repeat the original wrongdoing. Closure, quarantine, University custody, and exploitative reopening should be explicit outcomes.

## Exact World Egg Placement

**The conversion:** [[Claim Seventeen]] is a Valcori crystal camp on Morrow Atoll, crewed by wage miners and passage-debtors. It misses three supply calls. The relief ship finds a tidy dock, current Tuesday ledgers, unattended meals, and nobody. Gallery Seventeen ends at worked stone beside the same bird glyph that old Viscaran charts use for sites found unnaturally empty.

**The torque:** the miners woke a Nythalum Return Annex overseen by [[The Curator Below]]. Its [[Hushwrights]] classify the camp as an unauthorized surface worksite, copy human memories into [[Mnemonic Lichen]], and refit emptied bodies as maintenance labor. The University wants the annex surveyed, the insurer wants the claim kept outside the bird-mark exclusion, and the workers' families want people and freedom papers rather than crystals.

**Hook:** the gold rush discovers a place that does not consider disappearance a form of damage.

## Translated Adventure Core

- **Premise:** a Castaran relief expedition reaches silent Claim Seventeen and must recover its missing miners before the next supply window closes.
- **Conflict:** rescuing copied minds and refitted bodies conflicts with the University of Castara's desire to study the Nythalum annex and the company's desire to preserve the claim.
- **Antagonist:** [[The Curator Below]] and its Hushwrights, whose orderly classification of humans as worksite material is the central evil.
- **Consequences:** workers and their freedom papers are recovered or lost, Morrow Atoll is evacuated or reopened, and knowledge of the bird-marked annex is contained, exploited, or spread.
