# The Undergrove

**Source:** *The Sunless Citadel* | **Tier:** starter W | **Region:** southern barrow-forest | **Band:** levels 1-3 analog

**The original:** a sunken fortress contains competing inhabitants and a corrupted tree whose miraculous fruit draws nearby families into danger.

**The conversion:** an Enaidfae shrine collapsed beneath a barrow. At its root, a damaged drake heart-gem grew into a law-tree. Its fruit cures one condition by imposing another law: silence, rootedness, seasonal sleep, or obedience to a forgotten boundary.

**The torque:** the Folk want the gem re-seated, the Bank wants harvest rights, and families want cures. The tree is enforcing ecological law without the context that made it livable. Fruit supply, cured villagers, and Faewild pressure are the practical stakes.

**Hook:** the first dungeon teaches that miracles can be accurate and catastrophically misplaced.
## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

A sunken citadel holds rival inhabitants and a corrupted tree whose fruit appears miraculous.

### Beginning

Missing locals and seasonal fruit draw the party to the collapsed entrance.

### Middle

The party navigates competing occupants, rescues survivors, and learns the tree's origin.

### End

They confront the tree's keeper and choose whether to destroy, transplant, or contain it.

## Conversion Ruling

### Preserve

Preserve the sunken site, rival inhabitants, missing explorers, miraculous fruit, and corrupted root.

### Replace

Replace Bank harvest rights as a default faction; use named Folk families, Enaidfae custodians, and desperate patients.

## Exact World Egg Placement

**Region:** Folk frontier. **Cultures:** Folk, Enaidfae. The frontmatter placement is controlling; later blocking must use named local offices and factions rather than an umbrella government.

## Translated Adventure Core

- **Premise:** descend beneath a collapsed Enaidfae shrine to reach a law-tree whose fruit cures one condition by imposing another.
- **Conflict:** families need the cures, the Folk want the damaged heart-gem re-seated, and outside buyers want repeatable harvest rights from a system missing its original context.
- **Antagonist:** the law-tree's damaged keeper and any claimant willing to turn its coercive fruit into an industry.
- **Consequences:** villagers are cured or transformed, the gem is restored or harvested, and Faewild pressure around the shrine recedes, stabilizes, or spreads.
