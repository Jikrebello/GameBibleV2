# Risen from the Glass

**Source:** *Risen from the Sands* | **Tier:** R-W one-shot | **Region:** Great Glass Desert margin and Covenant caravan roads | **Band:** level 1-3 analog

## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

Sekh-pa-Mefer III, the ancient Pharaoh of Sphinxes, claimed friendship with sphinxes and promised that he would return from death as a god. His embalmers removed his brain as part of mummification, taking the wisdom that had restrained his pride. He rose as a furious mummy instead and remained bound to his pyramid until he could kill the first living person to despoil it. His anger also animated attendants buried nearby.

Thousands of years later, a sandstorm exposes the lost pyramid. Archaeologists and tomb robbers race across the desert for its history and treasure.

### Beginning: The Exposed Pyramid

1. The party hears that a recent storm uncovered a remote pyramid and is assumed to join the race to reach it.
2. It can make a seven-day journey on foot or pay for camels and arrive in roughly four and a half days.
3. Despite the stated race, arrival time does not change who reaches the tomb first or what happens inside.
4. The pyramid complex begins with an outer temple, a long processional causeway, and sealed funerary doors.
5. A falling-stone trap and closing portcullis threaten the entrance. A heavy rolling pyramid construct then charges down the narrow causeway and can overwhelm a low-level party before the tomb proper begins.

### Middle: The False Tomb

1. The interior is an eighteen-room, mostly linear tomb. Nearly every room contains an isolated trap or monster, including pits, collapsing ceilings, a swinging axe, undead, animated statues, a swarm, elementals, and a mimic.
2. Disease, curses, and difficult-to-detect hazards accumulate faster than a starter party can reasonably recover from them.
3. A false burial chamber contains the murdered architect, reanimated to protect the deception he was forced to build.
4. A sequence puzzle involving carved sphinx heads opens the route toward the true royal chambers.
5. An apparent harem tomb presents a permanent illusion of luxurious chambers and beautiful royal wives. Beneath it are plain sarcophagi and hostile undead.
6. Funerary carvings, guard chambers, and religious rooms eventually reveal that the obvious tomb is not the pharaoh's real resting place.
7. Most rooms do not react to alarms, retreat, damage, or events elsewhere, so the complex plays as a succession of disconnected tests rather than one functioning tomb.

### End: The Pharaoh of Sphinxes

1. The party reaches the true burial chamber, where Sekh-pa-Mefer waits beside an undead hieracosphinx guardian.
2. The pharaoh speaks before fighting. He orders the intruders to prostrate themselves and acknowledge his divinity.
3. If they comply, he demands the sacrifice of the first person who entered and despoiled his tomb. Refusal leads to combat.
4. Defeating him opens access to valuable grave goods and historical wall records.
5. The published adventure gives little attention to rival expeditions, ownership, rescue, or the consequences of removing the treasure. It largely ends when the dungeon is cleared.

## Conversion Diagnosis

### What Is Worth Keeping

- A violent storm revealing a tomb that should have remained buried.
- Rival expeditions racing toward a short-lived opening.
- A monumental forecourt and processional route before the enclosed tomb.
- A false burial chamber protecting the true destination.
- Funerary art that tells the ruler's preferred version of history.
- One iconographic sequence puzzle in place of another combat.
- A beautiful court illusion concealing the brutal reality of those buried to serve it.
- A final antagonist who speaks, demands submission, and can be weakened through understanding his tomb.
- A compact ancient-ruin adventure suitable for an early party.

### What Needs Rebuilding

- The race must alter the adventure. Arrival order should determine rival access, preparation time, and whom the tomb recognizes as claimant.
- Desert travel needs a real purpose without becoming a week of random encounters. Route choice should trade speed, supplies, evidence, and allies.
- The eighteen disconnected rooms should become a smaller funerary system whose hazards all express Ishkatar's theology of ownership.
- Starter-tier threats need warnings, alternate solutions, and fail-forward consequences rather than abrupt death by inflated numbers.
- Direct pharaonic and Egyptian imagery must become specifically Ishkatar: serpent lords, Avarix, confiscated estates, slave names, and funerary wealth as perpetual possession.
- The harem room should become a house of sacrificed retainers with identities, choices, and a possibility of release.
- The final curse should arise from Sethrak's estate doctrine rather than an arbitrary requirement to murder the first trespasser.
- Covenant law should provide a few potent tools, not turn every scene into an audit. Stone, glass, traps, rivals, dead servants, and exploration remain the adventure's substance.
- The grave goods need an ethical and political future. Gold stolen from slaves cannot simply be awarded to the fastest looter or presumed to belong to the dead tyrant.

## World Egg Conversion Seed

**The conversion:** a glass-storm exposes [[The Glass-Risen Estate]], a minor Ishkatar funerary complex at the desert's edge. Its master was [[Sethrak the Final Receiver]], a human priest of Avarix who converted confiscated homes, names, and lives into the perpetual property of his temple. A Jirahar land-ark, Covenant officials, and the [[Brass Jackals]] converge before the next storm buries it again.

**The torque:** the tomb is an estate machine. It classifies everyone who enters as claimant, witness, creditor, servant, or property and then enforces the role. [[Breach-Assessor Samir Veyad]] carries a narrow closure writ that can break those classifications if the party recovers the dead cult's seal, inventory, and proof of extinction. The writ is one weapon among ropes, observation, negotiation, and steel.

**Hook:** a lost tomb rises from the Glass, and the people buried inside it want their names back before anyone takes the gold.
