# The Bailiff's Ram

**Source:** *A Wild Sheep Chase* | **Tier:** comic R | **Region:** road near Vorthara | **Band:** levels 4-5 analog

## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

Finethir Shinebright is an accomplished but vain elven transmuter whose reputation owes much to a Wand of True Polymorph. His human apprentice, Ahmed Noke, spends decades cooking, cleaning, and repeating lessons while Shinebright continues treating him like a child. Noke protests that a human cannot surrender a century to an elven apprenticeship, but Shinebright does not change.

Noke eventually steals the wand and turns Shinebright into a sheep. He takes over the wizard's living-tree home, hires guards, and transforms several of them into stronger animal bodies. His guilt, ambition, and fear that someone will overthrow him as he overthrew Shinebright make him increasingly paranoid.

Shinebright remains a prisoner in his own garden for two years. When Noke leaves a door open, he steals a modified Scroll of Speak with Animals and escapes into town looking for adventurers.

### Beginning: The Sheep and the Strong-Arms

1. During an ordinary afternoon of rest, a frantic sheep charges toward the party carrying an elaborate scroll.
2. Reading the scroll lets everyone understand the sheep. He introduces himself as Finethir Shinebright and asks for help recovering the wand that can restore him.
3. Before he can finish, a half-orc named Guz arrives with three intelligent-looking wolves and a cloaked brown bear. The animals are transformed human guards.
4. Guz demands the return of “Master Noke's sheep.” He may offer bribes or magical favors, but turns to violence if the party refuses for too long.
5. The transformed guards try to capture Shinebright rather than kill him. If they succeed, the party can pursue them toward Noke's home.
6. After the encounter, Shinebright explains the theft, his imprisonment, the wand, and the layout of his former compound. He promises rewards he cannot actually afford.

### Middle: The House in the Woods

1. The trail leads to a home grown from four large oak trees. Its platforms hold a garden, laboratory, living space, bedroom, guard huts, and an outhouse.
2. Three transformed guards in ape bodies lounge on the grounds with greatswords. Another guard, transformed into a brown bear, occupies the outhouse.
3. The party can assault the compound, sneak into the platforms, steal the wand, lure Noke away with promises of fame or patronage, or return Shinebright as part of a double-cross.
4. Noke explains his resentment if allowed to speak. His grievance against Shinebright is real, but he refuses to return the wand or release the transformed guards.
5. The animal-bodied guards retain human intelligence and do not all fight to the death. Some flee when badly hurt.
6. Noke uses enhancement and control magic to make his transformed allies more dangerous while avoiding direct confrontation.

### End: The Bed Dragon and the Broken Wand

1. When the fight turns against him, Noke retreats to his bedroom and uses the wand on the largest available object: his bed.
2. He bursts through the roof riding a wooden dragon with bedsheet wings, a pillow-tipped tail, and a breath of splinters.
3. If defeated or deprived of concentration, the dragon becomes a badly damaged bed again.
4. Desperate to prevent Shinebright's victory, Noke uses the overworked wand on himself. It catastrophically misfires and transforms him into a gibbering mouther that attacks until destroyed.
5. The recovered wand is visibly unstable. Using it on Shinebright may restore him or kill him through another failure.
6. If restored, Shinebright cannot pay the promised fortune because Noke sold most valuables to fund his guards and research. He instead offers discounted transmutation and promises to restore surviving guards.
7. If the party refuses the risk, Shinebright retakes the house in sheep form and searches for a safer cure. If the spell fails, the party may notify his old college, claim the tower, or salvage its remaining laboratory.

## Conversion Diagnosis

### Preserve

- A ridiculous animal messenger interrupting an ordinary meal.
- A speech-scroll that lets an arrogant transformed person explain the problem personally.
- Immediate arrival of the antagonist's collection crew before the full story can be told.
- Animal-bodied guards who retain intelligence, loyalty, fear, and the ability to retreat.
- A compact living-tree compound supporting assault, stealth, trickery, theft, or negotiation.
- A genuine grievance beneath an apprentice's villainy.
- A boastful claimant who is worth helping but not entirely trustworthy.
- Transformation magic used for physical comedy and tactical improvisation.
- Turning a bed into a profoundly unconvincing dragon for the climax.
- A damaged restoration tool that makes the final choice meaningful.

### Replace

- Shinebright and Noke's century-scale apprenticeship should become Vorthara's tuition-bond system, where human lifetimes and post-study service are already priced directly.
- The ram cannot be an innocent victim whose own exploitation of a poorer assistant is ignored. His wrongdoing needs restitution without validating bodily enslavement.
- The bailiff's original grievance should remain real while her use of debtors as transformed agents becomes a clear moral line she chose to cross.
- Permanent transformation must have a defined source, licensing regime, reversal method, and failure state rather than behaving however the joke requires.
- The transformed guards need names, debts, and individual reasons to cooperate. Defeating them should not require treating trapped people as ordinary monsters.
- The adventure needs ways to release or recruit collection agents before the final fight.
- A furniture-dragon must not be confused with a true World Egg drake. It is an unstable object-form without heart-gem, court law, or living lineage.
- The final misfire should create a setting-specific [[Form-Ruins|Form-Ruin]], not import a disconnected monster.
- Contract clauses should create options and reversals, but the module must remain a fast physical comedy rather than a prolonged hearing.
- The aftermath must decide who regains a body, what debt remains, who receives credit for stolen work, and whether the school may keep writing body-collateral bonds.

## Exact World Egg Placement

**The conversion:** a ram named [[Tavian Sorel]] interrupts lunch carrying a notarized speech-scroll. He is a Vortharan washout transformed under a tuition-bond clause after fleeing compulsory service at the [[House of Mutable Form]]. The school's recovery bailiff, [[Nera Voss]], arrives with [[Collateral Beasts]] made from other debtors and demands her ram back.

**The torque:** Tavian once claimed Nera's research and left her to finish their joint work while he cultivated patrons. Nera is genuinely owed credit and compensation. She is also using the unstable [[Reversion Crook]] beyond its license, converting debtors into collection agents and treating their bodies as equipment. Restoration can come through theft, repayment, rescue, renegotiation, or turning the unsafe-collateral clause against its drafter.

**Hook:** the ram is insufferable, the bailiff has a point, and the large dog asking for a receipt used to be an accountant.

## Translated Adventure Core

- **Premise:** protect and restore a transformed Vortharan student when his school's private recovery officer arrives to reclaim him as House property.
- **Conflict:** Tavian owes real credit and compensation to Nera, but the House's bodily collateral practice and Nera's unsafe methods remain wrong.
- **Antagonist:** Nera Voss in her capacity as House bailiff, backed by Collateral Beasts and an unstable Reversion Crook.
- **Consequences:** Tavian is restored, surrendered, or left transformed; Nera receives justice or revenge; and the House of Mutable Form's Fleshweaver practices are reformed, exposed, or emboldened.
