# The Tower That Kept Its Year

**Source:** C2 *The Ghost Tower of Inverness* | **Tier:** 3 one-shot | **Region:** ruined Atherian observatory | **Band:** levels 5-7 analog

**The original:** a company assembles four keys beneath a ruin, enters its intact form in the past, climbs elemental trials, and confronts a soul-stealing jewel.

**The conversion:** an Atherian observatory appears intact only when current stars align with the Year of Standing Stars. Four calibration seals open the vanished structure, where the heavens remain fixed. The summit chart records souls as positions in an unmoving sky.

**The torque:** the tower is a weird made walkable and explains nothing. The chart changes what scholars can prove about the Scattering sky, but every use may add the observer to it. The party must decide whether evidence that observes back belongs in an archive.

**Hook:** the tournament tower becomes one preserved year of impossible Atherian astronomy.
## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

A patron sends a company to recover four keys and enter a tower preserved outside normal time.

### Beginning

The party gathers the keys beneath the ruined present-day site.

### Middle

The restored tower subjects them to elemental trials while revealing its vanished age.

### End

At the summit they confront a soul-stealing jewel and escape before the tower recedes.

## Conversion Ruling

### Preserve

Preserve the four keys, time-shifted tower, escalating trials, and dangerous summit treasure.

### Replace

Replace an unspecified Atherian ruin; place it near Virelos and connect its fixed sky to Thalmyric scholarship and imperial memory.

## Translated Adventure Core

- **Premise:** use four calibration seals to enter an Atherian observatory that appears only beneath the sky of the Year of Standing Stars.
- **Conflict:** its summit chart could transform knowledge of the Scattering, but every observation risks fixing the observer into the same unmoving celestial record.
- **Antagonist:** the star-chart's reciprocal observation and the tower mechanisms preserving a dead year against the living sky.
- **Consequences:** the chart is archived, destroyed, or exploited, observers are recovered or added to it, and scholars gain proof whose use creates further disappearances.
