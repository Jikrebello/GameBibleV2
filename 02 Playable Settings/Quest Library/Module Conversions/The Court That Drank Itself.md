# The Court That Drank Itself

**Source:** S1 *Tomb of Horrors* | **Tier:** 3 | **Region:** deep Great Glass Desert | **Band:** levels 10-14 analog

**The original:** a legendary death-trap tomb tests observation and restraint more than combat while protecting an ancient soul-remnant.

**The conversion:** an Ansharan court-magus built a tomb-palace that treats intruders as presumptuous petitioners. Entrances are consent gates, traps are forfeiture clauses, and false treasure is an admission of motive. The final remnant is a chamberlain enforcing protocol for a sovereign dead before the Exodus.

**The torque:** the tomb is not a fair challenge. It punishes entitlement and the assumption that ancient builders wished to be understood. Surviving expands knowledge of Giant-era legal magic; looting may restore the chamberlain's standing.

**Hook:** the adversarial tomb becomes a dead empire's last contemptuous audience.
## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

A legendary tomb was designed by an ancient master to kill intruders and preserve his soul-remnant.

### Beginning

Explorers locate a true entrance among lethal false approaches.

### Middle

Observation, restraint, and experimentation carry them through traps that punish assumptions.

### End

The final chambers confront them with the architect's surviving remnant and the cost of taking its treasure.

## Conversion Ruling

### Preserve

Preserve the adversarial puzzle-tomb, false solutions, and lethal final remnant.

### Replace

Replace contract language as the answer to every trap; Ansharan court protocol and Giant contempt should shape the site.

## Translated Adventure Core

- **Premise:** enter an Ansharan tomb-palace that treats explorers as petitioners before a sovereign dead since before the Exodus.
- **Conflict:** every apparent choice becomes an admission of motive, and understanding the court's protocol can preserve lives while strengthening its surviving claim.
- **Antagonist:** the dead chamberlain who still enforces the absent sovereign's hierarchy and turns presumption into punishment.
- **Consequences:** the court remains sealed, gains new standing, or yields evidence that reshapes modern understanding of Giant-era rule.
