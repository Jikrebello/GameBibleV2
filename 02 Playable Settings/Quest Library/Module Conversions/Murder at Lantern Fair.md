# Murder at Lantern Fair

**Source:** *Murder's Mark* | **Tier:** R to W | **Region:** Halmenth, Eclessia | **Band:** level 1 analog

## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

The traveling Umbra Carnival arrives outside Ilsurian. Its proprietor, Almara Delisen, has gathered performers and wanderers into a mobile community. The local thieves' guild, the **Gilded Hands**, is secretly controlled by Borvius and Robella Monchello, who publicly operate storage, hauling, and curiosity businesses.

The guild wants room to expand its theft, smuggling, and commercial control without provoking the sheriff. It decides to commit murders and robberies while blaming the carnival, relying on local hostility toward traveling Varisians. Its chosen scapegoat is **Jherizhana**, a supposedly captive sphinx and the carnival's leading attraction. Unknown outside the carnival, Jherizhana is an elaborate illusion performed by the bard Ika.

### Beginning: Carnival and First Murder

1. The Umbra Carnival parades through Ilsurian and opens its games, sideshows, menagerie, and big-top performances.
2. The party explores the carnival and demonstrates its competence during midway games and an incident involving an escaped monitor lizard advertised as a baby dragon.
3. After the opening show, moneylender and pawnbroker Archivin Walder is found mauled on his route home. Claw marks and rumors of a winged beast point toward Jherizhana.
4. At the same time, thieves use Walder's stolen keys to enter his Locked Box business. Drug-dependent skulks kill a guard and attempt to loot the vault.
5. Almara hires the party as independent investigators. Sheriff Feldane permits them to inspect the body, route, and Locked Box while warning that their conduct will reflect on the carnival.
6. Investigation can reveal that the large claw marks were produced by alchemical alteration, the body was staged, the burglary used Walder's keys, and the skulks are being controlled through addiction.

### Middle: Second Murder and Mob Pressure

1. Sister Esrelda Woodmere, a respected priest and mediator of mixed heritage, publicly announces that she will determine the truth and speak directly with the sphinx.
2. The Gilded Hands create a mass pickpocketing incident and raise the alarm themselves. The resulting anger pulls carnival guards away from their posts.
3. Robella and guild rogues enter the sphinx tent in clown disguises, murder Esrelda, and again imitate large claw wounds. Her body is found inside the carnival, sharply increasing pressure against it.
4. Clues include alchemical vials, disturbed tent stakes, false clown costumes, unusual tracks, and witnesses who saw performers where they did not belong.
5. The party may interview Jherizhana and discover that she is an illusion created by Ika. Almara initially hides this secret because exposing the carnival's greatest attraction will not identify the actual killers.
6. Sheriff Feldane quarantines the carnival. A mob catches and nearly kills the young Varisian performer Milandru when he slips into town to say goodbye to a local boy. The party can save him and de-escalate the crowd.

### End: Third Murder, Framed Investigators, and Guild Hideout

1. Recovering addict Filton Legg knows that the Gilded Hands covet the river business of merchant Arhaneem Braeton. After seeking honest work with Braeton, Filton is murdered.
2. His body is staged with Varisian cloth, a threatening message, a sphinx image, a severed hand, and a forged employment note from Braeton. The obvious clues blame the carnival; the apparently incidental note is bait for the investigators.
3. The guild anticipates the party following the note to Braeton Manor. Guild members murder the adults and servants, spare the children as witnesses, and prepare an ambush.
4. Borvius disguises himself as a party member and gives incriminating orders in front of the surviving children, intending to discredit the investigation and redirect the blame onto the heroes.
5. Surviving evidence or Sheriff Feldane's recognition of Borvius points toward Ilsurian Storage and Hauling.
6. The party enters the warehouse and hidden Gilded Hands complex, confronting Robella, guild members, addicted skulk agents, traps, stolen property, and records of the murder plan.
7. Defeating or exposing the Monchellos exonerates the carnival. Ilsurian returns stolen goods, memorializes the victims, and may improve relations with the traveling community.

## Conversion Diagnosis

### What Is Worth Keeping

- Letting players enjoy and learn the fair before the first murder changes its meaning.
- An ordinary criminal conspiracy using prejudice as camouflage.
- A first crime scene with planted physical evidence and a simultaneous commercial robbery.
- An investigator mandate from both the accused community and a skeptical local official.
- A second murder aimed at the person most capable of reconciling the town.
- A concealed fairground truth that is embarrassing but not evidence of murder.
- Escalating public hostility that becomes a danger independent of the killers.
- A later victim whose apparently incidental clue leads toward the real commercial motive.
- An attempt to frame the investigators once they become effective.
- A warehouse finale containing stolen goods, disguises, plans, and hard evidence.

### What Needs Rebuilding

- The original sometimes gates essential deductions behind individual skill checks. Every necessary conclusion needs several independently obtainable clues and a fail-forward route.
- The sphinx illusion is memorable but pulls the investigation toward a fantasy creature rather than the social mechanism of scapegoating. The translated frame should use a distinctive but ordinary performance tool.
- The skulks function as an exploited monstrous underclass and complicate the story without being necessary. Human porters, debtors, and coerced employees can carry the same evidence more cleanly.
- Three completed murders plus an entire household massacre is excessive for a starter mystery. The third target should remain savable, and the investigators' ambush need not require prior slaughter.
- The criminal plot should directly serve the conversion seed: revoking the fair charter, restoring warehouse monopoly, and stealing the portable credit chest.
- The fair company needs ordinary flaws, secrets, and commercial interests so innocence does not mean idealization.
- Public proof must matter. Killing the warehouse owners without preserving evidence should not automatically clear the accused.

## World Egg Conversion Seed

**The conversion:** [[The Turning Lantern Company]] arrives in Halmenth for Lantern Week. Public weighmaster Gerhard Lenz is murdered with Saltborn performer's forked rigging knife while his bonded warehouse is robbed. When Sister Alena Naris is killed inside the fair during a manufactured disturbance, the town prepares to revoke the company's charter and close its camp.

**The torque:** [[The Chainhouse]], Halmenth's dominant warehouse partnership, wants traveling merchants forced back through its stores and loans. Its proprietors plan to use the riot to seize the company's portable Sestaran credit chest. The murder evidence is mundane: fish-oil lampblack, cargo fibers, cart ruts, forged delivery rolls, witness times, and tools whose owners can account for where they were.

**Hook:** a starter investigation where proving that the obvious suspect is innocent is only half the work; the party must also prove who actually benefits before the fair burns.
