# The Bragging Vault

**Source:** S2 *White Plume Mountain* | **Tier:** F/W | **Region:** Vortharan geothermal district | **Band:** levels 5-8 analog

## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

Thirteen centuries ago the wizard Keraptis descended into a volcanic mountain with a company of gnomes and vanished. In the present day the mountain smokes again. Three famous magic weapons — a sentient sword, a sentient war hammer, and a sentient trident — are stolen from their wealthy owners, and each owner receives a taunting poem signed by Keraptis, boasting that the weapons now lie inside White Plume Mountain and daring anyone to come and take them back. The owners hire adventurers to retrieve them. The mountain is not a lair so much as a showpiece: a funhouse gauntlet built to be admired, packed with traps, riddles, and guardians, designed to challenge intruders for a wizard's amusement.

### Beginning: The Boast and the Way In

1. The party takes the recovery contract and follows the poem to White Plume Mountain, a steaming volcanic cone.
2. The entrance is hidden and mildly puzzle-locked; getting in is the first test of whether the party is clever enough to be worth the wizard's attention.
3. Inside, a corridor leads to a central junction that splits into three branches, each ending in one of the three stolen weapons. The layout announces its own game: three prizes, three routes, pick your order.
4. The dungeon is a curated obstacle course rather than a defended stronghold. Every room is a trick, a hazard, a monster, a riddle, or several at once, arranged for spectacle rather than military sense.

### Middle: The Three Branches

1. **The war hammer's branch** runs through a cavern where the floor is a sea of boiling mud crossed only by hanging metal disks, while geysers erupt on a timer. Beyond it lies a room of permanent magical darkness where an undead guardian keeps the hammer.
2. **The trident's branch** ends in a giant, fragile glass bubble sunk in a lake of boiling water. A monstrous crab guards the trident inside the bubble and is careful never to break the glass — the puzzle is whether the intruders are that careful too.
3. **The sword's branch** climbs an inverted stepped structure stocked with an incongruous menagerie of dangerous creatures, culminating in the current wielder of the soul-drinking sword, who does not intend to give it up.
4. Between and around the branches are the builder's signature cruelties: a frictionless corridor studded with pit traps, water tubes that run against gravity, and ambushers who use the environment as sport.
5. The weapons themselves are hazards. The sword hungers to consume life and pushes its wielder toward killing. The hammer carries the weight of every owner before it. The trident wants worshippers and disciples for its patron.

### End: The Prizes and the Price

1. A party that survives the branches can recover one, two, or all three weapons.
2. The more prizes they carry out, the harder the mountain fights to keep its show intact: taking two or three weapons triggers a final confrontation with fire-elemental guardians on the way out.
3. The mystery of the builder is deliberately left open. He may be dead, immortal, watching, or a name worn by something else. The module never resolves whether the intruders truly won or simply completed a performance staged for an audience they never saw.
4. The reward is the weapons and the reputation of having beaten the mountain — and the unspoken suggestion that beating the mountain is exactly what its builder wanted the best of them to do.

## Conversion Diagnosis

### What Is Worth Keeping

- A boastful builder who steals famous weapons and dares recovery teams to come get them.
- Three sentient trophy-weapons, each a liability as much as a prize.
- A three-branch structure with an honest "pick your order" promise.
- The boiling-mud disks-and-geysers crossing to a lightless guardian.
- The fragile glass bubble in boiling water that punishes brute force.
- The stepped menagerie climb to a boss who is the current wielder.
- Signature hazards: the frictionless corridor, the against-gravity flumes, ambushers who use terrain as sport.
- A final toll on the way out that scales with how much you take.
- The suspicion, never dispelled, that the whole gauntlet was watched — that winning was the point.

### What Needs Rebuilding

- The vanished-immortal wizard should not import a thirteen-century mystery. The builder becomes a living, careerist Vorthara magus running a spectacle for a concrete purpose: recruitment.
- "Because a wizard is amused" is not a stake. The vault needs a reason to exist that the setting already prices — a talent audition broadcast to arena patrons, with the intruders unknowingly on display.
- The volcanic mountain becomes a working fumarole in Vorthara's geothermal district, not a mythic peak.
- The soul-drinking sword must not read as a generic evil blade. It becomes a native arena arm with a documented liability history — bouts won, wielders consumed, owners owed.
- The undead guardian in the dark cannot be a wandering monster. Vorthara has a reason for it: the [[Pale Hand]] holds the mortgage on the vent and title to anything that dies below, so the guardian is a harvested former contestant.
- The three weapons' "curses" become setting mechanics with sources, registries, and costs, not GM-convenient compulsions.
- The fire-elemental exit toll must be bound service native to the geothermal district, not summoned genies.
- The open builder-mystery converts into a live decision: completing the gauntlet is a job offer the party can accept, refuse, or expose — and a debt the Pale Hand is quietly filing the whole time.

## World Egg Conversion Seed

**The conversion:** a rogue Vorthara impresario, [[Corsin Marlo]], called *the Vaunt*, steals three celebrated pit-champion weapons — [[The Trophy-Arms|Forfeit, Ovation, and Cairn]] — and posts a public boast daring anyone to retrieve them from his gauntlet under the [[The Ashplume Vault|Ashplume]] fumarole. The three branches are showpieces, scried and broadcast to arena patrons. The gauntlet is an audition: the Vaunt is shopping for a new stable, and everyone who runs it is on display whether they consented or not.

**The torque:** completion is a job interview. Marlo makes an arena-partnership offer to whoever beats his vault — while the [[Pale Hand]]'s under-factor [[Odalie Vench]] reveals the Pale Hand holds the mortgage on the vent and title to anything that dies below. Every contestant who fell before the party is now [[Vault-Claimed]] collateral. Custody of the arms, reputation in the arena, and the debt on the mountain all move independently: you can win the weapons and still owe the floor.

**Hook:** the funhouse dungeon is a spectacularly unethical recruitment exercise, the prizes have killed the people who carried them, and the guards used to be the last team that tried.
