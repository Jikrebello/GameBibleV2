# The Garden of Stolen Names

**Source:** *Broken Chains* | **Tier:** W | **Region:** a Covenant trade city | **Band:** level 6 analog

**The original:** search an underground slave operation for a missing abolitionist and uncover the slavers' deeper monstrous allegiance.

**The conversion:** a lawful redemption-house asks the party to find an agent who vanished beneath the old water channels. The hidden auction does not sell bodies openly; it strips captives of names, kinship claims, and legal personhood, then sells the resulting labor-contracts as abandoned obligations. The trade is illegal even under Covenant law, but several respected jurists have certified its paperwork.

**The torque:** freeing the captives physically is only half the rescue. Their stolen names remain lodged in a binder's archive, and destroying it may erase the only proof that they were ever free. The deepest contracts bear the Creditor's architecture, though no public court will use that name.

**Hook:** an abolitionist dungeon where identity, evidence, and escape must all survive.
## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

A missing abolitionist has infiltrated an underground slave operation backed by monstrous allies.

### Beginning

The party follows the disappearance into hidden channels beneath a trade city.

### Middle

They locate captives, identify collaborators, and discover the deeper power protecting the slavers.

### End

A rescue and confrontation determine whether the network survives under new leadership.

## Conversion Ruling

### Preserve

Preserve the rescue, hidden slave market, abolitionist ally, and deeper patron.

### Replace

Replace making law itself the only obstacle; Qahuri law condemns the fraud while corrupt jurists and the Creditor's architecture protect it.

## Translated Adventure Core

- **Premise:** descend beneath an Ahl-e-Ahd redemption-house to recover a missing agent and captives stripped of legal identity for sale.
- **Conflict:** physical rescue is incomplete while the binder's archive still owns the victims' names, but destroying it may erase the only proof of their freedom.
- **Antagonist:** the name-stripping slaver network, its binder, and the respected jurists who knowingly certified fraudulent abandonment.
- **Consequences:** captives regain identity or remain legally vulnerable, the archive is preserved or destroyed, and the network is exposed, displaced, or allowed to continue behind lawful forms.
