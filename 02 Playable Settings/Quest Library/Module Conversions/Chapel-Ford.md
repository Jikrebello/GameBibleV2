# Chapel-Ford

**Source:** T1 *The Village of Hommlet* | **Tier:** W | **Region:** Chapel-Folk country | **Band:** levels 1-3 analog

## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

Hommlet grew prosperous after nearby forces defeated the Temple of Elemental Evil and drove its servants from the region. Years later, banditry and rumors of renewed evil draw poor, inexperienced adventurers to the village. The settlement appears peaceful, but agents of the old Temple have returned and are recruiting from a ruined moathouse several miles away.

The module is primarily a **home-base sandbox**, not a tightly plotted story. Most of its pages describe village households, trades, defenses, supplies, potential retainers, hidden loyalties, and rumors. The moathouse is the included expedition site and the first link to the larger Temple campaign.

### Beginning: Arrival in Hommlet

1. The player characters arrive with little money and a general expectation that the region offers work, treasure, and danger. The original text does not provide a strong inciting mission.
2. They explore Hommlet, especially the Inn of the Welcome Wench, the church, the new defensive tower, and the traders' establishment.
3. They meet possible allies such as Elmo, Burne, Rufus, and village clergy, as well as mercenaries, adventurers, merchants, and retainers whose loyalty is uncertain.
4. Rumors, suspicious behavior, and local conversation point toward the ruined moathouse. Traders **Rannos Davl** and **Gremag** are secretly connected to the returning evil and monitor adventurers who show interest.

### Middle: The Ruined Moathouse

1. The party travels to the abandoned outpost without a prescribed quest beyond rumor, opportunity, and the implication that bandits use it.
2. The marshy approach and ruined upper works contain giant frogs, vermin, dangerous animals, bandits, and environmental hazards.
3. Clearing or bypassing the upper ruins reveals organized occupation below. The dungeon contains undead, humanoid guards, an ogre, supplies, prisoners or recruits, and evidence that the site is more than an opportunistic bandit camp.
4. The moathouse's inhabitants react to intrusion, reinforce defensible rooms, retreat, or warn their master. The party may withdraw to Hommlet, recruit help, and return.

### End: The New Master

1. The lowest chambers contain **Lareth the Beautiful**, a charismatic priest rebuilding influence on behalf of the larger Temple of Elemental Evil.
2. Lareth commands guards and cult recruits, attempts to kill or capture intruders, and may escape if the party gives him time.
3. Defeating or driving him off ends the immediate operation but reveals that the moathouse is only an outpost. Symbols, correspondence, and village contacts point toward the still-greater Temple network.
4. Surviving characters can use Hommlet as their continuing base. Hidden village agents may flee, retaliate, follow the party, or continue feeding information to the Temple.

## Conversion Diagnosis

### What Is Worth Keeping

- A village detailed enough to become a genuine home rather than a quest dispenser.
- Ordinary trades, prices, defenses, gossip, and domestic relationships.
- Helpful, compromised, and hostile NPCs sharing the same public spaces.
- A nearby ruined outpost that can be scouted, entered, abandoned, and revisited.
- A local charismatic villain whose defeat reveals a larger network.
- Player freedom to recruit help and choose how deeply to investigate.

### What Needs Rebuilding

- The original gives the party no strong reason to investigate the moathouse.
- Many villagers have statistics and hidden alignment but little playable motive.
- Suspicious retainers and traders can feel like arbitrary traps rather than people with interests.
- The moathouse's mixture of animals, bandits, undead, and humanoids lacks a shared ecology or purpose.
- Village investigation rarely changes the expedition beyond supplying rumors and hirelings.
- The module ends with a larger threat but gives little closure for the village itself.

## World Egg Conversion Seed

**The conversion:** Chapel-Ford has prospered since a trading house began funding weddings, funerals, and repairs. Its apparitions comfort converts whose families mourn the old pacts. A Weeping Courts cell manufactures the miracles from a ruined toll-house, refining conversion-grief for sale.

**The torque:** the villagers are genuinely happier and safer. Ending the cell means separating charity from predation without telling wounded converts that every consolation was false. Grain, household conversions, and confidence in the chapel all move with the party's method.

**Hook:** a starter village where exposing a false miracle may destroy the people it helped.
