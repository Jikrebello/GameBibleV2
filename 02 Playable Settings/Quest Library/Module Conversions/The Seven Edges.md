# The Seven Edges

**Source:** D2 *Seven Swords of Sin* • **Tier:** W→3 • **Region:** Vorthara • **Band:** level 7 analog

**The original:** seven swords, each forged around a Thassilonian sin, must be kept from an ambitious thief in a trap-vault.

**The conversion:** the cleanest 1:1 in the catalogue — **seven blades, each an Idol's mask made metal**: the Rival's edge that cuts what its wielder covets, the Beloved's that will not harm what loves you, the Sovereign's that cannot be sheathed by another's hand, and their four siblings. A pre-Split Vorthara master forged them as a *containment study*; his sealed vault has been bought at auction by someone who has read exactly half his notes.

**The torque:** the blades feed. Carrying one out is the adventure's second half, and the seven Left-Hand networks each learn, within the month, that their patron's mask has a handle now.

**Hook:** the Masks canon weaponized — literally.
## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

A thief seeks seven ancient blades embodying dangerous vices inside a lethal research complex.

### Beginning

The party follows the theft to the city and identifies the hidden facility.

### Middle

Each section tests a different vice and reveals what the blades do to those who carry them.

### End

The party confronts the thief and decides how to contain weapons that continue influencing their bearers.

## Conversion Ruling

### Preserve

Preserve the seven themed weapons, trap complex, ambitious thief, and dangerous aftermath.

### Replace

Replace citywide Vortharan custody; one named school created the study and one faction bought the sealed site.

## Translated Adventure Core

- **Premise:** reach a purchased Vortharan vault before an ambitious owner removes seven blades forged as material studies of Idol behavior.
- **Conflict:** each weapon grants a distinct advantage by feeding the corresponding vice, so carrying a blade out may be more dangerous than leaving it with the buyer.
- **Antagonist:** the half-informed owner and the seven predatory patterns embodied in the blades themselves.
- **Consequences:** the Edges are contained, destroyed, divided, or circulated, and every surviving blade gives an Idol network a new material foothold.
