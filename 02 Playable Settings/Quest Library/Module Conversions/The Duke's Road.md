# The Duke's Road

**Source:** GameMastery Module W1, *Conquest of Bloodsworn Vale* | **Tier:** frontier mini-campaign | **Band:** level 5-7 analog | **Status:** fully blocked

**Completed translation:** [[The Duke's Road — Full Adventure]]

## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

Bloodsworn Vale once carried an important road between two kingdoms, but wilderness and hostile inhabitants swallowed the route after the old political order failed. A new king commissions the veteran Sir Tolgrith to reopen it from Fort Thorn. The road is already about half complete when the player characters arrive, and only sixty days remain before the charter's deadline.

The valley's dangers do not all belong to one faction. Construction has introduced camp problems, workers disappear into ordinary wilderness hazards, local inhabitants have claims and needs, and a hidden enemy uses the confusion to resist the road.

### Beginning

The party arrives at the persistent base of Fort Thorn and meets the road expedition. Its immediate crisis is a missing work crew. The workers have been murdered with small weapons and mutilated, pointing toward dark roseblood sprites rather than an ordinary animal attack.

This opening establishes the fort, the deadline, the workforce, the dangerous vale, and the pattern of a clever enemy attacking isolated crews.

### Middle

The adventure opens into a broad sequence of assignments that can be attempted in different orders. The party protects and rescues workers, investigates foul water at the fort, deals with giant spiders and other predators, encounters the hermit Oakbrow, negotiates with lizardfolk, confronts bandits and hostile groups, discovers useful resource sites, and helps the road continue.

Success is cumulative. The party's choices improve the fort, protect the workforce, secure local cooperation, and make construction more likely to finish on time. Later, flame drakes attack Fort Thorn. Investigation of the drakes and their roost points toward a more organized threat.

### End

The hidden sorcerer Vardak declares his claim over the vale and delivers an ultimatum. He possesses the Seed of Fire, a magical weapon capable of destroying Fort Thorn. The party follows the threat into his mountain fortress, confronts his followers and controlled drakes, and must defeat him and destroy or disable the control that lets him threaten the fort.

The final result reflects more than the last battle. The expedition's survival and the road's completion depend upon the party's accumulated successes across the sandbox.

## What the Translation Preserves

- a sixty-day road-building wilderness campaign;
- a persistent fort and changing worker community;
- murdered crews as the first sign of deliberate opposition;
- an open middle of rescues, camp failures, predators, bandits, diplomacy, and resource discoveries;
- progress measured across the whole expedition rather than one decisive fight;
- a drake attack that reveals the hidden sorcerer;
- an ultimatum backed by a delayed fire weapon;
- a mountain-redoubt finale whose outcome determines whether the project survives.

## What the Translation Replaces

- **Fort Thorn** becomes [[Fort Alderwatch]].
- **Bloodsworn Vale** becomes [[Alderway Vale]], north of Wolfsmere on Grenzburg's western relief corridor.
- **Sir Tolgrith** becomes [[Road-Captain Hanne Eber]], a professional subordinate of Pierre Hohenwall.
- the original survey and expedition conflicts become the disagreement between Hanne, [[Master Surveyor Oskar Pell]], and the [[Alder-Crossing Clan]].
- the original lizardfolk diplomacy becomes negotiation with one named Green March kindred; it does not imply universal Folk authority.
- dark roseblood sprites become [[Blood-Briar Guests]], specifically the murderous host called [[The Red Garland]].
- flame drakes become pain-collared [[01 Canon Reference/Creatures/20 Faewild and Menhir/10 Drakes/Ruby Drakes.md|Ruby Drakes]].
- Vardak becomes rogue siege-warlock [[Magister Caspar Rauk]].
- the mountain fortress becomes [[Cinder Crown]].
- the Seed of Fire becomes [[The Cinder Seed]], a delayed Shell-fire script beneath Fort Alderwatch.
- a single assumed road line becomes three materially distinct routes, with the just route requiring more labor and negotiation.

## Exact World Egg Placement

[[Alderway Vale]] lies west-northwest of Grenzburg and north of [[01 Canon Reference/Peoples and Cultures/Folk/30 Society and Daily Life/The Folk of the Frontier and the Wolfsmere Lineage.md|Wolfsmere]]. The unfinished corridor points toward Dornwatch and the Brauvalian supply network. It gives Grenzburg a plausible second supply direction without becoming part of the main campaign's mandatory seasonal sequence.

The [[Alder-Crossing Clan]] holds local crossing, fishery, refuge, and sanctuary rights. It speaks only for its territory. Its claim to [[Still-Hearth Glen]] is valid, but neither the clan nor the land authorizes the Red Garland's murders.

## Translated Adventure Core

- **Premise:** help a half-complete ducal road survive sixty more days of work in a dangerous and politically contested vale.
- **Public conflict:** Grenzburg genuinely needs the corridor, while the cheapest route violates a real sanctuary and creates an occupied road.
- **Hidden antagonist:** Caspar Rauk intends to destroy the project publicly and replace ducal and local authority with a private warlock lordship.
- **Method:** Rauk trades with the Red Garland, plants false clues around independent dangers, collars ruby drakes through pain, and activates a Shell-fire siege script beneath the fort.
- **Player work:** investigate murders, keep people alive, solve or endure frontier problems, gather construction resources, choose a route, negotiate or coerce passage, defend the fort, and assault Cinder Crown.
- **Consequences:** the road becomes a shared relief line, a contested ducal cut, a costly ridge fortification, an abandoned project, or a burned refugee corridor.

## Translation Rulings

The road is not a Bank project and carries no private rider. Ordinary construction, food, weather, disease, pay, fear, and local authority are enough to make the campaign politically meaningful.

Not every danger belongs to Rauk. Camp sanitation causes the foul water. Predators and residue are local hazards. Deserters choose banditry for human reasons. Rauk's conspiracy is narrower and therefore more legible.

The morally strongest road is the **Shared Way**, a river-bend detour that protects Still-Hearth and recognizes Alder-Crossing passage rights. It is slower and more expensive than the Straight Cut. The Duke's need remains legitimate even when his cheapest option is not.

Ruby drake collars weaponize pain, heat, and directional panic. They do not reveal a natural obedience or make a freed drake tame.

The module's starting situation is canon. No player-selected route, road completion, fort survival, or fate for Rauk is canonical.
