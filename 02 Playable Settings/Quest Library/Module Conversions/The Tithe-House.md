# The Tithe-House

**Source:** *Death House* | **Tier:** W horror one-shot | **Region:** Aurelium | **Band:** levels 1-3 analog

**The original:** a welcoming townhouse traps visitors inside a murderous household cult's history, ending in a basement rite demanding sacrifice.

**The conversion:** an Aurelium household devoted itself to a living saint so completely that nobody admitted when she died. Meals, confessions, gifts, punishments, and rites continued from habit. The Devoted architecture still distributes approval and shame although no Beloved remains.

**The torque:** there is no mastermind. Every room recruits a replacement household role, while the empty altar demands sacrifice because that is what it learned. Escape requires ending the pattern without installing a new object of devotion.

**Hook:** domestic horror becomes the Beloved's machinery running after its operator is gone.
## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

A haunted townhouse traps visitors inside the history of a murderous household cult.

### Beginning

Apparent children in danger lure the party through the door before the house seals.

### Middle

Rooms reveal abuse, starvation, secret rites, and the family's victims while the building attacks intruders.

### End

The basement cult chamber demands sacrifice and the party must break the pattern to escape.

## Translated Adventure Core

- **Premise:** enter an Aurelium household still performing devotion to a saint who died long ago and escape before its routines assign the visitors permanent family roles.
- **Conflict:** there is no living mastermind to defeat; ending the danger requires breaking a learned pattern of approval, shame, and sacrifice without installing a new beloved center.
- **Antagonist:** the household's surviving Devoted architecture, enacted through rooms, rituals, and residents who cannot admit the saint is gone.
- **Consequences:** the pattern ends, migrates, or recruits replacements, and surviving household members emerge freed, bereft, or still dependent on a new object of devotion.
