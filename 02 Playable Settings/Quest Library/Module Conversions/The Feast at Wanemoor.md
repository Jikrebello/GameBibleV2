# The Feast at Wanemoor

**Source:** *Feast of Ravenmoor* | **Tier:** W folk horror | **Region:** Wanemoor | **Band:** level 3 analog

## Original Adventure: Spark Notes

### Background

A tax collector disappears after visiting an isolated village. The settlement appears poor but festive, and its people prepare a local harvest celebration while concealing an old cult and the creature that sustains it.

### Beginning

The party follows the missing collector to the village, receives conspicuously warm hospitality, and takes part in festival games. Small inconsistencies suggest that the villagers are rehearsing normality.

### Middle

Investigation reveals the collector's fate, the village's private rites, and the role of its leaders. Festival merriment gives way to pursuit as the cult realizes the visitors are asking the right questions.

### End

The celebration becomes a sacrifice. The party must survive the congregation, confront the thing in the fields, and decide what becomes of villagers who benefited from and participated in the cult.

## Conversion Ruling

### Preserve

- The missing official, hospitable isolation, festival investigation, and abrupt turn into communal horror.
- Villagers with ordinary affections who nevertheless protect a real evil.

### Replace

- The Bank assessor. Wanemoor has ample Eclessian reasons to send a traveler: a diocesan almoner, roads clerk, or kin seeking a missing relative.
- Audit language and an abstract Long Table network. The horror should be local, embodied, and old.

## Exact World Egg Placement

The village of Merewick lies in Wanemoor's reed fens, beyond reliable parish visitation. A diocesan almoner vanished after reporting that Merewick's poor-box was full during a failed harvest. The village's plenty comes from the **Reed Mother**, a predatory field-idol grown around generations of concealed offerings. It grants fertile mud, easy births, and full nets, then demands a stranger or dissenter at the autumn feast.

The people are not grey innocents. Some are terrified, some deceived, and some knowingly murder for prosperity. Ending the idol is right; keeping Merewick alive through the winter is the harder work that follows.

## Native Adventure Spine

- **Premise:** find the missing almoner before Merewick's autumn feast reaches its final procession.
- **Conflict:** expose a beloved local custom without letting the guilty hide among the frightened.
- **Antagonist:** the Reed Mother and the village elders who knowingly feed it.
- **Consequences:** survivors face failed crops, confessional scandal, and judgment for specific crimes rather than collective absolution or punishment.
