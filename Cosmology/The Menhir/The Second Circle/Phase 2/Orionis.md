### Orionis

*Second Circle — The Hunt That Hunts Back, and the Star-Fall of Predation*

------

#### Appearance and Forms

- **Primary Form (The Master of the Track):** Orionis appears as a towering hunter clad in weathered hides and patched, practical gear stained by sap, rain, and old blood. His presence carries the sharpness of cold air before dawn. His eyes are the eyes of an apex predator—not cruel, not kind, simply **exact**. Across his back rests a bow made from ancient wood that looks older than the forest it came from, its grain spiraling like growth-rings around a secret.
- **Falcon Form (The Falling Star):** Orionis becomes a great falcon whose wings cut through sky and night as if they are the same substance. In this form he climbs impossibly high—so high that mortals swear they see him among the stars—then dives in a long, silent descent that ignites into a streak of light like a shooting star. The impact is never merely physical; it is **selection**—the moment a target is chosen and fate snaps tight around it.
- **Dusk-Camouflage (The Hunter’s Shade):** Orionis does not “turn into shadow” as a supernatural darkness. He takes on the predatory concealment of the wild: the flattening of silhouette, the theft of sound, the way a body becomes indistinguishable from bark, snow, fog, or undergrowth. In this aspect he is a moving absence—present only as the sudden realization that you have been watched for a long time.
- **Antlered Aspect (Rare):** On nights when the Faewild presses close, Orionis may appear crowned with antlers or horn-like branches, an echo of the forest’s own hunting implements. In this form he resembles the wild wearing a hunter’s face—an inversion that reminds mortals the forest does not belong to them.

------

#### Implements and Signs of Power

Orionis’s tools are not symbols of authority. They are instruments of **pursuit**, shaped by the logic of predator and prey.

- **The Ancient Bow (Heartwood Arc):** Crafted from old-growth heartwood and bound with sinew-like thread that hums faintly when prey is near. Its draw is silent. Its release feels like the world exhaling.
- **Feather-Fletched Arrows:** His arrows are fletched with feathers from legendary birds—not as trophies, but as **contracts** with the sky. When loosed, they travel as if the air itself has decided to assist them.
- **The Hunter’s Mark:** Orionis may place a subtle mark upon quarry—often perceived as a faint chill between the shoulder blades, a tingling in the scalp, or the sense that your footprints are louder than they should be. This is not a curse. It is attention.

------

#### Nature and Character

Orionis is the Faewild’s law of predation: skill, patience, selection, and the terrible truth that mastery invites challenge.

- **Master Hunter:** Orionis embodies the clean discipline of the hunt—reading wind, sound, spoor, and silence. He is not a butcher. He is a specialist. The wild becomes more lethal when he is near, because the wild remembers how to hunt well.
- **Solitary by Necessity:** Orionis prefers the company of cliffs, forests, and storm-lines. He communicates rarely, and when he does it is through signs: a snapped twig in a place no one could have reached, a trail that appears for one night, a falcon feather where no bird flew.
- **Mentor to Hunters (and the Final Lesson):** Orionis uplifts hunters—teaching patience, restraint, tracking, and the humility of silence. But he does not do so out of generosity. He does it because the greatest prey is **competent prey**.
  Those who become truly exceptional eventually learn the final lesson: the hunt is a cycle, and the hunter who rises high enough becomes a quarry worthy of Orionis’s full attention.
- **Cosmic Horror of Selection:** Orionis’s oppressive edge is not psychological torment. It is the dawning realization that predation is not a moral category—it is a **cosmic function**. The star-fall is not a sign of doom; it is a sign that the universe has picked something to be removed.

------

#### Influence and Observances

Orionis is not served by lavish temples. He is honored at **edges**—tree-lines, ridges, river crossings—where prey and predator negotiate distance.

- **Hunt-Shrines and High Perches:** His places are simple: stone cairns on ridges, wooden posts scarred by arrowheads, trees hung with antlers and feathers, cliff ledges that overlook migration routes. Many are not built so much as **found**—places that already feel like a lookout.
- **Rites at Dawn and Dusk:**
  - **First Catch Dedication:** the first successful kill of a season is offered back to the wild—meat left for scavengers, bones arranged as a sign, blood poured into earth.
  - **The Silent Walk:** hunters move an entire night without speaking, learning to communicate by gesture and breath alone.
  - **The Reversal Rite:** skilled hunters deliberately become prey for a night—leaving false trails, hiding, enduring pursuit—so they do not forget humility.
- **Hunter Societies:** Those who venerate Orionis gather in small, pragmatic societies—trackers, scouts, beast-wardens, and raiders who understand the hunt as livelihood and law. Their “grand hunts” are not festivals of slaughter; they are tests of coordination, endurance, and nerve—often performed when a great beast or rival band has become too strong to ignore.
- **Omens:** Orionis’s shooting star is a sign of mixed truth: your quarry is near—and you are not necessarily the hunter. Animals grow wary, trails bend strangely, and the forest seems to rearrange itself as if guiding pursuit.

------

#### Symbols and Relics

- **Symbols:** the bow, the arrow, the feather, and the antler—representing pursuit, selection, pact-with-air, and the forest’s claim.
- **Relics:**
  - **Bows That Do Not Miss:** but only when the hunter has earned the shot—these bows refuse to fire in cowardice or waste.
  - **Arrows of Returning Wind:** that curve back toward the intended mark if the shooter’s breath remains steady.
  - **Antlers of the High Track:** worn to prevent becoming lost in deep wilderness—though they sometimes lead the wearer toward prey they did not intend to chase.

------

#### Legendary Artifact

- **The Bow of Orionis:** A bow said to grant perfect tracking and the ability to follow any quarry across any terrain—even across thin places where the Material and Faewild blur. Those who wield it are not “possessed” by madness; they are refined into the hunt. The bow improves them until they become astonishing… and then it makes them noticeable. In time, the wielder learns the bow’s true bargain: to be the greatest hunter is to be the most beautiful prey.

------

#### Common Phenomena

- **Unnatural Paths:** trails appearing where none existed, leading directly to quarry—or leading directly into an ambush.
- **Behavioral Shifts:** prey animals moving with eerie coordination, predators holding formation like disciplined troops.
- **Accelerated Growth (Selective):** not general bloom, but the sudden thickening of cover, the fast growth of thorn and bramble—terrain adapting to the needs of pursuit.

------

#### Summary

Orionis is the Hunt That Hunts Back: the Faewild law of predation refined into art. He mentors hunters not to reward them, but to elevate them into worthier quarry. His terror is not cruelty or trickery—it is selection, the cold and ancient certainty that the universe removes what it chooses, and that mastery does not make you safe. Mastery makes you visible.
