_A practical overview of the major actors, what they are, where they belong, and how they behave in the system._

---

## The Lord

**What the Lord is:** The absolute source of existence and authority. The Lord is not a “big god among gods” and not part of the inner ecosystem of powers. The Lord is the terminal rightward reality: the foundation that everything else depends on, even when it opposes Him.  
**How the Lord relates to the world:** The Lord permits genuine free will, which is why competing powers and rival trajectories can exist at all. That permissive allowance is not weakness; it is the metaphysical condition that makes moral and spiritual choice real.  
**Where the Lord belongs:** Beyond the World Egg, at the far-right extremity of Heaven.  
**How the Lord manifests:** Rarely, and never as “just another entity showing up.” The Lord’s influence is typically experienced as coherence, revelation, sanctification (White Flame), and ultimate authority rather than as a localized presence.

---

## The Redeemer

**What the Redeemer is:** The Lord’s decisive act within history—the rightward authority by which truth, judgement, and restoration are made operative in the created cosmos. The Redeemer is not merely a teacher or prophet; he is the figure through whom final disposition becomes both just and volitional.  
**What the Redeemer does now:** The Redeemer’s presence structures judgement and makes the church’s mission meaningful: souls are not merely processed by mechanism; they are encountered by truth.  
**Where the Redeemer belongs:** In Heaven, at the Lord’s right hand; operative across creation through the church and the White Flame.  
**Why this matters in-world:** Every power that depends on misalignment (Idols, drift patrons, leftward cults) treats the Redeemer’s doctrine of future restoration as an existential threat.

---

## The Adversary

**What the Adversary is:** The absolute opposing pole: refusal, negation, corruption, exile. The Adversary is not simply “a demon king.” He is the terminal leftward reality—a metaphysical endpoint that stands in opposition to the Lord’s communion.  
**Where the Adversary belongs:** Beyond Hell at the far-left extreme: the Abyss.  
**How the Adversary exerts influence:** Usually indirectly—through leftward regimes, cults, pacts, and pressure systems that push souls and societies toward refusal. His influence tends to manifest through depth, heaviness, distortion, and corrosion rather than flamboyant spectacle.

---

## The Boatman

**What the Boatman is:** A necessary infrastructure-being of the Material plane: the psychopomp office that ensures souls do not become prey to yolk regimes or drift economics. He is closer to a metaphysical civil servant than a god, but his authority is real and difficult to contest.  
**What the Boatman does:** He receives the dead into the correct intake process, prevents interception where possible, and ferries souls onto their rightful routes after judgement is resolved.  
**Where the Boatman belongs:** He operates at the boundary between Material death and the Vestibule of Weighing, and along the official soul-roads toward the Gates.  
**Why he’s important:** Without him, the dead would routinely snag in thin spaces, be harvested by Idols, bargained away to patrons, or dissolved in drift.

---

## The Vestibule of Weighing

**What the Vestibule is:** A near-material holding demiplane where souls arrive intact enough to be judged without yolk predation or Egg White drift interference. It is not a “third afterlife”—it is intake and disposition.  
**What happens there:** The soul encounters truth and resolves disposition through the Redeemer’s authority and its own consent—communion or refusal—after which the Boatman routes it.  
**What it means for the living:** Most spiritual warfare is about preventing clean arrival: trapping souls before they ever reach the Vestibule.

---

## The Menhir

**What the Menhir are:** The sovereign powers of the Faewild: natural laws and mythic cycles given agency, scale, and court. They are not “nature gods” in a sentimental sense; they are nature as inevitability—predation, migration, metamorphosis, storm, thresholds—expressed as ruling intelligences.  
**Where the Menhir belong:** The Faewild (the right yolk), though their effects can bleed into the Material through thin spaces, pacts, or rites.  
**How they interact with mortals:** Mortals can bargain with them, offend them, serve them, or be inducted into their regimes—but the Menhir are not dependent on worship. They would remain what they are even if humanity vanished.  
**How they are organized:** By tiers (“circles”) that reflect how fundamental and large-scale the law is.

---

## Father Sun and Mother Moon

**What they are:** Second-circle anchors within the Menhir hierarchy—dominant luminary sovereigns whose portfolios are so foundational that they shape entire regimes of natural behavior and mythic law.  
**What they govern (in-setting logic):** Not “sun worship” and “moon worship,” but the deeper natural realities those luminaries represent: exposure, dominance, heat, fertility pressure, tides, instinct, cyclic return, and transformational timing.  
**Where they belong:** The Faewild’s higher structure, with projection influence across Material nature.  
**Why they matter:** They are “main players” because their logic touches everything living.

---

## The Star Children

**What they are:** Specialized lesser offspring of Father Sun and Mother Moon—Solar Sons and Midnight Daughters—each embodying a narrower, sharper natural function within the parent’s portfolio.  
**What they do:** They handle specific “sub-laws” (particular kinds of heat, particular kinds of night, specific timings, instincts, exposures) and are often the powers mortals encounter first when dealing with the Second Circle.  
**Where they belong:** Still within the Faewild’s regime, but smaller than the great circle powers and below the seasonal Horsemen in raw authority.  
**Why they work well narratively:** They give you high-myth “nature divinities” that can interact with stories without feeling like ultimate gods.

---

## The Idols

**What the Idols are:** The sovereign engines of the Shadowlands: devouring conceptual patterns given rule. They are not mere moral metaphors; they are predatory structures that feed through identity, society, attention, obsession, desire, shame, envy, appetite, and other psychic pressures.  
**Where the Idols belong:** The Shadowlands (the left yolk), with intrusion into the Material through thin spaces, cult infrastructures, and psychological terrain.  
**How they interact with mortals:** Idols thrive when communities become “legible” to their feeding logic. Their worship is less about hymns and more about building conditions—habits, institutions, and social architectures—that continuously produce the right kind of psychic output.

---

## Saints

**What Saints are:** Mortals sanctified during life by the White Flame, serving as advanced officers and exemplars of the Redeemer’s single church. Saints are not only fighters; they are builders, shepherds, founders, and living proofs of rightward coherence.  
**What Saints do:** They expand and strengthen the church and contest rival claims on souls. They stabilize thin spaces, dismantle predation infrastructure, protect clean routing to the Vestibule, and shape communities into resilience against Idol mapping and patron debt.  
**Why relics matter:** Saints often leave behind relics that function as portable rightward anchors—high-density coherence objects that stabilize reality and disrupt corruption vectors.  
**How they vary internally:** Different traditions emphasize different methods (institutional/juridical versus mystical/liturgical/ascetic), while remaining one body.

---

## Sinners

**What Sinners are:** Mortals who deliberately align with refusal and with Adversary or Adversary-adjacent forces. Unlike Saints, they do not emerge from one unified church. They arise from countless splinters, cults, bargains, and heresies.  
**What Sinners do:** They compete for souls by widening fractures: building cult infrastructures, corrupting communities, imposing debts and contracts, staging false rites, and creating the conditions for leftward influence to manifest.  
**Why they’re dangerous:** They are adaptive. “Evil” is plural and opportunistic; Sinner organizations mutate faster than the church can predict, especially in times of crisis.

---

## Verdants

**What Verdants are:** Mortals inducted into Faewild pacts—Menhir-aligned operatives who embody local natural regimes and enforce mythic ecological law.  
**Where they belong:** Primarily at the edges where Faewild influence is strong: old groves, thresholds, migrating corridors, stormlands, sacred waters.  
**How they differ from Saints:** They are powerful locally and mythically, but they are not oriented toward the Redeemer’s restoration project. Their loyalty is to living law and pact, not universal salvation.

---

## Hollows

**What Hollows are:** Mortals shaped into Shadowlands logics—Idol-aligned operatives who run vice architectures and psychic mapping infrastructures.  
**Where they belong:** Wherever communities are vulnerable: cities under strain, courts of envy, markets of greed, cultures of shame, cycles of violence.  
**How they differ from Sinners:** Hollows may cooperate with Adversary-aligned forces, but their core allegiance is usually to the Idol regime that feeds them, not necessarily to a unified “hell project.”

---

## Patrons (Egg White Powers)

**What patrons are:** Anchor-owners in the Egg White: entities, enclaves, or intelligences that possess metaphysical mass or stability cores and can lease reality to others. They are powerful not because they are ultimate, but because they control scarce stability in a drifting medium.  
**Where they belong:** The Egg White—demiplanes, dead-god debris fields, anchored cities, stability reefs near the elemental shell.  
**Why they make pacts:** They cannot easily project influence into yolk-space without intermediaries. Mortals can. So patrons trade power for agency: warlocks become extensions of the patron’s influence in the Material and the yolks.  
**How they feel in-world:** They are transactional sovereignties—some noble, some predatory, many simply solvent. Their “religion” is contract and upkeep.
