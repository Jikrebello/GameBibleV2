## **"Steel in the Ashes: The Confession of Sir Renwalt of Dornheim"**

Knight-Brother of the Order of the Silver Cross  
Recorded in the Monastery of Saint Halvar, Year 1173 Post-Ascension

> _“I speak now as one who stood where angels wept and demons bled. If I lie, may my sword rust and my soul be bound.”_

---

I was twenty-six when the horns called us to muster at **Caldros**. The Ahl-e-Ahd had taken the northern Velian states, and their war host was moving east—_not west, not to Thalmyria,_ but to **us**. They had found our weakness. Our division.

The High Duke, may he rest with the Flame, called for **all banners to rise**. We were not ready. We were never ready.

They came with **siege engines taller than towers**, their **Janissaries in black steel**, and behind them, the **bound ones**—creatures whose names the clergy will not write. Their magic came in **contracts**, written in blood and ash. I saw a single line of their scribes collapse a wall that had stood since the days of Atheria.

And still—we held.

---

We **fought for fourteen days** under storm and fire. **Stagfort** burned. **Varendor** was lost twice and won back by noon the next day. My brother fell on the third day. My squire on the fifth. On the sixth, I killed a thing that bled sand and flame.

By the seventh, we were ready to break.

Then came the deal.  
A courier arrived—hooded, eyes stitched shut—and bore a message: **aid was coming**. From **Vorthara**.

We spat.

The warlocks of the highlands were forbidden, exiled, anathematized by every council of flame. Their magic reeked of shadow and selfishness. But Caldria was dying, and dead men cannot afford purity.

The Duke **accepted their pact**.

---

Three days later, they arrived.

Not as an army—but as a **storm**. Warlocks with fire at their heels. Fleshweavers bringing beasts grown in vats. Golems etched with scripts we dared not read. They said little. They ate less. But when they fought, the **sky cracked**.

I will not glorify what I saw. Some things even a knight should not name. But I remember this: we **won**.

The Ahl-e-Ahd faltered. Their pacts bent. Their shock troops broke upon our shieldwalls, and their siege-beasts were **dragged into the ravines** and crushed beneath falling stone.

---

The final blow came in the **Valley of Halberths**, where our scouts led their vanguard into a death-trap. We had coated the slopes in oil and blood. When the sun rose behind them, we lit the flame.

I still see it—an entire legion **burning alive** in the morning light. They did not scream. Their throats had been cut first by the **Knights of Saint Aveline**, who fell upon them from above.

We call it the **Valley Ambush**. The Church calls it a **miracle**. I call it **atonement**.

---

Afterward, I walked the field. My boots squelched in ash and gore. And there, among the smoking ruins, I saw a banner: not ours, not theirs—but a **white standard** bearing the Redeemer’s flame and a serpent entwined.

It was the first sigil of the **Thalmyric rebels**, who had struck west while we bled east. They would retake **Caleran**. They would be crowned. And we would be **forgotten**.

But I remember.

I remember **Caldria’s stand**. I remember the **silvered dead**. I remember the warlocks, and the **oaths we broke to stay alive**.

If the Redeemer judges me for what we did, let Him judge with the whole truth.

We did not fight for glory. We fought because the flame was fading. And we lit it again—not with scripture, but with **steel and sacrifice**.

---

> _“Flamma in Cinere. Flame in the Ashes.”_  
> — Motto carved into the new gate of Dornheim, rebuilt after the siege.
