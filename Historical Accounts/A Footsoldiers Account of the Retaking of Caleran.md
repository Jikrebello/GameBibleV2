_By Erion Falne, farmer of Lysvale, conscript of the Southern Ashen Host_

---

> _"They say history is written by victors. But I was not a victor—I was just a man trying to get home. This is what I remember."_

---

I was a farmer before the wars. Turned soil, not swords. My name wouldn’t have meant anything to anyone—not to the nobles, not to the priests, and not to Caedros himself, though I marched beneath his banner.

When Caleran fell the first time, we heard nothing in Lysvale for three days. Then came the screaming.

By the time we reached the mountains, the world we knew was ash. The empire gone. The Redeemer's flame extinguished. The Holy City blackened, like a coal in a brazier gone cold. They said the emperor’s blood turned the stones red.

I joined the rebel host because I had nowhere else to go. They called us the **Ashen Host**, the ones who came out of the south barefoot and ragged, our banners stitched from burnt altar cloths and funeral linens.

And at our front—**Caedros**. Not a crowned man. Not a priest. Just a commander who looked at us like we were men, not meat.

He had a sword like I’d never seen. **White flame**, they said. It didn’t burn him. Some said it only burned the guilty. Others whispered it was just a trick of light. All I know is, when he drew it before a charge, men stopped doubting and started believing.

---

### The March to Caleran

We took back **Virelos** first. Then the towns along the Iron Veins. We crossed fields salted by fear and roads stained by the dead. Every village we passed had some broken thing nailed to a church door. Some were signs. Some were people.

When the scouts came back saying the outer walls of Caleran were held by thin lines of Ahl-e-Ahd loyalists and mercenaries, we knew it was time.

Caedros didn’t give a speech. He gave us rations. He gave us orders. Then he rode out front and pointed Soltirael at the sky.

That was enough.

---

### The Assault

I fought through the **Basilica Gate**, a breach where the wall had never been repaired after the first sacking. They had lined it with pikes and burning pitch, and the first three waves of our men died screaming. I thought I would, too.

Then came the thunder. Not divine, not storm—but drums and hooves. Caldrian knights on the left flank, roaring down the narrow corridor like the charge of angels. Behind them, mages from **Vorthara**, cloaked in chain and shadow. I saw a spell tear a golem in half. I saw a woman bleed fire from her hands and kiss a prayer into the air before falling dead.

We got through.

I don’t remember the killing. Not most of it. Just flashes. A man begging in a tongue I didn’t know. The sound steel makes when it strikes bone. My hand shaking so bad I couldn’t unclench it even after the fight.

We found the square where they’d burned the relics. Where they’d strung the emperor. We put the banners back up.

---

### Caedros

He didn’t shout. He didn’t gloat. He walked through the city like a mourner.

They say he found the sanctuary. That he drove the sword into the steps. I didn’t see that.

What I saw was a man covered in dust, his shoulders bent like he carried not just a blade, but every broken promise of a dead empire.

We cheered. He didn’t.

---

### After

I went home to Lysvale when it was over. Tried to grow barley again. The soil's better now, they say. The ash from the mountains makes it richer.

Sometimes the children ask me, "Did you see the Flamebearer? Was his sword real? Did the flame burn blue or white?"

I don’t know.

But I remember the light on his face when the sun came through the smoke. And I remember thinking—

_We may not have rebuilt the empire. But we remembered how to stand._
